# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**HeritageDoor Suites** controls complete matched pre-war internal door suites for premium Warsaw, Lodz, Krakow, and Poznan renovations.

The product is a physical suite of 4-12 reclaimed internal doors from one apartment, villa, or institutional floor, with frames/casings where recoverable, hinges, handles, escutcheons, locks, and trim. Each suite is measured, photographed, numbered, stabilized, de-nailed, grouped by height/profile/panel pattern/casing depth/hardware/condition, and sold as project-ready historical continuity to HNW owners through interior architects, heritage renovation firms, boutique developers, premium property managers, and restoration-minded joinery shops.

This is not a generic antique-door shop, salvage catalog, renovation coordinator, interior-design sourcing service, or restoration contractor.

Scores and gates:

- Simulated Zero to One score: **86/100**.
- Working-chat Zero to One score: **87/100**.
- Fresh-chat Zero to One validation score: **87/100**.
- Control-point tier from real validation: **A-/B+** for owned suites, **B** until repeated pickup rights are proven.

Exact scored idea wording used in the real gates:

> HeritageDoor Suites, matched pre-war internal door sets for premium Warsaw, Lodz, Krakow and Poznan renovations. It controls complete reclaimed suites of pre-war internal doors, frames/casings where recoverable, hinges, handles, escutcheons, locks and trim removed from one property before demolition, gut renovation or modernization. It sells project-ready historical continuity to HNW owners through interior architects, heritage renovation firms, boutique developers, premium property managers and restoration-minded joinery shops. It does not sell generic antique doors, renovation management, design advice, or a salvage catalog.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Premium heritage renovations need visible architectural continuity, but the market destroys the valuable unit by splitting complete pre-war door suites into single salvage pieces; a founder can create a narrow monopoly wedge by buying or optioning full matched suites upstream before fragmentation.

## Founder Fit

The founder can validate this without status, a camera audience, or elite introductions because the first control point sits on the operational supply side: demolition contractors, estate clear-out firms, small developers, renovation crews, building administrators, antique dealers, and wood salvage yards.

The founder's data-science background helps build the structured suite record: dimensions, profiles, hardware, condition, fit constraints, photo matching, restoration path, pricing, and buyer-response memory. The family installation-company background helps with disassembly credibility, transport, storage, site etiquette, and basic condition assessment.

This is part-time feasible during POC because the hard proof is narrow: 3 controlled suites, 3 paid reservations, and one paid handoff.

## Customer And Pain

Primary buyers and gatekeepers:

- Premium interior architects working on pre-war apartments and villas.
- Heritage renovation firms.
- Boutique developers restoring high-end units.
- Premium property managers for HNW owners.
- Joiners/restorers who need coherent old doors for a specific project.

Pain:

- HNW owners pay for expensive heritage interiors, but missing or mismatched internal doors break the whole visual rhythm.
- Custom reproduction can be clean but lacks authentic patina and may be expensive on a full-apartment basis.
- Single salvage doors are usually useless for full projects because size, profile, hardware, casing depth, and condition do not match.
- The useful asset often disappears before the buyer sees it: crews remove doors during demolition, clear-outs, or modernization and sell, scrap, repaint, damage, or split the set.

The first customer does not need to trust the founder inside a HNW home. They inspect off-site controlled inventory and pay a reservation deposit on a numbered suite.

## Product Wedge

The wedge is **a complete matched suite large enough for a real premium project**:

- 4-12 matching doors from one property or floor.
- Frames/casings where recoverable.
- Hinges, handles, escutcheons, locks, and trim where recoverable.
- Measurement sheet: height, width, thickness, hinge side, casing depth, damage, glass, hardware.
- Condition grading and refit notes.
- Photo set showing every door and detail.
- Reservation contract with deposit and handoff terms.
- Optional handoff to the buyer's selected restorer/joiner.

Initial pricing target:

- Raw/prepped matched suite: **8,000-25,000 PLN**.
- Restored/refit-ready package through partner restorer/joiner: **20,000-60,000+ PLN**, depending on size, rarity, condition, and buyer scope.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

Outsiders think this is about antique doors. It is not. The scarce object is a **matched architectural rhythm**: height, panels, casings, brass, patina, and proportions repeated across a whole apartment.

The market misprices the unit because suppliers usually think in waste removal, individual door resale, or decorative salvage. Premium renovation buyers think in rooms, sightlines, and interior continuity. HeritageDoor captures the valuable unit before the supply chain destroys it.

Architectural coverage supports the premise: current Warsaw pre-war renovations often emphasize restored original elements such as interior doors, herringbone floors, brass handles, windows, and historic detail. The business does not need to prove that all HNW owners want old doors; it needs to prove that a small number of premium heritage projects will pay for complete matched suites.

## Underused Momentum

- Polish premium housing remains active in Warsaw and other large cities even as buyers are more analytical and slower-moving.
- Warsaw, Krakow, Lodz, and Poznan have visible pre-war housing stock and renovation activity.
- Premium real estate differentiation is moving beyond location into finish quality, services, and uniqueness.
- Sustainability and reuse are useful secondary narratives, but the core willingness to pay comes from authenticity and project continuity.
- Online antique and salvage markets show individual old doors and decorative pieces, but not a disciplined controlled-suites product for full premium renovations.

## Primary Control Point

The primary control point is **physical ownership plus written first-refusal/exclusive pickup rights over complete matched door suites before they are split, scrapped, overpainted, damaged, or sold as singles**.

Resource needed:

- Complete matched door suites from one property or floor.
- Upstream first-look/pickup rights from demolition, clear-out, renovation, small-developer, administrator, antique, and wood-salvage channels.

How it is secured:

- Call and visit 80 operational suppliers.
- Offer above-scrap/above-clearout cash, fast removal, and no need for seller photography/listing/storage.
- Use simple one-page pickup/first-refusal agreements: seller sends photos/video before disposal; HeritageDoor has a 24-48 hour accept window; seller gets fixed per-suite or per-door minimum plus removal.
- Buy or option 3 complete suites in the first 60 days.

Why an unknown solo founder can secure it:

- Supply-side sellers are not elite buyers; they have a timing and bulk-removal problem.
- The founder is offering cash, speed, and simplicity.
- Family installation-company credibility makes removal and transport believable.
- The ask is small: call before splitting a bulky lot, not sign an exclusive national partnership.

Concrete proof before serious capital:

- 3 written first-refusal/exclusive pickup agreements.
- 3 physically controlled suites totaling 15-30 doors.
- Each suite documented with measurements, hardware, condition, and restoration/refit path.
- 3 paid deposits of 2,000-10,000 PLN from serious gatekeepers for full suites.
- One paid handoff to a buyer-selected restorer/joiner/installer.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture 

Illustrative first-suite economics:

- Acquisition/removal: 2,000-6,000 PLN for a full suite, depending on seller and condition.
- Transport/storage/stabilization/cleaning/measurement/photos: 1,500-5,000 PLN.
- Total controlled cost: 3,500-11,000 PLN.
- Raw/prepped sale: 8,000-25,000 PLN.
- Target gross margin before founder salary: **>50%**.

Value capture comes from turning bulky, timing-sensitive supplier inventory into a scarce project unit. The founder is not paid for advice; he captures spread because the suite is controlled before the premium buyer can access it.

### Distribution Moat 

The first distribution moat is upstream memory and reliability:

- Supplier list of who sees complete suites before fragmentation.
- Fast-pickup reputation among crews and clear-out firms.
- Payment history with sellers.
- Suite documentation that architects can trust quickly.
- Buyer-response history by profile, height, panel style, city, and condition.

The second distribution moat is gatekeeper habit:

- Architects and restorers learn to ask HeritageDoor before specifying reproduction.
- Boutique developers use controlled suites to differentiate restored apartments.
- Joiners/restorers get repeat supply without hunting single pieces.

## Monopoly Path

Phase 1: Become the known buyer for complete pre-war door suites in Warsaw.

Phase 2: Build a controlled inventory of 20-40 suites, with a searchable internal profile map by style, height, count, hardware, and condition. The public surface can be minimal; the asset remains the controlled inventory.

Phase 3: Expand to related matched architectural elements only when they share the same upstream control point: casings, brass, wall panels, built-ins, and staircase/entry components from complete lots. Avoid drifting into generic antiques.

Phase 4: Own the "full-suite before fragmentation" category across major Polish heritage-renovation cities.

## Local Adaptation Defense

This is local and operational:

- Door sizes, tenement layouts, renovation norms, supplier behavior, and restoration networks are city-specific.
- Relationships with small demolition/clear-out operators are fragmented and non-obvious.
- Pickup speed and cash reliability matter more than brand at the supply layer.
- Polish-language local classifieds, small crews, administrators, and informal renovation channels are hard for a generic marketplace to aggregate cleanly.

The defense is not secrecy. It is upstream density, removal reliability, inventory discipline, and paid demand for full suites.

## Why This Could Compound

The first few suites create learning that improves every later purchase:

- Which panel profiles and heights sell.
- Which conditions kill buyer confidence.
- Which suppliers reliably preserve sets.
- Which gatekeepers reserve full suites instead of browsing.
- What restoration/refit constraints must be priced upfront.
- Which cities and neighborhoods produce coherent lots.

Over time, HeritageDoor can become a narrow transaction layer between the hidden pre-fragmentation supply market and premium heritage-renovation demand. The compounding asset is not a database alone; it is controlled inventory plus first-look access plus buyer memory.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or pivot if any of these are true:

- 80 supplier contacts do not produce at least 3 complete-suite leads.
- Sellers refuse first-refusal/pickup agreements unless the founder buys random singles.
- Acquired suites are mostly incomplete, damaged beyond economic restoration, or too inconsistent for project use.
- 120 gatekeeper contacts produce praise but fewer than 3 paid deposits for full suites.
- Buyers mostly want individual decorative doors, photo inspiration, or generic sourcing.
- Restoration/refit quotes destroy the margin or make timelines unacceptable.
- Fire/acoustic/fit concerns repeatedly block use in real premium projects.
- Gross margin before founder salary cannot reach 50% on at least one handoff.
