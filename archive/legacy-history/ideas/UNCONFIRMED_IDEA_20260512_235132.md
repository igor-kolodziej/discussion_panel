# Unconfirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**CarrierProof DPD/DHL Recovery Rail** is an embedded parcel-claims recovery layer for one Polish ecommerce 3PL serving roughly 30-100 fashion and beauty D2C merchants.

The initial product does not try to automate every shipping problem. It focuses only on:

- lost domestic DPD or DHL parcels after the carrier-defined waiting deadline;
- damaged parcels where the 3PL already has packout-photo evidence plus shipment metadata.

The 3PL adds a default opt-in recovery authorization to merchant addenda for a pilot cohort. The founder receives an exclusive 18-month mandate for that cohort and handles:

- claim-pack generation;
- carrier filing;
- appeals;
- payout reconciliation;
- merchant settlement.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Small merchants under-recover courier compensation, but a 3PL aggregates enough repeated parcel exceptions to support a founder-controlled recovery rail that monetizes missed claims through delegated authority and routed transaction flow.

## Founder Fit

This fits a solo, technically strong founder because the workflow is:

- narrow enough to test manually first;
- document-heavy and suitable for AI-assisted triage, evidence extraction, and claim-pack generation;
- B2B and embedded-channel driven rather than consumer-brand driven;
- testable under 100,000 PLN without inventory, field hardware, or a large team.

The weak fit is that the first proof still requires real business development with 3PL owners or operating leads and careful contract design.

## Customer And Pain

### Primary Channel Customer

One mid-sized Polish ecommerce 3PL that:

- handles recurring parcel exceptions for many merchants;
- already sits on shipment records, support tickets, and some warehouse evidence;
- wants service differentiation without hiring a specialist claims team.

### End Economic Beneficiaries

Merchant clients of that 3PL, especially smaller fashion and beauty sellers that:

- lose margin on undisciplined claim recovery;
- do not want staff chasing carrier portals, evidence gaps, and appeals;
- often ignore small claims even when they add up over time.

### Core Pain

The loss is not one catastrophic parcel. It is a repeated stream of eligible compensation that is missed because:

- deadlines are easy to forget;
- evidence is incomplete or scattered;
- merchants do not know which cases are worth pursuing;
- the 3PL treats recovery as support overhead rather than a routed revenue process.

## Product Wedge

The wedge is a **revenue-share recovery pilot** run through a single 3PL channel.

The founder:

1. reviews the prior 90 days of recoverable exception data;
2. identifies claimable cases in the narrow DPD/DHL scope;
3. converts the 3PL into a pilot partner with exclusive recovery rights over the initial merchant cohort;
4. files and manages claims;
5. keeps a success fee only from recovered money or carrier credits.

This is intentionally not sold merchant-by-merchant. The 3PL channel is the multiplier.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

Most people would frame this as "claims automation software" or "shipping support outsourcing." The sharper insight is different:

> The scarce asset is not the claim form. It is delegated recovery authority over a concentrated parcel-exception stream before the 3PL decides the leakage is worth internalizing.

If the founder controls:

- authority to act;
- evidence flow;
- claim routing;
- and outcome data,

then the business is closer to a recovery rail than an admin service.

## Underused Momentum

- Ecommerce 3PLs are under pressure to add merchant-facing value without expanding headcount.
- Small merchants are sensitive to margin recovery but often lack process discipline.
- Carrier claims are structured enough to standardize around narrow case types.
- AI can reduce founder labor on classification, evidence completeness checks, drafted appeals, and payout reconciliation.

## Beachhead Market

Start with:

- one Polish 3PL;
- one 10-merchant pilot cohort;
- DPD and DHL only;
- two claim classes only:
  - lost parcels after deadline;
  - damaged parcels with warehouse photo evidence.

That keeps the workflow narrow enough to prove or kill quickly.

## Primary Control Point

The intended control point is:

- **delegated recovery authority** from merchants through the 3PL;
- **exclusive claim-processing mandate** over the pilot cohort;
- **routed exception flow** from the 3PL into the founder's process;
- **claim-outcome dataset** for these specific carrier and claim combinations.

### Cold Start & Resource Bootstrapping

#### Resource Needed

One 3PL must grant:

- a signed pilot agreement;
- access to the prior 90 days of relevant exception records;
- ongoing exception-feed access for the pilot scope;
- permission to deploy a merchant authorization addendum;
- an exclusive recovery mandate for the pilot cohort.

#### How It Is Secured

Use a no-upfront, revenue-share offer:

- "We review historical recoverable exceptions at no fixed cost."
- "We only earn from recovered money or credits."
- "You gain merchant value and differentiated service without hiring."

The initial ask is narrower than a platform sale. It is:

- one merchant cohort;
- one historical archive review;
- one narrow claim scope;
- one pilot recovery rail.

#### Why An Unknown Solo Founder Can Plausibly Secure It

- The 3PL does not need to trust a large deployment before seeing value.
- The founder is not asking for new carrier contracts or core operational replacement.
- The proof artifact is money recovered from claims the 3PL was not systematically monetizing.
- The founder can reduce friction by starting from an archive review and converting only cases with visible recoverable value.

#### Proof Before Serious Capital Is Spent

Within two months, the founder should produce:

- one signed 3PL pilot agreement;
- one approved authorization/addendum template;
- 10 opted-in merchants;
- access to 90 days of historical exceptions;
- at least 50 claims screened;
- at least 25 evidence-complete claims submitted;
- first paid recoveries or carrier credits;
- recovery-rate and founder-hours-per-claim metrics.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture

The base monetization model is:

- success fee on recovered PLN or carrier credits;
- optional later platform fee only after repeatable recovery economics are proven.

Key variables:

- average recovery value per eligible claim;
- success fee percentage;
- evidence-complete claim rate;
- paid/approved recovery rate;
- founder time per claim;
- merchant/3PL expansion rate.

The business becomes attractive only if:

- recovered value is meaningful enough;
- manual effort falls quickly;
- and the 3PL channel keeps adding claims without equivalent founder labor growth.

### Distribution Moat

The intended distribution moat is:

- one 3PL can unlock many merchants at once;
- the founder embeds into an existing service workflow;
- successful recoveries create expansion pressure inside the same 3PL;
- claim-outcome data should improve carrier-specific triage and expected-value scoring over time.

The moat is weak if the founder never gets exclusivity or routed transaction flow.

## Monopoly Path

The monopoly-oriented path would be:

1. dominate one recovery lane inside one underserved 3PL stack;
2. expand to all merchants under that 3PL;
3. extend to adjacent carrier and claim classes only where economics are clearly proven;
4. replicate into other 3PLs after the first operating playbook is hard to dismiss;
5. compound carrier-specific outcome data and evidence scoring until the founder becomes meaningfully better than ad hoc in-house handling.

## Why This Is Not Merely A Service Wrapper

It is more than "we file claims" only if all of the following hold:

- exclusive mandate exists;
- merchants authorize action by default through the 3PL;
- exception flow is routed automatically;
- the founder owns outcome learning from repeated claim classes;
- the process improves recovery rates while lowering manual effort.

If those do not happen, the concept collapses into a specialized admin service and loses its strategic edge.

## Two-Month POC / POV

### Weeks 1-2

- identify 20-30 Polish ecommerce 3PLs with fashion/beauty merchant books;
- contact founder/operations leads with a no-upfront recovery pilot;
- screen for historical parcel-exception volume and available evidence;
- select one partner with the clearest archive and willingness to test a merchant addendum.

### Weeks 3-4

- finalize pilot agreement and authorization structure;
- gain archive access;
- build manual-plus-AI claim triage workflow;
- screen historical exceptions and rank cases by claimability and expected value.

### Weeks 5-6

- submit first evidence-complete DPD/DHL claims;
- create appeals workflow;
- track labor time, missing evidence, and rejection causes.

### Weeks 7-8

- reconcile recoveries and credits;
- calculate net recovered PLN per founder hour;
- ask for merchant cohort expansion or contract extension only if the economics are strong.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or radically change the idea if:

- no 3PL is willing to grant exclusive pilot authority;
- merchant authorization cannot be made default-opt-in in practice;
- carriers require claimant behavior that prevents efficient delegated handling;
- fewer than 25 evidence-complete claims can be submitted from the pilot corpus;
- first recoveries are immaterial relative to labor;
- the 3PL wants only a tool but will not route claims through the founder;
- there is no measurable recovery lift over the status quo.

## Budget And Time

### Budget Ceiling

Keep the first proof under roughly:

- legal/template review;
- light workflow tooling;
- data extraction/automation;
- small outreach and pilot-ops costs.

The pilot should stay far below the 100,000 PLN maximum and should not require major capital deployment.

### Founder Time

The early version is compatible with part-time execution only if:

- outreach is tightly targeted;
- the claim scope remains narrow;
- evidence scoring and claim-pack assembly are aggressively automated.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

1. Build a target list of 20-30 Polish ecommerce 3PLs with visible D2C specialization.
2. Draft one-page pilot offer and one-page claim-scope definition.
3. Run outreach to operations/founder-level contacts.
4. Hold discovery calls only with 3PLs willing to discuss archive review and authorization mechanics.
5. Pre-audit one candidate 3PL's exception corpus:
   - DPD/DHL shipment count;
   - lost/damaged ticket volume;
   - claimable value;
   - photo/evidence completeness;
   - current claim recovery baseline.
6. If one partner qualifies, negotiate the narrow pilot agreement and begin archive triage.

## What Needs To Be Improved Before This Could Be Reconsidered As Confirmed

The idea does **not** currently qualify as a confirmed business idea. To become strong enough for reconsideration, it needs a materially harder control point and stronger operating proof.

Specifically, the next version must prove:

- one 3PL routes claims automatically by SOP, not casually or case-by-case;
- 10 or more merchants authorize by default through signed addenda;
- delegation and settlement mechanics work cleanly for the chosen claim classes;
- at least 50 cases are screened and at least 25 evidence-complete claims are actually submitted;
- at least 10-15 recoveries are paid or formally approved;
- recovery performance is materially better than the 3PL's prior process;
- founder labor per claim drops to a level that makes the economics scale;
- the 3PL extends, expands, or deepens the mandate after seeing results;
- the founder's data and process become more than paperwork, namely a reusable carrier-specific decision system that is difficult to rebuild casually in-house.

Without those improvements, CarrierProof remains an interesting recovery-service wedge rather than a confirmed control-point company.
