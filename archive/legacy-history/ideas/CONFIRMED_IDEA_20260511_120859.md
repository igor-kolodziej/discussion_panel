# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**CBAM Import Continuity Desk** is a Polish broker-first B2B service for customs brokers and freight forwarders serving SME importers of CBAM-covered goods, starting with repeated steel and aluminium import flows.

The first version is manual, not SaaS. A broker sends existing import documents for an importer client. The desk returns a broker-branded CBAM continuity memo covering CN/TARIC exposure, 50-tonne threshold risk, authorised CBAM declarant readiness, missing supplier-emissions evidence, default-value cost exposure, supplier evidence requests, and 2026 declaration preparation.

The broker can forward the memo to its client as a proactive import-continuity asset. The importer gets a concrete risk and action packet before goods are delayed, mispriced, or escalated into a CBAM compliance mess.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Polish customs brokers already control the import-document bottleneck for SME steel and aluminium importers, and CBAM turns that bottleneck into a paid continuity-risk service because missing authorisation, weak supplier evidence, or default-value exposure can now affect customs release and future certificate cost.

## Founder Fit

- The founder can start part-time with AI-assisted document analysis, structured research, templates, and written B2B delivery.
- The work fits a Warsaw data scientist: classification logic, document extraction, risk scoring, regulatory synthesis, supplier-response tracking, and repeatable analysis.
- It avoids camera content, influencer marketing, heavy networking, and large upfront capital.
- Poland/EU context matters because the workflow involves Polish brokers, PUESC/KOBiZE/Gov.pl guidance, Polish import documents, Polish-language client communication, and EU CBAM rules.
- It is not dependent on the founder's renewable-energy family access, which makes it meaningfully different from the prior energy ideas.

## Customer And Pain

First customer:

- Polish customs brokers and freight forwarders handling repeated non-EU steel and aluminium imports for SME clients.
- Secondary economic beneficiary: SME importers of CBAM-covered goods who do not have internal CBAM expertise.

Broker pain:

- Brokers are already asked by clients whether goods can be released, which codes apply, and what documents are missing.
- CBAM creates a new client-retention risk: the importer may blame the broker when customs declarations are delayed, authorisation is missing, or CBAM document codes are wrong.
- Brokers do not want to become full CBAM consultants, but they do want a forwardable memo that makes them look proactive.

Importer pain:

- Importers may not know whether their goods count toward the CBAM mass threshold.
- Supplier emissions data may be missing, late, unverifiable, or commercially weak.
- Default values and certificate-price exposure can change purchasing economics.
- Authorisation and annual declaration preparation are easy to ignore until they threaten imports or cash planning.

## Product Wedge

Start with a manual **broker-branded CBAM exposure triage**.

Input from broker/importer:

- CN/TARIC code.
- Country of origin.
- Supplier name.
- Product description.
- Invoice and packing list.
- Import volume and mass.
- Customs declaration history if available.
- Existing CBAM authorisation/application status if known.

Output:

- One client-ready memo under the broker's brand.
- Pass/risk/urgent status for CBAM import continuity.
- CN/TARIC exposure and threshold notes.
- Missing evidence list.
- Supplier evidence request in Polish and English.
- Default-value cost exposure notes where supplier data is absent.
- Recommended next action: apply, gather supplier data, use default values, correct codes, escalate to customs/tax/legal specialist, or pause further imports until status is clear.

Pricing after free triage:

- Free: 30-minute exposure triage for up to 3 importer clients per broker.
- Pilot: 1,500-3,000 PLN for 5 broker-branded memos.
- Follow-on: 400-900 PLN per importer continuity memo, depending on document complexity.
- Do not sell ongoing software until at least 20 real importer cases show repeated patterns.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

The obvious market narrative is that CBAM is an ESG reporting or carbon-accounting problem.

The hidden wedge is that, for SME importers, CBAM first becomes an import-continuity and commercial-document problem. The immediate bottleneck is not a dashboard. It is whether the broker and importer can answer:

- Is this shipment CBAM-covered?
- Has the importer crossed or approached the relevant threshold?
- Is an authorised CBAM declarant/application path in place?
- What evidence can the supplier actually provide?
- If supplier evidence is missing, what default-value exposure should the buyer price into the relationship?
- What should be sent to the client, supplier, or customs specialist this week?

Most CBAM vendors sell platforms to importers or enterprise compliance teams. This idea starts one step earlier, inside the broker workflow, where the documents already exist and the first trust channel already exists.

## Underused Momentum

- CBAM entered its definitive regime on 1 January 2026, so the market moved from abstract preparation to operational customs and financial consequences.
- EU and Polish authorities now publish practical authorisation, threshold, registry, certificate-price, default-value, benchmark, and customs-code guidance.
- The European Commission published the first 2026 CBAM certificate price, making cost exposure less theoretical.
- Customs brokers and freight forwarders are already trusted intermediaries for import documents but often lack a packaged CBAM memo product they can resell or use for retention.
- AI-assisted extraction and structured review can make a manual desk fast enough to serve small brokers without building software first.

## Beachhead Market

The beachhead is **Polish customs brokers and freight forwarders handling repeated SME steel and aluminium import flows from outside the EU**.

Why this niche:

- Steel dominates early CBAM volume in official EU operational data.
- Steel and aluminium flows are document-heavy and broker-mediated.
- SME importers are large enough for CBAM exposure to matter but often too small for enterprise CBAM software or Big Four advisory.
- Brokers can aggregate cases across multiple importer clients, solving cold start faster than direct importer-by-importer selling.
- A broker-branded memo gives brokers an immediate reason to participate: client retention, differentiation, and a paid advisory upsell.

## Monopoly Path

The long-term monopoly path is not a generic CBAM report. It is a Polish broker workflow asset:

- Repeated CN/TARIC exposure mappings by product type, origin, and supplier.
- Supplier-response library showing which suppliers provide useful emissions evidence and which default to weak responses.
- Polish/English supplier request templates that improve with real response data.
- Default-value exposure heuristics by product/origin pattern.
- Broker-facing memo formats that become familiar and trusted.
- A case library of common import-continuity risks and escalation paths.

If the desk owns enough broker workflows, it can expand into lightweight software, partner training, supplier evidence tracking, and broker network intelligence. Broad CBAM platforms can move downward, but they are less likely to win the local broker-branded memo layer if this service captures trust and data first.

## Local Adaptation Defense

This is not a simple foreign SaaS copy.

Local defense comes from:

- Polish customs broker relationships and Polish-language sales/support.
- PUESC, Gov.pl, KOBiZE, and Polish implementation context.
- Polish import-document formats and local broker practices.
- Local knowledge of which brokers serve which import niches.
- Broker-branded delivery rather than generic importer-facing software.
- Polish/English supplier evidence requests suited to SME trade relationships.

Foreign CBAM platforms may be stronger at enterprise reporting. This idea should not fight them there. It should become the easiest way for a Polish broker to turn existing documents into a client-ready CBAM action memo.

## Why This Could Compound

- Every case improves a practical dataset of CN/TARIC ambiguity, supplier-response quality, missing evidence patterns, and default-value exposure.
- Brokers can bring multiple importer clients, so one relationship can compound into many cases.
- Repeated memos create reusable templates and triage logic.
- Supplier evidence requests become more effective as response patterns accumulate.
- The service can later expand from triage to declaration-prep support, supplier evidence tracking, broker training, and importer renewal checks.
- The founder's data-science and AI leverage can turn manual review into a rules-backed workflow without prematurely building a dashboard.

## Validation Plan

Cold start mechanism:

1. Build a list of 20 Polish customs brokers/freight forwarders handling non-EU industrial imports, using sources such as PICLIS member lists, PISiL, broker websites, LinkedIn, and customs-agency pages.
2. Send a written offer: "I will prepare 3 broker-branded CBAM exposure memos for importer clients using documents you already collect. If the memo is not useful enough to forward, do not pay."
3. Ask brokers for anonymized or lightly redacted importer cases first, not full client introductions.
4. Convert into 3 broker-led pilots with real import documents or 2 paid importer follow-on packets.
5. Only after broker pilots exist, use the memos as proof artifacts for direct importer outreach.

Data acquisition mechanism:

1. Minimum data packet: CN/TARIC code, country of origin, supplier, product description, invoice/packing list, mass/import volume, and customs declaration history if available.
2. If the broker cannot share client names, accept anonymized cases as long as CN/TARIC, origin, mass, supplier country, and product description remain intact.
3. Send a Polish/English supplier evidence request template asking for embedded emissions data, production route, installation data, verification status, and permission to use default values if real data is unavailable.
4. If supplier data is absent, still deliver value through default-value exposure, missing-evidence risk, authorisation/import-continuity status, and buyer negotiation language.
5. Track every case in a private spreadsheet: broker, importer segment, product, CN/TARIC, origin, supplier response, evidence gaps, default-value exposure, memo status, and paid conversion.

Validation success:

- At least 3 brokers provide real or anonymized importer cases with actual import documents.
- At least 2 importers or brokers pay for follow-on packets.
- Brokers say the memo is useful enough to forward under their brand.
- The first 10 cases produce repeated risk patterns, not one-off research work.

## Kill Criteria

Strict kill condition from independent confirmation:

Kill or materially pivot if, after contacting 20 relevant Polish customs brokers/freight forwarders, you do not obtain either 3 broker-led pilots with actual import documents or 2 paid importer follow-on packets; lack of real documents means the cold-start/data-acquisition thesis is false.

Additional kill signals:

- Brokers like the idea but refuse to share even anonymized CN/TARIC, origin, invoice/packing-list, and mass data.
- Brokers will not forward the memo to clients under their brand.
- Importers view the memo as generic CBAM consulting and will not pay.
- The work collapses into final legal/tax advice or verified emissions assurance, which the founder should not provide.
- Customs agencies already bundle an equivalent broker-branded memo cheaply for the same SME segment.
- The first 10 cases do not create reusable CN/TARIC, supplier-response, or evidence-gap patterns.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

Week 1:

- Build the target list of 20 brokers/freight forwarders plus 30 direct backup importers.
- Draft the broker offer, NDA/light confidentiality note, data request checklist, and memo template.
- Create one synthetic sample memo for a steel or aluminium import case.

Week 2:

- Contact the first 10 brokers with the sample memo and free 3-client triage offer.
- Ask for anonymized import cases using the minimum data packet.
- Encode official CBAM sources, default values, certificate-price references, and TARIC/document-code notes into a working checklist.

Week 3:

- Contact the next 10 brokers and begin backup importer outreach only if broker response is weak.
- Deliver the first 3-5 free memos.
- Track missing data, supplier response blockers, broker objections, time per memo, and whether the broker forwards the memo.

Week 4:

- Ask brokers/importers to convert to a paid 5-memo pilot or per-case follow-on packet.
- Decide whether the strict kill condition is trending pass or fail.
- If paid pilots close, automate only repeated pieces: document intake, CN/TARIC checklist, supplier request generation, memo template, and case tracking.

## Non-Redundancy Check

Deduplication map of existing confirmed ideas:

| Existing idea | Customer | Wedge | Market/domain | Validation gate | Kill criteria |

| BatteryFit Retrofit Pack | Small Polish PV installers | Customer-facing retrofit qualification packet | PV batteries, EMS, heat storage, net-billing, subsidies, OSD paperwork | 3 paid installer pilots after 20 outreach attempts | Kill if installers already trust existing tools or fewer than 3 pay/commit |
| PromoLeak Recovery Desk | Polish challenger FMCG/beauty/supplement/household brands | Recovered or avoided cash from retailer deductions and promotion leakage | FMCG trade finance, retail-media charges, marketplaces, drugstores, modern trade | Real commercial document-access pilots or paid recovery audits | Kill if brands will not share documents/pay or first audits do not find material recoverable/preventable leakage |

Why CBAM Import Continuity Desk is not a duplicate:

- Different buyer: customs brokers/freight forwarders and SME importers, not renewable-energy installers/distributors or FMCG brands.
- Different domain: customs/import continuity and CBAM, not PV batteries, heat pumps, OSD paperwork, or retailer deductions.
- Different wedge: broker-branded import-risk memo from customs/import documents, not installation passports, retrofit sales packets, or leakage registers.
- Different proof artifact: CN/TARIC/import-continuity memo with supplier evidence requests and default-value exposure.
- Different economic gate: broker-led access to importer cases and paid continuity packets, not warranty recognition, installer conversion, or recovered retailer cash.

Rejected or deferred candidates:

- EUDR Lot Passport Desk: plausible but less urgent now because application timing is later, and data acquisition depends more heavily on supplier geolocation/source evidence.
- Battery Passport Evidence Desk: interesting, but more manufacturer/distributor compliance-heavy and less immediately tied to customs release than CBAM.
- Construction Product CPR Evidence Desk: too fragmented for a first wedge and weaker immediate pain.
