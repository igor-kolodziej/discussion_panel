# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**BatteryFit Retrofit Pack** is a narrow Polish B2B service for small photovoltaic installers that helps them decide when a battery, EMS, or heat-storage retrofit is worth selling to an existing PV customer.

The first version is concierge/manual. An installer sends a customer electricity bill, current PV and inverter details, rough usage pattern, installation photos or notes, and subsidy status. BatteryFit returns a customer-facing qualification packet with conservative retrofit scenarios, compatibility notes, subsidy and OSD paperwork checks, and explicit "do not sell" warnings.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Small Polish PV installers need a defensible way to sell battery and EMS retrofits under net-billing without making weak ROI promises or losing customers in subsidy and OSD paperwork.

## Founder Fit

- The founder is a Warsaw-based data scientist with strong AI, quantitative, and automation leverage.
- The work can start part-time and mostly asynchronously through written inputs, structured analysis, templates, and automated research.
- The family renewable-energy installation company creates unfair access to real installer workflows, customer objections, sample cases, and first pilots.
- The idea avoids personal-brand content, heavy networking, and large upfront capital.
- The founder can turn messy local energy rules, tariffs, bills, and equipment constraints into a repeatable analytical product.

## Customer And Pain

The first customer is a small Polish PV installer with an existing customer base and limited internal analytical capacity.

Pain points:

- Customers with PV ask whether batteries or EMS now make sense, but the answer depends on net-billing, RCE/RCEm, consumption timing, subsidy status, and hardware compatibility.
- Installers risk overpromising savings, especially under regulatory scrutiny and changing energy prices.
- Subsidy and OSD documentation is painful, time-boxed, and error-prone.
- Vendor tools are often ecosystem-specific, while CRMs and generic calculators do not produce a trusted retrofit decision packet.
- Bad-fit retrofit sales create support burden, reputational risk, and customer disputes.

## Product Wedge

Start as a paid concierge packet for installers:

1. Installer submits customer bill, PV size, inverter model, battery/EMS candidates if known, usage pattern, and subsidy status.
2. BatteryFit returns a PDF or Doc packet within 24-48 hours.
3. Packet includes battery size range, EMS or heat-storage alternatives, conservative payback ranges, subsidy and OSD checklist, compatibility notes, and "do not sell" flags.
4. Initial pricing: 149-299 PLN per packet, or 1,500-3,000 PLN for a 10-case installer pilot.
5. The first customer-facing promise is not "maximum savings"; it is "a defensible retrofit recommendation the installer can safely show a customer."

This must not become a generic PV calculator, CRM, or B2C lead-gen site.

## Why This Could Compound

- Each case improves a proprietary dataset of Polish retrofit economics, objections, hardware combinations, and conversion outcomes.
- Tariff, subsidy, and OSD rules can become a maintained rules engine.
- Inverter and battery compatibility notes can become a practical library small installers trust.
- Installer conversion benchmarks can show which packet elements actually close compliant retrofit sales.
- Once validated manually, the highest-repeat parts can become semi-automated workflows, then installer software.
- Distribution can start through the family installer network and expand through regional installer referrals.

## Validation Plan

1. Build a one-page example packet using one anonymized or synthetic Polish PV customer case.
2. Interview the family renewable-energy company and 3-5 friendly installers about current retrofit selling, paperwork errors, and tools used.
3. Send a written pilot offer to 20 small PV installers: 3 free sample reviews or a discounted 10-case pilot.
4. Ask for money early: target at least 3 paid or clearly committed pilots before building software.
5. Measure whether the packet changes installer behavior: faster customer response, higher close rate, fewer weak-fit proposals, fewer paperwork corrections.
6. Manually track every objection and competitor substitution, especially EasySolar, GreenCRM, OpenSolar, vendor design tools, NEXBE, wholesalers, and internal spreadsheets.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or radically pivot if either condition is true:

- 10 target installers say existing tools already provide a trusted subsidy-safe, OSD-ready, multi-brand retrofit ROI packet that they use and would not replace.
- Fewer than 3 installers agree to pay for a pilot after 20 targeted outreach attempts.

Secondary kill signals:

- Installers want only free lead-gen, not paid analysis.
- Liability risk turns the packet into quasi-legal or quasi-engineering certification.
- Bill and usage data are too weak to make defensible recommendations without expensive site audits.
- Wholesalers or inverter distributors already bundle the same decision packet for free.

## Budget And Time

- First 30 days: 0-5,000 PLN, mostly for domain, basic landing page, sample packet design, paid data if needed, and small outreach tools.
- Do not spend the full 30,000 PLN validation capital unless paid installer demand is already visible.
- Time requirement: 8-10 focused hours per week.
- No hiring, paid ads, or custom SaaS build before paid pilots.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

Week 1:

- Create a sample BatteryFit packet from a synthetic or friendly installer case.
- Define the minimum input form: bill, PV size, inverter model, installation date, current tariff/settlement, rough usage pattern, battery candidates, subsidy status.
- Interview the family installer team for real failure modes and customer objections.

Week 2:

- Research and encode the first checklist for net-billing, RCE/RCEm, current subsidy assumptions, OSD documents, and common battery/EMS compatibility constraints.
- Prepare 2 outreach scripts: one for installers and one for wholesalers or equipment partners.
- Contact 10 installers with a concrete example packet.

Week 3:

- Deliver 3-5 manual packets, free or discounted only if the installer agrees to feedback and a paid pilot decision.
- Track time per packet, missing data, objections, and whether the recommendation is "sell", "defer", or "do not sell".
- Ask each installer what they currently use instead.

Week 4:

- Contact 10 more installers.
- Convert at least 3 into paid pilots or kill/pivot.
- If paid pilots close, automate only the repeated parts: input intake, subsidy checklist, payback scenario template, and compatibility lookup.
