# Fact-closure report — W2-L2-001

**Candidate:** Purchase of signed construction warranty retentions  
**Closure recorded:** 2026-08-22T15:19:57+02:00  
**Status:** `unknown`

## Closure finding

Polish public and commercial evidence confirms that construction warranty security exists, can remain outstanding for years, and may be supplied as cash, a bank guarantee, or an insurance guarantee. It does **not** establish a repeatable volume of completed-work claims that are simultaneously assignable, sufficiently documented, acknowledged by the debtor, and available at an economically workable discount.

Current substitutes are material:

- PKO advertises ordinary invoice financing at 0.068% per financed day.
- A current commercial guarantee source quotes approximately 1%–3% of the guarantee amount annually.
- An actual 2025 construction contract permits cash security to be replaced by an accepted bank or insurance guarantee.

These alternatives can remove future cash retentions or compete for sellers with clean invoices. Conversely, Pekao’s published factoring requirements leave a possible gap among smaller contractors and unusual long-dated claims.

The indispensable premise remains a private, contract-file-level premise.

## Indispensable premise

> A repeatable supply of accepted, assignable, debtor-acknowledged claims exists at discounts sufficient to cover legal work, funding, delays and realized deductions.

**Directly contradicted:** **No.**

An individual public contract demonstrates that warranty security is used, but not that the resulting asset is assignable or debtor-acknowledgement-ready. No public denominator, retention-purchase quotation, seller price-acceptance evidence, or matured portfolio was found.

## Exact public facts closed

1. **The legal form remains unsettled enough to attract a legislative petition.** Poland’s Ministry of Justice records receiving a January 2025 petition seeking statutory regulation of the status and form of construction warranty deposits; a response was issued in April 2025. This establishes an active legal-clarity concern, not the petitioner’s factual claims or any change in law. [Ministry of Justice petition record](https://www.gov.pl/web/sprawiedliwosc/petycja-dotyczaca-uregulowania-kaucji-gwarancyjnych-w-umowach-o-roboty-budowlane)

2. **A real 2025 contract uses substantial, conditional security.** The contract sets security at 5% of net remuneration for performance and another 5% for warranty and statutory-defect obligations. It permits money, bank guarantees, insurance guarantees, and specified sureties. Cash is returned without interest only after the security period and confirmation that defects were removed and subcontractor claims do not exist. [2025 construction-contract template](https://bazakonkurencyjnosci.funduszeeuropejskie.gov.pl/api/files/2023910)

3. **The same contract provides a direct route around cash retention.** Security may be replaced during the contract by another permitted form, subject to the customer’s express written acceptance. A warranty guarantee must be irrevocable, unconditional, renewable, payable on first demand, issued by an accepted institution, and remain effective beyond the warranty period. This confirms payer control over substitution and document form. [2025 construction-contract template](https://bazakonkurencyjnosci.funduszeeuropejskie.gov.pl/api/files/2023910)

4. **Ordinary invoice financing supplies a public willingness-to-pay proxy.** PKO advertises financing of up to 100% of a gross invoice, less a fee of 0.068% per financed day, with no limit-establishment fee and a limit up to PLN 500,000 subject to approval. On a simple basis, that equals 6.12% for 90 days, 12.24% for 180 days, and 16.32% for 240 days. These are invoice-finance costs, not retention-purchase prices. [PKO invoice financing](https://www.pkobp.pl/firmy/kredyty-firmowe/finansowanie-faktur)

5. **The modeled economics are highly sensitive to funding cost.** Applying the PKO public price only as a stress proxy—not as an available funding facility—to the dossier’s PLN 21,000 purchase consideration for 240 days gives approximately PLN 3,427 of financing cost. The dossier’s PLN 4,000 spread would then fall to about PLN 573 before file cost and losses, or approximately negative PLN 1,177 after its PLN 1,000 file cost and PLN 750 loss provision.

6. **An incumbent factor publicly excludes some smaller sellers.** Pekao states that its relevant factoring customer should have operated for at least 24 months, keep full accounts, and generate at least PLN 5 million of annual turnover. It requires the full turnover for a financed counterparty and seeks counterparty consent where the underlying agreement restricts assignment. This supports a possible underserved segment, but it does not show that warranty retentions qualify for Pekao financing. [Pekao factoring](https://www.pekao.com.pl/male-i-srednie-firmy/finansowanie-dzialalnosci/faktoring.html)

7. **Guarantees are a substantially cheaper preventive substitute when available.** A May 2026 commercial source quotes insurance-guarantee premiums of approximately 1%–3% of the guarantee amount annually, generally with a PLN 250–1,000 minimum. It also notes guarantor recourse after payment. This is commercial guidance rather than a binding quotation for a particular contractor. [2026 guarantee-cost guide](https://polisoteka.pl/slownik-ubezpieczeniowy/gwarancja-nalezytego-wykonania)

8. **The regulatory boundary is only partly closed.** KNF lists purchasing and selling monetary claims among activities treated as banking activities when performed by banks. Its explanation reserves accepting and risking other persons’ funds to authorized banks. It does not expressly determine the licensing, AML, tax, or accounting treatment of a nonbank company purchasing B2B claims solely with its own capital. That remains `counsel-required`. [KNF banking-activities guidance](https://www.knf.gov.pl/dla_rynku/procesy_licencyjne/bankowy/banki/czynnosci_bankowe_inne_rodzaje_dzialalnosci)

9. **A founder-accessible regional directory exists, but it is not a qualified seller list.** The Wielkopolska Chamber of Industry and Commerce publishes an industry-filterable member directory spanning eleven result pages. It supplies a lawful prospect-discovery route but reveals neither retention ownership nor document quality, assignment rights, debtor identity, or liquidity demand. [WIPH member directory](https://wiph.pl/czlonkowie/)

## Remaining private premises

- Number and face value of retained balances after signed acceptance.
- Whether each balance is retained remuneration, a transferred-money deposit, or another form of security.
- Remaining maturity and release-condition distribution.
- Frequency of contractual assignment prohibitions or consent requirements.
- Frequency with which debtors will acknowledge amount, maturity, defenses, and payment destination.
- Seller acceptance of an exact net purchase price after all fees.
- Open defects, penalties, set-offs, prior assignments, and competing claims.
- Realized deductions and payment delays after apparent acceptance.
- Legal-review time and cost after contract forms begin repeating.
- Availability and price of capital suitable for long-dated purchased claims.
- Frequency with which cash retention can instead be replaced by a guarantee.
- Conversion from the family-installer, accountant, lawyer, or chamber channel.
- Debtor concentration within the accessible regional seller network.

## Cheapest resolving test

Run a non-funded document-and-price census before purchasing anything:

1. Assemble complete retention files from unrelated subcontractors and debtors using the existing family-installer and accountant channels.
2. Record contract, invoice, acceptance, defect correspondence, retention ledger, maturity conditions, assignment clause, and prior-disposal evidence.
3. Have Polish counsel classify each distinct contract form and prepare one debtor-acknowledgement form.
4. Give each seller a fully loaded, non-binding cash price including legal, servicing, funding, delay, and deduction assumptions.
5. Obtain written indications of seller acceptance and debtor acknowledgement without transferring money.
6. Record the proportion and face value that survive every step, along with review cost and elapsed time.
7. Only then purchase one small, short-dated claim using founder capital and hold it through contractual payment.

The first six steps resolve the supply, assignability, acknowledgement, and price-acceptance components at document-review cost. The final step closes actual payment timing and deduction behavior.

## Lawful-structure boundary

**Status:** `counsel-required`

- Confine the initial activity to individually identified B2B claims.
- Use company or founder capital only until counsel confirms the boundary for external funding.
- Require written assignment, precise asset schedule, debtor notice, and written debtor acknowledgement.
- Obtain contract-level classification of retained remuneration versus a separate deposit.
- Preserve all debtor defenses, set-offs, defect rights, and contractual release conditions.
- Require seller representations concerning title, authority, authenticity, completeness, prior disposal, defects, and offsets.
- Decline disputed, consumer, undocumented, previously assigned, or restructuring-affected claims.
- Do not treat the KNF page as a nonbank licensing exemption.
- Obtain Polish opinions on financial-regulation, AML, tax, VAT, accounting, insolvency, and investor-liability treatment before funding purchases.
- Where an accepted guarantee can replace cash, treat replacement as the incumbent route rather than manufacturing a receivable purchase.
