## Evidence calibration

### Verified

- Construction warranty security and retained remuneration exist, sometimes with long and conditional release periods.
- Polish claims are generally assignable, subject to statute, contract, claim nature, and surviving debtor defenses.
- Retained remuneration and a separately funded deposit can have different legal treatment.
- Debtors may retain defect, deduction, set-off, and contractual-condition rights after assignment.
- Ordinary invoice financing exists, with a cited public price of 0.068% per financed day.
- Some incumbent factoring criteria exclude smaller businesses.
- Contracts may permit replacement of cash security with bank or insurance guarantees.
- A lawful regional prospect-discovery channel exists through installer, accountant, and chamber relationships.

### Interpreted

- Small contractors can have genuine liquidity pain after completing accepted work.
- A narrow gap may exist for clean but unusual retention balances that ordinary factors decline.
- Contract-template repetition and realized payment data could eventually lower underwriting costs.
- The cited 1%–3% annual guarantee price is a meaningful route-around indicator, but not a binding quotation for any particular seller.

### Counsel-required

- Classification of every claim as retained remuneration, deposit, or another security form.
- Assignment restrictions, required consent, and transfer of related rights.
- The nonbank own-capital regulatory perimeter.
- AML, tax, VAT, accounting, insolvency, and investor-liability treatment.
- Referral compensation and professional-rule constraints.
- The final purchase agreement, representations, notices, and debtor acknowledgement.

### Unknown

- Number and face value of claims satisfying every eligibility condition.
- Seller acceptance of fully loaded prices.
- Debtor willingness to acknowledge amount, defenses, maturity, and redirected payment.
- Remaining-maturity distribution.
- Realized deductions, delays, defaults, fraud, and correlated debtor losses.
- Repeat legal-review and servicing cost.
- Channel conversion and debtor concentration.
- Whether data and template reuse produce meaningful operating leverage.

## Founder Wealth assessment

### Conservative case

With a small, own-capital book, fixed legal costs and one material deduction can consume several successful spreads. Capital preservation is plausible; substantial wealth creation is not established. The dossier’s conservative case ends around PLN 70,000 of recoverable founder wealth despite using leverage, reinforcing the downside concern.

### Expected-success case

The illustrative claim produces PLN 1,690 contribution on PLN 21,000 over eight months: approximately 8.0% per capital turn before corporate tax, central overhead, idle cash, and founder labor.

If that contribution were continuously repeatable, applying 19% corporate tax and 95% reinvestment implies roughly 9.3% annual capital growth. PLN 100,000 would become only about PLN 186,000 after seven years, before central overhead. This is an interpretation, not a forecast.

The dossier’s PLN 2.38m expected case is not applicable because it depends on debt and outside equity. Even that case misses 5m PLN.

### Strong case

The stated PLN 6.61m strong case is likewise inapplicable because it depends on PLN 9.34m of debt and PLN 1.20m of outside equity.

Starting from PLN 100,000, reaching PLN 5m solely through retained own-capital growth requires approximately:

- 75% annual compounding over seven years;
- 30% annually over fifteen years; or
- 21.6% annually over twenty years.

None is supported by the bound loss, pricing, review-cost, or payment-history evidence. An additional PLN 100,000 from the founder’s parents cannot be counted without knowing whether it is a gift, debt, or equity; liabilities or dilution must be recognized.

### Wealth conclusion

- **Double counting:** Face collections are not revenue, and retained earnings cannot be added both to company value and founder cash. No speculative sale multiple should be added without support.
- **Ownership:** The frozen model preserves 100% ownership only by refusing outside equity.
- **Capital:** Own capital limits book size and diversification; reinvestment alone is slow.
- **Operating leverage:** Possible after repeated templates and matured data, but continuing counsel, acknowledgement, and exception handling may remain labor-intensive.
- **Plausibility:** The conservative and expected cases do not approach the target. The strong case relies on prohibited capital sources.
- **Path to 5m PLN:** Not credibly established for the frozen candidate.

## Decisive commercial premise

> A repeatable supply of accepted, assignable and debtor-acknowledged claims exists at discounts sufficient to cover legal work, funding, delays and realized deductions.

**Closure:** Public evidence leaves this premise **private**. It neither closes nor contradicts it. Contract-file access, seller price indications, debtor acknowledgements, and matured collections are required.

## Principal failure modes

**Strongest route-around:** Replacing cash retention with an accepted bank or insurance guarantee, reportedly costing approximately 1%–3% annually. This prevents the receivable from becoming available and may be much cheaper than a 12%–18% sale discount.

**Distribution failure:** Accessible contractors do not produce enough complete, unrelated files that survive legal classification, and debtors refuse acknowledgement even when sellers accept pricing.

**Economic sensitivity:** The PLN 4,000 headline spread on the illustrative PLN 25,000 claim falls to PLN 1,690 under dossier allowances. At the cited 240-day funding proxy it becomes approximately negative PLN 1,177. Maturity, capital turnover, legal cost, deductions, delay, and concentration can each erase the spread.

**Execution risks:** Incorrect asset classification, anti-assignment clauses, undisclosed defects or prior assignments, debtor insolvency, set-offs, tax or AML errors, document fraud, concentration, manual review burden, and a long feedback cycle before payment outcomes are known.

**Lawful role boundary:** The SPV acts only as principal purchasing specifically identified B2B claims with founder equity. It must not accept deposits, client money, external investor funds, leverage, consumer claims, disputed claims, undocumented balances, or restructuring-affected claims. Every purchase requires counsel-reviewed classification and assignment mechanics, precise written documentation, notice, acknowledgement, preserved debtor defenses, and seller representations.

## Fastest credible first-customer evidence test

The existing path is a non-funded document-and-price census followed by one short-dated purchase:

1. Obtain complete files from unrelated contractors.
2. Have counsel classify the contract forms and assignment mechanics.
3. Issue fully loaded, non-binding prices.
4. Obtain written seller indications and debtor acknowledgements.
5. Purchase one eligible claim and hold it through payment.

**Time:** A price indication is targeted within two working days after receiving a standard complete file; file diligence is modeled at up to ten days. Total sourcing and acknowledgement time is unknown. Realized payment evidence then requires the remaining contractual maturity, intended to be no more than approximately six months for the proof claim, plus any delay.

**Maximum capital at risk:** The frozen text defines it as purchase consideration plus no more than PLN 3,000 of legal/accounting work. For the illustrative PLN 21,000 purchase, that is **PLN 24,000**. Because the frozen candidate does not separately cap purchase consideration, PLN 24,000 is illustrative rather than a formal absolute ceiling.

**Dependencies:** Family-installer access, contractor documents, Polish counsel, accounting review, debtor cooperation, seller price acceptance, secure records, and a dedicated payment process.

**Approval-gated actions:** Yes. The census requires paid counsel/accounting engagement and third-party cooperation. The paid proof additionally requires signed sale and assignment documents, debtor acknowledgement, and founder-capital transfer. This advisory report authorizes none of those commitments.
