## Factor findings

1. **Core insight — 7.0:** The distinction between commodity QR hosting and long-lived, provenance-preserving multi-brand data custody is sound. The regulatory architecture supports third-party operation under authorization.

2. **Customer pain and willingness to pay — 5.6:** Compliance and lifecycle-data work are real, and managed commercial pricing exists publicly. Realized willingness to pay among accessible Polish operators remains unknown.

3. **Business model and economics — 5.2:** Prepayment, custody minimums and reusable mappings could produce attractive economics. However, the first proof ranges from a 10,000 PLN loss to a 6,000 PLN contribution before founder labor, while long-duration support, insurance and continuity costs remain unpriced.

4. **Market and beachhead — 5.3:** The proposed importer/private-label segment is focused, and Poland has a growing battery base. Physical system counts do not establish the number or purchasing volume of responsible operators.

5. **Competitive position — 4.4:** The candidate faces OEM portals, Siemens and other horizontal providers, inexpensive self-service products, ERP extensions and continued spreadsheet use. It does not yet demonstrate a position those alternatives cannot route around.

6. **Expansion potential — 6.2:** Reusable OEM mappings, installer capture, adjacent CEE markets and multi-brand portfolios provide logical expansion paths if the initial custody relationship works.

7. **Defensibility — 4.6:** Trust, integrations, templates, audit history and operational continuity can compound. Open export, prohibited secondary use and the customer's control of the data correctly limit lock-in, but also weaken structural defensibility.

8. **Strength of advantage — 5.1:** Managed reconciliation and commissioning capture can be materially better than bare software. Whether that difference is large enough to change purchasing behavior is unverified.

9. **Timing — 7.6:** The 18 February 2027 passport start creates urgency. Final access and implementation mechanics remaining unresolved also create execution uncertainty.

10. **Distribution — 5.0:** The family-installer route is unusually relevant and can identify brand chains. It may still stop at installers or resellers rather than reaching the legally responsible payer.

11. **Execution and risks — 4.8:** A narrow proof fits the founder's time and capital, with AI and contractors helping implementation. AI cannot replace written authority, counsel, OEM cooperation, insurance or durable custody operations. Scaling would require staff and standardized operations rather than indefinite founder labor.

## Evidence calibration

**Verified**

- Covered industrial batteries above 2 kWh require passports from 18 February 2027.
- The placing operator retains responsibility but may authorize another operator in writing.
- Continuing availability, differentiated access, integrity, security and interoperability are required.
- Authorized processors cannot resell or reuse protected data outside the custody service.
- Both low-cost self-service software and substantially more expensive managed-service offers are publicly advertised.
- Managed passport work can include data readiness, APIs, supplier onboarding, workflows and training.
- A local installer-to-brand route exists, although the responsible placing entities were not established.

**Interpreted**

- Data reconciliation and lifecycle operations, rather than QR generation, are likely to be the principal customer burden.
- Installer commissioning capture may differentiate the offer.
- The proposed premium is plausible only where the contract buys significant managed operations and continuing custody.
- Reusable model mappings and exception handling could eventually generate operating leverage.

**Counsel-required**

- Final Article 77(9) access rules.
- Field-specific rights to manufacturer, restricted and BMS data.
- GDPR controller/processor roles, liability allocation, insurance and business-failure continuity.
- Treatment of repurposing, waste-status transfers and recycling closure in production contracts.

**Unknown**

- The accessible Polish payer denominator and annual covered-unit volume.
- Whether a responsible operator will authorize this company.
- Whether manufacturers will provide complete and contractually usable data.
- Realized willingness to pay above commodity pricing.
- Installer-to-payer conversion, lifecycle-update frequency, manual workload, retention and renewal.
- Actual long-duration hosting, incident, insurance and migration costs.

**Contradicted**

- The cited €250,000 construction-product DPP procurement cannot serve as direct evidence of battery-customer willingness to pay.
- No indispensable candidate premise is directly contradicted by the bound evidence.

### Conservative case

The arithmetic reconciles: `3.39m revenue × 1.5 = 5.085m operating value`; 90% ownership gives approximately 4.58m, plus 0.10m distributions gives approximately 4.68m. There is no stated retained-cash double counting because operating value excludes surplus retained cash.

It nevertheless assumes 80 operators and 75,000 annual passports without a verified payer denominator. At only a 10% operating margin, the 1.5× revenue valuation equals roughly 15× operating profit. The target would require approximately 5.44m PLN of equity value before the stated 0.10m receipts, versus 5.09m modeled.

### Expected case

The arithmetic also reconciles: `12.15m × 3 = 36.45m`; 76% ownership gives 27.70m, plus 0.25m receipts gives approximately 27.95m.

The weakness is premise quality: 220 paying operators, 400,000 annual passports, 20% operating margin and a 3× revenue value for a mixed software/service operation are not evidenced. The case also requires 1.5m PLN in outside equity. If the optional 0.5m debt facility is drawn and not offset by cash, the modeled equity value should be reduced for net debt.

### Strong case

The calculation is `42.60m × 4 = 170.40m`; 58% ownership gives 98.83m, with no distributions. It requires 650 operators, two million annual passports, 30% operating margin and 7m PLN of external capital. Those assumptions require substantial automation and international distribution. Any drawn 2m PLN debt facility would also need to be deducted when moving from operating/enterprise value to founder equity.

### Overall wealth verdict

There is a mathematically plausible path to 5m PLN, and the conservative case comes close without outside equity. It is not yet an evidence-supported commercial path because customer count, managed pricing, renewal, operating leverage and valuation quality are unresolved. The expected and strong cases should not be used to justify the economics score until the payer and workload assumptions are observed.

## Decisive commercial premise

> A responsible placing operator will pay continuing managed-custody prices above commodity software because reconciliation, commissioning capture and lifecycle updates require material ongoing operations.

**Public-evidence verdict:** **Leaves it private.** Public offers establish that managed pricing and implementation work exist, but they do not establish realized purchases by accessible Polish operators or purchases from this independent custodian.

## Principal failure modes

- **Strongest route-around:** The OEM supplies a complete passport portal and upstream data as part of the battery relationship, eliminating both the independent custodian and its data-access route.
- **Distribution failure:** Installer introductions identify brands but never reach the responsible placing operator with authority and budget.
- **Economic sensitivity:** The proof can lose 10,000 PLN before founder labor. At scale, margins depend on reconciliation and support becoming repeatable; long-lived records, incidents and manual exceptions can erase the modeled 72–80% gross margins.
- **Execution risks:** Missing upstream/BMS data, unsettled access mechanics, role mistakes, cyber or continuity failures, uninsurable liability and excessive manual processing.
- **Lawful role boundary:** Operate only under explicit written authority; leave Article 77 responsibility with the placing operator; preserve provenance and role controls; permit open export; prohibit resale and unrelated analytics; and make no conformity-certification or notified-body claim.

## Fastest credible first-customer evidence test

Use the frozen candidate's paper-first paid authorization test through the family installer:

1. Identify the exact placing operator for one model.
2. Offer the fixed ten-passport readiness batch for 8,000 PLN.
3. Require signed Article 77 authorization, access to manufacturer and serial data, agreed commissioning fields, defined update authority, and a cleared 4,800 PLN advance before technical work.
4. Commission scoped legal review only after commercial acceptance.
5. Complete up to ten live or pre-production records and obtain acceptance within six weeks.

**Time:** Up to six weeks; the indispensable first-customer premise is materially resolved earlier when authorization, usable data access and the advance payment are obtained.

**Maximum capital at risk:** 18,000 PLN gross for the complete proof. With the minimum 4,800 PLN advance cleared, the maximum net funding requirement is 13,200 PLN, although 18,000 PLN remains the prudent gross-risk ceiling because refund and liability terms require counsel.

**Dependencies:** Correct payer identification, customer authority, upstream data, installer commissioning information, legal terms, basic identity/access/logging/backup infrastructure and customer acceptance.

**External commitments or approval-gated action:** **Yes.** The test requires a customer contract, written authorization, data-access commitments and advance payment. Production custody additionally requires external legal review and counsel confirmation of access, liability, privacy and continuity terms.
