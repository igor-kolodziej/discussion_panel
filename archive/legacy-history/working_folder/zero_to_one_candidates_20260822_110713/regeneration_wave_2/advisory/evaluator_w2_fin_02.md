## Factor assessment

- **Core insight — 7.0:** Reaching projects through installers before the first binding commitment addresses a genuine timing failure in the certificate process. It is narrower and more useful than generic consulting, but the workflow itself is established.
- **Customer pain and willingness to pay — 6.5:** Missing the filing window can destroy a valuable right. A documented 90/10 transaction and percentage-based procurement establish occurrence of willingness to pay, but not acceptance frequency in this installer channel.
- **Business model and economics — 5.5:** Success-based compensation aligns incentives and can produce attractive contribution on suitable 50-toe projects. Margins become fragile at smaller volumes, while audit funding and a several-to-more-than-twelve-month process create working-capital exposure.
- **Market and beachhead — 6.5:** Regulatory issuance volume is economically meaningful, and 30–50 toe commercial projects form a comprehensible wedge. The accessible number of such pre-commitment projects is unknown.
- **Competitive position — 4.5:** The family installer may provide a useful initial opening, but owners can self-file or use KAPE, TAURON, NAPE, auditors, or other providers. There is no exclusive supply, authorization, or regulatory position.
- **Expansion potential — 6.0:** The workflow could extend across HVAC, controls, lighting, insulation, industrial equipment, auditors, and installer networks. Expansion still depends on acquiring channel relationships individually.
- **Defensibility — 3.5:** Process data and installer integration could improve screening, but the data asset is not inherently exclusive and incumbents can reproduce the workflow. Durable switching costs or network effects are not established.
- **Strength of advantage — 5.0:** Early intervention may prevent lost eligibility and reduce owner effort. No evidence yet establishes materially better economics, speed, trust, conversion, or outcomes than incumbent services.
- **Timing — 6.0:** The statutory mechanism is active and supported by meaningful issuance. No evidence establishes an unusually favorable new regulatory or competitive window.
- **Distribution — 5.5:** Family-installer access makes the first lead unusually credible for this founder. Repeatable distribution is private: installers may resist introducing another party before securing their own order.
- **Execution and risks — 5.0:** A single project can be tested within the founder’s capital and time limits using specialists. Scale requires auditors, counsel, operations staff, channel management, brokerage access, and substantial working capital; AI cannot replace licensed or accountable roles.

The modeled 350-project year clears that threshold, but the required channel volume, margins, financing, and valuation are all unverified.

## Decisive commercial premise

The single decisive premise is:

> Installer channels repeatedly expose eligible, still-uncommitted projects whose owners accept the proceeds share and whose complete project-specific costs preserve positive contribution.

Public evidence **leaves this premise private**. It verifies that the transaction can occur and is not directly contradicted, but it does not establish installer-channel frequency, acceptance rates, or complete contribution economics.

## Principal failure modes and boundary

- **Strongest route-around:** TAURON can provide nationwide end-to-end service, aggregate even sub-10-toe projects, and defer settlement until certificates are obtained. This directly weakens differentiation based on project size, financing timing, and completeness of service.
- **Distribution failure:** Installers do not introduce the operator before binding orders, or their pipelines produce too few properly documented 30–50 toe projects. The family relationship may yield a first lead without becoming a repeatable channel.
- **Economic sensitivity:** At the 2,216 PLN/toe inferred anchor, a 30-toe project at a 10% fee leaves only 845 PLN after the weak 5,803 PLN audit proxy—and before dossier, referral, transaction, financing, and overhead costs. A modeled 50-toe project at 15% produces about 10,000 PLN gross profit, but relatively small changes in price, fee share, audit cost, issuance volume, rejection rate, or duration materially alter contribution.
- **Execution risks:** Missing the pre-commitment window, inadequate baselines, owner project changes, partial or refused issuance, long clarification cycles, unreliable auditor capacity, client-money errors, insufficient brokerage access, and working-capital exhaustion.
- **Lawful role boundary:** The company may act only as a disclosed authorized representative and transaction operator. It must not claim to be URE, TGE, a broker, an obligated party, or the independent auditor. No backdating, concealed commitments, fabricated baselines, duplicate savings, undisclosed title arrangements, off-registry sales, or commingling of owner funds is permissible. Technical responsibility stays with a competent independent auditor; exact fiduciary and tax mechanics require Polish counsel.

## Fastest credible first-customer evidence test

Use the family installer’s live quotation pipeline before any owner signs a binding implementation order:

1. Screen five current projects using equipment, baseline-consumption, order, and implementation information.
2. Obtain one independent auditor’s fixed-price eligibility screen and complete audit quote.
3. For one suitable 30–50 toe project, present the owner with the exact proceeds split and every deduction.
4. Obtain a counsel-reviewed, signed, commencement-conditional mandate and authorization.
5. File the audit-backed application before commitment and follow it through completion, issuance, and sale.

- **Time to signed first-customer evidence:** approximately 30–90 days if a suitable live lead exists; this is a planning estimate.
- **Time to realized fee:** plan for **6–18 months**, with longer cases possible.
- **Maximum founder capital at risk:** **20,000 PLN** of unrecoverable audit, counsel, dossier, and submission expenditure for the first project.
- **Dependencies:** timely installer access, adequate records and savings, fixed audit economics, owner acceptance, counsel-approved terms, independent audit capacity, project completion, URE issuance, registry/brokerage access, and sufficient sale liquidity.
- **Approval-gated actions:** **Yes.** The test requires an owner contract and authorization, auditor and counsel engagements, payment commitments, a pre-commencement regulatory filing, and later brokerage/registry arrangements. It cannot be completed as a purely internal desk exercise.
