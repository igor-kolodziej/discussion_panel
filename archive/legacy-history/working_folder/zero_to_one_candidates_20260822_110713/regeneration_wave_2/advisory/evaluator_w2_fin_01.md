2. **Pain and willingness to pay — 6.2:** Grid scarcity and institutional demand for grid-secured BESS projects are verified. Willingness to pay for a complete RTB project does not establish willingness to pay for this substantially thinner option package.

3. **Business model and economics — 5.7:** Founder loss is capped, the developer funds capital-intensive work, and modeled gross margins are attractive. Reservation prices, assignment prices, close rates, expired-option losses, cash-cycle duration, repeat demand, debt terms, and mature margins are not observed.

4. **Market and beachhead — 6.3:** One connected owner and one qualified storage developer form a suitably narrow first transaction. The number of technically suitable sites and unique qualified payers remains unknown.

5. **Competitive position — 4.9:** Time-limited exclusivity provides transaction control, but owners and developers can reproduce the process, and integrated utilities can develop internally. No basis yet exists for local dominance.

6. **Expansion potential — 6.4:** Successful execution could expand across DSO territories, referral networks, repeat institutional buyers, portfolios, and complementary technologies. Expansion depends on standardization surviving operator-, lender-, and site-specific variation.

7. **Defensibility — 5.1:** Conversion data, document standards, buyer relationships, operator knowledge, and referral channels could compound. Options expire, procedural knowledge can diffuse, and neither data nor contracts presently form a demonstrated moat.

8. **Strength of advantage — 5.6:** A screened, exclusive data room could save developers time and failed-origination cost. There is no evidence yet that the saving is sufficiently large to change purchasing behavior or justify the modeled fees.

9. **Timing — 7.4:** The 2026 reform, substantial connection refusals, storage-development activity, and completed cable-pooling arrangements create favorable timing. Post-reform storage-option adoption remains unmeasured.

10. **Distribution — 5.7:** The family installer relationship is a credible first supply route, and named storage developers are prospectable. The model nevertheless requires synchronized two-sided acquisition, and directory membership does not establish budget, qualification, or willingness to transact.

11. **Execution and risks — 5.8:** The first proof is affordable and uses available specialists. Execution remains exposed to incomplete data, technical failure, owner authority, land rights, lender consent, operator practice, legal enforceability, and a founder operating part-time.

## Founder Wealth assessment

### Conservative case

- Six annual transactions produce PLN 0.90m revenue and PLN 0.12m operating profit.
- At 4× operating profit, plus PLN 0.20m excess cash and approximately PLN 0.002m prior distributions, modeled founder wealth is **PLN 0.682m**.
- This case does **not** provide a path to PLN 5m by year 7.

### Expected-success case

- Twenty-four annual transactions at PLN 225,000 produce PLN 5.40m revenue and PLN 1.68m operating profit.
- `5 × 1.68m = 8.40m` enterprise value.
- `8.40m − 0.60m debt + 0.70m excess cash = 8.50m` equity value.
- `85% × 8.50m + 0.015m distributions = PLN 7.24m` founder wealth.
- Under the same price, 31.1% margin, 5× multiple, debt, cash, and ownership assumptions, roughly **17 annual transactions** would mathematically cross PLN 5m. The dossier’s 24-transaction case provides numerical headroom, but none of the commercial inputs is observed.

### Strong-success case

- Sixty annual transactions at PLN 300,000 yield PLN 18m revenue and PLN 8m operating profit.
- At 6× operating profit, with PLN 2m debt offset by PLN 2m cash and 70% ownership, founder wealth is **PLN 33.6m**.
- The arithmetic reconciles, but 60 complex transactions annually, 75% gross margin, and 44.4% operating margin require substantial standardization and operating leverage that the evidence does not establish.

### Wealth-quality checks

- **Double counting:** No explicit double counting appears if the valuation multiples produce enterprise value excluding excess cash and the stated cash is genuinely surplus. Cash required for ongoing options or working capital cannot also be treated as excess.
- **Ownership:** The 100%, 85%, and 70% assumptions are internally used correctly. Actual adviser/team dilution is unknown.
- **Capital and liabilities:** The PLN 30,000 first-proof ceiling fits the founder’s PLN 100,000 capital. The expected and strong cases introduce PLN 0.60m and PLN 2m debt, correctly deducted from equity. Terms, personal guarantees, and founder-level liabilities are unknown.
- **Operating leverage:** Standardized screening, documents, buyer diligence, and repeat referrals could create leverage. Site-specific legal, lender, operator, and engineering work may instead keep the business service-heavy.
- **Plausibility:** The conservative case is plausible but insufficient. The expected case is possible, not evidence-backed. The strong case is numerically coherent but aggressive.
- **Path to PLN 5m:** A path exists through business equity, but it is valuation-dependent and requires repeatable deal flow at approximately two transactions per month or comparable economics. It is not yet a robust wealth path.

## Decisive commercial premise

The single decisive premise is:

> One technically credible owner will grant an enforceable short option, and one qualified developer will pay a non-refundable reservation while funding all operator-facing costs.

**Public-evidence finding:** the premise is **left private**. Public evidence neither closes nor contradicts it. The RTB acquisition establishes appetite for a much more complete asset, not this product.

## Principal risks

- **Strongest route-around:** A developer or integrated owner can source the site, hire its own engineer and counsel, develop against its existing connection, or buy a complete RTB project.
- **Distribution failure:** The founder may fail to secure an eligible owner option and paid developer reservation within the same 60–90-day window. Named prospects do not prove active budget or willingness to pay.
- **Economic sensitivity:** Outcomes are highly sensitive to assignment price, reservation size, technical pass rate, option expiry, cycle duration, owner participation, professional costs, repeat buyers, and whether the developer truly bears every operator-facing cost.
- **Execution risks:** Incomplete interval or connection data, unsuitable profiles, land limitations, lender vetoes, applicant-party requirements, operator delay, protection costs, founder bandwidth, and bypass by owners or introducers.
- **Lawful role boundary:** The SPV may control contractual cooperation, exclusivity, data, nomination, and conditional land/project rights. It must not claim ownership or guaranteed transfer of grid capacity; statutory parties and applicants remain visible, approvals remain conditions, and operator-facing funds should be paid directly or through ring-fenced arrangements subject to Polish counsel.

## Fastest credible first-customer evidence test

**Time:** eight weeks.  
**Maximum founder capital at risk:** **PLN 30,000**.  
**Target:** one cleared PLN 15,000–30,000 non-refundable developer reservation.

Required sequence:

1. Counsel reviews the owner option and developer reservation.
2. One real owner supplies complete connection documents and 12–24 months of interval data.
3. A licensed engineer conducts the limited screen.
4. The owner executes a 60-day conditional option.
5. The redacted package is presented to at least 15 qualified developers.
6. One developer signs and pays the reservation while committing separate funds for operator-facing work.

Dependencies include the installer introduction, owner authority, complete records, engineer availability, counsel, usable land rights, preliminary technical credibility, and a qualified developer.

**Approval-gated nature:** Yes. The test requires external professional engagements, an executed owner NDA/data-access arrangement and option, and an executed developer reservation with cleared funds. These are external contractual commitments and require explicit founder authorization before money is spent or binding obligations are accepted. Operator approval is not itself necessary to observe the reservation, but remains essential to later transaction completion.
