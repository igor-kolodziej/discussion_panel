## Exact Candidate

Use a founder-owned Polish SPV and only its own equity to purchase narrowly screened, signed and accepted B2B construction warranty-retention receivables from small contractors. Every first-cohort claim must have a fixed face amount and maturity, completed-work acceptance, document evidence for release conditions, seller representations, counsel-confirmed assignment mechanics, and written debtor acknowledgement of amount, defenses, maturity and payment destination. The SPV pays an agreed discounted purchase price and receives the debtor payment at maturity. It accepts claim-specific deduction and delay risk but never takes deposits or external investor funds and never presents an unacknowledged dispute as a certain receivable.

The first product is not broad factoring. It is a tiny unleveraged purchase book of unusually clean, short-dated warranty balances that ordinary factors do not serve.

## Customer, Payer And Trigger

- Seller/customer: a small contractor with an accepted retention balance and a genuine liquidity preference.
- Cash payer at maturity: the construction customer/debtor.
- Paid trigger: debtor pays the acknowledged assigned balance to the SPV.
- Seller value: immediate cash at a transparent discount without a loan repayment obligation if the sale is a true purchase under counsel-reviewed terms.

## Contract And Risk Boundary

1. File-level counsel classifies the asset and confirms assignment or required consent.
2. Debtor acknowledges balance, maturity, known defenses and redirected payment.
3. Seller warrants authenticity, prior assignment status, acceptance, tax/accounting facts and disclosed disputes.
4. Purchase price reflects maturity, funding cost, expected deductions and file cost.
5. Concentration limits apply by debtor, seller and project.
6. No external client money, deposits, leverage or onward retail investment is used in the proof portfolio.

## First Paid Proof And Maximum Loss

- Source ten candidate files through installers, accountants and the regional chamber directory.
- Reject any file lacking signed acceptance, documentable release conditions or debtor acknowledgement.
- Buy one small claim only after counsel and accounting review.
- Founder-funded ceiling for the first claim: the purchase consideration plus no more than 3,000 PLN of legal/accounting work.
- Do not fund a second claim until the first debtor acknowledgement process and realized economics are recorded.

## Economics — Planning Assumptions, Not A Market Quote

The dossier illustrates purchasing a 25,000 PLN face-value claim for 21,000 PLN, producing a 4,000 PLN gross spread before file cost, loss provision and funding cost. A public invoice-finance price used only as a stress proxy makes a 240-day version unattractive: modeled funding cost of about 3,427 PLN leaves approximately negative 1,177 PLN after the dossier's file and loss allowances.

The founder-wealth path therefore requires either materially shorter maturity, deeper seller discounts, lower repeat file cost, low realized deductions and efficient reuse of retained capital. Preventive bank or insurance guarantees priced around 1%–3% annually are a strong route around future cash retentions.

## Acquisition And Compounding Control

- First supply route: family installer relationships, regional accountants, construction counsel and chamber members.
- Demand-side control: debtor acknowledgement before purchase.
- Compounding asset: debtor payment history, contract-clause library, deduction/loss data, document standards and repeat seller/accountant channels.
- Incumbent route-around: ordinary factoring, bank credit, invoice finance and replacement of cash security with bank or insurance guarantees.

## Closed Facts And Indispensable Unknown

Public contracts verify meaningful construction security and long release conditions, and public sources verify ordinary factoring and guarantee substitutes. Public evidence does not identify a repeatable pool of claims that are simultaneously accepted, assignable, debtor-acknowledged and available at workable discounts. The nonbank regulatory, AML, tax and accounting perimeter remains counsel-required.

> The indispensable premise is that a repeatable supply of accepted, assignable and debtor-acknowledged claims exists at discounts sufficient to cover file work, funding, delay and realized deductions.

Stop if counsel cannot create a lawful own-capital purchase structure, debtors will not acknowledge, sellers prefer cheaper substitutes, stress-tested contribution is non-positive, or repeat file review remains too expensive for the available spread.
