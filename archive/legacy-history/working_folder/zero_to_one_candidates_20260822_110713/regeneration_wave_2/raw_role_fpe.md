## W2-FPE-01 — Metered electric drying capacity for grain stores

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture owns a modular electric heat-and-airflow unit at a grain store, purchases electricity through the site’s dynamic retail contract, and sells measured moisture removal per tonne of grain.
- **Economic mechanism:** Energy spread plus improved storage yield.
- **Loss bearer:** Grain owner or warehouse paying for fuel, slow drying, pest treatment and quality degradation.
- **Payer:** Grain warehouse, cooperative or large farm.
- **Beneficiary:** Grain owner and downstream buyer receiving more stable grain.
- **Authority holder:** Site owner, electricity supplier, network operator and the party controlling grain-handling procedures.
- **Distributor:** Renewable installers serving farms, grain-equipment technicians and agricultural cooperatives.
- **Information holder:** Warehouse operator holding moisture, batch, meter and storage-condition records.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears equipment utilization, electricity-price and drying-performance risk; grain owner retains title and disclosed crop-quality risk.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns the drying equipment and the metered drying-service contract.
- **First paid event:** Payment for moisture removed from the first completed grain batch.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Site-specific load curves, crop-drying response data, dispatch software and a fleet of movable heat modules.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **35,000–75,000 PLN** for a rented or fabricated 30–60 kW unit, certified electrical integration and independent moisture/energy metering at one family-accessible agricultural customer.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q08/S06:** Poland harvested about **35.0 million tonnes of grain in 2024**, including 9.4 million tonnes of maize; the ministry also reported storage problems involving pests and fumigation. [MRiRW market memorandum](https://www.gov.pl/attachment/7db41037-4c86-4843-9a2c-9aa6c8c9726d), accessed 2026-08-22.
  - **Q09/S07:** On 23 June 2024, Polish day-ahead electricity prices remained negative for seven consecutive hours, ranging from **−30.43 to −88.81 PLN/MWh**. [URE negative-price notice](https://www.ure.gov.pl/pl/oze/swiadectwa-pochodzenia/ceny-ujemne/12037%2CWytworcy-energii-z-OZE-po-raz-kolejny-musza-skorygowac-wnioski-o-wydanie-swiadec.html), accessed 2026-08-22.
  - **Q09/S10:** Dynamic-contract prices tied to the day-ahead market are published at 14:00 on the preceding day and require remotely read settlement metering. [URE dynamic-contract analysis](https://ure.gov.pl/pl/urzad/informacje-ogolne/aktualnosci/12692%2CRaporty-i-analizy-URE-w-ubieglym-roku-135-odbiorcow-skorzystalo-z-umow-z-cena-dy.html), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Available connection headroom; moisture-removal kWh per crop and weather regime; ventilation compatibility; attainable service price relative to gas, LPG and biomass; seasonal utilization.
- **Constraint class:** Electrical capacity, seasonal demand, equipment integration and commodity-quality liability.
- **Lawful structure:** Sell a drying service rather than unlicensed electricity; buy energy under an approved site contract; use certified electrical contractors and calibrated meters; leave grain handling and food/feed compliance with the licensed operator.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Unmetered savings claims, unsafe heater integration, speculative negative-price frequency or undocumented grain-quality representations.

## W2-FPE-02 — Options on verified powered industrial space

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture purchases exclusive, time-limited options to lease industrial space with documented unused import capacity and resells or assigns the powered-space lease to flexible-load tenants.
- **Economic mechanism:** Reserved-capacity option spread.
- **Loss bearer:** Property owner with idle contracted capacity and tenant or developer losing time and deposits on sites that cannot be connected.
- **Payer:** Battery, charging, refrigeration, compute or flexible-manufacturing tenant.
- **Beneficiary:** Tenant gains a documented site; property owner monetizes unused electrical and physical capacity.
- **Authority holder:** Property owner, existing connection-account holder and distribution-system operator.
- **Distributor:** Commercial property agents, renewable installers, electricians and equipment developers.
- **Information holder:** Property owner, electricity account holder, meter operator and network operator.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture loses diligence costs and option premiums when a site cannot support the disclosed load.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns the contractual site option, not the grid connection itself.
- **First paid event:** Tenant pays a non-refundable reservation or option-assignment fee after receiving the site data room.
- **Control/compounding asset:** A proprietary inventory of contractually optioned sites, interval-load histories, transformer facts and reusable diligence documents.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **8,000–25,000 PLN** to inspect approximately twenty family-network commercial sites, obtain interval data and electrical documents, commission an electrician’s report, and option one qualifying powered space.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q02/S01:** Network companies reported **7,817 connection refusals covering 73,586.12 MW in 2024**; 4,443 refusals were technical and 2,196 economic. [URE 2024 report, pp. 107–108](https://www.ure.gov.pl/download/9/15926/BiuletynURE1-2025.pdf), accessed 2026-08-22.
  - **Q10/S09:** Poland added approximately **4.2 GW of solar PV during 2024**, increasing the population of energy sites and associated flexible-load use cases. [IEA PVPS, Trends in Photovoltaic Applications 2025](https://iea-pvps.org/trends_reports/trends-2025/), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Whether unused contractual capacity is physically firm; change-of-use requirements; assignability of the lease option; site fire and planning constraints; tenant willingness to pay before equipment financing closes.
- **Constraint class:** Property rights, connection-contract interpretation, technical diligence and long enterprise cycles.
- **Lawful structure:** Option the real estate and documented use rights only; require written owner consent and network/supplier confirmation where needed; describe capacity as existing measured headroom rather than a transferable grid right.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Future network approval, political intervention, undisclosed connection applications or claims that an existing connection can automatically be transferred.

## W2-FPE-03 — Title-taking liquidation of sealed short-date food lots

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture buys title to sealed, traceable, shelf-stable food lots before their best-before dates and resells consolidated lots to canteens, outlets and discount wholesalers.
- **Economic mechanism:** Faster inventory turns and owned merchandise spread.
- **Loss bearer:** Producer, distributor or retailer facing a full write-off as remaining shelf life contracts.
- **Payer:** Institutional buyer, outlet or discount wholesaler.
- **Beneficiary:** Original owner recovers cash; buyer obtains discounted, traceable inventory.
- **Authority holder:** Food-business operator releasing the goods, sanitary authority and purchasing customer.
- **Distributor:** Regional food manufacturers, wholesalers, contract caterers and outlet operators.
- **Information holder:** Original seller holding batch, expiry, storage and recall records.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears unsold inventory, storage, recall and buyer-credit risk after title transfer.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns the food inventory and determines the resale channel.
- **First paid event:** Invoice payment for the first consolidated lot delivered to a business buyer.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Batch-level sell-through curves, buyer demand by remaining shelf life and recurring purchase rights over surplus.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **15,000–35,000 PLN** for one shelf-stable product category, compliant third-party dry storage, batch scanning, insurance and two small inventory purchases.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q07/S05:** Poland wastes more than **4.8 million tonnes of food annually**; household research cited spoilage and loss of freshness among the dominant causes, illustrating the physical expiry mechanism. [IOŚ-PIB food-waste release](https://ios.edu.pl/informacje-prasowe/mniej-marnowania-wiecej-radosci-problem-swiatecznego-marnotrawstwa-zywnosci-w-polsce/), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Recurring B2B surplus volume; achievable discount by remaining shelf life; category-specific storage requirements; recall allocation; buyer concentration; transport cost per kilogram.
- **Constraint class:** Food traceability, inventory spoilage, working capital and route density.
- **Lawful structure:** Register the relevant food activity, retain original labels and dates, maintain batch traceability and recall procedures, and begin with sealed ambient goods stored through an approved facility.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Altered dates, obscured provenance, donation economics, unsafe relabelling or sale after legal expiry.

## W2-FPE-04 — Options on recurring empty truck legs

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture pays carriers for fixed-price options on specified recurring return legs and resells exercised capacity to shippers needing those exact lanes and windows.
- **Economic mechanism:** Transport-capacity option spread.
- **Loss bearer:** Carrier pays fuel and driver cost on an empty return; shipper faces volatile last-minute transport prices.
- **Payer:** Manufacturer, wholesaler or agricultural shipper.
- **Beneficiary:** Carrier earns option premium and loaded revenue; shipper obtains reserved transport capacity.
- **Authority holder:** Carrier dispatch, vehicle operator and shipper controlling loading and delivery.
- **Distributor:** Route-specific manufacturers, carrier accountants, freight associations and warehouse operators.
- **Information holder:** Carrier TMS, telematics provider, dispatcher and shipper’s order system.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears unsold-option premiums and shipper non-performance; carrier retains vehicle-operation and cargo-carrier liability.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns a documented capacity option for a defined lane, vehicle class and time window.
- **First paid event:** Shipper purchases and exercises one optioned return movement.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Lane-level fill history, recurring shipper demand and a portfolio of contractual capacity rights.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **15,000–40,000 PLN** for one Polish corridor, three to five carriers, manual dispatch and four weeks of option premiums and cargo-liability support.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q04/S03:** Polish-registered vehicles produced **20.3% of EU road freight tonne-kilometres in 2023**; the same Eurostat publication reports **22.4% of Polish vehicle-kilometres as empty**, against 21.8% for the available EU aggregate. [Eurostat, Key Figures on European Transport 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/15216629/20875401/KS-01-24-021-EN-N.pdf), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Repeatability of specific empty legs; carrier cancellation frequency; shipper lead times; cargo compatibility; option-premium size; responsibility when schedules slip.
- **Constraint class:** Two-sided route density, operational reliability, freight liability and international transport rules.
- **Lawful structure:** Use written freight-forwarding and capacity-option contracts, licensed carriers, cargo insurance and lane-specific compliance with driving-time, cabotage and customs rules.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Informal dispatch promises, illegal cabotage, falsified empty-mile data or operator-appointed local delivery monopolies.

## W2-FPE-05 — Purchase of documented truck-detention receivables

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture purchases assignable, evidenced detention receivables from small carriers after delivery and collects the contractual charge from the shipper or broker.
- **Economic mechanism:** Recovered cash and receivable discount.
- **Loss bearer:** Carrier whose truck and driver are immobilized at a customer dock and whose small claim is uneconomic to pursue.
- **Payer:** Contractual shipper, consignee or freight broker responsible for detention.
- **Beneficiary:** Carrier receives immediate, certain cash.
- **Authority holder:** Contracting debtor, carrier, and ultimately the competent court or arbitrator.
- **Distributor:** Carrier accountants, transport-management providers and small fleet associations.
- **Information holder:** Carrier and loading site holding GPS, gate, proof-of-delivery and appointment timestamps.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears documentation defects, debtor disputes and collection time after purchase.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture takes legal title to the receivable through assignment.
- **First paid event:** Debtor settles the first assigned detention invoice.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Debtor payment curves, evidence templates, dock-delay histories and repeat carrier purchase agreements.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **20,000–60,000 PLN** to purchase a small batch of signed, business-only claims and commission legal review and collection support.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q05/S04:** In its 2025 payment-delay work, UOKiK reported examining almost **320,000 invoices from more than 9,000 suppliers** across sectors including automotive, packaging, wind energy, construction, machinery and food. [UOKiK payment-delay enforcement](https://uokik.gov.pl/public/zatory-platnicze-dzialania-prezesa-uokik), accessed 2026-08-22.
  - **Q04/S03:** Poland’s large share of EU freight activity supplies a substantial transaction denominator for narrow transport receivables. [Eurostat transport figures](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/15216629/20875401/KS-01-24-021-EN-N.pdf), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Frequency of contractually valid detention charges; anti-assignment clauses; evidence completeness; debtor dispute rates; average collection duration and legal cost.
- **Constraint class:** Receivable title, documentation quality, debtor concentration and working capital.
- **Lawful structure:** Purchase only B2B claims supported by an underlying contract, timestamps and invoice; notify debtors properly; use licensed lawyers for court work and documented, non-harassing collection.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Invented accessorial charges, coercion, consumer claims, duplicate assignments or collection of claims already waived by the carrier.

## W2-FPE-06 — Purchase of signed construction warranty retentions

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture buys assignable rights to construction retentions that become payable after an installation’s documented defect-liability period.
- **Economic mechanism:** Accelerated cash conversion and maturity spread.
- **Loss bearer:** Small electrical or renewable subcontractor funding payroll and materials while accepted work remains partially unpaid.
- **Payer:** General contractor, developer or commercial property owner holding the retention.
- **Beneficiary:** Subcontractor receives cash before contractual maturity.
- **Authority holder:** Contractual debtor, supervising engineer or acceptance certifier, and courts.
- **Distributor:** The seven-person family renewable installer, its subcontractor network, accountants and electrical trade groups.
- **Information holder:** Subcontractor and general contractor holding the contract, acceptance protocol, defect notices and payment history.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears debtor insolvency, hidden-defect and documentation risk after assignment.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns the specifically assigned retention receivable.
- **First paid event:** Contractual debtor pays the first retention at maturity.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Contractor payment histories, defect-frequency data and standardized document requirements by contract type.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **15,000–50,000 PLN** to purchase two to five small retentions supported by signed acceptance protocols and short remaining maturities.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q05/S04:** UOKiK’s recent payment-delay actions explicitly included construction and wind-energy companies and involved thousands of suppliers. [UOKiK payment-delay enforcement](https://uokik.gov.pl/public/zatory-platnicze-dzialania-prezesa-uokik), accessed 2026-08-22.
  - **Q10/S09:** Poland installed approximately 4.2 GW of PV in 2024, supporting a continuing flow of installation contracts and post-completion obligations. [IEA PVPS 2025](https://iea-pvps.org/trends_reports/trends-2025/), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Prevalence and size of assignable retentions; anti-assignment wording; debtor default correlation; undisclosed defect claims; practical notification mechanics.
- **Constraint class:** Legal title, construction defects, counterparty concentration and capital turnover.
- **Lawful structure:** Obtain contract-specific legal review, signed acceptance evidence, seller representations and debtor notification; exclude disputed claims and consumer contracts.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Concealed defects, forged acceptance, unenforceable verbal assignments or aggressive collection disconnected from the underlying contract.

## W2-FPE-07 — Owned roof-material lifting cells rented by the day

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture owns certified material hoists, temporary edge-protection components and handling fixtures and sells installers prepaid access to complete equipment cells by the day.
- **Economic mechanism:** Faster crew throughput and physical-asset turns.
- **Loss bearer:** Installer paying skilled crews during manual panel and material movement or waiting for fragmented rental equipment.
- **Payer:** Solar, roofing or electrical installer.
- **Beneficiary:** Installer, crew and building owner.
- **Authority holder:** Equipment owner, installation employer, site controller and relevant technical-inspection authority.
- **Distributor:** Family renewable firm, roofing wholesalers and local installer referrals.
- **Information holder:** Installer holding job dates, roof geometry, access restrictions and material counts.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears equipment utilization, maintenance and damage risk; installer retains site supervision and work-at-height responsibility.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns the equipment fleet and reservation calendar.
- **First paid event:** Installer prepays for one complete equipment-cell day.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Physical fleet, utilization records, job-configuration library and installer reservation history.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **25,000–55,000 PLN** for one inspected material-only hoist, compatible handling fixtures, edge-protection inventory, trailer and insurance.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q10/S09:** Poland’s 4.2 GW of new PV installations in 2024 represents continuing physical panel-handling work, independent of software adoption. [IEA PVPS 2025](https://iea-pvps.org/trends_reports/trends-2025/), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Achievable paid days per month; equipment compatibility across roofs; transport radius; breakage and theft rates; winter utilization; inspection requirements.
- **Constraint class:** Capital utilization, equipment safety, logistics and seasonality.
- **Lawful structure:** Supply inspected equipment with documented load limits and setup procedures; restrict material hoists from lifting people; require trained users, fall protection and contractual allocation of site duties.
- **Prohibited reliance:** People-lifting with material equipment, untrained setup, bypassed fall protection or exclusive control of installer dispatch.

## W2-FPE-08 — Title-taking reuse of tested PV-module lots

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture purchases intact decommissioned PV modules before waste designation, tests and matches them into documented lots, and resells the lots with a limited measured-output warranty.
- **Economic mechanism:** Owned refurbishment spread and recovered asset yield.
- **Loss bearer:** System owner facing removal cost and a weak resale channel; buyer facing unknown degradation and mismatched modules.
- **Payer:** Off-grid integrator, agricultural user, replacement buyer or legally compliant export customer.
- **Beneficiary:** Original owner receives a removal value; buyer receives measured, matched equipment.
- **Authority holder:** Module owner, electrical-safety tester, reuse buyer and waste authority if any item becomes waste.
- **Distributor:** Renewable installers, repowering contractors and off-grid equipment suppliers.
- **Information holder:** Original system owner, inverter portal, test laboratory and module manufacturer.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears inventory, testing, warranty and rejected-module disposal cost.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns each accepted module and its serial-level test record.
- **First paid event:** Buyer pays for the first tested, serialised module lot.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Serial-level provenance, degradation curves, matching software, buyer demand and deinstallation supply agreements.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **20,000–45,000 PLN** for 50–100 modules from one documented deinstallation, outsourced flash/electroluminescence testing, transport racks and compliant disposal of rejects.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q10/S09:** Global cumulative PV capacity passed 2,260 GW at the end of 2024, while Poland added 4.2 GW during the year; this growing installed base creates a future stream of replacements, repowering and damaged-array matching needs. [IEA PVPS 2025](https://iea-pvps.org/trends_reports/trends-2025/), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Polish reuse-versus-waste classification at title transfer; deinstallation lot frequency; test cost per module; resale price by measured output; product-liability exposure; permitted export destinations.
- **Constraint class:** Waste classification, electrical safety, warranty liability, inventory density and resale demand.
- **Lawful structure:** Transfer title while modules remain documented reusable products; test every accepted unit; route rejects through an authorised WEEE partner; retain transport and serial records.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Exporting waste as reusable goods, dumping rejected modules, fabricated performance certificates or undisclosed fire and insulation defects.

## W2-FPE-09 — Acquisition and redemption of deposit-bearing containers from venues

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture buys eligible intact deposit containers from offices and venues for immediate cash and redeems the acquired batches through contractually confirmed return channels.
- **Economic mechanism:** Deposit-recovery spread and batch handling efficiency.
- **Loss bearer:** Venue occupants or operators surrendering refundable containers because individual sorting and return take too much time.
- **Payer:** Confirmed return point or system operator settling accepted containers.
- **Beneficiary:** Venue receives a predictable share of recovered value without handling individual returns.
- **Authority holder:** Container holder, authorised return point, system operator and environmental authority.
- **Distributor:** Office facility managers, event venues, hotels and workplace caterers.
- **Information holder:** Venue holding consumption data and operator holding label/SKU eligibility data.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears rejected labels, storage, transport and legal-classification risk.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns the acquired physical containers and any lawfully transferable redemption entitlement.
- **First paid event:** A return point settles the first accepted commercial batch.
- **Control/compounding asset:** Recurring venue supply contracts, label-recognition data, contamination history and dense indoor collection routes.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **5,000–15,000 PLN** for manual scanning and storage of one venue cluster after receiving written commercial-batch acceptance from independent return channels.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q03/S02:** Poland’s deposit system has operated since 1 October 2025; collection points may exist outside retail stores, multiple system operators are permitted, and beverage producers finance collection, transport, recordkeeping and settlement. [Ministry of Climate deposit-system rules](https://www.gov.pl/web/klimat/system-kaucyjny-zasady), accessed 2026-08-22.
  - **Q03/S02:** Required selective-collection levels are stated as 77% from 2025 and 90% from 2029. [Ministry of Climate deposit-system rules](https://www.gov.pl/web/klimat/system-kaucyjny-zasady), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Whether commercial transfer of the redemption entitlement follows possession; batch limits at return points; container rejection rate; BDO implications; operator settlement terms; storage cost per container.
- **Constraint class:** Deposit entitlement, waste classification, operator interfaces, fraud controls and route density.
- **Lawful structure:** Obtain written operator or return-point terms before custody; document purchase and provenance; register transport or collection activity if legally required; retain audit-ready container records.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Duplicate redemption, foreign-container arbitrage, fabricated labels, unlicensed waste transport or informal promises from a single operator.

## W2-FPE-10 — Owned pressure-control equipment paid from recovered water

- **One-sentence transaction:** The venture funds and owns district pressure-control and temporary leak-localisation equipment while a water utility pays per reconciled cubic metre of reduced network input at unchanged billed consumption.
- **Economic mechanism:** Shared physical-loss savings and infrastructure utilization.
- **Loss bearer:** Water utility and ultimately tariff payers funding abstraction, treatment and pumping for water that never reaches a customer.
- **Payer:** Municipal or private water utility.
- **Beneficiary:** Utility, municipality, customers and the local water resource.
- **Authority holder:** Utility operator, municipality, water regulator and public-health authority.
- **Distributor:** Regional water engineers, municipal associations and certified utility contractors.
- **Information holder:** Utility holding bulk-meter, pressure, pump, repair and customer-billing data.
- **Risk bearer:** Venture bears equipment and measured-performance risk; utility retains network operation, repair decisions and water-quality responsibility.
- **Transaction owner:** Venture owns the pressure-control equipment and the measured-output performance contract.
- **First paid event:** Utility pays the first invoice tied to reconciled avoided cubic metres.
- **Control/compounding asset:** District baselines, pressure-response models, leak signatures and a reusable equipment fleet.
- **Founder-accessible first proof with capital range:** **25,000–70,000 PLN** for rented bulk meters and acoustic equipment, one owned pressure-control skid, independent baseline work and a certified utility contractor at a small district.
- **Evidence atoms:**
  - **Q12/S08:** In more than half of the audited water networks, losses remained between **20% and nearly 58%**. [NIK water-loss audit](https://www.nik.gov.pl/najnowsze-informacje-o-wynikach-kontroli/straty-wody-w-wodociagach-zachodniopomorskie.html), accessed 2026-08-22.
  - **Q12/S08:** NIK reported an average direct production cost of approximately **0.55 PLN per lost cubic metre**, exceeding 10% of the water price, and found that utilities often lacked systematic loss analysis. [NIK water-loss audit](https://www.nik.gov.pl/najnowsze-informacje-o-wynikach-kontroli/straty-wody-w-wodociagach-zachodniopomorskie.html), accessed 2026-08-22.
- **Decision-critical unknowns:** Utility procurement route; baseline stability; proportion of economically recoverable leakage; pressure-related service constraints; payment treatment under approved tariffs; sales-cycle duration.
- **Constraint class:** Municipal procurement, baseline integrity, public-health safety and slow contracting.
- **Lawful structure:** Utility retains all operational authority; certified personnel install approved materials; an independent protocol reconciles bulk input against billed consumption, outages and seasonal demand.
- **Prohibited reliance:** Monitoring without physical intervention, unauthorised valve operation, unsafe pressure reduction, compromised water quality or savings inferred solely from model output.
