# W2-L2-009 — Principal buyer of separated demolition fractions

**Research timestamp:** 2026-08-22T14:52:37+02:00  
**Geographic beachhead:** Ostrów Wielkopolski and processor routes reachable without entrant-operated storage  
**Development status:** Desk-research dossier only. No contractor interviews, processor commitments, legal opinion, BDO registration, insurance quotation, or paid load has been obtained.

## 1. Transaction boundary

The entrant contracts in its own name to purchase and resell specified construction-waste batches while the waste moves directly from the contractor’s site to a permitted processor.

The initial model must distinguish commercial title from physical possession:

1. The contractor segregates and classifies the waste.
2. The entrant conditionally purchases the compliant batch and invoices an acceptance/capacity fee.
3. A BDO-registered carrier transports it directly to the designated processor.
4. The processor weighs, inspects and legally takes possession.
5. Final price and acceptance are reconciled against weight and contamination.
6. The entrant pays the carrier and processor, receives any material proceeds, and retains the spread.

Under the Waste Act, a waste dealer may buy and resell waste in its own name without physically possessing it. Such dealers and brokers require registration in BDO. A non-possessing dealer does not assume statutory responsibility for waste management merely by participating in the sale. The KPO must still represent the actual transfer between holders and identify participating dealers or brokers. [Waste Act, definitions and Articles 27, 50, 66–69](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2023/1587/text.html)

“Acceptance” should therefore mean **commercial acceptance subject to processor confirmation**, not possession at an entrant yard. Any entrant-operated consolidation, storage or physical re-sorting would move the activity toward regulated collection or processing.

## 2. Problem and payer evidence

### Verified evidence

Since 1 January 2025, the professional generator of construction and demolition waste must ensure segregation of at least:

- wood;
- metals;
- glass;
- plastics;
- gypsum; and
- mineral waste, including concrete, bricks, tiles, ceramics and stones.

The statute describes six named fraction groups, with several materials inside the mineral group—not seven independent groups. The duty can be delegated only by a written agreement to an eligible entity under Article 27(2), with joint and several responsibility among the specified parties. [2024 Waste Act amendment, Article 101a](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2024/1834/text.html)

The environmental inspectorate likewise describes source segregation and written delegation to an authorised undertaking. [WIOŚ guidance](https://www.gov.pl/web/wios-gdansk/obowiazek-segregacji-odpadow-budowlanych)

A commercial collection tariff illustrates an observable price distinction between separated and mixed loads. In one 2025 schedule:

- mixed renovation waste plus rubble and gypsum costs PLN 450 for an open bag/load up to 1.5 tonnes and PLN 550 up to 2 tonnes;
- separated rubble costs PLN 340 and PLN 390 respectively;
- separated gypsum or glass costs PLN 390 and PLN 490;
- separated wood costs PLN 370 and PLN 430;
- separated plastics cost PLN 370 and PLN 460.

The same schedule excludes municipal waste, asbestos, hazardous waste, roofing felt and other unlisted material. This is a directional commercial benchmark from northern Poland, not an Ostrów processor quote. [Konbrud 2025 tariff](https://konbrud-bigbag.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/CENNIK-uslug-BIG-BAG-09.2025-1.pdf)

NIK describes mineral material as the principal component of construction and renovation waste, with concrete, brick and ceramic material capable of relatively simple crushing and screening. Painted wood may instead require thermal treatment, illustrating why identical-looking “wood” batches can have different destinations and costs. [NIK circular-economy report, pp. 187–188](https://www.nik.gov.pl/plik/id%2C30907%2Cvp%2C33980.pdf)

### Interpretation

The payer’s economic loss is not merely the statutory obligation. It is the combined cost of:

- excess containers for poorly planned segregation;
- processor rejection or downgrade;
- emergency re-sorting;
- repeat transport;
- crew and equipment delay;
- uncertain capacity and quotation effort; and
- inability to recover value from clean metal or mineral fractions.

The entrant is useful only if it reduces this combined cost more reliably than a container supplier, processor or contractor’s own dispatcher.

## 3. Payer denominator

### Defined denominator

The payer is a professional generator or holder of construction and demolition waste, principally:

- small demolition contractors;
- refurbishment and fit-out firms;
- roofing contractors;
- specialist installers removing old materials;
- general contractors managing subcontractor waste; and
- property-maintenance firms running repeated renovation work.

Households are outside the initial payer denominator.

### Numerator required for a defensible count

The serviceable denominator is:

> Unique professional contractors within economical direct-haul distance of at least two eligible processors, producing at least three specification-compatible batches annually.

A usable count requires deduplicating:

1. relevant REGON construction activities;
2. BDO-registered waste generators and holders;
3. local tender and permit activity;
4. container-company account lists obtainable through partnerships; and
5. evidence of actual construction-waste loads during the previous twelve months.

The fixed searches did not produce a target-specific regional count. The current numerical denominator is therefore **unknown**. “First 100 customers” is an operating acquisition objective, not evidence that 100 suitable repeat payers exist in the initial catchment.

Before committing substantial capital, a list of at least 300 named local prospects should be assembled and tagged by fraction, expected annual loads, processor route and decision-maker.

## 4. Incentive, power and risk map

| Participant | Receives | Controls | Bears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contractor/generator | Predictable outlet, one quote, fewer rejected loads | Source segregation, waste code inputs, loading, project timing | Statutory generator duties, contamination created on site, agreed charges |
| Entrant/dealer | Acceptance fee, processing spread, recovery proceeds | Commercial quote, downstream reservation, carrier selection, specifications | Contractual rejection, adverse pricing, credit and reservation risk |
| Processor | Gate revenue or clean feedstock | Final inspection, weighing, acceptance specification and capacity | Permitted processing and physical-site compliance |
| Carrier | Transport fee | Vehicle availability and documented movement | Transport execution; undocumented transport can create possession exposure |
| Recovered-material buyer | Specification-compliant output | Purchase specification and commodity price | Output-market demand |
| Environmental authority/BDO | Traceable lawful movement | Registration, permit scope, audit and enforcement | No commercial risk |
| Container/skip supplier | Container and haul revenue | Site access and contractor relationship | Asset utilisation and haul cost |

### Power concentrations

- **Processor:** strongest short-term power because it can reject the load after transport cost has been incurred.
- **Contractor:** controls advance information and contamination at source.
- **Container incumbent:** controls the habitual purchasing channel.
- **Entrant:** initially controls little beyond coordination and conditional capacity. Bargaining power appears only after accumulating repeat supply and credible alternative processor routes.

## 5. Price, unit economics and cash timing

### Recommended quotation structure

For early batches:

> Actual transport + actual processor charge + PLN 650–1,000 coordination/capacity fee + explicit contamination reserve, less any contractually shared recovery proceeds.

Fixed all-in quotes should be offered only after obtaining processor-specific composition history. Each quote should state:

- waste code and plain-language description;
- photos or sampling method;
- maximum contamination;
- weight or volume basis;
- permitted destination;
- rejection and downgrade schedule;
- waiting-time charges;
- who receives material proceeds; and
- when commercial acceptance becomes final.

### Illustrative 10-tonne batch

These are modeling assumptions, not verified local prices.

| Item | PLN/batch |
|---|---:|
| Contractor acceptance/capacity charge | 4,300 |
| Expected recovered-material proceeds | 500 |
| **Revenue** | **4,800** |
| Processor/gate cost | 1,450 |
| Licensed transport/container | 1,250 |
| Inspection or sampling | 150 |
| Contamination/rejection reserve | 550 |
| Variable BDO/administration | 100 |
| **Variable cost** | **3,500** |
| **Gross contribution** | **1,300** |
| **Gross margin** | **27.1%** |

A clean mineral batch may have lower revenue but lower processor cost. A mixed wood, gypsum or contaminated batch can invert the economics. Metal proceeds should not subsidise quotes for other fractions until actual ownership, quality and buyer settlement are documented.

### Cash timing

Initial target terms:

- contractor: 50–70% deposit when capacity is booked;
- remaining contractor balance: within three days after accepted scale ticket;
- carrier: 7–14 days;
- processor: prepayment or seven days until credit history exists;
- recovery proceeds: 7–30 days.

If PLN 3,500 of variable cost is prepaid, ten simultaneous batches require PLN 35,000 before rejection losses. At 25 batches, exposure reaches PLN 87,500. The available PLN 100,000 therefore supports proof and tightly controlled direct-haul trading, not an owned fleet, yard or broad unsecured credit.

## 7. Acquisition route

### First ten customers

1. Three roofing or refurbishment contractors introduced through the family installer’s builder relationships.
2. Three contractors referred by two container or skip suppliers that cannot efficiently price every separated fraction.
3. Two demolition or fit-out firms reached through construction estimators.
4. Two local contractors won with a processor-backed quote for a named upcoming batch.

The offer is a written, fraction-specific quote—not general “waste management.” Each first customer should have a scheduled batch, photographs, estimated mass and a named processor route before signing.

### First one hundred

Proposed source mix:

- 20 from renewable-installation and builder networks;
- 25 from skip/container partnerships;
- 25 through outbound prospecting to demolition, roofing and refurbishment firms;
- 20 through estimators, quantity surveyors and general contractors;
- 10 from existing-customer referrals.

Planning assumptions:

- blended acquisition cost: PLN 1,000–2,000 per activated repeat account, excluding founder time;
- an account should produce at least three annual batches;

The sales unit is the **next named batch**, not an annual framework agreement without committed volume.

## 8. Control and compounding asset

The defensible operating asset would consist of:

- processor-specific acceptance specifications by code and composition;
- photos, scale tickets, downgrades and rejection outcomes for every load;
- predicted-versus-actual batch-margin history;
- recurring contractor supply agreements;
- conditional processor slots;
- carrier performance and waiting-time records;
- contamination scores by contractor and site supervisor; and
- rapid quotation rules for recurring batch types.

The most valuable data is not generic waste pricing. It is the relationship:

> contractor + site supervisor + fraction description + photographs + processor + season → accepted weight, downgrade and realised margin.

This data can reduce reserves for reliable contractors while charging more—or declining work—from recurring contamination sources.

## 9. Incumbent route-around

Incumbents can bypass the entrant in several ways:

- processors can quote contractors directly;
- container companies can bundle container, transport and disposal;
- contractors can hire an internal waste coordinator;
- larger demolition firms can reserve their own processor capacity;
- processors can reproduce the entrant’s acceptance checklist;
- clean metal can be sold directly to scrap merchants; and
- an entrant’s carrier can solicit the contractor after learning the route.

Practical responses are multi-processor coverage, customer-specific composition history, rapid multi-fraction quotation, non-circumvention clauses where enforceable, and making the contractor’s workflow simpler than dealing separately with five outlets. Exclusivity without service levels or committed capacity is weak protection.

## 10. Rules and constraints

### Binding rules evidenced

- Professional construction-waste generators must ensure segregation under Article 101a.
- Delegation of that statutory segregation duty requires a written agreement with an eligible Article 27(2) entity and creates specified joint responsibility.
- Waste dealers and brokers require BDO registration.
- A dealer may buy and resell waste without physical possession.
- A non-possessing dealer does not automatically assume waste-management responsibility.
- A KPO is prepared before transport by the transferring holder; the actual carrier and receiving holder confirm their roles.
- The person ordering transport must identify the destination and receiving holder.
- Physical collection or processing generally requires the applicable permit.
- Simplified or absent record-keeping is fact-specific; BDO’s guide notes exemptions and simplified records but does not remove the need to analyse each participant and waste stream. [BDO registration and record-keeping guide](https://bdo.mos.gov.pl/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Informacje-dot.-wpisu-do-Rejestru-BDO.pdf)

### Initial lawful structure

- Register the entrant in BDO as a waste dealer and, if applicable, broker.
- Accept only non-hazardous codes covered by written processor confirmation.
- Keep physical movement contractor → processor.
- Show the entrant’s commercial role in the relevant documentation.
- Make processor acceptance a condition precedent to final commercial acceptance.
- Do not represent the entrant as performing the contractor’s Article 101a segregation duty.
- Use only a BDO-registered carrier.
- Do not unload, consolidate, store, sort or reclassify waste.
- Obtain written advice on title, invoicing, KPO entries, VAT and insurance before the first load.

### Classification

| Constraint | Class | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Article 101a segregation | Binding law | Creates demand but does not authorise the entrant |
| Dealer/broker BDO entry | Binding registration | Required before principal trading |
| Collection/processing permit scope | Binding law | Direct-haul, no-storage structure initially |
| Waste code and contamination | Technical/legal | Quote and acceptance must be code-specific |
| Processor rejection | Unit economics | Requires deposits and contractual pass-through |
| Five founder hours/day | Founder constraint | Dispatcher or contractor required before daily scale |
| PLN 100,000 capital | Capital constraint | No owned fleet or yard during proof |
| Commodity proceeds | Market | Treat as upside only after repeat settlement data |

## 12. Fastest paid proof and capital at risk

### Proof design

Start with one clean mineral, gypsum or uncontaminated wood batch of approximately 5–15 tonnes:

1. Obtain written specifications and conditional capacity from two processors.
2. Obtain carrier quotes for direct transport.
3. Inspect and photograph the contractor’s batch.
4. Quote an actual processor-backed all-in price.
5. Collect at least 50% before dispatch.
6. Deliver directly to the processor.
7. Reconcile scale weight, acceptance, invoice and realised margin.
8. Count proof only when the contractor pays the invoice.

### Capital allocation

| Item | PLN |
|---|---:|
| Waste-law and contract review | 2,000–6,000 |
| BDO, accounting and administration | 500–2,000 |
| Processor/carrier deposits | 2,000–7,000 |
| Inspection, sampling and PPE | 1,000–3,000 |
| Insurance | 1,500–4,000 |
| Rejection/re-sort contingency | 1,000–3,000 |
| **Total initial exposure** | **8,000–25,000** |

The remaining PLN 75,000–92,000 should stay available for working capital and rejection events. It should not fund a vehicle or storage site during proof.

## 13. Kill criteria

Stop committing new loads if any of these occurs:

- Twenty processor approaches fail to produce two written conditional acceptance routes.
- Fifty qualified contractor quotes over eight weeks produce fewer than three paid batches.
- Five completed batches average below PLN 700 contribution or 15% gross margin.
- More than 10% of the first twenty loads are rejected or materially downgraded.
- A single ordinary non-hazardous batch can create an uninsured loss above PLN 3,000.
- Contractor payment terms require more than PLN 25,000 working capital at ten monthly batches.
- A legal opinion concludes that the proposed principal structure requires physical-collection permissions unavailable within the capital and time constraints.
- Suitable insurance costs more than 5% of revenue or excludes ordinary contamination and misdescription risk.
- Twenty monthly batches require more than five founder hours per working day and a paid dispatcher cannot preserve positive operating contribution.
- A processor or container incumbent can consistently quote below the entrant’s variable cost while providing equivalent multi-fraction service.
