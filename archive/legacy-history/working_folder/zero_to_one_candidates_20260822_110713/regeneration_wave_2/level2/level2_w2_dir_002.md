# W2-L2-002 — White-certificate proceeds for energy-efficiency projects

Research completed **2026-08-22 14:37:34 CEST**. Exactly eight web queries and eight sources were used. No files were edited.

## Research finding

The transaction is legally recognizable and already used commercially: an authorized service provider can prepare the audit and application, arrange sale of the resulting property rights, retain a disclosed success fee, and remit the balance to the project owner. A published tax interpretation describes this structure with a **15% net success fee plus VAT**.

The narrow opening is not generic energy consulting. It is capturing eligible projects **before the first binding implementation commitment**, initially through installers that know about projects before owners sign orders. The material constraints are:

- Applications must precede commencement.
- Documented average savings must reach the applicable 10 toe application floor, including permitted aggregation of same-type projects.
- Cash normally arrives only after implementation, completion documentation, URE processing, registry entry and sale—commercially reported as several to more than twelve months.
- A 15% success fee does not support small or technically complicated projects unless direct preparation costs are tightly controlled.
- Current executable TGE pricing, exact audit quotes, tax treatment of the proposed company, and assignment language remain unconfirmed.

## Verified problem and payer evidence

URE registered **1,979 new applications** in 2025 and issued **1,330 certificates** covering **195,851.216 toe**. It estimated the associated property rights at approximately **434 million PLN**. Applications issued during the year included filings originally made as far back as 2017, showing that the 45-day statutory period does not describe the complete commercial cash cycle. [URE 2025 report](https://bip.ure.gov.pl/download/3/20723/SprawozdaniezdzialalnosciPrezesaUREw2025r.pdf)

**Interpretation:** the loss is credible. Owners who order eligible work before filing, cannot assemble an adequate baseline, or simply do not know the program can forgo a monetizable property right.

**Unknown:** no source in the fixed budget measures how many eligible projects are permanently missed each year or how often installer customers decline because of application complexity.

## Exact transaction and incentive/power map

| Actor | Transactional role | Power or dependency | Incentive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project owner | Supplies project, baseline, designs, invoices and completion evidence | Can authorize filing, stop the project or withhold documentation | Receive net certificate proceeds without building an internal regulatory workflow |
| Installer/contractor | Identifies project before commitment and executes it | Controls the earliest useful lead and the date work becomes committed | Improve project economics and win a higher-value installation |
| Service company | Screens eligibility, contracts auditor, assembles filing, tracks URE and arranges sale | Bears agreed preparation expenditure and documentation risk | Retain the contractual share of realized proceeds |
| Energy auditor | Produces the required technical audit and later confirmation where applicable | Controls technical sufficiency; bears professional responsibility for calculations | Audit fees and repeat workflow |
| URE President | Issues certificate and checks completion conditions | Can request clarification, reduce accepted savings or refuse issuance | Administer the statutory mechanism |
| TGE registry/exchange and brokerage chain | Records and monetizes the property rights | Controls registry and trading access | Registry, clearing and brokerage fees |
| Certificate purchaser | Pays market price for the rights | Selects price and purchase timing | Obtain rights for resale or compliance |
| Obligated energy-market participant | Ultimate economic demand | May instead complete qualifying projects, use programs or pay the permitted substitute fee | Satisfy the efficiency obligation at lowest lawful cost |

The immediate contractual fee source can be the owner’s sale proceeds, while the **economic payer** is the buyer of the property rights and ultimately the obligated market participant.

URE provides current forms for the application, authorization, completion notice and auditor confirmation. [URE application materials](https://www.ure.gov.pl/pl/efektywnosc-kogenerac/efektywnosc-energetyczn/swiadectwa-efektywnosci/9501%2CWniosek-o-wydanie-nowego-swiadectwa-efektywnosci-energetycznej-skladany-po-dniu-.html)

## Payer denominator

The most defensible annual denominator is the value of newly issued property rights rather than the number of energy sellers:

- 2025 issued rights: approximately **434 million PLN**
- Issued certificates: **1,330**
- New applications: **1,979**
- Issued volume: **195,851 toe**
- Implied aggregate value: approximately **2,216 PLN/toe**
- Illustrative fee pool at 15%: approximately **65.1 million PLN**

The 2,216 PLN/toe figure is an inference from URE’s rounded aggregate value, **not** an executable TGE quote. TGE’s official market page did not expose a current quote through its static interface at the research timestamp. [TGE property-rights market](https://tge.pl/prawa-majatkowe-rpm)

URE recorded **79,017 toe** of redemptions in 2025. Issuance and redemption are not synchronized annual cohorts, so they should not be treated as identical demand measures. [URE 2025 report](https://bip.ure.gov.pl/download/3/20723/SprawozdaniezdzialalnosciPrezesaUREw2025r.pdf)

## Price, unit economics and cash timing

A published individual tax interpretation describes an authorized contractor receiving the certificates in its own name for the owner’s account, selling the rights, deducting transaction costs, charging a **15% success fee plus VAT**, and remitting the balance. It also identifies exchange, registry, clearing and brokerage expenses. The interpretation is evidence that this contract form has been used; it does not bind a new company with different facts. [KIS interpretation reproduced by Inforlex](https://www.inforlex.pl/dok/tresc%2CFOB0000000000005715232%2CInterpretacja-indywidualna-z-dnia-29-marca-2023-r-Dyrektor-Krajowej-Informacji-Skarbowej-sygn-0113-KDIPT1-2.4012.23.2023.2.PRP.html)

### Illustrative 50-toe project

| Case input | Conservative | Expected | Strong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Certificate price assumption | 1,800 PLN/toe | 2,200 PLN/toe | 2,500 PLN/toe |
| Gross rights proceeds | 90,000 PLN | 110,000 PLN | 125,000 PLN |
| Service share | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Service revenue | 13,500 PLN | 16,500 PLN | 18,750 PLN |
| Assumed audit, dossier and transaction cost | 6,000 PLN | 6,500 PLN | 7,000 PLN |
| Gross profit | 7,500 PLN | 10,000 PLN | 11,750 PLN |
| Gross margin | 55.6% | 60.6% | 62.7% |

The direct-cost figures are planning assumptions. No binding audit quotation was found within the fixed source budget.

A 10-toe project at 2,200 PLN/toe would produce only 3,300 PLN of revenue at a 15% share. That is generally insufficient if the service company pays a bespoke audit. Practical targeting therefore begins around **30–50 toe**, or uses lawful aggregation of same-type projects.

### Cash sequence

1. Owner and service company sign before the first binding project commitment.
2. Service company pays for screening and audit.
3. Application is filed before commencement.
4. Owner completes the project and supplies evidence.
5. Completion is notified and any required confirmation is supplied.
6. URE completes its review and sends the relevant information to TGE.
7. Rights are registered and sold through the required market access.
8. Sale costs and the disclosed service share are deducted; the balance is remitted.

KAPE states that URE nominally has 45 days but that the complete process commonly lasts **several to more than twelve months**. It also says trading occurs through brokerage access and requires participation in the certificate registry. [KAPE FAQ](https://kape.gov.pl/faq)

## Acquisition route

### First ten customers

1. First eligible, uncommitted commercial or industrial project from the family installer.
2. Second heat-source or controls project from the same installer.
3. An installer customer whose project can be screened before quotation acceptance.
4. Referral from the initial audit partner.
5. Second audit-partner referral.
6. Regional HVAC contractor customer.
7. Electrical/lighting contractor customer.
8. Commercial-property accountant’s client.
9. Property manager with a building-system modernization.
10. Industrial energy consultant’s client.

The channel message is: **“Before you sign the implementation order, let us check whether the planned savings can create a saleable right.”**

### First one hundred customers

A concrete channel mix:

- 40 from renewable/heat/HVAC installers;
- 25 from electrical, controls and lighting contractors;
- 15 from energy auditors;
- 10 from commercial-property accountants and managers;
- 10 from customer referrals and regional energy consultants.

Channel partners should be paid only from realized service revenue, with an assumed referral share of 10–20% of the company’s fee. The service company retains control of eligibility screening, contract execution, application status and owner communication.

## Control and compounding asset

The compounding asset is a permissioned project dataset containing:

- original and replacement equipment;
- baseline consumption and calculation method;
- modeled and accepted toe savings;
- documentation completeness;
- URE clarification requests and response patterns;
- time in each process stage;
- issued volume versus audit volume;
- realized price and transaction costs;
- auditor accuracy and turnaround;
- installer/channel conversion and cancellation rates.

Its practical benefit is faster pre-screening, fewer unsuitable audits, better cost estimates and increasingly accurate document checklists. Contract terms must distinguish customer-owned source documents from derived operational data and permit lawful reuse only in aggregated or anonymized form.

This asset is not inherently exclusive. Its value depends on proprietary outcome history, disciplined data capture and being embedded at the installer quotation stage.

## Incumbent route-around

- Owners can use URE’s published forms, hire an auditor and file directly.
- KAPE already offers identification, audit, application support and sale in a success-fee model. [KAPE FAQ](https://kape.gov.pl/faq)
- NAPE offers end-to-end support, charges an initiating fee for part of the initial work and takes its principal compensation after success. It explicitly tells owners to file before signing the contractor agreement or buying materials. [NAPE service](https://nape.pl/biale-certyfikaty/)
- Public purchasers already procure the work by asking bidders to quote a commission percentage, making fee competition transparent. [Procurement example](https://platformazakupowa.pl/transakcja/991840)
- Installers can add the service internally or refer directly to a national energy consultancy.
- Owners can wait for an obligated energy company’s financing program instead of monetizing certificates separately.

The defensible route is therefore early channel access, rapid screening and lower process friction—not exclusive access to the legal mechanism.

## Rules and constraints

### Binding or authority-controlled

- Application before project commencement.
- Applicant must be the project entity or a properly authorized entity.
- Eligible project and documented final-energy savings.
- Required audit and completion documentation.
- Restrictions where combined investment aid would exceed permitted support.
- URE issuance and transmission to the registry.
- Registry, brokerage and market rules for monetization.
- Certificate rights and their transfer are governed by the consolidated Energy Efficiency Act. [Consolidated Energy Efficiency Act](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/download.xsp/WDU20240001047/U/D20241047Lj.pdf)

### Technical

- Defensible baseline and counterfactual.
- Correct equipment boundary and conversion to toe.
- No duplicate savings attribution.
- Reconciliation of planned and achieved results.
- Auditor competence, independence and professional responsibility.

### Commercial

- Success-fee percentage.
- Which party carries audit and clarification costs.
- Treatment of cancellations, partial issuance and price movements.
- Sale timing and permitted price instructions.
- Referral compensation and data rights.

### Prohibited reliance

- Backdating or disguising the project start.
- Fabricated consumption baselines.
- Double counting savings.
- Undisclosed fiduciary ownership or assignment.
- Claiming to be URE, TGE, a broker or the responsible auditor.
- Selling rights outside the lawful registry/trading route.

## Lawful operating structure

The initial structure should be a Polish limited-liability service company with:

1. A written owner agreement executed before commencement.
2. A project-specific power of attorney using the URE authorization pattern.
3. The owner remaining disclosed as beneficiary.
4. A competent audit subcontractor with clear responsibility and insurance.
5. A success fee calculated on documented net or gross sale proceeds, with every deduction defined.
6. Sale through an authorized brokerage/registry route.
7. Separate owner approval for price and sale timing.
8. VAT invoices and an explicit treatment of exchange, registry and brokerage costs.
9. Data-processing, confidentiality and derived-data provisions.
10. A client-money process that prevents commingling.

The fiduciary alternative—service company as authorized applicant and named rights holder acting for the owner—is evidenced by the published KIS case, but should not be copied without Polish legal and tax advice or a company-specific interpretation.

## Dependencies

- Continued statutory availability of the mechanism.
- Project discovered before a binding commitment.
- Savings large enough to support the audit and success fee.
- Complete owner and installer records.
- Reliable audit capacity.
- Owner completing the project as described.
- URE accepting the audit and completion evidence.
- Registry and brokerage access.
- Sufficient market liquidity at an acceptable price.
- Enforceable proceeds-sharing language.
- Adequate working capital for the long cash cycle.
- Correct VAT, CIT/PIT, fiduciary and public-aid treatment.

## Fastest paid proof and capital at risk

The fastest proof is one uncommitted project sourced through the family installer:

1. Obtain design, expected equipment, consumption history and planned order date.
2. Pay for a rapid auditor screen.
3. Obtain a fixed audit quote and legal review of the owner agreement.
4. Sign the agreement and authorization.
5. Complete and file the audit-backed application before commitment.
6. Track implementation and completion.
7. Sell the issued rights and collect the contractual share.

Expected founder capital at risk is **5,000–20,000 PLN**, primarily audit, counsel and submission work. The success-fee-only paid event is unlikely to occur quickly: the evidence supports planning for **6–18 months**, with longer cases possible. Application acceptance is an operational milestone, not cash proof.

## Kill criteria

Stop committing additional founder-funded audits if any of the following occurs:

- Screening 50 installer leads produces no uncommitted project with adequate documented savings.
- Three quoted audits each consume more than half of the expected 15% service fee.
- No owner among the first ten suitable leads accepts a disclosed 15% proceeds share.
- The first paid project requires more than 20,000 PLN of unrecoverable preparation capital.
- Two of the first five filed applications fail for avoidable documentation or timing errors.
- Median application-to-cash time exceeds 18 months without financing sufficient for the resulting work in progress.
- After 90 days, signed channels generate fewer than two suitable uncommitted projects per month.
- Counsel cannot produce an enforceable, transparent owner-beneficiary and proceeds arrangement within the initial capital budget.
- Available TGE price and transaction costs reduce expected gross profit below the cash risk on a normal 30–50 toe project.

- Audit price for the first actual project.
- URE treatment of the specific heat-pump, controls or PV-adjacent configuration.
- Exact event constituting commencement for each installer contract form.
- Distribution of application-to-issuance times for current filings.
- Frequency and cost of URE clarification requests.
- Owner cancellation and project-change rates.
- Broker, registry and clearing fee schedule for the chosen route.
- Whether rights should remain with the owner or be acquired fiduciary by the service company.
- VAT, CIT/PIT and accounting treatment for the exact proceeds flow.
- Public-aid interaction for customers receiving grants or subsidized finance.
- Installer willingness to introduce the service before signing its own order.
- Actual percentage of eligible installer projects that owners currently fail to monetize.
