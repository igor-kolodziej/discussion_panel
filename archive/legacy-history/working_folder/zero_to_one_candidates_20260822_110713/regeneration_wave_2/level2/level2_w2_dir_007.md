# W2-L2-007 — Exclusive options over underused grid-connection headroom

**Research date:** 2026-08-22  
**Access window:** 14:40–14:43 CEST  
**Concept:** An entrant-controlled SPV obtains time-limited, conditional exclusivity over complementary headroom at an existing renewable installation, then earns a reservation and assignment/development fee from a qualified co-located generator or storage developer.

## 1. Evidence boundary

### Verified evidence

- In 2024, Polish operators received **130 cable-pooling applications**, issued connection conditions for **62 installations**, signed **49 agreements**, recorded **47 refusals**, and completed **12 shared connections**. These counts are not a transaction-conversion series because they measure different stages and, in places, different units. [URE cable-pooling monitoring](https://www.ure.gov.pl/pl/urzad/informacje-ogolne/aktualnosci/12985%2Cdok.html)
- Operators told URE that the principal technical risk is exceeding the registered connection capacity. The most common 2024 use involved adding an installation where one source was already connected. [URE cable-pooling monitoring](https://www.ure.gov.pl/pl/urzad/informacje-ogolne/aktualnosci/12985%2Cdok.html)
- Poland recorded **6,259 refused OZE connection applications representing nearly 42.4 GW in 2024**. URE warns that developers may submit the same project repeatedly, so these figures overstate unique projects and cannot be treated as a unique-site count. [URE 2024 summary](https://www.ure.gov.pl/pl/urzad/informacje-ogolne/aktualnosci/12656%2CPodsumowanie-dzialan-URE-w-2024-r-postulaty-deregulacji-i-nowe-kompetencje-Regul.html)
- The Act of 13 March 2026 amending the Energy Law was promulgated on 15 April 2026 as **Dz.U. 2026 poz. 516**. [Dziennik Ustaw](https://dziennikustaw.gov.pl/DU/2026/516)
- PSE’s implementation notice says the reform, effective from 30 April 2026, extended cable pooling to all installation types, including electricity storage, except where the connection point is an end-customer installation. It also introduced a **PLN 1/kW application fee, capped at PLN 100,000**, and increased the connection advance to **PLN 60/kW, capped at PLN 6 million**, for connections above 1 kV. [PSE implementation notice](https://www.pse.pl/komunikaty-osp/-/asset_publisher/UORxdCP4f0LA/content/komunikat-dotyczacy-zmiany-przepisow-okreslajacych-zasady-przylaczania-do-sieci-elektroenergetycznej/exclusive)
- PSE materials identify new cable-pooling application forms, documentary requirements and an operator verification period of 60 days, while allowing requests for additional documents. This is PSE guidance; individual DSO procedures still need confirmation. [PSE connection briefing](https://www.pse.pl/documents/20182/30599281/Przy%C5%82%C4%85czenia_spotkanie%2Binformacyjne%2B21%2Bmaja%2B2026.pdf/70142b47-a064-4cdc-bc32-dd2ff92b02a6)
- A public asking-price example for a complete Polish PV project at ready-to-build status was **EUR 65,000 per MW DC**. It included permits, land and connection arrangements, so it is not evidence that connection headroom alone commands that price or that a transaction closed at the asking price. [Enerad project listing](https://enerad.pl/ogloszenie/sprzedam-projekt-pv-rtb-woj-lodzkie/)
- S&P modelled Polish BESS economics using capex of **EUR 329.85/kWh for one-hour storage** and **EUR 196.61/kWh for four-hour storage**, 2.6% annual operating expense, 85% round-trip efficiency, a 15-year life and an 11% nominal discount rate. These assumptions show why avoiding a fresh connection may matter, but do not establish what a developer will pay for an option. [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/blog/energy-transition/052925-unlocking-polands-energy-storage-potential)
- TAURON disclosed a 90 MW PV project sharing a connection with an existing 100 MW wind farm, demonstrating that established utilities can execute cable pooling internally or by acquiring project SPVs. [TAURON management report](https://en.tauron.pl/-/media/offer-documents/english/general-meetings/2024/documents-presented/report-of-the-management-board-on-the-operatios-of-tauron-and-tauron-capital-group-for-2023.ashx)

### Interpretation

The 2026 reform removes the earlier general exclusion of independent storage, but it does not turn unused hourly export capability into freely transferable property. The commercial asset is therefore a **conditional private-law package**: owner exclusivity, data access, nomination or assignment rights, land cooperation and obligations to pursue the operator-approved structure.

The strongest initial sites are likely existing wind or ground-mounted PV projects above 1 kV with:

- A material difference between registered export capacity and actual interval output.
- Clear meter, protection and connection documents.
- Land suitable for a complementary installation.
- An owner and lender willing to cooperate.
- Enough expected developer value to fund statutory deposits and technical work.

Commercial rooftop PV behind an end-customer installation may fall within the statutory exception described by PSE. It should not be assumed eligible merely because it has unused export capacity.

## 2. Problem, payer and denominator

### Problem and payer evidence

A developer can have land, equipment economics and financing but still lack a practicable connection. The 2024 refusal figures show substantial connection scarcity, while the cable-pooling figures show that shared connections are already being processed and built.

The direct payer is a storage or complementary-generation developer that gains:

- Exclusive access to a screened site.
- A usable interval-output dataset.
- Owner cooperation and land documentation.
- A prepared technical and contractual pathway.
- Less originator work than sourcing and negotiating the site independently.

The existing owner benefits from an option premium, a closing payment or continuing participation without funding the incoming plant.

## 3. Exact transaction and incentive/power map

### Proposed transaction

1. The originator identifies an existing connection through an installer, adviser or owner.
2. Owner and originator sign an NDA and limited data-access letter.
3. A licensed grid engineer screens 12–24 months of interval data, connection capacity, clipping, outages, protection and plausible complementary profiles.
4. An entrant-controlled option SPV pays a modest premium for 60–120 days of exclusivity.
5. The option grants the SPV a conditional right to nominate a qualified incoming developer and obtain the owner’s cooperation with a cable-pooling application, land arrangements and required agreements.
6. The SPV prepares a redacted data room and seeks developer bids.
7. A developer pays a non-refundable reservation fee for exclusivity and diligence access.
8. Owner, developer and SPV execute the definitive conditional package. Any operator application fee, connection advance, security and detailed engineering are funded directly by the developer or through ring-fenced developer funds.
9. The assignment/development fee becomes payable in stages: definitive signing, operator condition or consent, and final transfer or notice to proceed.
10. If required approvals do not arrive by the long-stop date, exclusivity expires under the agreed unwind provisions. No party represents that capacity exists independently of operator approval.

### Incentive and power map

| Actor | Contributes | Receives | Power or veto | Principal failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Existing owner | Connection position, data, cooperation, possibly land | Option premium, closing fee or participation | Can refuse exclusivity or definitive documents | Poor data, lender restriction, competing option |
| Incoming developer | Reservation fee, engineering, statutory payments, capital | Conditional site and connection package | Can reject technical or commercial terms | Site fails diligence or economics |
| Option SPV | Origination, exclusivity capital, packaging and buyer process | Reservation and assignment/development fees | Controls option and buyer nomination within contract | Option expires before buyer or consent |
| Network operator | Technical review, conditions, connection agreement | Fees, security and compliant operation | Binding technical and contractual gate | Refusal, reduced capacity, delay or costly works |
| Landowner | Lease, easement or amendment | Rent or premium | Can block construction or cable route | Site rights do not cover incoming plant |
| Secured lender | Consent or waiver | Protection of collateral and cash flows | Often an effective veto through finance documents | Consent delayed or conditioned on repayment |
| Installer/adviser | Owner introduction and technical history | Referral fee or continuing work | Controls relationship access, not grid approval | Bypassed after introduction |
| Engineer and counsel | Technical screen and lawful documents | Professional fees | Determine whether package is fit to present | Liability scope, incomplete records |

The entrant bears the option premium, preliminary professional costs and buyer-conversion risk. The qualified developer retains plant design, construction, financing and operating risk.

## 4. Price, unit economics and cash timing

### Proposed pricing architecture

These are operating assumptions for paid testing, not observed closed-deal prices.

- **Owner option premium:** PLN 10,000–25,000 for initial exclusivity.
- **Developer reservation fee:** PLN 15,000–35,000 after accepting the data room.
- **Assignment/development fee:** PLN 50,000–100,000 per usable MW, with a minimum total fee of PLN 100,000–150,000.
- **Owner closing consideration:** fixed payment, per-MW payment or a minority participation chosen site by site.
- **Grid charges and securities:** funded by the developer, not from the founder’s unrestricted PLN 100,000.

A two-megawatt application above 1 kV illustrates the working-capital danger: the current PSE figures imply a PLN 2,000 application fee and a PLN 120,000 connection advance, before possible security and engineering. The founder should not fund that amount speculatively.

| Other third-party diligence | (5,000) | Before definitive documents |
| **Gross profit** | **145,000** | Before company overhead |
| **Gross margin** | **64.4%** | Assumption |

The public EUR 65,000/MW asking-price example concerns a complete ready-to-build project. Proposed fees for a consent-ready headroom package must remain materially below full-project value unless the entrant also controls land, permits and other development rights.

### Cash sequence

- **Days 0–30:** PLN 10,000–20,000 at risk for the option, data extraction and preliminary screen.
- **Days 30–60:** up to another PLN 10,000 for counsel and the buyer-facing data room.
- **Days 45–90:** target PLN 15,000–35,000 developer reservation, recovering a meaningful part of the initial outlay.
- **Months 3–9:** developer funds formal engineering, application fees, advances and securities.
- **Months 6–18:** staged assignment fee if contractual and operator conditions are met.
- **Long-stop:** automatic expiry or renegotiation; no guaranteed approval.

## 6. Acquisition route

### Supply-side acquisition

Use the seven-person installer relationship as an introduction channel, while keeping owner consent explicit.

1. Identify the installer’s non-residential and utility-scale customers with connections above 1 kV.
2. Offer a no-cost initial interval-data and document check.
3. Pay the installer a disclosed fee only when a developer pays a reservation.
4. Add renewable advisers, O&M providers, land agents and project-finance counsel as referral channels.
5. Do not ask installers to promise available grid capacity.

### Buyer acquisition

- Build a named list of storage developers, renewable IPPs, utilities, EPC-led development teams and infrastructure investors.
- Send redacted one-page site briefs showing region, operator, voltage, registered export, interval profile, potential technology, land status and required approvals.
- Require a reservation fee before disclosing owner identity or full documents.
- Give repeat buyers a standard diligence schema and portfolio-first access without granting a blanket right over unsourced sites.

### First ten paying customers

“Customer” means a developer or investor paying a reservation or assignment fee.

- Customers 1–3: regional storage developers already seeking Wielkopolska or Łódzkie sites.
- Customers 4–6: renewable IPPs adding storage or complementary generation to existing portfolios.
- Customers 7–8: EPC/developer consortia able to fund applications and construction.
- Customers 9–10: utility or infrastructure-capital buyers seeking multi-site pipelines.

Operating funnel: 30 screened owner introductions → 12 signed data-access letters → 6 conditional options → 20–30 buyer discussions → 10 paying counterparties across the first 18–30 months. Actual conversion must be recorded rather than presumed.

### First one hundred paying customers

The source set does not establish that Poland contains 100 independent qualified payers. A route to that customer count would require:

- Coverage across the five major DSO territories and PSE-level projects.
- Approximately 300 technically screened sites and 150 buyer-ready data rooms.
- A census of at least several hundred credible Polish and cross-border developer accounts.
- Five to ten installer/adviser channels per operating region.
- Three regional originators plus centralized engineering and legal standards.
- Portfolio transactions and repeat purchasing, while still tracking unique payer accounts separately.

If the credible payer census is materially smaller, the business should be managed around repeat transactions with 15–40 institutional buyers rather than claiming 100 unique customers.

## 7. Control and compounding asset

The durable asset is not grid capacity itself. It is the combined proprietary record of:

- Enforceable, time-limited site options.
- Interval-generation and curtailment profiles.
- Connection agreements, operator correspondence and consent outcomes.
- Operator-specific application checklists.
- Lender and landowner consent language.
- Developer demand by region, technology and site size.
- Observed reservation prices, closing prices and failed-site reasons.
- Consent-ready document templates and a reliable referral network.

Each completed or failed site improves screening, contract language, price discovery and buyer matching. Exclusivity expires, however, and data loses value when grid rules or plant configurations change.

## 8. Incumbent route-around

- A renewable owner can hire a grid engineer and energy lawyer and approach developers directly.
- A storage developer can source owners through land agents, installers or public project records.
- An integrated utility can add a complementary plant within its own portfolio; TAURON’s disclosed project demonstrates this route.
- Large developers can acquire entire project SPVs rather than pay a separate originator.
- Installers and O&M providers can add storage origination to their existing customer relationships.
- Standardized operator procedures could reduce the value of procedural expertise.

The entrant’s response is speed, transparent owner economics, credible buyer competition, disciplined screening and contracts that preserve exclusivity without pretending to own operator-controlled capacity.

## 9. Rules and constraint classification

### Binding law

- Energy Law connection requirements as amended by Dz.U. 2026 poz. 516.
- Operator approval and registered import/export limits.
- The end-customer-installation exception described by PSE.
- Application fees, connection advances, securities and statutory deadlines.
- Planning, environmental, construction and land requirements applicable to the incoming plant.
- Licensing or registration obligations applicable to the plant owner and operator.

### Operator and technical constraints

- Available network capacity at the actual connection point.
- Protection, metering, communications and control requirements.
- Export-limit enforcement and possible curtailment.
- Compatibility of the existing and incoming production or charging profiles.
- Operator-specific forms and document practice.

### Contract-control constraints

- Existing connection agreement and its amendment provisions.
- Whether the owner must remain a party to the shared-connection structure.
- Assignment, nomination and change-of-control restrictions.
- Land lease, easement and cable-route rights.
- Lender security, negative pledges and consent rights.
- Existing support, PPA, balancing and insurance agreements.

### Development economics

- Actual usable headroom rather than nameplate difference.
- Battery or complementary-generation capex and revenue stack.
- Developer cost of capital and permitted construction schedule.
- Duration and probability of operator and lender consent.
- Owner’s required share of created value.

## 10. Lawful structure

- Create one ring-fenced option SPV per site or small portfolio.
- Define the option as a right to owner cooperation, data, land arrangements and conditional project participation—not as a sale of guaranteed grid capacity.
- Make nomination, assignment and closing conditional on operator, owner, landowner and lender approvals.
- Prohibit overlapping options, hidden encumbrances and representations that approvals are certain.
- Keep the registered connection limit enforceable through approved control, metering and protection.
- Require the developer to fund application fees, connection advances, security and detailed engineering.
- Use licensed grid engineers and Polish energy counsel.
- Give the owner disclosure of buyer identity, economics and conflicts before definitive signing.
- Use escrow or direct operator payment for statutory funds rather than routing them through the originator’s working account.

## 11. Dependencies

- Access to complete interval data and outage/curtailment history.
- Existing connection agreement and operator correspondence.
- A licensed engineer able to model the combined profile.
- Current forms and practice for the relevant operator.
- Owner authority and corporate approvals.
- Land rights for the incoming installation and cable route.
- Secured-lender consent.
- A developer with technical competence, capital and market participation arrangements.
- Transaction counsel, tax advice and suitable professional liability allocation.
- A buyer-funded mechanism for statutory advances and security.

## 12. Fastest paid proof and capital at risk

### Eight-week paid proof

1. Use the installer relationship to obtain one owner NDA, 12–24 months of interval data and the connection documents.
2. Spend PLN 5,000–10,000 on an engineer’s initial profile and protection review.
3. Spend PLN 5,000–10,000 on a 60–90-day conditional option and data-room terms.
4. Pay the owner PLN 5,000–10,000 for exclusivity.
5. Present the package to at least 15 qualified developers.
6. Seek a PLN 15,000–30,000 non-refundable reservation before commissioning detailed work.
7. Require the developer to fund all operator-facing costs.

**Founder capital at risk:** PLN 10,000–30,000 for the first proof, consistent with the assigned packet. A further PLN 20,000–30,000 should be authorized only after paid buyer interest. The founder’s PLN 100,000 can support two or three staggered proofs, but not speculative connection advances.

## 13. Kill criteria

Stop committing new option capital to this structure if any of the following occurs:

- Ten suitable owners are approached and none accepts a short conditional option at PLN 25,000 or less.
- Three sites with complete data are screened and none has at least one megawatt of credible complementary capability.
- Twenty qualified developers review a properly prepared teaser and none will pay a PLN 15,000 reservation.
- Counsel concludes that the proposed option cannot preserve meaningful exclusivity or participation rights under the required multiparty structure.
- Operators or lenders consistently require lead times beyond what buyers will accept.
- A successful site cannot produce at least PLN 100,000 gross profit after owner, engineer, legal and referral costs.
- Failed-site expenditure makes expected contribution per signed option negative.
- Buyers require the entrant to fund connection advances, securities or construction risk from the founder’s PLN 100,000.
- Owners or introducers repeatedly bypass the SPV despite enforceable, commercially reasonable documents.

## 14. Decision-critical unknowns

- How ENEA, ENERGA, PGE, TAURON and smaller operators are processing storage cable-pooling applications after 30 April 2026.
- The exact boundary of the end-customer-installation exception for commercial PV sites.
- Required applicant and agreement parties for each operator.
- Whether connection agreements permit the proposed nomination, assignment or change-of-control mechanics.
- Lender consent language and normal processing time.
- Actual interval headroom after clipping, outages, reactive-power requirements and curtailment.
- Whether storage charging import requires additional connection capacity.
- Owner option prices and buyer reservation/assignment prices.
- Reservation-to-consent and consent-to-closing durations.
- The number of unique qualified developer payers.
- Post-reform interactions with support schemes, PPAs and balancing arrangements.
- Tax treatment of option, assignment and participation payments.
- Liability allocation if the operator later reduces available capacity.

## 15. Development status

Cable pooling is legally operative and had reached signed agreements and completed connections before the 2026 expansion. The reform now includes storage more broadly, but the fixed source set contains no post-reform adoption counts or closed-price evidence for independent storage options.

For this entrant, the work remains at a desk-researched transaction-design stage. No owner option, paid developer reservation, operator response or lender consent has been observed for the proposed SPV structure.
