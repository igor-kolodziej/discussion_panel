# W2-L2-001 — Purchase of signed construction warranty retentions

**Research timestamp:** 2026-08-22T14:43:30+02:00  
**Concept:** Purchase assignable B2B construction-retention receivables after documented acceptance and before contractual maturity.

## Executive finding

The transaction is legally possible in principle, but the asset cannot be underwritten from the contract’s label alone. Polish Supreme Court authority distinguishes:

- retained construction remuneration whose payment date was deferred; and
- a true warranty deposit created by transferring money into the beneficiary’s assets.

That distinction can change the claim’s legal basis, available debtor protections and potential investor liability. Every purchase therefore requires contract-level legal classification, confirmation of assignability, and evidence of the amount remaining after defects and set-offs. The Supreme Court says the particular contractual provisions are decisive. [Supreme Court, II CSKP 1384/22](https://www.sn.pl/sites/orzecznictwo/Orzeczenia3/II%20CSKP%201384-22-1.pdf)

Commercially, this is a narrow specialty-finance product rather than ordinary invoice factoring. Its opportunity comes from contractors’ working-capital pressure; its principal weakness is that sellers may not accept the discount needed to cover long maturity, insolvency, documentation and defect risk.

## 1. Problem and payer evidence

### Verified evidence

- UOKiK’s payment-delay enforcement has included construction and wind-energy businesses and transactions involving thousands of suppliers, confirming that delayed B2B payment is material in adjacent sectors. [UOKiK payment-delay actions](https://uokik.gov.pl/public/zatory-platnicze-dzialania-prezesa-uokik)
- NBP reported, using Coface data, approximately 983 insolvencies in construction and real-estate activity during the first three quarters of 2025, versus 793 a year earlier. It also cited about 45,300 construction businesses owing nearly PLN 1.77 billion. [NBP, property and construction conditions, Q3 2025](https://nbp.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Informacja-o-cenach-mieszkan-w-III-2025.pdf)
- GUS estimated that 2024 construction-and-assembly production fell approximately 8%. Production among companies specializing in electrical, plumbing and other building installations fell 11.7%. This supports cyclical pressure on subcontractor cash flow, although it does not establish retention volume. [GUS construction conditions](https://ssgk.stat.gov.pl/01.2025/Budownictwo.html)
- The packet supplies a renewable-project flow indicator: about 4.2 GW of Polish PV capacity installed in 2024. That supports continuing installation and post-completion obligations but does not reveal how often installers have cash retained.
- For public procurement, performance security is ordinarily limited to 5% of contract value and may reach 10% in justified cases. Separate procurement guidance permits up to 30% of that security to remain during the warranty or statutory-defect period. This is a useful public-contract comparator, not evidence of private subcontract terms. [Public Procurement Office](https://www.gov.pl/web/uzp/w-jakiej-wysokosci-zamawiajacy-moze-zadac-zabezpieczenia-nalezytego-wykonania-umowy-2020-10-29)

### Interpretation

The seller’s pain is real when accepted work has produced an unconditional or narrowly conditional future payment but the contractor must fund payroll, VAT, materials and new projects today. The party ultimately paying the purchased claim is normally the general contractor, developer or commercial owner named in the underlying contract.

The venture’s customer and debtor are different:

- **Customer/seller:** subcontractor seeking early liquidity.
- **Debtor/payer:** general contractor, developer or property owner paying at maturity.
- **Economic buyer of the service:** the subcontractor, through the discount from face value.

### Unknown

No researched source supplies the prevalence, aggregate face value, average maturity or assignable share of Polish subcontractor retentions. General construction-debt and output figures cannot be presented as that market.

## 2. Exact transaction

1. A business subcontractor supplies the signed contract, invoices, acceptance protocols, defect correspondence, retention ledger and payment history.
2. Counsel classifies the amount as retained remuneration, a separately created deposit, or another contractual claim.
3. The venture checks assignment restrictions, maturity conditions, set-offs, prior assignments, debtor credit and any investor or guarantor rights.
4. Seller and venture execute a written sale and assignment identifying the specific receivable.
5. The debtor receives notice and, for the initial product, acknowledges:
   - the assignment;
   - face amount;
   - contractual payment date;
   - payment account;
   - known defects, deductions and competing claims.
6. The venture pays the agreed purchase price.
7. Seller remains responsible for title, authenticity, prior assignment, concealed notices and contractual misrepresentation. Credit and documented defect risk transfer only to the extent stated in the purchase agreement.
8. At maturity the debtor pays the venture. Any deduction is reconciled against the underlying contract and seller representations.

## 3. Incentive and power map

| Party | Incentive | Power or information | Principal adverse action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subcontractor/seller | Obtain cash immediately | Holds contract, acceptance and defect records | Conceal defects, prior assignment or offsets |
| Venture | Buy below collectible face value | Controls purchase decision and document standard | Misprice long-tail defect or debtor risk |
| General contractor | Preserve security and avoid double payment | Controls payment, notices, deductions and often assignment consent | Refuse acknowledgement, invoke set-off, delay release |
| Developer/owner | Ensure completed, defect-free asset | Holds acceptance and contractor-performance information | Assert defects or rely on contractual conditions |
| Supervising engineer/certifier | Document completion and defects | Can make maturity dependent on certification | Delay or qualify certification |
| Investor potentially liable under construction law | Avoid expanded payment exposure | May have records of approved subcontractor and accepted work | Contest whether the assigned sum remains remuneration |
| Seller’s accountant | Improve liquidity and balance-sheet planning | Sees receivable aging and cash need | Direct seller to cheaper factoring or overdraft |
| Court/restructuring supervisor | Apply contract and insolvency law | Determines enforceability and creditor treatment | Stay enforcement or recognize competing defenses |

## 4. Legal and constraint classification

| Matter | Classification | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Assignment under Civil Code arts. 509–514 | Binding law | A claim is generally transferable unless prohibited by statute, contract or its nature; related rights normally follow it |
| Written underlying claim | Binding law/form | Assignment should be documented in writing, with a schedule precise enough to identify the asset |
| Anti-assignment provision | Contract and binding-law interaction | Consent or a contract-specific opinion may be required; the venture should not rely on obscure drafting exceptions |
| Debtor notice | Payment-control requirement | Before notice, payment to the original creditor may discharge the debtor |
| Debtor defenses and set-off | Binding law | Assignment does not cleanse defects, set-offs or other defenses available to the debtor |
| Retained remuneration versus deposit | Legal-title classification | Contract wording and actual money flows determine what has been acquired |
| Investor’s joint liability | Construction-law issue | It may exist for retained remuneration in qualifying circumstances but cannot be presumed for a true deposit |
| Future or conditional maturity | Legal-title and documentation issue | The claim must be sufficiently identifiable and its release conditions documented |
| Public-procurement security | Binding rule for covered contracts | Public rules are not automatically applicable to private subcontract retentions |
| Debtor insolvency/restructuring | Credit and recovery constraint | Payment can be delayed, reduced or subjected to restructuring treatment |
| Own-account receivable purchasing | Regulatory/tax unknown | Polish counsel must confirm licensing, AML, accounting, VAT and corporate-income-tax treatment before launch |
| Consumer receivable | Excluded | Initial activity is confined to business-to-business claims |

Authoritative starting points are the consolidated [Polish Civil Code](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2025/1071/ogl) and [Supreme Court judgment II CSKP 1384/22](https://www.sn.pl/sites/orzecznictwo/Orzeczenia3/II%20CSKP%201384-22-1.pdf).

## 5. Lawful operating structure

- Polish limited-liability operating company or bankruptcy-remote purchasing SPV, subject to Polish legal and tax advice.
- Purchase only individually identified B2B receivables.
- Written assignment, purchase schedule and debtor notice for every transaction.
- Debtor acknowledgement required during the proof phase.
- Seller representations covering authority, title, authenticity, absence of prior disposal, completeness of defect notices and accuracy of the retention ledger.
- No dependence on forged acceptance, verbal assignment, concealed defects or collection unrelated to the underlying contract.
- Exclude disputed claims, consumer contracts, undocumented future work and receivables already involved in restructuring.
- Keep purchase funds and collections in dedicated accounts with transaction-level reconciliation.
- Obtain a legal opinion on each contract template until repeated templates have a documented treatment.
- If external debt is later used, grant security only over verified purchased assets and collection accounts.

## 6. Payer denominator

### Correct denominator

The operational denominator is:

> Polish B2B construction and renewable subcontractors with completed work, a documented retained amount, determinable release conditions, an assignable claim, and an acceptable contractual debtor.

Neither all Polish construction businesses nor all PV installations meet that definition.

A bottom-up denominator should be calculated as:

`completed subcontract value × retained percentage × unreleased share × assignable share × debtor-approved share × documentation-complete share`

### Available adjacent figures

- About 45,300 construction companies reportedly had PLN 1.77 billion of debt. This is a distress indicator, not the number of potential sellers.
- Poland had 2.368 million microenterprises across all industries in 2024. This is far too broad for use as the customer denominator. [GUS microenterprise activity](https://stat.gov.pl/obszary-tematyczne/podmioty-gospodarcze-wyniki-finansowe/przedsiebiorstwa-niefinansowe/dzialalnosc-przedsiebiorstw-o-liczbie-pracujacych-do-9-osob-w-2024-r-%2C1%2C14.html)
- Public-procurement security percentages indicate that retained security can be economically meaningful, but they do not reveal private subcontractor practices.

The initial denominator must therefore be built from contract files supplied by the installer network, not from a top-down national market estimate.

## 7. Pricing, unit economics and cash timing

### Market anchor

The Polish Factors Association’s 2025 publication describes ordinary factoring charges including:

- operational commission commonly around 0.5%–3% of invoice value;
- credit-risk commission around 0.1%–1%;
- interest and other fees;
- one example with an effective annual cost of approximately 22% after the preparation fee.

Those examples concern ordinary short-dated invoices, not conditional construction retentions. [Polish Factors Association, *Faktoring 2025*](https://faktoring.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Faktoring-2025_Internet.pdf)

### Proposed indicative purchase bands

These are underwriting assumptions, not observed retention-purchase quotations:

| Remaining contractual maturity | Indicative purchase price | Conditions |
|---|---:|---|
| Up to 6 months | 88%–92% of verified face | Acknowledged amount, strong payer, no open defect notice |
| 6–12 months | 82%–88% | Additional loss reserve and deeper payer review |
| 12–24 months | 70%–82% | Not included in the initial proof; materially higher duration and defect risk |
| Disputed or unacknowledged | No purchase | Legal collection is not the initial product |

### Illustrative first-unit economics

Assume one acknowledged PLN 25,000 claim with eight months remaining:

| Item | PLN |
|---|---:|
| Face collection | 25,000 |
| Purchase consideration at 84% | (21,000) |
| Gross purchase spread | 4,000 |
| File review, notice and servicing | (1,000) |
| Modeled credit/defect loss provision, 3% of face | (750) |
| Funding cost | (560) |
| Modeled contribution | **1,690** |

The contribution is 6.8% of face and 8.0% of purchase consideration over eight months. One meaningful deduction can eliminate it, so portfolio profitability depends on documentation quality and correlated debtor losses rather than the headline discount.

### Cash timing

- **Day 0–10:** diligence, legal classification, debtor confirmation and assignment.
- **Closing:** seller receives purchase consideration; venture records the purchased asset.
- **Holding period:** normally no cash receipt. Funding cost accrues.
- **Contractual maturity:** debtor pays after the required warranty or defect-liability event.
- **If defects or restructuring intervene:** collection may be reduced or delayed; the venture uses seller recourse only for breached representations, not merely because the debtor failed.

## 9. Acquisition route

The family installer should act as a trusted introducer, not as an undisclosed broker or credit approver.

### First ten customers

1. Obtain retention schedules from the family installer and five known electrical or renewable subcontractors.
2. Ask two regional accountants to identify clients carrying accepted but unreleased construction balances.
3. Hold private document-review sessions rather than public content marketing.
4. Offer a written, non-binding price indication within two working days after receiving the standard file.
5. Pay introducer compensation only under a disclosed agreement and only after legal review of referral rules.
6. Seek ten sellers across at least four unrelated contractual debtors.

Likely winning message:

> “Cash now for an acknowledged, accepted-work retention; fixed purchase price, no monthly repayment, and no sale of disputed work.”

### First one hundred customers

- Four to six accounting-firm partnerships serving construction trades.
- Electrical and renewable trade-group introductions.
- Referral agreements with construction-contract lawyers, subject to professional rules.
- Repeat purchases from sellers with clean files, while retaining debtor concentration limits.
- A debtor whitelist based on actual payment behavior.
- A standardized contract-upload and document-completeness report.
- Regional expansion from Wielkopolska only after the first matured collections.
- No reliance on founder-led video, influencer or mass-audience distribution.

## 10. Control and compounding asset

The defensible asset is the transaction history, not the assignment form. For each file, record:

- contract and clause type;
- whether the amount remained remuneration or became a separate deposit;
- debtor, investor, project and certifier;
- original and remaining maturity;
- acceptance wording;
- defects and deductions by category;
- acknowledgement behavior;
- days late;
- restructuring events;
- recovery cost;
- seller document accuracy;
- realized loss and annualized return.

Repeated observations can produce debtor-specific purchase prices, document requirements and early warnings. Initially, competitors can copy the legal structure; differentiation appears only after a meaningful number of matured claims.

## 11. Incumbent route-around

### Seller alternatives

- ordinary factoring of current invoices;
- overdraft or secured working-capital loan;
- replacement of cash security with a bank or insurance guarantee;
- negotiated early release;
- supplier-credit extension;
- retaining the claim until maturity.

Ordinary factoring will be cheaper for clean 30–90 day invoices. The venture should not compete for those assets.

### Debtor responses

General contractors can:

- add anti-assignment clauses;
- refuse acknowledgement;
- insist that defects remain unresolved;
- replace cash retention with guarantees;
- exercise contractual set-offs;
- centralize payments through approved factoring programs.

The lawful response is to decline unsuitable files, not to obscure the assignment. A debtor-approved program could later reduce payment risk but would also give the debtor pricing and distribution power.

### Financial incumbents

Banks and established factors can enter once loss history and documentation become predictable. The venture’s temporary advantage is willingness to perform small, contract-specific construction review. Its longer-term protection must come from payment data, trusted distribution and low servicing cost.

## 14. Kill criteria

Stop further purchases if any of the following occurs:

- Counsel cannot produce a repeatable lawful assignment structure for the sampled contracts.
- Fewer than eight of the first forty complete files contain an assignable, identifiable and acknowledgement-ready claim.
- Debtors routinely refuse to confirm the amount or payment destination.
- Sellers will not accept pricing that covers funding, file work, expected deductions and capital return.
- More than one of the first ten purchased claims contains a material undisclosed defect, prior assignment or set-off.
- Realized deductions and losses exceed 4% of face across the first twenty matured claims.
- Legal and servicing cost remains above 3% of face after twenty files.
- A single debtor would account for more than one quarter of purchased face during the proof period.
- A secured funding facility is unavailable after clean evidence from twenty matured claims.
- Founder work regularly exceeds five hours per day for three consecutive months without enough margin to fund operational help.
