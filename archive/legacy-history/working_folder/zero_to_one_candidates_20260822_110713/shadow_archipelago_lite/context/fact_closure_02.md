# Level-2 D-036 — Curtailment Claim Ledger

## Problem And Payer Evidence

Polish curtailment exposure is verified. URE reports that in 2024 PSE ordered 597.26 GWh of photovoltaic reduction—up 2,362% year over year—and nearly 125.1 GWh of wind reduction. Most photovoltaic reduction was attributed to national-system balancing rather than network constraints. [URE 2024 redispatch report](https://www.ure.gov.pl/pl/urzad/informacje-ogolne/aktualnosci/12923%2CRynek-energii-elektrycznej-sprawozdanie-Prezesa-URE-dotyczace-mechanizmow-redysp.html)

PSE states that owners of renewable installations implementing a non-market redispatch instruction have a financial-compensation right under Article 13(7) of Regulation (EU) 2019/943, subject to applicable national rules. PSE also states that, under Article 93(18) of the Polish Renewable Energy Sources Act, a producer generally has 14 days from the instruction to tell the connected operator what portion of reduced energy should count within the auction support system. Missing that notification prevents the reduced energy from counting toward the support obligation and excludes the associated lost support revenue from that part of compensation. [PSE non-market redispatch guidance](https://www.pse.pl/pl_PL/redysponowanie-nierynkowe) [EU Regulation 2019/943](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32019R0943)

The direct payer is an exposed renewable generator, portfolio owner, or asset manager. O&M firms, energy lawyers, PPA advisers, and aggregators are channel partners because they already hold parts of the operational and contractual evidence. PSE reported 26,020 MW of photovoltaic capacity as of February 2026, but total capacity is not a count of exposed, eligible, independently paying portfolios. The payer denominator remains unknown.

A 2026 European Commission country report says Polish curtailment and negative-price frequency have grown, while fair-payment calculation remains unclear. It reports almost 500 GWh of renewable redispatch between June and September 2025, approximately 75% photovoltaic. This is counterevidence to any assumption that public operator intervals translate mechanically into a clear claim amount. [European Commission country report](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A52026SC0221)

A direct willingness proxy exists but remains commercially unverified: OdzyskOZE advertises automated Polish claims with a partner-law-firm fee of 5% of amounts recovered. It explicitly labels its examples hypothetical and says it has not yet published real customer case studies. It also states that actual coverage can be low when relevant market prices are negative or near zero. [OdzyskOZE](https://odzyskoze.pl/en/)

The closest substitute is a spreadsheet maintained by the generator, O&M provider, asset manager, trader, or counsel. The incumbent route-around is to ask the O&M provider to reconcile intervals, engage an energy lawyer after a material event, change PPA risk allocation, participate in balancing markets, add storage, or accept the operator’s calculation. Storage and PPA changes address future economics but do not reconstruct past evidence or deadlines.

## Proposed Transaction

The transaction should separate technical monitoring from regulated legal representation:

- Portfolio monitoring and deadline ledger: approximately 1,500–6,000 PLN per month, or 250–750 PLN per monitored asset where data are standardized.
- One-time setup and historical reconciliation: approximately 2,000–8,000 PLN per asset or 8,000–30,000 PLN per heterogeneous portfolio.
- Technical outcome fee: potentially 1–3% of compensation actually accepted or paid, capped and payable only if counsel confirms that the structure is lawful.
- Legal filing, representation, negotiation, and litigation: a separate engagement directly between the customer and qualified counsel.

These ranges are interpreted. The only direct commercial proxy found is a vendor advertising a 5% partner-law-firm success fee; actual Polish customer acceptance and ethical permissibility require confirmation.

The customer-facing ledger should link each operator instruction to asset, interval, instructed reduction, actual generation, modeled available power, metered data, availability/outage status, public operator data, market and settlement prices, support-scheme treatment, PPA treatment, notice deadline, evidence gaps, filing status, operator response, and final amount. It should never label an estimate as an entitlement or booked receivable.

Exact access requires asset-owner authorization; a data-processing and confidentiality agreement; a current grid-connection agreement; operator instructions and correspondence; 15-minute or finer SCADA and meter exports; availability and outage records; production forecasts; balancing and settlement statements; PPA and support-scheme documents; and read-only access using CSV, API, historian replica, or a segregated data room. No control commands should be possible through the monitoring credentials. SCADA cybersecurity architecture, remote-access approval, critical-infrastructure obligations, powers of attorney, filing authority, and legal fee arrangements are counsel-required.

A ten-portfolio historical proof can be performed through O&M firms or counsel, but the lowest-cost version should start with ten assets or asset groups whose owners authorize review. Estimated capital is approximately 25,000–60,000 PLN for secure ingestion, interval reconciliation, counsel validation, and manual review. Production SCADA connectors, deadline operations, and legal workflow could consume 60,000–100,000 PLN before repeatability is known.

Economic sensitivity depends on exposed value, not curtailed MWh alone. Low or negative settlement prices, support-scheme rules, PPA allocation, outages, inaccurate available-power models, and excluded costs can reduce the claim. A subscription of 3,000 PLN per month plus an outcome fee requires recurring preserved value materially above that amount; public national curtailment totals do not demonstrate account-level value.

## Acquisition Route

The first-customer route is a historical one-year review through an O&M firm, energy lawyer, PPA adviser, or local solar operator. The partner should supply ten authorized files representing different asset sizes, grid operators, support arrangements, and PPA structures, without exposing unrelated customer portfolios.

The proof procedure is:

1. Obtain written asset-owner authority and a counsel-approved scope.
2. Inventory every public or customer-recorded redispatch interval.
3. Reconcile operator notices, SCADA, metering, availability, settlement, support, PPA, correspondence, and deadline records.
4. Identify missing notices, unreconciled intervals, unsupported availability assumptions, and missed or approaching deadlines.
5. Have the generator and qualified counsel decide whether each gap has a claim consequence.
6. Record accepted, rejected, uncertain, and time-barred outcomes without filing automatically.

The decisive private test is whether at least 20% of authorized portfolios contain a missed deadline, incomplete evidence set, or unreconciled interval with a material consequence confirmed by the generator or counsel. “Material consequence” must exclude cosmetic documentation errors and speculative headline-price multiplication.

A compounding asset could be a segregated case library mapping operator, asset type, support scheme, PPA structure, interval evidence, calculation treatment, evidence gap, response time, and final outcome. Legal memoranda, customer contracts, SCADA data, and claim strategies must remain customer-confidential unless an explicit reuse right exists.

The government proposal itself describes shortcomings for pay-as-produced PPAs; it must not be applied as current binding treatment until verified in enacted legislation. [Government legislative proposal](https://www.gov.pl/web/premier/projekt-ustawy-o-zmianie-ustawy--prawo-energetyczne2)

# Level-2 D-020 — Merchant Fee Reprice

## Problem And Payer Evidence

Card-acquiring costs combine transaction percentage charges, per-transaction charges, terminal rental, scheme and processing fees, gateway charges, chargeback fees, and termination terms. The UK Payment Systems Regulator found that acquiring did not work well for small and medium merchants and merchants with annual card turnover below £50 million. Providers often did not publish prices, headline-rate structures varied, contracts could continue indefinitely, and terminal agreements discouraged switching.

That evidence verifies the mechanism but not its transfer to Poland. Polish pricing has at least one transparent low-cost reference: Bank Pekao publishes a standard offer of 0.7% plus PLN 0.04 per transaction and terminal rental of PLN 30, 40, or 50 per month. Its transfer offer advertises PLN 1 monthly rental for 24 months. eService advertises 12 subsidized months followed by 18 months at PLN 1 monthly rental, while describing subsequent transaction rates without publishing a single comparable percentage.

The EU interchange regulation caps consumer debit interchange at 0.2% and credit interchange at 0.3%. It also requires acquirers to specify merchant service, interchange, and scheme fees by category and brand unless the merchant requests otherwise. The cap applies only to one cost component; it does not cap the full merchant service charge.

The NBP reported 572,400 Polish merchants equipped with terminals and about 1.3 million terminals at the end of 2025 Q1. This is the broad payer denominator, not the qualified opportunity count. New merchants eligible for subsidized terminals are poor targets because their near-term savings may already be exhausted by promotional pricing. The plausible initial payer is an existing retail or hospitality SME with at least PLN 500,000–5 million annual card turnover, a non-promotional agreement, and enough remaining contract term or negotiating leverage to obtain a repricing.

Willingness to pay is unverified. The proxy is observable recurring spend and the UK regulator’s finding that merchants could save by shopping, switching, or renegotiating. No public evidence found in the assigned query block establishes that at least 25% of audited Polish SMEs can save enough to pay twice the proposed fee.

The closest substitute is direct negotiation with the incumbent, requesting offers from Pekao or other acquirers, using an online terminal comparison page, or asking an accountant to calculate the effective rate. These routes persist because merchants already possess statements, providers commonly negotiate bilaterally, and switching advice can be bundled into banking or bookkeeping relationships.

## Proposed Transaction

The paid event occurs only after a lower price is contractually effective and verified on a settlement statement.

The service would:

- Obtain the merchant’s recent acquiring statements and current agreement.
- Normalize percentage charges, fixed transaction fees, rental, gateway, scheme, chargeback, and termination costs.
- Calculate effective cost by card mix and transaction value.
- Request written, comparable quotes under limited merchant authorization.
- Model switching and retention offers over the same 12- or 24-month period.
- Leave provider selection and contract signature to the merchant.
- Verify the first post-change statement against the agreed tariff.

Possible commercial terms are:

- Fixed success-verification fee of PLN 750–2,500; or
- Disclosed 20–35% share of verified first-year savings, subject to a fixed minimum and cap; or
- Fixed audit fee credited against a later success fee.

These are unverified planning ranges. A fixed fee is easier to explain and less likely to create disputes over card-volume changes. A savings share aligns payment to outcome but requires an agreed counterfactual, treatment of taxes and termination charges, and continuing access to statements.

Sensitivity examples:

- At PLN 1 million annual card turnover, 0.10 percentage-point savings equals PLN 1,000 before fixed charges.
- At the same turnover, 0.30 points equals PLN 3,000.
- At PLN 3 million, 0.30 points equals PLN 9,000.
- A merchant already paying Pekao’s published 0.7% plus PLN 0.04 may have little achievable saving after switching costs.
- A merchant paying a materially higher blended rate may obtain savings through an incumbent retention offer without changing terminals.

A document-led proof can fit approximately PLN 15,000–40,000 of capital, covering a normalization model, secure intake, agreements, Polish legal review, contractor case support, and quote tracking. Direct integrations and automated provider negotiation are unnecessary before proof.

Required rights and access are:

- Merchant-signed authority to inspect statements and the existing agreement.
- Merchant-signed, revocable permission to request non-binding quotes from named providers.
- No authority to accept a quote, sign an acquiring contract, cancel a terminal lease, route transactions, hold funds, or access cardholder data.
- Redacted statements whenever full cardholder or bank details are unnecessary.
- Written conflict and referral-compensation disclosure.
- A data-processing agreement and retention schedule.
- Counsel confirmation of whether the exact negotiation, referral, or success-fee model triggers Polish payment-services, agency, insurance, tax, or other intermediary requirements.

## Acquisition Route

Independent accountants and bookkeepers serving Polish retail, hospitality, personal-service, and multi-location merchants are the first route. The opening offer is a historical statement audit requiring no system integration.

The partner kit should contain:

- A statement checklist covering at least three recent months.
- A contract checklist covering term, renewal, rental, termination, gateway, and ancillary fees.
- A normalization worksheet that separates percentage and fixed charges.
- A merchant authorization form for quote requests.
- A savings-verification rule fixed before negotiations begin.
- Plain disclosure of all provider commissions and partner payments.

The first cohort should contain 15–25 existing terminal users. Merchants in a free introductory period should be recorded but excluded from the core savings test. The audit should request at least two written offers where providers are willing, while also recording incumbent retention offers and refusal rates.

The route fails if accountants will not introduce the service, statements cannot be normalized consistently, providers refuse comparable written quotes, or switching requires operational work disproportionate to the saving. Provider downtime, terminal replacement, staff retraining, settlement delay, and cancellation charges must be treated as costs rather than ignored.

## Decision-Critical Unknowns

**Verified:** The Polish terminal-merchant denominator is substantial. Public Polish offers contain percentage, per-transaction, rental, and promotional components. EU law caps interchange and requires itemized fee information. UK evidence verifies comparison and switching frictions.

**Interpreted:** Existing Polish merchants with PLN 500,000–5 million annual card turnover are the likely initial payers. UK market behavior suggests Polish retention negotiation may work, but transfer has not been established.

**Unknown:** Polish fee dispersion; the share of merchants materially above available rates; provider quote comparability; realized savings; accountant introduction rate; switching downtime; termination cost; retention-discount frequency; customer preference for fixed versus contingent payment; and whether savings persist beyond a promotion.

**Contradicted:** A blanket claim that merchants are overpaying is contradicted by transparent offers as low as 0.7% plus PLN 0.04, low promotional rental, and subsidized first-year transaction fees. UK dispersion cannot be assumed to exist at the same magnitude in Poland.

**Counsel-required:** The scope of limited agency; success-fee and referral treatment; mandatory disclosure; provider terms restricting third-party negotiations; handling of statements and merchant personal data; tax treatment; liability for quote comparisons; and any activity that could constitute regulated payment-service intermediation.

Not every critical premise is verified. The decisive premise—that at least 25% of audited Polish SMEs can save more than twice the fee without downtime—is unknown.

The cheapest private proof is to normalize three months of statements and current agreements for 15–25 merchants, then request directly comparable offers under written authorization. A PLN 5,000–15,000 proof budget is plausible. Record gross quoted saving, all switching costs, first-statement realized saving, time per case, and merchants declining an economically favorable offer. Do not treat an unaccepted quote as a successful outcome.
