# Level-2 D-007 — Progress Claim Acceptance Preflight

## Problem And Payer Evidence

The payer is a subcontractor’s commercial manager, accountant, or owner responsible for recurring progress claims. Quantity-surveying consultancies and subcontractor accountants are plausible channel partners because they already prepare valuations and supporting records.

Document-heavy monthly work is verified. AIA’s G702/G703 process reconciles contract value, completed and stored work, retainage, prior payments, and change orders. A Polish public-procurement notice required partial-acceptance protocols signed by the site manager, supervision inspector, and contracting authority before partial invoices. A Polish Ministry of Finance interpretation describes contracts requiring acceptance protocols, test records, certificates, declarations, and as-built documents.

The payer denominator remains unknown. Relevant firms must submit recurring contract-specific claims, possess accessible evidence, and bear preventable administrative revisions. Public evidence that construction SMEs experience late payment establishes financial exposure, but it does not establish that at least 20% of qualifying claims are revised for preventable packet defects.

Direct willingness proxies are broad software purchases rather than preflight purchases. Plumb lists construction-payment plans at US$99–US$299 per month. BuilderPad lists broader construction-management plans starting at US$199 per month, including progress billing and retainage in higher tiers. These verify budgets for payment workflow, not European willingness to pay 300 PLN for a single report.

Counterevidence is substantial. Claims are often reduced because of disputed valuation, defective work, unapproved variations, missing site progress, or contractual interpretation—not packet completeness. Generic payment and construction-management tools already track values, attachments, approvals, and retention. A perfectly indexed packet cannot make a substantively unsupported claim acceptable.

## Proposed Transaction

The service produces a preflight report and indexed submission pack for one claim. Inputs include the executed subcontract, payment clauses, schedule of values, prior certificates, variations, progress evidence, delivery records, retention terms, required declarations, invoice details, and main-contractor instructions.

The output contains:

- A clause-to-check matrix with contractual deadline, submission route, format, signatory, and evidence requirements.
- Reconciliation of current, prior, cumulative, retained, varied, and requested amounts.
- Missing approvals, unsupported line items, inconsistent dates or values, and unresolved contractual interpretations.
- An indexed pack ready for the subcontractor’s authorized employee or adviser to submit.

The paid event is delivery of that report and pack, not acceptance, certification, payment, or legal validation. The client approves the valuation and every representation.

An interpreted initial price is 300–800 PLN per claim, with higher prices for first-time contract configuration. Manual cost could be 150–600 PLN per claim depending on clause complexity, document volume, and quantity-surveyor escalation. A private manual proof should require 5,000–15,000 PLN; secure contract parsing, extraction, audit logging, and reusable clause mappings could require 40,000–90,000 PLN.

Sensitivity depends on recurrence and reuse. A first claim priced at 500 PLN but requiring five professional hours is uneconomic. Later claims under the same contract may require one to two hours. At ten repeat claims monthly, a 300 PLN contribution per claim yields 3,000 PLN before acquisition and overhead. If fewer than 20% suffer preventable revision or an avoided round is worth less than 300 PLN, the purchasing case weakens sharply.

## Acquisition Route

The first-customer route is through quantity surveyors, subcontractor accountants, and commercial advisers. Ask for one previously revised claim and the corresponding rejection or correction correspondence. Configure the contract manually, classify the causes with the client, then sell a preflight for the next live submission.

The closest substitutes are the subcontractor’s commercial staff, external quantity surveyors, spreadsheets, AIA-style forms, main-contractor portals, and construction-payment platforms. These persist because valuation judgment, site knowledge, variation approval, and contractual responsibility remain human functions.

Required access includes client authorization, the executed contract and amendments, current payment schedule, all relevant variations, previous applications and certificates, progress evidence, required insurance or compliance records, main-contractor submission instructions, and named authorized signatories. Portal credentials should remain with the client. The service should not certify work completed, determine legal entitlement, sign a payment notice, or act as quantity surveyor without qualified professional engagement.

Dependencies include reliable contract parsing, version control, document linkage, project-specific configuration, deadline calendars, client responses, secure handling of employee and contractor data, and professional escalation. Public-project records can include names, contact details, licences, powers of attorney, bank details, and contract-related opinions. A data-processing agreement and least-privilege access are therefore necessary.

## Decision-Critical Unknowns

- **VERIFIED:** Progress claims reconcile contract value, prior payments, retainage, change orders, completed work, and supporting documents.
- **VERIFIED:** Polish contracts can condition partial invoices on signed acceptance protocols and extensive evidence.
- **VERIFIED:** Businesses buy construction-payment and progress-billing software.
- **INTERPRETED:** A clause matrix and indexed pack can reduce administrative defects without replacing valuation or legal judgment.
- **INTERPRETED:** Repeat claims under one configured contract may support 300–800 PLN transaction pricing.
- **UNKNOWN:** The number of reachable European subcontractors with sufficient qualifying claim volume.
- **UNKNOWN:** Representative preventable-revision incidence and whether it reaches 20%.
- **UNKNOWN:** The staff cost, delay, financing cost, and payment risk attributable to one avoidable revision.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether an avoided round is worth at least 300 PLN and whether clients will pay before acceptance.
- **UNKNOWN:** Contract and data standardization across main contractors, portals, and project types.
- **UNKNOWN:** Partner conversion, repeat purchase, and the amount of deadline-bound operational support required.
- **CONTRADICTED:** A premise that late payment evidence proves packet-quality failure; the evidence covers many causes, including disputes and complex verification.
- **CONTRADICTED:** A premise that completeness alone determines acceptance; valuation, workmanship, approvals, and contractual interpretation remain substantive.
- **COUNSEL-REQUIRED:** Professional-practice boundaries, negligence exposure, contract interpretation, reliance disclaimers, portal agency, employee-data processing, and insurance requirements.

The cheapest private proof is to review previously revised claims from at least two subcontractors, have their commercial staff classify each revision as preventable or substantive, and sell a manual preflight for the next claim. Evidence of proof is fewer actual administrative revisions and a repeat purchase—not a model-generated “acceptance probability.”
