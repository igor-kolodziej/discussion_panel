# Advisory Zero-to-One Evaluation: CEE One-Stop Sync Rights Catalogue

## Diagnosis

Clearance fragmentation is real and exact track rights can be owned, but the candidate confuses asset ownership with durable demand power. Producers can choose thousands of substitute tracks from scaled libraries; 240 annual high-value licences requires a large catalogue, repeat buyer trust and hit-like selection that the founder does not possess. The first 30 tracks are controllable, yet do not credibly bridge to the conservative wealth case.

## Evidence state

Sourced: legal fragmentation, broad studio and production-company counts, incumbent offerings. Hypotheses: artist option cost, 30,000 PLN blended licence value, 240 annual licences, 55% gross margin, targeted-outbound conversion and catalogue-level repeat demand.

## Gates, vetoes and control point

- **Control point:** Tier 1 for each track, Tier 2 for the business. Strongest copy risk is not identical copying but substitution: an incumbent offers another fully cleared track with the same functional mood.
- **Reachability Gate:** pass. The exact paid-proof test can fit employment and 35,000 PLN.
- **Founder Wealth Gate:** fail. A broad 6,000-studio upper bound does not validate 240 annual 30,000 PLN licences, and 3.7m PLN post-employment distributions are used to rescue weak equity value.
- **Fatal vetoes:** hit/taste concentration; no repeat-frequency evidence; catalogue supply scales linearly; founder lacks distribution trust.

## Recommendation

**kill.** Do not pivot into a music agency, custom-composition studio or larger undifferentiated catalogue. The salvageable insight—complete chain-of-title as a product attribute—is insufficient by itself.
