# Finalist: Mobile Sludge Dewatering Blocks for Food Processors

## Thesis and founder fit

Sell deposit-backed sludge mass-balance trials and annual dewatering blocks to Polish food processors, initially using a reserved rental screw press and later company-owned standardized rigs, with fees anchored to avoided disposal volume. A contracted operator and environmental technologist perform site work; the founder manages targeting, underwriting, scheduling and route economics beside employment. This is an asset-controlled service, not an online-first product; its possible compounding advantage is equipment utilization, route density, feedstock benchmarks and repeat contracts.

## Customer, pain, wedge and insight

The payer is a technical/environmental director at a dairy or meat plant with DAF/biological sludge and high transport/disposal cost, inadequate fixed press capacity or a temporary outage. The workaround is hauling wetter sludge, owning underused equipment, or calling a general wastewater contractor. The wedge is a two-day paid mass-balance campaign with inlet solids, polymer, cake dryness, tonnes avoided and an auditable savings statement; an annual block is offered only when measured savings exceed fees.

The commercial insight is that customers do not primarily want a press. They want lower wet-tonnage cost without buying, staffing and maintaining an occasional-use asset. Deposit-backed blocks transfer utilization across plants.

## Beachhead, acquisition and control point

Beachhead: larger dairy and meat plants within a 250 km initial route that operate suitable treatment. The official veterinary registers list 691 dairy and 1,684 meat establishments, but only a working hypothesis of 250–400 larger plants qualifies. First acquisition uses the named registers and public environmental permits; 75 plants are screened before any rig purchase.

The first controlled resource is a written rental reservation for a specific press, technician, SLA and customer-job dates, signed only after a 50% customer deposit. After three annual contracts, the company purchases or finances its first containerized rig and owns process settings and telemetry. Tier 2 during a binding reservation; Tier 1 after owned equipment. Strongest copy risk: wastewater contractors or plants rent/buy the same equipment and duplicate the offer with stronger local credibility.

## Economics and compounding

- Trial/campaign price 22,000–45,000 PLN; rental, operator, polymer, haulage and tests 12,000–25,000 PLN; target 42% gross margin.
- Annual block 120,000–240,000 PLN. CAC hypothesis 9,000 PLN per plant and first/second-event payback.
- Compounding comes from owned rigs, feedstock-specific recipes, operator playbooks, annual schedules, reference mass balances and route density. The data are useful only if contractually ownable and comparable; customer data donation is not assumed.
- Expansion: one food-processing route → two regional rigs → selected CEE routes. It must not become unrelated wastewater contracting.

Sixty Polish accounts could be 15–24% of the hypothesized 250–400 qualified plants and is not conservative without adjacent food categories or CEE expansion. The Founder Wealth Gate therefore depends on a bottom-up qualified census of at least 1,200 route-addressable plants so that 60 is ≤5%, plus proof of annual recurrence.

## Reachability and capital tranches

- Days 1–30: qualify 75 plants and obtain non-binding fit/transport quotes; spend ≤5,000 PLN on environmental/contract review.
- Days 31–75: secure a rental reservation contingent on customer deposit; contractor operator terms ≤5,000 PLN at risk.
- Days 76–150: after outreach authorization, sell one 8,000–15,000 PLN paid mass-balance trial or a 50%-deposited 22,000–45,000 PLN campaign.
- By day 180: deliver one independent paid campaign, demonstrate ≥25% wet-volume/disposal-cost reduction and positive contribution.

Maximum pre-proof commitment: 38,000 PLN, with the expensive rig/crew triggered by customer deposit. Additional founder capital only after successful delivery and positive contribution; full 100,000 PLN after three unrelated plants and one annual block. Family capital only after repeatable demand. Employment ends only after multiple annual accounts, positive route contribution, repeatable acquisition and 12 months' runway.

## Dependencies and kill criteria

| Dependency | Proof | Kill |
|---|---|---|
| Technical fit | Paid trial and lab/mass-balance results | Cake/disposal route is non-compliant or savings <25% |
| Recurrence | 3 of 12 trials convert to ≥80k PLN annual blocks | Events are rare outages rather than annual demand |
| Control | Binding reserved rig then owned asset | Supplier can revoke dates or margin at will |
| Part-time operation | Qualified contractor runs two jobs without founder on site | Founder must personally supervise routine jobs |
| Wealth denominator | ≥1,200 qualified Polish/CEE plants | 60 accounts require >5% share or 160k PLN ACV is unsupported |
| Fleet economics | >40% gross margin, >35% owned-rig utilization | Environmental, haulage or downtime destroys margin |

If killed, retain plant qualification, mass-balance template and equipment knowledge; do not pivot into generic environmental consulting.
