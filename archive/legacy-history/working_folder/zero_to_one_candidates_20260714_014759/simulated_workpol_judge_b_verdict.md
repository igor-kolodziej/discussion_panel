## Reasoning

The distinction between conversational Polish and noisy role-specific operating comprehension is sharp and commercially relevant. The beachhead is narrow, asynchronous and founder-aligned; the manual paid-pilot plan is disciplined. Official data supports substantial staffing flow, while employers retain workplace-specific training responsibility.

The central weakness is that every profit-critical input remains hypothetical: buyer loss per failed placement, WTP, renewals, CAC, sales cycle, data access and predictive lift. Cold registry outbound is a test, not an advantage. The agency may be the wrong economic beneficiary if user-employers bear induction/performance cost, creating a two-party data and purchasing dependency.

## Objections

- Pricing, margin and payback lack paid or avoided-cost evidence.
- The denominator mixes workers, contracts, occupations and geographies.
- Calibration depends on employers sharing induction, retention or supervisor data.
- Noise/playback/accessibility and language-linked disparate impact can overwhelm validity.
- Each hiring corridor needs new psychometric equivalence and job relevance.
- First-year calibration/low concentration requires much faster acquisition than the proof plan shows.

## Control and copy risk

Copyright control over the item bank is real but not scarce economic control. Equivalent scripts/audio/scoring can be independently commissioned; the outcome loop is not secured. Strongest copy risk: TestGorilla or a staffing platform bundles equivalent noisy Polish role modules before WorkPol gains decisive calibration. ## Gates

Reachability passes: a manual module and prepaid pilot fit the time/capital/employment limits. The defensible outcome-data control within year one is much less certain.
