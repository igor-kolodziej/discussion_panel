Verdict: Advance

Copy risk: Medium-high, but not fatal. Large security agencies and regional installers can copy the playbook, but the wedge is not “alarm monitoring” itself. The wedge is acquiring neglected micro-books from retiring installers where incumbents often do not bother with seller trust, messy handoff, credentials, records, and small-account continuity.

Objections:
- Regulatory/operational gate: Polish security services may require an MSWiA concession, and MSWiA says proceedings take about 2 months from a correct application. This must be solved via existing licensed entity, licensed partner, or seller transition. Source: gov.pl MSWiA concession page.
- Contract assignability is the core kill risk. If customers must re-sign one by one, the control proof weakens sharply.
- Retention risk: customers may treat the installer relationship as personal, not transferable.
- Founder must handle or subcontract field service fast. Dead batteries, GSM modules, panel codes, and false alarms are operationally unforgiving.
- Incumbents can copy locally once the acquisition channel is visible.

Economics:
Credible if acquisition price stays below roughly 6-12 months gross profit, not revenue. One seller can deliver 50-150 accounts, so customer acquisition is batched rather than retail CAC. Monitoring MRR plus maintenance/service upsells gives fast cash conversion, and 5 paid jobs in 60 days is a concrete proof of owner earnings. The model becomes weak if dispatch/monitoring partner fees, truck rolls, support time, or churn eat the margin.

Why it clears 87:
This has real control proof: assignable contracts, phone/domain, records, invoices, credentials, and live monthly collections. It is not just a sales idea. It is a small local monopoly acquisition wedge with immediate cash flow and practical expansion by buying more orphaned books. Advance, but only if the 60-day test proves assignability, licensed operation, cash collection, and paid service work.
