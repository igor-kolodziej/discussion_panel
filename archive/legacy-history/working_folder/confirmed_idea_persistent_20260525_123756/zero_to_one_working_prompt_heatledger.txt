Founder constraints: unknown solo founder in Poland, max 100,000 PLN starting capital, no privileged introductions, part-time at first, must prove a hard control point in <=2 months, must solve cold start with concrete acquisition, and must have plausible gross margins, willingness to pay, CAC/payback, retention/repeatability, and owner earnings.

Also be skeptical of generic services, reports, dashboards, databases, marketplaces, or partner-dependent coordination.

Scored idea wording:

HeatLedger Remote-Read Contract Book Roll-Up.

Buyer: housing communities, property managers, and small cooperatives currently served by tiny local heat/water submetering, reading, and billing operators.

Acute trigger: heat/water meter readings and resident settlements cannot fail, while the EU remote-reading deadline for meters and heat cost allocators by 1 January 2027 forces old manual or semi-manual operators to upgrade, sell, or lose customers. Small operators with 300-1,000 flats may have real contracts and records but no succession, capital, or software discipline.

Product wedge: option or acquire one tiny operator's installed-base workflow, then preserve continuity for the buildings while converting the book into remote-read and monthly/periodic consumption-information service. The founder does not cold-sell a new dashboard; he buys/controls a live settlement workflow and upgrades it only after the building or administrator pays.

Primary control point: a signed option or acquisition agreement covering assignable service contracts, installed-device serial database, reading schedules, historical resident billing/settlement files, administrator contacts, phone/domain routing, meter-reading equipment, supplier credentials where transferable, non-compete, and 30-day owner handoff. A book does not count as controlled unless at least one housing administrator gives written transfer consent or pays the next invoice/deposit through the new operator.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one option/earnout with a retiring or overloaded micro-operator covering 300-1,000 flats; secure written transfer consent from at least one administrator/community; collect one reading/settlement invoice or paid remote-read upgrade deposit; import and reconcile the installed-device database; produce one building-level continuity report proving readings, device inventory, billing files, and administrator contact flow transferred.

Cold start acquisition: supply-side outreach to retiring local meter-reading/billing operators, old submetering installers, small firms advertising manual/radio readings, local business-sale listings, and property-management referrals. Buyer CAC is inherited through the acquired operator's contracts, phone/domain, administrator contacts, and owner handoff. No vague partnership is required.

Economics: option/earnout instead of upfront acquisition; existing annual reading/settlement fees; 10-25% margin on remote-read device upgrade ordered and paid by communities; 1-3 PLN per device per month for data/consumption-information service after upgrade; replacement/calibration upsells. A 500-flat book with several devices per flat can become meaningful if it converts to recurring remote-read data revenue, and the first book proves whether more micro-books can be rolled up.

Why it is not a generic service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace: the company must control assigned contracts, installed-device records, billing history, administrator communication flow, and next-invoice/payment routing. The bottleneck is the transferred settlement workflow and installed base, not software alone.

Main risks: contracts may not be assignable; administrators may refuse transfer; old records may be messy; large incumbents like Techem/ista can sell directly; hardware margins may be thin; residents may dispute heat-cost allocation; the first book may be too small unless repeatable micro-operator acquisitions are proven.
