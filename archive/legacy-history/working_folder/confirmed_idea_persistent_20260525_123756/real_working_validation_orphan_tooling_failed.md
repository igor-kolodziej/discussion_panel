## Strongest Objections

- Title and usage rights are the central bottleneck, not a small admin detail. A toolshop may physically possess a mold or fixture without the right to sell it, disclose its files, route production, or let the founder contact former customers.
- Dormant supply does not equal live demand. Many tools with reorder history may correspond to discontinued, redesigned, moved, or replaced products.
- Quality and liability can destroy contribution margin if the founder is treated as the accountable seller for routed production.
- The stated 15-35% margin may leak through inspection, trial shots, repair, transport, scrap, measurement, complaints, payment delays, and seller earnout.
- Local toolshops and liquidators can copy the simple version if they are motivated.
- Seller handoff is uncertain because the real asset is often tacit memory, buyer credibility, and customer contact context, not just the mold.

## Pivot Implication

The next search round should avoid assets whose value depends on a triple coincidence of legal usability, physical condition, and reactivated stale demand. Prefer assets tied to live downtime, cash-collected contracts, prepaid capacity, or exact-SKU emergency replacement where:

- the asset can be owned or contractually assigned cleanly;
- the buyer's acute trigger is current, not historical;
- 2-month proof can include paid emergency transactions, first cash collection, or signed contract transfer;
- the founder can prove control without relying on a seller's vague memory or broad reactivation hope.
