Evaluate this business idea.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist; max 100,000 PLN starting capital; unknown solo founder or very small team; part-time start; no celebrity/influencer access; no privileged introductions; strong analytical, AI, and operations leverage; Poland/EU context. The first POC/POV must be doable in 2 months and under 100,000 PLN.

Idea: DieLock Gasket Archive Roll-Up.

DieLock acquires or options the transferable production memory of tiny Polish gasket/rubber-cutting workshops whose owners are retiring or succession-blocked. The first asset is not a generic gasket shop. It is a signed option over one real operator's phone number, domain, recent customer book, last 24 months invoices, CAD/scan files, material notes, physical dies/templates, supplier list, non-compete, and 30-day owner handoff.

Buyer: maintenance managers, plant engineers, machine shops, small food/packaging factories, farms, and local MRO suppliers whose machines, pumps, mixers, packaging lines, tanks, conveyors, ovens, or old equipment still depend on exact gaskets, washers, spacers, rubber/felt/PTFE cut parts, pads, guards, and other recurring cut MRO profiles.

Acute trigger: a machine leaks or stops, an old custom gasket/profile is needed again, or a planned maintenance window cannot restart because the customer does not have the original dimensions/material/die/spec. The original local supplier may be retiring, closed, or poorly digitized.

Control point: acquired production memory plus inherited transaction flow: phone/domain, customer book, invoice history, CAD/scan files, physical dies/templates, material notes, supplier list, owner handoff, and paid customer reactivation deposits. It is not speculative inventory. The demand is already embedded in the archived invoices and reorder history.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one option/earnout with a retiring micro-shop covering phone/domain, customer files, invoice history, dies/templates, CAD/files, material notes, non-compete, and owner handoff; catalog 200-500 usable archived items; call 40 active/recent customers; collect 10 paid reorder deposits of 200-1,000 PLN; complete 5 paid reorders at 500-5,000 PLN each with >45% gross margin through subcontract cutters or a leased/simple press; secure 2 recurring/standing reorder customers before closing any deeper acquisition. Exclude high-liability certified/pressure-critical parts unless the archive contains clear specs and the customer signs professional-use responsibility language.

Distribution: first acquisition is seller-succession outreach to small gasket/rubber/cutting shops, not paid ads. First customers come from acquired invoices, inherited phone/domain, owner introduction, and exact-query SEO around archived parts. No vague partnerships are required; subcontract cutting is commodity and can be tested after deposits.

Why it is not a generic service, marketplace, report, dashboard, or database: the startup controls transferable physical/digital production assets and inherited customer reorder flow. It does not just advise customers or list suppliers. It owns the old die/file/spec/order path that makes a reorder faster and lower-risk than reverse engineering from a failed sample during downtime.

Rate this idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score. Cap the score below 82 if it lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Also cap below 82 if the 2-month proof depends on vague partnerships, speculative inventory, untransferable customer goodwill, or a resource that cannot be legally/contractually controlled. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, gross margins, payback, retention/repeatability, cash conversion, and owner earnings more heavily than originality.
