## Repeated Fatal Objections

1. Emergency inventory alone is not a strong control point because incumbents can source or stock similar parts.
2. Retiring-technician books help, but if they are not contractually exclusive and repeatably acquirable, they are not enough.
3. Buyer demand is episodic; relying on search/Allegro during panic creates weak CAC proof.
4. Trust and liability matter when the asset can halt production or regulated lab work.
5. Contract-book ideas are attractive but transfer, consent, and licensing friction make the 2-month POC fragile.

## Pivot Lesson

The next round should move from "we own emergency stock" to "customers or operators prepay/reroute a workflow into our custody." Stronger candidates should control:

- prepaid customer-specific continuity kits;
- backups/programs/configurations that customers cannot afford to lose;
- assigned inbound emergency call flow from an operator;
- escrowed records plus physical spares paid for by customers;
- regulated proof-of-continuity files that become sticky once built.

This is a material pivot because the control point becomes prepaid custody of live customer assets and configurations, not speculative resale of acquired inventory.
