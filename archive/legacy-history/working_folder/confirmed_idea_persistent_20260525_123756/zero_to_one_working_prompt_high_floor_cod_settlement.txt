Evaluate this business idea. Rate it from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Please cap the score below 82 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under the constraints below. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, gross margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and owner earnings more heavily than originality.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist/software-capable operator; can start part-time with contractors; max 100,000 PLN before proof; no celebrity/influencer access; no privileged marketplace/platform introductions; strong analytical/data/software diligence skills; can cold outbound to plugin authors, small agencies, Polish/CEE e-commerce software houses, e-commerce integrator freelancers, and merchants.

Idea: High-Floor COD/Settlement Continuity Buyout.

Buy neglected Polish/CEE COD settlement/reconciliation connectors only after customers prepay continuity. This is not a broad abandoned-plugin roll-up, not a generic e-commerce connector acquisition, and not an accounting service. It targets old PrestaShop/WooCommerce/ERP/COD reconciliation connectors with 30-100 active merchants where the original developer is tired, inactive, overloaded, or exiting support.

Buyer: Polish/CEE e-commerce merchants that still use cash-on-delivery and courier payout reconciliation workflows, especially where COD settlements, returns, missing payouts, order-status matching, courier reports, accounting imports, and bank/payment reconciliation are handled by an old connector or agency-built module.

Acute trigger: COD is still meaningful in Poland/CEE, and unreconciled courier payouts create cash timing, missing-payment, return, and accounting problems. If the old connector breaks after a platform/API/PHP/courier/export-format change, the merchant may not know which COD orders were paid, returned, underpaid, delayed, or incorrectly reconciled. Manual spreadsheet matching and accounting cleanup can consume time and create liquidity uncertainty.

Primary control point: the founder does not buy source code or a support tail speculatively. The founder signs a conditional option with the seller and closes only after customer cash and transfer rights are locked.

Control exists only if, before close, the founder secures:
- code/IP/repo/update credentials and release path;
- support inbox, issue history, and implementation notes;
- anonymized ticket-history review before any meaningful option payment;
- seller-mediated customer continuity notice;
- explicit customer re-consent to support/data transfer;
- at least 15 paying merchants;
- 40,000-60,000 PLN prepaid 12-month maintenance/transition-support cash;
- proof that at least 70% of historical tickets are reusable connector-level maintenance, not bespoke accounting cleanup, manual reconciliation, or custom merchant mapping;
- seller earnout funded only from retained customer revenue;
- support contract that strictly excludes or separately prices accounting cleanup, manual reconciliation, custom ERP/store mapping, PHP/server/theme/store debugging, courier dispute work, and emergency bespoke fixes.

Reject anything solved by Base/BaseLinker, official integrations, cheap marketplace modules, carrier exports, spreadsheet matching, or bespoke accounting cleanup. Reject if the workflow can be replaced by one cheap integrator contract.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace wrapper: the startup controls code/update/support assets plus signed prepaid merchant contracts before closing. It is not selling advice, a dashboard, a list, or a brokered support marketplace. It closes only when payment flow and asset custody are locked.

60-day proof under 100,000 PLN:
- Screen 60 COD/reconciliation connector sellers in one stack family.
- Hold 15 seller calls.
- Inspect anonymized ticket history from 3 candidate assets.
- Sign one conditional option with no more than a small refundable/creditable option fee before customer cash.
- Have the seller send continuity notice to 40-60 named active customers.
- Collect at least 15 signed re-consents and 40,000-60,000 PLN prepaid before close.
- Reject if prepaid average is under 2,000 PLN/customer or reusable connector maintenance is under 70% of historical support.
- Close source/update/support transfer only after cash and re-consent clear.
- Ship one scoped shared compatibility/security/API/export-format patch within 14 days of close.

First acquisition mechanism:
Cold outbound to Polish/CEE e-commerce agencies, freelancers, plugin authors, and small software houses that built COD/courier settlement/reconciliation connectors or support-tail modules. Seller pitch:
"You have an annoying COD reconciliation support tail. I will not buy blind. Let us run a seller-endorsed continuity campaign first. If your customers prepay maintenance and re-consent, I take over the asset and you get a small option fee plus earnout funded from retained customer revenue."

Economics:
- Customer prepaid floor before close: 40,000-60,000 PLN.
- First patch/developer cost: 8,000-20,000 PLN only if one shared patch helps many merchants.
- Legal/privacy/templates: 5,000-12,000 PLN.
- Seller earnout: 20-30% of retained maintenance revenue, funded only from customer cash.
- Annual merchant pricing: 2,000-4,000 PLN/year for COD settlement connector continuity, compatibility/API/export-format maintenance, and transition support. Custom accounting cleanup, manual reconciliation, courier disputes, ERP mapping, and emergency fixes are paid separately.
- One book should be cash-positive after option/legal/first-patch/earnout costs if 40,000-60,000 PLN prepaid clears. Owner earnings become credible only if the founder can hold 3-5 similar COD/settlement books in one stack with shared developer context and strict support scope.

Defensibility:
A small agency can rebuild a reconciliation script, but cannot instantly copy the seller-endorsed handoff, re-consented prepaid customer contracts, update/release channel, issue history, implementation notes, support inbox, and the fact that customers already paid to keep the current connector alive. The wedge is the continuity transfer and COD-specific settlement knowledge, not generic plugin maintenance.

Repeatability:
After the first acquisition, repeat only within the same COD/settlement stack family so diligence, developer context, ticket patterns, escalation pricing, and customer messaging improve. Do not expand into broad e-commerce plugins unless they meet the same 40,000-60,000 PLN prepaid and 70% reusable-ticket thresholds.

Kill criteria:
- No seller with 30-100 active COD/reconciliation customers signs a conditional option within 45 days.
- Seller refuses to send continuity notice or provide anonymized support history.
- Fewer than 15 merchants re-consent and prepay before close.
- Prepaid cash is below 40,000 PLN or under 2,000 PLN/customer average.
- Historical tickets are mostly accounting cleanup, manual reconciliation, custom merchant mapping, courier disputes, server/debugging, or bespoke store work.
- Base/BaseLinker, official integrations, cheap modules, carrier exports, spreadsheets, or one cheap integrator contract solve the workflow.
- First patch is customer-specific rather than reusable.
- Seller requires meaningful upfront cash before customer prepayment clears.
