Score: **84**

Tier: **Strong but not Zero To One finalist-advance**

Copy risk: **Medium-high**
The control proof is concrete: optioning a retiring operator’s contracts, records, schedules, contacts, phone/domain, and inventory is a real acquisition wedge. But the model is not structurally hard for a metering vendor, regional property manager, or incumbent billing operator to copy once the opportunity is visible. The moat is local execution speed and relationship capture, not a deep asymmetric barrier.

Objections:
- Assignment risk: communities or administrators may not accept transfer cleanly, especially if trust is personal with the retiring operator.
- Fragmentation risk: 300-1,000 flats may prove too small unless the founder can repeat acquisitions cheaply.
- Copyability: one incumbent vendor can bundle readings, billing, replacements, and legal metrology into a more credible offer.
- Economics still underproved: annual readings alone may be thin; the thesis needs calibration/replacement upsells and low churn to create attractive owner earnings.
- Operational burden: billing and settlement errors are high-trust, high-liability work, not just data transfer.

Economics:
Plausible but not yet strong enough for >87. If acquired cheaply, CAC can be excellent because the customer base is bought through operator retirement rather than sold one community at a time. Retention could be high if records transfer and billing must continue. The question is whether annual workflow revenue per flat plus upsells produces enough gross margin after support, error handling, travel, software, and administrator communication.

Verdict: **Do not advance**
This is a good securable small-business acquisition rail with a real 60-day proof, but it is capped by copyability and still-uncertain owner earnings. It becomes advance-worthy only if the first option proves very cheap acquisition cost, clean contract transferability, paid settlement execution, and a repeatable pipeline of retiring operators.
