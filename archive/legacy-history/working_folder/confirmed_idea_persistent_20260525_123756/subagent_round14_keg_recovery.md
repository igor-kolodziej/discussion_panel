**Score:** 84/100  
**Tier:** Strong niche service, not yet Zero To One dominant

**Copy Risk:** Medium-high. The control proof is real: signed recovery mandates, serialized keg IDs, authorization letters, custody of recovered assets, and settlement ledger. That clears the concrete control threshold. But the model is copyable by a brewery’s internal ops team, a keg logistics provider, debt collector, or one regional recovery vendor. That caps it below 85 unless there is exclusive brewery coverage, proprietary venue intelligence, or network-wide recovery data that compounds.

**Objections:**
- Defensibility is operational, not structural yet.
- Pub/venue resistance may make recovery slower and more legally delicate than assumed.
- Breweries may prefer fixing deposits/tracking internally once the loss is visible.
- Logistics cost can eat the success fee unless recovery density is high.
- Need proof that breweries will pay meaningful success fees, not just “thanks for finding our property.”
- Expansion depends on building a repeatable recovery network, not merely signing mandates.

**Economics:** Plausible but not fully credible above 87 yet. The asset value is tangible, buyer pain is measurable, and success-fee pricing creates low-friction purchase logic. CAC can be low if direct brewery outreach works and the founder targets breweries with poor deposit discipline but existing keg serialization. The missing piece is hard unit economics: average recovered keg value, fee per keg, recovery cost per keg, hit rate per trip, owner earnings at 200/500/1,000 kegs recovered per month.

**Verdict:** Do not advance.  
This is one of the better control-proof candidates because the 60-day test is concrete and commercially falsifiable. But it needs stronger proof of non-copyability and owner-level economics before it deserves >87. Current judgment: pursue the proof sprint, but not finalist-advance yet.
