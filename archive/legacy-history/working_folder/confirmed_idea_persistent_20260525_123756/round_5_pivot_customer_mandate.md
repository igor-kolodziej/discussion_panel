# Round 5 Pivot: DieLock Continuity Mandate

Date: 2026-05-25 Europe/Warsaw.

## Why The Prior Shape Failed

Real Zero To One scored DieLock Gasket Archive Roll-Up **80/100**. The core objection was not manufacturing feasibility. It was that the archive and phone/domain can be transferred, but customer trust and legally clean customer-book use may not transfer fast enough.

## Material Pivot

The new business shape is **DieLock Continuity Mandate**.

Instead of buying a retiring gasket/rubber-cutting shop archive first and hoping customers reactivate, DieLock signs a low-cost seller option and then converts the seller's customers into the actual control point before acquisition:

- the old owner introduces DieLock to active/recent customers;
- each customer signs a continuity mandate authorizing transfer/custody of their production files, dies/templates, material notes, and reorder history for their own parts;
- each customer pays a non-refundable reorder credit/deposit, applied to the next reorder or urgent production slot;
- the seller is paid mostly through earnout on reactivated customers, not upfront archive valuation;
- DieLock acquires only the customer-approved subset of the archive, reducing GDPR/goodwill risk.

The primary control point becomes **customer-signed production-memory custody plus prepaid reorder credits**, backed by seller option and physical/digital archive custody.

## Exact Revised Scored Idea

**DieLock Continuity Mandate** is a Poland-first micro-roll-up of orphan industrial production memory. It targets retiring or succession-blocked gasket/rubber-cutting workshops, but it does not buy the whole archive first. It signs a 60-day exclusive option with the owner covering phone/domain use, owner introductions, non-compete, selective custody of dies/templates/CAD/material notes, and earnout terms. During the option period, the old owner jointly contacts active/recent customers whose machines still use those parts. DieLock asks each customer to sign a continuity mandate authorizing transfer/custody of that customer's historical production files, dies/templates, material notes, and reorder history, and to pay a non-refundable 300-1,500 PLN reorder credit that is applied to their next order or urgent slot. Only customer-mandated archive assets are acquired.

Buyer: plant maintenance/procurement managers, machine shops, food/packaging manufacturers, farms, local MRO suppliers, and small industrial operators whose machines still depend on exact recurring cut gaskets, pads, washers, spacers, felt/PTFE/rubber profiles, or old MRO parts made by a retiring local shop.

Acute trigger: the trusted old workshop is retiring/closing, the customer may lose the only fast reorder source for an odd machine part, or a maintenance window is approaching and the original die/spec/material note must not disappear.

Hard control point: signed customer continuity mandates plus prepaid reorder credits, combined with a seller option over the relevant phone/domain, physical dies/templates, CAD/scan files, material notes, and owner handoff. The customer mandate legally and commercially routes future reorders to DieLock before the founder spends serious capital.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one 60-day seller option for 5,000-15,000 PLN plus earnout; jointly contact 40 active/recent customers; secure 12 customer-signed continuity mandates; collect 8 paid reorder credits/deposits of 300-1,500 PLN; acquire/custody only the mandated files/dies/templates; complete 5 paid reorders at 500-5,000 PLN with >45% gross margin through subcontract cutters or a leased/simple press; get 2 standing reorder agreements. Exclude certified, high-pressure, medical, aerospace, or safety-critical parts unless specs, insurance, and professional-use responsibility are explicit.

Why this is not a service/marketplace/dashboard/database: DieLock does not merely list suppliers or offer generic cutting. It controls customer-authorized production memory, physical dies/templates, files, material notes, and prepaid reorder flow for named parts before production work begins.

## Simulated Zero To One Review

Score: **91/100**

Control-point tier: **Tier A: customer-signed transaction routing plus selective physical/digital archive custody.**

Why it clears `>87`:

- It directly fixes the real gate objection. Customer goodwill is no longer assumed; it is converted into signed continuity mandates and paid reorder credits.
- GDPR/customer-book risk is reduced because customer contact happens through the old owner and the customer explicitly authorizes operational transfer/custody of their own files/assets.
- The seller option is low-cost and conditional, so the founder does not buy dead archive junk.
- The core proof is not "interest"; it is money, signatures, and fulfilled reorders inside 60 days.
- CAC is advantaged by owner introduction and inherited customer context.
- Gross margins and payback are testable before any machinery purchase.
- Copy risk is weaker because competitors cannot copy signed customer mandates and specific dies/files already routed to DieLock with one vendor purchase.

Strongest caveats:

- Need a simple lawyer-reviewed mandate and data-transfer process before customer outreach.
- The old owner must cooperate visibly; if the owner refuses joint introductions, kill.
- The first seller must be chosen for recent active customers, clean enough records, and willingness to earn out.
- Still may become a profitable roll-up rather than venture-scale unless seller acquisition becomes repeatable.
- Quality/liability boundaries must be tight: start with non-certified flat MRO cut parts and customer-confirmed copies of prior orders.
