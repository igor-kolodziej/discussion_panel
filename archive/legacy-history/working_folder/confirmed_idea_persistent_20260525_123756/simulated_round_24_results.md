## Fatal Pattern Extracted

Round 24 showed that reserving regulated capacity is not enough. A good emergency service can still be capped in the 70s if:

- the same capacity can be reserved by an incumbent with one vendor agreement;
- customer trust already belongs to the incumbent technician, workshop, carrier, or rental vendor;
- the emergency is lumpy and SKU/equipment-specific;
- the proof requires running the business before any structural moat appears;
- the regulatory deadline decays into ordinary service brokerage after the window passes.

## Next Pivot

Round 25 should target non-obvious **legal-use assets** rather than capacity:

- rights, allowances, quotas, permits, registrations, approvals, or accounts that already exist and can be acquired/leased/assigned;
- the buyer cannot create a substitute quickly because the legal-use asset has a waiting period, grandfathered value, registry friction, or approval bottleneck;
- the proof must include a signed option or ownership/control of the legal-use asset plus a buyer deposit or executable buyer bid.

Candidate territory examples:

- surplus F-gas quota/authorisation/accounts or legally transferable reclaimed stock with buyer bid;
- active waste permits/BDO-capable micro-operator acquisition where permit continuity is the asset, not the route;
- transport licences/community permits/company acquisition where active licence continuity unlocks vehicle operation;
- food/animal by-product approved premises or cold-chain authorisations where physical site approval is scarce;
- EU ETS/CBAM-style allowances are excluded because existing confirmed ideas already cover CBAM/compliance and broad allowance trading is not founder-fit.
