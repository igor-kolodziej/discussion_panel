Idea: Orphan Tooling Reorder Rights Vault.

The company options and monetizes dormant production-enabling assets from retiring or closing Polish micro-toolshops, plastics subcontractors, CNC job shops, and small mold/fixture owners. It does not sell generic CNC or injection-molding services. It acquires a narrow control packet around specific orphaned tools:

1. signed 60-90 day option/earnout from the toolshop covering named injection molds, CNC fixtures, jigs, CAM files, drawings, material/process notes, reorder history, phone/domain or customer handoff, and 30-day owner support;
2. written title or usage authorization for every tool before any customer order is taken, because many molds are customer-owned even when stored at the toolshop;
3. physical custody or inspection access, photo/video condition record, storage location, machine compatibility, and backup production quote from a capable local molder/CNC shop;
4. reactivation outreach to the exact former customers tied to each tool, asking for a paid retrieval/condition audit deposit or small repeat-order PO.

Beachhead: Polish SME manufacturers, appliance/industrial spare-part buyers, maintenance buyers, and small product companies that need a repeat run of a plastic/metal part where the original supplier is retiring, gone, or holding the only mold/fixture/files. Their alternative is redesign, new tooling, reverse engineering, or a long supplier search that can cost 10,000-100,000+ PLN and weeks/months of delay.

Cold start:
- supply side: build a list of 100-150 small Polish toolshops/plastics subcontractors/CNC job shops from public directories, local searches, liquidation notices, retirement/succession listings, and industry groups; offer a no-upfront "archive monetization" option where the founder photographs/catalogs dormant tools, calls former customers, and pays the seller only from recovered orders or a small option fee;
- demand side: use the seller's reorder history and owner handoff, not broad ads; contact named former customers with the exact tool/part context: "Your old tool for part X is still available; we can verify condition and restart a small run if you pay a retrieval/condition audit deposit."

2-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- sign one option/earnout with a retiring or underutilized toolshop covering 10-30 named tools/files/reorder records and owner handoff;
- produce written title/usage confirmation for at least 5 tools;
- get at least 2 former customers to pay a 500-2,000 PLN retrieval/condition audit deposit or issue a small repeat-order PO;
- route at least one sample run or production quote through a backup molder/CNC shop;
- document that the first jobs can produce 15-35% gross margin after outsourced production, transport, inspection, and seller earnout.

Unit economics:
- 500-2,000 PLN retrieval/condition audit deposit per reactivated tool;
- 10-30% production-routing margin on repeat runs;
- optional 200-500 PLN/month storage/continuity fee for tools customers want kept production-ready;
- low upfront option/earnout so cash is spent only after buyer demand appears. A single 15,000-50,000 PLN repeat production run can pay back the first archive option if the tool is usable.

Why it can compound:
- each acquired archive teaches part families, materials, machine compatibility, tool condition signals, former buyer behavior, and seller succession sources;
- the control point is not a database or marketplace; it is signed custody/option/usage rights over specific production-enabling assets plus the reorder relationship around them;
- broad on-demand manufacturers such as Xometry or Protolabs can quote new parts from CAD, and local toolshops can make/repair tools, but they do not own the long-tail orphaned mold/fixture/customer-history archive before demand resurfaces;
- the first wedge can expand from injection molds and CNC fixtures into dies, gauges, packaging dies, obsolete spare-part tooling, and retiring operator books.

Primary risks to evaluate hard:
- tool title/usage may be unclear;
- former customer demand may be stale;
- production quality/liability may be non-trivial;
- toolshops/liquidators can copy locally if they already hold the assets;
- the founder may struggle to find enough retiring operators with commercially useful archives.
