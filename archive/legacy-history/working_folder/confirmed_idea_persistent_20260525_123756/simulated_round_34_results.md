## Candidate That Cleared Simulated Gate

**KSeF/e-Delivery Accounting Book Succession Lockbox** scored **88/100**.

## Why It Passed Simulated `>87`

- The 60-day proof is concrete: conditional seller option, client notices, signed agreements, KSeF/e-Delivery mandates, prepaid transition cash, and first billing run.
- The control point is the specific transferred client book plus seller-endorsed handoff, document/deadline history, admin mandates, and monthly billing flow.
- CAC/payback are credible at concept stage only because clients are reached through the seller and cash is collected before close.
- Target sourcing is more plausible than orphaned software assets because small accounting offices and bookkeepers are more abundant.

## Simulated Caveats To Carry Into Real Validation

- This can collapse into a low-margin accounting-office roll-up.
- Seller trust transfer is fragile; without seller-led handoff there is no control.
- Hidden liability from messy historical books can destroy economics.
- Delivery capacity is the bottleneck: competent bookkeepers, insurance, legal/privacy boundaries, and tax-advisory exclusion must be secured before client transfer.
- Regulatory urgency is real, but not equally acute for all micro firms.
