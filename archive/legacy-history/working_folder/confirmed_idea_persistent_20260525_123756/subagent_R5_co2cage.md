**Control Tier: Tier 2 now, Tier 3 if POC clears.**  
Tier 2 = optioned/acquired local routebook with assignable assets. Tier 3 = customers keep calling the route phone after handoff, pay you directly, and unit economics hold excluding deposits, VAT float, and owner labor games.

**Diagnosis:** pursue narrowly, not as a venture-scale startup. This is a credible small cash-flow acquisition if the route is real, dense, and underpriced. It is not strongly defensible: Warsaw already has CO2/gas delivery vendors offering same-day, weekend/24h positioning, beverage gas, cylinder rental/service, and ADR-aware delivery. The POC is unusually concrete, so it avoids the worst “idea-only” problem, but the upside is owner-operator income plus roll-up optionality, not a monopoly.

**Strongest Copy Risk:** an existing Warsaw/Mazowieckie gas distributor with filling capacity, fleet, cylinders, and ADR process can copy the “never-run-out + backup cylinder + emergency delivery” package quickly and cheaper. Nor-Gaz advertises CO2, beer/pub/event gas, own CO2 filling, fast deliveries, service, and ADR transport; KamiGaz advertises 24/7 availability and same-day delivery; Emi-Gaz already lists beer CO2 6kg/10kg plus bottle rental/sale. That caps this below 85.

**Main Objections**
- Part-time founder fit is weak because the value prop includes weekend emergency delivery. That is operations, not analytics.
- 20k PLN cash from 40 swaps means 500 PLN/drop. That only works if it includes multi-cylinder drops, deposits, rentals, upgrades, or event orders. Do not count refundable deposits as revenue or margin.
- The acquired “asset” may be mostly habit and phone number, not loyalty. Restaurants switch if a cheaper vendor answers faster.
- Cylinder ledger risk is real: missing bottles, expired certification, wrong deposits, bad regulators, and customer-owned vs route-owned confusion can eat the deal.
- Supplier dependence matters. If you do not control refill terms, incumbent distributors keep the margin advantage.
- Legal/compliance is not optional: gas cylinders are pressure equipment under technical supervision in Poland, and CO2 transport has ADR rules. CO2 UN1013 is Class 2, category 3 with a 1000 limit under ADR small-load logic, but you still need correct handling and documentation discipline.

**Economics**
- Market anchor: public Polish retail shows 6kg CO2 gas-with-delivery around 89 PLN in Poznań, while a new full 6kg food-grade cylinder is advertised around 265 PLN. That makes 80 cylinders roughly 20k-35k PLN replacement value before cages, regulators, deposits, and route goodwill.
- If 30 active accounts average 1-2 paid swaps/month at 120-180 PLN gross ticket, the base route is probably 4k-10k PLN monthly revenue, not huge.
- A good route can hit 35-50% gross margin only with dense drops, disciplined refill pricing, low cylinder loss, and minimal failed delivery time.
- At 20k PLN cash over 2 months and 35% gross margin, you have only 7k PLN gross profit before owner wage. A 70k PLN upfront deal then looks like 18-30 month payback unless monthly run-rate is actually near 20k.
- Credible owner earnings require either 80-120 active accounts, high route density, rental/backup subscriptions, or multiple small routes on one delivery schedule.

**Verdict: Pursue, With Hard Gates**

Pursue only under option/earnout. Kill the deal if any of these fail: 30 verified active accounts, 80 traceable certified cylinders, assignable supplier terms, ledger reconciliation, 40 paid deliveries excluding deposits, >35% true gross margin, and 5 paid backup upgrades. The best narrow pivot inside this idea is not “CO2 delivery”; it is “backup-cylinder reliability plan for bars/events that cannot run dry.”
