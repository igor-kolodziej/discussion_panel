## Finalists

### R4F1: FiskalBook KSeF Service-Book Roll-Up

Acquire or option one tiny Polish fiscal-device/POS service operator's active service book: phone number, domain/Google profile, customer list, device serials, review due dates, support inbox, spare-parts drawer, service history, manufacturer-authorized technician roster, and 30-day owner handoff. Then sell prepaid 2026 KSeF readiness and mandatory fiscal-register review/repair bundles to that customer base.

Buyer: small retailers, restaurants, salons, taxis, clinics, kiosks, and local service businesses that already depend on a cash register/fiscal printer/POS setup.

Acute trigger: mandatory KSeF 2.0 rollout, two-year technical review deadline, cash-register/printer failure that can stop legal sales, current service provider retiring/disappearing, or accountant pressure to fix invoice workflow.

Control point: signed 60-day option/earnout over the operator's routed service book and communication channels, plus contracted access to at least one KAS-listed/manufacturer-authorized technician. The first asset is not software; it is routed recurring service demand.

2-month proof: one signed option over at least 300 active/recent devices, phone forwarding and owner transition notice, verified authorized technician coverage for the top device types, 30 prepaid review/KSeF readiness slots, 15 completed paid service events, and at least 25,000 PLN collected gross revenue before buying the whole book.

First acquisition: call 40-80 one-person fiscal/POS service shops from Google/KAS/producer directories; target older owners overloaded by KSeF and succession; offer 10,000-25,000 PLN option plus earnout on converted customers instead of an upfront acquisition.

## Why R4F1 Clears The Simulated Gate

R4F1 changes the failed service-book pattern in three ways:

1. The transaction is legally recurring and time-bounded: Polish fiscal registers require periodic technical reviews, authorized service, service-book entries, and service events.
2. The cold start is not generic outbound. The startup options an existing service book and communication channel, so the first customers already know the selling operator.
3. KSeF 2.0 creates a one-time demand spike at the same local POS/accounting interface where fiscal-service owners already have customer trust but may lack capacity or succession energy.

The idea is not a generic KSeF consultancy because the founder does not sell public information. The POC must control an actual book of device serials, due dates, phone flow, owner introduction, and authorized technician capacity. Without that signed option, the idea is killed.

## Strongest Objections

- Customers can change service providers, so the book is not a legally captive monopoly.
- Delivery requires authorized technicians; the founder cannot personally perform regulated fiscal-device service.
- Many KSeF setup tasks may be absorbed by accountants, banks, ERP/POS vendors, or existing fiscal-service firms.
- Low-ticket inspections alone do not make a large company; value capture must include KSeF readiness, repair, device replacement, closure/readout packets, recurring reminder/continuity plans, and roll-up expansion.
- GDPR/customer-data transfer and transition notices must be handled carefully.
- If the acquired operator has stale, low-trust, or price-shopping customers, the option may not convert.
