**Control point tier:** Tier 2, potentially Tier 1.5 after real assignment/re-consent, file handoff, and first paid cycle.

**Does it clear simulated >87?** No.

This is a plausible cash-flow acquisition idea, but not yet a >87 Zero-to-One candidate. The control point is real if contracts, device databases, settlement files, schedules, phone/domain, and administrator acceptance actually transfer. The problem is economics: annual reading/settlement ARPU may be low, payment cycles are lumpy, administrator decisions can be slow, and meaningful owner earnings probably require repeated book acquisitions or higher-margin upgrade revenue.

**Strongest objections/caveats:**

- Contract assignability and administrator re-consent are mandatory; without them this is only goodwill.
- 300-1,000 flats may prove transferability but may not prove meaningful earnings.
- Historical files may be messy, legally constrained, incomplete, or costly to normalize.
- Annual settlement timing creates cash-conversion risk.
- Meter replacement/calibration upsells are not yet credible enough as a profit engine without prepaid orders or vendor-credit terms.
- Property managers may use the retirement event to switch to a larger incumbent instead of accepting the transfer.

**Strongest copy risk:** Techem/Ista/Apator-style incumbents or large property-service firms can launch a “retiring operator transition” offer and bundle reading, settlement, and remote-meter migration. They cannot copy a specific assigned book once transferred, but they can copy the acquisition playbook.

**Cap logic:** It avoids the below-82 cap because the 2-month proof is concrete. It does not deserve >87 because CAC/payback/margins/cash conversion/owner earnings are not yet strong enough. The Orphan Tooling risk is present: value depends on legal transfer, usable operational files, and a live settlement/payment event all lining up.

**Concrete improvement:** Require the 2-month proof to include not only 3 transferred communities, but also at least one prepaid remote-read/meter-replacement migration deposit or signed upgrade order, with hardware financed by the community/vendor credit rather than founder inventory. This turns the book from low-ARPU annual service into a cash-transfer plus upgrade wedge.
