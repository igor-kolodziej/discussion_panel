No clean simulated pass. One adversarial scorer gave `88/100`, but ambiguity checks returned `86/100` and `74/100`, so the candidate was not sent to real validation.

## Why The Near-Pass Did Not Qualify

The `88` scorer argued that signed source archives from retiring integrators, customer authorization, verified custody, and first-call restore contracts cannot be copied with one generic vendor contract. The `86` scorer agreed on control feasibility but capped below the gate because SLA pricing and fulfillment were under-specified. The `74` scorer saw the same structure as workflow control rather than hard monopoly.

## Fatal Objections To Address

1. The `500-1,500 PLN/month` SLA may be too low if it implies real 24/7 industrial restore responsibility.
2. Liability must be scoped as custody, triage, verified restore-pack access, contractor coordination, and paid emergency labor, not guaranteed production recovery.
3. Factory trust requires stronger cyber-safe custody and audit trail from day one.
4. The POC must prove at least one paid annual/quarterly first-call retainer, not just one-time release deposits.
5. The control point must be exclusive release and break-glass authority for named installations, not generic file backup.

## Pivot Lesson

Improve only if the idea becomes a paid break-glass recovery-retainer business:

- higher annual retainer pricing;
- paid contractor bench/restore verification;
- explicit liability limits;
- customer-owned encrypted custody with access logs;
- named emergency contractors and break-glass authorization;
- first-call workflow paid in advance.

If that still cannot clear simulated `>87`, abandon the PLC source-release shape and move to a different hard-control domain.
