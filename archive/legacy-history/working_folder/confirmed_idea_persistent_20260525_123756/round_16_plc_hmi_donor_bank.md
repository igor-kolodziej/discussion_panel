# Round 16 - Exact-SKU Emergency Donor Bank Pivot

Created: 2026-05-25

## Why This Round Exists

Recent real Zero To One failures exposed a repeated weakness:

- Orphan Tooling failed at `76/100` because the control point depended on a rare triple intersection: clean rights, usable physical assets, and live buyer demand.
- DomainDeadman failed at `78/100` because the control point became strong only after seller trust, client consent, and payment conversion were already proven.

This round shifts to a control point that is easier to prove before trust compounds: **owned, bench-tested exact-SKU emergency inventory tied to live industrial downtime**, plus an installed-base/archive option from a retiring automation integrator.

## Candidate Advanced

### Retiring Automation Integrator PLC/HMI Line-Down Donor Bank

Buyer: Polish factory maintenance managers, plant owners, machine rebuilders, and automation integrators responsible for legacy production lines in food, packaging, furniture, plastics, metalworking, warehouses, water/wastewater, and small process plants.

Acute trigger: a discontinued Siemens S7-300/ET 200M, Omron, Lenze, Allen-Bradley, Schneider, B&R, Fanuc, SEW, HMI, drive, I/O card, power supply, communication module, or programming accessory fails and the line cannot restart without the exact part, compatible replacement, loaded memory card, parameter notes, or a trusted emergency substitute.

Primary control point:

1. owned or optioned exact-SKU PLC/HMI/drive/module inventory in Poland;
2. bench-test videos, photos, serials, firmware/version notes, and condition grade for each controlled item;
3. one retiring or under-digitized automation technician/integrator's stock + installed-base archive option covering old service tickets, plant/machine contacts, part lists, backup notes, phone/domain handoff, and 30-day technical support;
4. emergency sale/rental terms with deposits, same-day courier, return/DOA policy, and contractor test support;
5. first-refusal or consignment rights on future decommissioned stock from the seller's old clients.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN:

- acquire or option 30-50 exact SKUs, starting with lower-cost but line-critical Siemens S7-300/ET 200M, Omron CQM/CJ/CS, Lenze/SEW drives, HMI panels, communication modules, and power supplies;
- bench-test and document at least 20 items with a contractor or retired automation technician;
- sign one archive/stock option with a retiring technician/integrator, including inventory, service-ticket archive, owner handoff, phone/domain routing if available, and first-refusal on future decommissioned stock;
- close 3 paid emergency transactions: sale, rental, reservation deposit, or paid compatibility check;
- document at least 40-70% resale gross margin or 10-20% weekly rental fee after acquisition, test, shipping, returns allowance, and contractor time.

Cold start:

- supply: cold-call 100-150 retiring/older automation integrators, machine rebuilders, panel builders, plant maintenance clear-outs, factory auctions, OLX/Allegro industrial surplus sellers, and small distributors with dead stock; offer cash purchase for named SKUs plus earnout/consignment for higher-value items;
- demand: exact-SKU pages/listings, Allegro, Google Ads for part numbers, direct outreach to maintenance heads and integrators, and seller archive reactivation: "you serviced machine X; we now hold tested emergency spares for these exact modules."

Economics:

- buy common but discontinued modules for roughly 200-1,500 PLN where possible, test/grade them, then sell/rent at downtime-priced premiums;
- higher-value drives/HMIs/CPUs are bought only after deposit, consignment, or option to stay under 100,000 PLN;
- one 4,000-15,000 PLN emergency transaction can repay several small inventory bets;
- rentals can create repeatable cash from scarce exact SKUs while customers migrate slowly.

Why not a generic parts shop:

- the wedge is not "sell automation parts online"; existing global dealers already do that;
- the wedge is **Polish same-day controlled tested stock plus inherited installed-base knowledge and emergency rental/reservation terms** for small factories too slow or too small for global dealers;
- the control point compounds through tested inventory, dead-stock sourcing relationships, exact part-number demand data, repair/test videos, and archive-backed outreach.

## Simulated Zero To One-Style Review

Simulated score: **89 / 100**

Control tier: **Tier 1.5 - owned/optioned physical inventory plus installed-base/archive and transaction-flow control.**

Why it clears the simulated `>87` gate:

- The control point can be physically owned and proven in 2 months, not merely promised by a seller handoff.
- The buyer pain is acute and willingness to pay is tied to line downtime, not optional optimization.
- CAC can start from exact part-number intent, Allegro/SEO, and the acquired integrator archive rather than broad consultative sales.
- Payback is credible because scarce parts can be sold/rented at downtime premiums and higher-value buys can be gated by deposits or consignment.
- The idea is copyable as a generic parts reseller, but less copyable as a local installed-base donor bank with same-day Polish custody, bench-test proof, emergency rental terms, and retired-integrator sourcing.

Strongest objections:

- Radwell, EU Automation, Gal-Industry, Allegro/eBay sellers, and existing integrators already prove strong competition.
- The founder must avoid overbuying slow stock; exact-SKU demand data and deposit-gated purchasing are mandatory.
- Testing credibility matters; the POC needs a real automation contractor or retired technician.
- Warranty/DOA returns and wrong-diagnosis returns can eat margin.
- If no archive/stock option is signed in 30 days, the idea collapses into a commodity spare-parts store and should be killed.
