Founder constraints: unknown solo founder in Poland, max 100,000 PLN starting capital, no privileged introductions, part-time at first, must prove a hard control point in <=2 months, must solve cold start with concrete acquisition, and must have plausible gross margins, willingness to pay, CAC/payback, retention/repeatability, cash conversion, and owner earnings.

Important cap: cap the score below 82 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under those constraints. Also be skeptical of generic services, reports, dashboards, databases, marketplaces, generic spare-parts resale, or partner-dependent coordination.

Scored idea wording:

Retiring Automation Integrator Emergency PLC/HMI Exchange Book.

Buyer: Polish factory maintenance heads, plant owners, machine shops, small food/packaging/furniture/metal/plastics manufacturers, and independent automation integrators running legacy PLC/HMI/drive systems.

Acute trigger: a production machine or line is down, degraded, or at high near-term risk because an obsolete or hard-to-source Siemens, Omron, Lenze, Schneider, Allen-Bradley, Pro-face, B&R, ABB, or SEW PLC, HMI, I/O module, power supply, communication card, or drive module fails. Full migration may require planned downtime, engineering work, and budget approval, while an exact tested exchange part can restore or de-risk production immediately.

Product wedge: acquire or option one retiring/under-digitized Polish automation integrator's emergency spare stock and installed-base memory, then turn it into a narrow same-day tested exchange/rental desk for known legacy SKUs. This is not a generic industrial parts shop. The company starts with the specific machines, part numbers, service tickets, plant contexts, and decommissioned shelves that the retiring integrator already knows, then adds purchased exact-SKU inventory and bench-test proof.

Primary control point: a signed option/acquisition agreement covering named PLC/HMI/drive/power-supply/I/O stock, the integrator's emergency phone/email/domain routing where transferable, installed-base and service-ticket records where legally usable, part-number compatibility notes, non-compete, first-refusal rights on future decommissioned stock, and 30-day owner support. A SKU counts as controlled only after physical possession or signed option, serial/photo record, bench-test video or contractor test note, working-order classification, price/rental terms, and same-day dispatch readiness. A plant/contact record counts only if seller authorization and required client permissions allow its use.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one option/earnout with a retiring or overloaded automation integrator covering 30-50 named legacy SKUs plus installed-base handoff; physically acquire or option at least 30 SKUs; bench-test and video-document at least 20; publish exact-SKU availability pages/listings; contact inherited plant/integrator records where authorized; close at least 3 paid emergency sales, rentals, or paid 30-day SKU reservations totaling at least 8,000 PLN gross revenue; document 40%+ gross margin after purchase cost, testing contractor, courier, returns reserve, and seller earnout.

Cold start acquisition: supply-side first. Call 150 small Polish automation integrators, retiring electricians/controls engineers, machine rebuild shops, plant maintenance clear-out sellers, industrial surplus dealers, and Allegro/OLX exact-part sellers with a simple offer: cash purchase or no-upfront consignment/earnout for dead stock, plus seller-protected handoff for old clients needing continuity. Demand-side starts with exact-SKU search intent, Allegro/Google pages, calls to local factories and integrators around the controlled SKUs, and the retiring owner's authorized phone/email/domain routing. No vague partnership is required; installation remains with the buyer's maintenance team or chosen integrator.

Economics: many surplus legacy modules can be bought or optioned cheaply relative to line-down value. Target purchase costs of 200-3,000 PLN per part, emergency resale prices of 800-12,000 PLN depending SKU scarcity, and rental/exchange fees of 10-20% of replacement value per week with deposit. Gross margin target is 40-70% after testing, shipping, returns reserve, and seller earnout. The first 3-5 paid events can recover the initial testing tools, contractor time, and option fee; the stock remains reusable for rentals or resale.

Why it is not a generic spare-parts resale business: the company must control a retiring integrator's specific stock, installed-base archive, service-ticket memory, phone/domain routing, and future decommissioned-stock rights, and it must prove each SKU through bench testing. The scarce asset is not an online catalog; it is local same-day custody of tested exact SKUs plus inherited machine-context knowledge before a plant's line-down event forces a global search.

Underused momentum: Siemens S7-300 / ET 200M phase-out and similar legacy-system obsolescence leave many factories choosing between expensive migration and carrying spare parts. Official Siemens material says S7-300 / ET 200M product cancellation takes effect on 1 October 2025 and spare-part availability is planned until 1 October 2033, but community operators still describe stocking spares, delaying upgrades, and relying on eBay/used modules because downtime windows are hard to schedule.

Main risks: global and Polish competitors already sell obsolete automation parts; the founder may fail to source a differentiated integrator archive; some stock may be dead-on-arrival or firmware/version mismatched; returns and misdiagnosis can destroy margin; plant contacts and service records may not be legally usable; safety-critical systems create liability; and a local integrator could copy the simplest version by stocking common modules.
