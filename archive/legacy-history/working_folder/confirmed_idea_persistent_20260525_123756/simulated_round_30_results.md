## Fatal Pattern Extracted

Installed-base custody improves the obsolete-controls idea but still does not beat the cap:

- incumbent OEMs and industrial automation service firms already sell installed-base assessments, obsolescence planning, spare-parts management, repair, and supply;
- internal maintenance teams can implement a minimum version by auditing cabinets and buying exact spares;
- trust is still captured by the existing integrator or technician;
- the startup's extra control point is only strong if it owns a transferred customer book, call routing, archive, and emergency stock;
- even the retiring-integrator version scored only `86` because sourcing that book quickly is fragile and owner earnings need a stronger immediate cash-flow base.

## Next Pivot

Round 31 should not keep widening industrial spare custody. The next shape must combine:

- exclusive transferred customer/account/payment flow;
- immediate recurring or invoiced cash already present before acquisition;
- low trust-transfer burden because the buyer is already paying through the account/book;
- under-100,000 PLN acquisition or option structure;
- a hard asset/title/account/route that incumbents cannot copy without acquiring the same book.

Search for micro-operator/account-book assets where the startup buys or options the payment flow, not merely the service idea.
