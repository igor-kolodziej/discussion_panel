# Round 4 Route-Book / Deployed-Asset Candidates

Date: 2026-05-25 Europe/Warsaw.

## Pivot Thesis

The last rounds failed because they tried to create control from inventory, workflow, or expertise before demand was routed. Round 4 starts from already-routed micro-operator cashflows where the founder can option or acquire:

- active customers;
- deployed physical assets at customer sites;
- phone/domain/call-routing habit;
- active invoices, delivery logs, and deposit ledgers;
- owner transition support;
- non-compete / reactivation rights.

The goal is not to "start a local service." The goal is to buy or option a small recurring transaction rail, then make it denser, more reliable, and more data-driven.

## R4F1: BeverageGas Cylinder RouteBook Roll-Up

Acquire or option one tiny food-grade CO2 / mixed-gas cylinder exchange route serving bars, restaurants, food trucks, small breweries, event caterers, and soda/draft-beer venues. The acquired bundle must include customer phone habits, phone number/domain, active/recent invoices, cylinder deposit ledger, 30-150 certified cylinders, regulator/line accessory stock, delivery routes, supplier refill terms, and 30-day owner handoff. The startup then sells "never-run-out" beverage-gas continuity: scheduled cylinder swaps, weekend emergency delivery, leak/usage checks, and optional two-cylinder changeover setup.

Buyer: bars, restaurants, food trucks, small breweries, cafes with house soda, event caterers, and small single-site venues that are too small for national bulk CO2 contracts but cannot run out before a weekend/event.

Acute trigger: CO2/beer gas runs out or supplier is late before service; soda/beer taps stop; staff discover empty cylinders; supplier has minimum-order, rental, weekend, or communication friction.

Hard control point: owned certified cylinder float already deployed or assigned to customers, deposit ledger, call-routing phone number, repeat delivery habit, active invoices, and owner handoff. A competitor can buy cylinders, but cannot instantly recreate the same deployed float, customer habit, and deposit ledger without buying the same route.

2-month proof: sign one option/earnout for a micro-route under 70,000 PLN upfront exposure; verify 30+ active/recent customers and 60+ cylinders; route phone/domain; complete 30 paid swaps/deliveries; collect 20,000+ PLN cash; prove at least 35% gross margin after refill, delivery labor, and cylinder depreciation; sell 5 two-cylinder "never-run-out" upgrades.

First acquisition: direct outreach to local gas/refill operators, beer-line technicians, homebrew shops, event suppliers, and retiring sole traders; "we buy your cylinder route/customer book with earnout and keep your customers served." Demand acquisition starts from the acquired route; incremental customers come from exact-search and venue-neighbor referrals.

Why it may clear: it has physically deployed assets plus recurring urgent delivery demand, not just a service calendar. It is also less healthcare/trust-heavy and can be tested locally with measurable cash conversion.

## R4F2: UCO-BDO Container RouteBook Roll-Up

Acquire or option a tiny used-cooking-oil collection route serving restaurants, fry-heavy bars, kebab shops, bakeries, and catering kitchens. The bundle must include site list, deployed barrels/containers, BDO/KPO documentation process, pickup history, offtaker invoices, phone/domain, route map, and owner handoff. The startup sells compliant UCO pickup and BDO/KPO hygiene, then resells oil to biodiesel/feedstock aggregators.

Buyer: restaurants and foodservice operators that generate used frying oil and want compliant, low-hassle pickup and documentation.

Acute trigger: containers are full, staff are tempted to dispose improperly, BDO/KPO documentation is confusing, or current collector is unreliable.

Hard control point: deployed containers, recurring pickup rights, BDO/KPO workflow, offtaker relationship, active route phone, and invoices.

2-month proof: option one micro-route under 50,000 PLN upfront; control 30 restaurant pickup points and 2+ tons/month of UCO flow; collect/sell two route cycles; produce KPO-confirmed pickup evidence; prove positive gross margin after transport and container cleaning.

Primary caveat: earlier "soft UCO rights" scored poorly. This version only counts if an actual operator route with containers, BDO process, invoices, and offtaker flow is optioned or acquired.

## R4F3: WaterCooler / 18.9L Bottle RouteBook Roll-Up

Acquire a micro water-cooler route with dispensers and returnable 18.9L bottles placed in offices, clinics, warehouses, salons, gyms, and small factories. Sell route density, bottle logistics, dispenser sanitation, and monthly delivery continuity.

Buyer: small offices and facilities that use bottled dispensers and do not want to manage deliveries, cleaning, or empty returns.

Hard control point: deployed coolers, returnable bottle float, active invoices, phone/domain, route map, and sanitation schedule.

2-month proof: option one route with 25-60 active sites and 50+ coolers/bottle float; complete two billing cycles; prove >30% gross margin; reduce churn via owner handoff.

Primary caveat: commodity and copyable unless the acquired route is dense and recurring.

## R4F4: Micro Vending Location Rights Roll-Up

Acquire a tiny vending route: installed snack/coffee/drink machines in warehouses, factories, schools, gyms, clinics, and offices with site contracts, keys/access instructions, payment accounts, restock routes, and revenue history.

Buyer: site owners want employee/customer convenience with no staff work; end users buy through machines.

Hard control point: installed machines with site placement rights and transaction flow. The control point is the location contract plus machine, not the snacks.

2-month proof: option or acquire 6-12 profitable machines under 80,000 PLN; verify revenue from payment terminal/cash logs; run four restock cycles; improve margin with SKU mix; collect 20,000+ PLN sales.

Primary caveat: many vending roll-ups exist; must be local, dense, and bought at a small-microoperator price.

## R4F5: AED Pad/Battery Expiry Custody Route

Acquire or build a route book of small facilities with installed AEDs whose pads/batteries expire, then sell replacement custody, expiry monitoring, and evidence logs. The acquired asset is a registered AED service book, not a generic reminder app.

Buyer: offices, hotels, gyms, schools, warehouses, clinics, and local public facilities with AEDs but no reliable expiry owner.

Hard control point: AED location registry, installed QR/service stickers, replacement stock, annual custody mandate, and phone routing.

2-month proof: sign/acquire 100 AED locations; complete 20 paid pad/battery replacements; collect annual custody prepayments.

Primary caveat: if no route book is acquired, this collapses into reminders and parts resale.

## R4F6: Commercial Mat / Hygiene Consumables RouteBook

Acquire a micro route delivering entrance mats, hygiene rolls, shop towels, soap/paper consumables, or cleaning textiles to small businesses. Control the deployed mats/dispensers, active invoices, route cadence, and laundry/supplier handoff.

Buyer: shops, clinics, offices, salons, warehouses, foodservice venues.

Hard control point: deployed physical mats/dispensers with recurring invoices and route cadence.

2-month proof: option one route with 50+ sites, complete one billing cycle, retain >90% of customers through transition, and prove margin after laundry/vendor costs.

Primary caveat: CWS/Elis-style incumbents and local laundries can copy; only attractive if the customer book is bought cheaply and niche density is high.

## Round 4 Shortlist Before Simulation

1. **BeverageGas Cylinder RouteBook Roll-Up** - strongest physical deployed-asset + urgent recurring buyer pain + cash conversion.
2. **UCO-BDO Container RouteBook Roll-Up** - good compliance-shaped material flow, but lower margin and prior soft-route failure.
3. **Micro Vending Location Rights Roll-Up** - strong installed transaction control, but commodity unless bought cheap and dense.
4. **WaterCooler RouteBook Roll-Up** - recurring physical route, but commodity.
5. **AED Pad/Battery Expiry Custody Route** - interesting custody logic, but likely needs a route book or institutional channel to avoid reminder-app copy risk.
6. **Commercial Mat / Hygiene Consumables RouteBook** - operationally real, but incumbent-copyable.
