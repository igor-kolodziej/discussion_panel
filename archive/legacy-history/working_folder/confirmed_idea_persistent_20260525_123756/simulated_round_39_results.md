## Gate Decision

No Round 39 candidate clears simulated `>87`. No real Zero To One browser validation is allowed.

## What Improved

The revised DNS/email succession structure fixed the main real-gate objection from DomainDeadman:

- no speculative book purchase;
- customer re-consent before close;
- prepaid cash before close;
- only consenting/prepaid clients acquired;
- explicit support exclusions.

The score still only reached 84 because the control point is not proprietary enough. It is a good micro-acquisition discipline, not a Zero-To-One wedge.

## Repeated Fatal Objections

- Admin custody is concrete but not durable when incumbents already own the customer trust layer.
- The support boundary is hard to hold under emergency conditions.
- Seller sourcing remains relationship-heavy.
- Prepaid consent proves a useful service, but not necessarily repeatable owner earnings or monopoly control.
- Security/privacy liability creates asymmetric downside.

## Pivot For Round 40

Move away from micro-operator/customer-book succession again. The next round should target **buyer-funded hidden supply mandates** where:

- the buyer pays a non-refundable search/reservation deposit before supplier identity is revealed;
- the founder controls exclusive access to a non-public lot/slot/account before buyer disclosure;
- the target asset is high-value and exact-match, but not generic surplus;
- supplier-side control is signed before buyer-side reveal;
- first proof includes cash deposits and one completed transaction.

Reject ideas where the asset owner or incumbent service provider already has a simple reason to internalize the process.
