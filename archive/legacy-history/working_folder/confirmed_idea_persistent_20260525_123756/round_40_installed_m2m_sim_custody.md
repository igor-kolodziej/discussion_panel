# Round 40 Candidate Set: Installed M2M SIM Custody

Created: 2026-05-25

## Why This Pivot

Rounds 37-39 proved that phone/admin/customer-book custody is too weak when the asset can be recreated by a vendor or MSP. Round 40 moves to an installed account-control asset where switching is physically and operationally costly:

- M2M/IoT SIMs already installed inside alarm panels, gates, pumps, meters, vending machines, industrial controllers, elevators, trackers, payment terminals, and telemetry boxes;
- reseller/telco portal admin, ICCID/MSISDN/device mapping, APN/data-plan settings, renewal calendars, active invoices, and customer billing flow;
- current 3G sunset and future 2G sunset pressure, which turns neglected M2M books into a continuity problem;
- prepaid customer re-consent before acquisition close.

This is not a generic telecom reseller, alarm monitoring book, MSP, or field-service roll-up. The primary control point is the installed SIM base plus billing/admin custody, where replacement by a competitor often requires identifying the device, opening the box, swapping SIM/module, reconfiguring APN, and testing connectivity.

## Candidate 1: Installed M2M SIM Continuity Book Buyout

### Buyer / Customer

Supply side: retiring or overloaded small installers, alarm/gate/intercom technicians, telemetry installers, vending service operators, parking/gate operators, pump-station maintenance firms, and small IoT integrators who currently rebill M2M SIM connectivity to customers through a telco or reseller account.

Demand side: SMEs, property managers, housing communities, warehouses, vending operators, industrial sites, and service firms whose devices depend on installed SIMs for alarms, telemetry, remote control, access, or monitoring.

### Acute Trigger

When the old installer retires, stops paying the master M2M account, loses SIM mapping, or ignores 3G/2G migration, devices can silently go offline. Customers often do not know the ICCID, APN, phone number, data plan, device location, or module type. Replacing the SIM may require a field visit, enclosure access, APN/device reconfiguration, and connectivity testing.

### Primary Control Point

The founder does not buy a vague service book. The founder signs a conditional option with the seller and closes only on re-consented, prepaid installed SIM accounts.

Control exists only if the packet includes:

- M2M/telco/reseller portal admin or transferable account/subaccount rights;
- ICCID/MSISDN/device/customer/location mapping;
- APN settings, data-plan profile, renewal/billing history, active invoices, and failure notes;
- seller handoff and non-compete for the specific installed base;
- customer re-consent to connectivity billing/admin transfer;
- prepaid annual or quarterly SIM-continuity invoices before acquisition close;
- kill switch for unsupported 2G/3G-only devices: migrate/swap only when separately paid;
- explicit exclusion of alarm monitoring, security response, PLC programming, device repair, and unlimited field service.

The scarce asset is the installed SIM map plus billing/admin custody. A telco can sell new SIMs, but it cannot instantly know where the old SIMs are installed or swap them without site access.

### Two-Month Proof

Within 60 days and under 100,000 PLN:

- contact 120 small installers/integrators/resellers likely to hold M2M SIM books;
- inspect 10 books and reject those without active invoices, SIM mapping, and portal/billing control;
- sign one conditional seller option covering at least 200 active installed SIMs;
- seller sends continuity notice to at least 100 device owners/customers;
- secure 70 customer re-consents or written account-transfer authorizations;
- move/delegate at least 150 SIMs into founder-controlled portal, billing, or subaccount workflow;
- collect at least 30,000 PLN prepaid annual/quarterly SIM-continuity cash before closing;
- complete 10 paid continuity actions: APN correction, SIM status audit, outage rescue, renewal, 4G/LTE module migration quote, SIM swap, or device-location map cleanup;
- prove gross margin above 50% after wholesale SIM cost, portal fees, payment costs, limited contractor work, seller earnout, and customer support.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Seller pitch:

"Your old M2M SIM book is a continuity liability. If the master account or 3G/2G migration breaks, your clients call you. I will not buy blind. You introduce the customers, they re-consent and prepay connectivity continuity, and you get an earnout only from retained SIM revenue."

Customer pitch:

"Your device has an installed SIM controlled by the retiring installer. We secure the ICCID/APN/renewal map, keep billing alive, test connectivity, and flag devices that need LTE migration before they silently fail. We do not take over monitoring or device repair unless separately contracted."

### Economics

- Wholesale M2M/SIM connectivity cost: assumed 3-12 PLN/month depending plan and volume.
- Customer price: 15-45 PLN/month/SIM or 180-500 PLN/year/SIM for low-data installed devices, with discounts for dense fleets.
- Setup/audit/migration fees: 100-500 PLN/SIM for messy or unlabeled devices; LTE module swaps separately priced through installers.
- Seller earnout funded only from retained prepaid SIM revenue.
- A 300-SIM book at 15-25 PLN gross margin/SIM/month can support 4,500-7,500 PLN monthly gross margin before upsells; three to five books can create meaningful owner earnings if support is low.

### Copy Risk

Telcos, MSPs, alarm firms, and IoT platforms can sell connectivity, but they cannot copy a specific installed ICCID/location/billing map once the founder controls it and customers have re-consented. Replacing the founder often requires physical device access, SIM swaps, APN reconfiguration, and customer coordination.

### Why It Is Not A Service/Report/Dashboard/Marketplace

The startup controls installed SIM admin, billing, renewal maps, ICCID/device/customer mapping, portal access, and prepaid customer cash. It does not sell monitoring, consulting, dashboards, or a lead marketplace.

### Kill Criteria

- No seller with at least 200 active installed SIMs signs an option within 45 days.
- Fewer than 70 customers re-consent or prepaid cash below 30,000 PLN.
- Telco/reseller account or SIM subaccount cannot be transferred, delegated, or cleanly rebuilt without mass truck rolls.
- Support exceeds 0.25 hours per SIM per year after onboarding.
- Most devices are low-value, consumer, prepaid, undocumented, or require urgent physical swaps before cash is collected.
- Customers view the founder as responsible for alarm monitoring, security response, device repair, or operational uptime beyond SIM connectivity.

## Candidate 2: Gate/Intercom GSM Opener SIM Book

Acquire installed SIM billing/admin books from gate, intercom, garage-door, and access-control installers. Strong because devices are scattered and site access is annoying; weaker because many customers are small buildings and support calls can leak into hardware issues.

## Candidate 3: Pump/Lift/Utility Telemetry SIM Continuity Book

Acquire installed SIM books in pump stations, lift controllers, utility meters, cold-room alarms, and industrial telemetry. Higher B2B urgency and lower consumer support, but seller supply may be thinner and field liability higher.

## Candidate 4: Vending/POS/Payment Terminal SIM Continuity Book

Acquire small vending/POS/M2M connectivity books. Clear recurring business value and low data needs, but POS/payment terminal providers and acquiring banks are closer to the flow.

## Candidate 5: GPS Tracker / Fleet Telematics SIM Book

Acquire neglected GPS tracker SIM billing books from small fleet-tracking installers. Recurring and device-specific, but churn is higher and fleet telematics incumbents can migrate customers.

## Candidate 6: Alarm Panel GSM Connectivity-Only Book

Acquire only the GSM/SIM connectivity layer from alarm installers, explicitly excluding monitoring/security response. Strong installed-base control, but customer confusion with alarm monitoring and security licensing can cap it unless contracts are clean.

## Gate Rule For Scorers

Apply the existing persistent-loop caps:

- cap below `82` if no concrete 2-month control proof;
- cap below `85` if copyable by one vendor contract, equipment purchase, or internal process;
- cap below `87` if CAC, payback, margins, owner earnings, telco transferability, seller sourcing, client re-consent, prepaid cash, or support scope are not credible;
- require simulated score strictly `>87` to reach the real Zero To One browser gate.
