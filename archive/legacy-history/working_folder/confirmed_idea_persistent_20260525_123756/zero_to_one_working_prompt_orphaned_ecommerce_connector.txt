Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist/software-capable operator; can start part-time with contractors; max 100,000 PLN before proof; no celebrity/influencer access; no privileged marketplace/platform introductions; strong analytical/data/software diligence skills; can cold outbound to plugin authors, small agencies, Polish/CEE software houses, e-commerce integrator freelancers, and merchants.

Idea: Orphaned Polish E-Commerce Connector Payment-Flow Acquisition.

Acquire abandoned or under-maintained Polish/CEE e-commerce connector plugins/modules where merchants already depend on the installed update/payment/support channel for checkout, shipping labels, COD, payment status, ERP export, invoices, parcel lockers, Allegro/Baselinker, or local carrier/payment integrations.

The target is not a generic WordPress plugin or a greenfield software build. The target is a revenue-critical, locally specific connector where there is already an installed base and some paid renewal/support/payment flow, but the creator is tired, inactive, under-maintaining it, or wants to exit a small support tail. Examples: WooCommerce/PrestaShop/Shoper/Magento/Baselinker connectors for InPost, DPD, DHL, Allegro, PayU, Przelewy24, Blik, Fakturownia, Subiekt, enova365, Comarch ERP Optima, invoice export, COD reconciliation, parcel-locker labels, tracking, or order/status synchronization.

Primary control point: acquire or option the software asset and its distribution/payment channel, not just the code. The acquisition packet must include source code and IP assignment, update/release credentials, plugin listing or seller account where transferable, license server, domain/docs, support inbox, customer list, renewal schedule, seller announcement/handoff, and a non-compete in the narrow connector category. If marketplace listing/payment/customer data cannot transfer, the fallback must be explicit customer re-consent and renewed support contracts collected through the seller handoff.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace wrapper: the startup owns or controls source code, update credentials, release path, license server, support inbox, and renewal/payment flow. Proof is transferred revenue plus shipped updates, not consulting or a dashboard.

60-day proof under 100,000 PLN:
- Build a screened list of 30-50 stale Polish/localized e-commerce connectors with abandonment signals: old changelog, support complaints, stale PHP/WordPress/PrestaShop compatibility, API-change complaints, inactive author, small agency owner, or tired solo developer.
- Sign one option/acquisition LOI for under 40,000 PLN upfront plus 20-35% earnout on retained renewals/support.
- Diligence and transfer the repo, IP, domain/docs, support inbox, license server, release/update credentials, customer list, and renewal/payment flow where allowed, or collect explicit customer re-consent through seller-mediated handoff.
- Verify at least 50 active paid installs or 20 recurring support/maintenance customers. Free installs alone do not count.
- Ship one compatibility/security/API update within 14 days of control.
- Collect 15,000-30,000 PLN in renewals, transition support, prepaid maintenance, retained license cash, or re-consented support contracts inside 60 days.

Cold start:
- Outbound to Polish/CEE plugin/module authors, small agencies, and freelancers who built local e-commerce connectors but no longer want maintenance/support.
- Use public screening sources such as WordPress.org plugin pages, PrestaShop Addons, GitHub repos, Polish e-commerce forums, module shops, support threads, changelogs, reviews, and "not updated / API broken" complaints.
- Do not start by selling to merchants from zero. First acquire the existing installed update/support/payment channel.

Economics:
- Acquisition structure: 15,000-40,000 PLN upfront plus 20-35% seller earnout on retained renewals/support for 12-24 months.
- If 50 customers pay 500-1,500 PLN/year, or 20-30 merchants buy 1,000-2,000 PLN transition support/prepaid maintenance, the upfront can be repaid in roughly 3-6 months.
- Later pricing: annual maintenance/support plans, compatibility updates, priority support, and paid emergency patch packages. Keep support scope limited to compatibility/security/API maintenance and explicitly exclude bespoke store debugging unless separately paid.
- Gross margins should be software-like if support is tightly scoped and one freelance PHP/WooCommerce/PrestaShop developer handles patches.

Defensibility:
- A competitor can build another connector, but cannot instantly copy the acquired installed base, update channel, reviews/history, support inbox, customer bug history, release path, license renewals, and seller-endorsed handoff.
- The repeatable wedge is a micro-acquisition playbook for neglected local connector assets below the size institutional buyers care about.

Kill criteria:
- No owner with meaningful active paid users will sign an LOI under 40,000 PLN upfront plus earnout within 45 days.
- Listing/update/payment/customer-data transfer or customer re-consent is not legally or technically possible.
- The first screened targets mostly have free installs but no active paid renewal/support flow.
- The first update requires a rewrite rather than a scoped compatibility/security/API patch.
- Support tickets show every merchant needs bespoke store debugging.
- Customers refuse to pay at least 500 PLN/year or 1,000 PLN transition support for a revenue-critical connector.
- A platform/carrier/payment provider already has or releases an official free connector that makes the acquired connector non-essential.
