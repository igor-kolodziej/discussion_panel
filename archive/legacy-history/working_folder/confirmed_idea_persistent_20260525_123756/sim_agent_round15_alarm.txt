Score: **80/100**

Control point tier: **Tier 2 cash-flow control point**  
Real control if the founder gets assigned/re-consented accounts, first-month cash, installer codes/records, and monitoring handoff. Not Tier 1 because customers can churn, incumbents can buy the same books, and the founder does not control a scarce technical asset.

Strongest objections/caveats:

- Economics are under-specified: no MRR/account, acquisition multiple, monitoring subcontract cost, service response cost, churn rate, or owner-earnings model.
- Unknown solo founder has a trust problem with both retiring installers and end customers, especially around alarm codes, security records, GDPR, and emergency response continuity.
- The first proof is concrete on paper, but hard: 20 re-consented/assigned accounts and first-month collection within 2 months is a real gate.
- Retention after transfer is the central risk. If customers use the ownership change as a reason to shop around, the book value collapses.
- Profitability depends on buying very small messy books cheaply. If sellers expect normal recurring-revenue multiples, payback gets weak fast.

Strongest copy risk: **existing local alarm/security companies.**  
They already have technicians, licenses/subcontractors, monitoring relationships, customer trust, and service infrastructure. They can copy the acquisition motion by adding a simple “retiring installer book purchase” process. Larger firms may ignore tiny books, but small incumbents will not.

Main cap applied: **below 85** because the roll-up can be copied by incumbents with an internal acquisition process plus existing monitoring/service capacity. Also stays below 87 because CAC/payback/margins/cash conversion/owner earnings are not yet credible enough.

Concrete improvement: define a hard acquisition formula and proof gate: **buy only books where 20+ accounts re-consent, first-month cash is collected before closing, seller consideration is mostly churn-adjusted earnout, and payback is under 6 months on net contribution after monitoring subcontract and service costs.** That would turn this from “possible recurring micro-rollup” into a sharper cash-transfer arbitrage.
