# Round 15: Live-Downtime Control Candidates

Created: 2026-05-25

Reason for this round: Orphan Tooling failed the real working-chat gate at 76 because the asset value depended on a rare triple coincidence: clear rights, usable physical condition, and reactivated stale demand. Round 15 shifts toward control points where the acute buyer trigger is live now: line-down parts, release-blocked lab modules, or cash-flow contract books.

Strict caps for simulated scoring:

- Cap below 82 if there is no concrete 2-month proof of owned/assigned/prepaid/routed control.
- Cap below 85 if incumbents can copy with one vendor purchase, one supplier contract, or one internal process.
- Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, retention, or meaningful owner earnings are not credible.
- Penalize any candidate where the first proof depends on vague partnerships, broad awareness, status, or slow institutional acceptance.

## Finalists

### 1. Retiring Automation Integrator Emergency PLC/HMI Exchange Book

Buyer: factory maintenance heads, plant owners, and automation integrators responsible for legacy production lines in Poland.

Acute trigger: a discontinued Siemens S7-300/ET200M, Omron CQM1/CJ, Lenze drive, HMI, I/O module, or communications module fails and stops a production line, or the plant buys insurance stock before an expected failure.

Control point: not a generic spare-parts shop. The founder signs an option/acquisition packet with one retiring or under-digitized Polish automation integrator covering:

- 30-80 obsolete PLC/HMI/drive modules and cables owned by the integrator or acquired from decommissioned projects;
- phone number/domain or emergency inquiry handoff for legacy Siemens/Omron/Lenze support;
- installed-base/service-ticket archive with part numbers, machine types, plant contacts, photos, cabinet notes, and known compatible replacements;
- bench-test videos, serial-number photos, firmware/version notes, and 14-day exchange/rental terms;
- 30-day owner transition support and first-refusal on future decommissioned stock.

2-month proof: sign one option/acquisition packet; take physical custody or reserved first-refusal over 30-50 SKUs; bench-test at least 20; publish exact part pages/listings; secure 3 paid emergency rentals/sales or insurance-stock purchases at 900-6,000 PLN each; document gross margin after acquisition, testing, courier, returns allowance, and owner earnout.

First acquisition: call 100-150 small automation integrators, service engineers, and electricians near manufacturing corridors; offer cash/earnout for obsolete stock plus a monetization plan for old emergency calls. Demand side uses exact-SKU SEO, Allegro, OLX, LinkedIn maintenance outreach, and the inherited installed-base archive.

Economics: buy/option modules at 150-1,500 PLN; sell/rent at 900-6,000 PLN depending on scarcity and downtime urgency; same-day courier premium; 50-75% gross margin target after returns allowance. Payback from 3-5 paid events.

Copy risk: Radwell, EU Automation, UNIS, Allegro/eBay, OEM channels, and local integrators. Defense is narrow local same-day tested stock plus inherited installed-base context, not broad catalog resale.

### 2. HPLC/GC Release-Block Exchange Module Bank

Buyer: QC labs in food, cosmetics, pharma-adjacent, chemical, university core labs, and CROs.

Acute trigger: an Agilent 1100/1200/1260, Shimadzu, Waters, or similar HPLC/GC autosampler, pump, detector, column oven, control PC, or license-linked component blocks analysis and delays batch release or client results.

Control point: owned tested legacy modules and instrument-PC/software bundles plus one retired service engineer's customer/contact book and diagnostic handoff. Narrow first SKU family: Agilent 1100/1200/1260 exchange modules only.

2-month proof: source 8-15 modules or one donor instrument bundle; bench-test 5 modules with photos/videos; secure a retired lab-service engineer for paid diagnostic slots and customer handoff; close 2 paid emergency rentals/exchanges or deposits at 1,000-4,000 PLN/week or 3,000-15,000 PLN sale/exchange.

First acquisition: exact-model SEO/listings, calls to lab managers, outreach to service engineers, and university/company surplus purchases.

Economics: high downtime value but testing and trust costs are high. Gross margin possible at 40-70% on used modules if acquisition is disciplined; payback requires 2-3 paid events.

Copy risk: OEM service, refurbished lab-equipment dealers, and service firms with better trust. Must avoid regulated diagnostic claims and frame as emergency bridge hardware plus technician referral.

### 3. Alarm Monitoring Cash-Transfer Micro-Book

Buyer: small commercial properties, warehouses, shops, offices, and residential customers currently monitored by a retiring local installer.

Acute trigger: installer retires, stops answering service calls, or can no longer maintain old panels/dialers; monitoring continuity and installer codes risk becoming stranded.

Control point: assigned or re-consented monitoring/service contracts, phone/domain, panel records, installer codes where transferable, customer list, monitoring-station reseller/subcontract agreement, first month cash collection, and 30-day owner transition.

2-month proof: sign option/acquisition for one 50-150 account micro-book; obtain re-consent or assignability for at least 20 accounts; collect first month from 20 accounts; subcontract licensed monitoring and emergency service response.

First acquisition: direct calls to 60+ older local alarm installers and small security firms; offer upfront cash plus churn-adjusted earnout.

Economics: recurring MRR plus service calls; payback if acquisition multiple is low and churn after transfer is controlled.

Copy risk: other alarm companies can buy books; telcos/security chains can compete. Defense is systematic roll-up of books too small and messy for larger firms.

### 4. Heat/Water Meter Cash-Transfer Micro-Book

Buyer: housing communities, property managers, and small cooperatives that need reading schedules, resident settlements, and device files to continue after a micro-operator retires.

Acute trigger: the meter-reading/billing micro-operator retires or misses an annual settlement cycle; readings, legal metrology dates, access process, and resident billing cannot fail.

Control point: assigned service contracts or administrator re-consent, installed-device database, reading schedules, billing files/templates, phone/domain, service inventory, and one first paid reading/settlement cycle.

2-month proof: option 300-1,000 flats; transfer or re-consent 3 communities; collect one paid cycle or prepaid transfer fee; secure file handoff and administrator acceptance.

First acquisition: call retiring local meter-reading/billing operators and property-management subcontractors.

Economics: annual reading/settlement fees plus meter replacement/calibration upsells; sticky if files transfer cleanly but ARPU and decision speed may be weak.

Copy risk: Techem/Ista/large property-service firms, Apator/remote-metering vendors, incumbent property managers, slow administrator decisions.

### 5. Elevator Legacy Board Installed-Base Rescue

Buyer: independent lift maintenance firms serving old buildings and home/equipment elevators.

Acute trigger: elevator down due obsolete controller/door board/drive, with full modernization quoted at a large multiple of the part repair cost.

Control point: owned tested boards/drives from modernization contractors and retiring technicians, programming/serialization notes where lawful, licensed elevator-service subcontractor, installed-base archive, and two signed buyer accounts from independent lift firms.

2-month proof: acquire 15-25 boards/drives; test/repair 5; sign two buyer/reseller agreements with licensed firms; close 2 paid emergency sales/rentals.

First acquisition: outreach to retiring lift technicians, modernization contractors, and small lift service companies.

Economics: urgent downtime pricing, but liability and diagnosis are heavy. Gross margin can be high on exact boards if sold only to licensed firms.

Copy risk: OEM channels, board repair shops, licensed lift companies, safety regulation, programming locks.

### 6. Cold-Room Controller Emergency Bank

Buyer: refrigeration installers, small grocery chains, restaurants, warehouses, florists, pharma/cold-storage operators with old cold rooms.

Acute trigger: refrigeration/cold-room controller, probe, compressor control, or older drive fails and spoilage risk starts immediately.

Control point: owned tested old controllers/probes/drives plus installer call-routing agreements, exact wiring compatibility notes, and local same-day courier dispatch.

2-month proof: acquire/test 30 controllers and probes; sign 3 refrigeration installers as routed emergency buyers; complete 5 paid emergency dispatches.

First acquisition: exact-part SEO, calls to refrigeration installers, OLX/Allegro listings, and buying old contractor stock.

Economics: small parts with high urgency; buy cheap, sell/rent at 3-8x. Payback possible from 5 dispatches.

Copy risk: refrigeration wholesalers and installers can copy if parts are easy to source; narrower obsolete exact-SKU archive and urgent dispatch are the only possible defense.

## Current Best Hypothesis

The strongest Round 15 candidate is **Retiring Automation Integrator Emergency PLC/HMI Exchange Book** because it converts the Orphan Tooling insight from "dormant asset might find demand" into "owned tested exact part solves live downtime." The hard question is whether the installed-base acquisition layer is enough to avoid being just a small Radwell/EU Automation clone.
