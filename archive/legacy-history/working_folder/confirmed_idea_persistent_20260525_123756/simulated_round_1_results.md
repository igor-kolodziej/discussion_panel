## Repeated Fatal Objections

- Route-book ownership is real but usually local-service defensibility, not domination.
- Customer calendars, expiry dates, access windows, and logs reduce CAC but are still copyable by a competent incumbent or another acquisition.
- Gross revenue targets did not prove owner earnings after technician time, travel, chemicals, partner fees, missed visits, insurance, admin, and churn.
- Several candidates depended on qualified technicians, authorized partners, or seller trust transfer, which weakens the founder's direct control.
- Most candidates can become profitable boring businesses, but the gate requires a stronger control point: credentials, assigned authority, transaction custody, unique inventory, or an asset customers cannot recreate easily.

## Pivot Instruction

Round 2 should not merely improve route-book wording. It must change the business shape toward acquired customer books with hard technical/admin custody attached:

- service/admin credentials;
- source codes, master passwords, config backups, or device registries;
- customer consent to transfer custody;
- phone/domain routing;
- owner handoff;
- prepaid emergency/takeover events.

The first Round 2 territory is low-voltage building systems where a retiring installer controls access-control/intercom/gate/CCTV admin credentials and building managers cannot safely switch vendors without those credentials.
