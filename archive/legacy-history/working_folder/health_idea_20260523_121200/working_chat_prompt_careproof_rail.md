Idea: CareProof Evidence Rail for small registered private elderly-care homes in Poland.

Context: Poland has a structural long-term-care pressure. A 2026 BGK/KIDO report says that at the end of 2024 Poland had 1,583 care homes, including 680 private 24-hour care homes, and more than 151,000 institutional-care places, but still lacked about 33,000 places to reach a stronger availability benchmark. Private care homes have been growing, but most small operators run on thin staff capacity, WhatsApp, paper notes, fragmented medication/hydration/meal/incident records, and ad hoc family calls. Legal private care homes are visible through voivode registers, so a founder can build a target list without privileged access.

Hidden insight: small private care-home owners do not mainly need generic care-home software. Their acute business fear is a family accusation, fall dispute, medication/hydration/pressure-sore complaint, resident deterioration argument, staff turnover gap, refund demand, bad Google review, or voivode inspection where they cannot prove what actually happened day by day. Families also do not want a dashboard; they want credible reassurance and evidence when something goes wrong.

Buyer and beachhead: owner-operated, registered private 24-hour elderly-care homes with 15-70 residents in Mazowieckie, Lodzkie, Wielkopolskie, or nearby regions. These homes are big enough that one serious complaint can threaten occupancy and owner income, but too small for enterprise care-management systems.

Product wedge: a mandatory live event evidence workflow for a defined pilot cohort of 10 residents. Staff route care events through simple mobile forms or a WhatsApp-style intake maintained by the founder: medication handoff confirmation, hydration/meal exceptions, falls, skin-risk checks, hygiene exceptions, family contact, consent changes, mood/behavior exceptions, transport, doctor/nurse visits, and incident photos when appropriate. The system creates timestamped owner evidence logs, family-ready monthly reassurance packets, incident packets, and inspection/complaint response bundles. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment planning, remote monitoring, a nurse replacement, or a generic CRM.

Initial acquisition: use public voivode registers to build a list of 100 registered private care homes. Send a very specific written offer to owners: "30-day complaint and inspection evidence sprint for 10 residents: if a family asks what happened, you can answer with a clean timeline instead of WhatsApp archaeology." Follow with phone calls. Offer a paid 4,000-8,000 PLN pilot, not a free trial. The founder can do one on-site setup day, then asynchronous support.

Primary control point and 2-month proof: secure one paid pilot where the owner makes the workflow mandatory for all defined care events for 10 residents during 30 days. Resource/control point: live resident-level care-event workflow access plus permissioned, anonymized complaint/incident template corpus. Proof before serious capital: signed pilot and DPA, 10 resident/family consent paths where needed, 250+ routed care events, at least 3 family-ready monthly packets, at least 1 incident or exception packet if it occurs, staff completion rate above 80%, owner confirms less time spent reconstructing events, and owner either renews or gives written refusal reason. Kill if the owner will not make routing mandatory or only wants a passive report.

Pricing and economics: 4,000-8,000 PLN setup/evidence sprint for first 30 days, then 1,500-4,000 PLN/month per home depending on resident cohort size, plus 20-50 PLN per active resident/month if expanded. Founder manual QA/support cost in POC 500-1,200 PLN/home/month; no hardware; no enterprise integration. Plausible 65-80% gross margin once templates and automations stabilize. Payback can be immediate for the home if it prevents one refund, vacancy, bad-review spiral, or owner/staff reconstruction day.

Domination path: start with one home and one painful promise: complaint-proof the facts of care. Then become the de facto "family dispute and inspection evidence rail" for small private care homes before expanding into insurer evidence, family premium reassurance packages, staff handover quality, legal-home verification, and eventually benchmarking across private care homes. Compounding assets: live workflow data, Polish care-event taxonomy, family objection memory, inspection/complaint packet templates, staff-compliance playbooks, and owner trust in a narrow painful workflow.

Why this is not merely a service/report/dashboard/database wrapper: the POC only counts if daily staff events are routed through the founder-controlled workflow. The control point is operational event flow plus owner-mandated use, not a static report or dashboard. Generic care software can be bought, but it does not own the complaint/inspection evidence habit or the family-ready proof packet workflow for small Polish private homes.
