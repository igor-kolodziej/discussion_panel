Idea: ShiftGuard Elder-Care Emergency Shift Bench for small registered private elderly-care homes in Poland.

Context: Polish long-term care has structural pressure: BGK/KIDO reported in 2026 that Poland still lacks around 33,000 institutional long-term-care places versus a stronger availability benchmark, while private 24-hour care homes are a visible and growing part of supply. Legal facilities can be found through voivode registers, so a founder can build a target list without introductions. The staffing bottleneck is immediate: small care-home owners cannot leave a night, weekend, holiday, sickness, or no-show shift uncovered, but generic staffing agencies are too slow, broad, or unreliable for small homes with fragile residents.

Hidden insight: small care-home owners do not mainly fear software gaps; they fear uncovered shifts. One sick caregiver or no-show can create resident safety risk, staff burnout, family complaints, inspection exposure, and owner panic. The scarce resource is not advice or a dashboard. It is a reliable local bench of vetted care workers who can fill emergency shifts fast.

Buyer and beachhead: owner-operated registered private 24-hour elderly-care homes with 15-80 residents in Warsaw/Mazowieckie first, then nearby Lodzkie and Wielkopolskie. The owner is the buyer because the economic damage of an uncovered shift is direct and urgent.

Product wedge: a paid standby bench for emergency replacement shifts. The founder recruits and vets a small pool of caregivers/opiekunowie and medical caregivers/opiekunowie medyczni willing to take priority shifts. Homes pay a monthly standby retainer for access plus a per-shift margin when a replacement is dispatched. The workflow is narrow: emergency absence, night/weekend/holiday coverage, sickness replacement, and short-notice rota gaps. It is not home-care for families, not a broad staffing marketplace, not medical treatment, and not software.

Legal/compliance path: before dispatching shifts, the founder spends the first two weeks on a labor-law template with a Polish employment-law advisor. If the operating model requires employment-agency registration or temporary-work agency registration, the POC either files the KRAZ/employment-agency registration immediately or uses a registered employment-agency/payroll partner as back-office of record while the founder owns demand, worker vetting, availability, scheduling, and home relationships. The final business should become compliant directly; the POC does not ignore labor-law risk.

Primary control point and 2-month proof: secure reserved caregiver capacity and paid care-home demand. Minimum proof under 100,000 PLN:

- 12-20 vetted caregivers signed to standby/priority-shift terms, with references, ID/work-right checks, role limits, preferred locations, hourly rates, and availability windows.
- At least 3 registered private care homes sign paid standby retainers of 1,000-2,500 PLN/month or prepaid emergency-shift credits.
- At least 5 real replacement shifts filled or, if shift demand does not arise, 5 paid simulation/on-call tests with response-time confirmation.
- Replacement confirmed within 4 hours for day/evening and 8 hours for night/next-day requests in the pilot geography.
- Founder owns the availability database, shift-request workflow, response-time history, worker reliability scores, and home-specific requirements.

Initial acquisition: build a list of 100-150 registered private homes from voivode registers and KRDO-style legal-home directories. Call owners with a concrete offer: "For 1,500 PLN/month, you get access to a vetted emergency caregiver bench so a sick leave/no-show does not leave your rota uncovered." Recruit workers through OLX, Facebook caregiver groups, local healthcare/caregiver schools, part-time worker groups, and referrals. No influencer marketing or high-status introductions.

Pricing and economics: homes pay 1,000-2,500 PLN/month standby retainer. Emergency shifts are billed at 45-90 PLN/hour depending on urgency, night/weekend, and worker qualification; worker pay is 30-65 PLN/hour. Gross margin on shifts is 15-35%, with retainer revenue mostly high-margin after screening/support costs. A plausible early target: 20 homes at 1,500 PLN/month equals 30,000 PLN monthly retainer before shift margin. POC budget: 20,000-45,000 PLN for legal setup/KRAZ path, recruitment ads, screening, trial retainers/standby bonuses, scheduling tools, insurance, and outreach.

Domination path: start with one narrow promise: emergency shift coverage for small private care homes in Mazowieckie. Build the most reliable local caregiver bench and response-time dataset. Expand to scheduled holiday-cover blocks, staff-vacation planning, verified caregiver training records, care-home worker quality scores, regional benches, and eventually insurer/association-backed staffing resilience. The compounding asset is not a job board; it is local availability, reliability history, home-specific fit data, worker trust, and paid standby relationships.

Why this is not merely a service/report/dashboard/database wrapper: the business only counts if the founder controls a real reserved labor bench and paid standby demand. A database of caregivers is not enough. A marketplace listing is not enough. The POC requires signed standby terms, paid home retainers or prepaid credits, and fulfilled emergency shifts or live response drills.
