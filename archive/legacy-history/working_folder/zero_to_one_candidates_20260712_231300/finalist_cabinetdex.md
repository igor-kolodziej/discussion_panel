# Zero-to-One Candidate: CabinetDex Skilled-Manipulation Task Capsules

## One-Sentence Thesis

Physical-AI companies will pay for reproducible electrical-cabinet task capsules that pair a shippable instrumented benchmark board with rights-cleared skilled-trade demonstrations, failures, recoveries, and tool-state ground truth that generic egocentric-video suppliers cannot provide.

## Founder Fit

The founder can combine data engineering, experiment design, machine learning, and AI-assisted annotation with lawful access to a renewable-installation workshop and paid electricians through the family business. He does not need to be a public personality. Initial selling is technical, written, and list-based. A part-time founder can design schemas, capture pipelines, baselines, and buyer-specific task packs while using electricians and hardware contractors for bounded production work.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The initial buyer is a European humanoid, dexterous-arm, VLA, or industrial-automation team preparing a manipulation milestone, investor/customer demonstration, or deployment involving electrical assembly.

The specific pain is not a shortage of generic video. Wiring and cabinet work combines deformable cables, occluded connectors, hand/tool coordination, torque, routing constraints, fragile terminals, and recovery from partial insertion or misalignment. The buyer currently uses simulation, generic human video, in-house teleoperation, or a general data-collection vendor. Those routes are expensive to configure and often omit physical ground truth, electrician-quality procedures, repeatable failures, or an identical evaluation object.

**Hypothesis:** A buyer will pay before capture when the physical board, sensors, task schema, acceptance metrics, and licence terms are fixed in advance.

## Product Wedge

A CabinetDex Task Capsule contains:

- Two identical shippable cabinet boards: one retained as the reference and one delivered or leased to the buyer.
- A defined task family such as wire stripping, ferrule crimping, terminal insertion, torqueing, cable routing, connector recovery, or DIN-rail component assembly.
- Synchronized egocentric/stereo/depth video, tool state, torque/force proxies, component state, phase labels, and outcome labels.
- Successful demonstrations plus deliberately captured common failures and safe recoveries.
- A machine-readable episode schema, dataset card, rights chain, baseline evaluation, and blind held-out challenge set.
- Optional buyer-funded teleoperation capture only after the buyer specifies and prepays for the exact robot/action space.

The first product is one narrow capsule—terminal insertion, torque, and recovery on a fixed cabinet family—not a broad “robotics data marketplace.”

## Hidden Insight or Market Misunderstanding

Most data suppliers assume physical-AI value scales mainly with hours. For skilled industrial manipulation, the scarce unit may instead be a reproducible physical task with measured state transitions, failures, recovery logic, and legal rights. The cabinet board makes training and evaluation portable across teams, while paid electricians produce a procedure and failure taxonomy that crowd contributors cannot safely improvise.

## Underused Momentum

- Robotics foundation models increasingly mix human video, real robot trajectories, and simulation.
- Industrial wiring and connector manipulation remains an active research problem in 2026.
- Physical-AI data vendors validate the existence of a commercial data category, but most advertise broad environments or large generic contributor networks.
- Poland offers skilled electrical labour at a lower capture cost than Western European robotics hubs, while the family installation company supplies a lawful initial environment and trade expertise.

## Beachhead Market

European physical-AI and industrial-robotics teams with fewer than roughly 300 employees that are actively demonstrating cable, connector, cabinet, or tool-mediated assembly and lack their own electrical-workshop data flywheel.

The niche is small enough to map directly and economically meaningful because each team can buy high-value task packs, custom collection, or annual updates. The first task is limited to low-voltage mock equipment; CabinetDex does not operate on energized production cabinets.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

All numbers are hypotheses until quoted and paid:

- Evaluation sample: 100 episodes plus board specification under a non-commercial licence, free or 2,000 PLN qualification fee.
- Existing capsule licence: 15,000–60,000 PLN per buyer, depending on sensor depth and exclusivity.
- Custom schema-confirmed capsule: 40,000–120,000 PLN, with 50% paid before capture.
- Physical challenge-board sale/lease: 8,000–25,000 PLN.
- Annual update licence: 20,000–60,000 PLN for new components, failures, and benchmark releases.
- Target mature gross margin: above 65% on standard capsules and above 50% on custom capture.

A first capsule costing 45,000–70,000 PLN to build becomes viable if two buyers pay at least 30,000 PLN each or one buyer prepays a custom pack. No robot is purchased during the POC without buyer funding.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First 10:**

1. Build a list of 100 European humanoid, dexterous-arm, VLA, industrial-automation, and robotics-data teams from public product launches, research papers, EU projects, and exhibitor lists.
2. Send 60–100 technical leads a short task-specification memo, 20 sample episodes, rights summary, and challenge-board CAD preview.
3. Run 15 discovery calls asynchronously where possible; require each prospect to rank task, sensor, action, and acceptance needs.
4. Offer ten paid or deposit-backed design commitments for a single task capsule.
5. Capture at scale only after at least two commitments or one 50%-prepaid custom order.

**First 100:**

- Publish versioned benchmark results that make CabinetDex discoverable to model teams.
- Add five adjacent capsule families and sell through direct technical outbound, robotics conferences, research citations, and integrations with common dataset formats.
- License non-exclusive standard capsules globally while reserving premium time-limited exclusivity for specific task/component combinations.
- Recruit specialized workshop operators as paid vendors only after demand is proven; the company keeps fixtures, schemas, QA, and rights custody.

## Primary Control Point

The control point is the company-owned task capsule: physical reference fixtures and benchmark boards, capture hardware, task ontology, procedural and failure taxonomy, synchronized recordings, derived annotations, held-out challenge set, and perpetual commercial training/evaluation/sublicensing rights.

This is controllable without a speculative partnership. The company buys the equipment, contracts electricians under work-for-hire and explicit data/IP assignment, and retains all raw and derived assets. Customer feedback may improve the benchmark only where contracts grant anonymized error-result rights.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Resource needed: one reproducible low-voltage cabinet task environment, skilled demonstrations, sensor-grounded outcomes, and commercial rights.

Bootstrap:

1. Spend 5,000–8,000 PLN on buyer interviews, legal templates, and schema design.
2. Spend 15,000–25,000 PLN on two or three identical cabinet boards, components, tools, cameras, depth sensing, lighting, and torque instrumentation.
3. Pay electricians 10,000–20,000 PLN for bounded capture sessions and exclusive rights assignment.
4. Spend 10,000–18,000 PLN on annotation, storage, QA, and a baseline model/evaluation.
5. Reserve 10,000–15,000 PLN for shipping, replacement parts, insurance, and contingency.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: equipment and contractor labour are purchased rather than requested as favors; the family company supplies a lawful workshop and relevant trade network; buyer acquisition uses public technical lists. Proof before serious capital is one shippable board pair, 1,000 synchronized episodes including at least 100 failures/recoveries, a reproducible baseline, signed rights, and at least one paid licence or collection order.

## Distribution Advantage

The initial channel is direct technical outbound tied to visible manipulation milestones, not broad content marketing. Every delivered board and benchmark result creates a referenceable artifact. If buyers allow anonymized error reporting, cross-model blind spots determine the next capsule and create a product roadmap unavailable to generic crowd-data vendors.

## Domination and Expansion Path

1. Dominate the narrow “instrumented electrical cabinet task capsule” category.
2. Expand through adjacent electrical actions: connectors, harness routing, inverter wiring, inspection, and maintenance recovery.
3. Add HVAC, industrial controls, and other CEE skilled trades where the company can own standardized environments and rights.
4. Become the neutral task/evaluation layer used by multiple robot models rather than competing as a general labour marketplace.
5. Add buyer-funded teleoperation and embodiment-specific adaptation after the catalogue and customer base exist.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland supplies relevant electrical talent, workshop access, manufacturing context, and lower capture costs inside EU commercial/privacy law. The family business shortens the first environment build. The defence is not Polish language; it is EU-lawful rights, electrician-grade procedures, reusable physical fixtures, and faster creation of neglected industrial task capsules than broad global vendors prioritize.

## Compounding Advantage

- Each capsule adds physical fixtures, task definitions, rights-cleared episodes, failure modes, and benchmark history.
- Buyer false negatives and recovery failures reveal what to capture next.
- Standard board families reduce future collection cost.
- A multi-model outcome history can become a trusted purchasing and readiness benchmark.
- Skilled-operator contracts and proven QA make it faster to open adjacent trade environments.

## Critical Evidence

- Foundation-model developers publicly use human video and real robot trajectories.
- Active research targets electrical-cabinet, connector, and wiring manipulation.
- Multiple commercial firms sell robotics demonstration data, proving category demand and establishing the competitive bar.
- The founder has lawful access to an initial workshop and can pay electricians rather than relying on outside institutional acceptance.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Human video plus tool/physical state and a standard board is valuable before robot-specific teleoperation.
- Two buyers will pre-commit to the same task/schema.
- A 1,000-episode capsule is decision-useful despite not being a massive foundation-model dataset.
- Buyers accept non-exclusive licensing and will provide enough error feedback to guide updates.
- Data and physical IP contracts are enforceable across the target jurisdictions.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than two paid/deposit-backed commitments after 100 qualified outreaches and 20 substantive buyer conversations.
- Buyers unanimously require their exact robot/action space but will not prepay teleoperation capture.
- A generic provider quotes an equivalent cabinet capsule at a price the founder cannot beat while retaining attractive margins.
- The board and human demonstrations fail to predict or rank model performance in a buyer's pilot.
- Rights, privacy, or workshop-safety requirements make the first capsule exceed 100,000 PLN.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Month 0–2: buyer schema discovery and pre-commitments; 8,000 PLN cap.
- Month 2–5: board/capture prototype and first 100 episodes; cumulative 35,000 PLN cap.
- Month 5–8: full capsule, baseline, and blind challenge; cumulative 70,000 PLN cap.
- Month 8–12: paid licence, update, or buyer-funded teleoperation; total 95,000 PLN cap.

Minimum stack: founder/data lead, one electrical-domain contractor, one part-time capture/annotation contractor, and legal review for rights templates. No robot purchase and no full-time hires during POC.
