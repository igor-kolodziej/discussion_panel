# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Textile-Bale Yield Underwriting — Follow-up

## Diagnosis

Follow-up research confirms that feedstock quality and realized yield are important, but a major EU consortium is already integrating multisensor sorting, recyclers, traceability, and business models. Experimental evidence also shows that the buyer's own process design materially changes yield, making cross-site transfer and external underwriting harder. Without contracted private outcomes and paid decisions, the candidate is a field-heavy measurement hypothesis rather than a controlled pricing rail.

## Evidence and Assumptions

New evidence: SORT4CIRC's scope and funding, experimental process/yield sensitivity, and established NIR classification. Still missing: representative sampling proof, buyer data assignments, paid underwriting demand, and reusable cross-site accuracy.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** Retained samples can be owned, but realized-yield rights and transaction access are outside the founder's current control. Strongest copy risk: consortium participants or individual recyclers link their existing sensor and line data internally.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is not sufficiently standardized across processes. Local access does not overcome incumbent operational data.

## Fatal Objections

1. Process specificity prevents reusable underwriting.
2. Customer outcome data is speculative.
3. A well-funded consortium already attacks the integrated problem.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Purchasing should be priced against process-specific usable yield, not fibre labels.
