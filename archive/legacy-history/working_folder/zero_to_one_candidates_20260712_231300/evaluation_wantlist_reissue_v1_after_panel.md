# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Wantlist Reissue v1 After Independent Panel

## Diagnosis

The two independent reviews converge on structural, not cosmetic, failures. The candidate does not control a title today; the proposed 250-order manufacturing trigger is below its own break-even; and public scarcity data offers no owned way for an unknown founder to reach 250 purchasers. Even a successful exact-title option remains a small temporary monopoly whose buyers, rights work, and economics may reset on every release. Changing numeric thresholds would not solve weak distribution, incomplete owner earnings, or the absence of a durable cross-title control point.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Both panel reviews evaluated the exact SHA-256 `53ecfff714761a007cd729003d68b2d715e4da0391229aa74975a5c7456451b9` and scored it 63 and 61. Their scores are advisory inputs here, not market evidence. The shared objections are supported by the candidate's own numbers and resource status: no signed option, 250-order exercise versus 300–350 break-even, at most 25,000–32,500 PLN contribution across 500 units before clearly deducting all fixed costs, and only hypothesized cross-title demand.

## Overall Score

**52/100.** Weighted sum `83.0 / 16`; `10 × 5.1875 = 51.875`, rounded to 52.

## Control Point

**Tier 3 today; conditionally Tier 1 only for one exact signed title.** The strongest copy risk is an established label using its artist trust, catalogue relationships, audience, and retail distribution to option adjacent superior titles. That competitor does not need the founder's contract to dominate the micro-genre.

## Expansion Check

The exact-title beachhead is testable but too economically small. Adjacent releases require new rights and largely new demand, so expansion does not reliably reuse the initial monopoly. Local chain-of-title skill cannot offset incumbent catalogue access and distribution.

## Fatal Objections

1. No controlled foundational resource exists before the panel.
2. The exercise gate is below break-even and does not fully customer-finance the run.
3. The founder has no owned, permissioned, or proven route to 250 buyers.
4. Cross-title demand, rights access, and owner earnings do not compound sufficiently.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Customer-funded exercise of an exclusive right is a strong downside-control mechanism, but only when the underlying rights portfolio or distribution flow already exists.
