### Strongest objections and caveats

1. **The foundational resource does not yet exist.** There is no named title, verified rights holder, source master, signed option, or authorized campaign. The candidate currently proves a disciplined search process, not that a suitable asset can actually be controlled below the 8,000 PLN fee cap.
2. **The demand thresholds do not reconcile.** A minimum of 100 wantlist accounts and 20 completed used transactions is weak evidence for 250 paid repress orders in 60 days. Original-pressing buyers may value provenance and scarcity rather than merely access to the music.
3. **The preorder gate does not fully fund the run under the candidate's own model.** The stated break-even is 300–350 copies, yet manufacturing begins at 250. That leaves 50–100 additional sales exposed after production, contradicting the strongest interpretation of “buyers have funded the run.”
4. **“Fully loaded contribution” appears incomplete.** Its listed deductions omit or ambiguously treat option fees, legal diligence, catalogue research, storefront setup, campaign production, and founder labor. At 50 PLN contribution per copy, selling all 500 produces 25,000 PLN before founder labor; the pre-threshold fixed spend could absorb most of that.
5. **Distribution is still title-dependent, not demonstrably repeatable.** Dormant rightsholders may have weak audiences, while public collector communities are accessible to every label. Requiring no channel to exceed 50% is a useful test, but makes the initial 250-order cold start harder for an unknown founder.
6. **The scale path is operationally heavy relative to the prize.** Each title renews legal, asset, approval, manufacturing, and launch work. A successful first run may establish a small profitable label, but the candidate has not shown economics capable of supporting the founder's very-large-wealth objective without many simultaneous releases.
7. **Founder feasibility is plausible only for the POC.** One release can fit five hours per day with contractors. Building a defensible catalogue while repeatedly negotiating fragmented legacy rights may not.

### Control-point tier and why

**Tier 1, conditional and limited to the exact title after signature.** A paid, executed exclusive option controlling a specified recording, format, territory, copy ceiling, preorder authorization, manufacturing, and sales rights is a directly controlled scarce right. A competitor cannot legally sell that exact repress during valid exclusivity.

Before signature, however, the candidate is **Tier 3—not Tier 2**. Screening data, a rights-holder list, and intended outreach are not controlled resources, and repeatable access has not been demonstrated. The business earns Tier 1 only when the proposed option is actually executed and survives chain-of-title diligence. Even then, the control is temporary, title-specific, and capped at 500 copies.

### Strongest copy risk

An established Polish or foreign label can copy the entire operating method and secure adjacent or superior titles first, using stronger artist relationships, catalogue credibility, distribution, and pressing economics. Exact-title exclusivity blocks direct copying only briefly; it does not protect the micro-genre opportunity.

The risk becomes decisive if the first buyer graph fails to transfer across titles. In that case, each option is a fresh acquisition campaign, and the company owns a sequence of temporary contracts rather than a compounding distribution asset. After the option or sell-off period ends, the rightsholder can also self-repress or license the title elsewhere.

### Strongest objections/caveats

- **The demand evidence does not support the 250-order threshold.** A title can qualify with only 100 wantlist accounts and 20 completed scarcity-priced transactions, yet must attract 250 paid repress orders. Wantlists, original-pressing trades, streaming activity, and community discussion are different behaviors from buying a new 149–169 PLN edition. Original scarcity may reflect collectors valuing the original artifact rather than latent demand for a repress.
- **The cold-start distribution plan is the main veto.** Discogs-style signals identify demand but do not provide an owned, permissioned way to reach it. The unknown founder must reach 250 purchasers without broad paid media, privileged access, personal influence, or any channel supplying over 50% of orders. Rightsholder channels may be small precisely because the catalogue is dormant, while collector communities can restrict commercial outreach. The 20 PLN preorder CAC ceiling is therefore an unsupported hypothesis.
- **“Contribution” is not demonstrated owner earnings.** At 50–65 PLN across all 500 copies, the run generates 25,000–32,500 PLN contribution before founder labor. The stated calculation does not clearly deduct all title-level fixed costs—option fee, counsel, rights mapping, campaign work, source testing, artwork preparation, and coordination. Deducting those costs could leave little or no first-run owner profit. A 500-copy ceiling also caps upside while retaining substantial bespoke work.
- **Expansion may reset rather than compound.** Every title requires fresh rights discovery, negotiation, chain-of-title diligence, asset verification, approvals, demand formation, and production. A buyer of one cult release is not necessarily a buyer of an adjacent release. Even the proposed 25% paid-reservation test would yield roughly 63 interested buyers from 250 preorder customers, far below the next 250-order gate.
- **Rights availability is still speculative.** No title, clean chain of title, source master, rightsholder response, or acceptable option has been secured. The best-demand titles may command more than 8,000 PLN, already have plans, lack usable assets, or be controlled by parties preferring an established label. Twenty cold contacts are a concrete experiment, but not evidence of repeatable access.
- **Founder fit is mixed.** Data screening, automation, financial gating, and written operations fit well. The core bottlenecks, however, are cultural judgment, rights negotiation, trust with artists or heirs, community legitimacy, production coordination, and exception-heavy customer service. Contractors help execution but do not create credibility or rights-holder access, and scaling several releases could exceed five hours per day.
- **The market and cost assertions remain candidate claims within the permitted evidence boundary.** The reported vinyl revenue, plant cost, option pricing, CAC, contribution, and rightsholder receptivity have not been independently verified here. The candidate correctly labels many of them as hypotheses, so the score credits test quality but not market proof.

### Control-point tier and why

**Tier 3 today; conditionally Tier 1 after execution.** At present, the foundational resource is an intended contract with an unidentified rightsholder, so access is speculative and cold-outreach-dependent. It does not qualify as Tier 2 merely because a sourcing process is described; repeatable access has not been demonstrated. A signed, paid, enforceable exclusive option containing the specified repress, preorder, artwork, master, manufacturing, and non-compete rights would become Tier 1 for that exact title and term. That would be genuine direct control, but narrow and temporary rather than control of the wider genre or distribution layer.

### Strongest copy risk

An established label can route around the exact-title exclusivity by securing the best adjacent titles. It can copy the screening-plus-preorder process using existing artist trust, catalogue relationships, genre credibility, customer lists, retail distribution, and fulfilment history. It cannot legally copy the contracted title during exclusivity, but collectors can switch to substitute releases and rightsholders can choose the proven operator. Unless fulfilled campaigns produce demonstrably lower CAC, preferential multi-title access, and paid cross-title demand, the company owns isolated temporary monopolies while competitors capture the more durable rights cluster.

## Shared Fatal Objections

1. No exact right is currently secured; the present control point is Tier 3.
2. Original-pressing scarcity does not prove 250 repress buyers, and the entry criteria are far below the order target.
3. The 250-order exercise gate is below the candidate's own 300–350-copy break-even range.
4. Distribution to 250 buyers is not owned or proven and is especially weak for an unknown founder.
5. Each title restarts rights, legal, audience, and production work; cross-title owner earnings and domination are not credible.

## Panel Decision

Kill the Wantlist Reissue / serial micro-rights-option business shape for this run rather than cosmetically adjusting thresholds. A viable revision would require an already controlled multi-title catalogue or owned distribution/payment flow, which is a different resource and business shape not evidenced in this round.
