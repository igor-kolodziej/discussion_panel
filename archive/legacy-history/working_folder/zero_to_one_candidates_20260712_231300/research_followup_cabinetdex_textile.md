# Decision-Critical Follow-up Research

## CabinetDex

The missing fact was whether the proposed human-video plus instrumented-board wedge is sufficiently distinct from commercial robotics-data supply and sufficiently useful before robot-specific capture.

- Amplibotics, Operant, Embodied AI Data Labs, Roborecs, Renlei Labs, 1to1.bot, Motionstack, and others already market real-world, egocentric, industrial, or task-specific manipulation data.
- Renlei explicitly lists electrical work.
- Operant states that useful data must match the robot, environment, and evaluation goal, supporting the embodiment objection.
- Active research uses simulation and robot-specific demonstrations for cabinet and connector tasks.

This evidence strengthens the timing thesis but weakens differentiated supply and catalogue reuse. No public evidence proves willingness to pay for a common human-video/board capsule before robot-specific work.

## Textile-Bale Yield Underwriting

The missing facts were whether composition-to-yield modeling is an open commercial gap and whether process transfer can support a reusable product.

- The EUR 5 million EU-funded SORT4CIRC consortium already combines industrial sorters/recyclers, AI, multisensors, NIR, traceability, and business-model work.
- Experimental literature shows process design can change yield dramatically, making each recycler's equipment and operating recipe central.
- NIR classification can be highly accurate on pure and binary mixtures, but that does not establish representative bale sampling or maximum-bid WTP.
- No public evidence found a recurring external underwriting fee paid by small recyclers.

The follow-up confirms the problem but raises competition, process-transfer, and customer-data-dependence risk. A market interview could revisit it later, but the current exact candidate should not proceed to a panel.
