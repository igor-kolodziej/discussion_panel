# Zero-to-One Evaluation: LyoShield Animal-Free Lyophilization Protectant

## Diagnosis

LyoShield has an unusually concrete starting resource: identified patents that the owner publicly offers to license or sell, and a paid blinded test can measure value without founder prestige. The problem is that the claims cover a simple ingredient combination in a field where commercial producers commonly use proprietary sugars, proteins, and process-specific protectants. No evidence yet shows that target producers use costly BSA at meaningful volume, will outsource this formulation decision, or will reorder enough concentrate to support the proposed technical-sales burden. The candidate should not enter a panel until those three facts are resolved.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Sourced evidence establishes UG ownership/offer status, TRL4, the composition and laboratory comparisons, and an ATCC BSA recipe. The licence price, European patent status by country, field exclusivity, freedom to operate, actual customer BSA spend, paid qualification demand, reorder volume, gross margin, and design-around difficulty are hypotheses. The founder can fund the first test, but technical feasibility is not willingness to pay.

## Overall Score

**62/100.** Weighted sum `99.5 / 16`; `10 × 6.21875 = 62.1875`, rounded to 62.

## Control Point

**Tier 2 today, potentially Tier 1 after an exclusive field option.** The patent owner and route are specific, but no right is yet reserved. Strongest copy risk: a culture producer or ingredients supplier uses another plant peptone/sugar/protein formulation outside the claims and leverages existing qualification relationships.

## Expansion Check

The non-pharma probiotic/starter-culture beachhead is coherent, and adjacent microbial categories reuse production and QA. Competitors can enter with alternative protectants, while large producers already control strain/process know-how. Poland improves access to IP and experiments but does not create downstream customer lock-in.

## Fatal Objections

1. The target buyer may not use enough BSA or may already have a cheaper proprietary formula, eliminating the paid trigger.
2. A simple formulation patent may be cheap to design around, so qualification data—not the right—must carry the moat.
3. Low reagent volume per customer may not fund lengthy technical qualification and support.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

An exclusively licensed animal-free microbial-process input can be sold through objective customer-strain qualification rather than scientific authority.
