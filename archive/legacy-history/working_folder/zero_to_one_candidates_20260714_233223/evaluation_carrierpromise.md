# Zero-to-One Evaluation: CarrierPromise

## Diagnosis

CarrierPromise identifies a real and commercially legible transaction boundary: freight terms negotiated by humans can lose meaning between the call, booking, and invoice. The brief is unusually disciplined on pricing, reachability, capital staging, and kill criteria, but the evidence only establishes that freight calls and digital freight automation exist; it does not establish the frequency or monetary value of commitment leakage, willingness to pay 2,800 PLN monthly, or repeatable acquisition. The proposed commitment-to-settlement graph could become useful, but it begins as a non-exclusive, contract-dependent data asset that TMS, telephony, and freight voice-AI incumbents can reproduce through a routine product extension. This is a credible paid-research candidate, not yet a defensible or proven profitable company.

## Evidence and Assumptions

**Sourced evidence**

- HappyRobot and FleetWorks show that carrier communication, negotiation, tracking, and related freight workflows are phone-heavy and already attracting voice-AI investment.
- Transporeon shows that structured offers, freight assignment, and electronic acceptance are established workflow patterns.
- The European Commission's eFTI material establishes continued freight-data digitization, but eFTI does not itself prove demand for live call commitment capture.
- The cited commercial performance claims come from vendor case studies and company marketing, not independent customer research.

**Candidate hypotheses**

- A 5–50-dispatcher forwarder loses several thousand PLN monthly through commitment ambiguity or rework.
- The economic buyer will pay approximately 2,800 PLN monthly for an independent overlay rather than use email recap, call recording, TMS notes, or an incumbent feature.
- Dispatchers will route calls through a new SIP or softphone workflow and carriers will respond to confirmation links.
- Customers will grant reusable anonymized rights to corrections and settlement outcomes.
- Critical-term extraction can reach at least 97% precision and recall across CEE freight languages.
- Direct CAC can remain below 7,000 PLN, annual gross logo retention can reach 88%, and mature gross margin can reach 76%.
- A bootstrapped company can acquire 180 CEE forwarders while preserving 85% founder ownership and a 27% operating margin.

**Consequential missing facts**

- No direct customer evidence quantifies disputes, booking corrections, unrecovered accessorials, or labor cost caused specifically by call-to-booking ambiguity.
- No paid discovery, deposit, pilot, or budget-owner statement supports the proposed price.
- No market denominator shows how many CEE forwarders fit the 5–50-dispatcher beachhead or how concentrated they are by TMS and telephony stack.
- No observed dispatcher-adoption, carrier-confirmation, legal-consent, transcription-accuracy, CAC, churn, or support data exists.
- The expected valuation multiple and operating margin are plausible modeling assumptions but are not evidence.

**External verdicts**

- None. This evaluation is advisory only and is not a simulated-panel, working-chat, fresh-chat, confirmation, or validation result.

## Overall Score

**65/100.** Total weight is 20.0; weighted points are 130.5. The calculation is `10 × (130.5 ÷ 20.0) = 65.25`, rounded to 65.

The score remains well below 80 because the control point is not yet scarce and adjacent incumbents can copy the core confirmation workflow through a routine product extension. The candidate earns credit for a strong insight, narrow beachhead, disciplined economics, and reachable proof plan, but it lacks direct evidence for the heavily weighted willingness-to-pay, distribution, retention, durability, and founder-wealth assumptions.

## Control Point

**Tier 2, conditional.** Direct call routing, customer contracts, and explicit outcome-data rights make the proposed commitment-to-settlement graph repeatably accessible and provable within 12 months; it does not depend on an exclusive platform partnership. It is not Tier 1 because the data is not scarce or controlled before customers adopt the workflow, customers may refuse reusable outcome rights, and every customer remains free to provide the same data to a TMS or competing vendor.

The strongest copy risk is a TMS, telephony provider, Transporeon-like platform, or freight voice-AI company adding human-call extraction, confirmation links, and booking-to-invoice reconciliation. That response requires software work and existing customer permission, not acquisition of a scarce resource. The candidate's only credible catch-up defense is accumulating a materially better multilingual outcome graph and embedding it into dispatcher habits before incumbents respond; that defense is currently a hypothesis.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, narrowly on the written model.** The candidate includes conservative, expected, and strong scenarios covering customers, price, revenue, gross and operating margin, capital, ownership, dilution, reinvestment, business value, distributions, liabilities, and timing. It applies 95% reinvestment in conservative and expected employment periods, 100% in the strong employment period, caps optional employment-period distributions at 5%, and does not separately count retained earnings in addition to business value.

The expected-case arithmetic is internally consistent: 180 customers at 2,800 PLN monthly produce 6.048m PLN annual revenue; a 27% operating margin produces approximately 1.633m PLN EBIT; 4× EBIT implies 6.532m PLN equity value with zero net debt; 85% founder ownership gives 5.552m PLN of founder equity, and 0.100m PLN of modeled after-tax distributions raises the total to 5.652m PLN.

The decisive weakness is credibility rather than arithmetic. There is no evidence yet that 180 suitable CEE customers can be acquired at this ARPA, 88% retention, sub-7,000 PLN CAC, 76% gross margin, and 27% operating margin while the founder retains 85%. The conservative case produces only 0.767m PLN of founder wealth, so failure to achieve the expected acquisition and margin assumptions removes the wealth path. The gate therefore permits continued research, not a conclusion that the wealth outcome is likely.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** The candidate seeks a 3,000 PLN paid pilot within 180 days, caps pre-proof spending at 38,000 PLN, keeps the total first-year founder exposure at 100,000 PLN, and excludes family capital. The pilot can use synthetic demonstrations, post-call extraction, dispatcher confirmation, CSV or email export, and manual review rather than a finished real-time system or TMS integration.

The main pre-proof dependencies are legal workability, economic-buyer interest, and dispatcher willingness to route or upload calls. Carrier confirmation, outcome-data scale, deep integrations, and fully automated accuracy can be tested after a paid commitment rather than solved simultaneously before sale. The proposed 20–25 founder hours per week is at the maximum available capacity but remains compatible with employment if engineering and freight-domain work are contracted as specified.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is appropriately narrow: Polish cross-border road-freight forwarders with 5–50 dispatchers, phone-heavy carrier procurement, and owner- or operations-led purchasing. Adjacent detention, invoice, tracking, carrier-onboarding, and shipper-procurement workflows reuse the same commitment schema, but moving into them increases integration, liability, and incumbent overlap.

Competitors would not struggle technically to enter. HappyRobot, FleetWorks, Transporeon, telephony vendors, and TMS companies already control parts of the call, workflow, customer relationship, or system of record. CarrierPromise can expand outward more easily only if it first captures permissioned multilingual correction and settlement outcomes that materially improve its product.

A Poland-based team can serve the initial market profitably if onboarding remains standardized, legal controls are reusable, and customers accept an API-light workflow. Polish and CEE specialization may lower CAC and improve extraction, but it does not by itself protect margin once a large incumbent localizes.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The paid pain is not evidenced.** Existing sources establish freight-call activity and automation, not that call-to-booking ambiguity produces enough frequent, recoverable loss for a 2,800 PLN monthly budget.
2. **The control point may never become controlled.** The proposed data asset requires dispatchers to route calls, carriers to confirm terms, customers to provide downstream outcomes, and customers to grant reusable rights; each can fail independently.
3. **Incumbent bundling is straightforward.** TMS, telephony, freight-platform, and voice-AI vendors already own adjacent workflow and distribution and can add structured confirmation without acquiring a scarce asset.
4. **Distribution is numerically asserted rather than demonstrated.** The expected wealth case requires 180 customers, yet cold-outreach conversion, sales cycle, CAC, partner contribution, and renewal behavior are unknown.
5. **The margin model may omit operational burden.** Multilingual accuracy review, legal support, customer-specific freight terminology, telephony issues, onboarding, and TMS requests could turn a 76% gross-margin product into a support-heavy service.
6. **The founder lacks freight-domain and distribution leverage.** Technical fit is strong, but building credibility with forwarding owners while employed may require more sustained industry selling than the profile naturally supports.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Human freight negotiation should be treated as a commercial transaction boundary: the valuable output is a counterparty-confirmed commitment linked to booking and settlement outcomes, not a generic call transcript.
