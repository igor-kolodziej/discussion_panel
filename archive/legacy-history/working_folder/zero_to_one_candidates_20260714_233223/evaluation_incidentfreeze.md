# Zero-to-One Evaluation: IncidentFreeze

## Diagnosis

IncidentFreeze targets a more acute event than generic post-market surveillance: preserving volatile physiological and software evidence before complaint investigation begins. MDR timing supports urgency, but the candidate does not establish that connected-wearable manufacturers actually lose this evidence, lack internal observability, or will insert an unknown external vendor into a regulated complaint SOP for the proposed price. The installed mapping and prepaid standby can create switching cost, yet QMS, device-cloud, and engineering incumbents can reproduce the preservation layer and already hold buyer trust. Both hard gates are structurally satisfied, but recurring willingness to pay and delivery leverage require evidence before pursuit.

## Evidence and Assumptions

**Sourced evidence**

- MDR imposes time-sensitive serious-incident and trend-reporting duties on medical-device manufacturers.
- European Commission guidance and EUDAMED establish structured vigilance and market-surveillance workflows.
- Greenlight Guru sells complaint, CAPA, and post-market workflows; Wipro sells complaint handling, vigilance reporting, recalls, CAPA, and trend reporting. These demonstrate real post-market budgets and strong incumbent coverage.

**Candidate hypotheses**

- Small connected-wearable OEMs have a material gap between complaint intake and immediate preservation of raw signals, application logs, configuration, firmware, calibration, and algorithm versions.
- Evidence is sufficiently volatile that a rehearsal prevents meaningful regulatory, engineering, or liability loss.
- Manufacturers will prepay 30,000–40,000 PLN for a rehearsal before an actual incident and later pay 100,000–150,000 PLN annually per product line.
- A manufacturer will amend its SOP, provide sandbox and production-export access, and route evidence preservation through a small external provider.
- Product mappings become at least 60% reusable while maintaining 60–80% recurring gross margins.
- Thirty retained European product lines are attainable without incident frequency or expert response labor undermining economics.

**Consequential missing facts**

- No evidence quantifies how often required telemetry is lost, how much investigation delay costs, or which OEM segment lacks adequate cloud logging and version traceability.
- No buyer evidence supports rehearsal price, standby ACV, procurement time, renewal, or willingness to change an SOP.
- The candidate does not establish what its snapshot component captures that ordinary observability, data lakes, device-cloud logs, or existing QMS integrations cannot preserve.
- Security validation, cyber risk, product-change classification, professional liability, insurance cost, and response staffing remain unknown.

There are no prior evaluator or chatbot verdicts in the candidate, and this advisory evaluation is not a validation result.

## Overall Score

**58/100.** Weighted points are 115.5 across a total weight of 20: `115.5 ÷ 20 = 5.775`; multiplied by 10 gives 57.75, rounded to 58. The score remains below 80 because an OEM, QMS provider, device-cloud platform, or post-market-services incumbent can add evidence freezing through routine engineering and an existing customer relationship.

## Control Point

**Tier 2, contingent on signed SOP routing and an installed component.** A prepaid standby agreement, explicit placement in the evidence-preservation SOP, product-specific telemetry mappings, and historical baselines are directly contractable within 12 months. They create practical switching costs and recurring access without relying on a speculative partnership.

The strongest copy risk is the manufacturer’s own cloud/quality team—or Greenlight Guru, MasterControl, Veeva, Wipro, or a device-cloud vendor—adding immutable event snapshots and version manifests to systems already trusted and integrated. The SOP annex can be cancelled, and IncidentFreeze does not own the underlying telemetry, complaint transaction, regulatory submission, or customer distribution.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, with material evidence risk.** Conservative, expected, and strong scenarios specify retained product lines, activations, incidents, pricing, revenue, gross and operating margins, later capital, ownership, dilution, reinvestment, debt, valuation, distributions, and timing. Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% while employed and cap optional distributions; strong success reinvests 100%. Debt is deducted, and post-transition distributions are not also treated as retained business value.

The conservative case reaches approximately 5.68m PLN by year 8 through 30 retained product lines at 120,000 PLN annually, incident and activation revenue, 88% founder ownership, and 800,000 PLN of post-transition distributions. Thirty European product lines are not automatically heroic, but the retainer price, renewal rate, staff leverage, and 4.25× multiple lack market evidence. The decisive weakness is unproven recurring ACV for an event that buyers may view as internal engineering hygiene.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass, but fragile.** The plan risks no more than 35,000 PLN before paid proof, retains corporate employment, uses mock telemetry and a sandbox, and seeks a 50% deposit on a 30,000–40,000 PLN rehearsal before production integration. It excludes clinical interpretation, reporting decisions, a clinic, patient recruitment, regulator access, and unspecified device partnerships.

The main dependencies are obtaining a senior QA/RA reviewer, demonstrating read-only capture without product or cyber risk, passing security and privacy scrutiny, and persuading a manufacturer to sign an SOP-routing annex inside 180 days. The technical rehearsal is reachable; the paid operational commitment is the weak link.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is specific enough to prospect: small European medical-wearable OEMs with an owned app/cloud stack and limited internal post-market engineering. Expansion within an existing manufacturer’s product portfolio is more credible than cross-OEM scaling because mappings, trust, and contracts can be reused internally.

Competitors can enter from several directions: QMS vendors control complaint workflow, cloud platforms control logs, OEM engineering teams control source systems, and post-market service firms control regulatory trust. A Poland-based team can serve EU OEMs profitably if delivery is remote, mappings reuse materially, and senior regulated expertise remains fractional; local cost alone does not overcome trust or bundling.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The evidence-loss problem is asserted, not demonstrated:** modern connected-device teams may already retain raw telemetry, logs, deployment versions, and audit history adequately.
2. **The buyer may refuse external workflow control:** regulated manufacturers may not insert an unknown part-time provider into a complaint SOP or grant production-export access.
3. **Recurring price exceeds demonstrated value:** a 100,000–150,000 PLN annual retainer is unsupported for smaller OEMs, especially when serious incidents are rare.
4. **Incumbents can bundle the function:** QMS, device-cloud, observability, and post-market providers already hold the relevant systems or relationships.
5. **Service and response intensity may destroy leverage:** each product stack, complaint, and reconstruction can require bespoke engineering, senior QA/RA review, security work, and time-sensitive availability.
6. **Integration may create new regulated and cyber risk:** the read-only snapshot component itself may require validation, change control, security assurance, and product-specific liability allocation.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

For connected medical wearables, the first operational requirement after a potentially reportable complaint is to freeze reconstructable physiological and software evidence before interpretation begins; generic complaint workflow is downstream of that preservation event.
