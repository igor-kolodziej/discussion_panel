# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Cartridge Sentinel

## Diagnosis

Cartridge Sentinel addresses a real safety and cartridge-utilisation problem, and the CIOP-PIB prototype gives it more technical substance than a blank-sheet sensor concept. The commercial thesis is nevertheless much weaker than the candidate implies: ESLI is a long-studied, patented category with a commercial 3M product, an active Honeywell European patent, and many alternative ammonia and cartridge-sensing approaches, so a licence to one pending Polish application is neither broad freedom to operate nor durable EU control. The paid Evaluation Pack is a legitimate proof format, but closing it within 180 days requires an unpriced public-institute option, usable technical transfer, adequate FTO screening, credible conformity advice, prototype reproduction, and a cold OEM purchase to work on an exact 40,000 PLN budget. The founder-wealth arithmetic is coherent, but its threshold-crossing result rests on unsupported OEM volumes, recurring calibration attachment, margins, and valuation; neither hard gate passes as written.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Sourced evidence

- The official [UPRP A1 publication for P.442235](https://api-ewyszukiwarka.pue.uprp.gov.pl/api/bup-or-wup/bup11_2024.pdf) names CIOP-PIB as applicant, reports a 9 September 2022 filing, describes a sensor with gold-coated interdigitated electrodes and a polystyrene/CNT-or-rGO/polyaniline active layer, and states that the application has four claims. This establishes an application and disclosed implementation, not a granted patent, useful claim scope, foreign family, freedom to operate, licence availability, or enforceable EU exclusivity.
- CIOP-PIB reports a prototype, electronics, housing, simulated-use work, technical documentation, and an approximately 1 ppm ammonia alarm response. Those are institute-reported technical results, not independent manufacturing validation, cartridge residual-life accuracy, certification, field reliability, or market evidence.
- [Honeywell EP2866901B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP2866901B1/en) is reported as active, granted in 2020, and expected to run to 2033. Its independent claims are directed to a UV lamp, an in-cartridge fluorescent sensor, and visible-light detection for organic-vapor ESLI. That architecture and analyte class differ from CIOP's resistive ammonia sensor, so this source does **not** establish infringement. It does establish active European ESLI IP and the need for claims-level FTO across the complete sensor/cartridge/reader/alarm system.
- [3M has sold visual ESLI cartridges](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/worker-health-safety-us/all-stories/full-story-detail/?storyid=d172297e-f78d-4b63-b109-b2ce8898c401) for certain organic-vapor environments since 2015. This supports the existence of user pain and at least one commercially viable ESLI configuration, but it contradicts any broad claim that smart cartridge-life indication is commercially empty. It is not evidence that 3M currently serves ammonia or that a smaller European OEM will buy Cartridge Sentinel.
- The 2025 review [Residual Lifetime Indicators for Respiratory Cartridges and Canisters](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/adsr.202400029) catalogs historical and current sensing approaches, many patents, early ammonia indicators, and CNT-based chemiresistor arrays tested for DMMP and ammonia residual lifetime. It says only a few technologies have become commercial products and identifies persistent integration, size, power, readout, calibration, shelf-life, outgassing, response-time, interference, ergonomics, cost, regulation, and human-factor problems. This is adverse evidence: commercial scarcity may reflect structural difficulty rather than an overlooked transfer opportunity.
- Under [Regulation (EU) 2016/425](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0425), protection from substances hazardous to health is Category III PPE. The route requires EU type examination under Module B plus either supervised product checks under Module C2 or production-process quality assurance under Module D. The regulation also includes interchangeable components essential to protective function within PPE. Whether this module is a component, accessory, or modification of an approved type requires specialist and notified-body judgment; calling the OEM the final manufacturer does not remove the programme dependency.

### Candidate hypotheses, not evidence

- CIOP will offer a nine-month field-exclusive option for 12,000 PLN, a licence for no more than 45,000 PLN additional upfront, a 4–6% royalty, technical-package access, inventor support, sublicensing, founder-owned improvements, and useful Poland/CEE/EU rights.
- CIOP has an unencumbered chain of title, usable confidential know-how, no conflicting prior option or project restriction, and enough control over the requested field to grant effective exclusivity.
- A small or mid-sized OEM will pay 24,000–36,000 PLN for an Evaluation Pack, provide a 50% deposit, and do so before certification and production economics are resolved.
- Eighty cold accounts will yield fifteen calls, six offers, and one paid pack inside 180 days; no interview, introduced buyer, quote request, budget confirmation, or paid transaction supports the funnel.
- Production NRE of 120,000–250,000 PLN, a 420 PLN module ASP, 52–56% hardware gross margin, a 96 PLN annual calibration/evidence fee, 71–79% service gross margin, and 15–24% operating margin are attainable.
- OEMs will bear the final conformity programme while continuing to pay the startup sufficient NRE and unit margin. No notified-body scope, test matrix, quotation, duration estimate, change-to-certified-type opinion, insurance quote, or liability allocation is supplied.

### Consequential missing facts

- A current official P.442235 prosecution and legal-status file; the full claims and amendments; search opinion; grant prospects; foreign-family status; inventorship and assignment documents; prior licences, options, project-funding restrictions, and encumbrances.
- A CIOP term sheet or written indication that the proposed option, field, territory, price, technical access, sublicensing, improvement ownership, and milestone structure are available to a new company on the stated timetable.
- A claims chart and jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction FTO screen covering Honeywell, 3M, and other sensor, in-cartridge placement, reader, signalling, calibration, and residual-life methods. A licence to P.442235 gives no immunity from third-party patents.
- At least one qualified OEM interview tied to a live product-refresh or certification event, a competing internal-development estimate, an approved budget, and a willingness to pay the proposed deposit.
- A notified-body or qualified specialist opinion identifying the applicable standards, whether an existing EU type certificate can be extended, expected tests and iterations, responsibility by party, calendar time, and capital required. The 4,000 PLN pre-assessment line is not a certification budget.
- Independent data on selectivity, drift, humidity and temperature compensation, dynamic breathing flow, shelf life, sensor placement versus breakthrough, false-negative risk, manufacturing yield, intrinsic-safety/EMC implications, cleaning, and warranty reserve.
- A named denominator of reachable European ammonia-cartridge OEMs, their annual unit volumes, product-refresh cadence, and current or planned ESLI solutions.
- No prior reviewer score or external verdict is treated as market evidence.

## Overall Score

**39/100.** The weights sum to **20.0**. Weighted points are **78.0**, so the calculation is `10 × (78.0 / 20.0) = 39.0`, rounded to **39**. The clear plan and good kill criteria do not substitute for control, market, regulatory, or transaction evidence.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** The option is proposed, not signed, and all of its commercially important terms are assumptions. If CIOP executed the exact agreement and supplied valuable non-public know-how, that contract could become a Tier 1 right **against CIOP within its defined field**. It would still not create EU patent exclusivity if P.442235 has no foreign family, prevent independent development, provide FTO against third parties, or guarantee that the pending Polish claims grant with useful scope.

The strongest copy risk is bypass rather than literal duplication. A respirator incumbent or OEM can select another visual, optical, electrochemical, colorimetric, CNT, or software-assisted residual-life approach; 3M already has a commercial ESLI platform and Honeywell owns adjacent active European claims. The 2025 review shows multiple candidate sensing routes, including prior ammonia work. An incumbent can combine its certified cartridge architecture, buyer channel, testing capability, and an alternative sensor vendor without licensing CIOP. The candidate's adapter, calibration, and evidence library becomes meaningful only after several programmes launch, leaving the startup vulnerable before compounding begins.

The FTO issue is a veto, not a footnote. Honeywell's cited independent claims appear materially different from CIOP's resistive construction, so no infringement conclusion is justified. But a 5,000 PLN unquoted legal line cannot be assumed to cover a complete multi-family, multi-jurisdiction system analysis, and the candidate supplies no claims chart. Owning or licensing one patent is not freedom to operate.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The candidate includes all three scenarios, OEM counts and volumes, pricing, revenue, gross and operating margins, capital, founder ownership and dilution, liabilities, 95%/95%/100% reinvestment, valuation, distributions, and timing. Its written arithmetic is substantially correct, debt is deducted, and retained earnings are not visibly double-counted.

The credibility test fails:

- The conservative year-eight case produces only 3.79m PLN of founder wealth, below the 5m PLN target.
- The written expected case is **five OEMs, 15,000 new modules per year, and 30,000 active service modules**, producing 9.43m PLN revenue and about 1.70m PLN EBIT in year seven. The 6.26m PLN founder result requires all of the unsupported module price, service attachment, 55% gross margin, 18% operating margin, 88% ownership, and 4× EBIT valuation assumptions to hold together.
- At 3× rather than 4× EBIT, the same approximate expected-case EBIT, debt, ownership, and 500,000 PLN distribution yield only about **4.77m PLN** to the founder. The target is therefore valuation-sensitive even before volume or margin underperformance.
- No OEM unit-volume evidence shows that five customers can produce 15,000 new modules annually. No renewal evidence supports charging 96 PLN across 30,000 active units, and no certification/insurance/support budget demonstrates that 18% operating margin survives safety-critical deployments.
- The requested **six-OEM × 10,000-module** stress case is not the scenario written in the candidate. It would mean 60,000 new modules per year—four times the written expected volume and even above the written strong case's 50,000. Treating that as “expected” would be materially more heroic, not a solution to the gate.
- The strong case reaches 25.66m PLN but assumes twelve OEMs, 50,000 new modules, 100,000 service units, 24% operating margin, 3.5m PLN of capital, and 78% founder ownership without market, certification, or repeat-order evidence.

The failure is not mathematical; it is the absence of a credible bridge from one engineering pack to repeat certified OEM volume and recurring high-margin service. Failure of this gate prohibits `pursue`.

## Reachability Gate

**Fail.** The proof instrument itself is well chosen: a 24,000–36,000 PLN bench and shadow-mode Evaluation Pack with a collected deposit can be sold before a finished certified respirator exists. The founder can nominally allocate 15–19 hours per week while keeping employment, and the file limits pre-proof cash exposure to 40,000 PLN.

The path is not yet credible because it depends on too many uncontrolled results before day 180:

1. CIOP must negotiate and execute a novel field-exclusive option, disclose chain of title and prior rights, provide a useful technical package, accept the hypothesized economics, and do so in roughly 30 days. A general commercialisation contact is not evidence of that cycle or outcome.
2. A lawyer must establish enough patent status and FTO confidence for an OEM-facing offer. The candidate has no quoted scope and allocates only 5,000 PLN.
3. Contractors must reproduce or validate the sensor, obtain safe laboratory access, and produce a credible demonstrator and regulatory memo within the remaining budget. The 3,000 PLN laboratory line and 2,000 PLN combined insurance/travel/contingency leave no demonstrated room for iteration.
4. A new solo founder with no PPE-OEM relationship must turn thirty cold, event-qualified approaches into a 12,000 PLN-or-higher cash deposit before the buyer has a notified-body pathway, production yield, full FTO view, or certified-product economics.

The 40,000 PLN budget is an exact ceiling rather than a conservative cap with slack. Certification does not need to finish before the evaluation sale, but the buyer still needs a believable route. Category III Module B plus C2 or D, and the possibility that the modified cartridge becomes a new or amended approved type, create schedule and capital exposure that the candidate has shifted to the OEM without obtaining an OEM commitment. Outsourcing responsibility does not make this dependency controlled.

The gate can be reassessed only from live rights terms, a bounded claims-level FTO scope, a notified-body pathway with time and cost, and buyer evidence. As written, it is a sequence of plausible activities rather than a credible 180-day paid-proof route. Failure of this gate independently prohibits `pursue`.

## Expansion Check

The ammonia refrigeration and fertiliser beachhead is coherent because ammonia is a high-consequence, specifically tested gas and not identical to 3M's cited organic-vapor product. It may also be too small or fragmented: the candidate supplies no count of qualified European OEMs, annual ammonia-cartridge volume, refresh events, or budgets. A Poland-first technical relationship with CIOP lowers early diligence costs, but the revenue thesis requires European OEM programmes, while the likely patent protection is Polish and pending.

The adjacent expansion sequence is not frictionless. A different gas, sorbent bed, cartridge geometry, mask interface, environmental envelope, or alarm logic can require new sensor recognition chemistry, calibration, residual-life mapping, FTO, human-factors work, and conformity evidence. That resets part of the learning curve and weakens the claim that the startup expands outward more easily than an incumbent moves inward. 3M, Honeywell, and other PPE manufacturers start with certified product families, channel trust, test infrastructure, liability systems, and installed users; Cartridge Sentinel starts with one prospective licence.

The local advantage is access and cost, not protected market control. The candidate has not shown that a Polish deployment creates switching costs or exclusive distribution that broad competitors cannot reproduce.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The proposed control point does not yet exist and may not be enforceable where the revenue is modeled.** P.442235 is evidenced only as a pending Polish application; every important option and licence term is hypothetical, and contractual know-how exclusivity cannot stop independent EU alternatives.
2. **A CIOP licence is not freedom to operate.** Active Honeywell European ESLI claims, 3M's commercial platform, and a dense research/patent landscape require system-level claims analysis. The candidate has no FTO opinion, claims chart, or quoted scope.
3. **Incumbents can bypass the sensor.** The 2025 review documents many sensing families, including prior ammonia and CNT approaches. A large OEM does not need CIOP's exact formulation to offer a competing residual-life indicator.
4. **OEM willingness to pay is absent.** No buyer has confirmed a live programme, evaluation budget, deposit, price, sales cycle, or willingness to assume the certification work. The cold funnel is entirely modeled.
5. **Certification is a concealed time-and-capital dependency.** Category III conformity work, applicable standards, product-type modification, notified-body timing, test iterations, quality surveillance, insurance, and liability allocation have no itemized commercial plan or quote.
6. **The 180-day proof requires too many upstream wins on a no-slack budget.** Rights, know-how transfer, reproduction, FTO, regulatory credibility, lab work, and a first cold OEM payment all precede the gate.
7. **The founder-wealth case is fragile.** The conservative case fails, the expected case falls below 5m PLN at a 3× EBIT multiple, and all threshold-driving volume, service, margin, and valuation inputs lack transaction evidence.
8. **Founder fit is partial.** Data analysis is relevant, but the company is initially a chemistry, PPE-conformity, patent-licensing, hardware-integration, and technical-enterprise-sales business whose essential skills all sit with contractors and counterparties.

These objections veto `pursue`. They do not prove that no ammonia OEM will pay or that CIOP's technology cannot be licensed; they show that the candidate has not earned either hard gate.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is the **paid OEM decision pack as the bridge between dormant institute research and a later certified product**: require the product owner to fund bounded integration, laboratory evidence, and a conformity decision before the startup commits to production tooling or final-PPE liability.
