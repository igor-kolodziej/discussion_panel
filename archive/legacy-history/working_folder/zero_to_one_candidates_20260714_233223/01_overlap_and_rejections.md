## Distilled lessons from 53 failed directions

1. A prototype, CAD file, firmware, future dataset, or manufactured SKU is not a scarce control point when the incumbent already owns the trusted workflow, certification decision, installed base, or distribution.
2. Generic phantoms, reference materials, test fixtures, QA coupons, and replaceable components are easily internalized unless they control a mandatory release decision or scarce recurring supply.
3. Consumer and assistive wearables need a bottom-up channel bridge from the first 100 units to expected annual volume, including VAT, compliance, support, returns, working capital, and channel margin.
4. Reachable first orders do not rescue a weak wealth case; attractive theoretical scale does not rescue an unreachable first sale.
5. Preorders are valid paid proof only when the first deliverable batch remains affordable within staged capital and positive contribution economics.
6. Clinical access, reusable data rights, OEM integration, sponsor acceptance, and partnerships count only when contracted, prepaid, reserved, owned, or otherwise provable during the first cycle.
7. Real-time HCI must own the enforcement point, workflow route, or distribution; a peripheral feature that a platform can bundle is not defensible.
8. Small developer, laboratory, or OEM universes need high recurring annual value and a credible account count; unsupported volume assumptions are terminal.
9. Advisory evaluator enthusiasm is not validation. Independent judges repeatedly exposed incumbent control and distribution failures missed by internal screening.
10. The most promising control archetypes are independently secured exclusive rights, scarce inventory with verified demand, legally or contractually mandatory transaction flow, and protected core physics or data rights acquired through the paid wedge.

## Closed shapes for this run

- Ordinary biosignal/wearable phantoms, reference coupons, artificial-sweat products, calibration tiles, and generic QA fixtures.
- Generic biosignal dashboards, coaching, claim reports, N-of-1 consumer tests, or clinic-capacity businesses.
- Wearable/assistive input features without a controlled channel, protected performance advantage, and affordable compliance path.
- AI approval terminals, recording/consent gates, agent isolation boxes, and other functions already controlled by platform, identity, PAM, AV, or security incumbents.
- Smart-workwear circulation, discontinued-wearable study inventory, and device-rental logistics without grounded installed-base utilization.
- Partnerships, participant recruitment, clinical datasets, derivative rights, or service authorizations that are merely hoped for.

## Re-entry standard

A prior family may re-enter only with a materially different buyer or budget owner, painful event, acquisition channel, economics, and first-sale control point. A threshold change, new name, geography, or feature does not qualify.

## Current search bias

- A paid operational wedge that itself acquires reusable physiological or behavioral data rights.
- Independently ownable inventory, exclusive rights, or transaction flow tied to a costly human-performance event.
- Real-time human-AI systems where the startup controls both the interface and the economic workflow outcome.
- Wearable systems sold through a narrow, reachable channel with high annual value per account rather than unsupported consumer volume.
- Data or hardware assets that retain value even if the initial expansion thesis fails.
