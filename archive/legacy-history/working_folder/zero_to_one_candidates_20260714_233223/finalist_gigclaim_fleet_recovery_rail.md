# Zero-to-One Candidate: GigClaim Fleet Recovery Rail

Candidate generation only — not validated.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build the worker-side system of record for algorithmically managed earnings: start by recovering disputed fares, bonuses, ratings, and account decisions for Polish ride-hail and delivery fleets, then compound a cross-platform claim→explanation→decision→settlement dataset and a real-time human–AI appeal interface.

## Founder Fit

- **Founder fact:** The founder is a Warsaw data scientist with AI/ML, analytics, behavioral-data, neuroscience, and business-analysis experience. The work begins as data normalization, anomaly detection, evidence assembly, and human-facing explanation rather than hardware or regulated clinical delivery.
- **Founder fact:** The first phase fits approximately 25 founder-hours per week alongside corporate employment: founder-led sales, one weekly statement batch per pilot, asynchronous claim review, and contractor-assisted product work.
- **Founder fact:** The founder has no privileged introductions. The acquisition design therefore starts from public fleet-partner pages and an offer paid from recovered or reconciled value.
- **Hypothesis:** Polish, English, Ukrainian, and Russian evidence explanations can become a local trust advantage, initially using contractors for translation and legal review.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The initial buyer is a Polish ride-hail or delivery fleet operator managing roughly 100–1,000 drivers/couriers across one or more platforms.

The buyer wants correct weekly settlement, fewer worker escalations, less owner/dispatcher time spent on screenshots and support tickets, and fewer unexpected suspensions that idle rented vehicles. Today, workers send screenshots through messaging apps; a dispatcher manually compares statements, campaign terms, trip histories, and support responses; small discrepancies are abandoned; similar failures are rediscovered repeatedly.

- **Sourced evidence:** [Bolt's fleet page](https://bolt.eu/en-pl/fleet/) explicitly gives fleet owners worker-level earnings and performance statistics, establishing both the buyer and a relevant recurring data flow.
- **Sourced evidence:** [Directive (EU) 2024/2831](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024L2831) addresses algorithmic decisions affecting earnings, work assignment, ratings, and account access, and provides explanation, human-review, and portability rights.
- **Hypothesis:** A 300-worker fleet loses or spends at least 5,000 PLN per month across recoverable discrepancies, vehicle downtime, and staff handling. The paid pilot must measure this rather than assume it.

## Product Wedge

Sell a 30-day **Fleet Earnings Recovery Audit**:

1. The fleet signs a scoped mandate and data-processing terms; participating workers authorize specified exports and internal-platform appeals.
2. The service ingests weekly statements, trip/campaign evidence, worker-submitted screenshots, and support history through permitted exports or forwarded files—not credential sharing or access-control bypass.
3. Software flags inconsistencies and assembles an evidence pack. A human reviewer approves every claim.
4. The fleet or authorized worker submits through the platform's ordinary support/review channel. The service tracks reason, response, correction, recovery, and time to resolution.
5. The buyer sees recovered cash, prevented settlement errors, unresolved categories, and handling time saved.

The first version is a managed, software-assisted recovery workflow. It does not promise legal representation, litigate employment status, or make unsupported claims about platform wrongdoing. Matters requiring licensed legal practice are outside the product and may be referred by the customer independently.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Platforms optimize the transaction and possess platform-side data. Fleet operators optimize vehicles and worker onboarding. Neither has a strong incentive to create an independent, cross-platform history of small worker-side discrepancies and whether appeals actually reverse them.

The overlooked buyer is the fleet intermediary: it aggregates many workers, already handles settlements and platform support, and experiences repeated low-value failures that are uneconomic one by one but material in a portfolio. The product can be paid from recovered value before it becomes software and can obtain outcome data without a platform partnership.

## Underused Momentum

- **Sourced evidence:** Directive (EU) 2024/2831 must be transposed by 2 December 2026. Article 9 includes portability of personal data generated through work, including ratings and reviews; Articles 10–11 require human oversight, reasons, and review for consequential algorithmic decisions.
- **Sourced evidence:** [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20240718-1) found 3.0% of people aged 15–64 in participating countries had performed paid platform work in the prior 12 months; Poland was included.
- **Sourced evidence:** Bolt publicly recruits and supports fleet owners in Poland and routes drivers without a business/licence toward [recommended fleet partners](https://bolt.eu/en-pl/support/articles/360001424593/), making the first buyer list observable without status-based access.
- **Hypothesis:** Implementation uncertainty before Polish transposition creates demand for practical evidence preparation, but the initial audit must create value under existing support and GDPR routes rather than depend on the future statute.

## Beachhead Market

Polish ride-hail fleet partners with at least 100 active workers, two or more platform relationships, weekly settlements, and an owner/operations lead still handling disputes manually. Prioritize Warsaw, Kraków, Wrocław, Poznań, Łódź, and the Tri-City, where a single buyer can provide a dense claim cohort.

The beachhead is deliberately not individual workers. A fleet consolidates acquisition, authorization, weekly files, payment, and repeated outcomes. Delivery fleets are an expansion after the trip/bonus/suspension ontology works for ride-hail.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- **Pilot hypothesis:** 5,000 PLN prepaid audit deposit for up to 50 workers and 30 days, credited against a 20% cash-recovery fee; the deposit is non-refundable once analysis starts.
- **Recurring hypothesis:** 20 PLN per covered worker per month plus 20% of cash actually recovered. No fee is charged on unsupported or unresolved amounts.
- **Expected mature 600-worker fleet:** 144,000 PLN annual base fee plus 60,000 PLN success fees if 300,000 PLN is recovered, for 204,000 PLN annual revenue.
- **Expected variable cost hypothesis:** Approximately 45,000 PLN per 600-worker account for review labor, support, payment fees, storage, and language handling, producing about 159,000 PLN contribution and a 78% contribution margin before central product, sales, security, and legal expense.
- **Proof condition:** One paid audit must create at least twice its fee in recovered cash plus conservatively valued handling-time savings; otherwise the price or target segment is wrong.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

### First 10

1. Build a list of 100 Polish fleet operators from platform-recommended partner pages, fleet websites, business registries, and public recruitment advertising.
2. Send a concise, individualized earnings-reconciliation offer to the owner/operations head: a five-day redacted-sample review followed by a 5,000 PLN prepaid 30-day audit.
3. Ask only for redacted samples during qualification. Full data arrive only after mandate, worker authorization, and data-processing terms.
4. Target one paid audit from the first 40 well-fitted accounts and three independent paying fleets by day 180.

### First 100

Use documented pilot recovery categories, accountant/fleet-operations referrals, multilingual onboarding material, and an annual benchmark report that contains only aggregated non-identifying patterns. Expand from Polish ride-hail fleets to delivery fleets and then Czech, Slovak, and Romanian fleet intermediaries. Do not assume a platform referral channel.

## Primary Control Point

The primary control point is a **contracted all-dispute routing rail**:

- the fleet routes covered earnings and account disputes through the system for the contract term;
- each worker/fleet supplies explicit, revocable authorization for defined data and internal appeal activity;
- the contract grants the startup rights to retain pseudonymized factual derivatives—claim type, evidence completeness, stated reason, decision, correction, recovery, and timing—while raw personal data remain purpose-limited and are deleted on schedule;
- the system records closed-loop outcomes rather than merely generating appeal text.

This is Tier 2 at inception: contractually routed workflow with compounding data and switching costs, but no statutory exclusivity. It can approach Tier 1 only if recurring fleet mandates and unique cross-platform outcomes become difficult to recreate.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

**Foundational resource needed:** Authorization and rights to a recurring flow of claim, decision, and settlement outcomes across multiple platforms.

**How it is secured:** A paying fleet signs the routing mandate and data-processing agreement; each participating worker uses a narrow authorization/consent flow appropriate to the legal basis established by counsel. The startup does not require a platform API or partnership. It uses customer-held statements, formal data exports, emails, and user-provided evidence.

**Why an unknown founder can secure it:** The offer is outcome-linked, starts with a small paid audit, reduces existing manual work, and asks the buyer to route a process it already performs. It does not ask the buyer to endorse medical accuracy, replace a safety system, or make an enterprise-wide platform migration.

**Proof within 12 months and 100,000 PLN:** Three independent paying fleets; at least 500 opted-in covered workers; at least 100 closed claim/reconciliation outcomes; two platforms represented; signed derivative-data rights; documented recovery and resolution-time baselines. Total original founder exposure remains capped at 100,000 PLN.

## Distribution Advantage

The buyer universe is observable through public fleet pages and recruitment activity. One fleet aggregates hundreds of end users, so acquisition cost is paid once per organization rather than per worker. Recovery reports create a referral artifact for fleet accountants and operations managers. The business does not rely on influencers, unions, a platform partnership, or a celebrity founder.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. **Polish fleet reconciliation:** weekly settlement and campaign discrepancy recovery.
2. **Account-decision evidence:** standardized reason, evidence, human-review, and outcome tracking.
3. **Cross-platform worker ledger:** portable history of earnings, ratings, and resolved disputes under explicit authorization.
4. **Fleet risk and forecasting:** predict which claim patterns are likely recoverable and where vehicle/worker downtime is rising.
5. **Worker-side HCI layer:** real-time multilingual explanations and evidence prompts before a support submission.
6. **CEE expansion:** reuse the data model while adapting labor, privacy, and tax handling locally.

Broad legal-claims management is not the expansion thesis. The compounding advantage is operational outcome intelligence in a repeated, economically measurable human–algorithm interface.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland has a visible fleet-intermediary structure, multilingual worker populations, local weekly settlement practices, and imminent implementation of an EU directive. The product begins with Polish file formats, payout terminology, support routes, language needs, and fleet accounting reality. Those adaptations create speed and trust but are not a legal barrier; defense must come from routed outcomes and switching costs before large platforms or horizontal worker-tech vendors adapt.

## Compounding Advantage

Every paid case improves:

- the statement and campaign parsers;
- the claim taxonomy and evidence-completeness model;
- expected recovery and response-time estimates;
- multilingual explanation and worker evidence prompts;
- the benchmark of stated algorithmic reason versus human-review outcome;
- the fleet's historical ledger, which becomes costly to abandon.

The future lab gains advanced AI/ML capability, high-frequency human behavioral/economic data, real-time human–computer interface experience, proprietary evaluation datasets, and distribution to algorithmically managed worker cohorts.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

- Conservative and expected scenarios reinvest 95% of after-tax cash otherwise available for distribution while the founder remains employed. Optional distributions are capped at 5%; the model uses zero.
- Strong success reinvests 100% during the compounding period.
- Reinvestment funds statement adapters, security and privacy controls, multilingual review, a fleet sales lead after paid repeatability, and automated evidence quality—not founder lifestyle.
- Retained cash is not added to the EBIT-based equity value. Any debt outstanding at valuation is deducted before founder ownership is applied.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder leaves corporate employment only when all are true:

- at least five independent paying fleets or contracted recurring demand covering at least 2,000 workers;
- positive contribution economics across three paid deliveries;
- at least one renewal or six months of contracted recurring fees;
- a documented outbound or referral channel with credible acquisition economics;
- privacy/security operations demonstrably manageable;
- at least 12 months of personal runway after taxes and liabilities.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If percentage recovery is weak but reconciliation saves time, the workflow can narrow to fleet payout reconciliation. If fleets reject reusable derivatives, parsers and secure evidence assembly remain sellable but the compounding thesis fails and the idea should not be scaled as proposed.

Reusable future-lab assets survive: multilingual human–AI explanation patterns, algorithmic-decision evidence ontology, authorized data-portability tooling, anomaly-detection models, secure behavioral-data pipelines, and a benchmark of evidence completeness versus human-review outcomes.

## Critical Hypotheses

1. Fifty reachable CEE fleet firms average approximately 600 covered workers at expected scale.
2. Average annual recoverable or preventable value reaches 500 PLN per covered worker.
3. Fleets accept 20 PLN per worker per month plus a 20% success fee without passing unacceptable cost to vulnerable workers.
4. At least 70% of relevant workers authorize the defined data and internal appeal workflow.
5. Cross-platform outcome rights are contractually and ethically usable for model training after minimization and pseudonymization.
6. Platforms provide sufficiently structured reasons and review routes for a recovery workflow to create repeated cash value.

## Kill Criteria

- No paid 5,000 PLN audit by day 120 after 80 qualified target approaches.
- Fewer than two independent paying fleets by day 180.
- Counsel finds the wedge requires licensed representation or an unlawful data basis that cannot be removed without destroying value.
- Fewer than 50% of invited workers authorize the necessary data flow.
- Pilot recovered cash plus conservatively valued handling-time savings is less than twice the pilot fee.
- Fewer than 20 closed outcomes or no repeated claim category after 500 worker-months.
- Buyers refuse both routing and reusable pseudonymized outcome rights; this kills the control point even if consulting revenue exists.
- A platform/fleet-system incumbent offers equivalent cross-platform recovery and historical outcomes before three recurring contracts are signed.
