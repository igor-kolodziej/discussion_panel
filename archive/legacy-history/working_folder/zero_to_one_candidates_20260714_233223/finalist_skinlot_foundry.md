# Zero-to-One Candidate: SkinLot Foundry

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build the CEE low-volume, named-lot supply route for skin-contact wearable materials by owning qualified rolls, customer-specific cutting tools, retained samples, and prepaid replenishment stock that lets biosensor and medical-wearable teams move from wear study to pilot production without changing an already selected adhesive construction.

## Founder Fit

The founder's neuroscience, biosignal, data-science, and business-analysis background fits the buyer's actual system problem: a biosignal device is only useful when its electrode or sensor remains attached, comfortable, electrically stable, and traceable for the intended wear period. The founder can understand ECG, EMG, EEG, PPG, temperature, hydration, and other wearable use cases well enough to structure material comparisons and identify decision-relevant failure modes without pretending to be a polymer chemist or medical-device certifier.

The initial wedge is compatible with corporate employment. It uses sample procurement, controlled inventory, simple peel/shear and environmental screening, data capture, customer-specific drawings, and outsourced cutting rather than a founder-operated factory. AI and automation can support material-document extraction, lot records, quotations, traceability, and outbound account research.

The minimum missing capabilities are medical-material conversion, quality management, and regulatory contracting. Before any patient-facing commercial component is supplied, cutting and packaging must be performed by a competent contractor under an agreed specification, and the final-device manufacturer must retain responsibility for biological evaluation, risk management, validation, and regulatory release.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The beachhead customer is a small or mid-sized European team developing a skin-worn diagnostic, monitoring, rehabilitation, safety, or human-performance device. The initial buyer is the product, R&D, operations, or quality leader responsible for a patch, electrode, or sensor assembly approaching a wear study, design freeze, verification build, clinical investigation, or first pilot-production batch.

Its inside view is:

> "We finally found a construction that adheres for the required period without unacceptable irritation or edge lift. Now the supplier will only quote a large roll, the converter wants a production-scale minimum, or the exact material is not available when our study build starts. If we substitute, we may have to repeat wear, compatibility, and biological-risk work. We need the same documented material, converted to our drawing, from the same traceable lot, with enough reserved for the next build."

Painful events include:

- a scheduled wear study or verification build blocked by material lead time or minimum order quantity;
- a selected adhesive, backing, liner, or tie layer becoming unavailable in the quantity needed for 50–5,000 units;
- a converter declining prototype or bridge volumes;
- a notified-body, quality, or design-review question about material identity, lot history, storage, or substitution;
- pilot production consuming the last available stock of the exact qualified construction;
- an urgent need to bridge from prototype cutting to a larger converter without changing the bill of materials.

Current workarounds are to buy hand-cut samples, use consumer or general-purpose tape during development, accept a large minimum order, ask a contract manufacturer to source an approximate substitute, or restart evaluation with a different construction. These workarounds trade working capital, schedule, and evidence continuity against material access.

## Product Wedge

The first paid product is a **Named-Lot Wearable Material Reservation Pack** for one device geometry and one planned build. It includes:

- a short list of manufacturer-documented candidate constructions matched to intended wear duration, substrate, moisture, sterilization, and skin-contact constraints;
- manufacturer technical information and available biocompatibility documentation, clearly separated from any final-device claim;
- small converted samples for customer-controlled bench and wear evaluation;
- one customer-selected material construction purchased by exact supplier product code;
- customer-specific die, digital cutting file, or converting drawing;
- a prepaid, segregated quantity from a recorded production lot;
- retained reference samples from that lot;
- certificate-of-conformance, storage-condition, expiry or retest-date, and chain-of-custody records when supplied by the manufacturer;
- a signed call-off schedule for the first build and a stated substitution prohibition without customer approval;
- a replenishment decision date before reserved stock falls below the agreed floor.

The first pack is for R&D, evaluation, or an explicitly controlled pilot build. SkinLot does not declare that a material or converted component is safe, clinically effective, sterile, or suitable for the customer's final device. The customer owns final-device biological evaluation, risk management, validation, release, and intended-use claims.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

**Evidence:** Major manufacturers sell broad portfolios because skin adhesion is application-specific. Solventum describes the selection problem as challenging and offers numerous adhesive, backing, and liner choices for medical wearables. Avery Dennison separates short-term, long-term, tie-layer, and overlay materials and emphasizes intended wear, body location, activity, moisture, and user characteristics. Lohmann offers a different long-wear construction for 28-day applications.

**Hypothesis:** The underserved commercial unit is not "medical tape by the metre." It is a small, traceable, customer-specific bridge between material selection and repeatable production: the exact documented construction, exact geometry, exact lot, retained evidence, and enough reserved replenishment to avoid a design change during a costly program event.

Large material manufacturers are optimized for material innovation and scaled supply. Production converters are optimized for qualified manufacturing runs. Very small product teams can fall between free samples and efficient production minimums. SkinLot must prove that this gap is frequent and expensive enough for customers to pay a reservation and continuity premium.

The candidate is not a generic reseller if the paid contract gives SkinLot physical custody of named-lot stock, customer-specific conversion tooling, a non-substitution rule, retained samples, and a recurring call-off route. It becomes generic resale if customers will buy the same small quantity directly or if an incumbent converter provides the same service without meaningful delay or minimum-order friction.

## Underused Momentum

- Medical and performance wearables increasingly require multi-day or multi-week skin attachment, creating more demanding adhesion, breathability, moisture, comfort, and substrate requirements.
- Solventum now markets extended-wear constructions such as Medical Tape 4578 and 4579 for several-week wear and wearable overlays.
- Lohmann markets DuploMED 85300 for 28-day-and-beyond wear, while Avery Dennison publishes distinct wearable material families and wear-study resources.
- ISO 10993-1:2025 places biological safety inside a lifecycle risk-management process and increases the value of controlled material identity and change records, even though component documentation does not replace final-device evaluation.
- Poland has medtech, diagnostics, life-science, university, textile, electronics, and applied-research clusters but is not a major headquarters location for the largest global medical-tape manufacturers. A local small-batch and traceability route may therefore reduce iteration and logistics friction.
- Contract digital cutting and die-cutting make it possible to prove demand without buying a production converting line.

These facts show enabling supply and regulatory momentum. They do not prove the proposed low-volume gap, customer willingness to prepay inventory, or the attainable spread between roll cost and converted-component price.

## Beachhead Market

Start with Poland-based or nearby CEE wearable developers that meet all of the following:

- skin-worn device or electrode with an intended wear time from several hours to 28 days;
- 50–5,000 units needed for a wear study, verification build, clinical investigation, field pilot, or early commercial bridge;
- no internal converting line and no economical production-scale converter route for the current volume;
- a named project event within six months;
- authority to pay for R&D materials or reserve pilot-production inventory;
- willingness to keep final-device validation and regulatory responsibility.

Initial use cases include reusable biosensor pods with disposable adhesive rings, ECG or EMG patches, rehabilitation and neuromodulation wearables, temperature or hydration monitors, worker-safety sensors, smart-textile skin interfaces, and research-grade physiological patches.

Exclude commodity wound dressings, finished consumer overlay patches, high-volume CGM production, sterile finished devices, pharmaceutical delivery systems, and any project that expects SkinLot to make an unverified biocompatibility or clinical-performance claim.

The target mature blended gross margin is 38–48%. It combines lower-margin material/conversion revenue with higher-margin continuity, expedited call-off, and documentation revenue. The business must not depend on a 60% margin on raw material resale.

Working-capital discipline:

- customer deposits or non-cancellable reservation fees finance customer-specific stock;
- the first roll is not purchased until the customer selects the exact construction in writing;
- stock beyond the prepaid floor requires at least two customers using the same standard construction;
- customer-specific converted inventory cannot exceed the customer's prepaid or contractually committed amount;
- expiry, shelf-life, storage, and obsolescence exposure are priced into the reservation fee.

Target mature CAC is 8,000–25,000 PLN per new design-in account, recovered through the sample/reservation pack and first pilot batch. A program should reach positive cumulative contribution before speculative replenishment.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10:

1. Use public medtech directories, grant databases, university spinout lists, conference exhibitor lists, patent publications, and product pages to identify 150 Polish and nearby CEE teams developing skin-worn devices.
2. Filter for a visible prototype, trial, grant milestone, certification step, or pilot-production event rather than sending sector-generic outreach.
3. Build one public, non-customer demonstration comparing die-cut geometry and retained-lot traceability across representative short-, medium-, and extended-wear constructions using non-clinical fixtures.
4. Offer a fixed 8,000–15,000 PLN comparison pack and an optional prepaid named-lot reservation. Do not offer free custom sourcing.
5. Acquire the first customers directly through technical email and small sample mailers; no supplier partnership or converter referral is necessary.
6. Expand a successful sample pack into the customer's pilot batch and annual continuity contract.

Customers 11–100:

- publish anonymized lead-time, edge-lift, substrate-compatibility, conversion-yield, and lot-change lessons that do not reveal customer designs;
- reuse standard material families while keeping customer geometries segregated;
- add CEE, Baltic, Nordic, and DACH developers that need sub-production-scale quantities;
- use ISO 13485 design houses, wearable engineering firms, and specialist converters as non-exclusive channels only after direct contribution economics are proven;
- seek manufacturer or converter referrals only after SkinLot can show it serves volumes they do not want to handle;
- add recurring constructions such as conductive hydrogel laminates, electrode backings, microfluidic spacer tapes, tie layers, liners, and protective overlays only when a paying account requests them.

The expected scenario requires roughly five to seven new durable design-ins per year for seven years, not consumer-unit acquisition.

## Primary Control Point

The first-sale control point is the combination of a **paid contractual reservation and physically segregated named-lot inventory**. Before SkinLot recognizes a reservation sale, the evidence bundle must contain:

1. a signed customer selection identifying manufacturer, product code, construction, and approved geometry;
2. a deposit or non-cancellable reservation fee;
3. a supplier or authorized-channel order confirmation for the exact construction;
4. a recorded supplier lot or batch identifier upon receipt;
5. founder-owned stock physically held in a controlled location or segregated at a contracted converter or warehouse;
6. a customer-specific die, digital cutting file, or controlled drawing owned by SkinLot or licensed for SkinLot's delivery;
7. retained samples from the received lot;
8. a signed non-substitution rule and call-off schedule;
9. a replenishment floor and customer decision date;
10. a record of storage conditions, quantity movements, conversion yield, and remaining reserved balance.

The control point is independently purchasable and provable; it does not require exclusivity from Solventum, Avery Dennison, Lohmann, or another manufacturer. A supplier relationship becomes helpful later but is not assumed.

Within 12 months and no more than 100,000 PLN of founder capital, the target control proof is:

- three independent paying wearable programs;
- at least five physically held named lots across three material constructions;
- three customer-specific tools or controlled geometries;
- at least two prepaid repeat call-offs;
- one replenishment order funded by customer cash;
- complete quantity, storage, retained-sample, and chain-of-custody records;
- no material substitution without written customer approval.

The control point fails if the material is freely available in the exact required converted geometry at equivalent small quantities and lead time, or if the customer refuses to prepay or contractually route repeat supply through SkinLot.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

The initial scarce resource is not a factory. It is a carefully limited set of authentic, documented, named-lot materials in customer-relevant geometry with prepaid demand.

Bootstrap sequence:

1. Request manufacturer samples and technical information for 12–15 short-, medium-, sensitive-skin, and extended-wear constructions.
2. Build a structured material library covering backing, adhesive chemistry, thickness, wear duration, moisture behavior, sterilization compatibility, substrate compatibility, storage, and available biological-safety evidence.
3. Qualify two Polish or nearby EU digital-cutting/die-cutting contractors for R&D samples under confidentiality and controlled drawings. Do not promise certified production before contractor capability is documented.
4. Buy only simple bench fixtures: thickness measurement, peel/shear fixtures, temperature/humidity logging, imaging, and packaging/storage supplies.
5. Create representative non-clinical sample geometries and a traceability demonstration.
6. Sell a paid comparison pack.
7. After written customer selection and deposit, purchase the exact roll or slit-roll quantity, create the customer tool, segregate stock, and deliver the first converted batch.
8. Use the first customer's contribution to buy only the next shared material construction or fund the first replenishment.

The founder must not perform human wear testing without an appropriate protocol, consent, insurance, and ethical/privacy review. The initial business sells material access and controlled conversion, not clinical research.

## Distribution Advantage

The acquisition mechanism is event-driven technical outbound rather than broad medtech networking. Public product announcements, grants, clinical-investigation registrations, design awards, technical hiring, patents, university commercialization pages, and crowdfunding or preorder pages can reveal a skin-worn product approaching a material decision.

The offer is concrete enough for low-social-energy sales:

- exact intended build quantity;
- intended wear period and body location;
- current substrate and adhesive problem;
- deadline and current minimum-order or lead-time blocker;
- paid sample/reservation package;
- explicit regulatory boundaries.

Each successful design-in may create repeat call-offs without reacquiring the customer. Shared standard constructions improve inventory turns across programs, while customer-specific geometry and records increase switching cost.

Poland supplies a practical first market because the founder can ship samples domestically, visit a contractor or customer when necessary, contract in Polish, and reach Warsaw, Łódź, Poznań, Wrocław, Kraków, Katowice, Gdańsk, and nearby CEE markets without building a high-cost Western European commercial team.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Direct competitors and substitutes include:

- Solventum, Avery Dennison Medical, and Lohmann application teams and sample programs;
- established medical-tape converters and ISO 13485 contract manufacturers;
- general precision die cutters willing to source medical materials;
- the customer's own contract manufacturer or engineering team;
- online sellers of finished overlay patches and commodity tapes.

SkinLot cannot win on adhesive invention, regulatory authority, or production scale. It must win on the narrow combination of:

- small customer-relevant quantities;
- multi-manufacturer choice;
- exact-lot physical reservation;
- controlled customer geometry;
- retained samples and traceability;
- rapid call-off and non-substitution;
- continuity from evaluation through pilot production.

Expansion path:

1. Paid comparison and named-lot reservation for one adhesive construction.
2. Repeat pilot batches and annual continuity fee.
3. Full multi-layer patch stack: skin-contact layer, tie layer, backing, overlay, liner, spacer, and conductive interface.
4. Shared inventory across multiple customers using the same standard construction.
5. Contracted regional stockholding or referral status with manufacturers that do not want prototype volumes.
6. ISO 13485-controlled outsourced or owned conversion after sufficient recurring throughput.
7. CEE/DACH bridge production for conductive electrodes, microfluidic patches, smart-textile interfaces, and disposable wearable consumables.

The business must remain material- and geometry-specific. A generic "wearable prototyping shop" would lose the control point and become project consulting.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

The local advantage is operational rather than statutory:

- fast small-batch shipment within Poland and CEE;
- local-language contracting and technical support;
- lower-cost inventory handling and conversion than a Western European specialist office;
- access to Polish converting, textile, electronics, medtech, and applied-research capabilities;
- EU-based custody and documentation;
- the ability to bridge Eastern European prototype volumes to larger Western European production converters.

PAIH's 2025 life-science report describes a broad Polish ecosystem of universities, research institutes, medtech, biotechnology, and life-science employment, including major clusters in Warsaw, Kraków, Poznań, Wrocław, Katowice, and the Tricity. This supports account discovery, but it does not quantify the number of skin-worn-device programs or prove a 45-account recurring market.

There is no tariff, patent, or regulatory moat. A large converter can enter. Defense must come from design-in history, physical stock already reserved for customers, recurring call-off contracts, shared inventory turns, and a cross-manufacturer knowledge base that a single-brand sales team will not prioritize.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid program can add:

- a reusable material-property and document record;
- conversion-yield and edge-quality data;
- substrate and geometry compatibility knowledge;
- lead-time and minimum-order history;
- storage and shelf-life observations;
- a customer-specific tool and reorder route;
- a recurring continuity fee;
- purchasing volume that improves supplier terms;
- shared demand for a standard construction.

Customer designs and confidential results remain segregated. SkinLot retains only generic material records, non-customer-specific methods, supplier and converter performance history, and anonymized operational failure patterns as contractually permitted.

The strategic future-lab asset is a physical and digital library of human-skin interface materials plus access to device teams that are building the next generation of biosignal, neurotechnology, rehabilitation, safety, and human-augmentation wearables. Even if SkinLot never becomes a large converter, that library and distribution can support future sensor, electrode, smart-textile, and wearable-device development.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

In the conservative and expected scenarios, 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution is reinvested while the founder remains employed. Optional employment-period distributions are capped at 5%. The strong scenario reinvests 100% and takes no employment-period distributions.

Reinvestment order:

1. customer-funded named-lot stock and safe storage;
2. additional shared constructions only after two independent demand signals;
3. quality system, traceability, insurance, and contractor qualification;
4. tooling and conversion automation that demonstrably improves yield or lead time;
5. technical sales and additional account coverage;
6. owned converting equipment only after recurring utilization supports it.

Customer deposits are liabilities until the reserved goods and call-off obligations are fulfilled. Cash allocated to customer-owned or customer-reserved stock is not treated as distributable profit. Retained earnings already reflected in business value are not added again to founder net worth.

After the full-time transition, distributions are allowed only after funding customer stock obligations, taxes, debt service, insurance, product-liability reserves, and at least 12 months of company operating runway.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder remains employed until all are true:

- at least eight independent paying device programs across at least five customers;
- at least three prepaid repeat call-offs;
- positive contribution margin on five delivered batches;
- blended recurring gross margin of at least 35%;
- no single customer represents more than 30% of contracted next-12-month gross profit;
- at least 50% of next-12-month direct material purchases are covered by deposits or binding call-offs;
- documented contractor quality, traceability, complaint, recall-support, and non-conformance processes are operating;
- product and professional liability insurance is in place;
- repeatable acquisition extends beyond personal contacts;
- personal and company runway each cover at least 12 months.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If customers will not pay for reserved stock, SkinLot can stop before production investment. Reusable assets include:

- a structured medical-wearable material and supplier library;
- conversion drawings, fixture designs, and yield methods;
- relationships with precision converters and device developers;
- experience in adhesive, substrate, liner, and wearable-stack selection boundaries;
- traceability and inventory software;
- a physical materials library for future biosignal and human-interface R&D.

Possible bounded pivots include a procurement tool licensed to established converters, a manufacturer-funded CEE sample service, or a narrower shared-stock program for one proven high-demand construction. Customer-confidential geometry and stock are returned, transferred, or destroyed according to contract.
