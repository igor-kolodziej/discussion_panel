## Complete Verdict

1. **Score: 56/100**

2. **Direct rationale:** The insight is commercially interesting: fleets aggregate fragmented, low-value claims, and a manual prepaid audit can test the problem without an API or platform partnership. The proposal is unusually disciplined about capital, legal boundaries, kill criteria, and part-time execution. But it is more precise than proven: official sources confirm that fleets receive worker-level earnings/reporting data and that the EU directive creates algorithmic-management rights, but they do not establish the loss pool, correction rate, fleet willingness to pay, or scalable margins ([Bolt fleet portal](https://bolt.eu/pl-pl/support/articles/360012344613/), [Directive 2024/2831](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024L2831)). The central economics and wealth case therefore rest on several correlated, optimistic hypotheses.

3. **Strongest objections/caveats:**

   - **Payer-beneficiary mismatch:** much recovered cash likely belongs economically to workers, while the fleet pays. The mature example charges 204,000 PLN annually against 300,000 PLN recovered—68% of recovered cash before valuing handling savings. That is a difficult value proposition unless fleet-owned savings or retention value are independently large.
   - **Loss incidence is unproven:** 500 PLN recovered per covered worker annually, repeated claim categories, and reliable platform correction are foundational assumptions, not evidence.
   - **Pilot arithmetic is strained:** scaling the mature recovery assumption to 50 workers for one month yields only about 2,083 PLN recovered. A 5,000 PLN pilot requiring 10,000 PLN of total value therefore needs unusually large backlog recovery or handling-time savings.
   - **Distribution is merely accessible, not repeatable:** a public fleet list supports outreach but does not overcome trust, security review, buyer conflict, or cold-sales friction for sensitive worker earnings data.
   - **Margin risk is understated:** 45,000 PLN annual variable cost for 600 workers may not cover multilingual human review, exception handling, authorization administration, security, customer support, and unresolved platform cases.
   - **Data-rights risk:** pseudonymized claim derivatives can remain personal data; worker authorizations are revocable, and fleets may refuse reusable derivative rights. The directive also contains scope qualifications, including an Article 11 exclusion for persons who are also P2B “business users.”
   - **Expected-success scenario is closer to strong success:** it simultaneously assumes 50 fleets averaging 600 covered workers, 500 PLN recovered per worker annually, 78% gross margin, 32% operating margin, 80% founder ownership, and no material external capital.

4. **Control point: Tier 2, conditional.** A signed recurring all-dispute routing mandate plus derivative-data rights would create genuine workflow access and some switching cost. It is not Tier 1 because worker authorization is revocable, the customer can terminate, no platform is bound, no statutory exclusivity exists, and the outcome dataset is valuable only after substantial accumulation. Before the first recurring mandate, it remains an unproven proposed control point.

5. **Single strongest copy risk:** An installed fleet/payroll-management vendor adds multi-platform reconciliation and dispute tracking using its existing customer access and source data. It could reach the same fleets faster, while GigClaim’s historical-outcome advantage would still be too sparse to matter.

6. **Founder Wealth Gate: FAIL.** The conservative case produces only 331,000 PLN of founder equity. The expected case clears 5m PLN only by stacking unverified penetration, account size, recovery incidence, margins, ownership, and capital-efficiency assumptions. The valuation multiple is not the problem; the operating case supporting the profit is not credible enough to count as expected success.

7. **Reachability Gate: PASS, narrowly.** A manual 5,000 PLN paid audit can plausibly be sold within 180 days while employed, with 18,000 PLN pre-proof risk and no full product, API, platform partnership, litigation, or hardware. The legal review, secure transfer, fleet trust, and worker authorization make the sale difficult, but they are sequentially testable within the stated 40,000 PLN ceiling. Three independent fleets in 180 days is much less credible than one paid proof.

8. **Fatal objection: YES—unresolved payer-beneficiary mismatch.** As written, the proposal has not established why a fleet will pay a recurring worker fee plus a recovery percentage when much of the recovered money belongs to workers and the total modeled fee consumes most of the recovery. Unless direct fleet-owned cash savings, labor savings, reduced downtime, or retention value independently justify the price, the recurring model is structurally unsellable.
