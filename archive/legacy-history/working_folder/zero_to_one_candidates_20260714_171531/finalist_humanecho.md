# Zero-to-One Candidate: HumanEcho

## One-Sentence Thesis

Sell warehouse AMR teams a compact active human surrogate with calibrated optical, thermal, radar, and geometry signatures plus replaceable randomized PPE/occlusion skins for repeatable human-detection and stopping regression.

## Founder Fit

The founder's ML/evaluation background fits multi-sensor experiment design and regression analytics. Mechanical, textile/coating, thermal-control, EMC, and calibration work is contracted; no factory access or privileged robotics relationship is assumed.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

AMR OEMs and integrators must verify human detection across releases and customer sites. They use foam obstacles, staff walk-throughs, open standard objects, or expensive automotive targets. These are hard to repeat across crouched/fallen workers, reflective/black PPE, occlusion, temperature, and aisle constraints.

## Product Wedge

A portable lower-cost active core reproduces measured human-relevant signatures and motion while serialized replaceable skins vary PPE, reflectance, occlusion, posture, and thermal contrast. Software records test state and stopping/detection outcomes. It supplies regression evidence, not statutory certification.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Automotive pedestrian targets are overbuilt/expensive for indoor warehouses, while open obstacles are cheap but provide weak multimodal repeatability. AMR teams may pay for a warehouse-native middle layer that catches perception regressions before site acceptance.

## Underused Momentum

ISO 3691-4 covers driverless industrial trucks, NIST researches human detection and perception performance, and warehouses are adding AMRs near workers. Open methods create demand context but cannot be the proprietary control point.

## Beachhead Market

European AMR OEMs and smaller integrators shipping mixed lidar/RGB-D/thermal perception into warehouses, initially those changing sensors or perception software before a customer acceptance test.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Paid feasibility/preorder: 25–40k PLN.
- Active system: 70–120k PLN, expected 80k with 25k BOM and about 62–69% gross margin.
- Randomized skin/calibration packs: 12–25k PLN, expected 15k with 4k direct cost.
- Expected blended gross margin 65%; mature operating margin 27% after hardware support, calibration, sales, R&D, and insurance.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

Build 100 named accounts from AMR association/vendor lists, ISO ecosystem suppliers, public installations, and perception job/paper authors. Offer a recorded comparison and mailed passive skin. First ten are direct engineering sales; first 100 use safety/perception distributors and recurring skin/calibration sales, but the first order needs no partner.

## Primary Control Point

Owned active-core design, multimodal signature calibration, skin formulations/geometries, serialized randomized inventory, and regression software/history. The first paid feasibility uses an owned physical surrogate and skin.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Spend up to 25k PLN on passive geometries, thermal insert, four PPE/occlusion skins, and measurements. Require a 25–40k PLN paid active-core feasibility before completing the system. Under 100k PLN by month 12: two customers, four skin SKUs, and calibration history.

## Distribution Advantage

The technical account list is finite and reachable through public product/install information. Mailed skins and recorded sensor comparisons reduce status-based trust. Every installed core creates recurring proprietary skin/calibration distribution.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with AMR human detection; expand to cobots, industrial vehicles, security robots, and wearable-robot coexistence. Warehouse-native size/price, calibrated multimodal signatures, and rapidly refreshed skins must let the company expand faster than automotive target firms move down-market.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland provides low-cost fabrication and a dense EU logistics/manufacturing market. ISO relevance is international. The defense is warehouse specialization and economics, not geography.

## Compounding Advantage

Installed cores pull recurring skins, calibrations, failure signatures, and regression software. Cross-sensor outcome histories improve skin design and establish a physical-AI human-perception platform.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash in conservative/expected cases while employed; optional distributions stay ≤5%. Strong success reinvests 100%. Milestones: passive signatures month 3; paid feasibility month 6; two customers month 12; ten systems/year 2; 40 accounts years 4–5; expected scale year 8.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Build passive skins and a recorded repeatability demo in 12 weeks, target 100 AMR engineering accounts, and request a 25–40k PLN paid active-core feasibility/preorder. Pre-proof exposure is 25–35k PLN, and the complete system is not built before payment.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

1. Up to 35k PLN pre-proof for skins, geometry, sensors, and comparison demo.
2. Complete active core only against paid feasibility and within 40k cumulative original exposure net of customer payment.
3. Up to 100k PLN only after successful delivery and second independent paid buyer/call-off.
4. Family capital excluded. Maximum pre-proof loss: 35k PLN.

## Critical Dependency Map

- Multimodal signature repeatability: testable in-house/contract lab, existential.
- Buyer accepts non-certification regression aid: paid feasibility tests this.
- Automotive incumbents: price/specialization defense required.
- Fabrication/calibration: outsourced from drawings/protocol; no exclusive partner assumed.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

At least three independent customers or recurring skin/calibration contracts, positive contribution after support, repeatable technical acquisition, and 12 months personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Retain surrogate mechanics, skin material lots, thermal/optical/radar measurements, regression software, and AMR account map. Do not fall back to custom safety testing or open task boards.

## Critical Evidence

ISO 3691-4 addresses driverless-truck safety/verification; NIST studies human detection and standard human-like obstacles; open arenas show reproducibility and substitution risk.

## Critical Hypotheses

Warehouse-specific skins alter release decisions; 120 global accounts are attainable; a passive comparison can earn a paid preorder; recurring skins attach to installed cores.

## Kill Criteria

- No paid feasibility/deposit after 100 qualified accounts and 180 days.
- Geometry-only dummies perform equivalently across buyer sensors.
- Stressed unit contribution becomes non-positive.
- Two buyers require automotive certification or an incumbent target before considering tests.
- Active core and four skins cannot be proven under 100k PLN.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Founder 15–20 hours/week; contract mechanical/embedded engineer; textile/coating fabricator; calibration lab. Pre-proof maximum 35k PLN; 12-month ceiling 100k PLN.
