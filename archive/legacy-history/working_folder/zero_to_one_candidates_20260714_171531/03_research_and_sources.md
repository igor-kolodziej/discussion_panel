# Research and Sources

Research date: 2026-07-14. Sources are primary/official, standards bodies, peer-reviewed research, or current company documentation unless explicitly labelled otherwise. No customer, supplier, laboratory, participant, manufacturer, or partner was contacted.

## Evidence: electrode and wearable-interface materials

- FDA ECG-electrode guidance explicitly includes premarket performance and shelf-life considerations for disposable electrodes: <https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/guidance-documents-medical-devices-and-radiation-emitting-products/electrocardiograph-electrodes-class-ii-special-controls-guidance-industry-and-food-drug>.
- A package-integrated electrode-impedance indicator has prior patent art; PouchSense requires a real FTO search and a materially different prediction/formulation path: <https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1365973A2/en>.
- Peer-reviewed work shows manufacturing paths for plated soft Ag/AgCl dry electrodes, but does not prove repeat commercial demand for a new supplier: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34733134/>.
- IEC 63203-201-3 covers conductive-textile resistance under simulated clothing microclimate; IEC 63203-201-4 covers sheet resistance after abrasion. These establish a measurement context, not demand for a proprietary consumable: <https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/64259>, <https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/68045>.
- FDA's current pulse-oximeter guidance and research on interchangeable skin phantoms support pigmentation as an engineering issue but do not make a bench film clinical evidence: <https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/pulse-oximeters-medical-purposes-non-clinical-and-clinical-performance-testing-labeling-and>, <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41306936/>.

## Evidence: biometric presentation-attack artifacts

- ISO/IEC 30107-3:2023 defines performance assessment and reporting for biometric presentation-attack detection and a classification of attack types: <https://www.iso.org/standard/79520.html>.
- FIDO biometric certification requires laboratory PAD testing and identifies accredited laboratories; certification trust remains with laboratories, so PAIForge can only sell pre-certification R&D artifacts unless accredited: <https://fidoalliance.org/specs/biometric/requirements/Biometrics-Requirements-v3.0-fd-20230111.html>, <https://fidoalliance.org/certification-biometric-component-fido-biometric-certification-faq/>.
- Android's current security documentation recommends modality-specific spoof materials, evidence that physical artifacts are an actual engineering input: <https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/biometric/measure>.
- NIST describes presentation attacks as an open-ended set of physical instruments, which supports rotating challenge materials rather than a static kit: <https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8491.pdf>.
- Peer-reviewed work demonstrates that new hydrogel/polymer materials can behave differently from common spoof materials. This supports technical possibility, not commercial willingness to pay: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9293316/>.
- Existing accredited labs including iBeta and BixeLab already own trusted test processes and are both potential customers and major substitutes: <https://www.ibeta.com/biometric-testing/>, <https://bixelab.com/>.

## Evidence: robot perception and human surrogates

- ISO 3691-4:2023 specifies safety requirements and verification for driverless industrial trucks; a third edition was at DIS ballot in June 2026. The operating zone materially affects safe operation: <https://www.iso.org/standard/88615.html>.
- NIST has published performance evaluation methods for human-detection systems in robot safety and standard-size human-limb obstacles for industrial vehicles: <https://www.nist.gov/publications/performance-evaluation-human-detection-systems-robot-safety>, <https://www.nist.gov/publications/safety-standard-advancement-toward-mobile-robot-use-near-humans>.
- NIST's program explicitly includes perception, human–robot interaction, mobile robots, and wearable robots. It also shows that open test methods are a substitute, so the physical signatures and randomized skins—not the protocol—must be proprietary: <https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/measurement-science-robotics-and-autonomous-systems-program>.
- Open NIST test arenas are intentionally reproducible. Bare boards or mannequins without protected telemetry/materials fail the control-point test: <https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/performance-metrics-and-test-arenas-autonomous-mobile-robots>.

## Evidence: haptic and teleoperation metrology

- IEEE 1918.1.1-2024 standardizes haptic codecs, providing a real interoperability context for time-correlated force/motion/command measurement: <https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1918.1.1/6835/>.
- NIST has demonstrated wireless time-sensitive networking advances, evidence that latency/jitter synchronization is an active engineering domain: <https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2022/07/nist-and-industry-researchers-make-advances-wireless-time-sensitive>.
- IEC 63203-402-1 defines test methods for glove-type motion sensors, illustrating a broader wearable measurement market but not directly validating LoopTruth demand: <https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/62633>.

## Evidence: secure human approval of AI-agent actions

- NIST has analyzed industry responses about AI-agent security, establishing active enterprise concern but not a budget for a separate hardware terminal: <https://www.nist.gov/publications/summary-analysis-responses-request-information-regarding-security-considerations-ai>.
- Microsoft documents a secure-agentic-systems architecture; this is evidence of buyer concern and a formidable platform substitute: <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/sfi/secure-agentic-systems>.
- FIDO secure payment confirmation shows precedent for binding a user's approval to transaction information. ActionKey must extend this to cross-vendor agent actions and a separately trusted display: <https://fidoalliance.org/white-paper-secure-payment-confirmation/>.
- Article 14 of the EU AI Act addresses human oversight for high-risk AI systems. It is contextual momentum, not a mandate for ActionKey: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng>.

## Evidence: smart-workwear pod circulation and service

- EU-OSHA discusses occupational exoskeletons and wearable-device deployment, establishing enterprise use and operational considerations but not the size of the smart-garment installed base: <https://osha.europa.eu/sites/default/files/MSDs_Occupational_exoskeletons_wearable_devices.pdf>.
- ETSA represents the European textile-service sector and provides a possible account universe; a reseller/laundry partnership is not assumed for the first paid employer pilot: <https://www.textile-services.eu/home/>.
- IPC research documents washability as a reliability issue in wearable electronics: <https://www.ipc.org/system/files/technical_resource/E41%26S04_02%20-%20Weifeng%20Liu.pdf>.

## Evidence: aftermarket wearable batteries

- Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 generally makes portable batteries replaceable from February 2027 and requires spare-part availability, creating aftermarket momentum: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/>.
- In February 2026 the Commission stated that a delegated act would add wearables to professional-only replacement derogations; in April 2026 it opened consultation on exemptions. The timing is therefore unstable and not a safe demand foundation: <https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/E-10-2025-004907-ASW_EN.html>, <https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-seeks-views-battery-removability-exemptions-2026-04-28_en>.

## Founder assumptions requiring paid proof

- A 5% positive response from 100 named accounts can be achieved with technical sample-led outreach by an unknown founder.
- Buyers will issue a paid feasibility order or deposit before a complete production product exists.
- The founder can use bounded contractors for polymer chemistry, embedded security, industrial design, calibration, and certification while staying within 25 hours weekly.
- Standard hardware gross margins of 55–70%, consumable gross margins of 65–85%, and 4–6× normalized EBIT values are attainable after production learning.
- Expected customer counts are global niche counts, not Poland-only counts. Poland is the low-cost engineering base; distribution must be EU/global.

## Hypotheses, not evidence

- The customer counts, prices, gross margins, CAC, sales-cycle lengths, renewal rates, and operating margins in the funnel.
- PouchSense's witness response predicts electrode usability well enough to survive OEM validation.
- PAIForge challenge lots find cross-sensor failures not covered by an internal laboratory's ordinary materials.
- HumanEcho's multimodal signatures and replaceable skins alter AMR release or acceptance decisions.
- LoopTruth reduces cross-vendor diagnosis time enough to justify a dedicated instrument.
- ActionKey's separately trusted display is preferred over software approval or existing FIDO/security keys.
- SmartPod can find enough already-deployed smart garments and save measurable downtime across brands.

## Second-wave evidence: accessible game haptics

- Xbox accessibility guidance treats redundant sensory channels as a design goal; this supports the pain but also makes native game support a substitute: <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103>.
- Audio Radar already converts 7.1 game audio into directional visual cues without studio integration and has an established accessory/distribution position: <https://audioradar.com/products/audio-radar>, <https://support.audioradar.com/portal/en/kb/articles/audio-radar-and-logitech-g-partner>.
- CueBand's direction-recognition performance, false-cue burden, 20,000-unit market, CAC, returns and subscription attachment remain hypotheses. The panel classified its first-sale control Tier 3.

## Second-wave evidence: jaw and muscle-switch interfaces

- Emego already markets a wireless body-worn dry-EMG assistive switch usable around the jaw: <https://emego.co.uk/>.
- GlassOuse already sells a muscle-activated switch inside an established assistive-input catalogue: <https://glassouse.com/product/glassouse-muscle-switch/>.
- Apple Switch Control already accepts external Bluetooth switches and built-in sound, camera, head, eye and AirPods gestures, increasing the substitution burden: <https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iphc9d32b862/26/ios/26>.
- Peer-reviewed work shows jaw-clench EMG can control an interface, supporting feasibility but not broad all-day four-to-six-command reliability: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25390372/>.
- Bluetooth SIG qualification and EU product/radio obligations are decision-critical launch costs: <https://www.bluetooth.com/develop-with-bluetooth/qualify/>, <https://www.bluetooth.com/fee-schedule/>, <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/general-product-safety-regulation-2023.html>.
- An active patent family claims earpiece facial EMG, inertial sensing and gesture commands, so freedom to operate cannot be assumed: <https://patents.google.com/patent/US11675437B2/en>.
- JawKey's daily false-trigger reliability, fitting burden, reachable population, CAC, returns, support cost, pad recurrence and 25,000-unit market remain hypotheses. The panel classified its first-sale control Tier 3.
