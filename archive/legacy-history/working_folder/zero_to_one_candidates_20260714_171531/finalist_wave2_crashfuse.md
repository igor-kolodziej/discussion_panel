# Zero-to-One Candidate: CrashFuse

## One-Sentence Thesis

Sell robot-hand integrators a compact breakaway interface with replaceable calibrated sacrificial overload cartridges that fail before a collision destroys a fragile dexterous hand, wrist sensor, or custom tool.

## Founder Fit

The founder fits instrumentation, analytics, failure data and SKU systems; a mechanical engineer/test lab handles flexures, fatigue, interfaces and safety/liability.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

Commissioning or operating fragile hands/tools exposes high replacement cost and downtime from collisions. Existing collision sensors/protectors can be bulky, expensive or resettable rather than a cheap replaceable mechanical fuse. Integrators use conservative software limits and custom brackets.

## Product Wedge

An 8–18k PLN starter interface with load-specific lattice/flexure cartridges; replacement packs cost 600–1,500 PLN. Begin on non-safety-rated dummy loads and commissioning applications, not human-safety functions.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Customers insure cheap electrical systems with fuses; fragile physical-AI end effectors may justify the same consumable failure hierarchy if the cartridge is predictable, compact and cheaper than downtime.

## Underused Momentum

More expensive dexterous hands/mobile manipulators are being deployed while established robot collision products prove an existing protection budget.

## Beachhead Market

Integrators commissioning fragile dexterous hands/custom EOAT where a defined overload envelope can be tested and the device is not part of a certified personnel-safety function.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Starter 8–18k PLN, expected 10k; cartridges 600–1,500, expected 900. Expected assembly BOM 3k and cartridge cost 180–300. Low-volume gross margin 55–65%; mature 10–18% operating margin after liability, test, distributors and support. Stress contribution is positive but thin.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten from public cobot integrator directories and hand-vendor integration announcements; first 100 through EOAT distributors and exact robot/hand load tables. First paid configuration is direct.

## Primary Control Point

Owned cartridge geometry/material process window, load maps, interface plates, calibration fixture and stocked spares. Customer prepays the first robot/tool load configuration.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

M3: 10/25/50-Nm benchtop cartridges under 15k PLN. M6: paid commissioning set on dummy load before 40k spend. M12: two robot interfaces, documented protected crash and repeat cartridge order under 100k.

## Distribution Advantage

Exact hand/tool load maps and stocked cartridges could make distributor selection easier than custom protection. This advantage begins only after multiple configurations.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with fragile hands, expand to cameras, force sensors, research tools and mobile-manipulator EOAT. The defence is a broad cartridge/interface load map, not a machined bracket.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers low-cost machining/additive manufacturing near EU automation. Existing global EOAT incumbents dominate distribution.

## Compounding Advantage

Crash/fatigue histories, load maps, interfaces, materials, stocked spares and distributors can compound into a robot protection consumable platform.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

95% reinvestment/≤5% distributions while employed conservative/expected; strong 100%. Paid configuration M6, two interfaces M12, distributor year 2, scale years 5–8.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Build benchtop cartridges and recorded protection demo under 15k, then target 100 integrators for a paid 8–18k commissioning set. Maximum pre-proof spend 40k; no certified application.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

15k prototype; ≤40k before paid proof; ≤100k after delivery/reorder; family capital excluded.

## Critical Dependency Map

Predictable failure, fatigue, liability, robot dynamics, device footprint, competitor distribution, certification boundaries and installed-hand volume.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Three integrators, repeat cartridge sales, positive liability-adjusted contribution, repeatable channel and 12 months runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Retain impact rig, material/process windows, interfaces, load maps and EOAT customers. Avoid custom engineering service.

## Critical Evidence

ATI sells collision protection and publishes technical manuals, proving budget and a strong substitute.

## Critical Hypotheses

Sacrificial cartridges beat resettable incumbents; 6,000 assemblies/year is attainable; product can remain outside safety functions; cartridges reorder.

## Kill Criteria

No paid configuration in 180 days; two failures outside calibration tolerance; ATI/DESTACO product is adequate; liability/certification erases margin; bottom-up 4× wealth case stays below 10m; no repeat cartridge order.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Founder 10–15 hours/week, mechanical engineer, test lab, IP/liability counsel, contract manufacturer. Pre-proof 40k PLN.
