# CueBand Duo — Exact Canonical v1

## Business thesis

CueBand Duo is a two-wrist tactile interface that converts ordinary multichannel PC-game audio into low-latency directional cues for deaf and hard-of-hearing gamers and, secondarily, people who must play silently. It does not require game-studio integration. Two four-motor wristbands and a USB audio interface classify five game-neutral event families—footsteps, shots, alarms, vehicles, and impacts—and render direction and intensity through a learned tactile vocabulary. The initial claim is information accessibility, not guaranteed competitive advantage.

## Customer, urgent event, and willingness to pay

The beachhead is adult deaf and hard-of-hearing PC players of five popular shooters and survival games who already lose spatial information encoded mainly in sound. Their alternatives are captions, visual sound radars, headset vibration, or avoiding audio-dependent play. Visual overlays consume the same visual attention needed for play, while game-specific accessibility support is inconsistent. A player pays when an audio-dependent game they want to play gives hearing players actionable direction that they cannot obtain through an acceptable redundant channel.

The preorder is 999 PLN, retail is 1,299 PLN, and optional model/cue updates are 149 PLN per year. The product is sold directly as a self-contained accessory; no studio, platform, clinic, or accessibility organization must authorize the first sale.

## Product and first-order control point

At the first paid order, the company controls the wristband design, serialized prototype inventory, haptic vocabulary, firmware and device keys, audio-event and direction models, calibration protocol, and direct customer relationship. The product works from ordinary 5.1/7.1 or headphone audio. Opt-in false-event reports, calibration traces, game/audio signatures, and cue-preference data improve later firmware and form a proprietary cross-game tactile-interaction corpus.

This is a Tier-1 product control point at launch, but its durability must be earned. Commodity motors are not the moat. The proposed compounding assets are cross-game low-latency classification, a tactile language that users learn across titles, measured information-transfer performance, an installed base, and direct accessibility distribution. The strongest copy risk is Audio Radar adding wrist haptics or platform owners exposing adequate native directional-cue APIs.

## Cold start and acquisition

The founder first publishes a transparent, instrumented demonstration for five games using a wired two-wrist prototype, including end-to-end latency, false-cue rate, direction-recognition accuracy, and a comparison with a visual overlay. The first ten customers come from direct participation in deaf-gaming and accessibility communities and exact problem-search traffic; the first 100 come from accessibility directories, game-specific search pages, a direct store, and small paid-search experiments. Community access is a channel to test, not an assumed endorsement or partnership.

This is a pre-implementation proposal: no sales, outreach, or paid evidence are claimed. The proposed paid-proof gate is at least one 999 PLN paid preorder within 180 days and 25 paid preorders before production commitment. Refundability, delivery date, supported games, and prototype status are disclosed. Fewer than 25 paid preorders by day 180 closes the project.

## Reachability, capital, and founder capacity

The founder remains employed and works at most 25 hours per week. A bounded embedded engineer and accessibility/user-research contractor cover specialist tasks. By month 3, the project produces a wired prototype and five-game corpus for at most 18,000 PLN. Cumulative pre-proof exposure is capped at 38,000 PLN through month 6. No injection tooling or production order is placed before the preorder gate. Spending may rise to 90,000 PLN only after at least 25 paid preorders and positive delivered contribution under a stress case of 20% lower price, 30% higher BOM/fulfilment cost, and doubled CAC. Family capital remains excluded until shipped satisfaction, multiple independent cohorts, and repeatable acquisition exist.

The target blended device revenue is 1,200 PLN, BOM and fulfilment are 400–500 PLN, gross margin is 55%, and operating margin at scale is 20%. Under the stated stress case, contribution must remain positive or the project stops. Full-time transition requires at least 500 paid units or contracted equivalent, positive delivered contribution, three independent acquisition cohorts, a repeatable acquisition signal, and 12 months of personal runway.

## Founder wealth path

The conservative case is 5,000 devices at 1,100 PLN plus 1,000 annual subscribers at 149 PLN: 5.65 million PLN revenue, 48% gross margin, 15% operating margin, 0.85 million PLN EBIT, 85% founder ownership, and about 2.9 million PLN founder equity at 4× EBIT in year 6. It does not pass the 10 million PLN wealth target.

The expected-success case is 20,000 devices at 1,200 PLN plus 5,000 subscribers at 149 PLN: 24.75 million PLN revenue, 55% gross margin, 20% operating margin, and 4.95 million PLN EBIT. With 75% founder ownership after an option pool or minority financing, the founder stake is 14.8–22.3 million PLN at 4–6× normalized EBIT in year 7, before debt. This case requires 20,000 global annual units and is invalid if acquisition cost, returns, or native substitutes make that volume implausible.

The strong-success case is 60,000 devices at 1,250 PLN plus 25,000 subscribers at 149 PLN: about 78.7 million PLN revenue, 60% gross margin, 24% operating margin, 18.9 million PLN EBIT, 65% founder ownership, and about 61.4 million PLN founder equity at 5× EBIT in year 8, before debt.

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of distributable after-tax cash while the founder remains employed and cap optional employment-period distributions at 5%; strong success reinvests 100%. Retained cash is not added to an EBIT-based company value, and debt is deducted.

## Expansion, defensibility, and failure tests

If the five-game accessibility wedge works, the tactile language can expand to console and VR, accessible navigation and alarms, and other low-visual-load spatial interfaces. The resulting future-lab assets are wearable-haptics hardware expertise, real-time HCI firmware and models, a proprietary tactile-language and game-audio corpus, accessibility distribution, and human information-transfer data.

Kill criteria are: fewer than 25 paid preorders in 180 days; no meaningful median improvement in blinded direction-recognition tests; unsafe or distracting false cues above a predeclared threshold; non-positive stressed contribution; returns or CAC that invalidate the 20,000-unit expected case; or a native/API/adjacent response that makes separate wrist hardware unnecessary. Chatbot or simulated review is only permission to test these hypotheses, not permission to spend serious capital.
