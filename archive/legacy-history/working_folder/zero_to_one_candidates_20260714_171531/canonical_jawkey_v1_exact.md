# JawKey — Exact Canonical v1

## Business thesis

JawKey is a behind-ear dry-EMG and inertial-sensing module that converts four to six deliberate jaw gestures into standard Bluetooth switch or keyboard commands. Its beachhead is people with limited or variable hand control who cannot reliably use ordinary input and do not want voice, eye tracking, large head movement, or an intraoral bite device. It is sold first as a non-medical computer-input accessory, not as a diagnostic or therapeutic product.

## Customer, urgent event, and willingness to pay

The initial customer is an adult with limited hand movement, tremor, fatigue, or episodic weakness who can deliberately contract jaw muscles and already needs iOS, Android, or Windows switch control. Existing choices impose visible head motion, mouth hygiene and bite hardware, public speech, camera line-of-sight, or high eye-tracking cost. Jaw movement may be a private, fast, low-motion input channel when those tradeoffs are unacceptable. The urgent paid event is configuring or replacing a daily computer/phone switch that the user can operate reliably without another person or a custom clinic installation.

The paid developer preorder is 899 PLN, retail is 1,299 PLN, and replaceable skin-pad packs are 60–120 PLN. Standard Bluetooth HID makes the first product demonstrable and sellable without clinic, platform, insurer, distributor, or medical authority approval.

## Product and first-order control point

The ear- or glasses-hook module combines a company-owned dry-electrode geometry, analog front end, IMU, enclosure, firmware, local calibration protocol, gesture model, Bluetooth HID implementation, serialized inventory, and replaceable skin-pad interface. The first release recognizes only four to six intentionally separable gestures and includes a physical/software pause state to prevent unwanted input during eating or conversation.

At the first paid order the startup controls the working device and pad SKU rather than promising future pooled data. With explicit opt-in consent, daily false-trigger events, calibration traces, electrode placement, skin/contact conditions, and successful jaw gestures form a proprietary neuromotor dataset. The compounding control point is the combination of electrode/pad geometry, low-power inference, cross-user calibration, measured daily reliability, installed devices, direct accessibility distribution, and recurring pads. The strongest copy risk is GlassOuse adding EMG or Apple, Meta, or another platform offering an adequate camera, wrist-EMG, or glasses gesture substitute through an installed base.

## Cold start and acquisition

The first ten users are recruited through assistive-technology forums, disability/accessibility device communities, and a public paid-beta application; the first 100 come from exact problem search, accessibility marketplaces and directories, direct device-loan deposits, and demonstrations using standard operating-system switch control. These routes are testable acquisition mechanisms, not assumed endorsements or partnerships.

This is a pre-implementation proposal: no sales, outreach, user studies, or paid evidence are claimed. The proposed paid-proof gate is at least one 899 PLN paid preorder or deposit within 180 days and 25 independent paid preorders before production commitment. Prototype status, intended use, limitations, refundability, and delivery conditions are disclosed. Fewer than 25 paid preorders by day 180 closes the project.

## Reachability, capital, and founder capacity

The founder remains employed and works at most 25 hours per week. A bounded analog/embedded contractor and an accessibility or occupational-therapy research contractor fill specialist gaps; no clinic partnership is a prerequisite. By month 3, the project produces ten prototypes plus bench and day-use protocols for at most 24,000 PLN. Cumulative exposure before paid proof is capped at 38,000 PLN. No production tooling is ordered before the preorder gate. Spending may rise to 95,000 PLN only after 25 paid preorders and positive delivered contribution under a stress case of 20% lower selling price, 30% higher BOM/fulfilment cost, and doubled CAC. Family capital remains excluded until delivered reliability, multiple independent paying cohorts, and repeatable acquisition exist.

The target blended hardware revenue is 1,100 PLN, BOM and fulfilment are 350–450 PLN, gross margin is 55%, and operating margin at scale is 18%. Stressed contribution must remain positive. Full-time transition requires at least 500 paid devices or contracted recurring demand, positive delivered contribution, three independent acquisition cohorts, a repeatable channel, and 12 months of personal runway.

## Founder wealth path

The conservative case is 6,000 devices at 1,000 PLN plus pad sales: 6.5 million PLN revenue, 48% gross margin, 12% operating margin, 0.78 million PLN EBIT, 85% founder ownership, and about 2.65 million PLN founder equity at 4× EBIT in year 6. It does not pass the 10 million PLN target.

The expected-success case is 25,000 devices at 1,100 PLN plus 1.2 million PLN of pad revenue: 28.7 million PLN revenue, 55% gross margin, 18% operating margin, and 5.17 million PLN EBIT. With 70% founder ownership after an option pool or minority financing, founder equity is 14.5–21.7 million PLN at 4–6× normalized EBIT in year 7, before debt. This case is invalid if daily reliability, preference, returns, CAC, or the reachable assistive and adjacent user population cannot support 25,000 annual devices.

The strong-success case is 75,000 devices at 1,200 PLN plus 6 million PLN of pad revenue: 96 million PLN revenue, 60% gross margin, 22% operating margin, 21.1 million PLN EBIT, 60% founder ownership, and about 63.4 million PLN founder equity at 5× EBIT in year 8, before debt.

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of distributable after-tax cash while the founder remains employed and cap optional employment-period distributions at 5%; strong success reinvests 100%. Retained cash is not added to an EBIT-based business value, and debt is deducted.

## Expansion, defensibility, and failure tests

If the assistive switch wins on reliability, comfort, privacy, and fatigue, the same neuromotor interface can expand to silent AI commands, hands-busy industrial control, and AR/VR input or licensing. Those expansions are not used to excuse failure of the assistive wedge. The future-lab assets are proprietary jaw-neuromotor data, dry-electrode and analog-front-end expertise, low-power wearable inference, real-time HCI, pad/skin-interface know-how, accessibility distribution, and research relationships formed through bounded testing.

Kill criteria are: fewer than 25 paid preorders in 180 days; speech, eating, clenching, or movement produce false inputs above a predeclared daily threshold; dry behind-ear sensing is unreliable across users or skin/hair conditions; median target users prefer existing switches; fitting becomes individualized clinical labor; stressed contribution is non-positive; CAC and returns invalidate the 25,000-unit expected case; or platform/adjacent substitutes remove the privacy, fatigue, or reliability advantage. Chatbot or simulated review is only permission to test these hypotheses, not permission to spend serious capital.
