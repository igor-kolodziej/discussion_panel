# Zero-to-One Evaluation: HumanEcho

## Diagnosis

HumanEcho connects a costly safety/perception release event to owned hardware and recurring physical skins, avoiding the open-task-board failure of the prior run. Its strongest wedge is a warehouse-native multimodal human signature at lower cost and complexity than automotive targets. The concept still faces automotive-target incumbents, internal foam dummies, uncertain repeat skin demand, and non-certification trust. The paid feasibility test is bounded enough to justify independent paneling.

## Evidence and Assumptions

ISO and NIST sources establish driverless-truck verification and human-detection measurement. They do not prove that AMR teams buy an external surrogate or randomized skin refresh. Prices, 120 expected accounts, repeat skin rate, and 27% EBIT are founder hypotheses. Stress economics remain positive.

## Overall Score

**75/100.** Weighted points = 150.0 of 200; `10 × (150 / 20) = 75`.

## Control Point

**Tier 1.** Owned active core, multimodal calibration, skin formulations, serialized inventory, and regression software exist at first paid feasibility. Strongest copy risk: automotive pedestrian-target firms offer a warehouse SKU or an AMR OEM builds an adequate internal dummy. Price, aisle fit, randomized skins, and cross-sensor history provide the catch-up case.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, expected case.** Expected 16.8m PLN revenue, 4.54m EBIT, 78% ownership and 4–6× yield 14.2–21.2m PLN in year 8. The conservative case is only 3.7m PLN. The 120-account case requires global AMR penetration and recurring skins; retained cash is not added again and debt is deducted.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** Passive geometries/skins and a recorded repeatability demo cost ≤35k PLN, after which a 25–40k PLN paid active-core feasibility/preorder can be requested. It remains part-time with fixed-scope mechanical/fabrication/calibration contractors.

## Expansion Check

Warehouse AMR perception is focused enough to win; cobots and industrial vehicles are credible later. Automotive target incumbents can enter but may be too costly/general. EU manufacturing/logistics density helps acquisition, not defensibility.

## Fatal Objections

1. A low-cost foam/standard obstacle may be good enough.
2. Buyers may require certified target suppliers, not a regression product.
3. Recurring randomized skins may not change release decisions.
4. Hardware support/calibration can erode margins and founder capacity.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Turn multimodal human appearance variability into controlled recurring physical inputs for machine-perception regression.
