# Zero-to-One Candidate: CueBand Duo

## One-Sentence Thesis

Sell two-wrist tactile wearables that convert ordinary multichannel PC-game audio into low-latency directional cues for deaf/hard-of-hearing gamers and people who must play silently, without requiring game-studio integration.

## Founder Fit

The founder fits signal classification, real-time models, experiments, and AI-assisted firmware. A contract embedded engineer and accessibility researcher cover hardware and user testing; manufacturing follows paid preorders.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

Many shooters and survival games encode footsteps, shots, alarms, and vehicles primarily in audio. Deaf/hard-of-hearing players lose competitive/spatial information; silent-play users rely on captions, visual radar products, or loud speakers/headsets. Visual overlays consume attention and game-specific developer support is inconsistent.

## Product Wedge

Two four-motor wristbands plus a USB audio interface classify five game-neutral audio event families and render direction/intensity through a learned two-wrist vocabulary. Initial claims are accessibility cues, not guaranteed competitive performance.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The player wants information parity, not stronger bass. Existing haptic vests emphasize immersion and visual radar emphasizes screens; a discreet cross-game tactile language can occupy the directional-information niche.

## Underused Momentum

Platform accessibility guidance recommends redundant channels, PC audio is already multichannel, local models can classify events, and direct-accessibility communities support preorder testing without studio partnerships.

## Beachhead Market

Deaf/hard-of-hearing adult PC gamers who play five popular shooters/survival games and already report directional-audio disadvantage; silent competitive players are secondary.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Preorder 999 PLN, retail 1,299 PLN, optional 149 PLN/year cue/model updates and replaceable straps. Expected blended device revenue 1,200 PLN; target BOM/fulfilment 400–500 PLN, 55% gross margin and 20% operating margin. Under 20% lower price, 30% higher BOM and doubled 100 PLN CAC, contribution remains narrowly positive.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten from deaf-gaming/accessibility communities and a downloadable five-game demo using wired prototype outputs; first 100 through accessibility directories, Steam/game search pages, direct store, paid search, and community partnerships that do not require a studio integration or founder influencer persona.

## Primary Control Point

Owned wristband design/inventory, haptic vocabulary, firmware/device keys, audio-event/direction models, calibration traces, and direct customer distribution. The product works on ordinary audio at first sale.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

M3: wired two-wrist prototype and five-game corpus under 18k PLN. M6: at least 20 paid 999 PLN preorders before cumulative spend exceeds 38k. M12: 150 shipped units, six cross-game cue packs, return/usage evidence, and total founder capital under 90k PLN.

## Distribution Advantage

Accessibility demonstrations and exact game/problem search intent are observable. Cross-game model updates and a direct installed base reduce dependence on game studios and make later cue packs cheaper to distribute.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with PC directional audio; expand to console, VR, alarms/navigation, esports training, and accessible non-game spatial alerts. The advantage must be latency, cross-game classification, and learned tactile language, not commodity vibration motors.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland provides engineering/manufacturing economics and a large gaming workforce, but sales are global. EU accessibility expectations help context, not exclusivity.

## Compounding Advantage

Opt-in calibration traces, false-event reports, game/audio signatures, cue vocabulary, firmware and direct distribution improve across customers and can seed broader haptic HCI IP.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative/expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash while employed and cap optional distributions at 5%; strong success reinvests 100%. Milestones: prototype M3, paid preorders M6, 150 units M12, 1,500 year 2, 5,000 year 3–4, expected scale year 7.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Demonstrate five games with a wired prototype, publish latency/false-event tests, and solicit 999 PLN refundable preorders. One paid preorder qualifies; 20 are required to continue. Maximum pre-proof loss 38k PLN; no mass tooling before payment.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

18k prototype; up to 38k before paid proof; up to 90k only after 20 preorders and positive stressed contribution; remaining 100k only after shipped satisfaction and repeatable acquisition. Family capital excluded.

## Critical Dependency Map

Cross-game classification/latency, directional information transfer, accessibility comfort, platform audio access, returns, Audio Radar/Woojer response, and consumer CAC.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Three independent acquisition cohorts, positive delivered contribution, repeat preorder/retention, at least 500 paid units or contracted equivalent, and 12 months runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Retain haptic hardware, vocabulary, audio corpus/models, accessibility relationships, and direct wearable distribution. Do not pivot to generic wellness vibration.

## Critical Evidence

Xbox guidance endorses redundant channels; Audio Radar and Woojer prove demand/substitutes; AbleGamers documents audio-accessibility problems.

## Critical Hypotheses

Two wrists convey direction faster than a visual overlay; cross-game models are accurate; 20k units/year is reachable; buyers accept price/comfort.

## Kill Criteria

Fewer than 20 paid preorders in 180 days; median users do not improve direction detection; false cues exceed predeclared threshold; stressed contribution non-positive; Audio Radar/native APIs make wrist hardware unnecessary; expected CAC/payback cannot support 20k units.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Founder 15–20 hours/week, embedded contractor, accessibility/user-research contractor, contract assembler after preorders. Pre-proof 38k PLN.
