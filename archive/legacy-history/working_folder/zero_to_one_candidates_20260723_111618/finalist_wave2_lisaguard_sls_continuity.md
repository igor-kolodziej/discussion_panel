Candidate generation only — not validated or scored. Research snapshot: 2026-07-23 CEST. This candidate is conditional on a signed exclusive legacy-asset option below the capital ceiling. ## One-Sentence Thesis

Use paid owner demand to acquire an exclusive, hard-capped Lisa/Lisa Pro service and firmware carve-out from Sinterit, then keep the global installed base productive through first-person AI-assisted diagnosis, model-specific parts, firmware continuity, and serialized uptime plans.

## Founder Fit

The founder can contribute AI/ML, data systems, financial diligence, experimental diagnostics, remote human–computer interfaces, and a disciplined asset-purchase structure. Being in Poland makes the Kraków company, records, equipment, and potential engineers more reachable than they are to a foreign aftermarket entrant.

The founder is not presently an SLS hardware engineer. The idea does not pretend otherwise. At least one former/current senior service or firmware engineer must sign employment or vesting terms before official support begins. Informal help, a reseller relationship, or reverse engineering alone is not the control point.

The founder can run asset diligence, owner outreach, paid audits, and a capped support cohort alongside employment. Hardware operations scale only after paid demand and the rights package exist.

## Customer And Pain

The first payer is a European service bureau, university laboratory, product-development team, orthotics/prosthetics workshop, or small manufacturer that actively uses a serialized Sinterit Lisa or Lisa Pro and cannot cheaply replace its qualified SLS workflow.

The painful event is a machine fault, firmware/software incompatibility, unavailable sensor or heater, missing material profile, or loss of model-specific expertise. The economic loss is:

- outsourced-print cost and delay;
- idle technicians and missed customer deadlines;
- requalification of parts/materials on a replacement platform;
- writing off a machine and peripherals that originally cost thousands or tens of thousands of euros.

Lisa Pro is officially shown as sold out. Sinterit's current service-plan terms and store pages focus on newer Lisa X and Suzy machines. Public reporting and the company's own history indicate thousands of machines installed globally and customers in more than eighty countries. This establishes a potential base, not a count of active Lisa/Lisa Pro machines.

Prompting an AI cannot create firmware rights, a tested spare, a donor assembly, a calibration fixture, or an engineer who knows a proprietary failure mode.

## Product Wedge

The founding sequence has two products separated by the asset gate.

### Before the asset gate: paid Continuity Audit

For 2,500 PLN, LisaGuard:

1. records the machine model, serial, firmware/software, usage, consumables, known faults, critical parts, and replacement economics;
2. checks what can be backed up lawfully by the owner;
3. produces a model-specific continuity risk map and deposit offer;
4. takes an optional 5,000 PLN conditional support deposit, credited to the first annual plan and refundable if the named exclusive asset option is not secured.

The audit is a paid diagnostic, not authorized manufacturer service.

### After the asset gate: serialized Uptime Plan

The plan includes:

- response SLA and a phone/smart-glasses first-person diagnostic session combining machine telemetry, visual stream, and engineer supervision;
- lawful firmware/software continuity and version validation;
- model-specific replacement parts and calibrated assemblies;
- preventive-maintenance schedule and repair history by serial;
- premium donor-unit/loaner or emergency production fallback where owned capacity permits;
- material-profile and print-quality knowledge for the legacy machine.

No Sinterit trademark, firmware, manual, customer record, or claim of authorization is used before the contract grants it.

## Commercial Insight Or Market Gap

Hardware startups often rationally prioritise new products while an installed legacy base becomes a support liability. In a restructuring or strategic refocus, an exclusive legacy carve-out can be positive-sum:

- the original company receives cash and sheds bounded obligations;
- owners avoid forced replacement;
- key engineers preserve economically useful know-how;
- the acquirer receives rights and routed demand that ordinary repair shops cannot buy from a component distributor.

The gap is not “repair old printers.” It is **buy the discontinued model's aftermarket before knowledge, firmware, fixtures, and customer routing fragment**.

The current manufacturer may survive and reject any carve-out. That is not a reason to improvise a weaker model; it is the decisive kill test.
