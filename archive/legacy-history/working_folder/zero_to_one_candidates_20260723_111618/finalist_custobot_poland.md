# Zero-to-One Candidate: LinePool (Custobot Poland)

Candidate generation only — not validated. Research snapshot: 2026-07-23 CEST. Savings frequency, provider acceptance, case labour, acquisition cost, margins, customer counts, and valuation are hypotheses.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Aggregate paid, time-boxed negotiation mandates from Polish microbusinesses into switch-ready batches of mobile and internet demand, then use a human-supervised AI agent to make incumbents and challengers compete inside each owner's approved boundary while earning an activation fee plus a share of verified first-year savings.

## Founder Fit

The founder's business analysis, finance, data science, LLM/agent engineering, experimentation, and automation experience fit bill extraction, provider playbooks, offer comparison, negotiation policies, human approval, and unit-economics optimisation. The first service can be human-supervised and delivered outside corporate hours in bounded cases.

The founder lacks telecom-law, power-of-attorney, call-centre, and high-volume customer-operations expertise. These are bounded through a fixed-fee Polish telecom/consumer counsel, explicit user-in-the-loop authentication, recorded audit trails where lawful, and a part-time case operator only after paid volume.

The family installation company may supply anonymised workflow examples with permission, but it cannot count as paid proof. The first paid cases must be independent microbusinesses.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first payer is a Polish microbusiness with three to twenty mobile, internet, voice, monitoring, or M2M services; at least 600 PLN monthly telecom spend; and a contract expiring within six months.

The owner does not primarily want the mathematically cheapest tariff. They want:

- no hours on retention calls, branch visits, and contradictory offers;
- certainty that a lower price does not hide device finance, missing roaming, slower service, or new lock-in;
- one approval screen showing current cost, proposed cost, term, risk, and verified savings;
- cancellation completed correctly if the provider will not improve the offer;
- the next renewal captured automatically.

Current workarounds are doing nothing, asking an employee, accepting the provider's first renewal, visiting a retail branch, switching through a reseller, or hiring a telecom adviser whose incentives and fee may be unclear. Microbusiness owners tolerate leakage because the absolute saving appears smaller than the hassle and service-continuity risk.

## Product Wedge

The founding service is a human-supervised **50-Line Renewal Batch**:

1. The owner uploads recent bills and contracts and identifies non-negotiable service requirements.
2. Custobot normalises lines, devices, discounts, end dates, penalties, and actual usage.
3. The owner pays for and signs a 30-day, category-limited exclusive negotiation mandate, approves a savings boundary, and states whether incumbent retention or switching is acceptable.
4. LinePool groups ten or more compatible businesses—initially about fifty mobile/fibre lines—into a standardised, anonymised demand lot and requests comparable incumbent and challenger offers through ordinary business-sales channels. No provider exclusivity is required.
5. Under counsel-reviewed authority, Custobot also uses permitted documentary channels or a user-present/verified call for account-specific retention. It never impersonates the customer.
6. Every proposed contract appears in a concise approval screen; the customer retains final e-signature/authorisation and continuity control.
7. The fee is calculated from documented first-year net savings after one-off charges and lost discounts.
8. The system records the next renewal and automatically forms a future batch before the notice window.

The first version supports two major providers and mobile/fixed internet only. It does not hold client money, sign on the client's behalf beyond the reviewed mandate, sell telecom service, act as a lawyer, or guarantee continuity.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Comparison sites solve advertised acquisition offers one customer at a time. They do not create a switch-ready pool of existing microbusiness contracts, spend time inside each incumbent's retention workflow, or prove that the winning counteroffer is actually better. Traditional advisers can do this for larger accounts but often sell expertise by the hour or earn opaque commissions.

The gap is a **buyer-side retention desk for the long tail**: paid demand is aggregated before suppliers are approached, incentives are transparent and customer-funded, and each owner keeps a final approval. Fifty ordinary lines are individually uninteresting; a recurring pool with comparable constraints can make a provider's business-sales team compete while still supporting account-specific negotiation.

The contrarian insight is that agentic AI becomes valuable first where it absorbs socially tedious, repetitive negotiation—not where it gives the owner more recommendations.

## Underused Momentum

- Poland has 2.37 million microenterprises and a 46 billion PLN telecommunications market.
- UKE reports more than 2,600 telecom undertakings, large mobile/M2M volumes, and documentary-form subscriber rights.
- Current multimodal models can extract bills, compare contract language, and draft provider-specific messages cheaply.
- Real-time voice agents exist abroad, while Polish provider language, identity, and contract detail remain local execution work.
- Provider interactions generate outcome-labelled data: offer, objection, escalation, channel, time, and accepted term.

## Beachhead Market

Begin with Polish owner-operated firms that have:

- three to twenty telecom services;
- monthly spend above 600 PLN;
- a renewal or cancellable contract in the next six months;
- no complex public procurement or multinational framework;
- an owner who can authenticate and approve promptly.

Prioritise small professional practices, trades, ecommerce firms, local logistics, and multi-person service companies. Exclude consumers, disputed debt, vulnerable customers, litigation, regulated emergency connectivity, public bodies, and accounts needing uninterrupted enterprise network redesign in the first year.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Non-refundable activation and 30-day batch mandate: 149 PLN.
- Success fee: 30% of verified first-year net savings, with a 390 PLN minimum total fee and 2,500 PLN cap for the founding product.
- No success fee if the final approved arrangement does not produce documented net savings.
- Optional renewal watch after the first case: 149–249 PLN annually, creditable against the next success fee.

Planning assumptions:

- conservative average completed-customer revenue: 750 PLN;
- expected: 900 PLN; strong: 1,100 PLN as larger line pools and renewal watch grow;
- initial manual handling: 2–4 hours per case;
- mature supervised handling target: under 45 minutes for supported providers;
- mature gross margin: 65%–78%;
- operating margin: 22%–35% after support, acquisition, legal/compliance, QA, and engineering.

A 750 PLN completed customer fails contribution if paid labour, telephony, document handling, refunds, and acquisition exceed 450 PLN. Automation and batch reuse must reduce labour before paid volume is scaled.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First ten**

- Use permitted data from the family firm only to construct a sample bill map and script.
- Build provider-specific landing pages and approach 150 publicly listed microbusinesses whose services or locations imply multiple telecom lines.
- Offer the exact 149 PLN activation/mandate plus 30% success fee; no free bespoke bill analysis.
- Require ten independent payments covering at least fifty lines, then send one standardised batch to multiple ordinary business-sales channels and complete at least five account-specific provider interactions before expanding beyond two providers.
- The owner joins any authentication step and approves every binding term.

**First hundred**

- Publish provider-specific savings examples with all identifiers removed and customer permission.
- Test high-intent search ads around business renewal/cancellation, direct outbound by contract-timing proxy, and a simple referral credit.
- Bookkeepers, IT service firms, and brokers may later refer customers, but no partnership is required for the first hundred and Custobot retains billing.
- Support no new category until a provider playbook yields positive contribution and a documented escalation path.

## Primary Control Point

The proposed control point is a **contractual Tier 2 transaction route**:

- paid, time-limited, category-specific exclusive mandates create a temporarily controlled pool of switch-ready demand before providers are approached;
- a customer-approved policy constrains what the agent may ask for or accept;
- the system receives every comparable batch offer and account-specific counteroffer and retains a provider/offer/objection/channel/outcome graph within lawful contractual rights;
- the renewal calendar creates the next transaction before the customer drifts back to the provider;
- direct billing aligns incentives with verified savings.

It is revocable and providers may refuse third-party interaction. The strongest copy risk is a telecom adviser, comparison site, operator, Billshark-like entrant, or general AI caller adding Polish support. Custobot must compound provider-specific outcome data and labour automation, not rely on “AI negotiates” as IP.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

**Resource needed:** real bills/contracts, customer authority, provider responses, a verified savings calculation, and rights to learn from non-personal interaction patterns.

**How it is secured**

1. Obtain a legal opinion and standard 30-day exclusive negotiation mandate for two supported provider workflows; customer final approval remains non-delegated.
2. Build a bill parser, savings ledger, approval screen, and case audit trail; keep negotiations human-supervised.
3. Sell ten 149 PLN activations covering at least fifty lines to unrelated microbusinesses before requesting a batch offer.
4. Send the standardised batch through ordinary provider business-sales channels, then use documentary channels or customer-present authentication for account-specific retention; never spoof identity or fabricate consent.
5. Deliver completed cases manually and store only data required by contract/law.
6. Within twelve months, secure 250 paid customers, 100 successful outcomes, 100 renewal-watch enrollments, and at least three successive batches whose interaction policy reduces median supervised time below 60 minutes per customer.

An unknown founder can buy legal review, reach public businesses, and prove savings without operator partnerships. Provider acceptance itself is deliberately tested before substantial software investment.

**Proof before serious capital**

- ten non-refundable activations and signed mandates covering at least fifty lines;
- five completed provider interactions;
- at least two comparable provider or incumbent-retention responses to the first batch, without a special partnership;
- at least three customer-approved improved outcomes or correctly completed exits;
- positive contribution on supported cases excluding founder product-development time;
- median customer-rated effort materially below doing it themselves;
- one repeat transaction, second service, or paid renewal-watch purchase.

## Distribution Advantage

Cold start uses provider- and problem-specific intent:

- “business Orange/Play/T-Mobile renewal” pages;
- contract/cancellation checklists that show exactly what Custobot handles;
- targeted outbound to businesses with multi-line signals;
- savings proof and transparent fee rather than a generic AI promise.

Distribution compounds through:

- renewal dates acquired through paid delivery;
- a growing pool of paid, time-boxed demand that makes future batches more attractive to ordinary provider sales channels;
- customer referrals when savings are verified;
- provider-specific outcome benchmarks;
- later bookkeeping/IT referrals after direct economics are proven;
- one approval interface that can support adjacent recurring vendor negotiations.

No exclusive provider access, bank partnership, influencer, or high-status introduction is assumed.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Dominate two Polish telecom-provider workflows and one repeatable 50–200-line batch format for qualifying microbusinesses.
2. Automate bill normalisation, message drafting, call navigation, offer comparison, and savings verification while retaining human supervision.
3. Expand line-pool density, additional telecom providers, and M2M/connectivity products.
4. Add one adjacent recurring contract—merchant acquiring, waste, security monitoring, or business software—only if the same customer base pre-pays and the mandate/legal workflow transfers.
5. Offer the policy/approval/negotiation engine to trusted SME service platforms only after Custobot owns direct outcomes and distribution.

The defensible product is a replenishing pool of paid renewal mandates plus a provider-response/outcome engine, not a generic procurement consultant or chat wrapper.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish documentary-form termination rights, provider language, bundle structures, identity processes, and a fragmented operator market create meaningful localisation work. A success-fee product for microbusinesses sits below the account size targeted by many traditional telecom advisers.

This is not a formal barrier. MOLL, Tarveo, comparison sites, and global bill-negotiation agents can move inward. Defence requires faster supported-case resolution, transparent verified savings, Polish provider playbooks, and a renewal relationship whose acquisition cost falls after the first case.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid case can create:

- normalised service and price structure;
- contract timing, constraints, and membership in a comparable demand batch;
- provider channel and identity path;
- initial offer and counteroffer;
- objection/escalation sequence;
- approved boundary and final customer decision;
- verified savings and case labour.

This builds advanced agentic AI, real-time human–AI approval interfaces, negotiation/outcome data, direct SME distribution, automation IP, and capital. It is a future-lab asset through agent-to-human and agent-to-agent interaction, even though it does not initially collect physiology or build wearable hardware.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected scenarios reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder remains employed; optional founder distributions are capped at 5%. Strong success reinvests 100% during scale.

Reinvestment order:

1. legal/compliance, auditability, and customer identity/approval controls;
2. provider-specific automation and quality assurance;
3. customer support and fraud/abuse prevention;
4. provider-specific search and renewal acquisition;
5. adjacent categories only after telecom contribution and renewal work.

No separate value is assigned to the dataset beyond company value.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- **Days 1–20:** review ten permitted historical bills; twenty microbusiness interviews; legal memo and narrow mandate; map two provider workflows.
- **Days 21–45:** launch two provider-specific pages, bill parser, 149 PLN activation/mandate payment, and approval screen.
- **Days 46–85:** obtain ten independent activations covering at least fifty lines, submit the first batch, and complete at least five provider interactions manually.
- **Days 86–130:** reach twenty paid activations, at least ten completed cases, and measure savings, handling time, refusal reasons, and customer effort.
- **Days 131–180:** reach thirty paid activations, at least fifteen completed outcomes, five renewal-watch purchases or second cases, and positive contribution for supported workflows.

The founder remains employed and limits the cohort so each case is supervised. Paid proof is the non-refundable activation and earned success fee; a free bill review or customer permission alone is not paid evidence.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- **Tranche 1 — maximum 17,000 PLN before paid proof:** telecom/mandate/privacy counsel 6k; parser/approval prototype 5k; provider workflow tests and telephony 2k; acquisition 2k; contingency 2k.
- **Tranche 2 — cumulative maximum 40,000 PLN:** after ten paid activations and five deliverable interactions, for QA tooling and a part-time case operator.
- **Tranche 3 — cumulative maximum 100,000 PLN:** after 100 independent paying customers, positive contribution, provider acceptance, repeat/renewal purchases, and a viable acquisition channel.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave corporate employment only after:

- at least 500 independent paying customers, three repeatable line batches, and 200 paid renewal-watch or repeat commitments;
- positive contribution after labour, telephony, refunds, legal/compliance, support, and CAC;
- two repeatable acquisition sources;
- supported-case handling that does not scale with founder hours;
- provider acceptance across at least three meaningful workflows;
- at least twelve months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If providers reject representation or savings are too small, stop rather than drift into generic cost consulting. Reusable assets include:

- counsel-reviewed approval/mandate patterns;
- bill parsing and contract-diff tooling;
- human-supervised negotiation policies;
- lawful provider workflow/outcome data;
- direct microbusiness acquisition learning.

Customer contracts and personal data are not transferable assets beyond their terms. Moving into utilities, insurance, debt, legal claims, or autonomous contract signing is a new regulated/business candidate and requires fresh evaluation.

## Critical Evidence

- UKE documents a large Polish telecom market, high SIM/M2M volume, many providers, and documentary-form subscriber rights.
- Statistics Poland documents more than 2.36 million microenterprises, giving a large but unqualified potential denominator.
- Billshark proves success-fee bill negotiation abroad; Dip proves real-time AI negotiation is technically current.
- Polish telecom advisers show local competition and buyer familiarity, preventing a false “empty market” claim.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Qualifying microbusinesses have enough avoidable telecom spend to support at least 650 PLN average case revenue.
- Providers accept a narrow mandate or user-present workflow.
- Customers prepay 149 PLN, sign a time-boxed exclusive negotiation mandate, and approve a 30% verified-savings fee.
- Ten unrelated firms can form a sufficiently comparable fifty-line batch that ordinary provider sales channels answer without a privileged partnership.
- Supervised case time falls below 45 minutes on mature provider workflows.
- Renewal timing lowers repeat acquisition cost enough to create a durable business.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than ten paid activations/mandates or fifty compatible lines after 300 qualified visits/outreaches.
- Providers block all counsel-reviewed delegated or user-present workflows across the first two supported providers.
- No provider/business-sales channel supplies a comparable response to the first two properly specified line batches.
- Median completed-customer revenue below 550 PLN or contribution below 40% after the first 30 customers.
- Fewer than 50% of paid activations reach a completed provider interaction for reasons within Custobot's control.
- Median mature supervised handling remains above 90 minutes after 100 cases.
- No renewal-watch or repeat purchase among the first 50 successful customers.
- CAC exceeds first-year gross profit after three acquisition tests.
- A provider, comparison site, or adviser offers a trusted equivalent that removes willingness to pay before Custobot's outcome graph improves economics.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Maximum before paid proof: 17,000 PLN.
- Maximum original founder capital in year one: 100,000 PLN, staged.
- Founder: bill/contract ML, agent policy, analytics, direct acquisition, case supervision, about five hours daily.
- Contractors: telecom/privacy counsel and, after paid proof, one part-time Polish case operator.
- No operator partnership, call centre, autonomous signing, external capital, or full-time hire before repeat paid demand.
