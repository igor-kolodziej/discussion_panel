# Zero-to-One Candidate: WardTalk Medical Polish

Candidate generation only — not validated. Research snapshot: 2026-07-23 CEST. Prices, conversion, retention, margins, market size beyond cited populations, and valuation are hypotheses for paid testing.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build the voice-patient practice layer for foreign clinicians and English-track medical students who can study Polish vocabulary but cannot cheaply rehearse the actual high-stakes interaction: taking a history from an impatient, indirect, frightened, colloquial Polish patient and explaining the next step clearly.

## Founder Fit

The founder can combine speech/LLM engineering, behavioural measurement, experiment design, neuroscience, and product analytics. A medical-student partner can expose unrealistic scripts and recruit paid clinical reviewers, but the company does not depend on her university granting access or authority. The initial product is software/content, can be built and sold asynchronously outside corporate hours, and requires no medical-device claim, school procurement, patient data, or clinical delivery.

The weak-fit areas are Polish-as-a-foreign-language pedagogy, examination detail, and clinical content review. They are bounded contractor roles: one qualified teacher, two paid clinicians, and actors or consented synthetic voices. The founder must not present himself as a doctor or official exam authority.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first payer is a non-EU doctor or dentist living in Poland and preparing for a Polish-language route that includes or is followed by real patient communication. A secondary early payer is an English-track medical student entering Polish clinical rotations.

What that person really wants is not “learn Polish.” They want to avoid:

- failing or delaying a consequential language/licensing step;
- freezing when a real patient answers indirectly, uses colloquial language, interrupts, or gives an incomplete chronology;
- appearing clinically unsafe despite knowing the medicine;
- paying repeatedly for scarce one-to-one tutors who may teach grammar but cannot reproduce varied patient behaviour;
- rehearsing with cooperative classmates who reveal information too easily.

Current workarounds are general Polish lessons, vocabulary lists, scripted role-play, classmates, occasional tutors, and generic English-language OSCE simulators. They trade realism and repetition for availability. The commercially meaningful gap is a Polish-speaking simulated patient that behaves like a patient and gives an English debrief tied separately to language and clinical communication.

## Product Wedge

The founding product is a paid eight-week **Polish Patient Interview Pack**:

1. Ten voice cases covering common non-emergency histories and advice conversations.
2. The patient reveals information only after appropriate questions, asks for clarification, misunderstands jargon, and varies pace or emotional tone.
3. A dual rubric scores functional Polish and interview communication; it does not score diagnosis or prescribe care.
4. The learner receives an English explanation of missed information, unsafe ambiguity, confusing wording, and more natural alternatives.
5. A retry changes surface language and patient behaviour while preserving the case objective.
6. A progress map identifies recurring language acts—opening, chronology, symptom description, medication, consent, safety-net wording—not a fake universal “clinical competence” score.

The first version is a browser voice experience with human review of edge cases. It is explicitly independent preparation, not an official examination, university assessment, medical service, or substitute for supervised clinical training.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Generic OSCE tools optimise for scalable case quantity in English. General language apps optimise for vocabulary and everyday dialogue. The user’s costly failure occurs between those categories: medical reasoning must be expressed through local, colloquial patient conversation under time pressure.

Poland's official examination regulation is unusually useful demand evidence because its practical component includes simulated history-taking and advice. The secret is not that AI can role-play a patient; many companies can. It is that small language markets have consequential professional conversations, while global simulation vendors rationally prioritise English and broad exam content. A focused company can acquire the local scripts, rubrics, performance data, and learner distribution before moving one language corridor at a time.

## Underused Momentum

- Voice models now make repeated conversational practice cheap enough for an individual payer.
- Polish law and professional practice make language ability consequential, while the official process remains independent of this product.
- English-language medical programmes create a visible population already in Poland.
- Migration of clinicians across Europe creates repeated language-corridor opportunities.
- Doctors, residents, teachers, and actors can be paid directly for bounded content rights; no institutional partnership is required for the first product.

## Beachhead Market

Start with English-speaking, non-EU doctors and dentists in Poland who are within twelve months of a Polish language/licensing step or first Polish patient-facing work. The product starts with outpatient adult history-taking and advice because the conversational patterns recur and avoid specialised emergency claims.

Exclude paediatric consent, psychiatric crisis, emergency triage, official exam prediction, and clinical decision scoring in the first year. English-track students may buy, but the first positioning is professional Polish patient interaction, not “another OSCE app.”

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Founding eight-week pack: 699 PLN prepaid.
- Mature exam-preparation pack: 1,090–1,490 PLN for twelve weeks.
- Ongoing practice library: 149–199 PLN monthly or 1,290–1,690 PLN annually.
- Later employer/language-school seat bundles: only after direct-user retention, at 900–1,300 PLN per learner-year; the institution never receives raw conversations without a separate lawful basis.

Mature planning assumptions:

- average annual paid learner revenue: 950–1,200 PLN across corridors;
- model/voice compute and storage: 6%–9% of revenue;
- clinician/teacher/performer royalties and content refresh: 8%–12%;
- support, refunds, and payment fees: 5%–8%;
- gross margin: 78%–84%;
- operating margin at scale: 24%–35%, after acquisition, engineering, privacy, and content operations.

A founding 699 PLN pack should retain at least 430 PLN contribution after variable delivery and bounded human review. If a learner requires recurring one-to-one founder or clinician time, the model is a course business and fails.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First ten**

- Build a public list of Polish-language exam queries, foreign-doctor job groups, English-division student societies, expat-clinician forums, and language-school offers.
- Recruit through compliant direct posts, search ads, and one-to-one outreach to people publicly asking about medical Polish. No university endorsement is implied.
- Show one five-minute sample patient and sell the complete pack at 699 PLN. Do not offer free bespoke tutoring.
- Require ten non-refundable payments, not waitlist signups, before producing the full case set.

**First hundred**

- Publish free short “patient did not answer because the question was unnatural” examples optimised for search.
- Offer a transparent referral fee to independent tutors only after direct sales prove conversion; WardTalk keeps billing and the learner relationship.
- Add a second case cluster only when at least 30 users complete four sessions and at least 15 buy more practice.
- Reach 100 cumulative payers through at least two independent channels so one group administrator cannot switch off distribution.

## Primary Control Point

The initial control point is a **conditional Tier 2 content/data/distribution position**, not a regulatory monopoly:

- exclusive three-year product licences for the initial authored cases, rubrics, performed voices, and variation trees;
- direct learner billing and a longitudinal skill baseline that improves with each completed conversation;
- explicit, granular permission to use selected de-identified interaction features for model/rubric improvement; raw audio is not assumed reusable;
- a corridor-specific search/referral position and outcome library.

The strongest copy risk is a global OSCE platform localising into Polish. WardTalk must therefore compound case behaviour, labelled language errors, and distribution faster than an incumbent can translate generic prompts. Content contracts alone do not prevent alternative cases.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

**Resource needed:** a rights-cleared core of realistic cases, language rubrics, voice performance, and paying learner interactions.

**How it is secured**

1. Pay two clinicians and one Polish-language teacher fixed fees to co-author and review ten cases.
2. Contract one or two performers for exclusive product-use rights, synthetic-voice permission if used, revision rights, and clear duration/territory; never use patient recordings.
3. Sell ten founding packs using a single demonstration before commissioning the remaining variations.
4. Use direct user agreements that separate service delivery, optional research/model improvement, retention, and deletion.
5. Within twelve months, secure at least 40 rights-cleared cases, 250 paid learners, and two independently working acquisition channels.

An unknown founder can buy these bounded inputs at market rates and reach users through public online demand. No school, hospital, examination body, or professional chamber must bless the first sale.

**Proof before serious capital**

- ten non-refundable paid packs;
- at least eight users complete four sessions;
- at least five users purchase an extension or annual preorder;
- clinician and language review find no systematic unsafe or unnatural output;
- dialogue features predict recurring error categories well enough to reduce manual review.

## Distribution Advantage

The cold-start mechanism is corridor-specific search and community demand: queries around Polish medical language, the practical exam, Polish patient interview phrases, and clinical rotations. The initial corpus creates examples that general language apps cannot credibly publish.

The distribution moat is built, not assumed:

- corridor-specific SEO pages tied to actual conversation mistakes;
- learner referral loops because a practice partner is not required;
- tutor affiliates with direct billing retained by WardTalk;
- progress portability from student to licensure to first employment;
- later replicated landing/content systems for German, Czech, and other language corridors.

Kill the claimed distribution advantage if customer acquisition remains dependent on one university group, one tutor, or paid ads whose cost exceeds first-year gross profit.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Dominate Polish medical patient dialogue for foreign clinicians.
2. Add dentistry and outpatient specialties only where the same language framework transfers.
3. Package instructor dashboards for independent language schools after direct-user evidence.
4. Replicate the content-rights and rubric pipeline into one larger corridor, likely German medical language, before adding broad CEE coverage.
5. Develop a corridor-agnostic model for identifying whether a clinician's phrasing elicits the needed patient information without pretending to score diagnosis.
6. License the practice engine to relocation and workforce providers only after keeping direct product/data rights.

The advantage is narrow: high-quality behavioural patients in a consequential small language market, with a corpus of how foreign clinicians actually fail and improve. Expansion is earned corridor by corridor; “translate the app” is not an accepted expansion strategy.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish regulation explicitly describes simulated history-taking and advice in the professional language examination, while English medical programmes create a locally reachable population. Polish grammar, colloquial symptom descriptions, forms of address, health-system vocabulary, and patient expectations make direct translation of an English OSCE engine inadequate.

There is no legal barrier preventing a global platform from entering. Defence comes from speed, Polish clinical-language depth, contributor relationships, local search distribution, and longitudinal learner data. The local wedge is credible only if conversion and retention are visibly better than a generic voice model plus tutor.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid conversation can—within the user's selected permissions—produce:

- language act attempted;
- question formulation;
- whether the simulated patient disclosed the intended fact;
- clarification loops;
- speech pace and hesitation features;
- rubric disagreement and human correction;
- retry strategy and subsequent improvement.

This builds advanced conversational AI, behavioural HCI, a proprietary clinical-language error corpus, content-production IP, clinical/educator relationships, and direct distribution. The asset exists because users pay to practise; it is not a speculative data-collection side project.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

In conservative and expected scenarios, 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution is retained while the founder remains employed; any optional founder distribution is at most 5%. In the strong scenario, 100% is reinvested during scale.

Reinvestment order:

1. content rights and medical/language review;
2. speech quality, evaluation, and safety tooling;
3. corridor-specific acquisition content;
4. privacy/security and lower-cost inference;
5. additional corridor teams only after the prior corridor has positive contribution and repeat acquisition.

No scenario adds the value of the dataset separately to company value.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- **Days 1–20:** twenty problem interviews; counsel review of claims, contributor rights, privacy, and user terms; record one demonstration case.
- **Days 21–45:** place the demonstration in public high-intent channels and offer 699 PLN founding packs; require ten payments.
- **Days 46–90:** deliver five cases with bounded human review; measure completion, corrections, support minutes, and willingness to continue.
- **Days 91–130:** complete ten cases, improve automatic error tagging, and sell a second independent cohort.
- **Days 131–180:** reach at least 30 cumulative paying users, 20 four-session completers, ten extension/annual purchases or preorders, and positive contribution excluding founder product-development time.

The founder remains employed and caps work at about five hours daily. Paid proof is a non-refundable pack purchase, not a free pilot, memorandum, school conversation, or waitlist.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- **Tranche 1 — maximum 23,000 PLN before paid proof:** legal/privacy/contributor templates 4k; demonstration content and reviews 6k; voice/product prototype 6k; acquisition tests 4k; contingency 3k.
- **Tranche 2 — cumulative maximum 45,000 PLN:** released after ten payers, completed delivery, acceptable content quality, and positive per-pack contribution; funds remaining cases and product automation.
- **Tranche 3 — cumulative maximum 100,000 PLN:** released only after 100 paying users, repeat/renewal demand, two working acquisition channels, and plausible contribution after CAC.
- Family capital is excluded until multiple independent paying cohorts, repeatable demand, and a clear scaling case.
- Maximum founder cash loss before paid evidence: **23,000 PLN**.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder leaves corporate employment only after:

- at least 500 active or annually prepaid learners across multiple independent cohorts;
- contracted recurring revenue or demonstrated renewal;
- positive contribution after inference, review, support, refunds, and CAC;
- two repeatable acquisition channels, neither exceeding 60% of new sales;
- a proven second corridor or contracted launch;
- at least twelve months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If direct demand fails, stop before building a general medical education platform. Retain only:

- rights-cleared original cases and performed voice assets;
- generic speech-evaluation and consent tooling;
- lawful aggregate error-taxonomy work;
- paid relationships with clinicians, teachers, and performers;
- evidence about which language tasks users will pay to rehearse.

Patient data is never an asset. A pivot into official assessment, university procurement, clinical advice, or another language corridor requires a new evaluation rather than cosmetic repositioning.

## Critical Evidence

- The Polish regulation explicitly includes simulated history-taking and advice in the professional language examination.
- Named Polish medical universities have English-language student populations, creating visible initial users.
- Generic OSCE platforms and virtual patients prove that users understand voice/case practice, while also demonstrating copy risk.
- All first-product inputs can be purchased directly; no speculative institutional partnership is required.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Medical-conversation specificity supports a 699 PLN founding purchase and annual value near 950 PLN.
- Behaviourally realistic Polish patients produce repeat use rather than novelty.
- Corridor-specific data and content improve outcomes fast enough to outrun global OSCE localisation.
- Contributor licences and optional learner-data rights are accepted on commercially useful terms.
- Four European corridors can collectively support 8,000 annual paid learners without privileged distribution.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than ten paid founding packs after 150 qualified visits or compliant direct outreaches.
- Fewer than 60% of paid users complete four sessions.
- Fewer than 25% of completers buy an extension, subscription, or annual preorder.
- More than 15 minutes of human review/support per routine session after the second cohort.
- Clinician/teacher review finds recurrent clinically unsafe or linguistically unnatural output that cannot be controlled.
- Paid acquisition plus support exceeds first-year gross profit after three channel tests.
- A second corridor cannot obtain ten paid deposits within twelve months without an institutional contract.
- A generic OSCE competitor matches Polish realism and acquisition economics before WardTalk establishes retention.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Pre-proof maximum: 23,000 PLN.
- First-year founder-capital maximum: 100,000 PLN, released in stages.
- Founder: product, speech/LLM, analytics, direct acquisition, about five hours daily.
- Contractors: one Polish-language teacher, two clinicians, one or two performers, fixed-fee privacy/IP counsel, and limited frontend/audio engineering if needed.
- No full-time hire, university contract, medical-device work, or proprietary hardware before repeat paid demand.
