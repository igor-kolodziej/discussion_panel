# Zero-to-One Candidate: Lab Analyzer Freedom Lease

Frozen: 2026-08-21 CEST. Candidate generation only — not validated. Every asset price, test volume, reagent margin, conversion, uptime cost, customer count, and multiple is a hypothesis unless explicitly sourced.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Independent Polish diagnostic laboratories prepay a conditional deployment deposit and sign a minimum-test lease for one serial-specific, unmodified, compliant used/open analyser with verified reagents, maintenance, and swap service, giving the venture title-based routing control without becoming the device manufacturer.

## Founder Fit

The founder's data, neuroscience, and financial skills fit assay-volume modelling, quality evidence, failure analysis, and asset economics. An IVDR regulatory specialist, laboratory diagnostician, biomedical service engineer, and asset-finance partner are mandatory. The founder does not interpret results or modify intended purpose.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first customer is one independent Polish diagnostic laboratory outside the major chains, with a current chemistry or immunoassay placement contract ending within six months, a stable test menu and volume, and concern about vendor lock-in, reagent pricing, downtime, or replacement flexibility.

Current workarounds are OEM reagent-rental/placement contracts, purchasing a new or used analyser, public procurement, distributor service, or manual backup routing. These are strong substitutes; the exact customer must prefer an independently verified open/used serial rather than simply renegotiate with the incumbent.

## Product Wedge

The first product covers one exact unmodified analyser serial and one defined test menu:

- PLN20k conditional deployment deposit, credited to the first contractual minimum;
- complete CE/declaration-of-conformity, UDI, instructions, history, seller, intended-use, and transport evidence;
- verified compatible reagent, calibrator, control, and quality-control route;
- documented preventive and corrective maintenance;
- swap or external-test-routing SLA with explicit exclusions;
- four-year minimum-test or minimum-monthly-payment lease.

The venture buys the serial below PLN100k only after the customer contract and evidence conditions are satisfied.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

OEMs validate that analyser placement plus recurring reagents/service is commercially durable. A used compliant open system could lower installed asset cost and diversify supply while retaining recurring routing economics. The gap is unproved: incumbents already bundle financing, service, reagents, and replacements, and “open” does not mean every reagent is valid for every intended use.

## Underused Momentum

IVDR makes serial-level compliance, intended purpose, distributor duties, and modification boundaries explicit. A narrow unmodified-device lease can use existing conformity rather than invent a new diagnostic product. The law supplies a boundary, not a demand mandate or access right.

## Beachhead Market

Initial filter:

- independent Polish general diagnostic laboratory, excluding Diagnostyka, ALAB, Synevo, and hospital systems;
- one chemistry or immunoassay analyser placement ending within six months;
- documented monthly volume and bounded test menu;
- qualified laboratory diagnostician and existing external-quality process;
- service engineer and lawful compatible consumable route available for the exact serial;
- willingness to sign a four-year minimum and pay PLN20k before asset purchase.

KIDL lists 2,554 sites and 1,851 exact entity strings. A deliberately loose filter leaves at most 613 sites/518 normalised organisations; it is not a qualifying buyer denominator.

## Incentive and Power Map

- Loss bearer: laboratory when testing stops, quality fails, or reagent cost rises.
- Payer: laboratory owner, director, or procurement lead.
- Beneficiary: laboratory, clinicians, patients, and referring customers.
- Authority holder: laboratory for clinical use; manufacturer and applicable conformity evidence constrain intended use.
- Distributor: venture directly; later laboratory consultants or service engineers under disclosed referral terms.
- Information holder: seller, manufacturer, laboratory, reagent supplier, and service engineer.
- Risk bearer: venture for title, scheduled service, and capped swap/routing obligations; laboratory retains clinical decisions.
- Transaction owner: venture for the lease and approved supply route; laboratory for test validation and reporting.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Hypothetical mature annual revenue per installed analyser: PLN100k–140k from minimum payments and gross margin on verified reagents/service. Expected-scale economics assume:

- gross asset acquisition below PLN100k per serial;
- external lease/asset finance after the first repeat proof;
- annual fully loaded gross margin of 35%–40% after reagent cost, service, quality controls, asset-finance charge, swaps, and bad debt;
- customer minimum term of four years.

No public source supports the acquisition price or PLN140k annual revenue. One hundred placements imply about PLN10m gross asset capital before swap stock.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten: map genuinely independent KIDL entities, identify an expiring placement and exact menu, then source only one matching serial. Do not buy speculative inventory. Each customer supplies actual volume and current total cost before a proposal.

First hundred: expand only after three serials repeat the same regulatory dossier, reagent menu, service route, and contribution. Use external asset finance and a small swap pool; no founder-funded fleet. Chain or hospital tenders are a separate channel and excluded from the base case.

## Rule and Constraint Classification

- LAW / BINDING RULE — verified: IVDR distributor checks apply to compliant used devices placed on the market.
- LAW / BINDING RULE — verified: own-branding, intended-purpose change, material modification, or full refurbishment can make the operator a manufacturer.
- TECHNICAL LIMITATION — verified in principle: each reagent/calibrator/control has its own intended-use and performance conditions.
- TECHNICAL LIMITATION — unknown: an exact serial has supportable history, parts, service, and quality performance.
- ECONOMIC LIMITATION — unknown: sub-PLN100k acquisition and PLN100k–140k recurring revenue.
- ECONOMIC LIMITATION — unknown: 100 financeable independent placements.

## Lawful-Structure Check

The venture distributes and leases an unmodified compliant serial under the manufacturer's intended purpose. It does not relabel, change intended purpose, materially modify, fully refurbish, or make clinical claims. The laboratory remains responsible for clinical validation and result reporting. Regulatory counsel must approve sourcing, import/distribution status, registration, vigilance, traceability, transport, data processing, service, and exact reagent route before purchase.

## Constraint-Circumvention Attempts

- Serial-specific diligence avoids assuming all used analysers are equivalent.
- Customer payment and contract precede purchase.
- Unmodified intended-purpose use avoids voluntary manufacturer status.
- Verified third-party service and reagents precede the offer.
- Minimum volume and external finance match asset cash flow.
- Capped swap or external-test routing avoids an unlimited uptime promise.

The remaining failure is incumbent control of parts, consumables, software keys, and customer trust.

## Primary Control Point

The intended control point is title to one compliant serial combined with a four-year minimum-volume lease and contractually exclusive routing of the verified consumables and maintenance for that installation. Current control is Tier 3 until title, conformity evidence, and one paid minimum contract align. It could become Tier 2 with a financeable multi-serial fleet; it is never control over clinical authority.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Hold no analyser inventory before a paid conditional contract. Pre-source one candidate serial and documentary pack; reserve the biomedical engineer; obtain a PLN20k customer deposit; complete counsel and technical conditions; then acquire below PLN100k using founder cash only for the first unit and only after all conditions. Customer funds installation-site preparation.

## Distribution Advantage

The KIDL registry enables targeted discovery and the founder can expose whole-life cost rather than quote only reagent price. There is no proprietary channel. OEMs, distributors, laboratory consultants, and existing service engineers begin with greater trust and installed access.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

If repeated, the venture can accumulate serial-level failure data, validated menu economics, seller quality scores, maintenance parts forecasts, and finance-underwriting evidence. Expansion is to the same analyser family and adjacent menu only after quality proof. A used-equipment listing or generic reagent catalogue is not an advantage.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish laboratory relationships, local service coverage, KIDL segmentation, Polish documentation, and EU IVDR diligence aid entry. OEM platforms and device rules are European/global; local adaptation does not prevent a well-funded distributor from copying the model.

## Compounding Advantage

Potential compounding assets are verified serial provenance, actual uptime and service-cost curves, menu-specific reagent performance, residual-value data, and lender confidence. They compound only if repeat deployments lower diligence and financing cost without increasing manufacturer obligations.

## Founder Wealth Model

Adverse: documentary or customer conditions fail before purchase; stop after at most PLN30k founder loss. Never acquire the analyser without the paid contract.

Conservative year-seven hypothesis: 20 analysers at PLN100k annual revenue = PLN2.0m revenue; 15% EBITDA margin gives PLN0.30m EBITDA. At 3x and 70% founder ownership, founder equity is about PLN0.63m.

Expected-success year-seven hypothesis: 100 analysers at PLN140k = PLN14m revenue; 22% EBITDA margin after asset-finance cost gives about PLN3.08m EBITDA. At 3.5x and 70% ownership, founder equity is about PLN7.55m, assuming the multiple is cash-free/debt-free and no unmodelled fleet debt remains.

Strong hypothesis: 200 analysers at PLN160k, 25% EBITDA, 60% ownership, and 4x value. It requires institutional asset finance, multi-city service, swap inventory, clinical trust, and repeatable lawful supply.

The conservative case does not reach PLN5m. Expected wealth is highly sensitive to external finance, dilution, equipment debt, test volume, reagent margin, and customer concentration.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

While employed, reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash and distribute no more than 5%. Customer deposits and asset debt are not counted as founder equity or counted twice in terminal value. No family capital is used before a delivered first deployment and paid reorder evidence. Fleet growth after the first repeat must use matched external asset finance.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: regulatory counsel fixes the permissible device/reagent boundary; identify 25 independent labs with expiring placements and three candidate serials.

Days 31–90: obtain full evidence and service quotes for one serial; complete five qualified customer interviews and one whole-life-cost proposal.

Days 91–150: one laboratory signs the four-year minimum and pays the PLN20k conditional deployment deposit before purchase.

By day 180: all conditions either clear and the exact serial is acquired/deployed, or the contract is unwound and the candidate killed. An interview, letter of intent, generic equipment quote, or refundable expression of interest does not count.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Pre-paid-proof founder maximum: PLN40k; target at-risk loss: PLN30k.
- PLN8k regulatory review, PLN6k biomedical inspection, PLN5k laboratory/quality advice, PLN5k acquisition and travel, PLN6k contingency.
- Asset purchase below PLN100k only after customer deposit and evidence; total founder capital remains within PLN100k.
- No second analyser from founder cash; external asset finance is mandatory.

## Critical Dependency Map

1. One laboratory has an expiring placement and measurable volume.
2. One exact used serial has complete lawful provenance and remaining life.
3. Compatible reagents, calibrators, controls, software, parts, and service remain available.
4. The customer accepts the menu and minimum term.
5. PLN20k is paid before purchase.
6. The asset can be acquired below PLN100k and financed thereafter.
7. Uptime/swap costs preserve recurring margin.
8. The structure does not make the venture a manufacturer.

Dependencies 1–6 must align for the first proof and are controlled by different parties.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Do not leave employment until at least five unrelated paid placements operate for six months, three customers reorder consumables, fully loaded gross margin exceeds 35%, one lender finances repeat units without personal guarantees beyond a bounded amount, swap/service coverage works without the founder, no vigilance issue is unresolved, and personal runway exceeds 12 months.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If the first deployment fails, do not buy another analyser. Reusable assets are the serial-diligence checklist, independent-lab map, service/vendor evidence, and whole-life-cost model. Equipment brokerage, regulatory consulting, or a one-off resale is not confirmation.

## Critical Evidence

- IVDR permits distribution of a compliant unmodified device but makes modifications and full refurbishment consequential.
- Open analyser systems exist, subject to reagent-specific intended use and performance.
- KIDL provides a large upper-bound registry, not the reachable independent buyer count.
- Sysmex and Roche validate incumbent placement economics and formidable direct competition.
- No public evidence supports a sub-PLN100k serial, PLN140k annual unit revenue, or 100 financeable placements.

## Critical Hypotheses

- One laboratory will pay PLN20k and sign a four-year minimum before purchase.
- One exact serial passes every conformity, history, service, software, and reagent check.
- The venture can obtain title below PLN100k without manufacturer status.
- Annual gross margin exceeds 35% after financing, service, QC, swaps, and losses.
- Independent labs value openness enough to switch from incumbent placement.
- External finance supports 100 units without wealth-destroying dilution or net debt.

## Kill Criteria

- No qualifying paid conditional contract by day 180.
- The device requires relabelling, intended-purpose change, material modification, or full refurbishment.
- Seller cannot prove serial-level conformity, provenance, support, and software rights.
- No validated reagent/service route exists for the customer's exact menu.
- Customer will not sign minimum volume before purchase.
- First-unit gross asset cost exceeds PLN100k or needs founder capital beyond the ceiling.
- Expected founder wealth disappears after real finance, debt, swap, and dilution terms.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

POC: founder at most five hours daily; IVDR regulatory counsel; laboratory diagnostician; biomedical service engineer; seller and reagent supplier. At scale: full-time quality/regulatory lead, service network, clinical applications support, supply-chain lead, asset-finance partner, account management, and swap inventory.
