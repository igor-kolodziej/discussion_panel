# Zero-to-One Candidate: Cloud Exit Bond

Frozen: 2026-08-21 CEST. Candidate generation only — not validated. Every price, conversion, margin, workload cost, service level, customer count, and multiple is a hypothesis unless explicitly sourced.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Polish mid-market B2B SaaS and regulated-service companies pay for a scoped annual cloud-exit option that maintains customer-owned exports, executable replacement infrastructure, and restoration drills for one revenue-critical workload, with the venture owing only predefined remedial labour and capped service credits—not cash indemnity for business loss.

## Founder Fit

The founder can model dependencies, automate data checks, coordinate cloud specialists, sell evidence-based continuity, and manage a staged proof within five hours daily. A senior cloud/SRE contractor and security counsel are required for delivery. The founder does not promise to personally execute emergency migrations around the clock.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first customer is a Polish B2B SaaS or regulated-service company with one revenue-critical PostgreSQL-and-container workload on AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud, at least one enterprise buyer/lender continuity request, and no independently restored exit environment.

The customer can obtain exports and migration tools, but an export is not a working system. Proprietary managed services, identity, networking, queues, secrets, infrastructure definitions, data consistency, and destination differences can make the nominal exit unusable. Current workarounds are backups, architecture documents, disaster recovery inside the same provider, professional-services projects, or an untested migration plan.

## Product Wedge

The first paid product is a PLN20,000 fixed-scope Exit Drill for one named workload:

- customer-controlled export destination;
- dependency and excluded-provider-IP inventory;
- executable replacement infrastructure for the agreed open components;
- data restore and integrity checks;
- application start and synthetic transaction;
- measured RTO/RPO and unresolved dependency list;
- a 12-month option proposal with one scheduled repeat drill.

The venture never promises functional equivalence for source-only managed features.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Hyperscalers increasingly provide free or formal exit-data transfer. The scarce event is not downloading bytes; it is independently proving that the customer's defined service can be restored to stated acceptance criteria. A paid drill can expose that gap before a migration, acquisition, lender review, enterprise renewal, or provider incident.

## Underused Momentum

Data Act Articles 23–30 require switching cooperation and eliminate provider switching charges from 12 January 2027, while preserving limits around provider IP and customer transformation. This makes export rights more usable but does not perform application conversion. The rule is momentum, not a guarantee of demand.

## Beachhead Market

Initial filter:

- Poland-based B2B SaaS or regulated-service operator;
- roughly PLN4m–80m annual revenue;
- one clearly isolated business-critical workload using PostgreSQL plus Kubernetes/containers;
- no mainframe, SAP, clinical production, payment-core, or multi-region active-active scope;
- customer owns application source and has authority over data and destination;
- a current buyer, lender, insurer, acquisition, renewal, or board continuity event;
- willingness to pay PLN20k for the first drill.

No public source proves the filtered denominator. Ten Polish public-cloud customer references establish examples, not a market count.

## Incentive and Power Map

- Loss bearer: cloud customer if exit or restoration fails.
- Payer: CTO/CIO/risk or enterprise-revenue owner.
- Beneficiary: customer, lender, enterprise buyers, and users.
- Authority holder: customer under its contracts and applicable Data Act rights; provider for protected assets.
- Distributor: direct founder outreach, later security/cloud advisers only after paid referral terms.
- Information holder: provider and customer engineering team.
- Risk bearer: venture for predefined drill/remedial labour and capped service credits; customer for business interruption and excluded dependencies.
- Transaction owner: venture for the drill/option; customer retains its data and production decisions.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Hypothetical pricing:

- PLN20k first drill;
- PLN120k–180k annual option for a bounded workload, including one scheduled drill, monthly export-verification checks, artefact maintenance, and up to a fixed number of remedial engineering hours;
- additional migration execution priced separately and never assumed in the wealth case.

Hypothesized mature gross margin is 55% after specialist time, test infrastructure, security review, and service-credit reserve. No public comparable supports the annual price.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten: founder maps public cloud case studies, Polish SaaS directories, procurement notices, lender/insurance continuity events, and enterprise-vendor security requests; offers only the one-workload drill. No channel is assumed.

First hundred: only after three repeatable drills, create provider/workload templates and obtain paid referral agreements from cloud-security advisers, boutique MSPs, and M&A technical-diligence firms. A customer requiring bespoke architecture beyond the template is rejected or separately priced.

## Rule and Constraint Classification

- LAW / BINDING RULE — verified: Data Act switching cooperation and future zero provider switching charge.
- LAW / BINDING RULE — counsel-required: exact insurance perimeter of an annual performance option and service credits.
- TECHNICAL LIMITATION — verified in principle: provider IP/trade-secret assets and source-only managed services need not become portable.
- TECHNICAL LIMITATION — unknown: one bounded workload can be restored to useful acceptance criteria inside the delivery budget.
- ECONOMIC LIMITATION — unknown: PLN20k drill and PLN120k–180k annual willingness to pay.
- ECONOMIC LIMITATION — unknown: 60 qualifying recurring customers and 32% operating margin.

## Lawful-Structure Check

The contract covers defined engineering work and service credits, not cash compensation for outage, regulatory fines, lost revenue, or an uncertain external event. Customer owns exports and destination accounts. The venture does not copy provider IP, defeat security, use employer data, or misrepresent statutory portability as functional equivalence. Counsel must approve the exact contract, security allocation, data processing, limitation of liability, and insurance boundary.

## Constraint-Circumvention Attempts

- Customer-controlled export avoids venture custody of production data as an asset.
- One bounded PostgreSQL/container workload avoids an all-estate migration promise.
- Scheduled drills, not emergency-only delivery, create observable performance.
- Fixed remedial hours/service credits avoid uncapped risk.
- Contractors perform specialist work only after customer payment.

The remaining circumvention failure is customer-specific complexity: too many unique dependencies can make labour scale one-for-one.

## Primary Control Point

The control point is a non-cancellable annual option for one exact workload plus recurring authority to verify customer-owned exports and maintain the executable restore package. Current control is Tier 3 until a paid annual contract and successful restore exist; it could become Tier 2 for repeated templates. It is not exclusive statutory data ownership.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Build no platform. Spend on:

- contract/security review;
- one senior cloud/SRE contractor;
- isolated test infrastructure;
- direct acquisition and a secure evidence room.

Sell the PLN20k drill before committing most contractor time. The customer funds destination resources and dual running.

## Distribution Advantage

The founder can translate technical failure into financial continuity evidence and target public cloud users without status-based distribution. There is no demonstrated proprietary channel. Hyperscalers, MSPs, destination providers, and internal SRE teams begin closer to customers.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

If three drills share a workload pattern, the venture can accumulate tested conversion modules, dependency taxonomies, restore checks, and accurate effort estimates. Expansion moves from PostgreSQL/container workloads to adjacent open managed services only after independent proof. The option contract and customer-specific artefacts create switching friction but not a monopoly.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish contracts, EU switching rules, local security expectations, Polish-language board evidence, and lower specialist cost may help. The technical workload is globally replicable; Poland is not a durable moat.

## Compounding Advantage

Potential compounding assets are tested restore modules, failure-pattern data, acceptance templates, and risk-adjusted pricing by workload. They compound only if customer confidentiality permits non-reconstructable reuse and bespoke labour falls as volume grows.

## Founder Wealth Model

Adverse: no paid drill after 40 qualified approaches; stop after ≤PLN25k loss.

Conservative year seven hypothesis: 35 annual-option customers at PLN120k plus 15 drills at PLN20k = PLN4.5m revenue; 50% gross margin and PLN2.0m staff/overhead produce PLN0.25m EBITDA. At 3x and 75% founder ownership, founder equity is about PLN0.56m.

Expected-success year seven hypothesis: 60 customers at PLN160k = PLN9.6m revenue; 55% gross margin and PLN2.21m staff/overhead produce about PLN3.07m EBITDA. At 4x and 65% founder ownership, founder equity is about PLN7.98m.

Strong hypothesis: 100 customers at PLN180k plus drills, 35% operating margin, 55% founder ownership, 5x value. This requires a professional 24/7-capable engineering organisation and is not proof.

The conservative case does not reach PLN5m. The expected case is sensitive to price, specialist utilisation, liability cost, and dilution.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

While employed, 95% of after-tax distributable cash is reinvested and distributions stay at or below 5%. Retained cash, service-credit reserves, and customer prepayments are not added again to terminal equity. Scaling is funded by customer prepayment first; no family capital is used before repeat paid demand.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: counsel review, one demonstrator on founder-owned synthetic workload, 40 exact targets.

Days 31–90: eight decision-maker calls, three scoped proposals.

Days 91–150: collect one PLN20k payment and execute the drill with one named contractor.

By day 180: customer signs a paid annual option or explicitly rejects it; obtain full delivery cost and acceptance evidence. Interest, unpaid pilot, or free restore does not count.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Pre-paid-proof maximum: PLN40k.
- Planned at risk: PLN8k legal/security, PLN5k synthetic test environment, PLN7k targeted acquisition, PLN15k conditional contractor reservation, PLN5k contingency.
- Post-payment work is funded from customer cash; the customer pays destination cloud costs.
- Twelve-month founder-capital ceiling: PLN100k.

## Critical Dependency Map

1. One customer has a genuinely bounded workload.
2. Customer can lawfully provide all source/data/credentials.
3. One senior contractor can reproduce the restore.
4. Destination accepts the workload and test criteria.
5. Customer pays for the drill and then annual option.
6. Exact warranty remains a service contract.
7. Reuse/templates reduce future labour.

Dependencies 1–5 must align for the first proof, making reachability uncertain.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Do not leave employment until at least five unrelated annual options are prepaid, two repeat drills meet acceptance, fully loaded gross margin exceeds 50%, customer acquisition repeats, contractor coverage handles incidents without the founder, liability is insured/bounded, and personal runway exceeds 12 months.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If annual options fail, stop risk-taking. Reusable assets are the synthetic demonstrator, restore scripts that contain no customer IP, contract learning, and a possible fixed-fee drill service. A profitable consultancy is not counted as confirmation.

## Critical Evidence

- Data Act switching rule and provider exit programs are real.
- Provider egress can be free while application conversion remains customer work.
- Ten public Polish cloud references exist, but no filtered denominator or price evidence exists.
- No observed comparable supports the annual option.

## Critical Hypotheses

- A customer will pay PLN20k for one drill.
- It will then prepay PLN120k–180k annually.
- One workload can be bounded and restored inside gross-margin limits.
- Service credits remain outside insurance.
- 60 qualifying buyers are reachable without heroic share.
- Templates reduce labour enough for 32% operating margin.

## Kill Criteria

- No PLN20k payment by day 180.
- Customer will buy only generic consulting or a one-off migration.
- Restore cannot meet agreed criteria without >PLN25k unplanned contractor cost.
- Annual option is classified as insurance or requires uncapped liability.
- Customer rejects recurring export authority.
- Three completed drills share too little reusable structure.
- No evidence supports the expected-case customer/price denominator.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

POC: founder ≤5 hours/day; one senior cloud/SRE contractor; cloud/security counsel; optional data-protection review. At scale: full-time technical lead, SRE bench, security lead, account lead, incident coverage, insurance, and audited controls.
