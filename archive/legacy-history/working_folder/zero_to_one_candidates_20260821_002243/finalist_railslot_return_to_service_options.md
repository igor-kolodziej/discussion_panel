# Zero-to-One Candidate: RailSlot Return-to-Service Options

Frozen: 2026-08-21 CEST. Candidate generation only — not validated. Every price, conversion, margin, slot count, intervention rate, customer count, and multiple is a hypothesis unless explicitly sourced.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Small independent Polish freight-wagon keepers and lessors pay a non-refundable option over a named maintenance-workshop date and work package, documented in a tri-party keeper–ECM–workshop contract, while the venture controls only the commercial reservation and never assumes ECM, release-to-service, or return-to-operation responsibility.

## Founder Fit

The founder can analyse fleet and register data, price queue risk, coordinate contracts, and build a disciplined option ledger within five hours daily. Rail-maintenance engineering, safety, and contract decisions require an independent ECM adviser and Polish rail counsel. The founder does not inspect wagons or make safety decisions.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first customer is a Poland-based independent keeper or lessor with roughly 20–200 freight wagons, one exact wagon due for a scheduled revision or bounded repair within 90 days, a selected ECM, and an economically material risk from an uncertain workshop date.

Current workarounds are bilateral workshop booking, flexible fleet planning, replacement wagons, larger lessors' internal maintenance networks, and accepting delay. Public registers prove many wagons and actors, not that an independent payer lacks access or values a priority right.

## Product Wedge

The first paid product is a PLN12,000 non-refundable priority option for:

- one identified wagon;
- one named workshop and date window;
- one predefined work package and intake requirements;
- explicit arrival, cancellation, excess-work, substitution, and refund terms;
- recognition by the keeper, its ECM, and the maintenance-delivery workshop.

The underlying maintenance is paid directly by the keeper or transparently passed through. The option does not guarantee technical release, completion after hidden damage, or network availability.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Small keepers may bear outage and replacement cost but lack the scheduling scale of large lessors. A commercially firm, wagon-specific capacity right could convert an informal promise into a priced option. No public source establishes that workshops will sell such rights, customers will pay PLN12k, or the option can be reassigned.

## Underused Momentum

Mandatory ECM structures make roles and accountability explicit, while public keeper and ECM registers make targeted discovery possible. That transparency could support a precise tri-party reservation, but the rule itself creates no transferable slot and leaves safety accountability with regulated actors.

## Beachhead Market

Initial filter:

- independent Polish freight-wagon keeper or lessor, not a vertically integrated large fleet;
- 20–200 wagons as a prospecting hypothesis, not a verified segment;
- one known revision/repair due within 90 days;
- selected ECM and exact maintenance-delivery workshop;
- measurable replacement-wagon, demurrage, or lost-hire exposure;
- willingness to prepay PLN12k before the venture owes the full workshop reservation.

ERA reports 71,818 Polish freight wagons and 142 ECM-certified organisations; a VKM extraction found 453 Polish keeper names. None is a qualifying buyer denominator.

## Incentive and Power Map

- Loss bearer: keeper/lessor when a wagon remains unavailable.
- Payer: fleet owner, asset manager, or operations director.
- Beneficiary: keeper, lessee, shipper, and network customers.
- Authority holder: ECM for return-to-operation responsibility; maintenance-delivery function for release to service; workshop for capacity.
- Distributor: founder direct outreach; later ECM/workshop referrals only under disclosed terms.
- Information holder: keeper, ECM, and workshop.
- Risk bearer: venture for the commercial reservation amount only; regulated parties retain safety decisions.
- Transaction owner: venture for the option, workshop for maintenance, ECM for the safety process.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Hypothetical economics per exercised or expired option:

- customer option fee: PLN12k, prepaid and non-refundable after conditions are satisfied;
- workshop capacity reservation: PLN4k–6k only after customer cash;
- legal, coordination, and service cost: PLN2k–3k at repeat scale;
- target contribution: PLN3k–6k before central overhead.

Maintenance, parts, transport, replacement wagon, and excess work are excluded or separately prepaid. No public slot price or margin validates these figures.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten: identify exact keeper–ECM pairs from public registers; ask only about a named intervention in the next 90 days; secure a workshop term sheet before offering the option. No broad fleet subscription is sold.

First hundred: add a second workshop only after one slot is delivered and three keepers sign the same allocation terms. Scale through a calendar of workshop-specific dates, not a generic marketplace. Large lessors with their own scheduling networks are excluded from the acquisition thesis.

## Rule and Constraint Classification

- LAW / BINDING RULE — verified: ECM certification and defined maintenance functions are mandatory.
- LAW / BINDING RULE — verified: outsourced delivery does not transfer the ECM's return-to-operation responsibility.
- CONTRACTUAL LIMITATION — unknown: workshop will make a firm, recognised, assignable or substitutable reservation.
- TECHNICAL LIMITATION — unknown: the work package can be bounded before intake.
- ECONOMIC LIMITATION — unknown: PLN12k willingness to pay and repeat option frequency.
- ECONOMIC LIMITATION — unknown: 500 annual options without concentrated-customer or workshop power.

## Lawful-Structure Check

The venture sells a commercial reservation, not maintenance certification or a safety outcome. The selected ECM remains responsible for return to operation and the maintenance-delivery party issues release to service. Rail counsel must approve the tri-party allocation, payment flow, consumer-credit/insurance perimeter, VAT, liability, and any substitution. The venture never represents a reserved date as regulatory approval.

## Constraint-Circumvention Attempts

- One named wagon and work package avoid selling abstract capacity.
- Customer cash precedes the venture's workshop deposit.
- Tri-party recognition prevents a private side letter from conflicting with ECM control.
- Late-arrival and hidden-damage rules bound workshop exposure.
- Direct payment of maintenance avoids financing the overhaul.

The remaining failure is workshop power: a workshop can contract directly or refuse firm commercial rights.

## Primary Control Point

The intended control point is a take-or-pay workshop reservation recognised in the tri-party contract, with a defined customer substitution right where lawful. Current control is Tier 3 until one workshop accepts the exact instrument. It could become Tier 2 only through a repeat calendar across at least two workshops; no statutory exclusivity exists.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Build no marketplace and hold no speculative slot inventory. Obtain one workshop term sheet, one ECM-approved contract, and one customer option payment. Use counsel and an ECM adviser conditionally. The founder never deposits more than customer cash plus the capped proof budget.

## Distribution Advantage

Registers make the first keeper–ECM pairs identifiable and the founder can quantify downtime. There is no demonstrated proprietary channel. Workshops, ECMs, lessors, and replacement-wagon providers already have direct relationships and can route around the venture.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

If repeated, a dated reservation ledger, intervention-duration data, standard excess-scope terms, and cross-workshop substitution may reduce uncertainty. Expansion is to additional bounded wagon work packages and adjacent workshops only after firm contracts. A lead list or booking interface alone is not an advantage.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish-language contracts, local workshops, VKM/ECM mapping, and domestic operational knowledge aid entry. The ECM framework is European and large lessors operate cross-border networks, so Poland is a beachhead rather than a moat.

## Compounding Advantage

Potential compounding assets are actual workshop lead-time distributions, late-arrival and excess-work frequencies, keeper payment behaviour, and exercised/expired option pricing. These compound only if the venture controls recurring reservations rather than merely brokering introductions.

## Founder Wealth Model

Adverse: no workshop signs firm terms or no customer prepays; stop after at most PLN25k loss.

Conservative year-seven hypothesis: 120 annual options at PLN12k = PLN1.44m revenue; 25% EBITDA margin gives PLN0.36m EBITDA. At 3x and 80% founder ownership, founder equity is about PLN0.86m.

Expected-success year-seven hypothesis: 500 annual options at PLN12k = PLN6.0m revenue; 33% EBITDA margin gives about PLN2.0m EBITDA. At 4x and 80% founder ownership, founder equity is about PLN6.4m.

Strong hypothesis: 1,000 annual options at PLN14k, 35% EBITDA margin, 70% ownership, and 5x value. This requires several workshops, operational staff, enforceable recurring capacity, and no safety-risk leakage.

The conservative case does not reach PLN5m. The expected case assumes an unproved 500 annual transactions and durable workshop cooperation.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

While employed, reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash and distribute no more than 5%. Customer prepayments fund reservations; they are not counted again in terminal value. No family money is used before one delivered paid option and two repeat orders.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: counsel and ECM adviser define the exact non-safety instrument; map 30 keeper–ECM pairs and five workshops.

Days 31–90: obtain one workshop term sheet for a named date/work package and three customer conversations tied to actual wagons.

Days 91–150: one customer prepays PLN12k before the venture pays the full shop deposit; all three parties sign late-arrival, excess-work, substitution, and responsibility terms.

By day 180: the reservation is exercised, expires under its terms, or is killed. A letter of interest, workshop conversation, or ordinary maintenance booking does not count.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Pre-paid-proof maximum: PLN40k; planned loss ceiling: PLN25k.
- PLN10k rail/legal review, PLN6k ECM engineering, PLN5k travel/acquisition, PLN4k document/insurance review.
- Workshop deposit only after the customer's PLN12k payment, except a separately approved de minimis hold inside the loss ceiling.
- Twelve-month founder-capital ceiling: PLN100k.

## Critical Dependency Map

1. A keeper has an exact painful scheduling event.
2. Its ECM accepts the commercial structure without transferring safety duty.
3. A workshop sells a firm bounded reservation.
4. Customer payment precedes material workshop cash.
5. Hidden damage and late arrival do not destroy the option economics.
6. The right can be repeated or substituted across customers.
7. Enough small keepers exist outside incumbent networks.

The first proof requires dependencies 1–4 to align in one 180-day window.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Do not leave employment until at least 30 paid options have been delivered or expired under identical economics, two workshops provide recurring calendars, three unrelated keepers reorder, trailing contribution covers a full-time operator and ECM adviser, safety/liability boundaries remain intact, and personal runway exceeds 12 months.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If firm reservations fail, stop buying or holding slots. Reusable assets are the keeper–ECM map, non-confidential schedule data, contract learning, and a possible analytics service. Ordinary maintenance brokerage or consulting is not counted as confirmation.

## Critical Evidence

- ECM roles and accountability are real and allow outsourced delivery.
- Poland has a material wagon fleet and identifiable keepers/ECMs.
- Large lessors already coordinate large workshop networks and replacement assets.
- No public evidence establishes slot pricing, assignability, or small-keeper willingness to pay.

## Critical Hypotheses

- A workshop will sign a take-or-pay, customer-recognised reservation.
- A keeper will pay PLN12k before the workshop deposit.
- One pre-intake work package can be sufficiently bounded.
- The venture can retain PLN3k–6k contribution per option without safety duty.
- Three keepers and two workshops can repeat the same instrument within 12 months.
- 500 annual options are reachable without heroic market share.

## Kill Criteria

- No firm workshop term sheet by day 90.
- No PLN12k customer payment by day 180.
- ECM or counsel says the venture assumes maintenance or safety responsibility.
- Workshop requires speculative deposits beyond the founder ceiling.
- Ordinary direct booking solves the same event without the option fee.
- Three attempts require materially different contracts/work packages.
- Expected founder wealth depends on the raw wagon or VKM count as a buyer denominator.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

POC: founder at most five hours daily; Polish rail counsel; independent ECM/maintenance engineer; part-time commercial coordinator only after payment. At scale: full-time slot operator, workshop account lead, rail counsel, finance/collections, and sufficient reserves—never an unlicensed safety function.
