# Simulated Score Round 4: StairLift Salvage & Rapid Refit Rail

## Candidate Tested

**StairLift Salvage & Rapid Refit Rail** - acquire unwanted stairlifts from estates, post-care moves, and apartment cleanouts through a 48-hour removal offer; reserve one qualified installer team; build a small Warsaw inventory of verified used straight-rail stairlifts and reusable components; sell 5-10 day lower-cost installations to 50-60 adult children and pre-elderly homeowners facing fall/discharge pressure, with optional PFRON barrier-removal paperwork support.

## Result

Simulated Zero To One score: **74/100**.

## Reviewer Diagnosis

The control point is more concrete than the earlier clinic workflow ideas: possession of used units, removal rights, and installer capacity can create a local speed/price wedge. The reviewer classified it as Tier 2 local operational/supply control, but not yet deep or proprietary.

## Fatal Objections

- **One-time purchase / weak retention:** profit must come from repeated install margin, not recurring LTV.
- **Inventory mismatch:** straight rails are reusable, but curved/custom rails can become dead stock.
- **Trust and liability:** families may still prefer established mobility-equipment dealers.
- **Installer capture risk:** the retained installer can copy or capture the margin.
- **Incumbent copy risk:** existing stairlift dealers can add "used/refurbished rapid install + free 48h removal" using stronger trust, SEO, technicians, and PFRON credibility.
- **Founder-time mismatch:** this is operationally heavy for a solo founder with about 5h/day.

## Control Point Tier

Tier 2: local operational/supply control, but not proprietary until seller acquisition and installer routing become repeatable and exclusive.

## Decision

Rejected for this run. The score is below the required simulated `>90` gate. Even though the resource is real, the company shape risks becoming a local resale/install service with limited retention and incumbent dealer copy risk.
