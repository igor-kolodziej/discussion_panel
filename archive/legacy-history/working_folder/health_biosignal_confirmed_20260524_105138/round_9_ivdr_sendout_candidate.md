# Round 9 Candidate: IVDR Send-Out Continuity Rail

## Candidate

**IVDR Send-Out Continuity Rail for low-volume genetic tests.**

## Scored Wording Draft

IVDR Send-Out Continuity Rail is a B2B sample-flow and reference-lab access layer for small Polish genetic laboratories, hospital labs, fertility clinics, oncology clinics, and specialist practices that will not maintain every low-volume genetic / molecular assay under EU IVDR pressure. Instead of selling IVDR consultancy, it signs send-out terms with 2-3 accredited EU reference labs for specific low-volume genetic tests, builds Polish consent/sample/courier/accessioning SOPs, and asks one Polish clinic/lab to route all cases for a narrow discontinued or non-maintained test category through the founder for 60 days. Revenue comes from per-sample margin, setup fee, and later monthly test-menu continuity retainers. The control point is routed sample flow plus contracted reference-lab capacity/pricing, not a report, dashboard, or generic marketplace.

## Hidden Insight

EU IVDR will not only create compliance work; it will reshape test menus. Small labs and clinics do not need to win a regulatory philosophy debate for every rare assay. When a low-volume in-house or RUO-based genetic test becomes too burdensome to maintain, the practical buyer question becomes: "Where do we send this sample tomorrow without losing the clinician relationship?" The commercially valuable layer is the replacement transaction route, not the compliance memo.

## Beachhead

Start with 1-3 low-volume but clinically meaningful test categories where Polish private clinics and small labs may not want to maintain in-house capability:

- niche reproductive genetics / known-family-variant confirmation;
- rare hereditary cancer subpanels not worth maintaining locally;
- PGx assays for small specialty clinics;
- molecular tests where sample logistics and report-language handoff matter more than local assay ownership.

## Primary Control Point

- Written reseller/send-out terms with 2-3 accredited EU reference labs for named test categories.
- Polish-language sample, consent, courier, and chain-of-custody SOPs.
- One signed Polish clinic/lab routing agreement for every case in one narrow test category for 60 days.
- First paid sample flow and accepted reference-lab reports.

## 2-Month Proof Under 100,000 PLN

- Build a list of 30-50 target Polish small labs, fertility/oncology/specialist clinics, and private genetic practices.
- Build a list of 20 EU reference labs with accredited genetic test menus and cross-border sample acceptance.
- Secure 2-3 written reference-lab commercial terms or reseller/referral terms for named tests.
- Secure one Polish clinic/lab paying a setup fee or prepaid 5-10 sample bundle.
- Route at least 5 samples through the SOP.
- Prove gross margin per sample, turnaround time, report acceptance, and whether the clinic/lab will continue using the route.

## Why This May Beat Earlier Genetic Ideas

- It does not require clinics to outsource a sensitive counselling decision to an unknown founder.
- It does not depend on consumer acquisition.
- It is not a retrospective document/report product.
- The founder controls a concrete cross-border transaction route: sample accessioning, courier, consent, reference-lab price, and report handoff.
- Reference labs want volume; small clinics/labs want continuity without carrying every test internally.

## Main Risks

- Existing large labs may already have send-out arrangements.
- Small clinics may prefer to refer patients directly to known labs.
- Cross-border biological sample logistics and GDPR/medical data handling must be handled carefully.
- IVDR pressure may be too slow if the chosen test category is not actually being dropped.
- If there is no routed sample commitment, this is only a brokerage idea.
