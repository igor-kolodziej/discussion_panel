# Zero-to-One Evaluation: HumanFactor Works v1 With Simulated-Panel Objections

## Diagnosis

The panel confirms that distribution-before-technology is the candidate's strongest insight and that an acquired contract book would be a genuine control point. It also exposes a hard-gate failure that the earlier advisory evaluation treated too leniently: the conservative founder-wealth outcome is a terminal valuation, not a reconciled acquisition-and-debt cash-flow path. Two independent reviewers identified the same fatal issue, while also noting that the human-augmentation product is optional to the credible BHP business. The candidate cannot proceed as written; any revision must leave the multi-book acquisition-roll-up shape rather than merely add a more favorable financing table.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence remains limited to fragmentation, recurring BHP demand, permission for qualified external service, commercial price categories and large-platform consolidation precedent. The panel adds no market proof, but correctly separates those facts from the joint acquisition hypothesis: a local target must simultaneously have transferable recurring revenue, replacement-owner profit, low concentration, a retained qualified operator, a backup specialist, a ≤3× price, ≥60% deferred consideration and high customer novation. The exact candidate supplies no annual schedule reconciling later purchase consideration, tax, interest, earnouts, working capital, operator costs and remaining debt. Its 8,500 PLN instrumented module, repeat purchase and data rights also remain unverified hypotheses.

## Overall Score

**72/100.** Weighted points are 144.5 out of 200; `10 × (144.5 / 20) = 72.25`, rounded to 72. This is an evaluator diagnosis after panel failure, not a simulated-panel score or validation gate.

## Control Point

**Tier 1 target, conditional on execution.** Novated contracts, brand, domain, renewal schedules, operating workflows and binding specialist agreements would be scarce owned or contracted distribution. The strongest copy risk is a better-capitalized safety-services buyer acquiring the remaining books and an incumbent provider copying the instrumented module with rented equipment. The first closed book cannot be bought by a rival, but the expansion mechanism is neither exclusive nor cheaply financed as written.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.** The conservative table reaches 5.36m PLN only through 700 clients, 22% operating margin, a 4× multiple, 90% ownership and 400,000 PLN residual debt. It does not show how roughly seven books are purchased from 100,000 PLN founder capital plus approximately 900,000 PLN of cumulative seller notes/retained cash, nor how taxes, interest, principal, earnouts, working capital and replacement operators affect reinvestment. The first modeled 267,000 PLN note alone requires about 66,750 PLN annual principal before interest, against 139,000 PLN operating profit. A modest miss breaks the gate, so this is a veto against `pursue`.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS, narrowly.** A conditional option, fifteen paid renewals and a 4,000 PLN module deposit can be sought within 180 days while the founder remains employed and pre-proof spend stays below 25,000 PLN. The pass requires irrevocable customer cash or escrow before more than 40,000 PLN is released; simultaneous bookkeeping at an unconditional close is insufficient. Seller, financing, lawful outreach, renewals, two specialists and module demand create a dense dependency chain, but the option conditions contain the initial loss.

## Expansion Check

The first local book is coherent. The claimed expansion, however, requires repeated acquisition sourcing, capital, customer transfer, key-person replacement and integration; it does not become sufficiently easier after the first deal. Large consolidators possess cheaper capital and management infrastructure. Technology vendors can enter the installed base only through a sale, but the candidate has not shown that its own module earns repeat demand.

## Fatal Objections

1. The conservative founder-wealth path is not cash-flow closed; both independent reviewers identified this as fatal.
2. The qualifying-deal intersection may be empty because favorable price, financing, margin, concentration, transfer and staffing terms must coexist.
3. The founder's weaker fit for sustained acquisition and integration work becomes more important with every book.
4. The core BHP roll-up can survive without the human-augmentation product, so the promised lab trajectory is not yet intrinsic to value creation.
5. Contract renewal, specialist mobility and better-funded acquirers limit the durability of the expansion control point.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Secure a paid, recurring route into consequential employer workflows before building human-performance technology; make distribution or transaction control—not the sensor—the first strategic asset.
