# Zero-to-One Candidate: FallbackLine

## One-Sentence Thesis

Sell Polish home-service firms an after-hours phone window in which an AI dispatcher handles routine calls and a trained human joins uncertain or valuable calls within 30 seconds, charging for booked jobs while accumulating proprietary human–AI handoff and recovery data.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build orchestration, uncertainty rules and handoff analytics. Renewable-installation domain language can be learned through paid interviews and optional family-company dry runs, but the first proof must be an independent paying installer. Contract dispatchers cover the live window; the founder does not operate a nightly call center personally.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is a 5–40 technician HVAC, heat-pump, electrical or plumbing firm that receives urgent or high-intent calls after office hours. The owner wants booked jobs without staying on the phone. Current choices are voicemail, personal call forwarding, a general answering service, or a fully automated voice agent that may fail on accents, safety ambiguity, price questions and caller insistence.

## Product Wedge

The customer forwards one published number during a defined after-hours window. AI collects identity, location, equipment type and urgency. A human dispatcher joins when confidence is low, safety keywords occur, the caller asks for a person, or probable job value exceeds a threshold. The service books only within the customer's rules and returns a structured transcript/outcome. It makes no technical diagnosis.

Pilot price hypothesis: 1,250 PLN monthly minimum plus 65 PLN per accepted booking; three-month minimum. Direct costs are telephony/model usage, dispatcher minutes, QA and expected credits.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The product is neither pure automation nor a generic call center. It sells continuity at the exact failure boundary: contractors tolerate automation for routine intake but value a human when a lost call may mean a valuable job. Contractual call forwarding routes the whole paid window and generates naturally occurring handoff examples.

## Underused Momentum

Voice agents are becoming inexpensive while evidence continues to show escalation judgment is difficult. The Polish-language home-service niche has fragmented buyers, repeatable scripts, public directories and high-value urgent calls. The underused asset is human rescue data tied to booking outcomes, not another voice model.

## Beachhead Market

Independent HVAC/heat-pump installers in one Polish region, limited to after-hours inbound intake and booking. The service excludes medical, legal, finance, emergency dispatch and technical repair advice.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- 1,250 PLN monthly base plus 65 PLN per accepted booking; hypothesized average account revenue 1,550 PLN/month.
- At 80 calls/month, hypothesized direct cost is 650 PLN: 300 dispatcher labor, 150 AI/telephony, 100 QA, 100 credits/support.
- Hypothesized gross margin is 58% before sales and product engineering; mature gross margin depends on reducing human minutes without lowering conversion.
- Customer CAC target is below 1,800 PLN and gross-profit payback below three months.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

The first ten come from outbound to public installer directories and live advertisements that expose an after-hours phone number or missed-call complaint. Each receives a call-volume audit and a paid three-month bounded pilot. The family business can test scripts without being counted as proof. Customers 11–100 come from the same trade in adjacent regions through measured outbound and customer referrals, not an assumed distributor partnership.

## Primary Control Point

A signed contract and call-forwarding configuration route all calls during the defined window. The company owns the handoff policy, trained dispatcher workflow and—subject to the DPA and caller notices—de-identified event and outcome features. The strongest copy risk is a BPO, telephony vendor or voice-AI provider adding the same human fallback.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Use standard telephony and model APIs; hire two Polish dispatchers as paid contractors with overlapping availability; obtain a fixed-scope telecom/privacy review; sell one independent three-month pilot before custom software. Unknown-founder access comes from public contractor lists and an offer tied to missed-booking value. Proof before serious capital is a paid invoice, 100 routed calls, measured human-intervention rate, accepted-booking value and cancellation rate.

## Distribution Advantage

Outbound is triggered by trade, region and after-hours availability. The company later publishes anonymized benchmarks for answer rate, rescue rate and accepted bookings. This is only an advantage if referrals and benchmarks lower CAC; generic content marketing receives no credit.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

After one trade and language work, expand to plumbers/electricians, then Czech and German scripts. The compounding product is a library of failure triggers, dispatcher actions and booking outcomes. A mature service can expose the handoff engine to selected voice-AI integrators, but the base case does not require their partnership.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish language, local addresses, trade vocabulary, customer consent notices and small-contractor economics are the adaptation layer. Local dispatchers can rescue cases that generic English-first systems mishandle. This defense is operational, not regulatory or exclusive.

## Compounding Advantage

More calls can improve routing thresholds, dispatcher training and conversion benchmarks; more contractors create reusable trade-specific scripts. If customers refuse usable event rights or an incumbent matches performance and price, the data advantage does not compound.

The conservative denominator—1,100 retained accounts—is the decisive challenge and must be supported by churn and CAC evidence before geographic expansion.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Cash funds dispatcher QA, scheduling redundancy, call tooling and language pods. No owned telecom infrastructure is purchased. A new trade pod opens only when the prior pod has positive contribution, sub-three-month CAC payback and six-month retention above 70%.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Interview 25 independent contractors in 30 days; build a manual concierge demo; pitch a three-month paid pilot to 30 qualifying firms; sign one independent account by day 75; route 100 calls by day 135; seek a second paid account and renewal/continuation signal by day 180. The founder remains employed and dispatchers cover live calls.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Discovery/legal: 7,000 PLN.
- Manual demo and dispatcher retainers: cumulative 15,000 PLN.
- Only after paid pilot: telephony integration, QA and acquisition to cumulative 35,000 PLN.
- Above 40,000 PLN only after successful delivery, positive contribution and a second payer; above 70,000 PLN only after multiple independent accounts and a viable acquisition channel. Family capital is excluded.

## Critical Dependency Map

| Dependency | Test | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor WTP | Exact paid three-month pilot to 30 firms | Kill if no payer |
| Reliable human coverage | Two-contractor schedule and simulated failover | Do not offer hours that cannot be covered |
| Handoff improves bookings | Compare rescued, AI-only and abandoned calls | Narrow or kill if rescue value is below labor cost |
| Lawful recording/data rights | Fixed-scope privacy/telecom advice and explicit notices | Store only permitted structured features |
| Retention and CAC | Six-month cohort before new geography | Stop expansion if payback/churn fail thresholds |

## Full-Time Transition Gate

At least 20 independent paying accounts, three months of positive contribution, two dispatcher shifts that do not depend on the founder, a repeatable CAC signal and 12 months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Reusable assets are a Polish trade-call taxonomy, handoff orchestration code, dispatcher training material and consent architecture. These are useful to a future HCI lab, but do not justify continued spend if customers will not pay or retain.

## Critical Evidence

Agent escalation is a recognized technical problem; enterprise readiness and HITL vendors confirm the category. Public installer directories permit direct outbound.

## Critical Hypotheses

The price, call volume, conversion lift, legal event rights, dispatcher cost, churn, 1,100-account denominator and cross-language transfer are unproven.

## Kill Criteria

- No paid independent pilot after 30 qualified pitches or by day 90.
- Gross contribution below 40% after 300 calls.
- Human rescue fails to produce at least twice its direct labor cost in accepted booking value.
- Six-month logo retention below 70% or CAC payback above four months.
- Data rights are too narrow to accumulate non-identifying handoff features.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

POC target 35,000 PLN; founder up to five hours/day; two part-time dispatch contractors; fixed-scope telecom/privacy counsel; no full-time engineering hire before two paying accounts.
