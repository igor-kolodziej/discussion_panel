# AndonBridge Accessible Workcell

AndonBridge is an offline industrial gateway and haptic interface that lets a deaf or hard-of-hearing employee independently perceive and acknowledge selected machine, andon, and coworker events at one workcell. It converts a concrete workplace-adaptation budget into a usable human–machine interface, while building a reusable library of machine-event semantics, PLC adapters, validated haptic patterns, and response data.

The first buyer is a Polish Zakład Aktywności Zawodowej, protected employer, or ordinary manufacturer that is hiring or retaining a specific deaf employee at a repetitive production cell. The urgent event is the workplace-adaptation decision before the worker can operate independently. The initial five signals are non-safety operational events such as cycle complete, material call, quality hold, assistance request, or supervisor/coworker call. Today the worker may depend on a colleague, continuously watch a stack light, or use a generic pager that does not understand the machine state. That creates waiting, interruptions, loss of autonomy, and a barrier to assigning the worker to the cell.

V1 is deliberately bounded. An offline edge gateway reads approved dry contacts, OPC UA/PLC tags, or andon events without changing machine control. It maps five events to a haptic vocabulary that the worker learns and validates, lets the worker acknowledge selected events, and stores only event/response timing. Raw worker location, health, emotion, and productivity scoring are excluded. AndonBridge does not replace fire, evacuation, proximity, interlock, or other mandatory audible/visual safety systems and does not control machinery. Safety-rated alerting is a later, separately funded conformity project only after repeated non-safety demand.

The commercial insight is that the buyer does not need another accessibility app or broad wearable-safety platform; it needs one adapted workstation that the employee, occupational reviewer, employer, and maintenance team can understand. Fire/vibration alarm products broadcast emergencies. Stack lights require gaze. Generic watches accept messages. PLC integrators can wire signals but usually treat the worker's haptic comprehension, event semantics, documentation, and recurring verification as one-off customization. AndonBridge productizes that narrow layer.

Poland has unusually concrete momentum. PFRON officially allows reimbursement for workplace adaptations, assistive devices, software, and technologies, with statutory maximums many times average pay per workstation. A 2026 government summary cites up to 141,300 PLN for qualifying workplace equipment. Poland also publishes a finite initial market map: at the end of 2025 there were 152 Zakłady Aktywności Zawodowej employing 8,178 disabled people, before counting protected and ordinary employers. Official workplace-sign rules require signals appropriate to worker perception. PFRON is optional budget support, not an existential partnership: the first proof may be a direct employer purchase or a signed budget-backed commitment, and no spending is assumed reimbursable before the employer's required public agreement.

The first product is sold as an 18,000–25,000 PLN five-event mapping and pilot, normally with a 40% deposit. A production workcell is expected to sell for 28,000–42,000 PLN including gateway, band, configuration, worker validation, and documentation. Annual support of 10,000–16,000 PLN covers device replacement, event-map versioning after machine changes, comprehension/response verification, and support. Early direct cost is expected at 7,000–11,000 PLN per pilot; mature hardware/deployment gross margin is targeted at 50–60%, support gross margin at 75–85%, and blended margin at 65–72%. These are hypotheses, not paid evidence. The model is killed if employers will buy only a one-off COTS pager or if recurring verification has no value.

The cold start is under direct control. For 12,000–20,000 PLN, the founder buys a rugged COTS haptic device, builds a simulated PLC/dry-contact bench, and contracts 60–100 hours of embedded/PLC work. Five patterns are tested in simulation before a site. A paying employer supplies the approved cell signals and employee participation as normal delivery obligations; no machine-maker, wearable-maker, disability organization, clinic, or research partnership is required. Site-specific hardware work begins only after a deposit or signed employer/public budget commitment.

The first ten customers come from direct, narrow outreach to the public list of 152 ZAZ sites, supported-employment resources, protected-workplace directories, and employers publicly describing disabled-worker hiring or adaptation. The offer is one worker, one cell, five operational events, one measurable result. The first 100 come from reference employers, standardized adapters for common PLC/andon families, accessibility/funding documentation packs, regional occupational-safety and supported-employment search, and later non-exclusive licensing to ordinary machine integrators after direct unit economics are proven. No association or reseller is assumed during proof.

The directly controlled asset exists at first paid deployment. The company owns the offline gateway firmware, haptic vocabulary and comprehension protocol, event ontology, documentation, and PLC/machine-family adapters; the signed cell event map and version history become embedded in the employer's workflow. Contracts preserve ownership of generic software, mappings, and non-identifying haptic-confusion/response derivatives while the employer owns identifiable employee and operational records. Within twelve months, proof of compounding is three independent paid workcells, two adapters reused across sites, validated event recognition and response, and at least one paid support/versioning renewal. If the second and third sites do not reuse adapters or if customers refuse generic rights, the business is treated as custom integration and stopped.

The strongest copy risk is a local PLC integrator combining a COTS vibrating band with machine signals and undercutting the deployment. Broad alarm/wearable vendors also have hardware and distribution. AndonBridge must therefore be preferred for faster small-workcell delivery, participatory haptic validation, funding/PIP-ready documentation, privacy-minimized design, and a cross-machine accessibility library. Buying a band does not reproduce the installed event semantics, worker-validated vocabulary, documentation, response history, or standardized adapters. The company is non-viable if an ordinary integrator can deliver the same decision value at less than half the price through routine work.

The beachhead is deaf-accessible, non-safety Polish production cells. Expansion follows the same installed asset: more machine families at the same employers, ordinary manufacturers retaining or hiring deaf workers, then CEE/EU workplace adaptations. Only after this niche produces reusable adapters and references does the company serve hearing-protected or very noisy workplaces, where haptic machine events reduce visual attention for a much larger workforce. Certified safety alerts are not required for the founder-wealth model and are excluded until funded separately.

Founder fit is strongest in human signals, neuroscience/biosignals, data, ML, behavior measurement, and AI-assisted product development. The founder lacks PLC and industrial safety experience, so a fixed-scope embedded/PLC contractor and short occupational-safety/legal review are mandatory. The founder remains employed, spends at most about five hours per day, and avoids the founder's corporate employer's time, data, contacts, facilities, and IP.

Founder wealth scenarios are commercial hypotheses. Business values are equity values after debt; founder cash is only actual distributions and is not retained earnings counted again. While employed, conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash otherwise available for distribution and cap optional distributions at 5%; strong success reinvests 100% until scale.

- Conservative, year 8: 350 supported cells at 12,000 PLN annual support plus 60 new cells at 32,000 PLN. Revenue is 6.12m PLN, gross margin 68%, operating margin 32%, and EBIT 1.96m PLN. With 88% founder ownership, 200,000 PLN debt, a 5× EBIT equity value, and 1.7m PLN actual post-transition founder distributions, estimated founder net worth is about 10.1m PLN. Milestones are three paid cells in year one and 35 supported cells in year three. This case is thin and requires expansion beyond the initial ZAZ list into protected/ordinary employers and CEE.
- Expected success, year 7: 800 supported cells at 15,000 PLN plus 150 new cells at 34,000 PLN. Revenue is 17.1m PLN, gross margin 72%, operating margin 38%, and EBIT 6.50m PLN. After 22% dilution, less than 600,000 PLN debt, a 5.5× EBIT equity value, and 2.0m PLN actual post-transition distributions, estimated founder net worth is about 29.5m PLN. Milestones are 70 supported cells in year three and ordinary-employer/CEE expansion in year four.
- Strong success, year 7: 2,500 supported cells at 18,000 PLN plus 450 new cells at 38,000 PLN. Revenue is 62.1m PLN, gross margin 75%, operating margin 42%, and EBIT 26.1m PLN. After 40% dilution and 3m PLN debt, a 6× EBIT equity value leaves the founder about 92.2m PLN. No distributions are added.

The conservative and expected cases, not strong success, support the 10m PLN founder-wealth path. The company must demonstrate that recurring support attaches and deployment becomes template-driven; otherwise these scenarios fail.

The 180-day paid-proof plan is staged. Days 1–30: build the bench gateway, test five-pattern comprehension, verify non-safety positioning, and create an 80-employer list. Days 31–75: demonstrate the system to qualified employers with a named employee/cell and offer an 18,000–22,000 PLN pilot with a 40% deposit or signed budget-backed commitment. Days 76–135: integrate approved non-safety events with employee participation. Days 136–180: measure event recognition, response time, coworker interruptions, and paid continuation/support. Spending is capped at 7,000 PLN before bench feasibility and 20,000 PLN before paid proof. Further spending up to 40,000 PLN requires paid/budget-backed evidence; up to 70,000 PLN requires successful delivery, positive contribution, and a second independent payer; the founder's 100,000 PLN ceiling requires three independent payers plus repeat support or a documented viable acquisition channel. Possible family capital remains excluded.

The founder does not leave employment until there are at least five independent paying employers or contracted recurring cells, positive contribution on three deployments, two support renewals, a repeatable acquisition signal beyond personal contacts, safe standardized delivery, no unresolved employer/IP conflict, and twelve months of personal runway.

Kill criteria are: no paid deposit or budget-backed commitment by day 180; fewer than four serious calls from 60 qualified employers; three employers say existing visual/fire/pager systems are sufficient; public/direct procurement consistently exceeds 180 days; the worker cannot recognize five patterns at at least 95% in the intended context; safety functionality is required before buyers will pay; an ordinary integrator can deliver equivalent value below half the price; or the tenth deployment still requires more than 24 custom engineering hours.

If the initial market is too small, the owned gateway firmware, machine adapters, haptic vocabulary, usability/response data, and accessibility procurement knowledge remain useful for noisy PPE work, warehouse alerts, training, and the future human-interface lab. Passing research or chatbot review would mean only that the business is validated enough to pursue with staged paid experiments; it would not authorize serious capital spending.
