# Zero-to-One Candidate: AndonBridge Accessible Workcell

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build the configurable gateway that translates selected machine, andon, and coworker events into a deaf worker's validated haptic vocabulary, enabling independent industrial work while giving employers a concrete, optionally PFRON-funded workstation adaptation.

## Founder Fit

The founder brings human-signal/HCI, ML/data, and AI leverage; a bounded PLC/electronics contractor supplies industrial integration. The business builds assistive HCI, haptic hardware, real-time event interfaces, response data, PLC expertise, and public-sector funding knowledge. A non-safety production-event pilot is compatible with employment and requires no institutional partnership.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is a Polish manufacturing employer, Zakład Aktywności Zawodowej, or protected employer hiring or retaining a deaf/hard-of-hearing operator at a specific cell. The painful event is a workstation adaptation decision: the employee cannot reliably perceive cycle-complete, assistance, material-call, quality-hold, or supervisor-call signals and depends on coworkers or visual attention. Existing fire pagers handle emergencies; stack lights require gaze; generic watches do not understand machine events.

## Product Wedge

An offline gateway reads five approved non-safety PLC/dry-contact/andon events, maps them to a user-validated haptic vocabulary on a rugged band, accepts acknowledgements, and stores only event/response timing. V1 does not replace mandatory audible/visual safety systems or control machinery. A later safety-rated product is pursued only after paid demand and conformity work.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Employers do not need another accessibility app; they need one adapted workstation that an employee can operate independently and that occupational/PIP reviewers can understand. General alarm products broadcast hazards, while machine integrators rarely productize deaf-accessible event semantics for small deployments. The narrow wedge is the signed cell event topology plus worker-validated patterns and documentation.

## Underused Momentum

PFRON can reimburse assistive technology and workplace adaptation up to large statutory limits, and Poland has a public map of 152 ZAZ sites plus employer-support programs. Official rules require signals appropriate to worker perception. Reimbursement is optional momentum, not a prerequisite: the first pilot must be payable directly or backed by a signed employer/public funding commitment.

## Beachhead Market

Polish ZAZ/protected and ordinary manufacturers adapting one repetitive non-safety production workstation for a deaf employee, where five events create measurable coworker interruptions or wait time. Exclude fire/evacuation, mobile equipment proximity, and safety interlocks during POC.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Hypothesized mapping/pilot: 18,000–25,000 PLN; direct cost 7,000–11,000 PLN.
- Production workcell: 28,000–42,000 PLN including gateway/band/configuration; 50–60% gross margin.
- Annual support, device replacement, event-map versioning, and response verification: 10,000–16,000 PLN per cell; 75–85% gross margin.
- Target mature blended gross margin 65–72%, CAC 6,000–14,000 PLN, payback inside first contract. All prices are hypotheses.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10: use the public list of 152 ZAZ sites, supported-employment/employer resources, protected-workplace directories, and employers publicly recruiting disabled workers. Offer one five-event non-safety pilot with worker participation and 40% deposit; PFRON paperwork is an optional procurement aid. First customers come from direct founder outreach, not an association partnership.

First 100: publish anonymized machine-event/haptic usability patterns, standardize adapters for common PLC/andon families, use reference employers and regional occupational-safety/accessibility search, and later license deployment to ordinary machine integrators without exclusivity. Expansion to hearing-protected/noisy workers occurs only after the deaf-accessible niche is won.

## Primary Control Point

At first sale, the company owns the gateway firmware, event ontology, haptic language/testing protocol, machine-family adapters, and signed customer event map; it contracts the right to retain non-identifying pattern/response derivatives. Within 12 months, proof is three paid workcells, two reused adapters, validated comprehension/response measures, and recurring support. This is Tier 2 unless the installed workflow and data create meaningful switching cost.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Resource needed: one safe offline gateway and band. Buy a rugged COTS haptic device, build a PLC/dry-contact bench, and contract 60–100 hours of integration for 12,000–20,000 PLN. Use simulated events before any site. The paying employer supplies the cell signals and worker access under consent; no manufacturer relationship is needed. Require deposit or budget-backed PFRON/employer commitment before site-specific hardware. Proof before capital is correct five-event delivery, worker recognition, and one paid adaptation decision.

## Distribution Advantage

The first-market institutions and funding mechanism are public, finite, and locally documentable. An unknown founder can sell a bounded adaptation, not a broad safety platform. Every PLC adapter and approved documentation pack reduces deployment cost. Installed support and employee-specific validation create switching friction.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Fire/vibration alarm products do not map production events; generic industrial wearables are broad and expensive; local integrators treat each adaptation as custom. AndonBridge specializes in accessible event semantics and funding-ready small workcells. Expand from non-safety events to more machine families, warehouses/noisy PPE environments, and certified safety alerting only after funded evidence.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish PFRON/starosta/PIP workflow, Polish documentation, ZAZ market map, and local PLC delivery create an entry wedge. EU expansion uses analogous reasonable-accommodation duties but requires country-specific channels. Local knowledge is a speed advantage, not an exclusion right.

## Compounding Advantage

Each workcell adds reusable PLC adapters, event semantics, haptic-confusion data, documentation, and references. Standard packs reduce engineering hours; recurring verification/device replacement raises retention. The response dataset and hardware experience support future accessible human–machine interfaces.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Ninety-five percent reinvestment while employed funds industrial enclosure, adapter library, product liability/QMS steps only after paid demand, and a deployment engineer; optional distributions stay below 5%. Strong success reinvests 100%. Inventory is bought against deposits. Retained cash is not double-counted.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: bench gateway and five-pattern comprehension test; map 80 target employers. Days 31–75: non-contact research and technical demo; offer 18,000–22,000 PLN five-event pilot with 40% deposit or signed budget-backed commitment. Days 76–135: integrate approved non-safety signals with worker participation. Days 136–180: measure recognition, response time, interruptions, and paid continuation. Maximum pre-proof spend 20,000 PLN; compatible with five hours/day and one contractor.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

Up to 7,000 PLN for bench feasibility; 20,000 PLN hard pre-proof cap; up to 40,000 PLN only against paid/budget-backed proof; 70,000 PLN after successful delivery and second payer; 100,000 PLN after three payers plus repeat support/acquisition evidence. Family capital excluded.

## Critical Dependency Map

- Buyer has a specific employee/workcell and budget authority; qualify before proposal.
- Events can be safely exposed without altering control; contractor verifies.
- Worker distinguishes patterns and accepts device; participatory test.
- PFRON timing is optional, not existential; direct payment remains valid.
- Safety scope remains excluded until conformity path is funded.
- Adapter reuse and recurring verification must reduce labor.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Five independent paying employers or contracted recurring cells, positive contribution on three, two support renewals, repeatable acquisition beyond personal contacts, safe standardized deployment, and 12 months runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Gateway firmware, haptic vocabulary, PLC adapters, usability data, and accessibility procurement knowledge can serve noisy PPE work, warehouses, training, or future assistive HCI even if the first niche is small.

## Critical Evidence

PFRON officially funds adaptation/assistive devices; ZAZ/employer lists are public; adjacent vibration alarms exist but focus on fire, hazards, or broad safety rather than production-event accessibility.

## Critical Hypotheses

Employers pay 18,000+ PLN; direct or budget-backed proof fits 180 days; non-safety independence has measurable value; worker acceptance is high; common adapters reuse; 350 cells are reachable; support attaches.

## Kill Criteria

No deposit/budget commitment by day 180; fewer than four serious calls from 60 qualified employers; three employers say fire/visual alarms already suffice; PFRON/direct procurement consistently exceeds 180 days; worker recognition below 95% for five events; ordinary integrator can deliver equivalent work below half price; or tenth deployment needs over 24 custom engineering hours.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Pre-proof 20,000 PLN, 20–25 founder hours/week; founder, contract PLC/embedded engineer, accessibility/haptic tester, short occupational-safety/legal review. No full-time hire or safety certification before paid proof.
