# Zero-to-One Evaluation: TrialFleet Release Custody

## Diagnosis

The pivot materially improves the control-point logic by moving from optional OEM QA to contracted golden-device custody and a routed release decision. However, the new buyer introduces a worse near-term dependency: a CRO may not control sponsor/vendor release authority and may require supplier qualification, QMS maturity, security review, and study-specific validation before trusting an unknown founder. The pivot supplies no conservative/expected/strong wealth model, and credible customer counts/pricing cannot be inferred. It therefore fails both hard gates as written and should be closed rather than polished into a trust-heavy clinical service.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Preserved evidence: wearable updates can affect validation; known-input replay can detect chain changes; hardware is affordable. New hypotheses: small CROs lack benches, control release decisions, accept custody, pay 80,000–180,000 PLN annually, contract within 180 days, and permit reusable adapters/failure derivatives. Prior panel verdicts apply to the original OEM buyer only; they do not validate CRO demand. Missing: buyer authority, procurement path, QMS threshold, target-account count, gross margin, retention, CAC, full wealth scenarios, and paid-discovery precedent.

## Overall Score

65/100. Weighted total 130 across total weight 20: `10 × (130 / 20) = 65`.

## Control Point

Tier 2 if signed, Tier 3 before contract. Golden-device custody and a contractual release-routing procedure create real workflow control and history. Strongest copy risk: the CRO or sponsor builds the same bench and custody process internally after the founder proves its value.

## Founder Wealth Gate

Fail. No conservative, expected-success, or strong-success model is supplied, so customer counts, revenue, margins, capital, ownership/dilution, reinvestment, value/distributions, and timing cannot be judged. Conservatively, a small number of trust-heavy CRO contracts with substantial quality overhead may not produce a scalable 10m PLN founder outcome.

## Reachability Gate

Fail. A sub-35,000 PLN technical prototype is feasible, but paid proof also requires a buyer who controls a real release decision and can qualify an unknown vendor within 180 days. Sponsor/device-vendor authority, QMS/security review, and study risk are simultaneous uncontrolled dependencies. A paid advisory discovery without routing authority would not prove the model.

## Expansion Check

The niche expands toward sponsors and clinical substitution, increasing authority, evidence, and procurement barriers. An established CRO/test lab can move inward. There is no meaningful Polish distribution advantage.

## Fatal Objections

1. The buyer may not own the release decision the product must control.
2. Trust, QMS, security, and clinical procurement may block 180-day proof.
3. No wealth scenarios or defensible market scale are provided.
4. Internalization remains available after value is proven.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Contractual control of a real release decision is superior to optional test advice.
