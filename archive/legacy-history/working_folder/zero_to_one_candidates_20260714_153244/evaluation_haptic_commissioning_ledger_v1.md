# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Haptic Commissioning Ledger

## Diagnosis

The candidate has unusually cheap reachability and a direct distribution list, but the first product is close to connected work instructions, field-service evidence, and OEM commissioning apps. The proposed connection graph becomes defensible only after many paid jobs and later failures, while the founder cannot prove that history before initial buyers can copy the workflow. The expected wealth case is mathematically strong but depends on per-job pricing and 350 installers that are not evidenced; the conservative case misses. The business shape is too generic and substitution-prone to panel.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: Poland's large micro-installation base; UDT's quality/warranty warning and public installer register; technical feasibility of scanning and connected tools. Hypotheses: installers pay 180–280 PLN per job; capture adds under eight minutes; callback/evidence savings are at least twice price; generic fault derivatives are permitted; OEM apps do not suffice; 350 firms can be served at high margin. No reviewer verdict exists. Missing evidence: callback frequency/cost, battery job volume, device-template burden, and incumbent workflow coverage.

## Overall Score

74/100. Weighted total 148.5 across total weight 20: `10 × (148.5 / 20) = 74.25`, rounded to 74.

## Control Point

Tier 3 at launch, with a possible Tier 2 future. The founder owns software/templates, but the valuable cross-brand fault graph requires later customer events and derivative rights. Strongest copy risk: an inverter/battery OEM, generic field-service app, or connected-tool vendor adds scanning and evidence capture through one product update or integration.

## Founder Wealth Gate

Pass on expected success, low confidence. The expected case models 45.6m PLN founder net worth in year 7 at 78% ownership and debt-adjusted value, with 95% reinvestment/max 5% employment-period distribution and no retained-cash double count. Conservative success reaches only 9.5m. The decisive weakness is the unproven combination of 235 PLN per job, 350 firms, and 78% gross margin despite device templates and support.

## Reachability Gate

Pass. The founder can use the family company only as a testbed, then sell an independent 8,000–12,000 PLN 10-job pilot within 180 days while risking 12,000 PLN. No OEM partnership or finished product is needed; manual evidence can substitute for unavailable torque APIs. Compatibility with employment is strong.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is clear and adjacent trades are large, but expansion makes the product more generic. OEMs already own device data/distribution, while field-service platforms own broad workflow. Poland gives accessible buyers and context but cannot prevent feature entry.

## Fatal Objections

1. The core product can be copied by an OEM or field-workflow vendor through routine product/integration work.
2. The control point does not exist at paid proof; it depends on future failure events and customer data rights.
3. Installer willingness to pay per job may be below the level required for 10m PLN founder wealth.
4. Device-family authoring/support may keep margins and onboarding service-heavy.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Physical-work records become valuable when individual invisible connection actions are joined to later service and failure outcomes across the full multi-brand asset.
