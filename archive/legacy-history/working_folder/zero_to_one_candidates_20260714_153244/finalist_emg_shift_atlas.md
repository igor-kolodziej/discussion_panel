# Zero-to-One Candidate: EMG Shift Atlas

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build the commercial benchmark that muscle-computer-interface teams cannot get from same-session public datasets: repeat-donning EMG/IMU with known electrode shifts, sweat, fatigue, forearm orientation, and gloves/PPE, created only after a buyer prepays the missing condition.

## Founder Fit

Direct fit with biosignal collection, noise, ML, neuroscience, data governance, and AI tooling. It creates proprietary physiological/behavioral data, EMG ML, real-time HCI expertise, participant operations, and technical customer access. No clinic is required; fixed legal/privacy and research-operations support covers gaps.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is an EMG-interface, prosthetic, assistive-device, exoskeleton, XR, or industrial HCI team whose model works in one session but degrades when the electrode is redonned or the user sweats, rotates, fatigues, or wears PPE. Public datasets exist but often omit the buyer's exact repeated-use condition or commercial rights. The urgent event is a model milestone or design freeze before recruiting an internal cohort.

## Product Wedge

A paid protocol reservation defines one absent condition/task. After deposit, the company recruits 20–40 healthy adult participants directly, records multi-session raw EMG/IMU with measured shift coordinates and failure labels, and licenses a private early-access module plus benchmark code. A later non-exclusive version is sold only under the agreed rights. This is not speculative broad data collection.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The scarce asset is controlled variation across sessions, not more clean gestures. Buyers repeatedly recreate cohorts because public datasets do not match electrode geometry, PPE, task, or commercial-use needs. Prepaid modules convert a future dataset into contracted demand and make the first buyer finance specificity.

## Underused Momentum

Recent sEMG interfaces validate technical demand while large proprietary datasets expose the data scale gap. Electrode-shift literature repeatedly names cross-session robustness as a failure. Affordable Shimmer/OpenBCI-class hardware and direct recruitment make a small commercial dataset feasible.

## Beachhead Market

European small EMG-interface teams needing one industrial/PPE repeat-use module, not academic gesture classification. Exclude medical efficacy claims and any buyer unwilling to prepay specificity or accept the consent/data-governance terms.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Paid protocol reservation: 12,000–25,000 PLN, credited to module.
- Task/condition module: 60,000–150,000 PLN exclusive-window license; direct participant/hardware/annotation 25,000–55,000 PLN.
- Later non-exclusive license: 25,000–80,000 PLN/customer; annual benchmark/API 25,000–60,000 PLN.
- Mature digital gross margin 75–90%; early custom module 45–65%. All prices are hypotheses.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10 from publications, product pages, CORDIS, patents, hiring, and public demos showing sEMG interfaces; sell one absent-condition reservation before cohort. Use benchmark content and technical outbound. Customers 11–100 come from non-exclusive modules, new sponsored conditions, referrals, and benchmark/API access; no university or clinic partnership required.

## Primary Control Point

Directly contracted participant panel, granular commercial-use consent, repeat-session raw EMG/IMU, known shift coordinates, PPE/task metadata, failure labels, benchmark code, and exclusive/non-exclusive licenses. Twelve-month proof is two prepaid modules, at least 20 participants, one repeat license, and a unique condition absent from free data.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Build a three-person, three-session demonstrator with purchased EMG/IMU equipment and legal/privacy review for 12,000–20,000 PLN. Sell a reservation before expanding to 20–40 participants; total pre-proof risk capped 25,000 PLN. Recruit directly with explicit consent and secure storage. No clinic, data partner, or speculative cohort.

## Distribution Advantage

Technical teams reveal missing conditions in papers, demos, issue trackers, and hiring. A concrete failure benchmark earns credibility. Sponsored modules become public acquisition signals while exclusive windows preserve first-buyer value.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Public datasets compete strongly; Atlas wins only on measured repeat-donning/shift/PPE conditions, commercial rights, rapid sponsored modules, and cross-module benchmark consistency. Expand from industrial wrist/forearm EMG to prosthetics/exoskeletons, assistive HCI, facial/neck EMG, and model robustness tooling.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers lower participant/technical cost and EU-grade consent/data handling. EU hosting and commercial-use clarity help but do not prevent foreign datasets.

## Compounding Advantage

Each sponsored module adds participants, tasks, failure modes, and licenseable combinations; benchmark code and panel operations reduce marginal collection cost. Cross-session robustness models and real-time calibration IP grow on top of the data.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Ninety-five percent employment-period reinvestment funds participant modules only after sponsorship, consent/security, data QA, and ML; optional distributions max 5%. Strong reinvests 100%. Retained cash not double-counted.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–45 demonstrator/legal; days 46–90 publish shift failure and list 80 teams; days 91–150 sell 12,000–25,000 PLN reservation; days 151–180 finalize consent/protocol and recruit only with deposit. Paid proof is the reservation, not dataset completion. Max risk 25,000 PLN, five hours/day.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

10,000 PLN demo; 25,000 PLN hard pre-proof; up to 60,000 PLN against reservation; 100,000 PLN after two payers/repeat license. Family capital excluded.

## Critical Dependency Map

Buyer prepayment; unique absent condition; lawful consent/anonymization; participant recruitment; dataset quality; repeat/non-exclusive licensing; public free-data substitution.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Five independent payers or contracted modules, positive contribution on two cohorts, at least two repeat/non-exclusive licenses, repeatable technical acquisition, and 12 months runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Owned consented data, benchmark code, hardware, participant protocols, and EMG models support future HCI R&D even if licensing demand is narrow; no clinic dependency remains.

## Critical Evidence

Electrode-shift/cross-session research and large public datasets prove the problem and competition; modern commercial sEMG proves demand for robust interfaces.

## Critical Hypotheses

Buyer prepays specificity; exact condition is absent from free data; cohort is recruitable; rights are durable; multiple licenses do not destroy sponsor value; 25 modules/year are reachable.

## Kill Criteria

No reservation by day 180; three buyers say free/internal data suffice; buyer refuses later non-exclusive rights without paying a fully exclusive price; legal review makes reusable licensing impractical; acquisition cost exceeds first license margin; fewer than two licenses per completed module.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

25,000 PLN pre-proof; founder, data/privacy lawyer, part-time research coordinator, optional EMG hardware contractor; no full cohort before deposit.
