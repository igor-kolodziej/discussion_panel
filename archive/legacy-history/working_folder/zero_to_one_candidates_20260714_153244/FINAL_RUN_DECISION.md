## Terminal objections

1. The strongest reachable hardware/software wedges could be copied or internalized by an incumbent with the existing trusted workflow before the startup's proposed data asset became scarce.
2. Several control points were contractual or data-compounding aspirations rather than first-sale control; independent judges consistently classified them Tier 3 or provisional Tier 2.
3. Founder-wealth scenarios often depended on unproved recurring support, adapter reuse, consumption, licensing, or customer counts; correct arithmetic did not make the operating assumptions credible.
4. More technically proprietary shapes—ScalpTile and EMG Shift Atlas—had direct control but failed credible demand/market-scale tests against DIY, free data, internal collection, FTO, and small buyer universes.
5. Structural pivots moved to CRO release custody, coating claims, and integrator OEM modules, but each introduced authority, trust, rare-event, channel, or hard-gate failures.

## Acceptance decision

The plan's acceptance rule requires the identical canonical wording to pass wealth, reachability, both simulated scores above 83, working-chat score at least 82, and fresh first-response score at least 82. That condition was not met. Writing `ideas/CONFIRMED_IDEA_*.md` would fabricate validation, so no file exists.

## Reusable assets

The research/source log, rejection patterns, seven economics models, canonical texts, judge objections, and pivot diagnoses remain available in this run for a future generator pass. The most useful negative insight is that future candidates should control a scarce consumable, transaction, exclusive right, or mandatory routed workflow at the first paid order—not merely promise that deployed data will later become a moat.
