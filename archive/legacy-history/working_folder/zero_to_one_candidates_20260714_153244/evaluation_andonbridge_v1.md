# Zero-to-One Evaluation: AndonBridge Accessible Workcell

## Diagnosis

AndonBridge has the strongest acquisition and budget mechanism in the run: a specific employer adaptation event, public target institutions, optional PFRON reimbursement, and a directly deliverable non-safety workcell under 20,000 PLN. Its defensibility is not the band but the installed event topology, machine adapters, validated haptic language, and funding/deployment workflow in a niche broad industrial vendors may ignore. The main risks are a small paid market, procurement timing, and local integrators copying the system. Both wealth and reachability gates pass as modeled, so a tightly scoped version can enter the independent panel.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: official PFRON adaptation/equipment funding; 2026 government funding summary; 152 ZAZ and 8,178 disabled employees; official signal-adaptation context; adjacent general alarm/wearable products. Hypotheses: employer pays 18,000–25,000 PLN; non-safety independence creates measurable value; 350 cells are reachable; support renews; five-event recognition exceeds 95%; adapters reuse. No external verdict exists.

## Overall Score

81/100. Weighted total 161.5 across total weight 20: `10 × (161.5 / 20) = 80.75`, rounded to 81.

## Control Point

Tier 2. The company owns firmware, ontology, haptic protocol, adapters, and signed per-cell map at first sale; installed validation/support can create switching costs. Strongest copy risk: a local PLC integrator combines a COTS vibrating band with dry-contact/OPC signals and undercuts the deployment. The answer must be standardized accessibility semantics, worker validation, documentation, and faster reuse—not hardware exclusivity.

## Founder Wealth Gate

Pass. Conservative year-8 founder net worth is about 10.1m PLN from 88% of debt-adjusted equity plus 1.7m actual post-transition distributions; expected is about 29.5m in year 7 at 78% ownership. Both respect 95% employment-period reinvestment/max 5% optional distributions; strong reinvests 100%; retained earnings are not double-counted. Decisive risk: 350 supported cells and recurring 12,000 PLN support may be too large for the initial Polish niche without ordinary-employer/CEE expansion.

## Reachability Gate

Pass. A five-event non-safety pilot can be sold for 18,000–22,000 PLN with 40% deposit or a signed employer/public budget commitment, under a 20,000 PLN pre-proof loss and within 180 days. It fits five hours/day with one contractor. PFRON reimbursement is optional rather than a speculative dependency; no manufacturer partnership or safety-rated product is required.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is specific enough to win; ordinary manufacturers and hearing-protection/noisy uses enlarge it. Broad wearable/alarm vendors could move inward, but local funding workflow, installed PLC adapters, and accessibility references slow them. Poland is a credible first region.

## Fatal Objections

1. Employers may treat accessibility as a one-off integrator project and refuse recurring support.
2. PFRON/starosta/PIP procurement can exceed 180 days if direct employer payment is absent.
3. The Polish paid cell count may not support the conservative wealth case.
4. Safety scope creep or liability can invalidate the low-cost POC.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

A deaf-accessible industrial workcell is a specific, fundable operational adaptation whose machine-event semantics and worker validation can be standardized across deployments.
