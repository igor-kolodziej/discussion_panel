Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
StreetlightFault Invoice Proof Rail

One-sentence thesis:
A live fault-to-invoice evidence rail for small Polish contractors maintaining municipal streetlights and road lighting, turning every reported fault, crew visit, material use, SLA response, DSO/escalation boundary, and before/after field record into gmina-ready invoice attachments and acceptance packets.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized Polish electrical, road-maintenance, and municipal-infrastructure contractors that hold recurring streetlight or road-lighting maintenance contracts with gminas, powiats, housing estates, or public facilities. The best first buyer has 1-5 active municipal lighting-maintenance contracts, several crews or subcontracted electricians, recurring invoice attachment requirements, and visible pain around rejected line items, unbillable call-outs, slow acceptance, missing photos, unclear pole IDs, DSO-versus-contractor responsibility, or material/vehicle-hour disputes.

Acute trigger:
The contractor has a current maintenance contract where faults arrive through resident reports, gmina emails, phone calls, public fault forms, or crew observations. Payment or acceptance is slowed when the contractor cannot cleanly prove which pole or circuit was handled, when the fault was received, when the crew arrived, what was found, whether the issue belonged to the municipal lighting network or a DSO/cable/supply boundary, what material was used, what bucket-truck or crew time was justified, what was fixed, what remains escalated, and how each event maps to invoice lines. The strongest first case starts from a recent disputed invoice, unpaid material line, rejected vehicle-hour/trip charge, repeated gmina back-and-forth, or contract renewal where evidence quality affects margin and reputation.

Control point:
Signed, prepaid, mandatory all-fault routing pilot for one named municipal lighting-maintenance contract. The contractor gives the startup access to the active fault stream and field workflow: imported or forwarded gmina emails/tickets, pole or lighting-point list where available, crew assignment notes, work-order categories, fault status, timestamps, geotagged before/after photos, material catalogue, truck/crew time records, DSO/escalation flags, acceptance-protocol requirements, invoice line mapping, and payment or rejection feedback. The startup controls the live fault ledger, evidence room, invoice attachment generator, missing-field rules, and gmina-specific packet format for that contract during the pilot.

60-day signed/prepaid proof:
One contractor signs a 90-day all-fault pilot for one gmina or public-site lighting-maintenance contract and prepays 8,000-20,000 PLN. The contractor routes all incoming and crew-found faults for that contract through the workflow. At least 80-150 fault or work-order events are captured, two invoice or acceptance packets are generated, and at least one concrete payment-progress outcome is documented: accepted packet with less back-and-forth, a previously disputed line category paid, a rejected material/trip/SLA line avoided, a gmina staff confirmation that the packet is easier to check, or a clean no-go showing the fault is outside contractor scope. A second contractor signs an LOI or pays a small reservation after seeing the redacted sample packet.

6-month POC:
Run 3-5 paid pilots across 3 contractors and 5-8 municipal lighting-maintenance contracts; capture 800-2,000 fault/work-order events; generate 12-20 invoice or acceptance packets; collect 80,000-250,000 PLN in setup, monthly, and urgent cleanup fees; document 6+ concrete payment-progress outcomes, 3+ gmina-specific accepted packet formats, 2+ repeat/expanded contracts from the same contractor, and gross margin above 65% after support and implementation costs. Continue only if the workflow becomes daily operating infrastructure for crews/admins rather than an after-the-fact report generator.

First acquisition mechanism:
Use public BIP, e-Zamowienia, BZP, and gmina contract notices to identify named contractors holding active streetlight maintenance, road lighting maintenance, lighting fault-removal, or electrical maintenance contracts. Build a target list of contractors with multiple public contracts or visible repeated awards. Send a specific outbound message referencing one live or recent contract and offer a redacted sample "fault-to-invoice proof packet" for a disputed or high-friction invoice line. The founder asks only for contractors with current invoice/payment friction and rejects buyers that just want a generic field-service app. The first sale is a prepaid 90-day all-fault pilot, not a free dashboard trial.

Economics:
Charge 8,000-20,000 PLN setup for the first contract pilot, then 1,500-4,000 PLN per month per municipal lighting contract depending on event volume and packet complexity. Add 1,000-5,000 PLN cleanup fees for disputed historical invoice packets or contract-start pole-list normalization. For larger contractors, price 15,000-40,000 PLN per quarter for multiple contracts. The buyer pays because one or two rejected material/trip/SLA line items, one slow invoice cycle, or one lost renewal advantage can exceed the pilot cost, while the workflow also reduces admin reconstruction time.

Copy risk:
Generic field-service software, ticketing systems, municipal asset systems, ERPs, WhatsApp/SharePoint/Forms workflows, or a competent contractor can copy the basic photo-and-ticket capture. The defense is narrow municipal streetlight invoice acceptance memory: gmina-specific packet formats, pole/fault/material taxonomies, missing-field rules, DSO/escalation wording, accepted/rejected line-item patterns, crew habit, and the live event ledger tied to invoice outcomes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific account/workflow:
Once the contractor signs the all-fault pilot, routes the current fault stream, trains crews to capture events in the workflow, and uses the generated packet for the next invoice cycle, the startup controls the active acceptance evidence for that named contract. A generic field-service vendor can copy the concept later, but it does not instantly own the contractor's live fault ledger, gmina-specific packet format, missing-field history, crew usage habit, or payment-progress feedback for that invoice cycle.

Why it is not a service, report, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic field-service app:
The startup accepts only live municipal lighting maintenance contracts where the contractor commits to route all faults/work orders through the rail for a pilot period. The output is an operational payment artifact: per-fault event ledger, evidence index, before/after photo set, material/crew/truck-time mapping, DSO/escalation/no-go notes, acceptance protocol, invoice attachment packet, and payment-feedback log. It is not electrical work, lighting design, tender bidding, legal advice, route optimization, a general CMMS, a photo archive, a static report, a dashboard, a database, or a marketplace.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not perform electrical work, supervise works as an engineer, act as public procurement representative, provide legal advice, certify safety, determine final contractual liability, contact residents as the contractor, or guarantee gmina acceptance or payment. The licensed contractor remains provider of record and is responsible for electrical safety, work quality, crew management, contract interpretation, final statements, public-buyer communication, and invoicing. The startup only structures and timestamps evidence that the contractor authorizes and approves.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs because it does not match lighting products, SKUs, energy labels, EPREL records, or public tender bids. It starts after a municipal lighting maintenance contract has been won and focuses on recurring faults, field events, invoice line proof, and acceptance packets. It is not TraceFaktura because it does not handle animal-care pickup, shelter, veterinary, adoption, quarantine, or animal-level municipal events; the asset is the streetlight fault/work-order ledger and municipal lighting acceptance format. It is not HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, Data Act Machine Data, CBAM, PromoLeak, REDBlocked, DORA, HeritageDoor, Supplement Stack, BioSignal, or Amazon Vendor recovery.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The pain may be too small if contractors rarely lose money on rejected line items or slow acceptance. Many contractors may already manage with WhatsApp, phone calls, Excel, photos, and accounting staff. Driver/crew adoption is the real product and may be hard in rainy/night field work. Every gmina may want a different attachment style, creating service-heavy onboarding. Existing field-service tools can add invoice packet templates. Gmina cooperation may be slow or inconsistent. Contractors may lose the municipal contract, hurting retention. The first proof must quantify payment-progress outcomes, not just produce cleaner documentation.
