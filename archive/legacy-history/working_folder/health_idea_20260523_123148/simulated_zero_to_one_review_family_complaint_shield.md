# Simulated Zero To One Review: Family Complaint Shield

## Exact Idea Evaluated

Family Complaint Shield for registered private care homes in Poland. Target only registered private 20-80 bed elderly-care homes with a live or recent urgency trigger: family complaint, bad Google review, incident documentation problem, inspection stress, staff turnover, or occupancy risk. The product is a 30-day paid crisis workflow, not generic care-home CRM/compliance SaaS. It installs a mandatory high-risk event rail for named residents and event classes: falls, medication handoff, pressure-sore checks, hydration/meal refusal, family contact, consent/incident notes, and owner response packets. A contracted nurse/care-quality reviewer and optional care-law reviewer review the templates and edge cases. Pricing: 4,000-8,000 PLN crisis setup/sprint plus 1,500-3,500 PLN/month if retained. Control point proof in 2 months: two paid homes sign that all high-risk events for named residents/classes are routed through the workflow for 30 days; produce staff compliance metrics, family-response packets, and one owner-confirmed retained-bed/reduced-refund/reduced-complaint outcome. Acquisition: target public Google review pain and voivode registers; direct owner outreach with "your next family complaint should not be reconstructed from WhatsApp and memory."

## Score

**91/100**

## Control Point Tier

**Tier 2+ routed high-risk care-event workflow access.**

It becomes meaningfully stronger if the pilot contract makes the rail mandatory for named residents/event classes, not optional documentation. It is not Tier 1 yet because the resource is workflow access and crisis-response habit, not exclusive legal rights, owned supply, or a hard regulatory monopoly.

## Strongest Copy Risk

Care-home CRMs, Polish care-law consultants, nurse auditors, or larger care groups can copy incident templates and complaint packets once demand is visible. The defensible part must become cross-home evidence patterns, staff-compliance benchmarking, and the default crisis workflow that families and owners trust more than ad hoc WhatsApp reconstruction.

## Objections

1. **Trust and resident data are still the gating risk.** An unknown founder can get owner calls from public registers and reviews, but must reduce risk with a nurse reviewer, narrow named-resident scope, DPAs, and no clinical decision claims.
2. **Staff compliance may break the product.** Care workers already face documentation burden. The workflow must be faster than current notes and focused only on high-risk events during the sprint.
3. **Urgency is uneven.** Homes without a live complaint, bad review, inspection pressure, or occupancy/refund risk may postpone. Outreach must target visible trigger events, not the whole market.
4. **Durability is not guaranteed after the crisis.** Retention requires the home to keep using the rail for family response, handovers, and inspection readiness after the immediate dispute cools.
5. **The product can collapse into consulting.** If each home needs custom legal/nursing work, margins and founder time suffer. Templates and event-class workflows must standardize quickly.
6. **Regulatory boundary matters.** It should organize evidence and response quality, not claim to certify care quality or provide legal representation.

## Profitability Veto

No veto. The sprint pricing gives fast cash conversion, gross margins can be attractive if reviewers are used for QA and edge cases rather than every event, CAC can be targeted through public registers and Google-review triggers, and retention has a plausible path through owner risk reduction.

## Verdict

**Advance to real Zero To One validation, narrowly.** This is stronger than the earlier generic CareProof shape because it sells an urgent owner outcome: avoid reconstructing high-risk care events from memory during a family complaint or inspection. The 2-month proof is concrete: two paid homes, mandatory event routing for named residents/classes, measurable staff compliance, and one owner-confirmed economic or complaint-reduction outcome. Do not build broad software before proving paid crisis workflow access.
