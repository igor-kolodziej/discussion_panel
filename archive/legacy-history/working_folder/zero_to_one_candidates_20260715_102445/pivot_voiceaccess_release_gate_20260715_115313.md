# Zero-to-One Pivot: VoiceAccess Release Gate

Created with `$pivot-zero-to-one` from SpeechBridge Workline v1 and its post-panel evaluation. Pivot only — not scored or validated.

## Preserved Insight

Non-standard speech causes measurable failure during economically important voice interactions. A user-controlled or task-grounded test can identify whether the system understood the person's intent, whether a correction/intervention recovered the task, and whether later versions improve. Repeated speech-to-intent and task-outcome records can become valuable AI/HCI infrastructure.

## Structural Change

- **Buyer:** from an individual professional to a European voicebot, IVR, contact-centre, or conversational-AI vendor that sells voice interactions into regulated or high-value service workflows.
- **Painful event:** from each member's important call to the vendor's recurring software release and enterprise acceptance test, where non-standard-speech failures create lost transactions, accessibility risk, and failed customer procurement.
- **Product:** from a persistent phone number and assisted-call subscription to a regression-testing release gate using a contracted panel, reusable consented benchmark utterances, task scripts, and automated replay/evaluation.
- **Value capture:** from 4,000 low-ARPU subscribers to paid diagnostics and annual per-product release-testing contracts.
- **Channel:** from sensitive global consumer/community acquisition to a finite public list of voice-AI vendors, contact-centre integrators, and enterprises operating voice interfaces.
- **Control:** from portable within-person call history to a cross-customer multilingual benchmark, contracted participant panel, version-over-version failure history, and an embedded recurring release workflow.

## Target Niche and Buyer

Initial niche: Polish/CEE vendors with a deployed Polish- or English-language voicebot/IVR product selling into banking, telecom, transport, e-commerce, or other consequential customer-service flows. The budget owner is the product, QA, accessibility, or enterprise-delivery lead who must show that a new release completes representative tasks for people whose speech differs from the model's standard training distribution.

The buyer is the technology vendor first, not a large regulated end enterprise. That shortens access and lets the vendor reuse a successful report in its own enterprise sales. Whether these buyers possess a specific recurring budget is a new hypothesis.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

Every material model, prompt, IVR-flow, or ASR-vendor change can regress task completion for non-standard speech without appearing in ordinary QA. A failed production interaction can lose a sale/service task; a failed accessibility evaluation can delay customer acceptance. The hypothesised willingness to pay is 12,000–20,000 PLN for a fixed diagnostic and 60,000–180,000 PLN annually for release regression across one product/language.

No compliance certification or legal guarantee is sold. The deliverable is reproducible user-outcome evidence, failure recordings permitted by consent, and a ranked remediation/retest report.

## Value and Profit Wedge

First paid offer: a 12,000 PLN Voice Access Failure Sprint against one live or staging voice flow, using eight contracted participants with defined non-standard-speech profiles, five customer-approved tasks each, a standard-speech comparison cohort, and one retest. The buyer receives task completion, turns/time, abandonment, recognition/correction, and severity evidence.

After two paid sprints, turn repeated scenarios and consented reusable utterances into a customer-isolated regression harness. Annual contracts charge per product/language/release cadence. Participant sessions remain a capped validation layer; most releases replay/score the growing benchmark automatically, with human reruns only for meaningful changes. If every release requires a fresh bespoke panel, the model fails.

## Domination Wedge

Become the default Polish non-standard-speech release benchmark before expanding to other CEE languages. Large accessibility-testing firms cover broad interfaces, and ASR vendors test their own models; the wedge is independent, task-level, version-over-version evidence for voice products selling into EU service workflows. Domination requires a denser local benchmark and faster release turnaround than a vendor can assemble internally.

## Acquisition Channel

- Build a named list from public voicebot/IVR vendor directories, conference exhibitor lists, public case studies, app/product pages, and job posts.
- Send a five-task outside-in sample showing one documented failure on the prospect's public demo, followed by the fixed-price sprint. Use only consented researcher speech; do not publicly shame the vendor.
- Target 60 founder-led contacts, 15 technical demonstrations, and one prepaid sprint within 180 days.
- Participant recruitment uses openly advertised paid research calls and condition/disability communities; no organisation endorsement or clinical referral is assumed.
- Later distribution may include accessibility consultancies or enterprise integrators only after direct paid proof; they are not required for cold start.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed compounding Tier 2:** contractually reusable, consented non-standard-speech task benchmark; longitudinal version/failure/remediation results across voice products; a paid participant panel with repeat availability; and customer contracts that route each material release through the regression gate. Customer recordings/configurations remain isolated, while benchmark tasks, participant-provided reusable assets, and de-identified failure taxonomies remain company-controlled under explicit rights.

This is not Tier 1 and is not presently proved. It becomes credible only if at least three independent customers route releases, reused tests reduce marginal delivery cost, and cross-release history catches failures that ordinary QA misses. A buyer or large testing firm can recruit its own panel, so speed, benchmark density, and embedded release history must make internal recreation uneconomic.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Pay and contract 12 participants for a small discovery panel; agreements separately cover one-off testing, reusable benchmark assets, withdrawal/deletion, and de-identified failure analysis.
2. Create ten task-grounded scenarios and a standard-speech comparison; verify outcome scoring without claiming medical representativeness.
3. Test only public demos until a customer prepays; do not handle production customer audio before security terms.
4. Sell one 12,000 PLN sprint before expanding the panel or building CI integrations.
5. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, secure 30 contracted participants, 1,500+ reusable consented test utterances/task traces, three independent annual customers, at least 12 routed releases, and one measured instance where the benchmark catches a paid release regression.

The founder can recruit and pay participants directly, build the harness, and sell to a public vendor list without an authority partnership. A speech-language specialist reviews the panel/task design on a fixed fee; the company makes no diagnostic or certification claim.

## Expansion Path

Polish voicebots → Polish and English IVR/contact-centre products → Czech, Slovak, German, and other European languages → noisy speech, accent, age-related change, and AAC-mediated interactions → procurement-grade human-interaction regression for multimodal agents and intelligent wearables.

Ordinary delivery builds rare speech/behavioral data, evaluation IP, advanced AI/ML, real-time HCI expertise, relationships with paid participants/research specialists, and distribution among human–AI system vendors.

## Minimum People Stack

Founder for AI/evaluation/product/outbound; contract speech-language/accessibility researcher for task and consent review; privacy counsel for participant/customer agreements; participants paid per session and reuse licence; contract security/telephony engineer only after a customer's staging integration. Pre-proof risk target: no more than 30,000 PLN while the founder stays employed and works no more than five hours daily.

## Principal Risk

The business may still be a bespoke accessibility-testing service whose panel and benchmark can be reproduced by established testing firms or in-house QA. The buyer may view non-standard-speech testing as optional, have no recurring release budget, or refuse cross-customer benchmark rights. Regulation may create accessibility pressure without creating demand for this specific evidence.

## First Disconfirming Test

Within 60 days, show a standardised outside-in failure/retest example to 20 qualified voice-product leads and offer the fixed 12,000 PLN sprint. Abandon or materially change the pivot if fewer than five accept a technical review, none exposes a named release/enterprise-acceptance budget, or every interested buyer requires bespoke one-off testing with no reusable benchmark rights.

## Evidence Preserved

- Voiceitt demonstrates commercial non-standard-speech recognition.
- Google reports that Project Relate required more than one million speech samples, confirming technical/data difficulty.
- Apple Personal Voice confirms rapid platform commoditisation of personal speech output, which is why the platform/vendor becomes the buyer rather than the direct competitive threat.
- SpeechBridge's cheap user-controlled call experiment is preserved as task-grounded participant testing, not as a consumer subscription.

## New Hypotheses

- EU accessibility requirements or enterprise procurement create budget for repeated voice-interaction outcome testing; exact legal applicability and evidence requirements need official research.
- At least one of 60 reachable voice-product vendors pays 12,000 PLN within 180 days.
- Participants will grant clear, ethical reuse rights for benchmark tasks at an affordable price while retaining required withdrawal/deletion rights.
- Reusable replay/evaluation reduces participant labour enough to support at least 65% scaled gross margin.
- Forty European product/language contracts at an average 300,000 PLN annual value or an economically equivalent mix are reachable without heroic market share; this needs a bottom-up buyer denominator.
- Cross-release history and benchmark density create enough switching cost to resist broad testing firms and in-house replication.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

The individual with dysarthria is now a paid participant and beneficiary of more accessible systems, not a fragmented low-ARPU subscriber responsible for funding global acquisition. Platform and voicebot vendors become paying customers instead of distribution-controlling substitutes. The recurring controlled event is a product release, the asset is a reusable cross-customer benchmark and version history, and the wealth case must rely on tens of enterprise contracts rather than thousands of sensitive consumer subscriptions. The pivot still fails if it cannot obtain reusable rights, repeated release routing, or productised margins.

## Return-to-Evaluation Priority

This is the only proposed pivot because it directly addresses all shared panel objections while preserving the strongest insight. It should return to `$evaluate-zero-to-one` first, after official research verifies regulatory timing, buyer lists, incumbent alternatives, and a bottom-up conservative wealth denominator. This prioritisation is not a score or validation.
