## Judge A

**Score:** 62/100

### Strongest Objections and Caveats

- The browser-room pilot is a proxy for the persistent business-call number and may miss integration, reliability, inbound-call, and receiver-adoption problems.
- Recognition may perform worst for the target speech; approval/correction can add disruptive cognitive load and latency.
- The 249–349 PLN monthly willingness to pay and the subset of users with frequent important calls, browser tolerance, and ability to pay are unknown.
- Public communities are not yet repeatable distribution; global acquisition of 4,000 members without trusted access could be expensive.
- Captioning, accessibility support, telephony, inference, and refunds may destroy the 70%–75% gross-margin hypothesis if important calls need human help.
- The “conservative” 4,000-member case is a substantial international acquisition success, not conservative without retention, CAC, employer conversion, and support evidence.
- The proof path is credible and inexpensive, but three renewals do not prove a durable dataset advantage.

### Control Point

Proposed Tier 2; present evidence Tier 3/unproved. Longitudinal mapping could become Tier 2 if it produces personalisation lift, retention, and costly-to-recreate history. It is non-exclusive, portable, deletable, and presently has no paid members, histories, lift, or switching-cost evidence.

### Strongest Copy Risk

Apple, Google, Microsoft, Zoom, Teams, a carrier, or Voiceitt can embed personalised recognition, captions, and restatement directly into the call surface they already distribute, while SpeechBridge has no exclusive channel or dataset.

## Judge B

**Score:** 74/100

### Strongest Objections and Caveats

- The pain and prepaid programme are credible, but recurring willingness to pay competes with free captions, platform accessibility features, employer tools, and informal help.
- The conservative wealth case has no bottom-up denominator for 4,000 professionals matching condition, work-call frequency, ability to pay, intervention acceptance, and retention.
- Community access is trust-heavy and may not become a repeatable commercial channel.
- The live workflow has multiple reliability dependencies; failure in any layer damages trust.
- Human support may be structurally necessary for the highest-value calls, turning the company into a specialist interpretation service.
- Personal histories are exportable/deletable and may transfer poorly across users, limiting aggregate compounding.
- Thirty members and 1,000 calls cannot establish scalable acquisition, retention, support, or the wealth denominator.

### Control Point

Compounding Tier 2 at best; present evidence Tier 3/unproved. It requires repeated-call outcome improvement and switching friction that do not yet exist.

### Strongest Copy Risk

The same platform companies and accessibility communications vendors already control distribution, identity, audio routing, speech infrastructure, and customer trust and can subsidise the feature.

## Official Result

**Official simulated score = lower independent score = 62/100.**

The candidate fails the simulated gate. It was not sent to the real Zero to One chatbot, and no confirmed idea file was created.

Both judges independently identify the same fatal shape-level objection: a direct-to-user subscription must build fragmented, sensitive distribution while integrated platforms can bundle the core assistance before the longitudinal history becomes valuable. Both also reject the 4,000-member case as conservative without acquisition, retention, and support evidence. The workflow therefore requires a post-panel advisory evaluation and a material pivot away from the same buyer/channel/control pattern.
