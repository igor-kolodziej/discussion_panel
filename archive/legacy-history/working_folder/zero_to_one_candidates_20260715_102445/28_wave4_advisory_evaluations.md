# Wave 4 Advisory Evaluations

Applied with `$evaluate-zero-to-one`. The company is at idea/planning/discovery stage: no pilots, contracts, proprietary datasets, owned control points, or customer outcomes are assumed unless documented. The evaluator judges whether missing evidence can be produced quickly/affordably, while still penalising implausible assumptions and uncontrolled dependencies. Scores are advisory only.

# Zero-to-One Evaluation: PulsePhantom Bench

## Diagnosis

PulsePhantom converts a documented repeatability/inclusivity problem into a tangible engineering product with a narrow paid-design-partner path. Its strongest feature is the owned fixture, calibration method, and regression library: ordinary sales build future-lab hardware and biosignal capability without requiring a data buyer. The principal uncertainty is whether wearable teams need this integrated bench beyond existing simulators/internal phantoms, and whether an unknown founder can achieve technically credible repeatability with contract optical expertise. The 500-customer conservative scale is unproved but globally plausible enough to test rather than veto at discovery stage.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- Evidence: IEC describes PPG wearable test methods; peer-reviewed studies demonstrate repeatable optical/dynamic phantoms and condition effects; WhaleTeq sells a PPG simulator.
- Hypotheses: three teams prepay, a beta is buildable below 100k, buyers value pressure/motion/optical integration, delivery becomes self-serve, and 500 active organisations are reachable.
- No prototype, freedom-to-operate review, paid reservation, repeatability result, customer outcome, or owned test corpus currently exists.

## Overall Score

Weighted points: 155.5 / 20; `10 × 7.775 = 77.75`, rounded to **78/100**.

## Control Point

**Proposed Tier 1 owned physical/IP control plus Tier 2 compounding data; Tier 3/unproved today.** The strongest copy risk is WhaleTeq or another test-equipment company adding modular pressure/motion/optical phantom cartridges, or an internal R&D team reproducing the fixture. Catch-up becomes harder only after repeatable calibration, broad condition/device support, and version histories produce faster diagnosis.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Provisional pass.** Conservative arithmetic: 11.5m PLN revenue, 2.875m operating profit, 3× value, 75% ownership = 6.469m founder equity. Capital, reinvestment, distributions, timing, and ownership are specified without double count. The buffer fails if 500 active organisations or self-serve 25% margin is implausible; the bottom-up denominator must be researched through the proposed 100-account/channel cohorts.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** A 10,000 PLN sprint/8,000 PLN reservation can be sold using a one-condition demo before the complete product. Pre-proof risk is 35,000 PLN, specialist work follows deposits, and employment/five-hour constraints are explicit.

## Expansion Check

Reflective wrist PPG is a coherent beachhead; pulse-oximetry and other biosignal modalities are gated. Existing simulator vendors move inward easily, so the product must win on physical-condition integration and workflow, not generic waveforms. Local engineering economics help but do not defend.

## Fatal Objections

None identified at discovery-stage pre-screen. Decisive caveats are technical repeatability, willingness to prepay, freedom to operate, and a bottom-up 500-customer route.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Physical human-contact conditions should become a deterministic regression fixture before scarce human studies, and the fixture/history can be sold as owned infrastructure.

## Recommendation

**pursue**

# Zero-to-One Evaluation: HarnessCell

## Diagnosis

HarnessCell fixes the earlier demonstration-data failure by selling a real manufactured output and collecting process data as a by-product. However, current competitors already promise no MOQ, rapid delivery, instant/fast quotes, traceability, engineering review, and IPC-trained workmanship—including in Poland. The proposed vertical revision history may improve operations, but customers buy harness quality/lead time and can switch without losing their product IP. The wealth case requires 60 recurring customers and 20% operating margin in a competitive labour/material business before the data advantage is proved.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- Evidence: accepted IPC standard/training; multiple rapid low-volume suppliers in Poland, Europe, and the US.
- Hypotheses: robotics/wearable specialisation produces higher repeat ordering and margin; customers sign reusable process-data rights; process telemetry lowers quote/training/rework costs.
- Missing: paid orders, repeat-family data, margin, channel conversion, and a qualified 60-customer denominator.

## 16-Area Scorecard

| Area | Score | Weight | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Core insight | 7 | 1 | Product revenue funding data is strong; application is established |
| Customer pain and willingness to pay | 7 | 2 | Urgent hardware delays matter; many suppliers already serve them |
| Business model and economics | 6 | 2 | Deposits help cash; manufacturing margins/labour are brittle |
| Profitable domination potential | 5 | 1 | Narrow vertical, but no exclusion from local incumbents |
| Beachhead quality | 7 | 1 | Clear low-voltage scope and buyer |
| Expansion path | 7 | 1 | Logical assemblies/workcells, but more capital/certification |
| Asymmetric defensibility | 5 | 1.5 | Data/fixtures owned; customers can switch and incumbents can instrument |
| Local or regional advantage | 6 | 0.5 | CEE proximity helps; strong Polish competition |
| Strength of advantage | 5 | 1 | Revision economics are unproved |
| Timing and underused momentum | 6 | 0.5 | AI guidance is cheaper, equally available to competitors |
| Repeatable distribution | 6 | 2 | Public list; no cheap asymmetric channel |
| Durability and profit retention | 5 | 1.5 | Price/capacity competition and labour limit retention |
| Founder fit and minimum people stack | 6 | 0.5 | Software/electrical access helps; production quality adds load |
| Risk clarity and first-test quality | 8 | 0.5 | Paid NRE and revision tests are clean |
| Founder wealth creation potential | 5 | 2 | 60 accounts/20% margin/80% ownership only narrowly clears |
| Reachability and capital efficiency | 8 | 2 | Paid setup/deposit is plausible below 30k |

## Overall Score

Weighted points: 123 / 20; overall **62/100**.

## Control Point

**Tier 3 current; possible compounding Tier 2, despite owned files/data.** Ownership is real if contracted, but the economically relevant control is weak because a competitor can remake a harness from customer drawings. Strongest copy risk: an existing rapid shop adds camera-based digital travellers and a robotics landing page.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** Although arithmetic is complete, the conservative 60-customer/200,000 PLN annual value and 20% operating margin are not conservative in light of direct rapid/low-MOQ competition. The 5.76m result has little buffer and depends on ordinary manufacturing execution before proprietary data has demonstrated value.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** One paid NRE/parts deposit and contract delivery fit 180 days, 30k, and retained employment.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is clear, but incumbent entry is already present. Adjacent assemblies require more certification/capital and do not strengthen initial control automatically.

## Fatal Objections

1. The advertised product is already available from local/global competitors.
2. Process data may improve internal margin but does not create customer lock-in or exclusive supply.
3. Conservative wealth depends on a large recurring book and above-ordinary margins.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Sell an independently valuable physical product whose production necessarily generates owned training data.

## Recommendation

**kill**

# Zero-to-One Evaluation: NeuroPort Uptime Library

## Diagnosis

NeuroPort has an unusually targeted acquisition method and tangible inventory control, and a cancelled research session can be expensive. Yet official manufacturers/distributors already sell upgrades, repairs, accessories, and cross-system adapters; labs can buy the critical spare once rather than pay 48,000 PLN annually. Shared-pool economics depend on repeated configurations and small-vendor procurement acceptance, while subject-safety boundaries sharply limit third-party work. The 250-lab recurring wealth case therefore lacks credibility as a conservative scenario.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- Evidence: BioSemi connector transition/pricing; official repair/upgrade paths; Brain Products' large publication footprint and existing adapters.
- Hypotheses: five labs pay annually, three configurations repeat, a shared pool prevents cancellations, and 250 labs sustain 48,000 PLN/year.
- Missing: cancellation frequency, annual budget, independent-vendor procurement, utilisation, renewal, and fully loaded liability/support costs.

| Core insight | 7 | 1 | Failure cost is session downtime, not component price |
| Customer pain and willingness to pay | 6 | 2 | Pain plausible; annual price versus one-off spares unsupported |
| Business model and economics | 5 | 2 | Pool can share cost; uniqueness/depreciation/support threaten margin |
| Profitable domination potential | 6 | 1 | CEE niche is finite; procurement/manufacturers strong |
| Beachhead quality | 7 | 1 | Exact configurations and research-only scope |
| Expansion path | 7 | 1 | Multimodal lab uptime logical, but capital/service grows |
| Asymmetric defensibility | 5 | 1.5 | Inventory/history owned; parts purchasable and vendor ecosystems controlled elsewhere |
| Local or regional advantage | 6 | 0.5 | Shipping/service proximity helps |
| Strength of advantage | 5 | 1 | Avoided downtime not yet quantified |
| Timing and underused momentum | 7 | 0.5 | Connector transition/multimodal studies create need |
| Repeatable distribution | 7 | 2 | Publication-method targeting is genuinely asymmetric |
| Durability and profit retention | 5 | 1.5 | Labs may buy once or use core facilities/distributors |
| Founder fit and minimum people stack | 6 | 0.5 | Software/inventory fit; neuroscience/electronics trust outsourced |

| Founder wealth creation potential | 4 | 2 | 250 recurring labs at high ACV unsupported |
| Reachability and capital efficiency | 7 | 2 | One audit possible under 30k; procurement timing uncertain |

## Overall Score

Weighted points: 119.5 / 20; overall **60/100**.

## Control Point

**Tier 1 for owned/reserved inventory, but enduring business advantage is Tier 3 until pool density/renewal are proved.** Strongest copy risk: manufacturer/distributor offers a spare plan, or a university core facility stocks the same accessories.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** Complete arithmetic does not make 250 labs at 48,000 PLN/year conservative. Annual WTP, renewal, and service margin are unsupported, and one-off sales cannot substitute for the stated wealth model.

## Reachability Gate

**Marginal pass.** Publication-based outreach, audit, and deposit precede inventory within 30k, but university procurement may exceed six months and independent support must overcome trust.

## Expansion Check

Cross-modal uptime is logical, but capital and configuration diversity expand faster than control. Manufacturer ecosystems can move inward through ordinary spare/service offers.

## Fatal Objections

1. Recurring annual budget versus one-off spare purchase is unproved.
2. Manufacturers/distributors control authorised accessories and upgrades.
3. Safety/liability limits third-party intervention.
4. Wealth denominator and pool utilisation are not conservative.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Publication methods provide a precise, low-cost equipment-specific lab acquisition channel.

## Recommendation

**kill**

## Routing Decision

- Freeze and independently simulate **PulsePhantom Bench** only.
- Kill HarnessCell and NeuroPort Uptime Library; do not panel them.
