## Durable Takeaways

1. Discovery-stage disclosure was applied consistently: judges did not require operating-company proof, but still rejected proof plans whose success would not establish wealth or control.
2. The strongest recurring lesson from prior history survived: the paid wedge must route an economically important recurring event and secure enforceable data/workflow/transaction/inventory/IP/distribution control through ordinary delivery.
3. Ownership of a product, equipment, or data file is not automatically an economic Tier-1 control point. Durability, exclusivity, routing, and buyer switching economics matter.
4. A plausible paid pilot is not enough. Several candidates passed Reachability while failing the independent Founder Wealth Gate.
5. Future search should begin from a pre-existing founder-controlled distribution/resource or a contractually mandatory transaction, not from a technically interesting asset that must first create its own budget.
