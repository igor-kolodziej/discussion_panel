# Zero-to-One Candidate: RingRelay

Candidate generation only — not validated. All commercial numbers are hypotheses to test.

## One-Sentence Thesis

RingRelay is an exact-model-and-size smart-ring recommerce service that removes fit and battery uncertainty with paid reservations, a one-swap guarantee, standardised diagnostics, and contracted buybacks—turning transactions into wearable failure data, inventory expertise, and direct buyer distribution.

## Founder Fit

The founder understands biosignal wearables, data, e-commerce analysis, and experimentation. The first operation needs no manufacturing or OEM agreement: source lawful used devices, test them, document condition, and sell one ring family in Poland. A part-time technician and consumer-law review cover bounded gaps.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first customer wants an Oura-class ring but resists the new-device price or subscription commitment. Used-ring listings are fragmented by exact model, colour, and size; a ring that does not fit is useless, battery condition is opaque, chargers differ, identity/account state may be uncertain, and peer-to-peer returns are unreliable.

Current workarounds are buying new, gambling on an Allegro/OLX/eBay listing, negotiating a private swap, or using broad refurbishers that do not guarantee an immediate exact-size replacement. Sellers face the inverse problem: a slow market for a narrow size and uncertainty about a fair buyback.

## Product Wedge

Start with one current or recent Oura generation and three high-liquidity sizes.

- Buyer pays a 99 PLN reservation/deposit for an exact model/size/colour band.
- RingRelay sources and tests devices for charging, account reset, identity, sensor operation, cosmetic condition, and measured short-cycle battery decline.
- Sale price is typically 950–1,450 PLN with a 14-day legal return right and one additional 30-day size swap from reserved inventory.
- Each sale includes a future six- or twelve-month buyback quote conditional on diagnostic grade.
- Sellers receive a fast fixed offer; trade-ins route future inventory to RingRelay.

No medical accuracy claims are made and no subscription bypass is promised.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Broad recommerce treats wearables like phones, but a smart ring is a thin market split into many exact sizes with concealed battery degradation. The overlooked product is liquidity insurance: reserve the fit, test the device, and guarantee one swap. That shifts the buyer from peer-to-peer risk to a trusted specialist and creates the demand map needed to buy the right inventory instead of guessing.

## Underused Momentum

- Oura reports a multi-million-unit installed base.
- Successive ring generations create upgrade supply and price-sensitive used demand.
- EU battery and repairability policy increases attention to lifecycle, even though it does not provide parts access.
- Marketplaces expose listings and search demand; the startup can begin with reservations rather than a warehouse.

## Beachhead Market

Polish buyers and sellers of one Oura ring generation, initially sizes selected from measured reservation demand. Expand to Germany/Czechia only after Polish inventory turns and return economics are proven. Exclude medical devices and obscure brands during proof.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Average sale hypothesis: 1,150 PLN net revenue per completed device cycle.
- Gross margin hypothesis: 28% after device purchase, payment fees, diagnostics, cleaning, packaging, warranty/return reserve, and average swap shipping.
- Contribution hypothesis: 190–260 PLN per cycle after variable acquisition and support.
- Optional seller inspection fee: 49 PLN, credited if the device is sold to RingRelay.
- Later annual “upgrade loop” membership: 149 PLN for priority buyback and size/generation matching.
- Scaled operating-margin hypothesis: 14%–18%, requiring automation, low return rates, and rapid inventory turns.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

- Create exact landing pages for model/size combinations already appearing on Allegro, OLX, eBay, Google queries, and wearable communities.
- First acquisition offer: 99 PLN refundable-if-unfilled reservation, a published diagnostic standard, exact delivered price band, and one-swap terms.
- Conversion event: five paid reservations across the selected three sizes before buying the first ten units; then ten completed paid sales with at least two signed buyback acceptances.
- First ten: compliant direct responses to public “wanted” posts, search ads to exact-size pages, and seller outreach offering a fixed quote; no inventory is bought merely to look stocked.
- First 100: SEO pages for exact model/size queries, referral credit on completed trade-ins, and buyback reminders. Marketplace sales acquire trust initially, while repeat buyers migrate to the direct reservation and buyback loop.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed Tier 1 at a small scale, not yet proven:** owned exact model/size inventory, paid reservation queue, written buyback commitments, physical device custody, and a device-level diagnostic ledger. It is scarce locally at a moment in time, but incumbents can buy inventory; durability requires faster turns, a denser buyback flow, and better device grading.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Needed resource: the correct exact-size inventory and a trusted diagnostic/return process.

Bootstrap:

1. Publish three exact-size reservation pages and collect five paid deposits.
2. Purchase no more than ten devices from verified sellers within the indicated sizes, reserving 30% of capital for returns and replacements.
3. Use official app/reset flows, charging tests, visual/sensor checks, and a documented battery-observation protocol; do not open sealed devices during the POC.
4. Complete ten independent sales, two swaps, and two buyback commitments; measure days in inventory, return reasons, charge retention, and contribution.
5. Twelve-month control proof: at least 100 completed cycles, 25 trade-ins/buybacks, fewer than 10% preventable returns, and a reservation-to-purchase channel whose gross profit covers acquisition.

An unknown founder can secure used devices and buyers through open marketplaces; no unspecified partnership or parts contract is required for the non-repair POC.

## Distribution Advantage

Exact model/size landing pages capture high-intent search that broad marketplaces fragment. Each transaction produces a future buyback date and referral. A denser local swap pool improves conversion and reduces returns, which lets RingRelay pay sellers faster while maintaining margin.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start as a no-open-device diagnostics and swap business. Expand only after evidence to:

1. more Oura generations and sizes;
2. other rings with proven liquidity;
3. paid battery replacement/repair only after parts, training, safety, and warranty economics are controlled;
4. B2B trade-in and returns services for smaller wearable brands;
5. a specialist wearable reliability index and procurement channel.

Back Market or an OEM can copy a guarantee. The defence must be local size liquidity, buyback contracts, device histories, diagnostic prediction, and customer acquisition economics they do not have in the narrow ring category.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers a large e-commerce market, lower operating cost, and fragmented local listings that a focused buyer can consolidate. EU consumer-return and warranty rules can increase trust when handled transparently. There is no formal protection from global recommerce; the local defence is dense inventory and direct repurchase flow.

## Compounding Advantage

Each device cycle adds model/size demand, days-to-sale, battery observation, sensor/charging faults, cosmetic grade, return reason, swap behaviour, buyback value, and seller reliability. This improves purchasing, pricing, warranty reserves, and future repair decisions. Ordinary delivery builds wearable hardware expertise, proprietary device-reliability data, direct wearable distribution, supplier flow, and cash/inventory capability for a future lab.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution during employment, with optional founder distributions capped at 5%. The strong case reinvests 100%. Reinvestment funds exact-size inventory, return reserves, diagnostics, local fulfilment, buyback acquisition, and later specialist repair capability after proof.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–21: consumer-law review, 30 buyer/seller interviews, diagnostic protocol, and three exact-size pages.
- Days 22–45: acquire five 99 PLN paid reservations; source the first five to ten matching rings only after deposits.
- Days 46–100: complete ten sales, document battery/fit/return outcomes, and secure two future buyback acceptances.
- Days 101–180: complete 30 total cycles, demonstrate positive contribution, inventory turn below 45 days, and a reservation/SEO acquisition cost below gross profit.

Paid evidence is a deposit and completed sale, not a listing click. The founder remains employed; fulfilment is batched around the five-hour daily cap.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Tranche 1: maximum 25,000 PLN before paid proof — legal 2.5k, testing/cleaning/packaging 2.5k, inventory after deposits 12k, return reserve 5k, acquisition 2k, contingency 1k.
- Tranche 2: cumulative 60,000 PLN after ten profitable sales and acceptable returns.
- Tranche 3: up to 100,000 PLN only after 30 sales, repeat demand, at least five trade-ins, and viable acquisition.
- Family capital excluded until multiple independent payers, repeat demand, and contribution economics.
- Maximum pre-proof loss: 25,000 PLN; sellable inventory reduces realised loss but is not assumed liquid at cost.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after at least 300 monthly completed cycles for three months, positive contribution after returns/warranty, inventory turns and acquisition are repeatable across at least three sizes and two supply channels, operations can run without the founder packing every order, and 12 months personal runway exists.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If the category cannot scale, inventory can be liquidated and the founder retains the diagnostic protocol, supplier/buyer list, device histories, and market data within legal permissions. A move into brand returns or repair is a new shape requiring rights, training, and re-evaluation.

## Critical Evidence

- Oura reports more than 5.5 million rings sold.
- Exact sizing and battery care are material parts of the product experience.
- Broad recommerce competitors validate demand while exposing the need for a specialist wedge.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Five paid exact-size reservations precede inventory buying.
- A one-swap guarantee increases conversion enough to cover its cost.
- Standard diagnostics keep preventable returns below 10%.
- A 28% gross margin and sub-45-day inventory turn are attainable.
- 16,000 annual EU cycles are reachable without inventory debt erasing founder wealth.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than five paid reservations after 100 qualified visits or direct prospects.
- Fewer than ten completed positive-contribution sales within 180 days.
- Inventory turn exceeds 60 days, preventable returns exceed 15%, or warranty/return losses remove contribution after 30 cycles.
- No seller accepts a buyback/trade-in mechanism or direct repeat supply after 100 cycles.
- Projected conservative founder equity remains below 5m PLN after real net debt, return, CAC, and labour data replace hypotheses.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Maximum 25,000 PLN before proof; maximum 100,000 PLN founder capital in 12 months.
- Founder: demand, pricing, diagnostics analytics, sourcing, five hours daily.
- Part-time fulfilment/technician help only after sales.
- Fixed-fee consumer-law/accounting review.
- No OEM partnership, custom hardware, or clinical relationship required for POC.
