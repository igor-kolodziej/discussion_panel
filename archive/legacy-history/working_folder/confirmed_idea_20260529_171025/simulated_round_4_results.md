## Why It Failed

The reviewer accepted that cyber-insurance underwriting has become more evidence-heavy and that the live bind/renewal blocker is sharper than generic cyber readiness. The broker-routed, prepaid named-case structure is meaningfully better than a dashboard or broad consulting offer.

The idea still failed the `>85` simulated gate because:

- broker routing may be weaker than assumed;
- case fees after broker share, reviewer cost, founder QA, liability control, and MSP/client chasing may not create enough owner earnings;
- 5,000 PLN rescue pricing is believable, but repeated 15,000 PLN SME willingness to pay is uncertain without a strong commercial consequence;
- annual refreshes create weak retention unless brokers repeatedly route cases;
- the pack format, task routing, and evidence taxonomy are copyable by brokers, MSPs, cyber consultants, and existing cyber-insurance evidence products;
- liability boundaries can drift into certification, policy advice, control attestation, or implementation pressure.

## Duplicate-Risk Assessment

The reviewer saw medium-low strategic duplicate risk versus DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because the buyer and trigger differ:

- DORA serves software vendors blocked by regulated-finance ICT supplier/procurement evidence.
- BindProof serves insured SMEs and brokers blocked by cyber-insurance underwriting/binder evidence.

The operational similarity remains high enough that this would compete for the same founder execution capacity unless paid validation is unusually strong.

## Pivot

Do not real-gate another evidence-pack shape unless the candidate has stronger routed demand than broker panic plus fewer labor/liability leakages. The next candidate must either:

- control payment/assignment rather than merely evidence;
- be embedded inside a buyer-paid transaction where outcome is not dependent on third-party discretionary acceptance;
- or have a sharper channel mandate than a broker referral.
