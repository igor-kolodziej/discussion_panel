# Round 5 Finalist

After real validation failures on lithium quarantine (`66`) and MV cable fault slots (`68`), the pivot moves away from regulated handoff workflows and emergency contractor capacity. The new candidate must let the founder directly secure the scarce asset during the first two months.

## Orphan Automation Recovery Vault

Target buyer: small Polish waterworks, wastewater operators, cold stores, food/packaging factories, feed mills, sawmills, farms/biogas sites, and small industrial plants running critical equipment on orphaned or poorly documented PLC/HMI/VFD/servo systems.

Hidden insight: for many small industrial sites, the catastrophic failure point is not "finding an electrician" or "buying a PLC." It is losing the last runnable machine logic, HMI project, drive parameters, I/O map, passwords, cabinet wiring context, cable adapters, and compatible obsolete modules. When a CPU, HMI panel, VFD, or servo drive dies and no one has a current backup, the site can be forced into emergency reverse engineering or full migration. That creates downtime and gives incumbent integrators pricing power.

Product wedge: a "Last Known Good" disaster-recovery vault for orphaned automation. A qualified automation technician extracts readable PLC programs, HMI projects, VFD/servo parameter sets, module configuration, cabinet photos, network settings, I/O lists, and wiring context. The startup stores the files in an encrypted customer-owned escrow, creates a restoration runbook, identifies compatible donor modules, and reserves tested donor hardware for selected installed bases.

The founder does not perform live control work. Qualified automation partners handle extraction and restoration. The founder owns the data workflow, escrow process, donor inventory, customer-specific emergency file, compatibility database, retainer contract, and dispatch coordination.

Primary control point:

- customer-authorized executable backups and parameter files;
- cabinet/module/I/O maps and restoration runbooks;
- owned tested donor PLC/HMI/VFD/servo modules for narrow families;
- signed annual update and emergency-retainer contracts;
- non-circumvention around backup extraction, donor inventory, and emergency dispatch;
- growing compatibility/failure database across orphaned machines.

Beachhead:

Start with one narrow regional segment within 90-120 minutes of Ostrow Wielkopolski/Kalisz/Konin/Lodz: small waterworks/wastewater operators and cold-chain/food plants using Siemens S7-200/S7-300, older Siemens panels, Omron, Mitsubishi, Schneider/Altivar, or similar systems with no verified backups.

Two-month proof:

- hire one credible automation technician/advisor on day-rate;
- buy 20-40k PLN of adapters, software access, and tested donor modules for 1-2 common families;
- extract and escrow backups from 8-12 sites;
- sell 4-6 paid disaster-recovery audits at 2-5k PLN each;
- convert 2-3 into annual retainers with named donor reservation at 5-12k PLN/year;
- run one bench restore or mock emergency restore from a saved backup onto donor hardware;
- produce signed customer proof that the startup holds the current emergency restore pack.

Why an unknown founder might secure it:

The initial sale is not "trust me to repair your factory." It is a written, low-disruption disaster-recovery audit: prove whether the site has a current backup and create one before failure. The founder can sell through written outreach to overlooked regional operators, use parents' local industrial/OZE network for introductions, and put a credible automation technician in front for technical trust. Capital stays under 100k because the startup buys narrow donor inventory and pays day-rate experts rather than building a repair fleet.

Main risks:

- existing automation integrators can offer backups if motivated;
- some OEMs/integrators may restrict source-code access or passwords;
- the backup may be stale unless annual update discipline exists;
- the founder needs a credible technician as technical face;
- customer willingness to pay before failure must be proven;
- safety/liability boundaries must be explicit because the founder does not alter live control logic.

Strongest copy risk:

Local automation integrators copy the backup-retainer bundle. Defense must be customer-specific escrow files, tested donor inventory, narrow regional orphan-machine focus, low-friction written outreach, and a growing compatibility/restoration database across neglected sites that broad integrators do not prioritize.
