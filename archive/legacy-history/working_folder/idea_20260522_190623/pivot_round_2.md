# Pivot Round 2

Round 1 failed because the strongest ideas still had soft control points, incumbent copy risk, regulatory liability, or inventory risk.

## Strongest New Pattern

Move from service/resource matching to **exclusive rights over scarce operational bottlenecks**:

- grid connection rights;
- removal/disposal capacity;
- warranty claim routing;
- prepaid recall/response mandates;
- owner-authorized revenue/settlement ledgers.

## New Candidate Pool

### 1. Underused Connection-Rights Option Bank

Target owners of underused OZE/MV connection capacity: small PV farms, old biogas/wind/PV sites, farms, agro-businesses, and SMEs with scarce connection agreements or grid-access assets.

Buyer: renewable developers who value interconnection more than land.

Control point:

- signed 6-12 month exclusive mandates/options from connection owners;
- connection agreement and technical conditions;
- meter/export history;
- site map and land/path notes;
- owner consent;
- structured data room for developer review.

Why stronger than prior OZE ideas:

- It controls a scarce right before a developer can reach it.
- It is not a service call, report, or inventory bank.
- A developer cannot copy a signed owner option with one vendor contract.

Two-month proof:

- 3 signed exclusive mandates/options;
- 1 complete connection-right data room;
- 1 developer LOI, paid review, or success-fee mandate.

### 2. Asbestos Removal Capacity Options

Target asbestos-cement roof owners, gminas, farms, housing co-ops, and small industrial property owners in central Poland.

Control point:

- reserved certified removal crew days;
- reserved hazardous-waste/disposal acceptance slots;
- owner deposit queue;
- mapped roof inventory.

Why stronger than an asbestos report:

- The scarce asset is removal/disposal capacity, not identification.
- NIK found the national removal pace far behind the 2032 target.

Two-month proof:

- 2 certified contractor capacity reservations;
- 1 disposal facility slot arrangement;
- 50 owner deposits or one paid gmina/co-op pilot;
- first 5 removals executed or scheduled with chain-of-custody evidence.

### 3. Manufacturer/OEM Warranty Runoff Administrator

Target under-served inverter, battery, heat-pump, or PV component importers/OEMs with Polish devices installed through fragmented installers.

Control point:

- authorized service/admin agreement;
- serial registry;
- manufacturer-routed claims;
- spare parts access;
- approved technician routing.

Two-month proof:

- one pilot agreement with a niche OEM/importer;
- routed claim intake for one brand/region;
- parts consignment;
- 10 live claims/paid out-of-warranty diagnostics.

### 4. Small-OZE Curtailment And Negative-Price Revenue Assurance

Target 50 kW-1 MW OZE producers without professional asset management.

Control point:

- owner-authorized interval data, invoices, seller contracts, support-regime records, curtailment/negative-price matching, and correction/claim history.

Two-month proof:

- 5 complete producer data rooms;
- at least 8-10k PLN/year recoverable/preventable value per 500 kW-scale asset;
- one submitted correction/claim packet.

### 5. GPSR Recall Execution Escrow

Target non-EU DTC brands selling low-complexity consumer products into the EU.

Control point:

- pre-authorized recall mandate;
- EU order data access;
- prepaid recall escrow;
- contracted returns/destruction logistics.

Two-month proof:

- 20 annual retainers;
- 10 brands with order-export/API access;
- two mock recalls.

### 6. Feeder Constraint Atlas For OZE Sales

Target local OZE installers and farm/SME developers wasting sales time on sites that later hit DSO connection or voltage constraints.

Control point:

- verified address/transformer-level connection outcome data from installer archives, DSO letters, voltage-trip logs, and quote failures.

Two-month proof:

- 150 verified addresses;
- 40 transformer areas;
- 10 paid pre-checks.
