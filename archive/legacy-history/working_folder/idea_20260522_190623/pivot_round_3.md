# Pivot Round 3 Scout

Round 2 failed because all candidates were useful but still too close to reports, conditional reservations, revocable vendor roles, or non-owned transaction paths. Round 3 moves outside the founder's obvious sectors and prioritizes hard first assets:

- signed reserved capacity;
- owned physical inventory;
- specialist access that is explicitly prepaid or optioned;
- evidence custody or routed legal workflow;
- non-public operating data that compounds because customers get direct economic value back.

## 1. Archaeology Stop-Work Rescue Option Desk

Buyer:

- small and mid-size developers, road/utility contractors, landowners, and construction project managers doing earthworks in heritage-risk zones;
- first beachhead: Wielkopolska / Lodzkie small developers and utility contractors who cannot absorb a 2-6 week surprise stoppage.

Hidden insight:

- Developers do not want "archaeology consulting"; they want a construction-delay option. The painful moment is when earthworks uncover a possible monument/site and work must stop, secure the place, notify authorities, and wait for qualified archaeologists / conservator decisions.
- Large infrastructure contractors already budget this. Smaller developers often treat it as rare, then lose far more in idle crews, equipment, financing delays, and buyer penalties than they would have paid for a pre-arranged rescue lane.

Control point:

- signed capacity options with 2-3 qualified archaeological field teams;
- pre-agreed emergency rates, response times, documentation templates, and non-circumvention;
- a conservator-ready evidence/custody workflow: site photos, GPS points, stop-work log, find handling, notification packet, and handoff documentation;
- optional retainer from developers/contractors for a named project/site, not generic advisory.

Why the founder can secure it in 2 months under 100k PLN:

- This is mostly contract work, not capital equipment. Use 20-40k PLN to reserve archaeologist days and pay a heritage-law template review.
- The founder can scrape/map WUOZ notices, monument registers, planned earthworks, permits, and developer projects, then approach only risk-exposed projects with a "delay option" offer.
- The first proof is not broad adoption. It is 2 signed archaeologist capacity options plus 3 paid project retainers from contractors/developers where the value is avoiding idle-site delay.

Copy risk:

- Existing archaeology firms can sell direct once they see the wedge. Defense has to be routed demand plus reserved cross-firm availability and the evidence workflow, not a website.
- If POC cannot get enforceable response-time terms and contractor retainers, cap below 85.

Likely Zero-To-One score range:

- 86-92.
- It can cross 90 only if capacity is truly reserved/prepaid and at least one buyer pays before an incident.

## 2. HPLC / GC Module Loaner Bank For Accredited Labs

Buyer:

- small ISO 17025 testing labs, food/environmental labs, pharma QC subcontractors, university commercialization labs, and private analytical labs using aging Agilent/Waters/Shimadzu HPLC or GC systems.

Hidden insight:

- Labs do not only fear repair cost. They fear losing accredited throughput and customer trust when a pump, detector, autosampler, column oven, or workstation module fails.
- OEM service and used-part markets exist, but small labs often lack a local, documented, ready-to-run loaner/exchange option that preserves calibration, maintenance, and audit evidence while the original module is repaired.

Control point:

- owned tested inventory of the 5-10 highest-failure legacy modules;
- reserved service engineer hours;
- calibration/qualification evidence packet tied to each loaner module;
- customer instrument registry and failure history that improves stocking decisions.

Why the founder can secure it in 2 months under 100k PLN:

- Start narrow: one brand/generation, e.g. Agilent 1100/1200 autosamplers/pumps or Waters Alliance modules.
- 60-80k PLN can buy a small used/tested module pool if sourced carefully; 10-20k PLN reserves a freelance service engineer and produces qualification paperwork templates.
- Validation is concrete: 3 labs sign paid emergency membership or deposit; 2 loaner swaps happen; every loaner ships with documented serial, test run, maintenance state, and return conditions.
- Founder fit is decent because the defensibility is data and operating discipline: failure frequencies, compatible modules, qualification docs, utilization, and customer instrument maps.

Copy risk:

- OEMs, used-lab-equipment dealers, and independent service engineers can copy parts of this. Defense requires local same/next-day inventory plus audit-ready documentation and recurring lab membership.
- If the POC becomes "we can source parts when asked," cap below 80. The hard control is owned inventory plus reserved technician capacity.

Likely Zero-To-One score range:

- 87-93.
- It has >90 potential if the POC proves customers pay to reserve availability before failures, not only after panic.

## 3. Counterfeit Test-Buy Evidence Custody For Underprotected EU Brands

Buyer:

- small and mid-size EU brands selling on Allegro, Amazon, CEE marketplaces, Instagram shops, and independent webshops;
- law firms handling IP/trademark enforcement for brands too small for global brand-protection vendors.

Hidden insight:

- Small brands do not lack infringement screenshots. They lack court/platform-grade evidence: timestamped listing capture, anonymous test purchase, sealed physical sample, chain-of-custody, marketplace seller identity clues, and a lawyer-ready packet before the listing disappears.
- Big brand-protection vendors exist, but many underprotected CEE brands need a narrow "buy, preserve, prove" workflow rather than a global monitoring platform.

Control point:

- routed legal workflow from 2-3 IP law firms;
- notary/bailiff-ready web evidence protocol;
- controlled test-purchase identities, shipping addresses, sealed storage, photo/video intake, and sample custody logs;
- physical evidence inventory that the infringer cannot erase after takedown.

Why the founder can secure it in 2 months under 100k PLN:

- Capital need is low: notary/bailiff protocols, storage, purchase floats, packaging/seals, and a few test purchases.
- The founder can use data skills to identify repeat seller clusters and generate prioritized evidence targets, but the product sold is not a dashboard; it is a custody packet with a physical sample.
- Two-month proof: 2 IP law firms route cases; 10 paid test-buy packets; 3 packets lead to marketplace takedown, settlement demand, customs/police complaint, or civil filing preparation.

Copy risk:

- Established brand-protection investigators and IP firms already offer parts of this. The wedge must avoid head-on enterprise monitoring and win by being cheaper, faster, Poland/CEE-native, and procedurally reliable for smaller brands.
- If law firms refuse to route cases or notary/bailiff evidence is too slow/expensive, cap below 85.

Likely Zero-To-One score range:

- 84-91.
- It only reaches the top of the range if routed law-firm flow is signed and custody packets produce actions brands already value.

## Research Notes

- Polish WUOZ offices publish forms and procedures for archaeological research/supervision permits and conservator permissions, confirming that archaeology/heritage work is a formal administrative workflow rather than informal advice.
- Public project environmental-management materials describe immediate stop-work, securing finds, notification, archaeological documentation/research, and further authority-guided handling when archaeological objects/sites are discovered during works.
- ISO 17025 equipment control requires equipment identity, calibration/maintenance records, and evidence that equipment was fit for use when testing was performed; this supports the loaner-bank wedge as an audit/documentation problem, not just a repair problem.
- Used HPLC modules are available in Europe at single-module prices compatible with a sub-100k PLN starter pool, but exact viable SKU selection needs calls with service engineers before buying inventory.
- Polish legal commentary and notary offices describe notarial protocols for securing website/email/SMS evidence, and CEE brand-protection firms already market test purchases for counterfeit evidence, confirming both demand and copy risk.
