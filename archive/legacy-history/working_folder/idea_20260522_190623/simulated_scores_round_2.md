## Candidate Notes

### Underused Connection-Rights Option Bank

Strongest insight: grid access may be more valuable than land, and Polish cable-pooling / connection-refusal data supports scarcity.

Fatal cap: connection rights may not be cleanly transferable or monetizable; OSD approval remains the gate; developers will not pay for weak owner access; owner acquisition and success-fee cycles may be slow.

Could exceed 90 only if the POC proves:

- 3 enforceable exclusive owner options or mandates;
- developer-grade connection-rights data room;
- paid developer review or success-fee mandate;
- lawyer-confirmed transaction structure;
- at least one technically feasible site.

### Asbestos Removal Capacity Options

Strongest insight: the scarce asset is trusted removal/disposal execution capacity before the 2032 deadline, not asbestos detection.

Fatal cap: true bottleneck may be roof-replacement financing, not removal capacity; gmina procurement and contractor copy risk may crush broker margin; field operations are hard under the founder's 5h/day constraint.

Could exceed 90 only if the POC proves:

- binding contractor and disposal reservations;
- owner or institutional non-refundable deposits;
- executed removals or fully scheduled removals with chain-of-custody evidence.

### Small-OZE Curtailment / Negative-Price Revenue Assurance

Strongest insight: unmanaged small RES owners may lose or risk settlement value across PSE, OSDs, sellers, support regimes, redispatch, invoices, and negative-price corrections.

Fatal cap: recoverable money is unproven; data-room intake may become manual consulting; O&M, energy-law, accounting, or asset-management firms can copy a checklist.

Could exceed 90 only if the POC proves:

- systematic 8-10k PLN/year recoverable or preventable value per around 500 kW asset;
- paid audits;
- accepted correction or useful written denial;
- recurring monitoring commitment;
- repeatable acquisition from installers, accountants, or O&M referrals.

### Manufacturer/OEM Warranty Runoff Administrator

Strongest insight: Poland's fragmented RES installer base leaves niche OEMs/importers with weak warranty operations and dirty serial/device records.

Fatal cap: the OEM owns the real control point; this risks becoming claims BPO; live claims and angry consumers are operationally heavy for a 5h/day founder.

Could exceed 90 only if the POC proves:

- signed exclusive OEM/importer pilot;
- routed inbound claims;
- serial/device record access;
- parts consignment;
- paid out-of-warranty diagnostics;
- evidence the OEM sees the company as Polish warranty infrastructure, not temporary cleanup labor.

### GPSR Recall Execution Escrow

Strongest insight: GPSR sellers buy compliance, but the scary unsolved moment is an actual EU consumer recall.

Fatal cap: buyers may not feel pain before an incident; retainers do not fund true recall costs; EU responsible-person providers can add recall readiness; legal liability and escrow/payment handling are non-trivial.

Could exceed 90 only if the POC proves:

- paid retainers with real order-data access;
- real recall mandates;
- higher-price incident drawdown / escrow formula;
- mock recalls that produce authority-facing evidence packs, consumer notices, remedy workflows, return/destruction proof, and timeline logs.

### Feeder Constraint Atlas For OZE Sales

Strongest insight: family installer archives plus local DSO outcome data could outperform public maps for pre-visit PV feasibility checks.

Fatal cap: official DSO maps are improving; prediction may be too probabilistic for paid decisions; 150 addresses is only a POC; data rights and privacy require clean handling; small installers may be weak buyers.

Could exceed 90 only if the POC proves:

- paid checks by non-family customers;
- continuous installer case contribution;
- changed behavior before site visits;
- materially better prediction than public Enea / DSO maps;
- renewals or multi-check purchases without heavy founder hand-holding.

## Round 2 Common Fatal Objections

The repeated objection across 2+ candidates is not lack of customer pain. It is that the first control point is still conditional:

- a reservation rather than a binding right;
- a report rather than routed workflow;
- a useful data room rather than an owned transaction path;
- a service promise rather than prepaid or mandated execution capacity;
- a one-off audit rather than a recurring ledger or system of record.

## Pivot Instruction

Round 3 should only include candidates where the two-month proof can secure one of:

- signed exclusivity over a scarce owner-held asset;
- routed inbound transaction flow;
- prepaid capacity or mandatory workflow access;
- legally required evidence custody;
- physical inventory or specialized talent reservation that cannot be copied by buying generic software;
- proprietary non-public data that continuously refreshes because contributors get direct economic value back.

Avoid candidates whose first monetization is merely a report, dashboard, map, generic audit, broker introduction, or compliance checklist.
