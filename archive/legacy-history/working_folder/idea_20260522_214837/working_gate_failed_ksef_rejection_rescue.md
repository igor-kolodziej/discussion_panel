## Exact Score Rationale Summary
The working validator said the cap-below-82 rule did not apply because the control point is concrete: accepted KSeF number/UPO, routed incident queue, rejected XML samples, vendor-specific fix notes, and a growing rejection-pattern corpus. It still scored the idea 81 because the emergency market may be a rollout spike, response-time expectations are difficult for a 5h/day founder, and ERP/accounting vendors will absorb common failure patterns over time.

## Strongest Caveats
- The market may be a temporary spike unless converted into recurring preflight, monitoring, mapping intelligence, or outsourced KSeF operations.
- "Same-day" emergency support is hard within the founder's 5h/day constraint.
- XML fixes can cross into tax/accounting semantics, so an accountant/KSeF practitioner must approve content boundaries.
- KSeF acceptance does not mean the invoice is substantively correct.
- ERP vendors are both potential partners and direct threats.
- Accounting offices may expect free vendor support unless the desk is clearly faster than vendor ticket queues.
- Sensitive invoice XML and authorization context require DPA, secure upload, retention/deletion rules, and audit trails.
