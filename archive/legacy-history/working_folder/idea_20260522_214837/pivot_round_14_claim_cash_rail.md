# Pivot Round 14 - Carrier Claim Cash Rail

## Why This Pivot

Many previous candidates were useful paid services but failed because the founder did not control a hard enough resource. The prior unconfirmed CarrierProof idea had a stronger shape because a 3PL can route repeated parcel exceptions and recovery authority. It still failed real validation at `81/100` under the old `85` gate because the control point was contractual/revocable, the 3PL could internalize the process, per-claim value might be too small, and the founder was mostly filing claims.

This pivot changes the economic control point. The startup does not merely file claims. It buys or advances against evidence-complete carrier claims where delegation/assignment is permitted, pays the merchant/3PL quickly, and owns the claim economics and outcome data. Where assignment is not legally/contractually possible, the product falls back to a success-fee mandate, but those cases do not count as the strongest proof.

## Candidate

**Carrier Claim Cash Rail** is a small-claim recovery-and-advance rail embedded into one Polish ecommerce 3PL's merchant operations. The beachhead is domestic DPD, DHL, and InPost parcels for fashion/beauty/home D2C merchants where the 3PL already has shipment metadata, warehouse packout/return photos, delivery-status records, damage notes, and merchant authorization.

The offer is not generic claims outsourcing and not parcel-management SaaS. The 3PL adds a default opt-in recovery clause for a pilot merchant cohort. Every week, eligible lost/damaged/short-paid parcel cases flow into the rail. For evidence-complete cases, the rail either:

1. pays the merchant/3PL an immediate fixed cash advance or purchase price for a documented claim right/settlement entitlement where allowed, then pursues the carrier payout and keeps the spread; or
2. if assignment/advance is not allowed, files under delegated authority for a success fee, but separately tracks those cases as weaker.

The first category is the control-point proof: assigned/delegated claim economics, not just paperwork.

## Wedge

Small merchants do not care about a better claims dashboard. They want cash and no admin. A 3PL does not want to hire a claims specialist, but it does want a value-added service that says: "eligible carrier losses are converted into cash or handled automatically."

The hidden insight is that the scarce resource is not claim-form knowledge. It is the combination of routed exception flow, evidence at the warehouse edge, merchant authorization, and the willingness to take small claim economics onto the rail's own book after scoring expected value.

## Primary Control Point

- Signed 3PL pilot agreement.
- Default opt-in merchant authorization/addendum for one cohort.
- Weekly routed exception feed.
- Evidence-complete claim purchase/advance rights where permitted.
- Claim outcome data by carrier, claim type, evidence completeness, and appeal path.
- A small underwriting/triage model that decides which claims deserve immediate cash versus success-fee handling versus rejection.

## Unit Economics

- Claim purchase/advance: pay 50-70% of conservative expected approved recovery; target 20-35% gross spread after rejection/appeal losses.
- Success-fee fallback: 25-40% of recovered carrier compensation/credit.
- 3PL revenue share: 10-20% of net spread or success fee to keep the channel aligned.
- Two-month profit target: 150-250 screened cases, 50+ evidence-complete submissions, 20+ assigned/advanced cases, first 8,000-15,000 PLN gross profit/spread or formally approved recoveries, unrecovered cash exposure capped at 20,000 PLN.

## Two-Month Proof

- One signed 3PL pilot.
- Ten merchants default-opted or signed into the recovery clause.
- Written legal/terms review of whether DPD/DHL/InPost claim proceeds can be assigned/advanced in the chosen setup.
- Ninety days of historical exception records screened.
- At least 50 evidence-complete claims submitted.
- At least 20 cases where the rail controls economics through assignment, advance, settlement entitlement, or an equivalent written mechanism.
- First recoveries/credits/approved claims reconciled.
- Labor per claim below 20 minutes after first template pass.

## Why It Is Not The Existing Unconfirmed CarrierProof Idea

CarrierProof DPD/DHL Recovery Rail was an admin recovery service with exclusive delegated filing rights. This version is a claim-economics rail: it pays/advances/purchases qualified claim value where permitted and uses underwriting plus capital discipline as the wedge. The stronger proof is not "claims submitted"; it is "the startup owns the economics of repeated small claims because the 3PL and merchants route them by default."

## Kill Criteria

- Carrier terms, Polish law, or practical settlement mechanics prevent assignment/advance/settlement-control for the first claim classes.
- No 3PL agrees to default opt-in merchant authorization.
- Fewer than 20 evidence-complete cases can be economically controlled in two months.
- Gross spread after rejections and appeals is below founder labor cost.
- The 3PL wants only a free audit or software, not routed claims/economics.
- The process cannot stay under 20,000 PLN unrecovered cash exposure.
