## Real Gate Verdict

## Key Feedback

The working chat judged this stronger than prior workflow ideas because it starts from ownership/control of recurring administrative relationships rather than a commoditized workflow. It saw the core insight as real: many small Polish entities buy outsourced remembering, continuity, custody, and administrative inertia rather than legal expertise.

The score was capped below `82` because:

- legal/control structure over customer books is not yet proven;
- customer transfer consent friction may be underestimated;
- archive/mail custody and recurring billing rights are still theoretical until proven in actual handovers;
- if the company becomes a lead broker to accountants/lawyers, margins and defensibility collapse.

## Strongest Objections

- Customer books may be goodwill, not a controlled renewal stream, unless consent, contact rights, recurring billing, and archive/mail custody transfer cleanly.
- Book quality can be poor: stale entities, unreachable owners, inactive/dissolved companies, underpriced retainers, and undocumented liabilities.
- The "not legal advice" boundary is necessary but thin; customers may still perceive the company as accountable for filings.
- Mail custody, archives, signatures, notaries, and reminders can become document chaos without strong SOPs.
- Government digitization/AI tools may compress some low-end admin value, though continuity/custody still matters.

## Pivot Instruction

Do not polish broad entity-renewal reminders. Pivot to the concrete control point the real judge identified as strongest: **registered-office / mail-custody continuity books**, where changing provider forces address-change friction, historical mail context matters, and recurring invoices are clearer.
