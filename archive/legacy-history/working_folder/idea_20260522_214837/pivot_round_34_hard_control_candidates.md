## Why This Batch

The last several failures were useful services with weak or contestable control points: tax/accounting acceptance, collector willingness, broker routing, regulatory final decisions, and customer trust all acted as veto points. This batch prioritizes things the founder can secure within two months: owned inventory, prepaid or optioned capacity, exclusive mandates, route acceptance, and emergency buyer demand.

## Finalists

### 1. CriticalControls Spares Bank

A Polish emergency reserve and tested spare-parts bank for obsolete industrial automation modules used by municipal water/sewage plants, district-heating plants, food processors, and older factories: Siemens S5/S7-300, ET200, discontinued HMIs, drives, I/O cards, power supplies, encoders, and PLC-adjacent components. The startup buys, tests, serializes, photographs, and holds a narrow inventory of high-failure modules, sourced from modernization contractors removing legacy cabinets and EU used-equipment markets. Buyers pay for guaranteed immediate replacement, reserved access, and emergency delivery, not generic marketplace browsing.

Control point: owned tested inventory, first-refusal/option agreements with modernization contractors, serial/provenance/test records, and prepaid emergency-reserve contracts with maintenance firms or plants.

Two-month proof: select 20-40 SKUs from interviews with 20 maintenance firms/plants; buy 40-100 modules under 50,000 PLN; sign 3 contractor first-refusal agreements; sell or reserve 5 modules above generic used-market price; sign 3 prepaid reserve contracts; verify testing through a contracted automation technician.

Hidden insight: many Polish municipal/industrial plants cannot modernize every legacy control cabinet on schedule, so a 20-year-old module can be more valuable in a Monday outage than a new automation project.

### 2. MedStart Slot Bank

A mandatory occupational-medicine appointment capacity bank for Polish staffing agencies, warehouses, security firms, food-production plants, cleaning firms, and logistics operators that lose start dates because new workers cannot complete required medycyna pracy exams quickly enough. The startup prepays or minimum-guarantees fixed same-day/next-day appointment blocks at small occupational-medicine clinics, standardizes referral packets, and resells prepaid legal-start capacity to employers and staffing agencies.

Control point: optioned/prepaid appointment blocks, clinic service-level commitments, employer prepaid start packets, no-show/start-date data, and recurring worker-flow agreements.

Two-month proof: 3-6 clinics option 200-500 slots/month; 3 staffing agencies or employers buy prepaid blocks of 50-150 exams/month; 30,000-60,000 PLN used for minimum guarantees; 200 exams routed; gross margin after no-shows and support is positive.

Hidden insight: the buyer is not buying medicine; they are buying legal start-date certainty for low-margin roles where one delayed cohort breaks shift planning.

### 3. LiftDonor Parts Harvest

A donor-vehicle spare-parts business for one narrow family of electric forklifts, pallet trucks, reach trucks, or warehouse vehicles used heavily in Poland. The startup options or buys end-of-life/non-running units, harvests controllers, chargers, batteries, mast parts, wheels, forks, pumps, handles, sensors, and boards, then sells tested traceable parts to independent repair shops with emergency availability.

Control point: owned donor vehicles, first-refusal disposal agreements with repair shops/leasing fleets/warehouse operators, teardown/test records, and pre-sold parts demand.

Two-month proof: interview 20 repair shops, select one model family, option or buy 3-6 donor units under 70,000 PLN, pre-sell 10-20 parts before teardown, and prove gross margin with parts plus scrap downside.

Hidden insight: repair shops often need cheap, immediate donor parts for old warehouse vehicles, but the supply is fragmented and undocumented; the scarce asset is not the listing, it is inspected donor-unit control.

### 4. DepositRouter Settlement Rail

A deposit and loss-settlement rail for reusable packaging between one regional producer/wholesaler and small retailers: crates, kegs, insulated boxes, glass bottles, Euro containers, or similar returnable assets. The startup becomes the accepted third-party ledger for one asset class, reconciles deliveries/returns/losses/disputes, and routes monthly net deposit settlements or recovery packets.

Control point: signed mandate over a live producer/wholesaler settlement file, counterparty acceptance of the ledger as source of truth, recurring transaction history, and disputed-deposit recovery data.

Two-month proof: one producer/wholesaler with 30-100 counterparties, one asset class, 4-6 weeks live settlement, 30,000 PLN disputed deposits resolved or prevented, and 10,000 PLN/month equivalent fees or retained recovery share.

Hidden insight: returnable packaging loss is treated as operational noise, but for fragmented routes the trusted settlement record becomes more valuable than another asset tracker.

### 5. PropertyTaxBack Industrial Refund Mandate

A success-fee/fixed-audit refund desk for Polish SME owners of warehouses, factories, logistics yards, hotels, retail parks, and mixed-use industrial properties that may be overpaying local real-estate tax because declarations do not match current floor plans, EGiB/land-building records, technical exclusions, unusable areas, renovations, mezzanines, building-use category, or municipal rates. It starts with one property type and uses a property-tax lawyer only for claim review.

Control point: signed refund mandate, property/tax document dataset, municipality response/refund database, and reusable property-classification rules by asset type.

Two-month proof: 5-10 owners, 20 properties, 300,000 PLN recoverable value identified, 2-3 correction/refund packets lawyer-approved, one signed success-fee mandate or paid audit.

Hidden insight: many industrial owners view local property tax as boring fixed overhead, while Polish municipal classification quirks can hide recurring overpayments that are large enough to share.

### 6. PFRON Payroll Leakage Recovery

A payroll/subsidy recovery desk for Polish employers of disabled workers in cleaning, security, BPO, manufacturing, care, and protected-work businesses. It audits SODiR/PFRON wage-subsidy history for missed/reduced/denied months caused by disability-degree changes, employee working-time errors, late filings, ZUS/tax arrears flags, de minimis limits, and documentation gaps, then creates correction packs with a payroll/PFRON specialist.

Control point: signed recovery mandate over employer subsidy history, recurring monthly subsidy workflow, employee-status change calendar, and denial/correction dataset.

Two-month proof: 3 employers with 20+ disabled employees, 150,000 PLN historical recoverable or recurring monthly leakage identified, 5 correction packs, one paid audit or success-fee deal, reviewer-approved boundaries.

Hidden insight: employers often treat PFRON subsidy administration as payroll compliance, but leakage behaves like recoverable revenue if someone owns the corrections and status-change calendar.
