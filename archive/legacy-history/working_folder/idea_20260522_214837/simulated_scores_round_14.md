# Simulated Scores Round 14 - Carrier Claim Cash Rail

## Idea Scored

Carrier Claim Cash Rail is a small-claim recovery-and-advance rail embedded into one Polish ecommerce 3PL's merchant operations. The beachhead is domestic DPD, DHL, and InPost parcels for fashion/beauty/home D2C merchants where the 3PL already has shipment metadata, warehouse packout/return photos, delivery-status records, damage notes, and merchant authorization. The offer is not generic claims outsourcing and not parcel-management SaaS. The 3PL adds a default opt-in recovery clause for a pilot merchant cohort. Every week, eligible lost/damaged/short-paid parcel cases flow into the rail. For evidence-complete cases, the rail either pays the merchant/3PL an immediate fixed cash advance or purchase price for a documented claim right/settlement entitlement where allowed, then pursues the carrier payout and keeps the spread; or, if assignment/advance is not allowed, files under delegated authority for a success fee while tracking those cases as weaker. The first category is the control-point proof: assigned/delegated claim economics, not just paperwork.

## Scores

- Boyle: `68/100`
- Kuhn: `66/100`
- Zeno: `72/100`

## Common Objections

- Claim assignment, advance, or settlement-entitlement mechanics are likely constrained by carrier terms and Polish carriage/claim practice.
- The 3PL is both the wedge and the main copy risk because it owns merchant relationships, evidence, exception flow, and carrier accounts.
- Per-claim value may be too small for legal setup, onboarding, appeals, and support.
- Transaction-level proof may be possible in two months, but company-level profitability is unlikely.
- Without enforceable economic control, the model collapses into the earlier admin-only claims filing service.

## Decision

Failed the lowered `82` simulated threshold. Abandon parcel claim recovery unless a future version starts with an existing enforceable right to proceeds or a higher-value claim pool.
