## 1. CEE Founder-Variant Positive-Control Lot Bank

- Buyer: Polish/CEE ISO 15189 molecular diagnostic labs running hereditary cancer, cardiology, carrier, and pharmacogenetic panels.
- Acute pain/trigger: A lab wants to validate, extend, or defend a genetic assay but cannot easily obtain positive controls for local founder or rare pathogenic variants; patient-derived controls are legally messy, scarce, and hard to share.
- Exact control point: Owned physical lots of sequence-confirmed synthetic DNA/plasmid/fragment controls for a named CEE founder-variant list, with lot IDs, third-party confirmation, and first-buyer deposit reservations.
- Secured within 2 months under 100k PLN: Interview 20 labs for variants blocking validation; collect 5,000-10,000 PLN refundable deposits from 3 labs for a 10-20 variant first lot; order synthetic constructs from gene-synthesis vendors; verify by an independent Polish/EU lab; spend roughly 35,000-70,000 PLN before scaling.
- Why incumbent cannot copy via one contract/process/equipment purchase: Large reference-material vendors optimize globally common variants and certified broad panels, not low-volume CEE founder long tail. Individual labs can order one synthetic construct, but they cannot amortize variant discovery, lot QC, acceptance notes, and replenishment across many labs. The defensibility must come from prepaid local demand, the variant-demand map, and recurring lot acceptance history.
- Likely unit economics: 8,000-15,000 PLN per mini-panel, or 15,000-35,000 PLN annual access/replenishment per lab. Gross margin can exceed 70% after first lot if variants are reused across labs.
- Biggest fatal risk: Labs or auditors may require ISO 17034-certified reference material, not an independent synthetic validation aid. This must be tested before making inventory.
- Internal guess: 88/100. Best genetics candidate so far, but probably not >90 until buyer deposits prove labs will accept the non-certified first lot.
- Selectivity verdict: Keep for simulated review only if sharpened around paid deposits and "validation aid, not certified reference material."

## 2. Forensic STR Discontinued-Kit Lot Bank

- Buyer: Small forensic, paternity, and university genetics labs with validated STR workflows.
- Acute pain/trigger: Court or client cases cannot wait, but a lab's validated STR kit, polymer, capillary, or fragment-analysis consumable is backordered, expiring, or discontinued; switching methods requires revalidation.
- Exact control point: Exclusive options on unopened, traceable, named lots of STR kits and fragment-analysis consumables from labs that are closing, upgrading, or overstocked.
- Secured within 2 months under 100k PLN: Map 40 labs and public-institution disposal notices; reserve 3-5 named lots with small option payments; collect buyer deposits before taking inventory; buy only lot-numbered unopened stock with storage history.
- Why incumbent cannot copy via one contract/process/equipment purchase: OEM distributors prefer new authorized channels and avoid near-expiry or surplus-lot liability; public labs want disposal simplicity, not resale operations. The control point is distressed named lots plus buyer deposits, not generic distribution.
- Likely unit economics: Buy surplus at 20-40% of list, resell at 55-75% of list; 10,000-40,000 PLN gross profit per meaningful lot if demand is real.
- Biggest fatal risk: Accredited forensic buyers may reject any non-authorized or uncertain-storage supply, making the inventory toxic.
- Internal guess: 83/100.
- Selectivity verdict: Keep as backup, but only if first buyer confirms surplus-lot acceptance in writing.

## 3. Legacy Capillary Sequencer Consumables And Donor-Parts Pool

- Buyer: Paternity, Sanger sequencing, fragment analysis, university, and veterinary genetics labs still running ABI-style capillary instruments.
- Acute pain/trigger: A legacy genetic analyzer is mission-critical but needs capillaries, polymers, plates, lasers, autosampler parts, or donor modules faster than OEM/support channels can provide.
- Exact control point: Owned or optioned donor instruments and unopened consumables from labs retiring legacy genetics equipment.
- Secured within 2 months under 100k PLN: Option 2-3 retired instruments or consumable lots from universities/private labs; pre-sell specific part reservations to labs with broken machines; avoid buying until deposits cover the first acquisition.
- Why incumbent cannot copy via one contract/process/equipment purchase: OEMs are disincentivized to support old platforms because it cannibalizes new-instrument sales and creates warranty/liability work. Generic service companies can copy some repairs, but cannot instantly source named donor machines and consumables.
- Likely unit economics: 2,000-15,000 PLN gross profit per part/consumable event; one donor instrument can be broken into multiple paid rescues.
- Biggest fatal risk: This drifts toward the previously failed device-service-book shape unless it is strictly inventory/options, not service continuity.
- Internal guess: 80/100.
- Selectivity verdict: Reject unless the first POC is three named paid part reservations, not a service offer.

## 4. CEE Microbial Genetics Positive-Control Panels For Food And Water Labs

- Buyer: Food, water, cosmetics, and environmental testing labs that run PCR/qPCR assays for pathogens or regulated microbial markers.
- Acute pain/trigger: A lab needs local or hard-to-source positive controls for assay validation, audit defense, or new client methods, but live pathogen controls are inconvenient, risky, or overregulated.
- Exact control point: Owned physical panels of non-infectious synthetic DNA controls for named pathogen genes and local assay targets, with lot-level sequence confirmation.
- Secured within 2 months under 100k PLN: Identify 10-15 assays where local labs lack positive controls; pre-sell a first synthetic-control bundle to 3 labs; synthesize non-infectious fragments; verify independently.
- Why incumbent cannot copy via one contract/process/equipment purchase: Broad reference suppliers sell common controls, not Poland/CEE-specific assay bundles matched to local audit language and buyer pain. The control point compounds if the company owns the local control library and replenishment demand map.
- Likely unit economics: 5,000-12,000 PLN per panel, 65-80% gross margin after first synthesis and verification.
- Biggest fatal risk: It may be less "genetics" in the user's intended sense and may become a lab-supply SKU business without a strong domination path.
- Internal guess: 84/100.
- Selectivity verdict: Keep as a serious adjacent candidate if human genetics stalls.

## 5. Pharmacogenomic Rare-Allele Control Microbank

- Buyer: Hospital, private, and CRO labs validating pharmacogenomic panels for oncology, psychiatry, cardiology, or trial enrollment.
- Acute pain/trigger: A lab cannot launch or defend a PGx panel without controls for rare alleles that materially affect drug choice or trial inclusion.
- Exact control point: Synthetic or cell-line-derived positive controls for named PGx alleles, reserved via buyer deposits and sequenced before shipment.
- Secured within 2 months under 100k PLN: Interview PGx and trial labs; choose 5-10 alleles with immediate validation bottlenecks; pre-sell deposits; synthesize and verify the first lot.
- Why incumbent cannot copy via one contract/process/equipment purchase: Big suppliers cover common PGx alleles; the hard part is knowing which rare alleles block local validation and amortizing them across a few buyers.
- Likely unit economics: 10,000-25,000 PLN per small control set; high gross margin but possibly slow sales.
- Biggest fatal risk: Demand may be too episodic, with long clinical-trial sales cycles and high documentation expectations.
- Internal guess: 77/100.
- Selectivity verdict: Reject for now unless a CRO gives a paid trigger.
