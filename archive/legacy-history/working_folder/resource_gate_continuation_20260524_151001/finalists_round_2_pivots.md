# Finalists Round 2 Pivots

Round 1 failed the simulated `>90` gate. The best scores were F1 SleepVault at 88 and F3 Orphan Dental Implant Compatibility Bank at 84. This round changes the business shape rather than polishing wording.

## R2-A. ImplantKey Orphan Implant Tooling And Traceable Component Escrow

**Idea:** ImplantKey controls exact-match continuity assets for older dental implant systems used by 50-60-year-old patients: discontinued torque drivers, reusable insertion/prosthetic drivers, scan bodies, lab analogs, CAD libraries, compatibility documentation, and only sealed, traceable prosthetic screws/abutments where distributor obligations are clear. The first wedge avoids informal patient-sourced implantable-device resale: dentists pay case deposits to access verified non-implantable tooling, digital compatibility, and traceable component escrow for named legacy systems.

**Buyer:** Dentists, prosthodontists, implant coordinators, and dental labs treating 50-60-year-old patients whose old implant crown, screw, abutment, or scan workflow is blocked because the exact system/tooling is discontinued or unidentified. The patient is the economic payer because the alternative can be explant surgery, bone grafting, months of delay, and a much larger treatment plan.

**Control point:** Owned and optioned named physical lots plus a compatibility matrix for only 3-5 obsolete implant systems. The POC asset is not a directory; it is exclusive control of tools/components that make a specific chairside or lab case possible.

**2-month POC under 100,000 PLN:** Spend 10-18k PLN on Polish/EU medical-device distributor boundary memo and no-go rules; contract a dental prosthetics technician or implant coordinator for 8-12k PLN; buy or option 300-600 sealed/traceable SKUs and reusable tool assets from retiring dentists/labs for 35-60k PLN; build a photo-verified compatibility matrix for 3-5 systems; collect at least 10 dentist-paid search/reserve deposits of 300-1,000 PLN and complete at least 2 documented matches approved by dentists before any clinical use.

**Why an unknown founder can secure it:** Closing labs and retiring dentists have drawers of low-liquidity obsolete assets that current distributors do not monetize. The founder pays cash/options, photographs and indexes assets, and does not need patient trust first; dentists only see a case-deposit workflow and documented compatibility.

**Why incumbents are disincentivized:** OEMs and distributors prefer current systems and new treatment workflows; holding obsolete traceability-sensitive drawers creates liability and low-volume operational annoyance. Dentists have local fragments but not a cross-system bank. This is a small, messy, high-urgency pool that the founder can accumulate before broad players notice.

**Threshold hypothesis:** This is the only round-2 candidate that plausibly clears `>90` because it combines owned inventory, paid deposits, high avoided-cost pain, fast cash conversion, and a POC proof that does not require broad clinical partnerships.

## R2-B. SleepVault Adult OSA PSG Archive Escrow

**Idea:** Narrow SleepVault to adult obstructive-sleep-apnea PSG/HST archives from private sleep labs, with no raw-data sale before a lawyer/DPO route memo. The asset is exclusive options over named archives plus buyer-paid secure inspection deposits for metadata-only and enclave-based evaluation.

**Why pivoted:** This reduces the legal risk by narrowing use case, buyer, archive type, and proof artifact. It still caps near 90 because health-data commercialization may remain unresolved inside the POC window.

**Expected strict simulated score:** 89-90.

## R2-C. MobilityLift Orphan Stairlift Continuity Bank

**Idea:** Buy and option discontinued stairlift control boards, chargers, remotes, rails, seats, safety edges, and deinstallation rights from estates and removal contractors, then sell urgent exact-model repair kits through independent installers to 50-60 homeowners/adult children trying to avoid full stairlift replacement.

**Why not leading:** Physical inventory and incumbent disincentive are strong, but existing used-stairlift service firms are closer to trust and can copy the offer by dedicating inventory from their own removals. It may be a good cash business, but the copy risk likely caps it below the threshold.

**Expected strict simulated score:** 86-89.
