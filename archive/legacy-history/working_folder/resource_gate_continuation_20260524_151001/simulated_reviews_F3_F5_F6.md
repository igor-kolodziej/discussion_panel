# Strict Simulated Zero To One Reviews: F3, F5, F6

Evaluation frame: strict Zero To One review, founder constraints fixed at Warsaw-based unknown solo founder, max 100,000 PLN, 2-month POC/POV, part-time start, low personal-brand fit. Profitability, CAC, payback, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility weighted above originality.

## F3. Orphan Dental Implant Compatibility Bank

### Score

**84 / 100**

### Diagnosis

This is the strongest non-data finalist because it starts from owned physical inventory and a painful direct economic trigger: a 50-60-year-old patient wants to avoid explant surgery, grafting, months of delay, and a large replacement plan because one legacy implant component failed. The business can plausibly secure named sealed lots and options within 2 months under 100,000 PLN, and exact-match urgency creates willingness to pay. The score is capped because regulatory traceability, dentist willingness, and liability may turn the asset into unsellable deadstock. If dentists will not place/use third-party sourced legacy components, the control point collapses.

### Control Point Tier

**Tier 2+: physical inventory with conditional clinical usability.**

Owned sealed, traceable discontinued components are a real control point. It is not Tier 1 until the founder proves that dentists will use the parts in live clinical workflow, that traceability documentation is acceptable, and that patient-paid search deposits convert into actual component sales.

### Strongest Copy Risk

An implant distributor, dental lab network, or large clinic group can copy the model after seeing demand by buying deadstock, building compatibility tables, and offering a legacy-component rescue desk. The startup’s defense is speed in pre-optioning fragmented named lots, compatibility know-how, and becoming the first known destination for exact-match rescue cases.

### Strongest Objections / Caveats

- Medical-device resale and traceability may kill the inventory value.
- Dentists may refuse components sourced outside standard authorized channels, especially if implantable.
- The market could be episodic: high pain per case, but limited repeat frequency.
- SKU complexity is severe; wrong identification creates clinical and liability risk.
- Incumbent dental distributors may copy if the wedge proves profitable, especially for non-implantable tools and scan bodies.
- Patient acquisition is indirect through dentists; the dentist owns trust and can bypass the company if supply becomes visible.

### Profitability / CAC / Payback View

Margins can be attractive on rare exact-match components: buy deadstock cheaply, sell at a high urgency premium, and charge search deposits. Cash conversion can be good if inventory is acquired only after mapping known systems and collecting deposits. CAC is the weak point: dentists must be convinced quickly, and direct patient marketing is trust-heavy. Payback can work for a focused owner-operated business if 10-20 urgent cases per month produce high gross profit, but repeatability and market depth remain unproven.

### Best Move

**Narrow, do not advance as-is.**

The viable pivot is to avoid broad implantable resale at first and prove a safer transaction gate: paid identification/search deposits plus owned/optioned non-implantable compatibility assets, scan bodies, drivers, CAD libraries, and traceable prosthetic components routed through dentists. If the first 10 dentist cases show that exact-match components are accepted and patients pay rescue premiums, reconsider. Without live dentist acceptance, kill.

## F5. Genetic Biobank Emergency Spares Bank

### Score

**78 / 100**

### Diagnosis

The pain is high because failed storage infrastructure can endanger irreplaceable DNA, tissue, cell, plasma, or IVF/genetics samples. The asset logic is better than a dashboard or service desk: the startup would hold specific model-compatible spares and surplus rights that labs do not want to stock. The score is capped because the trusted emergency provider is usually the OEM, freezer service contractor, facility engineer, or internal lab operations team. Unknown-founder trust in a live sample emergency is hard to win in 2 months, and incumbents can copy the narrowest profitable SKUs by stocking them.

### Control Point Tier

**Tier 2-: owned inventory, weak transaction control.**

Physical spares are controllable, but the company does not control the emergency call, the service workflow, or the lab’s decision. Emergency retainers could strengthen this, but LOIs alone are not enough.

### Strongest Copy Risk

OEM service partners, lab-equipment distributors, and biomedical service firms can add the best-moving legacy racks/controllers/canisters once demand is visible. They already own service trust and buyer relationships.

### Strongest Objections / Caveats

- Labs may already rely on service contracts, backup freezers, LN2 suppliers, and internal contingency SOPs.
- The founder may buy the wrong long-tail inventory and tie up cash.
- Emergency logistics and installation may require technicians, not just parts.
- Retention exists only if labs pay retainers; one-off sales are lumpy.
- The control point is inventory, but not an unavoidable transaction flow.

### Profitability / CAC / Payback View

Gross margins on distressed spares could be good, but cash conversion is uncertain because SKU demand is hard to predict. CAC is moderate-to-high because trust-sensitive labs must sign emergency retainers before a crisis. Payback is only attractive if a narrow set of models has repeated failures and labs prepay for access. Otherwise this becomes capital-tied spare-parts brokerage.

### Best Move

**Research further / likely kill unless a named model niche is found.**

Only revisit if one unsupported freezer/LN2/sample-storage platform has many active Polish/CEE installations, recurring failures, and no fast OEM support. The POC must produce paid retainers, not LOIs.

## F6. Genotype-Clear Dog Germplasm Option Pool

### Score

**81 / 100**

### Diagnosis

This is a real genetics inventory idea because named frozen semen lots are unique, genotype documentation can raise value, and serious breeders may pay for scarce traits with lower carrier risk. The business could option assets within 2 months and prove buyer deposits in one breed. The score is capped because breeder politics, veterinary reproduction trust, small market depth, and repeatability are hard. The founder may be structurally outside the trust network that controls the best supply and demand.

### Control Point Tier

**Tier 2: unique physical inventory options with fragile network access.**

Exclusive options over named semen lots are real. The control point is still fragile because sellers and buyers may transact privately if trust is weak, and genotype documentation may not be enough to overcome breeder-network dynamics.

### Strongest Copy Risk

An established canine reproduction clinic, breed insider, or kennel network can copy by optioning semen directly and bundling genetic testing. They already have trust and context that an unknown founder lacks.

### Strongest Objections / Caveats

- The buyer universe is narrow and relationship-driven.
- Deposits may require reputation, not just genotype documentation.
- Market size and repeat purchase frequency may cap the business.
- Breed politics can block supply or create hidden adverse selection.
- Expansion across breeds may not compound cleanly because each breed has separate insiders, norms, and genetic problems.

### Profitability / CAC / Payback View

Margins can be strong on a small number of scarce lots, and cash conversion can be protected with buyer deposits before option exercise. CAC is the problem: trusted breeders and reproduction vets are gatekeepers. Payback might work in a narrow breed-specific brokerage, but owner earnings and repeatability are uncertain unless the founder becomes a trusted genetic-risk clearinghouse.

### Best Move

**Narrow or kill.**

Do not advance unless the founder can secure one breed-specific supply cluster and buyer deposits inside 60 days. A better version would start with an established breed insider as co-founder or supply partner, but that violates the solo unknown-founder constraint if not already secured.
