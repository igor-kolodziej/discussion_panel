## Diagnosis

This pivot materially improves the round-1 dental idea because it stops trying to be a broad obsolete implant-parts reseller and instead controls a narrow, high-urgency transaction gate: exact discontinued tooling, scan bodies, lab analogs, CAD libraries, compatibility evidence, and only sealed traceable components where the regulatory boundary is clear. The customer pain is commercially serious: one missing legacy driver or scan body can convert a repairable crown/abutment case into a surgical replacement discussion with much higher cost, delay, and patient fear. The POC is unusually concrete for an unknown solo founder because the founder can buy/option named assets, index them, contract one dental technical specialist, and demand dentist-paid case deposits before deep inventory spend. This is still not a guaranteed company, but it clears the simulated threshold because it has owned inventory, urgent avoided-cost economics, fast cash conversion, and a proof path that does not depend on status, broad partnerships, or future software.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier A-: owned named physical inventory plus transaction-gated compatibility workflow.**

The control point is the combination of: (1) owned or optioned obsolete dental implant tooling and traceable component lots for 3-5 named systems; (2) photo/lot/manufacturer/reference documentation; (3) a compatibility matrix that turns unknown cases into exact-match searches; and (4) dentist-paid deposits that route urgent cases before buyers can bypass the founder. It is not pure Tier A because medical-device distributor obligations and dentist acceptance must be proven. It is stronger than Tier B because the founder can physically own/reserve the assets inside two months and the buyer cannot solve a named case with a generic vendor contract if the exact tool/component is unavailable.

## Strongest Copy Risk

The strongest copy risk is a dental distributor, implant-system specialist, large dental lab, or respected prosthodontist creating its own obsolete-system desk after seeing demand. The defense is to start with assets that competitors cannot retroactively obtain: hidden deadstock purchases, signed 90-day options from retiring dentists/labs, a concentrated compatibility matrix for a few systems, and a paid case-submission workflow that builds first-call status among dentists before broad distributors treat the long tail as worth operational attention.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **Regulatory boundary must be explicit.** The POC must not sell unsealed, unknown-provenance, expired, repackaged, relabelled, or patient-sourced implantable stock. It must stay within reusable tooling, digital compatibility, lab analogs, scan bodies, and sealed traceable components reviewed under MDR distributor rules.
- **Dentist acceptance is the gating trust issue.** The patient may have high willingness to pay, but the dentist controls clinical use. The first proof is dentist-paid deposits and dentist confirmation of acceptable matches, not patient anxiety.
- **Inventory quality may disappoint.** Retiring dentists and labs may mostly hold junk drawers, incomplete kits, or systems with too few active cases. The POC must count clinically usable exact-match assets, not total SKUs.
- **Market size is episodic.** This is not recurring at the patient level. It becomes repeatable only if dentists and labs send repeated orphan-system cases.
- **Liability handling cannot be vague.** Sales terms, storage records, photos, original packaging, no-go categories, and professional-only B2B use must be set before any transaction.
- **Founder needs one dental insider.** A contracted prosthetics technician or implant coordinator is mandatory; generic data-science competence is not enough.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

Profitability is plausible and fast-testable. Asset acquisition can be cheap because the supply sits in low-liquidity drawers at retiring dentists and closing labs; urgent sale/rental/search value is anchored to avoided explant/rebuild cost, not to original part price. A simple case flow can charge 300-1,000 PLN search/reserve deposits, 500-2,500 PLN tool rental or scan-body access, and materially higher margins on traceable sealed components when compliant. CAC is practical because the first channel is dentists and prosthodontists with orphan cases, reached through direct outreach, dental forums, lab relationships, and paid exact-match case intake rather than broad consumer ads. Payback is proven if the first 10 dentist deposits arrive before 60,000 PLN of inventory is committed; retention comes from dentist/lab repeat submissions, not individual patients.
