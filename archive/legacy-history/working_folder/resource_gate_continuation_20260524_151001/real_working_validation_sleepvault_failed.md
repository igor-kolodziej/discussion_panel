## Key Objections From Zero To One

- The idea is serious and not a generic data marketplace, but a lab mandate may not equal a legally usable commercial data asset.
- EHDS/HDAB positioning helps, but near-term revenue must work under current GDPR/member-state/controller legal routes; most secondary-use EHDS rules apply later.
- Buyer willingness to prepay an unknown intermediary before legal clearance is unproven.
- 180-day exclusivity is not enough for durable advantage unless it converts into longer conditional commercial rights.
- Legal, QA, security, sleep-physician review, and buyer diligence costs may be materially higher than budgeted.
- Distribution is weak because an unknown solo founder must sell to medtech/regulatory buyers with high trust and long sales cycles.

## Decision

Do not continue polishing the SleepVault wording. The failure is structural: the POC controls a workflow around data readiness, but the commercial asset remains conditional on a legal route and buyer trust. Pivot away from health-data reuse as the primary control point.
