# 50-60 Resource-First Candidate Batch

Generated for the continuation run. Scope is limited to cases where 50-60-year-olds soon to become elderly are the direct buyer or the direct demand trigger. I killed weak shapes inline when the control point collapses into generic trust, coordination, a report, a dashboard, or an easily copied vendor relationship.

## 1. Orphan Dental Implant Compatibility Bank

- Status: strongest watchlist candidate, not yet threshold-ready.
- Buyer: 52-64-year-old patients with 10-25-year-old dental implants who need a crown, abutment, screw, driver, or scan-body match now, usually routed through their dentist.
- Acute pain / trigger: one failed crown or loose abutment can turn into explant surgery, bone grafting, months of delay, and a far higher bill if the original implant system or prosthetic component cannot be sourced.
- Exact control point: exclusive physical inventory and 90-day options on named discontinued implant prosthetic components, abutment screws, drivers, scan bodies, and CAD library dongles from retiring dentists, closed clinics, and small dental labs.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: call and email 200 retiring dentists, dental labs, and liquidation sellers; pay 20-50% option deposits for sealed, lot-traceable component drawers; buy the best 500-800 SKUs for 40-70k PLN; publish only model/size availability, not a generic catalog; collect 300-700 PLN paid search deposits from dentists/patients before releasing search time.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: the supply is not a normal distributor SKU. It is fragmented dead stock in clinic drawers, often mixed across brands and generations. Manufacturers prefer selling new implant systems, and large distributors avoid slow-moving, liability-sensitive orphan components.
- Likely unit economics: buy components at 20-150 PLN each from liquidation drawers; resell urgent matches at 300-1,500 PLN plus 500-1,500 PLN search/compatibility fee; target 60%+ gross margin if only sealed or traceable parts are sold.
- Fatal risk: medical-device resale and traceability may make many drawers unusable; dentists may bypass once a source is revealed; the POC must prove sealed stock, dentist willingness to pay, and buyer urgency.
- Internal guess: 88/100. Strong asset logic, but likely capped below 90 unless traceability and dentist-side paid deposits are proven fast.

## 2. Retiring Scleral / RGP Lens Design Rights

- Status: watchlist candidate.
- Buyer: 50-60-year-olds with keratoconus, post-LASIK ectasia, corneal grafts, severe dry eye, or irregular corneas who depend on custom scleral or RGP lenses and panic when a lab, optometrist, or lens design disappears.
- Acute pain / trigger: a broken or lost custom lens can mean weeks without functional vision, and a full refit may be expensive, uncomfortable, and clinically uncertain.
- Exact control point: acquisition or exclusive option over retiring independent contact-lens lab assets: trial lens sets, base-curve design libraries, lathes, specialty blanks, edge-shape know-how, and rights to reproduce specific legacy designs through licensed optometrists.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: identify 20-40 independent labs/optometrists nearing retirement in Poland/CEE; buy or option one specialty trial set and design library for 20-50k PLN; sign two optometrists for paid remake/refit cases; take 500-1,000 PLN deposits from 10 patients who need continuity.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: large optical chains optimize for standard soft lenses and new refits, not low-volume legacy hard-lens designs. The hard asset is the retiring lab's physical trial set plus accumulated design rules, not a commodity supplier account.
- Likely unit economics: production/refit cost 300-700 PLN per lens; urgent remake/refit package 1,500-3,000 PLN; high gross margin if the company owns the design library and outsources only regulated fitting/manufacturing steps.
- Fatal risk: medical-device compliance and the need for licensed fitting could turn this into optometry service work; the first proof must show that ownership of trial sets/design rights materially reduces time or cost.
- Internal guess: 86/100. Good resource scarcity, but the control point may be too dependent on clinical execution.

## 3. Legacy Neurostimulator Controller / Charger Rescue Inventory

- Status: reject unless manufacturer-lock proof changes.
- Buyer: 50-60-year-old chronic-pain, DBS, or implanted-neurostimulator patients whose external charger, remote, or controller breaks, is lost, or becomes unsupported.
- Acute pain / trigger: the implant can become unusable or stuck at a poor setting while the patient waits for a replacement device, manufacturer support, or a clinic appointment.
- Exact control point: physical inventory of verified legacy external controllers, chargers, wands, and patient remotes for named neurostimulator families, sourced from explant cases, clinic closures, estates, and surplus dealers.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: buy 10-25 verified external accessories for 30-80k PLN; document exact model/serial compatibility; run paid intake with patients and pain clinics; prove at least three devices can be lawfully paired or supplied through a physician.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: manufacturers do not want to maintain broad obsolete cross-generation accessory stock, while hospitals avoid holding used patient accessories. The actual supply is fragmented, model-specific, and low-volume.
- Likely unit economics: buy at 300-1,500 PLN; emergency replacement fee 1,500-6,000 PLN if legal pairing is possible; strong margin but low volume.
- Fatal risk: if devices cannot be re-paired, transferred, cleaned, or resold legally without manufacturer involvement, the asset is worthless. This is likely the fatal blocker.
- Internal guess: 74/100. Resource-first, but probably killed by regulatory/manufacturer control.

## 4. Salt-And-Pepper Human Hair Lot Options For Chemo Wigs

- Status: watchlist candidate.
- Buyer: mainly 50-60-year-old women starting chemotherapy or experiencing sudden alopecia who want a wig that preserves their real age, status, and professional identity instead of looking synthetic, over-young, or obviously dyed.
- Acute pain / trigger: chemo often starts within days or weeks; the buyer has a narrow window to secure a natural-looking wig before visible hair loss.
- Exact control point: exclusive physical inventory/options on named salt-and-pepper, gray-transition, and naturally mature European human-hair lots, reserved before production with buyer deposits.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: buy 25-40 named hair lots from salons, individual sellers, estate clearances, and small wig workshops for 30-60k PLN; reserve 2-3 piecework wigmakers on per-unit terms; sell 10 deposit-backed custom matches through paid search, oncology prep keywords, menopause/cancer communities, and discreet referral cards.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: wig chains can buy generic hair, but mature gray-transition hair is fragmented, emotionally sensitive, hard to grade, and unattractive to fashion-oriented hair suppliers. The control point is named lots matched to anxious buyers before the hair enters the generic wig supply chain.
- Likely unit economics: buy lot at 500-2,000 PLN; finished wig price 3,500-9,000 PLN; gross margin 30-50% after wigmaker labor if inventory turns quickly through deposits.
- Fatal risk: trust and quality control may dominate the resource advantage; if buyers prefer local wig salons and in-person fitting, this becomes a boutique retail business.
- Internal guess: 82/100. Emotionally real and asset-backed, but likely not hard enough to copy for a >90 score.

## 5. Orphan Dental Ceramic Shade / Porcelain Lot Bank

- Status: reject or fold into candidate 1.
- Buyer: 50-60-year-olds with older crowns, veneers, or bridges who need one visible tooth restored without replacing a whole matched set.
- Acute pain / trigger: a front-tooth chip or failed crown can force expensive replacement of multiple otherwise good restorations if old porcelain powders, stains, or ceramic systems are unavailable.
- Exact control point: distressed inventory of discontinued dental porcelain powders, ceramic blocks, stains, and shade tabs from closed dental labs, plus exclusive options on technician-owned vintage shade systems.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: buy 20-40 discontinued ceramic lots and shade tabs from lab closures for 10-40k PLN; secure one senior dental technician for paid cases; collect case deposits from cosmetic dentists.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: distributors no longer sell discontinued lots and labs throw them away or leave them idle. However, a good technician may solve many cases with modern systems, weakening the inventory control point.
- Likely unit economics: buy lots cheaply; charge 800-2,500 PLN premium per restoration case; margin depends on technician labor, not only inventory.
- Fatal risk: the scarce asset may be technician skill rather than owned inventory, making this a service business. Also overlaps too much with the stronger dental implant inventory thesis.
- Internal guess: 70/100. Reject as standalone.

## 6. Retiring Ocularist Mold And Shade Continuity Bank

- Status: reject unless a supply desert is discovered.
- Buyer: 50-60-year-olds with an ocular prosthesis who need a remake or refit after socket changes, cosmetic wear, or loss of the original prosthesis.
- Acute pain / trigger: a poorly matched eye prosthesis is socially visible and psychologically costly; exact iris/sclera matching and old fit knowledge matter.
- Exact control point: acquisition of retiring ocularist assets: casts, physical molds, iris-painting references, acrylic stock, and specialty equipment tied to hard-to-recreate prosthesis styles.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: identify retiring ocularists in Poland/CEE; buy one small shop's transferable non-patient-identifying fabrication assets for 30-80k PLN; take deposits from three remake/refit cases referred by ophthalmologists or patient groups.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: ocularists are scarce and the know-how is craft-heavy. The closest hospitals may not want a low-volume continuity inventory.
- Likely unit economics: 2,000-8,000 PLN per prosthesis; attractive gross margin if a contracted ocularist can execute.
- Fatal risk: market may be too small, patient records/privacy may be non-transferable, and execution depends on a specialist craftsperson. That makes it closer to buying a tiny practice than building a scalable control point.
- Internal guess: 72/100. Reject for narrowness and craft dependence.

## 7. Frozen Embryo / Gamete Storage Exit Rights

- Status: reject.
- Buyer: 50-60-year-old individuals or couples still paying storage fees for embryos, eggs, or sperm they no longer intend to use and want a legally clean disposition, donation, or transfer path.
- Acute pain / trigger: recurring storage bills, divorce/remarriage, approaching retirement, or estate planning forces a decision they have deferred for years.
- Exact control point: signed consent mandates and assigned administrative rights over named cryostorage disposition cases, with prepaid research-donation or disposal transaction slots.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: collect 20-30 paid mandates from storage-fee payers, obtain legal review, and secure at least one clinic/lab transaction route.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: clinics are disincentivized to push emotionally sensitive disposition decisions. But the founder still cannot move the asset without clinic cooperation.
- Likely unit economics: 1,000-4,000 PLN per resolved case if legal and clinic workflows are available.
- Fatal risk: clinic dependence, legal/ethical complexity, and trust burden. This also drifts toward IVF clinic-dependent add-ons from the rejected prior corpus.
- Internal guess: 65/100. Reject.

## 8. Rare-Size Lymphedema Compression Deadstock Bank

- Status: reject.
- Buyer: 50-60-year-old breast-cancer, gynecologic-cancer, or venous-disease patients who suddenly need a hard-to-find compression sleeve, gauntlet, stocking, or wrap in a rare size.
- Acute pain / trigger: post-surgery or flare swelling can require compression quickly, while custom garments may take weeks and local pharmacies usually stock only common sizes.
- Exact control point: physical deadstock of sealed compression garments, wraps, donning aids, and rare sizes from closed lymphology clinics, pharmacies, and medical-supply shops.
- 2-month / under 100k PLN proof path: buy 200-500 sealed deadstock items for 20-60k PLN; classify by size/compression class; take urgent paid orders through search ads and patient groups; clear only items with intact packaging and expiry/safety status.
- Why an incumbent cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase: in the narrow deadstock version, the supply is fragmented and annoying to classify. But in the actual customer problem, pharmacies and medical suppliers can simply stock more sizes if they care.
- Likely unit economics: buy at 20-40% of retail; sell at 70-100% of retail with urgent shipping; gross margin plausible but ticket size is modest.
- Fatal risk: the control point is too easy to copy by a motivated pharmacy or supplier, and the business likely becomes low-margin ecommerce inventory rather than a defensible resource gate.
- Internal guess: 58/100. Reject.

## Batch Takeaway

The only two worth carrying forward immediately are:

1. Orphan Dental Implant Compatibility Bank.
2. Retiring Scleral / RGP Lens Design Rights.

The salt-and-pepper chemo hair lot thesis is emotionally sharp and asset-backed, but its copy defense is weaker. Neurostimulator accessories are resource-first but likely killed by manufacturer/regulatory control. The rest should not consume simulated-review slots unless new evidence appears.
