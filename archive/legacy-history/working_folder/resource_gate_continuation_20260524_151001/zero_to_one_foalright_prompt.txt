Founder constraints: Warsaw data scientist with neuroscience, data science, and business-analysis background; unknown solo founder; max 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; part-time start; no celebrity, privileged introductions, or influencer distribution.

Idea: FoalRight Legacy Semen Reassignment Bank.

FoalRight secures exclusive 180-day sale mandates or options over named EU-stored frozen sport-horse semen doses whose owners are retiring, exiting breeding, handling estates, or tired of storage fees. For each dose it verifies clinic storage, owner authority, transferability, EU/IETS or local release status, nomination/registration constraints, and shipping workflow, then sells escrow-backed reservations to mare owners during breeding season.

Buyer: sport-horse mare owners, embryo-transfer programs, and small breeders looking for specific legacy bloodlines, deceased/retired stallions, or finite performance genetics that are not actively marketed through normal stallion channels.

Hidden insight: the scarce asset is not "finding stallions"; it is cleaning up ownership, storage, transferability, and release authority for already-frozen EU-stored doses that owners no longer actively market but mare owners would buy if the right were certain.

Primary control point: signed exclusive 180-day sale mandate or option over a named lot: stallion, owner, clinic, dose/straw count, storage status, release terms, transfer constraints, and buyer-deposit authority. This is assigned control over named finite genetic inventory, not generic brokerage.

2-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- Secure 15-30 named-dose mandates/options from owners exiting breeding, handling estates, or paying storage for unused doses.
- Pay storage arrears or small option fees only where transferability is clear.
- Contract an equine reproduction/legal specialist to verify release terms, nomination rules, and shipping constraints.
- Collect 5 buyer deposits for specific mandated inventory before buying deep inventory.
- Kill if transferability is unclear, clinics will not confirm storage/release, or buyers will not deposit against named mandated doses.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: exiting owners and estates may prefer a no-sale/no-cost exclusive mandate, storage arrears payment, or small option fee over continued storage bills and inactive inventory. The founder can start with precise owner outreach, clinic-confirmed chain of custody, escrow, and specialist review instead of needing a celebrity breeding reputation.

Why not easy to copy: existing equine semen depots, stallion agents, or sport-horse brokers can copy the model after demand is proven, but they cannot copy exact mandated doses once signed. The defensible wedge is quietly securing orphaned, storage-fee-burdened, estate-held, non-promoted, or administratively messy lots before marketing the category.

Economics: 300-1,000 EUR buyer deposit plus 15-30% success fee or spread on a 1,000-8,000 EUR dose. Supply cost can be low where the founder pays storage arrears, option fees, or offers a no-sale/no-cost mandate. CAC is narrow and targeted through mare owners, sport-horse breeders, embryo-transfer clinics, reproduction vets, and discipline-specific groups. Repeatability comes from serious breeders repeatedly seeking genetic matches and from trust with clinics and mare owners after completed transactions.

Main risks: transferability restrictions, nomination/registration limits, owner authority disputes, clinic release rules, breeding seasonality, trust with mare owners, semen quality, and the possibility that existing stallion agents capture the most valuable inventory first.
