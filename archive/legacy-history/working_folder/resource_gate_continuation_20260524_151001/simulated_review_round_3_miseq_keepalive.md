## Diagnosis

MiSeq KeepAlive clears the simulated threshold because it fixes the two real-gate failures from FoalRight and SleepVault. It does not depend on patient-data legality, breeder trust, or transferability of biological material. The control point is concrete: signed salvage-right mandates or options over named EOL sequencing instruments and parts lots, plus buyer deposits for specific modules before deep inventory spend. The buyer pain is credible because small genetics and sequencing labs often have validated workflows, assays, staff training, and grant/sample schedules around older instruments; a failed module can be cheaper to replace than a whole platform migration.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier A-: assigned control over named distressed physical assets.**

The control point is not generic parts brokerage. It is a mandate over a specific instrument, serial/model, location, ownership proof, accessory list, and resale/harvest rights. A competitor can hunt other dead sequencers, but cannot copy the exact mandated lot once signed. It is A- rather than full A because part testing and buyer acceptance must be proven module by module.

## Strongest Copy Risk

Independent sequencing-instrument service technicians and refurbishers are the nearest copy risk. They already have trust and technical knowledge. MiSeq KeepAlive's defense is to start upstream from them: control distressed instrument supply and use service technicians as buyers/contractors rather than trying to out-service them.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- Parts may be hard to test without a working instrument and technician access.
- Firmware/software locks, calibration requirements, or hidden service procedures may make some modules unusable.
- Buyers may prefer OEM-only or existing service-provider parts even at higher cost.
- Acquisition prices may be too high if sellers understand part-out value.
- Demand could be episodic unless the founder expands across multiple EOL instrument families.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

The economics are plausible under 100,000 PLN. The founder can option supply cheaply, buy only one or two lots, and use buyer deposits to avoid speculative inventory. Search/first-call deposits of 1,000-5,000 PLN can validate demand quickly. Module sales of 2,000-20,000 PLN can repay acquisition costs. CAC is reachable by precise outbound to labs with publications/protocols mentioning older Illumina systems, equipment liquidators, university cores, and independent service technicians. Payback can occur inside a two-month POC if one or two urgent modules sell.

## Best Move

Advance to real Zero To One validation with strict wording. Do not position it as a broad lab-equipment marketplace. Position it as **EOL genetics-instrument continuity through assigned salvage rights over named sequencing assets and buyer-funded part reservations**.
