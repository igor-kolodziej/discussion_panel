# Research Notes

## PSG / Sleep-AI Data Rights Candidate

### Market / Technical Demand

- The AASM scoring manual is the definitive reference for scoring polysomnography and home sleep apnea tests, which supports PSG as the reference layer for algorithm validation.
- AASM has an autoscoring certification path for in-lab PSG sleep-stage software with a rigorous validation process, showing that sleep autoscoring needs external validation rather than only product claims.
- Sleep-AI and sleep-apnea companies visibly use PSG comparison in validation:
  - SleepAI validation compared oximetry/PPG algorithms with one in-centre PSG night.
  - Apneal says its European study validates against polysomnography across 20 centers.
  - SleepActa says its algorithm was compared to PSG and trained/tested on thousands of actigraphy exams.
  - Insai says it builds sleep/EEG foundation models and works across clinical PSG and legacy data.
- Public datasets exist, e.g. NSRR MrOS PSG and newer academic datasets, so the business cannot be "generic PSG data." The wedge must be controlled, local, underused, commercial-rights-ready archives with provenance, quality inventory, and secure access.

### Regulation / Legal Timing

- Polish healthcare providers generally retain medical documentation for 20 years. That means small labs can have legally retained archives that are expensive/annoying to manage but not monetized.
- Polish patient-rights material confirms patients have access rights to medical documentation and that providers generally retain medical documentation for 20 years from the end of the year of the last entry.
- GDPR treats health data as a special category. Article 9 prohibits processing such data unless an exception applies; one available route is explicit consent for specified purposes. This supports a reconsent-first POC more than an assumption that pseudonymised archived PSG can simply be licensed.
- EU EHDS Regulation (EU) 2025/327 entered the EU rulebook in 2025 and creates a framework for secondary use of health data for research, innovation, policy-making, and regulatory activities. Full secondary-use operation is phased in later, so this is momentum, not the immediate legal basis.
- European Commission health-AI materials state that EHDS supports training, testing, and evaluation of algorithms, including medical-device and digital-health algorithms.
- EDPB pseudonymisation guidance reinforces that pseudonymised data is still personal data, so a POC must not assume that simple de-identification creates freely licensable data. The control-point proof should be signed options + legal/data-protection memo + secure processing/anonymisation plan, not uncontrolled data export.
- AASM's full PSG autoscoring certification program, available April 2026, explicitly frames independent evaluation of PSG autoscoring software as valuable and uses real-world patient studies from accredited sleep centers. This is strong market-timing evidence for third-party PSG validation demand, even though AASM's program is US-centered and does not remove EU data-protection constraints.

### Candidate Implication

The strongest version is not "sell a database." It is:

- secure exclusive options/mandates over named raw PSG/HSAT archives from small Polish/CEE sleep labs;
- run a paid inventory and legal-readiness process;
- sell buyer inspection deposits for a secure validation benchmark once legal clearance is complete;
- use secure enclave / no raw-export / fully anonymised release only after DPO review.

The two-month proof is signed exclusive options over named archives, redacted inventory, a lawyer/DPO memo for the data route, and 2-3 buyer LOIs or refundable deposits. If labs will not sign options or buyers will not deposit before data transfer, kill it.

The stronger Round 2 version is narrower: signed exclusive lab mandates to run explicit patient reconsent campaigns over named PSG/HSAT archives, plus the first consented records and buyer inspection deposits. This addresses the Round 1 legal cap by making the control point the reconsent right and consented data conversion, not merely old archive possession.

## Dental Implant Compatibility Candidate

### Regulation / Control-Point Implication

- EU MDR treats medical-device distributors as economic operators with verification, traceability, cooperation, vigilance, and good distribution obligations. This does not automatically kill a sealed new-old-stock dental component bank, but it means the wedge must be a compliant B2B distributor-like inventory operation, not informal resale.
- Distributor traceability is especially important for implantable and dental medical devices. A viable POC must therefore limit inventory to sealed, identifiable, CE/UDI/lot-traceable stock with documented origin and storage history, and sell only to dentists/labs who decide clinical use.
- The dental candidate should be narrowed away from unsealed implantable parts. The strongest lower-liability inventory is exact-match scan bodies, drivers, torque adapters, lab analogs, CAD/library assets, and sealed abutment/screw components only where traceability is complete.

### Candidate Implication

The candidate is not dead, but its threshold version must prove:

- real traceable sealed lots can be secured under 100,000 PLN;
- at least several dentists will pay search/reserve deposits for a named implant system;
- a regulatory consultant/lawyer can define distributor obligations and no-go inventory rules quickly;
- the founder can hide/buy the supply before revealing sources, reducing dentist/seller bypass.

## Grave-Right Candidate

### Legal / Control-Point Implication

- Polish commentary sources consistently describe the right to a grave as not ordinary ownership and not freely saleable to third parties. Some transfers or changes of disposer may be possible, but only under cemetery rules, family/legal status, and administrator control.
- This weakens the candidate below the control-point threshold because the founder cannot simply option or buy scarce grave rights like property. The route would depend on cemetery administration and legal/family status verification, making it too close to a regulated coordination/legal-service workflow.
