Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, patient-data businesses, and prescription-drug businesses without asset/payment-flow control.

Scored idea:

Molecular Lab qPCR/NGS Line-Down Donor Bank buys or options a retiring biomed/service technician's molecular-lab support book and acquires titled, tested spare qPCR/thermal-cycler/extraction/NGS-prep instrument units, modules, dongles/workstations, plate blocks, power supplies, rotors, and critical accessories used by Polish private molecular, IVF, veterinary, food, research, and small diagnostic labs. Labs pay deposits or emergency rental/resale fees when a failed instrument blocks assays, EQA deadlines, validated workflows, or customer commitments.

The founder does not run tests, interpret results, handle patient samples, access patient data, validate clinical assays, or sell diagnostic conclusions. Labs remain responsible for validation, accreditation, reporting, and clinical/research use. The startup controls titled tested donor instruments/spares, service files, installed-base support book, compatibility map, emergency rental/resale contracts, and paid deposits.

Buyer / payer:
- private molecular diagnostic labs;
- IVF/genetics labs;
- veterinary/food/water/environmental qPCR labs;
- CRO/research labs with grant/customer deadlines;
- biomed service technicians and lab managers who need exact replacement units fast;
- closing/upgrading labs that want cash for old but working instruments.

Acute trigger:
A qPCR instrument, extraction unit, thermal cycler, NGS-prep workstation, plate block, dongle/workstation, power supply, rotor, or accessory fails shortly before a run, EQA/PT deadline, customer delivery, validation check, grant milestone, or clinic/CRO batch. OEM repair/import can take days or weeks. A tested compatible loaner or donor unit saves revenue and continuity.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is:
- signed option/handoff over a biomed/service technician or small lab-equipment reseller support book;
- titled tested donor instruments/spares with serials, service records, photos, accessories, software/dongle/workstation status, and known limitations;
- first-look rights to closing/upgrading molecular/IVF/research lab lots;
- compatibility map by lab installed base, assay workflow boundary, power/accessory/software needs, and "not compatible" exclusions;
- emergency rental/resale/deposit contracts from named labs;
- service partner inspection pathway and electrical/basic functional test records;
- no patient samples, no patient data, no assay validation claims, no diagnostic interpretation.

The hard control is: specific titled tested donor assets + installed-base support book + prepaid lab downtime deposits.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN option over a retiring/overloaded service technician or lab-equipment reseller book;
- 40,000-75,000 PLN first tested qPCR/thermal-cycler/extraction donor asset lot, bought only after deposits;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN legal/IVD/RUO/used-equipment/liability/insurance review;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN inspection, electrical/basic functional testing, compatibility documentation, photos, and logistics;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN courier/insurance/damage reserve and storage.

Required proof:
1. Signed support-book/first-look option with at least 20 named molecular/IVF/research/vet/food labs or installed systems.
2. Titled inspected donor asset lot under 75,000 PLN, with serials, service/condition records, required accessories, and no questionable origin.
3. Compatibility map covering at least 10 named installed systems or workflows.
4. 3 labs sign paid downtime deposits, standby reservations, or conditional rental/resale commitments before final inventory purchase.
5. 25,000+ PLN in deposits, prepaid standby fees, or one paid emergency rental/resale within 60 days.
6. Gross margin above 40% after acquisition cost allocation, inspection, logistics, insurance, damage reserve, seller earnout, and service partner fee.
7. Legal review confirms no patient-data handling, no assay-validation claims, and clear RUO/IVD/used-equipment boundaries.

Economics:
Revenue comes from emergency rental/loaner fees for qPCR/thermal-cycler/extraction assets, resale margin on tested donor instruments/spares, standby reservation deposits for labs with high-value recurring workflows, service coordination margin when a biomed partner performs install/checks, and later acquisition of more donor lots only against named compatibility demand.

Payback logic:
The startup does not buy random lab equipment. It options a support book, maps installed systems, secures paid deposits from labs with known downtime risk, then buys one exact donor lot. One emergency rental/resale or three paid standby deposits can validate cash conversion. Payback is asset-backed: if the first donor unit does not rent, it can be resold.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
This is a hard-inventory continuity business. It owns specific donor assets and installed-base support knowledge. It does not run diagnostics, interpret genetic results, resell patient data, or broker testing services.

Copy risk:
OEMs, distributors, used-equipment dealers, and biomed firms can compete. The wedge exists only where the startup controls a specific donor asset, support-book handoff, first-look lab-closure rights, and prepaid lab deposits before failure. If the founder has only a used-equipment listing, the score falls below 78.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- No patient samples, no patient data, no testing service, no result interpretation, no assay-validation claims.
- Labs remain responsible for validation, accreditation, reporting, and clinical/research use.
- Startup sells/rents B2B professional donor equipment/spares only.
- Legal review covers IVD/RUO/used-equipment boundaries, medical-device/economic-operator obligations, warranty/returns, liability, storage/transport, and no questionable stock.

Main risks acknowledged:
- OEM service and used-equipment dealers may bypass the founder.
- Instrument validation and software/dongles can kill compatibility.
- Labs may refuse standby deposits and wait for failure.
- Used instruments may be obsolete, unsupported, or hard to legally place into clinical workflows.
- One asset can still strand a large part of the budget.
- Demand may be lumpy unless the installed-base book is real.
