Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
VEXRelease Customer Security Exception Pack

One-sentence thesis:
B2B software, industrial software, and connected-device vendors increasingly lose or delay enterprise deals when a customer's scanner or security team flags CVEs, missing SBOMs, unsupported components, or ambiguous affected/not-affected status; the startup sells a fast, fixed-scope, prepaid exception packet for one named customer review: SBOM, VEX/affected-status rationale, version/build evidence, exploitability notes, remediation/no-go table, and buyer-safe wording.

Exact buyer:
20-200 person B2B SaaS, industrial software, developer-tool, data-infrastructure, cybersecurity-adjacent, embedded software, and connected-product vendors with live enterprise customers asking for SBOM, VEX, CVE, open-source dependency, or affected/not-affected evidence before a PO, renewal, pilot expansion, production deployment, security review, or milestone payment. Channel buyers are appsec boutiques, SOC2/ISO27001 consultants, fractional CISOs, product-security advisers, DevSecOps consultancies, and security-questionnaire firms that already own vendor trust but do not want to staff urgent component-exception packets.

Acute trigger:
A customer's security scanner, procurement review, deployment gate, or product-security team flags one or more CVEs, vulnerable components, missing SBOM, unsupported dependency, stale container image, transitive package, open-source risk, or unclear remediation timeline. The commercial event is named and material: PO approval, renewal, deployment, go-live, pilot conversion, customer security exception, or milestone payment.

Control point:
Live customer exception thread or scanner report with exact finding IDs, product version, artifact, component, dependency, CVE, deadline, and commercial event; vendor authorization, NDA, and evidence-room access; build, release, image digest, package-lock, dependency tree, repo metadata, SBOM export, scanner output, vendor advisory, release notes, patch status, deployment notes, and compensating-control evidence where available; paid product-security reviewer or appsec reviewer for issue spotting, not formal security certification; buyer-specific packet with SBOM/VEX artifact, affected/not-affected rationale, fixed/mitigated/not-applicable/no-go table, evidence index, remediation timeline, and safe wording; submitted customer response, customer follow-up reduction, security exception progress, deployment approval, or documented no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three appsec/SOC2/fractional-CISO/product-security partners agree to route urgent SBOM/VEX/CVE exception cases; eight prepaid live customer-exception cases at 8,000-25,000 PLN each; at least five cases tied to commercial events above 100,000 PLN equivalent; at least five completed packets accepted into customer security reviews, evidence rooms, procurement threads, or partner reports; at least three documented outcomes such as buyer progress, narrowed follow-up, waiver/exception, deployment approval, remediation/no-go, or formal-assurance escalation; at least one partner routes a second case; and gross margin above 60% after reviewer, partner share, security tooling, and secure workflow costs.

6-month POC:
Deliver 35-60 paid packets through 5-8 repeat partners and direct vendor cases; produce 25+ buyer-progress, narrowed-follow-up, waiver/exception, deployment-approval, remediation/no-go, or formal-escalation outcomes; collect 350,000-900,000 PLN revenue; keep average evidence-ready case delivery under five business days; keep reviewer and partner cost below 40% of revenue; reject at least 25% of inbound cases when evidence is missing, the vendor wants invented answers, remediation is the real blocker, legal/license advice is needed, or formal pentest/security certification is required; and build a private case library of customer wording, scanner findings, component patterns, buyer-accepted VEX language, no-go triggers, and remediation/referral outcomes.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to appsec boutiques, SOC2/ISO27001 consultants, fractional CISOs, product-security advisers, DevSecOps consultancies, and security-questionnaire firms. Partner pitch: "When your client has a live enterprise buyer blocking a PO or deployment over SBOM, VEX, CVEs, or vulnerable dependencies, I produce a fixed-scope buyer packet under your approval: component evidence, affected/not-affected rationale, remediation/no-go table, and safe wording. No pentest, no legal license opinion, no invented answers, no engineering fixes unless you route that separately." Direct vendor pitch: "If a named customer scanner or security review is blocking a PO, renewal, deployment, or milestone over CVEs or missing SBOM/VEX evidence, send the exact finding and product version. I turn existing build/dependency/release evidence into a customer-ready exception packet or a paid no-go."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-12,000 PLN for a narrow customer exception around one product/version and a limited finding set; 14,000-25,000 PLN for a multi-component SBOM/VEX packet tied to a customer security review; and 25,000-45,000 PLN for urgent multi-product or strategic-account packets. Partner share is 20-35% where routed. Reviewer cost is 1,500-6,000 PLN per case depending on complexity. The end vendor pays because the packet is small relative to a blocked 100,000-1,000,000 PLN enterprise deal, renewal, deployment, or customer milestone.

Copy risk:
Appsec firms, product-security consultancies, SOC2/fCISO partners, DevSecOps shops, scanner vendors, SBOM tools, GRC tools, and internal security teams can copy pieces. The defense is current-case control: exact buyer exception, product version, artifact evidence, partner trust path, submitted packet, buyer outcome, and a private memory of which VEX/affected-status formats reduce customer friction.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the vendor or partner authorizes the startup into a named customer exception, provides the artifact evidence, prepays the packet, and routes the response through the current security review, a competitor cannot instantly own that same packet without the vendor restarting under deadline pressure. Incumbents can copy future workflows, but not the active exception file already under mandate.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a prepaid customer security exception packet tied to a named buyer, product version, finding set, evidence room, and commercial deadline. It is not generic SBOM tooling, a vulnerability scanner, a dashboard, marketplace, appsec retainer, penetration test, remediation sprint, compliance report, or brokered referral. No live customer exception, no product/version evidence, no prepaid fee, no work.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No legal advice, open-source license opinion, security certification, penetration testing, formal exploitability certification, vulnerability disclosure handling, incident response, production remediation, code changes, SOC2/ISO audit opinion, CRA compliance certification, or guarantee of customer acceptance. Vendors remain responsible for actual remediation, disclosure, product security, final statements, and customer commitments. Formal assurance, legal, pentest, and remediation work is routed to qualified partners or rejected.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because the buyer trigger is not regulated-finance ICT third-party-risk or DORA supplier evidence. It is not RAGProbe, AIQOverflow, or any AI/appsec questionnaire branch because the controlled artifact is SBOM/VEX/CVE affected-status evidence for a named product/version and customer scanner exception, not AI questionnaire wording or RAG/agent testing. It is not PCI TPSP evidence, CRA vulnerability reporting, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Appsec and product-security firms already own this credibility and can copy the workflow. Scanner and SBOM tools increasingly generate much of the raw artifact. Buyers may reject a scoped packet and demand formal pentesting, remediation, vendor advisory, security certification, or actual component upgrade. Vendors may lack the build, dependency, release, or exploitability evidence needed for a defensible VEX statement. Affected/not-affected claims can create reputational and contractual risk if wording is loose. Open-source license issues can contaminate the case and require lawyers. Reviewer cost can crush margin if every case is bespoke. Partner routing may be soft referrals rather than guaranteed current cases. Buyer acceptance remains outside the startup's control. The product can become ordinary appsec consulting unless intake is limited to live customer exceptions.
