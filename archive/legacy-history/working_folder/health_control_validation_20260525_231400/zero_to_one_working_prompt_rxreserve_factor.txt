Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality.

Scored idea:

RxReserve Factor is a B2B receivables-control business for licensed high-risk healthcare merchants in Poland/CEE, especially legal obesity/diabetes/GLP-1 consultation clinics, psychiatry, pain, addiction, medical-cannabis, and other Rx-adjacent clinics whose PSP/acquirer reserves, rolling reserves, chargeback holdbacks, or settlement balances are frozen or delayed. It is not an Ozempic marketplace, pharmacy, telemedicine clinic, prescription broker, ad platform, or drug seller. It never sells, advertises, sources, forwards, or touches prescription medicines.

Core guardrail:
The business only works with licensed legal clinics/providers and lawful clinic/service revenue. If prescription medicine is involved, the medicine sale/dispensing remains inside licensed pharmacy/healthcare channels as legally required. The startup only buys or advances against a specific assigned B2B receivable owed by a PSP/acquirer/processor to a clinic/provider. It does not influence prescribing, drug choice, pharmacy fulfillment, or patient treatment.

Buyer:
- licensed obesity/diabetes clinics charging for consultations, not medicine sale;
- licensed psychiatry, pain, addiction, medical-cannabis, or other high-risk telemedicine/private clinics;
- legal clinic groups whose PSP/acquirer withholds rolling reserves because of refund, chargeback, or vertical-risk concerns;
- small clinic owners with legitimate revenue trapped while payroll, marketing, rent, and doctor payments are due.

Acute trigger:
GLP-1 and controlled-Rx scrutiny, high refund/chargeback ratios, processor risk review, acquirer rolling reserves, or settlement holds trap cash even when the underlying clinic activity is legal. A clinic may have 30,000-200,000 PLN in reserves or delayed settlements but cannot use it for payroll, doctors, rent, or growth.

Control point:
RxReserve does not sell compliance software. It buys or advances against one specific acknowledged receivable:
- PSP/acquirer reserve, rolling reserve, settlement holdback, chargeback reserve, or released-but-unpaid balance;
- evidenced by PSP/acquirer statement, reserve schedule, settlement report, or written balance confirmation;
- assigned by the clinic/provider under a written receivable assignment or factoring agreement;
- not blocked by anti-assignment terms, or expressly consented to by the PSP/acquirer;
- acknowledged by the PSP/acquirer/debtor, or accompanied by written payment instruction to a controlled account.

The hard control point is debtor-acknowledged receivable custody. Without debtor/PSP/acquirer acknowledgment or payment-direction control, the idea should be capped around 80 and not pursued.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- identify one licensed clinic/provider with 30,000-80,000 PLN trapped PSP/acquirer reserve or settlement receivable;
- verify license/legal status and underlying revenue source;
- obtain PSP/acquirer reserve statement, settlement report, or balance confirmation;
- legal review of assignment, anti-assignment terms, factoring/loan boundary, AML/KYC, tax/VAT/accounting treatment, data minimization, and clinic compliance red flags;
- sign assignment/factoring agreement and debtor acknowledgment or payment-instruction confirmation;
- advance a conservative amount at discount, or buy the receivable outright at a risk-adjusted price;
- collect partial or full release into controlled account inside 60 days.

Required POC proof:
1. One 30,000-80,000 PLN assigned PSP/acquirer reserve receivable from a licensed legal clinic/provider.
2. Written debtor/PSP/acquirer acknowledgment of balance or payment-direction confirmation to controlled account.
3. Legal memo confirming the assignment route is not blocked and the clinic/provider activity is licensed/legal.
4. Partial or full cash release collected within 60 days.
5. Net economics showing discount/factoring fee covers legal review, adverse-selection risk, capital cost, and expected defaults/disputes.

Economics:
Revenue comes from discount/factoring spread or advance fee on short-duration, acknowledged high-risk merchant receivables. Example: buy or advance against a 50,000 PLN acknowledged reserve at a 5-12% risk-adjusted discount/fee depending on release date and risk; use one deal to prove underwriting, legal workflow, and collection. The business can expand from Rx-adjacent clinics to other legally operating high-risk healthcare merchants with trapped acquirer reserves.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The asset is an assigned receivable plus debtor acknowledgment or payment-direction control. Service work exists only to diligence and collect that receivable. If a competitor wants the same cash flow, it must obtain the legal assignment and debtor acknowledgment before the receivable is paid out.

Why this fits the user's requested domain:
It is GLP-1 / controlled-Rx adjacent in a legal way. It monetizes payment-flow friction created by high-risk prescription-related clinic demand without selling or advertising prescription medicines and without touching patients or drugs.

Main risks acknowledged:
- PSP/acquirer terms may prohibit assignment or require consent.
- PSP/acquirer may refuse acknowledgment or payment-direction changes.
- Underlying clinic may be non-compliant, gray-market, misleadingly advertising prescription drugs, or exposed to refunds/chargebacks/regulatory risk; those are immediate kill criteria.
- Adverse selection is high; distressed merchants may have disputed receivables, fraud flags, chargeback spikes, or frozen funds that never release.
- Factoring/advance structure requires legal/tax setup and may create lending, AML/KYC, or accounting issues.
- One small receivable proves collection mechanics, not repeatability.
- Generic factoring firms can copy if the risk is simple, but many avoid high-risk Rx-adjacent merchants; defensibility depends on niche underwriting, clinic-compliance diligence, PSP/debtor acknowledgment workflow, and fast small-ticket execution.
