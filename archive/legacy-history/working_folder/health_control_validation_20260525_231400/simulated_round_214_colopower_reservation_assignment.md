## Why It Passes Internal Simulation

- AI-ready power and high-density colocation capacity are scarce enough that exact rack/kW reservations can have transaction value.
- The control point is not generic lead generation: a seller signs an option over an already contracted or reserved provider-approved rack/kW slot, a buyer places a deposit for that specific capacity, and the colocation provider confirms assignment, novation, or sponsored sublease path.
- The founder does not build a data center, sell electricity, operate cloud compute, or resell hardware.
- The first proof is current buyer cash and provider non-objection, not a report.
- A Warsaw founder can target smaller CEE/European tenants with stranded or underused commitments that large brokers may ignore.

## Internal Cap Check

- Concrete 6-month control proof: yes, provider-approved transfer paths, signed seller options, buyer deposits, and first assignment fee.
- Copyable by one consultant/hire: partially, but exact signed rack/kW options and deposits control named capacity before competitors can sell it.
- CAC/payback/margins/cash conversion: credible if focused only on 20-100 kW high-density reservations with deposits, not ordinary low-density racks.
- Vague partnerships: avoid by requiring provider written non-objection and buyer deposit before counting control.
- Legal/regulatory authority: manageable if all assignment/sublease/novation is provider-approved and lawyers review transfer terms.
