## Lead Candidate

**CaliberLock Watchmaker Parts Bank**

### Internal Simulated Score

89 / 100

### Why It Clears Simulation

- The scarce unit is exact: a named movement caliber, part reference, donor movement, dial, crown, stem, wheel, bridge, balance, staff, date component, pusher, crystal, bracelet link, or case component.
- Logistics are trivial relative to value.
- Supply is structurally fragmented in retiring watchmaker drawers, estate lots, old jewelry shops, defunct repair benches, and unsorted hoards.
- The first proof is not "interesting inventory"; it is watchmaker deposits or paid orders against active repair jobs.
- The startup can avoid craft-provider dependence by selling parts to professional watchmakers rather than repairing watches.
- Buyer-side search pain is real, and a single rare part can unblock a paid repair on a valuable vintage watch.

### Internal Objections

- Existing vintage parts dealers, eBay sellers, watch forums, and brand-service channels already serve parts demand.
- Authentication, condition, compatibility, originality, and counterfeit risk are serious.
- The founder lacks horology credibility and needs a paid watchmaker reviewer from day one.
- Seller sourcing is hard because retiring watchmakers or estates may know value or prefer known dealers.
- Cataloging unsorted parts can be slow and expert-heavy.
- The niche may become a profitable specialist parts desk rather than a large company.

### Gate Decision

Advance to working Zero To One validation because the simulated score is strictly above 87 and the control point is titled exact inventory plus active watchmaker repair-demand deposits, not a generic marketplace or service.
