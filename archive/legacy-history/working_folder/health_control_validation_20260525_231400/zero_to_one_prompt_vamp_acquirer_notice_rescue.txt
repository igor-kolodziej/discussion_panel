Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
VAMP Acquirer Notice Rescue Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Build a paid live-case desk for ecommerce merchants and payment-risk agencies whose Visa VAMP, acquirer, PSP, ISO, or payment-facilitator notice threatens fines, reserves, processing limits, MID termination, or offboarding, and turn the exact notice plus merchant evidence into an acquirer-ready remediation, evidence, no-go, or reserve/fine release file.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE ecommerce merchants, subscription SaaS merchants, digital-goods sellers, ticketing/event sellers, travel/service merchants, and payment-risk or chargeback agencies serving merchants that have received a written acquirer/PSP/ISO/payment-facilitator risk notice tied to Visa VAMP, excessive disputes, fraud/TC40 pressure, chargeback ratio, refund behavior, descriptor confusion, order evidence gaps, high-risk transaction patterns, reserve increase, fine pass-through, processing cap, remediation demand, or MID termination risk. The first buyer has meaningful card revenue and a current written payment-account consequence, not a general desire to lower chargebacks.

Acute trigger:
The merchant receives a current acquirer or PSP message saying its card-not-present dispute/fraud/monitoring profile is over threshold or under review, with a deadline to explain, remediate, reduce ratios, submit evidence, accept reserves, pay fines, change operations, or risk limits/offboarding. The merchant is not buying a dashboard or chargeback tool; it is paying because card-processing continuity, cash reserves, network/acquirer fines, processor relationship, or current payment acceptance is threatened.

Control point:
Signed case mandate over a named merchant account or MID notice; merchant authorization; exact acquirer/PSP/ISO/payfac notice text; processor statements; dispute and fraud monitoring reports where available; TC40/fraud/dispute counts as supplied by acquirer or PSP; sales volume and transaction denominator data; order, delivery, refund, cancellation, descriptor, customer-service, 3DS, fraud-screening, CE 3.0, representment, and prior-warning evidence; reserve/fine/fee statement; acquirer correspondence chronology; clean no-go criteria for bad-risk merchants; and client-approved remediation response. The startup controls the response file, evidence map, timeline, agency channel, and fee right for that specific notice, not the merchant account, acquirer decision, card-network rules, payment processing, or fraud tooling.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five paid live acquirer/PSP notices under written merchant authorization; at least 60,000 PLN collected in prepaid triage and rescue fees; one payment-risk/chargeback agency or PSP-adjacent partner signs referral terms; all accepted cases have written notice text, deadline, MID/account identifiers, reserve/fine/termination consequence, available dispute/fraud/order/refund evidence, and merchant authority; at least two cases produce concrete outcomes such as acquirer-accepted remediation plan, reserve reduction/release path, fine credit/waiver, deadline extension, processing cap avoided, MID retained, or paid clean no-go; and no case is accepted if the merchant appears fraudulent, sanctioned, illegal, deceptive, or unwilling to change the underlying behavior.

6-month POC:
Process 25-40 live notices from 4-6 agency/PSP-adjacent channels; collect 250,000-600,000 PLN in triage, rescue, and outcome fees; document at least 15 acquirer/PSP outcomes or clean no-go decisions; build a private notice taxonomy by acquirer/PSP wording, VAMP/dispute/fraud issue, merchant category, evidence object, remediation lever, reserve/fine path, and accepted response pattern; keep gross margin above 60%; keep evidence-ready response turnaround under five business days; reject 30%+ of cases where no truthful remediation path exists; and continue only if at least two referral channels send five or more paid cases each.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with chargeback agencies, payment-risk freelancers, PSP integration consultants, ecommerce finance operators, and agencies serving merchants with live account-health or payment-hold issues. Partner pitch: "When a merchant receives a VAMP/acquirer/PSP risk notice that is bigger than ordinary representment, send it to us. We assemble the notice-specific survival file, evidence map, remediation response, and no-go decision while you keep the client relationship and representment/fraud tooling work." Direct pitch to merchants: "If your processor has sent a written notice threatening reserves, fines, limits, or termination, I turn the exact notice, dispute/fraud reports, order/refund evidence, and remediation actions into a response file for your acquirer or PSP."

Economics:
Charge 3,000-6,000 PLN for triage and a submit/cannot-submit/no-go decision, 12,000-45,000 PLN for a notice rescue file, and optional 5-12% outcome fee only on measurable reserve release/reduction, fine credit/waiver, or specifically quantified cash avoided by written acquirer/PSP confirmation. Agencies keep a 20-35% referral or resale margin. Buyer ROI is credible when account termination, reserve increase, network fine pass-through, processing cap, or lost card acceptance threatens current revenue. Founder capital stays under 100,000 PLN because the work is evidence reconstruction, case management, partner referral, and specialist review, not reserve financing, payment processing, or fraud-tool buildout.

Copy risk:
Chargeback firms, fraud-prevention vendors, PSP risk teams, payment consultants, payment brokers, ecommerce agencies, acquirers, and in-house risk teams can copy pieces. The defense is not generic chargeback knowledge. It is live acquirer-notice case flow, exact wording taxonomy, evidence-object memory, no-go discipline, referral relationships, and response patterns tied to concrete reserve/fine/termination outcomes. Once the startup controls a named notice mandate and filed response chronology, a competitor cannot take that specific case without the merchant or agency switching mid-notice.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup acts only after a signed mandate over a specific acquirer/PSP notice, with exact account data, deadline, evidence map, correspondence chronology, and fee agreement. A fraud vendor or chargeback firm can sell future tools or representment, but it cannot instantly control the already-authorized response file, acquirer correspondence, agency fee right, and submitted remediation package for that named MID/account notice unless the merchant replaces the startup before the deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell generic fraud consulting, chargeback representment, PSP introductions, payment brokerage, dashboards, fraud tools, legal advice, or merchant-account applications. It accepts only current written notices with a named payment-account consequence and produces a bounded acquirer-ready response package: notice interpretation, evidence map, remediation log, no-go findings, response wording, and outcome tracker. Proof is payment-account progress, reserve/fine path, deadline extension, or clean no-go, not a report about possible chargeback reduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not process payments, hold merchant funds, act as acquirer/ISO/payment facilitator, underwrite merchants, provide legal advice, advise on card-network rule interpretation beyond evidence mapping, represent merchants in litigation/arbitration, submit false evidence, handle illegal/high-risk goods, or guarantee Visa/acquirer/PSP acceptance. Merchants remain responsible for their sales practices, refunds, customer communication, fraud controls, disclosures, shipping, cancellation flows, product legality, and processor relationship. PSPs/acquirers, fraud vendors, lawyers, and chargeback specialists remain responsible for their domains. Reject deceptive merchants, fake evidence, illegal products, sanctions/fraud concerns, intentionally abusive subscription flows, and cases where the merchant refuses operational remediation.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, PSP Reserve Release Mandate, Cloud Marketplace Remittance Recovery, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, PartnerCenter incentive release, COD courier remittance, carrier credit recovery, KSeF payment release, VoP payment exception release, cyber-insurance evidence, or generic chargeback representment. The buyer trigger, control object, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: live card-network/acquirer/PSP risk notices threatening payment-account continuity, reserves, fines, or MID survival.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Chargeback, fraud-prevention, and PSP-risk incumbents already serve this market. Many merchants with VAMP/acquirer notices are genuinely bad risk and should be rejected, not rescued. Acquirers may ignore a third-party response or require all communication through the merchant or ISO. Sensitive payment, fraud, customer, order, refund, and dispute data creates trust friction for an unknown founder. Outcome attribution is hard if ratios improve naturally or the merchant changes behavior independently. The work can drift into fraud-tool implementation, chargeback representation, legal/card-network interpretation, merchant-account brokerage, or high-risk payment consulting. Agency channels may internalize the checklist after seeing it. Case flow may be lumpy and concentrated in low-quality merchants. The founder needs strict no-go rules and a credible payment-risk reviewer from day one.
