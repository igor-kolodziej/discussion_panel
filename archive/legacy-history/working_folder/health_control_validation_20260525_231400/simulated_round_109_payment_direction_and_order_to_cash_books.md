## Simulated Pass Candidate

### Retail EDI Order-To-Cash Continuity Book Buyout - 91

One-sentence thesis: option or acquire a retiring/overloaded micro-EDI integrator's live retail-supplier order-to-cash maintenance book, transfer customer-approved admin/payment rights, and keep production EDI/KSeF-adjacent order, ASN, invoice, and remittance flows alive under prepaid continuity contracts.

Buyer: Polish/CEE SME suppliers, distributors, and challenger brands selling to large retailers, marketplaces, wholesalers, or buying groups through existing EDI/order-to-cash flows. The first customers are already paying a small EDI maintainer for exact production mappings, not shopping for a new ERP implementation.

Acute trigger: a broken EDI flow can block or delay purchase orders, order responses, despatch advice/ASN, goods-receipt matching, invoice acceptance, KSeF-to-retailer handoff, remittance matching, or payment. KSeF 2026 increases invoice-flow change pressure, but the buyer pays because an existing production order-to-cash dependency must not break.

Transferable control point: signed seller option plus source scripts, mappings, AS2/SFTP/API/mailbox credentials, certificates, GLN/partner profiles, retailer-specific runbooks, support history, renewal calendar, customer contacts, seller endorsement, customer-approved admin transfer, and prepaid continuity contracts.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:

- One signed option/acquisition agreement with a named micro-EDI vendor covering code, mappings, credentials, runbooks, support inbox, customer list, renewal rights, seller handoff, and earnout.
- Clean IP/assignment review and data-processing/security terms for message handling.
- At least 8 supplier customers approve transfer of support owner, access, and payee.
- At least 6 supplier customers prepay 3 months or one annual continuity term, with at least 35,000 PLN cleared before close.
- Credentials/certificates are rotated or placed under customer-approved access control.
- At least one live production EDI/order-to-cash incident or change ticket is resolved under the new owner.
- At least one current message batch is monitored or processed under new custody: order, order response, ASN/despatch advice, invoice, or remittance/status.
- Support history proves reusable maintenance, not bespoke ERP cleanup; target contribution margin above 55% after contractor support, security ops, legal amortization, and seller earnout.

First acquisition mechanism: target one-person EDI freelancers, retired ERP/Comarch/ECOD/GS1/retail integration specialists, small integration shops, and overloaded micro-MSPs serving suppliers that sell to Polish and CEE retail chains. The founder offers an option plus earnout, not an upfront buyout. Customer prepayment before close proves the book is live.

Gross margin and payback logic: sell continuity at 1,500-4,000 PLN per supplier per month, plus 5,000-15,000 PLN transition/KSeF compatibility sprints only for inherited customers. Six customers prepaying 35,000 PLN funds diligence and transition. A 20-40 customer book can produce meaningful owner earnings if support remains reusable and net-new implementation scope is excluded.

Copy risk: Comarch, ECOD/EDI providers, ERP vendors, GS1/EDI consultants, MSPs, and freelancers can replace or build flows. The defense is not generic EDI skill. It is the seller-endorsed custody of exact production mappings, credentials, retailer profiles, support history, and prepaid customer contracts before competitors see the handoff risk.

Why incumbents cannot copy before control is secured: once the founder controls the seller option, customer-approved admin transfer, credentials, runbooks, support inbox, renewal/payment rights, and prepaid continuity contracts, a competitor cannot recreate those exact live production flows without winning a replacement project from each customer.

Why this is not a service/report/app/dashboard/database/marketplace/broker: the founder is not selling a KSeF report, dashboard, or new integration marketplace. The business acquires an existing paid production dependency and its recurring payment stream. Service work exists only to maintain the acquired book under strict scope.

Reasons this could still fail:

- The exact seller profile may be scarce or messy.
- Customer approvals may fail if they prefer an established EDI platform.
- IP, credentials, certificates, or data-processing terms may be non-transferable.
- Support history may reveal bespoke ERP/accounting cleanup.
- KSeF scope can become tax/legal/accounting advice.
- Incumbents can replace customers over time if continuity trust is weak.
- Part-time operation requires a reliable contractor bench and clear incident response.

Decision: advance this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
