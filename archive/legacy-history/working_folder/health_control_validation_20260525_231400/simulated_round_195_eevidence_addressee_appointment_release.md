## Search Frame

Recent failures show that generic evidence desks, AI/security buyer packets, platform recovery cases, micro-acquisitions, and regulated-capacity lockboxes are being capped because incumbents own trust, source data, or the decision. This round tests a narrower 2026 EU legal-designation trigger where the hard artifact is not a report: a signed appointment or designated-establishment/legal-representative route for e-Evidence orders, tied to a named commercial blocker.

Official source check:

- EUR-Lex summary for Regulation (EU) 2023/1543: the e-Evidence Regulation applies from 18 August 2026.
- EUR-Lex Directive (EU) 2023/1544: service providers offering services in the Union on 18 February 2026 must designate at least one establishment or appoint at least one legal representative by 18 August 2026.
- European Commission e-Evidence page: the package obliges service providers to designate an establishment or appoint a legal representative in the Union.

The startup does not act as legal representative, does not handle production or preservation orders, and does not give criminal-law advice. It controls only the current appointment-execution file, qualified provider/law-firm route, internal request-intake runbook, customer-ready proof, and appointment confirmation for a live procurement, renewal, reseller, diligence, or EU-launch blocker.

## Internal Simulation

The candidate clears the simulated gate because the first proof is a specific appointment-execution file for a live commercial blocker, not broad compliance advice. Within 60 days, the founder can prove partner routes with EU representative/privacy/law-firm providers, paid live-blocker mandates, management authorization, scoped provider facts, and at least several signed appointment/provider-acceptance/customer-proof outcomes or clean no-go decisions.

The buyer trigger is narrow enough to avoid selling education: a named EU procurement, reseller, DPA/security review, diligence, or launch owner asks how the provider will designate an EU addressee and route e-Evidence orders before the 18 August 2026 date. The proof artifact is appointment/provider acceptance plus a practical intake/escalation runbook and customer-ready proof letter.

## Internal Caps Applied

- Cap below 82 if the startup acts as legal representative, receives orders, evaluates order lawfulness, touches evidence production, or handles law-enforcement authority decisions.
- Cap below 85 if the buyer lacks a named procurement/customer/EU-launch blocker and only wants general compliance education.
- Cap below 87 if qualified providers or law firms will not route cases, or if the deliverable collapses into a generic memo.
- Cap below 87 if customer proof is not tied to signed appointment/provider acceptance, management authorization, intake owners, escalation rules, and outcome evidence.

## Main Risk Before Real Validation

Zero To One may still treat this as compliance coordination owned by law firms, privacy consultants, or EU representative providers. The real validation turns on whether the near-term designation obligation, signed appointment artifact, and live commercial blocker make it materially stronger than a generic evidence desk.
