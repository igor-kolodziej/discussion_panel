Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Approved RMA Labor-Credit Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
A recovery mandate for PV, heat-pump, refrigeration, EV-charger, security, and other installer/service firms that already have RMA, warranty, labor-reimbursement, return, freight, or distributor-credit cases but have not converted those payor-acknowledged cases into credit memos, account offsets, or reimbursements.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE installers, service contractors, and small distributor-integrators with recurring warranty replacement work: PV installers, heat-pump installers, refrigeration service firms, EV-charger installers, security/low-voltage integrators, industrial maintenance firms, and equipment resellers that have approved or account-evidenced RMA/warranty/credit lines with OEMs, wholesalers, or distributors.

Acute trigger:
The buyer has already repaired or replaced equipment, received an RMA number, filed a warranty case, sent a defective part back, or received distributor/OEM acknowledgement, but the expected credit memo, labor reimbursement, replacement offset, freight credit, return credit, or account balance correction has not arrived. The missing credit ties up cash, hides job profitability, and forces the installer to finance replacement work or write off earned reimbursements.

Control point:
Signed installer mandate over named RMA/warranty/credit cases; distributor/OEM account statement or portal export; RMA numbers, serial numbers, installation and commissioning dates, product registrations, claim windows, photos, service reports, return labels, shipment proof, invoices, and job notes; payor acknowledgement, claim status, or portal evidence showing an eligible reimbursement or credit path; submitted follow-up packet; credit memo/payment trail; and first recovered credit, account offset, or reimbursement. The startup controls the active recovery file for exact claim lines, not the technical warranty decision.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five installers or service firms sign recovery mandates covering at least 40 named RMA/warranty/credit cases; at least 150,000 PLN of claimed credits or reimbursements are documented through distributor/OEM statements, RMA portals, service records, or written payor acknowledgements; two distributor/OEM payor paths are confirmed in writing for credit follow-up; at least 40,000 PLN of credit memos, account offsets, or reimbursements are recovered; and at least three installers pay fixed screened-ledger fees or agree to success fees from recovered credits before total spend exceeds 100,000 PLN.

6-month POC:
Handle 20-35 installer ledgers; accept 250-500 named claim lines; recover or credit 300,000-900,000 PLN; collect 80,000-220,000 PLN in fixed and success fees; maintain gross margin above 65% after admin reviewers; build a private recovery map by OEM/distributor, portal, serial evidence, claim window, return proof, and credit memo failure mode; reject at least 35% of cases as no-acknowledgement, warranty-disputed, consumer-facing, stale, technical, unsafe, or legally contested.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target installers and service firms with many replacement jobs but weak admin: PV/heat-pump/refrigeration/EV/security contractors, wholesalers' installer communities, industry Facebook/LinkedIn groups, accounting offices serving installers, and distributor sales reps who know customers are carrying old warranty credits. The opening offer is: "Send only your named RMA, warranty, labor-credit, return-credit, or freight-credit lines where a payor path already exists. I recover credit memos or account offsets; I do not argue technical warranty eligibility or handle consumer disputes."

Economics:
Charge a 1,500-5,000 PLN screened-ledger fee for the first account review plus 15-25% of recovered credits or 400-2,500 PLN per recovered claim line, depending on value and complexity. The fee is credible when installers are carrying old credits, replacement costs, labor reimbursements, or account offsets they already earned but have not collected. Costs are admin reviewers and occasional technical document checks. The model avoids inventory, receivable purchase, client-fund custody, warranty adjudication, and repair labor.

Copy risk:
Distributors, OEMs, installer ERPs, warranty automation tools, outsourced admin providers, accountants, and service managers can copy pieces. The defense is narrow case ownership: signed mandates over exact RMA ledgers, portal histories, distributor account trails, return proof, claim-window evidence, and credit memo follow-up patterns. Longer-term advantage comes from repeated payor-path memory across fragmented CEE distributors and installers, not from generic warranty advice.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the installer signs the mandate and provides the RMA ledger, portal exports, serial evidence, account statements, and distributor/OEM contact path, the startup controls those exact pending credit cases. A distributor, OEM, accountant, or admin contractor can offer a similar process later, but cannot collect or reassign the already-authorized claim queue without the installer moving the mandate and exposing the same account evidence again.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell warranty consulting, repair services, technical diagnosis, consumer advocacy, product registration software, dashboards, or introductions. It accepts only named RMA/warranty/credit lines where an installer or service firm already has account evidence and a payor path. The output is recovered cash, credit memo, account offset, or documented no-go for that exact credit line.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not decide warranty eligibility, certify repairs, inspect equipment, perform installation work, represent consumers, provide legal advice, hold funds, handle disputes, or guarantee manufacturer outcomes. OEMs, distributors, authorized service providers, installers, engineers, and lawyers remain responsible for warranty interpretation, technical assessment, repair quality, product safety, and legal claims. Reject consumer complaints, clinical/medical equipment, disputed warranty coverage, missing serial identity, unsafe installation evidence, fraud flags, chargebacks, insolvency, export-control or sanctions concerns, and any case requiring litigation or regulated claims handling.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not BatteryFit because it does not sell retrofit advice or battery economics to installers. It is not HeatQuiet because it does not handle noise complaints or heat-pump siting. It is not Solar Inverter Replacement Options, GridFund, BESSGuard, PV GridTrip, or other energy-procurement ideas because the founder is not advising project replacements or funding release. It is not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL, freight audit, PSP reserve, KSeF, DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, ShowComms, LeaseClear, or any platform evidence desk. The payment event is payor-acknowledged RMA/warranty/labor credit recovery for contractor accounts.

Strongest anticipated objections:
OEMs and distributors may already provide portals and account managers, so recoverable cases may be small or caused by the installer failing basic admin. Warranty automation vendors, distributor ERPs, accountants, and service managers can copy the workflow. Many apparent credits will not be collectible because the claim window expired, registration was missing, return proof is absent, installation evidence is weak, or the warranty decision is genuinely disputed. Per-line economics can be too small unless the founder screens for high-value ledgers. Installers may resist sharing portal/account access with an unknown founder. A part-time founder may struggle to chase many distributors quickly. If most value is technical eligibility argument rather than payor-acknowledged credit follow-up, the model becomes warranty consulting or dispute handling and should be killed.
