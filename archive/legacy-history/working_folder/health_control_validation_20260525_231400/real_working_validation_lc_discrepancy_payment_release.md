## Strongest Objections

1. Incumbents are very close to the workflow: banks, forwarders, export departments, chambers, LC consultants, trade-finance advisers, and lawyers.
2. Many discrepancies cannot be cured and require applicant waiver or no-go.
3. Banks and applicants control acceptance and payment timing; the startup cannot promise payment release.
4. Legal and fraud boundaries are dangerous around forged documents, backdating, cargo/title disputes, sanctions, dual-use issues, buyer refusal, and legal interpretation.
5. Unknown Warsaw founder credibility is weak for an exporter under LC cash pressure.
6. Case volume may be lumpy because many SMEs avoid LCs or already use open account, advance payment, CAD, factoring, guarantees, or credit insurance.
7. Repeated LC users may already have trained export-document staff; occasional LC users are a better target.
8. Reviewer cost can eat margin if every file needs senior LC expert hours.
9. Timing can be brutal when corrections require originals, couriering, chamber/legalization, carrier amendments, insurance endorsements, or applicant waiver.
10. The business can drift into trade-finance consulting unless it accepts only written discrepancy notice plus named payment hold.

## Evaluator Advice

Best beachhead: Polish/CEE SME exporters with occasional but material LC shipments, routed by freight forwarders, export accountants, chambers/export desks, and trade-finance advisers.

Accept only cases with signed exporter mandate, named LC, LC copy and amendments, full presented document set, written bank discrepancy/refusal/reserve notice, payment value above roughly 300,000-500,000 PLN, bank deadline and channel, source-document contacts, applicant waiver path if authorized, and prepaid fixed fee.

Reject no written discrepancy notice, fraud or forged-document risk, sanctions/export-control uncertainty, cargo/title dispute, buyer refusal unrelated to documents, legal interpretation as the core issue, missing original documents with no correction route, and attempts to pressure the buyer.

## Lesson

This is a near-miss cash-control branch. A one-case LC mandate is still not quite hard enough under the real gate. Any continuation must improve the distribution/control point, for example by controlling already-sold forwarder/accountant/chamber overflow cases or a recurring LC document-prep payment stream, not by merely rewording the same discrepancy desk.
