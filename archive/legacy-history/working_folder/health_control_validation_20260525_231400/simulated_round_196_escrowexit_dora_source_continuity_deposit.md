## Selected Candidate

**Idea:** EscrowExit DORA Source-Continuity Deposit Pack

**Simulated score:** 88.5/100

## Why It Clears The Simulated Gate

- The proof artifact is objectively harder than an evidence response: signed escrow/provider acceptance, source snapshot, build manifest, dependency list, runbook, checksum, access/release-condition summary, and buyer-ready deposit receipt.
- The buyer trigger is narrow and acute: the bank/insurer/payment institution renewal or procurement has specifically asked for source-code escrow, exit support, recoverability, or step-in continuity evidence.
- A small vendor may not be able to use a generic escrow provider without technical packaging, build/runbook triage, evidence wording, and boundary review.
- Cash conversion is strong because the fee protects a regulated-finance renewal, procurement, or strategic customer.

## Internal Caps Applied

- Cap below 82 if the request is only a broad DORA questionnaire or exit-plan question with no escrow/source/build artifact.
- Cap below 85 if the startup acts as the escrow agent, negotiates release clauses, or gives legal opinions.
- Cap below 87 if escrow providers/law firms can solve the whole case with no technical packaging gap.
- Cap below 87 if source/build verification requires open-ended engineering, security audit, or product remediation.

## Main Risk Before Real Validation

Zero To One may treat this as too close to the confirmed DORA idea or as ordinary software escrow work owned by escrow providers, law firms, and security/SRE consultancies. The prompt must therefore distinguish the exact control artifact and avoid broad DORA evidence-pack language.
