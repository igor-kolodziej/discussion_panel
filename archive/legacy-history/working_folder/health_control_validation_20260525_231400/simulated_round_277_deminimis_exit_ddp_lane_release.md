## Internal Simulation

Score: **88.3 / 100**

### Why It Clears Simulation

- The trigger is current and cash-linked: low-value US ecommerce shipments cannot be priced, cleared, or promised using old de minimis assumptions.
- The first proof is not a memo. It is a named lane converted into a broker-cleared entry flow with first shipments released, landed-cost approval, or written no-go.
- The buyer has immediate pain: stalled US orders, cancelled shipping promises, margin shock, customer complaints, parcel returns, warehouse holds, or loss of a US market lane.
- A solo founder can operate as lane coordinator and evidence packager while licensed customs brokers, freight forwarders, IOR/DDP providers, tax advisers, and counsel remain providers of record.
- Channel acquisition can run through ecommerce 3PLs, Shopify/WooCommerce agencies, DTC accountants, fulfillment consultants, and freight forwarders that see the mess but do not want to rebuild every seller's SKU/landed-cost file.

### Internal Cap Pressure

- Customs brokers and 3PLs are natural incumbents.
- HTS/classification, IOR, duties, product admissibility, FDA/CPSC/other PGA flags, sanctions, and tax advice can overwhelm the narrow lane.
- Some DTC sellers cannot economically survive the post-de-minimis cost structure; no-go decisions must be paid outcomes.
- US legal and broker authority must stay outside the startup.

The idea still advances because the control point is a current named lane plus broker-authorized conversion and first released shipments, not a broad customs-consulting promise.

## 60-Day Control Proof

- 3 ecommerce 3PL, agency, or DTC exporter channel agreements.
- 8 paid seller lane triages.
- 4 accepted lane-conversion files with named SKUs, order lanes, broker route, landed-cost approval, and seller-authorized shipment plan.
- 2 live lanes release first formal/informal entries or produce documented no-go.
- 60,000-160,000 PLN collected or invoiced.
- Rejection/no-go rate above 40% for bad unit economics, missing SKU truth, regulated goods, weak IOR route, tax/legal issues, or insufficient margin.
