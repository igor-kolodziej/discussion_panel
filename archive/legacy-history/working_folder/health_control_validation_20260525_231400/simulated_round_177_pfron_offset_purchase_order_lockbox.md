# Simulated Round 177: PFRON Offset Purchase-Order Lockbox

Date: 2026-05-29

## Prior Lessons Applied

Most recovery, evidence, micro-acquisition, and platform-block ideas failed because they did not control cash movement. This round looks for a recurring statutory cash outflow where the founder can control a purchase order, supplier evidence, payment direction, and first-month reduction before any build.

The lead candidate is not a PFRON advisory memo. It works only if a buyer redirects a real recurring purchase to an eligible supplier, pays on time, receives reduction information, and its payroll/accounting owner recognizes a lower current PFRON payment.

## Simulated Pass Candidate

Idea name: PFRON Offset Purchase-Order Lockbox

Simulated score: 88 / 100

One-sentence thesis: Route existing recurring B2B purchases of Polish employers that pay mandatory PFRON contributions to verified eligible suppliers, but only get paid after a real invoice, timely payment, reduction information, and recognized current-month PFRON cash reduction.

Exact buyer: Polish employers with at least 25 FTE that pay meaningful monthly PFRON contributions because they do not reach the required disabled-employment threshold. Best first buyers have 50-500 employees, recurring purchases that can be switched without operational risk, CFO/payroll pain, and current monthly PFRON payments above 10,000 PLN.

Acute trigger: The employer is paying PFRON monthly while also buying services or production that could be sourced from eligible suppliers. The 2026 rules make buyer requests, seller eligibility, invoice timing, payment timing, and reduction information operationally important. Cash savings show in the next payroll/accounting cycle if the purchase is valid and paid on time.

Control point: Signed buyer purchase-routing mandate; verified eligible supplier list; supplier written confirmation of right to issue reduction information for the relevant production/service; buyer request to supplier; purchase order; invoice; payment proof; seller reduction information; payroll/accounting signoff; and first monthly PFRON payment reduction recognized by the buyer's accountant/payroll owner.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof: Three buyers sign purchase-routing mandates; five suppliers pass eligibility and documentation checks; at least 150,000 PLN of existing monthly/quarterly purchases are rerouted; invoices are paid on time; seller reduction information is issued; at least 30,000 PLN of current PFRON contribution reduction is recognized; one buyer signs a recurring monthly retainer or savings-share after first reduction.

6-month POC: Control 20 buyers and 30 eligible suppliers; route 1,500,000-3,000,000 PLN of purchases; document 300,000-800,000 PLN recognized contribution reductions; collect 60,000-200,000 PLN in setup fees, supplier-paid onboarding fees, and/or 15-25% first-year savings share; keep accountant rejection below 10%; keep disputes/invalid reductions at zero.

First acquisition mechanism: Build a target list of 150 Polish employers with 50-500 staff likely to pay PFRON and a parallel list of eligible suppliers in services/production categories that can replace existing spend. Outreach to CFO/payroll/HR: "If you are already paying PFRON and already buying cleaning, printing, packaging, assembly, facility services, call-center/admin support, or similar services, I will route one existing purchase to a verified eligible supplier and get paid only after your accountant recognizes the first valid reduction."

Economics: Charge 2,000-5,000 PLN setup only after a buyer approves a specific purchase route, plus 15-25% of the first 12 months of recognized PFRON reductions, capped per buyer. Optionally charge eligible suppliers onboarding/QA fees only after they receive a purchase order. A buyer saving 10,000-30,000 PLN per month can justify a material success fee.

Copy risk: Accountants, payroll firms, PFRON consultants, eligible suppliers, HR consultancies, and procurement brokers can copy pieces. The defense is control of exact buyer mandates, supplier eligibility evidence, purchase orders, payment calendar, reduction information, accountant-approved reduction memory, and category-specific supplier quality before rivals know which purchases are movable.

Why it is not a service/report/app/dashboard/marketplace/broker: The startup does not sell PFRON advice, a calculator, a supplier directory, a dashboard, or a generic procurement marketplace. It controls a purchase-order and evidence lockbox for a named buyer/supplier transaction and is paid only when the buyer's current cash outflow is validly reduced.

## Internal Risk Caps Applied

- Payroll/tax/legal-adjacent variants without purchase/payment control were capped below 82.
- PFRON advanced only because first proof is cash movement: purchase order, paid invoice, seller reduction information, accountant recognition, and lower current PFRON payment.
- The idea remains capped at 88, not higher, because accountants and eligible suppliers are strong natural competitors.

## Decision

Advance PFRON Offset Purchase-Order Lockbox to real Zero To One validation.
