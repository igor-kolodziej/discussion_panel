Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CineMatched Lens Set Option Bank

One-sentence thesis:
Option coherent, serial-numbered vintage and high-end cinema lens sets from retiring DPs, small rental houses, studios, and estate liquidations, then resell only against buyer deposits or escrow from rental houses, production companies, cinematographers, and dealers that need the exact matched set.

Exact buyer:
Independent rental houses, cinematographers, production companies, film schools, boutique studios, camera dealers, and post-production or commercial production groups that want coherent cinema lens sets rather than single random lenses. The first buyer wants named focal-length coverage, mount compatibility, optical character, known service condition, and a set that can be rented or used immediately after inspection. The source seller is a retiring cinematographer, small rental house, studio, estate, production company, or owner-operator liquidating a coherent set.

Acute trigger:
A buyer needs a matched set for a rental-house inventory gap, production package, film-school kit, commercial job, expansion into a specific look, or replacement of scattered single lenses. A seller wants liquidity, succession, or estate cleanup without handling international listings, buyer inspection calls, escrow, shipping insurance, fraud risk, and negotiation across multiple single-lens buyers.

Control point:
Signed seller option over a named lens set; serial list; focal lengths; mount type; rehousing status; accessories and case list; invoices/provenance where available; service history; optical and mechanical condition notes; test stills or footage; collimation/optical check; fungus, haze, coating, scratch, dust, iris, focus, mount, declick, and witness-mark notes; title and no-lien declaration; expert inspection path; buyer deposit or escrow instruction; and closing terms that prevent founder capital exposure before buyer demand is real.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign two seller options over coherent cinema lens sets worth at least 150,000 PLN each; obtain three buyer deposits or refundable escrow instructions from rental houses, production companies, cinematographers, or dealers for named sets or set types; complete expert inspection or rental-house check for at least one set; close one title transfer or buyer-funded resale; and collect at least 35,000 PLN gross spread, option fee, or success margin. Reject every lot without serials, clear title, coherent focal-length set, inspection path, and buyer deposit before purchase.

6-month POC:
Build a pipeline of 25-50 coherent lens sets across Poland, CEE, Germany, Baltics, and nearby EU markets; sign 8-15 seller options; collect 10-20 buyer deposits or escrow instructions; close 5-10 transactions; generate 200,000-700,000 PLN gross margin before expert, escrow, shipping, insurance, and travel costs; maintain realized gross margin above 15% after all transaction costs; build a private buyer-request and set-condition memory by lens family, mount, rehousing, focal range, service state, inspection issue, buyer type, price band, and no-go reason.

First acquisition mechanism:
Source from retiring DPs, small rental houses, production companies, studio owners, estate liquidators, film schools, equipment finance remarketers, local camera communities, and cine technicians who see coherent sets before they hit public marketplaces. Demand starts with a buyer-request board built from rental houses, DPs, production companies, camera dealers, film schools, and production managers looking for named lens families or focal-length coverage. The first offer is narrow: "I option coherent serial-numbered sets only after inspection path and buyer deposit; I do not buy random single lenses or unsupported gear."

Economics:
Use option-first spread economics. Buyers pay a 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable search/deposit or escrow instruction for named sets or set types, and the startup earns 10-30% gross spread where the seller values speed, privacy, one-buyer simplicity, or avoided international sale friction. No title purchase occurs unless buyer deposit or escrow covers exposure. Inspection, escrow, shipping insurance, and expert review are charged to the transaction or deducted from spread. The 100,000 PLN budget covers options, inspections, travel, escrow setup, insurance, small deposits, and expert contractors rather than speculative inventory.

Copy risk:
Camera dealers, rental houses, DP networks, used-gear marketplaces, auction houses, and cine technicians can copy pieces. The defense is early seller options over exact serial-numbered coherent sets, buyer deposits before purchase, inspection discipline, and a private map of buyer demand by lens family, focal coverage, mount, look, rehousing, and price band.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs an option over a named serial-numbered set and the buyer places a deposit or escrow instruction for that exact set, competitors cannot sell or inspect that same set without displacing the signed parties or waiting for the option to expire. They can source other lens sets, but not the controlled serial-numbered lot, inspection file, buyer deposit, and transaction terms.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not sell gear reviews, listings, a marketplace, an app, a dashboard, consulting, or introductions. It only controls named coherent lens sets through seller options or title and sells against buyer deposits or escrow. No controlled set and no buyer deposit means no transaction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not guarantee artistic performance, rental yield, production suitability, tax treatment, investment value, manufacturer warranty, or future resale. Do not handle stolen gear, unclear estate authority, sanctions/export concerns, fake serials, suspicious provenance, counterfeit rehousings, undisclosed damage, unpaid finance liens, or deals without escrow/clear payment terms. Use cine-lens technicians for optical/mechanical inspection; use insured shipping; use escrow or direct buyer-seller closing; disclose condition limits; and reject any set where title, provenance, inspection, or buyer acceptance is unclear.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from HeritageDoor and other architectural salvage because the controlled lot is compact, serial-numbered cinema optics, not building materials or restoration objects. It is distinct from hotel FF&E, industrial spares, watch parts, POS hardware, and general used equipment because the value is in coherent matched lens sets with known serials, optical condition, mount compatibility, buyer deposits, and immediate rental/production utility. It is not a platform, evidence desk, recovery mandate, compliance pack, or software book.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Existing camera dealers and used-gear networks already know many good sellers. Good lens sets may be efficiently priced and sellers may not need an unknown intermediary. Buyer trust is hard because optical condition, provenance, service history, fungus, haze, collimation, rehousing quality, mount wear, and coating condition matter. Inspection costs and disputes can erase spread. The founder lacks cine-gear reputation unless a credible technician or rental-house partner is attached. Some sets are rare but illiquid; others are liquid but priced at market. Fraud, theft, fake serials, undisclosed liens, international shipping damage, escrow disputes, and return expectations create transaction risk. One set is defensible only for that deal; building a durable company requires repeated privileged supply access and buyer-demand memory.
