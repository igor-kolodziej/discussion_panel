## Strongest Objections Captured

1. **The right seller may be rare.** The founder needs real customer tail, clean IP, low support burden, no unresolved vulnerabilities, customer prepayment, and earnout-heavy seller terms.
2. **Customers may prefer known Atlassian Solution Partners.** Enterprise Data Center customers may already trust migration partners more than an unknown Warsaw operator.
3. **Marketplace and license transfer can be restrictive.** App listing, vendor account, renewals, support obligations, Marketplace terms, DPA, and payee transition may not transfer cleanly.
4. **Security patching can become existential.** Third-party app vulnerabilities may require rapid patching, CVE handling, disclosure, and audit evidence.
5. **Cloud migration can kill revenue quickly.** The tail exists only until customers migrate, export, retire, or replace the app.
6. **Prepayment before close is hard.** Customers may see handoff as a weakness signal and demand clarity on SLA, ownership, support, export, and renewal validity.
7. **The 100,000 PLN budget is thin.** It covers diligence and a small option fee, not an upfront purchase or security incident.
8. **One app is not repeatability.** A single app could be a cash-flow asset, but not a company unless seller sourcing and support economics repeat.

## Useful Narrowing From Evaluator

Best wedge:

**Customer-prepaid continuity acquisition for one small Jira/Confluence Data Center app with low support burden.**

Only accept an app where:

- seller can prove clean IP and build path;
- 5 customers will prepay continuity before close;
- seller sends an endorsed customer notice;
- app has no severe unresolved vulnerability;
- support is narrow and mostly maintenance/export/compatibility;
- no privileged security, secrets, payment, medical, regulated decisioning, or heavy custom services;
- seller payout is mostly earnout from retained cash.
