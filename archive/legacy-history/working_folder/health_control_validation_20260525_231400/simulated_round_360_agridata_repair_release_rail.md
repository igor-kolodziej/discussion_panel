# Simulated Round 360: AgriDiag Data-Release Repair Rail

Date: 2026-05-31 Europe/Warsaw

## Search Frame

Recent recovery, AP, import, platform, and micro-acquisition candidates keep failing because the founder controls only a case file, while a natural incumbent controls the decisive outcome. This round tests a different hard-control pattern: user-authorized connected-machine data under the EU Data Act, routed through trusted independent repair shops, with proof tied to a paid repair ticket, no-go decision, or avoided OEM lock-in.

The confirmed Data Act idea is compressed-air energy savings for independent service firms. This candidate is only eligible for testing if it stays materially different:

- no compressed-air rooms;
- no energy-savings memo;
- no white-certificate or grant readiness;
- no generic Data Act consulting;
- no owner-direct legal dispute with OEMs;
- first proof must be a paid machine-specific repair/diagnostic ticket routed through an independent agricultural mechanic.

Current fact check used for simulation only:

- European Commission materials state that the EU Data Act applies from 12 September 2025.
- Commission Data Act materials explicitly frame connected-product data access as enabling aftermarket services including repair and maintenance.
- The current market substrate is visible: modern tractors, combines, sprayers, balers, telehandlers, and precision-ag equipment generate telematics, fault, controller, implement, service-history, and usage data, while independent workshops often lack clean OEM portal access.

## Internal Simulation

### Why It Clears

- The controlled unit is specific: a named machine, owner authorization, live repair ticket, service/fault-data request, exported file or documented refusal, and repair/no-go outcome.
- It uses a trusted channel: independent agricultural mechanics already have farmer trust and repair context; the founder is the data-request and evidence operator behind the shop, not a cold legal claimant.
- It is not emergency field repair. The first scope excludes harvest-AOG cases where a machine must be fixed within hours. It targets stalled repairs, recurring faults, post-season/pre-season work, service-history gaps, implement/controller conflicts, and high-value repair quotes where a 2-7 day data-access packet is useful.
- It avoids becoming a repair shop. The mechanic diagnoses, repairs, invoices, and owns technical decisions. The startup runs authorization, Data Act request path, export interpretation boundaries, evidence room, source log, and no-go discipline.
- The first proof is commercial: prepaid repair-release cases, mechanic-paid data packets, repair invoices, no-go notes, and repeated shop use.
- Case memory can compound by OEM, model, portal, controller, data field, request wording, response timing, refusal reason, and repair use.

## Duplicate-Risk Analysis

This is adjacent to the confirmed Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, but materially different if kept narrow:

- Confirmed idea: compressed-air service firms use Data Act/OEM/controller data to produce partner-branded energy-savings evidence memos.
- AgriDiag: independent agricultural repair shops use farmer-authorized connected-machine data to move a named repair ticket, quote, service-history question, or no-go decision.
- Different buyer trigger: repair stall and machine downtime, not energy-efficiency sales.
- Different payment event: paid repair/data packet and mechanic invoice progress, not plant savings memo or energy-project lead.
- Different proof artifact: machine-specific repair data room, request trail, exported fault/service/telematics file, mechanic decision log, repair/no-go outcome.

If the validation evaluator treats "Data Act access for machines" as the same company path, this should fail and the branch should be abandoned.

## Principal Objections

1. OEM dealers may own trust, tooling, and service data for serious repairs.
2. Data Act request timing may be too slow for urgent harvest downtime.
3. Some required information may be repair information, software, diagnostic tool access, or service documentation rather than connected-product data.
4. Mechanics may already have aftermarket diagnostic tools or informal dealer relationships.
5. Repair and safety liability must never sit with the startup.
6. Machine data can include personal/location/farm-business-sensitive data.
7. Farmers may not want to authorize an unknown third party even through a mechanic.
8. The market is seasonal; 60-day proof may depend on spring/harvest/post-season timing.
9. OEMs may respond narrowly, delay, or cite trade secrets/security.
10. The business fails if it becomes generic right-to-repair advocacy or legal letters.

## Gate Decision

Advance to working Zero To One validation because the simulated score is strictly above 87 and the candidate changes the control point from energy memo to paid repair-ticket data release.
