## Internal Cap Check

- Concrete 6-month control proof exists: signed borrower mandates, lender deficiency requests, lender-specific certificate format, AR/inventory/cash exports, eligibility schedule, submitted corrected certificate, acceptance, draw, reserve release, narrowed exception, or clean no-go.
- The first proof is cash movement or availability: a borrower gets incremental availability, current draw release, reserve release, or a lender-accepted no-go that prevents wasted CFO work.
- Durability is stronger than one-off recovery: borrowing-base certificates recur monthly or weekly; accepted lender formats and no-go memories can compound by lender/facility type.
- Copy risk is high. Competent CFOs, controllers, lenders, factors, ABL brokers, accountants, ERP consultants, and field examiners can copy pieces. The candidate only survives if it starts where a specific lender has already blocked current availability and the borrower pays for rapid tie-out.
- Legal/accounting/credit boundaries are dangerous. The startup must not opine on collateral eligibility, loan covenants, legal perfection, debtor credit, inventory valuation, true sale, factoring law, or lender discretion. It maps facts to the lender's existing checklist and flags no-go issues.
- This is materially different from failed KSeF, true-up, unapplied-cash, and factor-reserve variants because the payment event is not customer AP follow-up or factor discretion on a paid invoice. It is a recurring collateral-reporting artifact tied to current borrowing availability under a named facility.
