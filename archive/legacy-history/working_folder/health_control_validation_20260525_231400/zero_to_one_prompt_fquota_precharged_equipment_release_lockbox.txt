Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
FQuota Pre-Charged Equipment Release Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
EU/CEE importers of pre-charged HVAC, refrigeration, air-conditioning, and heat-pump equipment will pay for a shipment-specific F-gas quota-authorisation lockbox when a named order, customs declaration, customer delivery, or supplier payment is blocked because accepted F-gas Portal quota authorisation or delegation is missing.

Exact buyer:
The buyer is an EU or CEE importer, distributor, only representative, or equipment trader importing pre-charged refrigeration, air-conditioning, stationary heat-pump, HVAC/R, cold-room, commercial cooling, or similar equipment containing HFCs. The first buyer is not a large global HVAC manufacturer with in-house quota and customs teams; it is a small or mid-sized importer with real equipment already ordered or shipped, a named customs broker or forwarder, a customer delivery deadline, and a shipment/order value large enough that a portal authorisation failure creates storage costs, cancellation risk, supplier-payment friction, or lost margin. Exclude bulk refrigerant trading, metered-dose inhalers, medical oxygen or clinical equipment, consumer one-off imports, illegal grey-market refrigerants, products prohibited by F-gas rules, shipments with false refrigerant data, and cases where the importer lacks authority or refuses compliant portal and customs handling.

Acute trigger:
A shipment, purchase order, customer delivery, customs declaration, or supplier payment is stuck because the importer does not have accepted F-gas Portal quota authorisation or delegation for the HFCs contained in pre-charged equipment; lacks a validated F-gas Portal registration or only-representative path; has mismatched refrigerant charge, CO2e, CN code, EORI, declaration of conformity, or broker data; or needs a quota holder to authorise a specific quantity before the customs broker can proceed with release for free circulation. The case is accepted only when there is a named shipment, container, airway bill, customs broker, PO, product/refrigerant table, customer delivery consequence, and importer officer who can approve lawful handling.

Control point:
The startup controls a shipment-specific authorisation case file after the importer signs a paid mandate and submits the shipment/order record, product model list, refrigerant type and charge data, CO2e calculation, customs broker details, EORI/entity documents, F-gas Portal account status, declaration-of-conformity evidence where applicable, supplier invoices, customer delivery deadline, and written authority for the startup to coordinate the authorisation workflow. The controlled object is the named import case: importer mandate, quota-holder offer or refusal, accepted F-gas Portal authorisation or delegation where lawful, broker acknowledgement, correction/no-go trail, fee right, and customs-release chronology. The startup does not own refrigerant, equipment, customs declaration authority, or legal interpretation.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign 2 importer mandates tied to named shipments or POs worth at least 250,000 PLN each and expected HFC quota need above a minimum agreed case size. Secure at least 1 written quota-holder offer, authorisation, delegation, or refusal for a specified CO2e quantity and importer. For at least 1 case, obtain accepted F-gas Portal authorisation/delegation, customs-broker acknowledgement that the F-gas quota path is cleared, shipment release movement, or a written clean no-go explaining why the shipment cannot lawfully proceed. Collect at least 30,000 PLN in fixed, success, or spread-linked fees, with no fee based on illegal release or false documents.

6-month POC:
Build a list of 150-250 EU/CEE importers and distributors of pre-charged HVAC/R, refrigeration, cooling, and heat-pump equipment, plus 30 customs brokers, F-gas advisers, and quota holders who see stuck or time-sensitive imports. Close 8-15 importer mandates, secure 3-6 quota-holder or delegated-authorisation relationships, complete 6-10 accepted authorisation/delegation or clean no-go files, produce 4 or more shipment-release or broker-accepted outcomes, collect 180,000-500,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after contractor, reviewer, and legal/customs boundary costs, and build a private library of authorisation quantities, accepted portal paths, broker evidence requirements, no-go reasons, quota-holder terms, timing, and document defects.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start from visible importer directories, HVAC/R distributors, heat-pump importers, cold-room equipment suppliers, customs brokers, logistics firms handling HVAC/R imports, F-gas advisers, and EU only-representative providers. The first outreach is not generic compliance consulting: "If a pre-charged HVAC/R or heat-pump shipment is stuck because F-gas Portal quota authorisation, delegation, registration, or customs evidence is missing, I assemble the lawful shipment-specific authorisation path or produce a clean no-go. I do not trade refrigerant or touch goods." In parallel, approach quota holders and authorised importers with small unused authorisable quantities and offer a documented, case-by-case importer authorisation workflow with KYC, shipment evidence, no prohibited goods, no false quantities, and payment only after portal acceptance or explicit refusal.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-10,000 PLN for paid case screening only when the shipment is unclear, and 15,000-40,000 PLN for a shipment-specific release file that reaches accepted portal authorisation/delegation, broker-acknowledged clearance of the quota path, release movement, or clean no-go. For larger shipments, charge a success fee or transaction spread agreed upfront and compliant with F-gas rules, quota-holder terms, and customs/legal boundaries. Reject shipments below a minimum value or where storage/delay economics cannot support the fee. Costs are document intake, refrigerant/CO2e calculation review, quota-holder coordination, customs-broker coordination, secure evidence handling, and legal/customs boundary review. The startup must not finance inventory, buy equipment, take refrigerant title, operate as a customs broker, or advance cash.

Copy risk:
Customs brokers, F-gas consultants, HVAC distributors, quota holders, legal advisers, only-representative firms, and large equipment importers can copy the workflow. The defense is narrow transaction control: signed importer mandates, shipment-level evidence, quota-holder relationships, accepted F-gas Portal authorisation/delegation trails, broker-response memory, and a private no-go library. A broad consultant can copy the checklist, but cannot displace a live named shipment file after the importer, quota holder, and broker path are already controlled.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The controlled object is a named import case and authorisation path, not general F-gas knowledge. Once the importer signs the mandate, the shipment file is assembled, the quota holder is matched to a specified CO2e quantity, and the F-gas Portal authorisation/delegation or refusal path is in motion, another party would need to restart importer authority, KYC, refrigerant data validation, quota-holder consent, broker coordination, and fee terms for the same shipment. Incumbents can handle future shipments, but not the controlled case without replacing the consent and portal chronology.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It is not a dashboard, quota marketplace, generic F-gas readiness report, customs brokerage, equipment brokerage, refrigerant trading, or compliance memo. It only accepts named shipment cases and produces one of four operational outcomes: accepted F-gas Portal authorisation/delegation, customs-broker acknowledgement that the quota path is cleared, shipment-release movement, or a written clean no-go. The startup gets paid for controlled transactional release files, not introductions or advice.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not import, export, transport, store, buy, sell, refill, test, certify, or handle refrigerant or equipment. It does not act as customs broker, legal adviser, quota owner, refrigerant trader, conformity-assessment body, auditor, or only representative unless a properly qualified provider holds that role. The importer, quota holder, only representative, customs broker, freight forwarder, supplier, and legal/customs advisers remain responsible for their statutory roles. Reject any case involving false refrigerant quantities, forged documents, prohibited equipment, illegal grey-market HFCs, sanctions risk, missing importer authority, medical/clinical equipment, active customs enforcement, bulk refrigerant trading, attempts to bypass F-gas Portal rules, or pressure to misdeclare the customs entry.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack, REDBlocked Amazon Connected-Device Evidence Rescue Desk, BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack, HeritageDoor Suites, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, KSC Supplier-Renewal Release Pack, EUDR Import Release, Organic COI Import Release, or generic customs document repair. The buyer trigger, control object, payment event, and proof artifact are different: accepted F-gas Portal quota authorisation or delegation for a named pre-charged equipment shipment, tied to customs/broker release movement.

Strongest anticipated objections:
1. Quota holders, large importers, and established HVAC/R distributors may not work with an unknown solo founder.
2. Customs brokers and F-gas consultants already sit close to the importer, broker data, and release decision.
3. The legal boundary is tight: illegal quota trading, customs brokerage, legal advice, false declarations, or prohibited equipment would kill the model.
4. Many cases will be impossible because the importer lacks valid registration, only-representative authority, accurate refrigerant data, declaration evidence, or lawful product status.
5. Accepted F-gas Portal authorisation may still not guarantee customs release if CN codes, EORI, invoices, conformity evidence, product restrictions, audits, or broader customs issues fail.
6. Demand may be lumpy and concentrated among a narrow set of importers that either already solved the quota path or are too risky to serve.
7. Quota-holder relationships may require volume, trust, and legal structure before any profitable authorisation flow exists.
8. The business can degrade into generic customs/F-gas paperwork if it accepts cases without named shipments, real quota need, and a portal acceptance path.
