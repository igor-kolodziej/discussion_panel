Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ExpoSlot Booth Transfer Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Option organizer-approved cancellations of sold-out B2B trade-fair booths and sponsorship packages, then transfer the exact slot to a late buyer that pays a deposit before the founder owes the seller.

Exact buyer:
Late B2B exhibitors, distributors, equipment vendors, and regional manufacturers that need presence at a named industry fair after the fair has sold out or category sponsorship has closed.

Acute trigger:
A key industry fair is sold out, the buyer missed the application deadline, a distributor principal demands presence, a competitor secured the visible category position, or a sales launch requires a booth/sponsor slot before the next annual cycle.

Control point:
Organizer-approved option or novation right over one exact booth/sponsorship package from a canceling exhibitor, plus a buyer deposit or escrow instruction for that exact slot before seller payout.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Two signed seller options over named booths or sponsorship packages; written organizer confirmation that each transfer or novation is allowed and the required documents and fees are known; two buyer deposits or escrow instructions over the exact booth/package; one completed organizer-approved transfer or a clean no-go where organizer refusal is documented before capital loss.

6-month POC:
Control 8-15 transfer files, complete 5-8 transfers, move 150,000-450,000 PLN of gross booth/sponsor package value, collect 50,000-150,000 PLN net fee/spread revenue, and make zero speculative booth purchases without organizer transfer approval and buyer deposit.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build lists from exhibitor catalogs and sponsor pages for sold-out or high-demand CEE/EU industry fairs. Contact exhibitors that downsized, changed distributor, lost budget, changed product launch timing, or may cancel. Separately contact distributors/manufacturers that missed the deadline, are on waitlists, or visibly sponsor competing fairs but not the target fair.

Economics:
Charge 8-15% of booth/sponsor package value or a fixed 5,000-25,000 PLN transfer fee depending on ticket size. Founder costs are outreach, organizer coordination, contract review, and escrow/payment handling. Cash conversion is fast if the buyer deposits before seller payout and the organizer confirms the transfer path.

Copy risk:
Organizers, exhibition sales agencies, stand builders, PR agencies, and industry associations can copy. The defense is exact slot control before the buyer sees it, a private map of cancel-risk exhibitors, organizer transfer rules by fair, and repeated late-buyer demand patterns by category.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The founder only takes a case after controlling a named booth or sponsor-package option with a documented organizer transfer path. A rival can generally broker future demand, but it cannot copy a specific seller option, buyer deposit, organizer approval trail, and transfer deadline once those are already signed for that exact slot.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell a list of fairs, a dashboard, or generic introductions. Each accepted case starts with a specific transferable booth/sponsorship right, written organizer path, and buyer deposit over that exact slot. Without control of the exact slot and deposit, there is no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No consumer ticket scalping, pass resale, or unauthorized transfer. No speculative purchases before organizer transfer permission and buyer deposit. Organizer rules, exhibitor contract terms, VAT/invoicing, insurance, stand build, shipping, booth staffing, marketing claims, and product compliance remain with the buyer/seller and their advisors. Reject non-transferable, lottery/allocation, personal-access, regulated medical/pharma, weapons/defense-sensitive, political, adult, gambling, sanctions-sensitive, or misrepresented exhibitor categories. Use lawyer-reviewed option/novation templates only after early validation, not as legal advice to parties.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not HeritageDoor or another physical salvage lot because no bulky asset is moved. Not PromoLeak because it does not audit retailer trade spend or promo deductions. Not an evidence desk, platform case rescue, DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, or TraceFaktura. The core proof artifact is organizer-approved transfer of a scarce booth/sponsor slot plus buyer deposit.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Organizers may forbid third-party transfers, route cancellations through waitlists, or reclaim valuable slots. The market may be too episodic because each fair has different rules, timing, categories, and economics. Late buyers may not deposit until organizer approval is final, and sellers may bypass the founder after buyer demand is surfaced. Exhibition agencies, stand builders, and organizers are closer to the market. Some packages may be low-value or include non-transferable sponsorship benefits, passes, speaking rights, digital listings, or category approvals. The business can become event brokerage if the founder accepts soft introductions instead of exact controlled transfer rights.
