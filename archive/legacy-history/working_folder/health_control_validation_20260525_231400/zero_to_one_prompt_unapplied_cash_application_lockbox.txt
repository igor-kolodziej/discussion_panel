Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
UnappliedCash Application Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
For B2B SaaS, API, data, infrastructure, and usage-priced vendors, convert customer cash that has already arrived in the vendor's bank or lockbox but is stuck in unapplied receipts, suspense, customer advances, wrong legal entity, wrong invoice reference, duplicate customer master, missing remittance, or portal mismatch into controller-approved applied cash on the correct invoice/account.

Exact buyer:
20-500 employee B2B SaaS/API/data/infrastructure-software vendors, usage-priced vendors, and PE-backed multi-entity B2B groups with enterprise wires, ACH, SEPA, SWIFT, or lockbox receipts. The buyer is the CFO, controller, billing owner, RevOps lead, or PE operating partner who can see cash in bank/lockbox reports but cannot close the matching and application loop quickly enough. Best first buyers have named unapplied or suspense cash above 250,000 PLN equivalent, enterprise customers, messy remittance data, recent billing migration, multiple legal entities, duplicate customer masters, or a customer account/renewal/license still showing unpaid despite actual receipt of cash.

Acute trigger:
The customer has already paid, but the vendor still shows the invoice, renewal, usage statement, customer account, implementation milestone, support entitlement, revenue schedule, or collections status as open because the receipt cannot be confidently matched and applied. The pain is urgent when a strategic customer's account is on hold, renewal remains blocked, revenue recognition or month-end close is delayed, customer success is escalating a "we already paid" dispute, audit support is weak, or cash is trapped in unidentified receipts while finance ops are overloaded.

Control point:
The startup accepts only named cash receipts that already exist in the vendor's bank, lockbox, PSP payout, or treasury report. Control requires a signed cash-application mandate for a named batch; bank/lockbox receipt evidence; AR aging or ERP extract; invoice/order/usage statement; customer master and legal-entity mapping; remittance advice, SWIFT/SEPA/ACH reference, AP ticket, buyer portal proof, or customer email where available; proposed application map; controller approval; before/after ERP or AR status; and fee right tied to applied cash, released account status, or clean no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three vendors sign mandates over named unapplied/suspense cash batches. At least 1,000,000 PLN equivalent of already-received cash is under review. At least 500,000 PLN is moved from unapplied, suspense, wrong-account, customer-advance, or unidentified-receipt status into controller-approved invoice/account application or documented no-go. At least one customer account, renewal, support hold, implementation milestone, or revenue-close blocker is released because the payment is applied. The startup collects or invoices at least 25,000 PLN in fixed/application fees. No accepted case depends on collecting unpaid receivables, pressuring customers, legal interpretation, debt collection, factoring, payment intermediation, tax advice, accounting-policy opinion, or holding client funds.

6-month POC:
Close 12-25 vendor mandates or portfolio-company batches; screen 8-25 million PLN equivalent of already-received unapplied/suspense cash; apply or no-go 4-12 million PLN equivalent; document 30+ account, renewal, support, close, audit, or customer-dispute release outcomes; collect 200,000-650,000 PLN in fixed and applied-cash fees; secure at least four repeat channels from billing-migration firms, NetSuite/ERP consultants, PE operating partners, fractional CFOs, billing-platform implementers, or RevOps consultants; keep gross margin above 65%; and maintain a strict rejection log for ordinary AR chasing, unresolved disputes, unclear bank proof, weak customer authority, or accounting-policy decisions.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with the partners who discover cash-application messes but do not want manual case labor: billing-migration firms, NetSuite and ERP consultants, Stripe/Chargebee/Maxio/Zuora implementers, fractional SaaS CFOs, PE operating partners, RevOps consultants, and outsourced finance teams. Direct outreach is narrow: "Do you have customer cash already in the bank but still sitting in unapplied receipts, suspense, wrong customer, wrong entity, or missing-remittance status? I only take named receipts where cash has already arrived and the result is applied cash, account release, or clean no-go."

Economics:
Charge 5,000-20,000 PLN fixed setup for a named high-value receipt batch, plus 1-3% of applied cash where before/after attribution is clear and the vendor accepts it. Use fixed fees as the core model to avoid disputes over whether already-received cash would have been applied eventually. The vendor pays because applying 500,000 PLN of cash can unblock revenue recognition, customer status, month-end close, renewal access, support entitlement, and customer relationship friction. Capital needs are low: secure evidence room, data cleanup, remittance reconstruction, ERP/cash-application contractor time, and controller review.

Copy risk:
In-house AR and cash-application teams, outsourced finance BPOs, BlackLine/HighRadius-style cash-application products, NetSuite/SAP/ERP consultants, billing-platform consultants, accountants, RevOps teams, and PE operating teams can all solve pieces of this. The branch is copyable if it becomes generic reconciliation, month-end close support, or AR outsourcing.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup does not claim a broad moat over cash application. It controls only the named receipt batch after the vendor signs the mandate and shares bank/lockbox evidence, ERP before-state, invoice/order facts, remittance trail, customer/AP proof, proposed application map, and fee terms. A BPO or ERP consultant can copy future work, but it cannot instantly own the current evidence room, receipt ID list, remittance reconstruction, controller approval path, before/after application trail, or account-release attribution without the vendor switching authority mid-case.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell a cash dashboard, AR analytics report, billing audit, generic finance-ops consulting, software implementation, or brokered referral. It accepts only named cash that already arrived and produces a specific operational outcome: applied cash, corrected customer/account status, released renewal/support/milestone blocker, documented offset, or clean no-go approved by the controller.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not hold client funds, move money, provide payment services, lend, factor, collect debts, contact customers without vendor approval, decide accounting policy, provide audit opinion, provide tax/VAT advice, alter commercial facts, or override controller authority. Vendor CFOs/controllers/accountants remain responsible for accounting treatment, revenue recognition, legal-entity decisions, tax treatment, write-offs, offsets, and customer communications. Customers, banks, PSPs, ERP owners, auditors, and legal/accounting advisers remain responsible for their own systems and decisions. Reject cases involving unpaid receivables, commercial disputes, customer insolvency, clawback/recall risk, fraud allegations, sanctions concerns, disputed delivery, service-credit claims, refunds owed to customers, or requests to misapply cash.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak because it is not retailer deduction recovery, trade-promotion leakage, retail-media charges, or FMCG remittance recovery. It is distinct from Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL, courier COD, retailer OTIF, credit-memo cash-out, cloud marketplace remittance, PartnerCenter incentive, and customer-acknowledged true-up recovery because the cash has already arrived in the vendor's bank or lockbox before acceptance; the work is application of received cash, not recovery from an external debtor or platform. It is distinct from DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, RAGProbe, and health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Competent CFOs, controllers, AR teams, billing teams, and outsourced finance providers should already handle cash application. The highest-value cases may be symptoms of broader ERP, billing, customer-master, legal-entity, contract, or accounting-policy problems. Sensitive bank, ERP, invoice, customer, and remittance data creates trust friction for an unknown solo founder. ERP and cash-application consultants can copy the workflow. Automation tools may solve many common remittance matching cases. Success fees can be disputed because the cash was already in the bank, so fixed fees must dominate. Some cases may hide real commercial disputes, refunds owed, wrong payments, fraud risk, or customer overpayment rather than application backlog. The business can become low-margin finance-ops cleanup unless intake stays limited to high-value named cash with clear release consequences.
