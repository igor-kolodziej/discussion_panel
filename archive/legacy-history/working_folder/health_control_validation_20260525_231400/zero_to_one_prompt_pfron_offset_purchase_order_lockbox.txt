Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections. Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time. Idea name: PFRON Offset Purchase-Order Lockbox. One-sentence thesis: Route existing recurring B2B purchases of Polish employers that pay mandatory PFRON contributions to verified eligible suppliers, but only get paid after a real purchase order, timely paid invoice, seller reduction information, and recognized current-month PFRON cash reduction. Exact buyer: Polish employers with at least 25 FTE that pay meaningful monthly PFRON contributions because they do not reach the disabled-employment threshold. Best first buyers have 50-500 employees, recurring purchases that can be switched without operational risk, CFO/payroll pain, and current monthly PFRON payments above 10,000 PLN. The other side is eligible Polish suppliers of production or services that can lawfully issue reduction information for valid buyer purchases. Acute trigger: The employer is paying PFRON monthly while also buying services or production that could be sourced from eligible suppliers. The 2026 rules make buyer requests, seller eligibility, invoice timing, payment timing, and reduction information operationally important. Cash savings show in the next payroll/accounting cycle if the purchase is valid, paid on time, and accepted by the buyer's accountant or payroll owner. Control point: Signed buyer purchase-routing mandate; verified eligible supplier list; supplier written confirmation of right to issue reduction information for the relevant production/service; buyer request to supplier; purchase order; invoice; payment proof; seller reduction information; payroll/accounting signoff; and first monthly PFRON payment reduction recognized by the buyer's accountant/payroll owner. The startup controls the transaction checklist, supplier evidence room, buyer purchase calendar, reduction-info follow-up, and accountant-approved outcome log for each routed purchase. 60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof: Three buyers sign purchase-routing mandates; five suppliers pass eligibility and documentation checks; at least 150,000 PLN of existing monthly or quarterly purchases are rerouted from ordinary suppliers to eligible suppliers without operational disruption; invoices are paid on time; seller reduction information is issued; at least 30,000 PLN of current PFRON contribution reduction is recognized by buyer accountants or payroll owners; one buyer signs a recurring monthly retainer or savings-share after first reduction. 6-month POC: Control 20 buyers and 30 eligible suppliers; route 1,500,000-3,000,000 PLN of purchases; document 300,000-800,000 PLN of recognized PFRON contribution reductions; collect 60,000-200,000 PLN in setup fees, supplier-paid onboarding fees, and/or 15-25% first-year savings share; keep accountant rejection below 10%; keep disputes, invalid reductions, and late-payment failures at zero; show at least five repeat monthly purchase routes. First acquisition mechanism: Build a target list of 150 Polish employers with 50-500 staff likely to pay PFRON and a parallel list of eligible suppliers in services or production categories that can replace existing spend. Outreach to CFO/payroll/HR: "If you are already paying PFRON and already buying cleaning, printing, packaging, assembly, facility services, call-center/admin support, or similar services, I will route one existing purchase to a verified eligible supplier and get paid only after your accountant recognizes the first valid reduction." Economics: Charge 2,000-5,000 PLN setup only after a buyer approves a specific purchase route, plus 15-25% of the first 12 months of recognized PFRON reductions, capped per buyer. Optionally charge eligible suppliers onboarding or QA fees only after they receive a purchase order. A buyer saving 10,000-30,000 PLN per month can justify a material success fee; a supplier wins recurring purchase orders. Copy risk: Accountants, payroll firms, PFRON consultants, eligible suppliers, HR consultancies, and procurement brokers can copy pieces. The defense is control of exact buyer mandates, supplier eligibility evidence, purchase orders, payment calendar, reduction information, accountant-approved reduction memory, category-specific supplier quality, and repeat monthly purchase routing before rivals know which buyer purchases are movable. Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: Once the buyer signs a mandate for a named purchase category, the supplier is verified, the purchase order is issued, the invoice is paid, and reduction information is routed into the buyer's payroll/accounting process, the startup controls that exact buyer-supplier payment stream and evidence trail for the current month. A payroll firm or supplier can copy the general idea later, but cannot instantly own the specific purchase order, payment calendar, supplier confirmation, buyer request, and accountant-approved reduction already under the startup's lockbox. Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: The startup does not sell PFRON advice, a calculator, a supplier directory, a dashboard, or generic procurement introductions. It controls a purchase-order and evidence lockbox for a named buyer/supplier transaction and is paid only when the buyer's current cash outflow is validly reduced. The artifact is not a report; it is a paid invoice, seller reduction information, accountant recognition, and lower PFRON cash payment. Boundaries and legal/provider limits: The startup does not file PFRON declarations, provide tax/legal/payroll advice, certify seller eligibility, employ disabled workers for the buyer, guarantee statutory treatment, or represent the buyer before PFRON. Buyer accountants, payroll providers, lawyers, and internal HR/CFO owners remain responsible for declarations, legal interpretation, employment calculations, accounting, payment timing, and final use of reduction information. Suppliers remain responsible for their own eligibility, employment status, production/service facts, invoices, and reduction information. Cases are declined if the supplier cannot provide written eligibility evidence or if the buyer's accountant will not approve the route before purchase. Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas: This is not PromoLeak because it does not recover retailer deductions, audit promotions, reconcile retail-media fees, or dispute trade terms. It is not TraceFaktura because it does not create municipal event evidence. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, Amazon recovery, KSeF payment release, or any health/Rx/clinical route. Strongest anticipated objections: Accountants and payroll firms may already manage PFRON reductions. Eligible suppliers may already market directly to PFRON-paying buyers. Buyers may lack enough switchable spend in eligible categories. Supplier quality, reliability, and pricing may be worse than incumbent suppliers. Accountants may reject the route. Success fees may be challenged if buyers see the saving as a statutory entitlement. Supplier eligibility can change. The market may become a procurement-broker service unless repeat purchase-order control and accountant-approved reduction memory compound.
