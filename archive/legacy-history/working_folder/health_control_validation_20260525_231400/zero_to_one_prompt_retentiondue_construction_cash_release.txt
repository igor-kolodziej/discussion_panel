Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RetentionDue Construction Cash Release.

One-sentence thesis:
Small construction subcontractors will pay a success fee to recover matured, undisputed retention cash and guarantee collateral after warranty or defect-liability periods have expired, using a signed limited mandate over named claims, a complete contract proof file, debtor acknowledgement, and payment-linked release workflow.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE SME construction subcontractors, specialist installers, facade firms, HVAC/electrical/fire-safety contractors, fit-out firms, and materials vendors that have 50,000-2,000,000 PLN of retention money, caution money, guarantee collateral, or bid/performance security still withheld after completed projects. The economic buyer is the owner, CFO, finance manager, or project-commercial manager who needs old cash released without turning every case into litigation.

Acute trigger:
A project has been accepted and the relevant defect-liability, warranty, or contractual retention period has expired, but the general contractor, owner, public buyer, landlord, or beneficiary has not released retained cash, collateral, bid security, or guarantee security. The subcontractor sees aged receivables, cash pressure, bank-line pressure, or a coming tax/payroll/supplier payment and wants money released from old jobs instead of taking new debt.

Control point:
The startup accepts only named, matured, documented, non-litigated claims where the contractor remains the contract party and signs a limited recovery mandate or assignment for the specific retention/security release. The controlled asset is the retention-release file: subcontract or purchase order, retention clause, invoices and deduction ledger, acceptance/handover protocol, warranty or defect period calculation, defect-clearance evidence, bank/insurance guarantee details where relevant, debtor/beneficiary contact route, formal release request, debtor acknowledgement, payment schedule, and success-fee invoice tied to recovered cash or released collateral.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign at least three contractors with at least five matured retention/security claims totaling 750,000 PLN face value. Each accepted claim must have a signed limited mandate, contract file, retained amount calculation, acceptance or handover proof, warranty/defect period expiry proof, and written release request. At least two claims must receive debtor acknowledgement, payment date, partial payment, released guarantee/collateral confirmation, or written objection that cleanly routes the case out to counsel. The POC succeeds only if at least one cash payment, collateral release, or guarantee release creates a success-fee invoice.

6-month POC:
Process 25-50 accepted claims from 12-25 contractors, with 5-10 million PLN face value reviewed and at least 1 million PLN of cash, collateral, or guarantee exposure released or scheduled for release. Maintain a strict rejection list for disputed defects, insolvency, litigation, public-law procurement disputes, tax questions, and claims without documents. Build three repeat channels: construction accountants/bookkeepers, bank-guarantee or insurance-guarantee brokers, and factoring brokers who see old retention receivables but do not want to run the release workflow.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with accountants and bookkeepers serving subcontractors, guarantee brokers who arrange bid/performance/warranty bonds, factoring brokers who see ineligible old retention receivables, and construction finance consultants. The first outreach asks for matured retention or security older than the contractual release date, not general unpaid invoices. The first paid offer is a retention-release sprint: 1,500-4,000 PLN intake fee per contractor or claim bundle plus 8-15% of recovered cash or 2-5% of released guarantee/collateral exposure, payable only from successful release.

Economics:
The founder does not finance claims, buy invoices, litigate, or take balance-sheet risk. A 300,000 PLN matured retention release at a 10% success fee produces 30,000 PLN revenue; even a 100,000 PLN claim can support a 8,000-15,000 PLN fee if documents are clean. Contractor/admin review, document reconstruction, release-letter preparation, and follow-up can be handled with project-commercial contractors and counsel only for boundary review. Gross margin can exceed 60% if the acceptance filter rejects disputed claims and the founder gets paid only from released cash or collateral.

Copy risk:
Construction lawyers, debt collectors, accountants, project managers, factoring brokers, and guarantee brokers can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is not software or legal novelty; it is controlling a specific matured cash claim through a signed limited mandate, complete proof file, debtor acknowledgement, and payment direction before anyone else acts. Repeat channel trust with accountants, guarantee brokers, and factoring brokers compounds because they prefer sending narrow matured-retention cases instead of running low-status collection admin themselves.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder holds the contractor's signed mandate or assignment for a named matured retention/security claim, the contract and handover file, the retained amount calculation, the release request, debtor acknowledgement, and success-fee payment instruction, a competitor cannot recover that same cash stream before the founder's current case closes. Incumbents can pursue future claims, but not the exact retained amount already under mandate and acknowledged in the release workflow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell receivables advice, a dashboard, litigation leads, a marketplace, generic collections, or a report about old receivables. It accepts only specific matured retention/security claims and produces a cash-linked control artifact: signed mandate, proof file, release request, debtor acknowledgement, payment or collateral release evidence, and success-fee invoice from the released amount.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Only matured, documented, non-litigated, non-insolvent, non-consumer, B2B construction retention/security claims are accepted. The startup does not provide legal advice, represent clients in court, threaten litigation, buy claims, finance claims, handle tax advice, practice as a lawyer, act as a regulated debt collector where a license is required, or pursue disputed defects. Cases with disputed workmanship, insolvency, public-procurement legal disputes, mechanic-lien issues, set-off disputes, fraud allegations, or unclear contract authority are rejected or referred to licensed counsel under a separate engagement. The contractor remains the contract party unless a legally reviewed assignment is used.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak and retailer deduction recovery because the payment stream is construction retention/security money after a project acceptance and warranty/defect period, not retailer promo leakage or operational deductions. It is distinct from TraceFaktura because the buyer is a subcontractor with matured private-contract cash withheld, not a municipal invoice evidence rail. It is distinct from DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, Supplement Stack, and health/Rx/clinical ideas.

Strongest anticipated objections:
General contractors and owners may claim defects, missing documents, set-offs, warranty extensions, or relationship concerns to delay payment. Many contractors already use lawyers, accountants, or project managers for large retentions. Smaller contractors may have weak contract files and poor handover evidence. Some claims will be too disputed or legally sensitive for a non-lawyer founder. Recovery cycles may exceed 60 days even when the claim is valid. A success fee may feel expensive if the contractor believes the money is already owed. Debtors may ignore release requests until counsel is involved. Channels may not repeat unless accountants, guarantee brokers, and factoring brokers see enough clean matured claims.
