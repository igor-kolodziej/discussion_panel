## Zero To One Summary

The evaluator viewed TowFaktura as a credible event-to-invoice rail but not a clear high-margin domination business. The strongest version is a current payment-batch release product for towing and impound contractors with unpaid or corrected public invoice batches, not towing software, legal recovery, or generic admin help.

The evaluator acknowledged that vehicle removal and guarded-storage payments depend on event-level evidence such as official removal orders, timestamps, photos, GPS, storage days, owner release, tariff line mapping, and abandonment status. The idea was considered testable, locally specific, and plausible when one batch can unlock or protect meaningful invoice value.

## Strongest Objections

1. Contractors may already have good-enough records through dispatch logs, photos, handwritten protocols, municipal templates, and invoices.
2. Public payers may not withhold enough current cash to justify 3,000-7,000 PLN small-batch fees.
3. Missing evidence cannot be recreated ethically after the event.
4. Legal disputes over unlawful towing, damage claims, ownership, notice, abandonment, scrap status, or title can contaminate the packet workflow.
5. Procedures vary by gmina, powiat, police path, municipal guard, road authority, and payer.
6. Towing contractors may be low-trust, low-admin buyers who resist prepaid fees or expect success-based recovery.
7. The workflow can become low-margin document chasing if every event needs calls, exports, searches, or payer follow-up.
8. Success fees may be awkward on public or quasi-public reimbursement flows.
9. Dispatch, GPS, impound, and towing software vendors could add evidence-packet workflows.
10. It is structurally close to TraceFaktura as another municipal event-to-invoice rail.

## Evaluator's Best Beachhead

Towing and impound contractors with active public contracts and currently corrected or unpaid batches above 100,000 PLN.

Best first cases:

- active municipal, police, or public contract;
- already performed events;
- current unpaid, rejected, or corrected invoices;
- clear tariff schedule;
- accessible removal orders, photos, GPS, and storage ledger;
- payer written correction notes;
- contractor likely to submit a second monthly batch.

Avoid:

- one-off private towing;
- active vehicle-damage disputes;
- owner litigation;
- unclear towing authority;
- missing core event records;
- scrap, disposal, or legal-title questions;
- contractors unwilling to prepay.

## POC Pass/Fail Suggested By Evaluator

Continue only if within 60-90 days:

- 3 contractors prepay;
- 100,000+ PLN unpaid or at-risk invoices are reviewed;
- 5+ invoices are accepted, corrected, paid, escalated cleanly, or no-go'd;
- one contractor submits a second batch;
- gross margin stays above 60%;
- average packet time is low enough to scale.

## Lesson

The municipal event-to-invoice pattern remains testable but is not scoring near the real gate unless payment friction is more acute and less dependent on post-event cleanup. This also reinforces the need to avoid documentation rails unless current cash is visibly blocked and buyers already experience material payment rejection.
