Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

CPSC eFile Entry Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:

Build a broker-routed certificate-data desk for EU/CEE consumer-product brands, Amazon sellers, 3PLs, and importers whose US shipments or FBA/retail inventory plans are blocked by the July 2026 CPSC certificate eFiling requirement for imported consumer products.

Exact buyer:

The first buyer is a customs broker, cross-border 3PL, Amazon/marketplace agency, or US importer of record serving EU/CEE consumer-product sellers that import products subject to CPSC certificate requirements. The economic buyer is the brand, seller, distributor, or importer with a named shipment, SKU family, PO, FBA inbound plan, or retail delivery window that cannot move cleanly because certificate data, CPC/GCC fields, lab reports, manufacturer/importer details, rule references, Product Registry records, or eFiling artifacts are missing or disorganized. First cases exclude food, cosmetics, medical devices, drugs, firearms, regulated chemicals, vehicles, and products with unresolved classification or safety disputes.

Acute trigger:

A broker, 3PL, importer, retailer, Amazon agency, or compliance team tells the seller that a named imported shipment or SKU family needs CPSC certificate data electronically filed or made ready for entry. The urgent trigger is not abstract product safety compliance. It is a dated shipment, FBA replenishment, customer PO, retailer delivery window, launch inventory, or import entry that may be delayed, rejected, held, rerouted, or forced into expensive rework if the certificate/eFiling packet is not assembled before entry.

Control point:

A signed importer, seller, broker, 3PL, or agency mandate over a named shipment or SKU batch; broker-supplied HTS/entry context; SKU/model/product identifiers; product photos and label images; manufacturer, importer, private-label, and factory information; applicable CPSC rule references supplied by broker, lab, or qualified product-safety reviewer; CPC/GCC certificate fields; existing test reports, lab accreditation evidence, batch/lot/date/manufacture details where required; Product Registry or eFiling data trail where used; broker ABI/PGA/eFiling requirements; source-document inconsistency log; draft certificate-data packet; broker questions; and a submit/cannot-submit/escalate decision. The startup controls the current certificate-data packet, broker-facing case file, and fee right for the named entry. It does not control CBP, CPSC, HTS classification, rule applicability, product safety, lab validity, or importer-of-record liability.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Within 60 days, sign two customs brokers, cross-border 3PLs, or Amazon/marketplace agencies on referral or resale terms; close 10-15 paid SKU/shipment certificate-data cases under written seller/importer authorization; collect at least 40,000 PLN in triage, batch, or shipment sprint fees; produce at least three concrete outcomes such as broker acknowledgement of a complete packet, Product Registry record created by the responsible party, eFiling data accepted by the broker path, entry/FBA/retailer progress, or paid clean no-go escalation; and reject every case that lacks broker-supplied entry context, existing source truth, product identifiers, and a cash-linked shipment/order consequence.

6-month POC:

Process 60-120 paid SKU/shipment packets across 5-8 broker, 3PL, or agency channels; collect 250,000-650,000 PLN in triage, batch, and urgent shipment sprint fees; document 40+ broker acknowledgements, eFiling progress outcomes, entry/FBA/retailer progress outcomes, or clean no-go decisions; build a private request taxonomy by product class, certificate type, missing field, lab-report gap, rule-reference issue, label mismatch, Product Registry path, broker requirement, source-country pattern, accepted packet format, and no-go cause; keep gross margin above 60%; keep evidence-ready case turnaround under five business days; and continue only if at least two channels each send eight or more paid cases.

First acquisition mechanism:

Start with customs brokers, cross-border 3PLs, Amazon agencies, marketplace-compliance agencies, and freight forwarders handling EU/CEE sellers shipping consumer products into the US. The pitch is: "When your EU seller has a shipment or FBA plan blocked by CPSC certificate/eFiling data, send us the SKU file and broker request. We assemble the certificate-data packet, identify missing source truth, flag no-go issues, and hand back a broker-ready file. You keep the brokerage/import relationship." Direct seller outreach is secondary and only targets brands with named US shipments already scheduled.

Economics:

Charge 2,000-4,000 PLN for triage and a submit/cannot-submit/escalate decision, 4,000-10,000 PLN per SKU family or certificate packet, and 15,000-45,000 PLN for urgent multi-SKU shipment sprints. Brokers, 3PLs, or agencies keep a 20-35% referral or resale margin. The seller pays because one delayed FBA replenishment, retail PO, customs entry, or launch shipment can cost more than the packet fee. Founder capital is spent on secure document handling, admin contractors, broker workflow mapping, and qualified product-safety review for edge cases, not inventory, advances, or speculative software.

Copy risk:

Customs brokers, product-safety labs, CPSC consultants, testing companies, freight forwarders, Amazon agencies, and compliance platforms can copy pieces. The defense is not CPSC expertise in the abstract. It is broker-routed live case flow, SKU/entry request memory, Product Registry/eFiling artifact patterns, CEE seller/factory document reconstruction, accepted packet templates by broker/product type, and strict no-go rules before brokers and labs productize a fast middle layer for small EU/CEE sellers.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

Once the broker, agency, importer, or seller signs a paid mandate for a named SKU/shipment packet and provides the broker request, source files, product identifiers, and entry context, the startup controls that live fee right, case file, inconsistency map, packet draft, and channel relationship for that entry. A broker or lab can handle future entries, but cannot instantly take over the already-authorized packet, channel terms, Product Registry/eFiling work trail, and paid work product without the client switching mid-case.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The startup does not sell broad CPSC readiness, customs brokerage, HTS classification, product-safety testing, lab reports, legal opinions, a dashboard, a certificate database, or introductions. It accepts only named shipments or SKU batches with written broker/importer/agency requests, product identifiers, source evidence, and a cash-linked shipment or order consequence. The deliverable is a bounded entry-release packet: request interpretation, SKU-to-source evidence map, CPC/GCC field assembly, missing-field log, broker questions, draft certificate-data file, Product Registry/eFiling artifact checklist, and submit/cannot-submit/escalate decision.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

The startup does not act as customs broker, importer of record, product-safety certifier, CPSC counsel, testing laboratory, manufacturer, responsible party, legal adviser, HTS classifier, freight forwarder, or Amazon appeal guarantor. Customs brokers handle entry filing and HTS/ABI/PGA workflow. Labs and qualified product-safety professionals handle testing, rule applicability, lab-report validity, and safety judgments. Importers and manufacturers remain responsible for product truth, certificate content, source records, test reports, labels, compliance, eFiling, and entry decisions. Reject products with missing test reports where testing is required, fake supplier documents, disputed classification, unresolved safety issues, unclear manufacturer/importer identity, or pressure to certify legality or safety.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, MoCRABlocked, ButtonCellBlocked, EPRBlocked, HazmatBlocked FBA, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, 3PL recovery, OTA recovery, KSeF payment release, AIQ, RAGProbe, AppExchange retest, or Russia-origin MTC customs release. The buyer trigger, control object, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: CPSC certificate/eFiling data for named US import entries and SKU batches, not Amazon listing evidence, RED/CE/GPSR response, retailer deductions, customs origin/sanctions proof, invoice acceptance, appsec, or buyer diligence.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Customs brokers, product-safety labs, and CPSC consultants may already handle the best cases. HTS classification, rule applicability, certificate sufficiency, lab-report validity, and product-safety judgment can become legal or professional compliance advice. Many sellers will not have valid test reports, accurate manufacturer data, correct labels, or product truth. Broker/eFiling acceptance does not guarantee CPSC or CBP will never question an entry. The July 2026 deadline may create a temporary spike that standardizes quickly. Case economics can collapse into SKU-by-SKU clerical labor unless pricing and source-truth filters are strict. Brokers may prefer to own the workflow directly. The founder needs product-safety reviewer access and broker trust from day one. Sellers may misunderstand eFiling as product approval and pressure the startup to make weak files look acceptable.
