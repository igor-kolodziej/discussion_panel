Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
MDF Claim Lockbox for Cybersecurity VARs and MSPs

One-sentence thesis:
Recover expiring, pre-approved vendor MDF/co-op funds for cybersecurity VARs and MSPs by taking portal-authorized claim control over completed campaigns and turning proof-of-performance into vendor approvals, credit memos, or cash reimbursements.

Exact buyer:
10-150 person cybersecurity VARs, MSPs, MSSPs, and cloud/security resellers with partner status at endpoint, firewall, identity, backup, MDR, email-security, cloud-security, or SIEM vendors, where allocated MDF/co-op funds or approved campaign budgets expire unused or get rejected for proof gaps.

Acute trigger:
A vendor MDF/co-op allocation, webinar/event/campaign reimbursement, not-for-resale/demo subsidy, or approved market-development budget is expiring in the current quarter, has been rejected for missing proof, or is stuck in a partner portal while the reseller has already paid for the eligible activity.

Control point:
Signed claim mandate plus partner-portal user role or screen-share filing authority, vendor/program allocation proof, campaign approval, proof-of-performance bundle, invoice/payment proof, attendee/lead/export evidence where allowed, submitted claim, vendor acknowledgement, and resulting approval, credit memo, payment, or clean no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five signed partner mandates; at least 250,000 PLN equivalent in identified allocated or rejected MDF/co-op claims; three complete claims filed through vendor portals or vendor channel contacts; one vendor acknowledgement, approval, credit memo, or payment; and 25,000-75,000 PLN in success/setup fees collected or invoiced after accepted claims.

6-month POC:
Screen 25-50 partner accounts; review 1,500,000-4,000,000 PLN equivalent in allocated, rejected, or near-expiry funds; file 40-90 claims; produce 15-35 approvals, credit memos, or payments; collect 250,000-700,000 PLN revenue from setup fees and success fees; keep gross margin above 65% after claim ops, partner portal QA, and subcontractor review.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target cybersecurity VARs/MSPs/MSSPs listed in public vendor partner directories and conference exhibitor lists. Use a concrete offer: "If your current-quarter MDF/co-op allocation is expiring, rejected, or sitting unclaimed after a webinar, lunch-and-learn, trade show, demo day, or demand-gen campaign, we file the portal claim under your authorization and get paid only from accepted value plus a small screening fee."

Economics:
Charge 1,500-4,000 PLN screening/setup per partner account plus 18-30% of vendor-approved cash, credit, or reimbursement value. Minimum claim value 15,000 PLN, preferred 40,000+ PLN. The buyer pays because the spend is already incurred, the vendor money is time-limited, and internal sales/marketing ops often treat the claim as annoying admin.

Copy risk:
Vendor channel managers, distributors, marketing agencies, accountants, partner operations consultants, and the VAR/MSP's own staff can copy. The defense is control of current claim authority, vendor-specific proof checklists, rejection/approval memory by program, and quarter-end urgency before funds expire.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the partner signs the claim mandate, grants filing authority, provides allocation and proof-of-performance evidence, and lets the startup file the current claim before expiry, a competitor cannot take that exact claim without the partner revoking and restarting under deadline pressure. Competitors can copy future workflows, but not the already-authorized claim queue, portal trail, and vendor acknowledgement for a named current allocation.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a current vendor claim filed under partner authorization against a named allocation or rejected reimbursement. It is not a dashboard, audit report, marketplace, introduction, marketing agency, or generic channel consulting. If there is no named vendor allocation/rejection, proof bundle, filing authority, and monetary outcome, there is no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No fabrication, backdating, fake attendees, fake invoices, or false proof-of-performance. No legal, tax, accounting, vendor-contract, or channel-program interpretation beyond operational claim assembly and routing. Vendor, partner, and their advisors remain responsible for eligibility, tax/accounting treatment, program compliance, marketing claims, and final submissions. Reject claims involving sanctions-sensitive customers, public-sector procurement restrictions, bribery/anti-corruption risk, personal-data exports outside allowed portal evidence, channel conflict, or suspicious proof.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not PromoLeak because it does not audit retailer trade promotion deductions or broad promo leakage. Not Amazon Vendor Central recovery, Retail OTIF, PartnerCenter incentive recovery, 3PL overbilling, or DRS settlement; this controls allocated vendor MDF/co-op campaign reimbursement in cybersecurity channel partner portals. Not an evidence desk because the output is vendor acknowledgement, approval, credit memo, or payment.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many partners will already have a marketing coordinator, distributor rep, vendor channel manager, or agency handling MDF claims. Some apparent funds are not truly recoverable because approval expired, campaign scope changed, proof-of-performance is invalid, lead lists are missing, invoice timing is wrong, or vendor rules exclude the spend. Partners may refuse portal access or may bypass the startup after seeing the checklist. Vendor programs may reject third-party-prepared filings or communicate only with the partner. Ticket sizes may be too small unless claims exceed 15,000-40,000 PLN. The business may become low-margin admin labor if every vendor has different rules. Quarter-end urgency creates lumpy revenue. Fraud pressure around fake events, fake attendees, and backdated invoices must be rejected. Channel agencies and distributors can copy once the playbook is visible.
