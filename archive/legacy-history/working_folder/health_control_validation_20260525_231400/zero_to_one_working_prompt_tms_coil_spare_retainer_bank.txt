Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, patient-data businesses, and prescription-drug businesses without asset/payment-flow control.

Scored idea:

TMS Coil Spare Retainer Bank buys or options a retiring neurostimulation service technician/distributor's active support book and acquires one or two titled, tested, model-specific spare TMS coils/cables/cooling modules/power modules for private psychiatry and neuromodulation clinics. Clinics pay emergency retainers/deposits for guaranteed access to a compatible spare or swap path when a coil or module failure would cancel high-ticket treatment sessions.

The founder does not provide TMS treatment, prescribe, diagnose, set protocols, touch patient records, interpret outcomes, or make patient-facing efficacy claims. Licensed clinics remain providers of record. The startup controls titled tested spare hardware, service files, serial/compatibility map, support-call routing, technician/OEM-service partner, and prepaid clinic retainers.

Buyer / payer:
- private TMS/neuromodulation psychiatry clinics;
- mental-health clinic groups with expensive TMS treatment schedules;
- distributor/service technicians that need a monetization path for spare inventory and installed-base relationships;
- clinics that lose revenue and patient schedule continuity when a coil, cable, cooling unit, or control module fails.

Acute trigger:
A TMS clinic has booked treatment sessions and a coil/module fails. Waiting for OEM service, import, quotation, or replacement can interrupt a treatment course and lose revenue. The buyer pays for downtime insurance: a tested compatible spare, fast dispatch, technician coordination, deposit/return terms, and clear no-clinical-role boundaries.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is:
- signed support-book option from a retiring/overloaded TMS service technician, distributor, or clinic group;
- titled tested spare coil/module/cable/cooling-unit inventory with serials and service history;
- compatibility map by TMS system model, coil type, connector, cooling requirement, firmware/service constraints, and clinic installed base;
- support phone/domain/mailbox and seller handoff to installed-base clinics;
- emergency retainer agreements with named clinics;
- technician/OEM-service partner availability for safe installation/testing where required;
- deposit, damage, insurance, return, decontamination/cleaning, and liability terms;
- no patient data and no treatment advice.

The hard control is: scarce titled tested spare + installed-base support book + prepaid clinic downtime retainers.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option over a retiring/overloaded TMS service/support book;
- 40,000-70,000 PLN for one tested compatible spare coil/module/cable lot, only after clinic retainers;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN legal/medical-device distributor/liability/insurance review;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN service inspection, serial documentation, compatibility mapping, logistics, cleaning/return procedure;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN technician reserve and emergency courier/transport.

Required proof:
1. Signed option/handoff over one support book or installed-base relationship set with at least 10 named TMS clinics/systems.
2. Titled tested spare coil/module lot under 70,000 PLN, with serials, service/condition records, compatibility notes, and no questionable origin.
3. Written technician/OEM-service partner pathway for safe installation/testing or replacement coordination.
4. 3-5 clinics sign paid emergency retainer/deposit agreements before final close.
5. 20,000+ PLN in prepaid retainers/deposits or one paid emergency rental/swap/resale within 60 days.
6. Gross margin above 40% on retained retainer/rental/resale economics after service, insurance, transport, damage reserve, and seller earnout.
7. No patient data, no treatment protocol advice, no efficacy claims, no clinic-of-record role.

Economics:
Revenue comes from 1,500-4,000 PLN quarterly/annual emergency-access retainers per clinic, premium rental/swap fees for downtime events, resale margin on titled tested spare coils/modules, and service coordination fees where the technician/OEM partner performs the work.

Payback logic:
The startup does not buy broad equipment inventory. It options an installed-base support book, signs retainers from clinics that cannot tolerate downtime, then buys one exact compatible spare. If 5 clinics prepay retainers and one emergency rental/resale happens, the first asset can pay back faster than commodity consumables.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
This is a titled-hardware and prepaid-retainer business. It does not sell TMS treatment, clinical protocols, diagnostics, software, or patient acquisition. A competitor can promise service, but cannot copy the specific titled spare, installed-base handoff, serial compatibility notes, and paid retainer contracts.

Copy risk:
OEMs, distributors, and service technicians can compete. The wedge exists only where the startup controls the specific spare and inherited support book before clinics need it. If the founder has no installed-base handoff or only a generic used coil listing, the score falls below 80.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- No diagnosis, no interpretation, no treatment, no patient recordings, no patient data, no clinical advice.
- Clinics remain providers of record and use TMS systems under their own clinical governance.
- Founder sells/rents B2B professional spare access only and does not advertise patient-facing medical claims.
- Legal review covers medical-device distributor obligations, advertising limits, used-device documentation, service/inspection records, liability, insurance, warranty/returns, and no questionable stock.

Main risks acknowledged:
- The market may be too small and downtime events too rare.
- OEM/distributor warranty and service channels may bypass the startup.
- Used coils/modules may have safety, compatibility, warranty, service, or legal constraints.
- A part-time founder needs a technician/service partner, not just inventory.
- Clinics may refuse retainers if they trust OEM service or have low utilization.
- The first spare can be stranded if compatibility is wrong.
