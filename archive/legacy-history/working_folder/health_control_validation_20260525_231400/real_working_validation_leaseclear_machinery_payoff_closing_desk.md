## Strongest Objections Captured

- Clean cases may not need the startup; messy cases may be too risky for a lightweight closing desk.
- Trust is the product, and an unknown part-time founder has little credibility around high-value machinery, title documents, releases, and payment coordination.
- Despite the framing, the evaluator regarded it as a specialized transaction-coordination service.
- Percentage success-fee pricing could resemble brokerage and increase role ambiguity.
- Legal boundaries would need to be engineered with written scopes, escrow partner agreements, liability limits, fraud screening, and named legal/tax providers, not merely stated.
- Lessor/lender cooperation may be too slow for the buyer's production deadline.
- The claimed control point is weak unless referral sources repeatedly send cases.
- Rejected cases may dominate the funnel, making part-time deal flow acquisition too hard.
- Liability can exceed fees if a serial number, release, pledge, VAT chain, or duplicate-sale issue is mishandled.

## Useful Lessons

- The evaluator accepted that leased/financed/pledged machinery payoff blocks are real in Poland and that the 60-day proof was concrete.
- The fatal weakness is role compression: the buyer may expect legal verification, title assurance, anti-fraud control, escrow reliability, and tax-chain confidence from a founder who explicitly cannot provide those services.
- Mandate/control over a closing file is not enough when the transaction's trust burden belongs to lawyers, accountants, lessors, and established brokers.
- Future recovery/payment-direction ideas need either cash already recoverable through a narrow administrative claim path, or a payment stream that can be transferred without creating quasi-legal/escrow/title responsibility.
