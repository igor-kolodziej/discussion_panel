Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AppExchange Security Retest Lane

One-sentence thesis:
A 5-10 business day Salesforce AppExchange failed-security-review rescue lane for small ISVs whose listing, managed-package update, or enterprise launch is blocked by concrete Salesforce security-review findings and who need a bounded patch, false-positive, evidence, and retest submission packet rather than generic appsec consulting.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized Salesforce ISVs, Salesforce implementation partners with packaged apps, AppExchange app founders, and micro-SaaS teams that already submitted a managed package or app to Salesforce security review and received specific failed findings, scanner output, false-positive disputes, CRUD/FLS/sharing issues, injection findings, session/auth findings, dependency findings, privacy/security documentation gaps, or retest requirements. Channel buyers are Salesforce consultancies, ISV accelerators, and AppExchange launch advisers that do not want to staff urgent security-review remediation.

Acute trigger:
The ISV has a live failed or at-risk AppExchange security review, retest deadline, listing delay, managed-package update block, prospect launch dependency, investor/customer milestone, or enterprise buyer that will not proceed until the AppExchange package is approved or the security findings are credibly remediated. The buyer does not need a broad penetration test; it needs the exact failed review converted into patch evidence, false-positive explanations, safe scope boundaries, and a retest-ready packet.

Control point:
Prepaid live review case with Salesforce review ticket or report, failed finding list, package/version ID, source/repository access under scoped terms, scanner output, reproduction steps, test org/scratch org, dependency list, CRUD/FLS/sharing model notes, auth/session settings, data-flow notes, prior false-positive history, allowed fix scope, release branch, retest submission calendar, and buyer-approved boundaries. The startup controls the finding triage, patch/retest plan, evidence room, false-positive writeups, retest packet, and submission checklist for that active review cycle.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Four ISVs or Salesforce consultancies sign prepaid failed-review cases at 12,000-45,000 PLN each; every case has a live Salesforce security-review finding list, package/version scope, source or reproducible test access, and commercial deadline; at least two cases submit retest packets; at least one case produces pass, reduced findings, accepted false-positive, reviewer progress, listing movement, or narrowed retest scope; one channel partner routes a second case; gross margin stays above 55% after Salesforce/appsec reviewer and developer costs; no case is accepted if it requires open-ended product rebuild, legal advice, privacy compliance redesign, or production incident response.

6-month POC:
Deliver 20-40 failed-review rescue cases; collect 400,000-1,500,000 PLN in prepaid sprint fees and urgent retest fees; document 12+ retest submissions, 8+ review-progress or approval outcomes, 6 no-go/rebuild decisions, and 5 partner-routed repeat cases; maintain a private finding taxonomy by Salesforce review issue type, package pattern, Apex/LWC/API risk, scanner output, false-positive wording, accepted evidence format, retest timing, patch effort, and outcome; keep delivery under 10 business days for bounded review-ready cases; keep fewer than 25% of cases drifting into generic Salesforce development or broad appsec work.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target Salesforce ISV consultancies, AppExchange launch advisers, Salesforce implementation partners, ISV accelerators, AppExchange founders, and small packaged-app vendors. Pitch: "If your AppExchange security review failed on concrete findings, send the review report, package scope, and source/test access. I run a bounded remediation/retest lane: finding triage, patch branch or false-positive evidence, retest packet, and submission checklist in 5-10 business days. No broad Salesforce development, no legal/privacy opinion, no guaranteed approval."

Economics:
Charge 12,000-18,000 PLN for narrow false-positive/evidence/retest packets, 20,000-35,000 PLN for bounded patch-plus-retest sprints, and 35,000-60,000 PLN for urgent multi-finding launch-blocker cases. Pay Salesforce security reviewers and Apex/LWC contractors only for scoped findings. The buyer pays because a failed review blocks AppExchange listing, enterprise launch, managed-package update, customer rollout, or milestone value that is materially larger than the sprint fee.

Copy risk:
Salesforce security consultancies, appsec firms, Salesforce developers, ISV accelerators, and larger implementation partners can copy pieces. The defense is narrow failed-review specialization, partner-routed live tickets, fast bounded retest workflow, accumulated accepted false-positive and evidence wording, finding-to-fix taxonomy, and refusal to become generic Salesforce development. Once the startup controls an active failed-review sprint with the review report, source/test access, patch branch, evidence room, and retest packet, another firm must displace an in-flight retest under a deadline.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After the ISV or channel partner signs the prepaid case and shares the review report, package/version scope, source/test access, failed findings, commercial deadline, and submission calendar, the startup controls the active retest packet and patch/evidence workflow for that review cycle. Competitors can offer general Salesforce security work, but they cannot instantly own that specific retest evidence room, false-positive writeups, patch branch, and partner-approved submission packet already in motion.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only prepaid live failed AppExchange security-review cases with concrete findings and a retest path. The output is an operational review artifact: finding triage, bounded patch evidence, false-positive explanations, scanner/reproduction notes, retest packet, and submission checklist for the active Salesforce review cycle. It is not generic appsec consulting, broad Salesforce development, SOC2 work, legal/privacy compliance advice, a dashboard, a marketplace, a database, or a brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, privacy-law opinions, SOC2/ISO certification, formal penetration-test certification, production incident response, managed security service, full product rebuilds, broad Salesforce implementation, or guarantees of Salesforce approval. The ISV remains responsible for product security, final code, legal/privacy compliance, user data, release decisions, and submission truthfulness. Cases requiring major architecture redesign, sensitive production data access, unresolved customer breach, prohibited data handling, or formal certification are declined or routed to qualified firms.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle regulated-finance ICT supplier evidence or bank renewals. It is not REDBlocked because it does not handle Amazon/Allegro product compliance, connected devices, CE/RED/GPSR, or seller account health. It is not BioSignal because it is not investor diligence. It is not RAGProbe, RAGModule, AIGate, or AIQ because it does not test AI/RAG/agent security or buyer AI questionnaires; it is a Salesforce AppExchange failed-review retest lane. It is not HeritageDoor, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Salesforce security-review consultants and appsec firms may already serve this market. Some failed reviews require real engineering fixes, architecture changes, secure coding discipline, dependency changes, auth redesign, or data-model changes that exceed a 5-10 day sprint. Salesforce final approval remains outside founder control. Unknown-founder credibility is weak without named Salesforce/appsec reviewers. Source access creates security and IP risk. Partner channels may internalize the workflow after one case. Deal volume may be lumpy because many ISVs fail once, learn, and do not repeat often. The business can drift into generic Salesforce development or broad appsec remediation unless it accepts only bounded failed-review retest cases.
