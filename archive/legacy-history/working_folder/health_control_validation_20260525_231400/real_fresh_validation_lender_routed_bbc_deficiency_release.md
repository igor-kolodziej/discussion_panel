## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Lenders and factors may not route reputationally sensitive borrower problems to an unknown outside desk.
2. A lender/factor deficiency can signal borrower weakness, collateral deterioration, fraud risk, or credit judgment, not mere administration.
3. The Polish market may have factoring pain but not enough explicit BBC-deficiency pain.
4. The best cases may be solved internally by lender account managers, borrower accountants, fractional CFOs, ERP consultants, collateral monitors, or risk analysts.
5. Inbound routed cases may be adverse-selection: broken books, weak controllers, disputed receivables, aged inventory, related-party issues, and distressed borrowers.
6. Liability boundaries are fragile because packet work can look like collateral validation even if the borrower formally certifies facts.
7. Part-time execution is weak for urgent draw, month-end, overadvance, and reserve deadlines.
8. ABL/factoring software and collateral-monitoring platforms can absorb parts of exception workflows and audit trails.
9. Partner referral economics can create compliance, conflict, and trust issues.
10. The 6-month POC target is too aggressive for an unknown, part-time Warsaw founder.
