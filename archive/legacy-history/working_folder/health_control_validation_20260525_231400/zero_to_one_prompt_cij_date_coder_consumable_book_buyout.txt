Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: CIJ Date-Coder Consumable Reorder Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or option a retiring niche continuous-inkjet/date-coder service reseller's live reorder book, exact printer compatibility map, supplier terms, phone/mailbox, and remaining consumable inventory, then transfer customer-approved recurring orders for ink, makeup fluid, wash, filters, nozzles, printheads, and cartridges before closing.

Exact buyer:
Food, beverage, cosmetics, household chemicals, pharma packaging, cable, construction-products, and industrial manufacturers using continuous inkjet or similar date/lot coding equipment on production lines. Best first customers are small and mid-sized plants that already buy consumables from the retiring reseller because the reseller knows their exact printer model, fluid compatibility, reorder rhythm, and emergency substitution limits.

Acute trigger:
Production lines need date, batch, expiry, lot, barcode, or traceability coding every day. Consumables are recurring and compatibility-specific; wrong ink, makeup fluid, wash, filters, nozzles, printheads, or cartridges can create print failures, quality holds, wasted batches, or line downtime. The buyer is not being sold prevention; they are already buying these exact consumables.

Control point:
Seller option covering customer list, last-24-month order history, printer model/serial/SKU map, compatible consumable SKUs, supplier terms, phone/mailbox, reorder calendar, current stock, seller endorsement, and customer-approved payment direction. The founder closes only after customers prepay current-month or next-cycle orders under the new payee.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. One signed seller option or earnout LOI over a retiring CIJ/date-coder consumable reseller book.
2. Twenty customer approvals for payment and reorder transfer.
3. Printer compatibility map covering at least 60 installed printers or coding lines.
4. Titled inventory or supplier back-to-back orders for the exact SKUs needed in the first cycle.
5. 50,000-120,000 PLN in prepaid current-month or next-cycle customer orders before final close.
6. First 15 orders fulfilled with fewer than two compatibility or delivery exceptions.
7. Seller warm handoff by phone/email to every transferred customer.

6-month POC:
Month 1: identify 30-50 small CIJ/date-coder service shops, consumable resellers, packaging-line technicians, and retiring owner-operators; sign one seller option that values only transferred paid orders.
Month 2: seller-led outreach to active customers, collect payment-transfer approvals, map installed printers and SKUs, and fulfill the first 15 orders from titled stock or back-to-back supplier orders.
Month 3: complete 20 transferred customers, collect 50,000-120,000 PLN in prepaid/current-cycle orders, and audit gross margin, delivery time, compatibility exceptions, customer churn, and support requests.
Months 4-6: transfer or originate 40 active customers, reach 250,000-500,000 PLN annualized run-rate revenue, maintain 25-40% gross margin after shipping/replacements, keep average founder handling under 20 minutes per order after setup, and prove a second seller source or customer referral channel.

First acquisition mechanism:
Find small CIJ/date-coder service shops and consumable resellers through Google Maps, Allegro/OLX/B2B listings, industry directories, packaging-service forums, retiring technician networks, and plant-maintenance referrals. The seller pitch is: "I will not value your old contacts; I will pay for live customers only after they approve payment transfer and place current orders." Customer outreach is seller-led and order-specific, not cold selling.

Economics:
Consumables can support 25-45% gross margin where compatibility, availability, and emergency delivery matter. The first book target is 20-40 active customers ordering 3,000-15,000 PLN/year each, with 80,000-300,000 PLN annual gross revenue and expansion through exact-SKU stock discipline. Acquisition is paid as a small upfront plus earnout from transferred gross profit, keeping the 100,000 PLN budget safe.

Copy risk:
OEMs, authorized distributors, MRO distributors, packaging integrators, and online suppliers can sell many consumables. The defense is not exclusive chemistry; it is current customer-approved payment transfer, exact compatibility memory, seller endorsement, emergency stock for named printers, and refusal to buy stale contacts. Once current orders and printer/SKU maps transfer, competitors cannot capture those specific orders without the customer switching.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After the founder controls the seller option, phone/mailbox, order history, compatibility map, current inventory, supplier terms, and customer-approved next orders, competitors cannot act on that payment stream unless customers leave. The compounding asset is installed-base compatibility memory by plant, printer model, fluid type, failure history, reorder rhythm, substitute risk, and delivery outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business is an acquired recurring consumable order stream with titled stock or back-to-back supplier orders and customer-approved payment direction. The proof is fulfilled paid orders for exact compatible consumables, not a report, dashboard, lead list, service visit, or marketplace listing.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify printer performance, provide safety engineering, modify production lines, claim OEM authorization unless true, provide pharma/GMP validation, or promise warranty preservation for third-party consumables. Customers remain responsible for QA approval, line validation, purchasing policy, and OEM warranty decisions. Emergency printer repair, safety issues, and line-down technical service are referred to qualified technicians or OEM/provider channels unless separately contracted through a qualified provider.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not HeritageDoor, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet. It is also not Legacy Line-Control First-Call Spares Retainer because this is not a preventive emergency retainer or random industrial spares bank. The material distinction is a seller-endorsed current consumable reorder book with customer-approved payment direction and paid current-cycle orders for exact compatible production consumables.

Strongest anticipated objections:
OEMs and authorized distributors may recapture customers.
Some third-party fluids or substitutes may create warranty, quality, or compatibility risk.
Retiring sellers with clean order books may be rare.
Customers may approve handoff but then switch when the seller leaves.
Supplier terms and stock availability may be worse than assumed.
Plant buyers may demand emergency support or printer repair beyond consumables.
Gross margin can collapse if products are price-transparent online.
A part-time founder must avoid becoming a 24/7 line-down printer technician.
