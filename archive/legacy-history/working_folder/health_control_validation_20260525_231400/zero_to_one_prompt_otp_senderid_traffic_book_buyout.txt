Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
OTP Sender-ID Traffic Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or option a small retiring SMS/CPaaS reseller's live transactional messaging traffic book only after customers approve billing transfer, sender IDs or numbers are handed over, upstream CPaaS terms are secured, and first current-month OTP/login/order-notification traffic is billed under the new payee.

Exact buyer:
Ecommerce brands, marketplaces, SaaS vendors, fintech-adjacent firms, logistics operators, appointment platforms, delivery networks, B2B services, and other Polish/CEE companies that already send OTP, login, fraud, delivery, order, appointment, or service notifications through a small SMS/CPaaS reseller, digital agency, CRM integrator, or messaging integrator.

Acute trigger:
The current reseller is retiring, closing, losing supplier terms, suffering deliverability failures, failing sender-ID registration, being repriced by upstream CPaaS providers, or wants to exit a low-touch but critical customer book. The customer cannot tolerate OTP/login/order messages failing, but switching providers risks broken sender IDs, whitelisted templates, webhook callbacks, delivery routes, monthly invoices, sender reputation, support continuity, and internal ownership confusion.

Control point:
Conditional seller option over active transactional-messaging customers only; 12-month traffic, margin, sender-ID, route, and invoice history; sender IDs, short codes, phone numbers, approved templates, webhook/API settings, and delivery-error history where applicable; customer LOAs and billing-transfer approvals; upstream CPaaS or aggregator terms; technical route handover; direct payment direction to the founder/new entity; and first current-month or next-cycle traffic billed under the new payee before seller payout. No value is paid for stale contacts or marketing-only broadcast lists.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One signed seller option or earnout LOI over a small transactional messaging book; at least 15 active customers approve billing and sender-route transfer; 12-month logs prove at least 1,500,000 transactional messages/year and 120,000 PLN/year gross margin before acquisition; upstream CPaaS terms or reseller account are secured for the transferred routes; at least 10 sender IDs, phone numbers, templates, or route configurations are transferred or re-authorized; at least 50,000 PLN of current-month or next-cycle messaging revenue is prepaid, invoiced, or billed under the new payee; and the first 100,000 messages are sent with delivery failure or support incidents below the seller's prior baseline.

6-month POC:
Transfer 25-40 active transactional messaging customers, keep at least 80% of gross margin from the acquired book, bill 600,000-1,500,000 messages/month, collect 180,000-450,000 PLN in revenue, keep gross margin above 20% after upstream messaging costs and support, keep support under 20 minutes per customer per month after migration, prove two customers expand volume or add a sender route, and source one second seller lead. Kill if traffic is mostly one-off marketing blasts, if OTP/login traffic is too sensitive for transfer, or if upstream terms compress gross margin below 15%.

First acquisition mechanism:
Find small Polish/CEE SMS resellers, CPaaS sub-resellers, CRM/SMS integrators, ecommerce agencies, old gateway operators, and SaaS vendors with embedded messaging resale through industry directories, CPaaS partner pages, LinkedIn, local dev/agency networks, and customers seeing sender-ID or delivery failures. Seller pitch: "I do not buy stale contacts. I pay only for live customers who sign transfer LOAs, approve billing direction, and send current transactional traffic after handoff." Customer pitch is seller-led: "Your messaging provider is changing; keep the same critical sender routes and invoices without broken OTP/order notifications."

Economics:
Pay the seller a small option fee plus earnout from retained gross profit, not upfront revenue multiple. Charge customers the existing messaging markup plus 500-3,000 PLN transfer/setup fee only where route changes, sender-ID reauthorization, template reapproval, or webhook work is needed. Target 20-35% gross margin on SMS/CPaaS resale where traffic is low-touch and transactional. The 100,000 PLN budget is protected because seller payout follows customer-approved billing transfer and current traffic revenue.

Copy risk:
Infobip, Twilio, Sinch, MessageBird, telecom operators, local CPaaS providers, CRM vendors, ecommerce agencies, and internal engineering teams can all provide messaging. The defense is not generic SMS resale. It is the seller-endorsed handoff of exact active customers, sender IDs, phone numbers, templates, route history, webhook settings, invoice history, and customer-approved payment direction before those customers start shopping broadly.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder controls the seller option, customer transfer LOAs, sender-ID or number handover, route configuration, upstream CPaaS terms, invoice history, and first current-month billing under the new payee, competitors cannot take that exact payment stream unless customers actively churn. They can offer substitute messaging later, but cannot retroactively control the already-transferred sender identities, route history, customer approvals, and payee change.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup acquires or options a current transactional messaging payment stream and sender-identity handoff. It does not sell messaging strategy, deliverability reports, a dashboard, a marketplace, a database of CPaaS vendors, or broker introductions. The proof is live OTP/login/order-notification traffic billed under the new payee with transferred sender IDs, numbers, templates, routes, and customer authorizations.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not claim telecom operator status unless properly contracted, does not send illegal spam, does not handle regulated financial promotions, does not originate phishing/fraud traffic, does not process sensitive authentication data beyond message routing metadata needed for service delivery, and does not make privacy/legal/security guarantees. Customers remain responsible for lawful message content, consent, authentication design, privacy notices, data-processing terms, and regulated communications. Upstream CPaaS providers and telecom operators remain responsible for carrier connectivity, sender-ID registration rules, route availability, and abuse controls. Reject traffic involving fraud flags, grey routes, illegal marketing, gambling/adult/high-risk promotions, political messaging, medical/Rx claims, phishing risk, sanctions risk, or unclear customer authority.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL, OTA, RMA, field-action recovery, KSeF, cyber-insurance, AI/security, or physical-lot ideas. The control point is a current transactional messaging payment stream with sender-ID/number/template continuity and customer-approved billing transfer, not a compliance evidence pack, platform appeal, claim recovery desk, route book dependent on a licensed technician, or physical inventory lot.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Large CPaaS providers and local telecom aggregators can sell directly to the same customers. Customers may use the handoff moment to switch to a known provider. SMS gross margins may be too thin after upstream repricing, fraud controls, support, and failed-message credits. OTP/login traffic is trust-sensitive and may require security, DPA, uptime, and incident-response credibility beyond a solo founder. Sender IDs, short codes, templates, WhatsApp accounts, phone numbers, or routes may not transfer cleanly across providers. Small resellers with clean books may be rare. Support can spike if message delivery breaks during migration. If the acquired traffic is mostly marketing blasts rather than critical transactional messages, churn and compliance risk increase and the idea should be killed.
