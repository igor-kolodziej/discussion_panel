Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ReturnPremium Fleet/Site Cancellation Cash-Out Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Recover or offset insurer/broker-acknowledged unearned-premium credits for fleet, multi-site, and PE-backed companies after vehicle sales, site closures, broker changes, legal-entity cleanup, or M&A carve-outs, without touching claims handling or insurance advice.

Exact buyer:
CFOs, controllers, PE operating partners, fleet finance managers, and post-close integration leads at logistics fleets, delivery fleets, multi-site retail/restaurant/service groups, manufacturers, and PE-backed SMEs with commercial insurance policy changes across many vehicles, sites, entities, or brokers.

Acute trigger:
Vehicles are sold, sites close, a broker-of-record changes, policies are cancelled midterm, companies are carved out or merged, or old legal entities are wound down. The insurer or broker has issued a cancellation endorsement, return-premium statement, credit note, account statement, bordereau, or premium-finance refund/payoff statement, but cash or offset has not landed where finance can close it.

Control point:
The startup accepts only named return-premium or cancellation-credit lines already acknowledged by an insurer, broker, program administrator, or premium-finance statement. Control requires a policyholder mandate, policy/endorsement schedule, return-premium statement or credit note, broker/insurer contact path, entity-authority evidence, before/after statement, action log, and fee right tied to cash refund, approved offset, or clean no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign three mandates covering named acknowledged return-premium or cancellation-credit batches; place at least 750,000 PLN equivalent of broker/insurer-stated credits into the ledger; recover, offset, or cleanly no-go at least 250,000 PLN equivalent; obtain at least one broker/insurer written refund, offset, or corrected-statement acknowledgement; and invoice or collect at least 25,000 PLN in fixed and success fees. No accepted case may require claim handling, coverage advice, policy placement, litigation, debt collection, payment intermediation, client-money custody, or insurance brokerage.

6-month POC:
Close 10-20 mandates from PE-backed groups, fleet operators, multi-site closures, and broker-change cleanup files; screen 4-12 million PLN equivalent of insurer/broker-acknowledged return-premium or cancellation-credit lines; convert or cleanly no-go 1.5-5 million PLN equivalent; collect 150,000-500,000 PLN in fees; secure repeat channels from PE operating partners, accountants, fleet-disposal advisers, new brokers, and post-close integration consultants; keep gross margin above 65%; and keep rejected cases above 40% if artifacts are weak.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with channels that see insurance schedule mess but do not want stale-credit chase work: PE operating partners, post-close integration consultants, accountants serving multi-entity groups, fleet-disposal advisers, and new brokers inheriting messy schedules from old brokers. The direct written hook is: "If an insurer or broker has already issued return-premium or cancellation-credit statements after vehicles/sites/entities changed, I recover or offset only those named credits and produce a paid no-go when the credit is not collectible."

Economics:
Charge 5,000-20,000 PLN fixed setup for a named high-value batch, plus 5-12% of cash refunded or offset accepted by finance where before/after attribution is clear. Do not advance cash or buy claims. Costs are secure evidence handling, statement reconstruction, insurer/broker follow-up, accountant/controller review, and limited insurance-operations contractor support. Minimum batch threshold is 150,000 PLN equivalent in stated credits or 50,000 PLN if bundled through a repeat channel.

Copy risk:
Brokers, accountants, premium auditors, insurance consultants, and internal finance teams can do parts of this. The wedge is old or post-change credits where incentives are weak: the old broker lost the account, the new broker does not want historical cleanup, finance sees stale statements but lacks ownership, or the policy/entity schedule changed during M&A. The model is copyable if it becomes broad insurance admin, so intake must stay tied to named acknowledged credits and cash/offset outcomes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup controls only the named credit batch after the policyholder signs the mandate and provides policy schedules, endorsements, return-premium statements, broker/insurer acknowledgements, entity authority, and fee terms. A broker or accountant can copy future work, but it cannot instantly own the current evidence room, action log, before/after statement, and fee right without the buyer replacing the mandate mid-case. Old brokers may lack incentive because the policy was cancelled or the broker-of-record moved.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell insurance advice, premium benchmarking, claims support, placement, a dashboard, broad AP recovery, or broker introductions. It works only on named insurer/broker-acknowledged return-premium or cancellation-credit lines and produces cash refund, accepted offset, corrected statement, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not place insurance, recommend coverage, negotiate policy terms, handle claims, adjust losses, advise on insurance law, hold client money, provide payment services, provide legal/tax/accounting advice, or decide whether a premium is legally owed. Brokers, insurers, premium-finance companies, policyholder finance teams, accountants, and lawyers remain responsible for their own decisions. Reject claims payouts, disputed coverage, policyholder insolvency, suspected fraud, premium-tax disputes, unpaid-premium setoff fights, lender or loss-payee disputes, unclear entity authority, minimum-retained-premium disputes, and cases without insurer/broker-stated credits.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, 3PL overbilling, carrier credits, credit-memo cash-out, approved insurance payout financing, KSeF/VoP payment release, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, or HeatQuiet. It is materially different from the failed approved-insurance-payout lockbox because it excludes claims, losses, policy coverage, liens, mortgagees, contractors, and payout financing; it starts only from unearned-premium or cancellation-credit artifacts.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Brokers and accountants should already handle return premiums. Clean credits may auto-refund or auto-offset. Messy credits may involve minimum retained premiums, unpaid premium setoffs, taxes/fees, premium finance, entity authority, cancellation wording, policyholder name changes, or old broker obstruction. CFOs may resist fees for money they believe should arrive anyway. Direct outreach can look low-trust. The opportunity may be episodic unless PE, fleet, accountant, or broker-change channels produce repeated high-value batches.
