## Evaluator Summary

The evaluator agreed that the pain exists: filed merchant settlement claims can be blocked by proof-of-authority, TIN, duplicate-claim, payment-election, payment-status, or correspondence defects. It also recognized the wedge as cleaner than generic claim filing because the work begins only after a merchant has already filed or has official portal correspondence.

A Warsaw solo founder would need US-facing credibility, attorney-reviewed solicitation and engagement language, secure handling of claimant IDs/EIN/TIN/payment details, and trusted warm-channel distribution. Free administrator and class-counsel help also reduce willingness to pay except for large, complex merchant groups.

## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Free official help destroys much of willingness to pay.
2. The category is fraud-sensitive and scam-adjacent.
3. A Warsaw solo founder is in the wrong trust position for US merchants sharing sensitive claim, tax, entity, and payment details.
4. Post-filing cure work may still sit close to legal advice when authority, duplicate claims, entity history, ownership, and TIN conflicts are involved.
5. Success fees may be hard to defend because payment amount and timing are controlled by court/administrator allocation rather than only the founder's work.
6. Most cases will be too small for USD 1,500-5,000 cure fees.
7. Distribution must be through trusted accountants, franchise back offices, POS/acquirer resellers, or settlement attorneys; direct outreach will look suspicious.
8. The opportunity is episodic unless a steady future stream of business-class settlement defects appears.
9. The 6-month POC target of 150-300 cases and 250,000-700,000 PLN is unrealistic part-time without an existing US claims channel.
10. Data security cannot be lightweight because the work touches claimant IDs, EIN/TIN data, payment elections, authority documents, ownership records, and correspondence.

## Useful Evaluator Narrowing

The only version the evaluator considered testable was large-claim post-filing cure support for US merchant groups, sold only through trusted accountants, franchise back offices, POS/acquirer resellers, or settlement attorneys.

Strict entry criteria suggested by the evaluator:

- already filed claim;
- official claimant ID or portal correspondence;
- expected payment likely above USD 25,000-50,000;
- clear entity authority;
- specific defect notice or payment-status blocker;
- attorney-reviewed engagement and solicitation;
- fixed cure fee preferred, with any success fee capped and secondary.

## Lesson For Next Rounds

Do not treat "official cash claim plus portal defect" as enough. If the work requires high-trust sensitive-data collection from US buyers, legal-boundary navigation, and warm partner credibility, the founder-fit penalty can dominate the control point.

The next round should favor cash/control shapes where the founder can control a payment stream, asset title, signed mandate, or creditor/debtor acknowledgement without becoming a quasi-legal, scam-adjacent claims intermediary.
