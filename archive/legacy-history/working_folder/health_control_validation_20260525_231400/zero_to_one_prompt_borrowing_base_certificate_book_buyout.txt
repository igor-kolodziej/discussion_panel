Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
BorrowingBase Certificate Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Instead of cold-selling borrowing-base cleanup, option a retiring or tired finance-ops consultant's existing borrower-reporting book, transfer customer-approved monthly payment direction, lender templates, accepted-report history, and secure evidence vault, then prove current-month borrowing-base certificates are submitted and accepted before seller payout.

Exact buyer:
Primary payor: SMEs already paying a finance-ops consultant, specialist bookkeeper, fractional controller, or borrower-reporting operator to prepare weekly or monthly lender/factor borrowing-base certificates, AR aging schedules, inventory schedules, reserve schedules, factor portal uploads, or collateral support packs.

Economic buyer at acquisition: the founder buys or options the seller's book on earnout-heavy terms after customers reauthorize payment direction and current reporting periods succeed.

Best first seller: a retiring, overloaded, or de-scoping solo finance-ops operator with 8-25 active borrower-reporting customers, prior accepted lender/factor submissions, clean enough workpapers, customer contact history, reporting calendars, lender templates, and willingness to do a warm handoff.

Acute trigger:
The seller wants to retire, drop low-status spreadsheet-heavy reporting work, exit messy but valuable client admin, reduce liability, or free capacity for higher-value CFO/advisory work. Customers cannot tolerate a reporting gap because missing borrowing-base certificates, AR aging, inventory schedules, reserve support, or factor uploads can reduce availability, delay draws, trigger lender exceptions, or create weekly/monthly lender pressure.

Control point:
The startup controls an option over the seller's named reporting book, seller endorsement, customer reauthorization, current invoices, payment-direction transfer, lender/factor templates, reporting calendars, accepted-report archive, workpaper vault, AR/inventory/cash tie-out procedures, exception history, secure access transition, first-month submitted reports, and customer/lender response trail.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. One signed option or earnout LOI over a specific borrower-reporting book with at least 8 active customers.
2. Prior accepted reports for at least 6 customers, including lender/factor templates and submission calendars.
3. Customer reauthorization from at least 5 customers before seller payout.
4. Payment direction transferred for at least 5 current reporting periods.
5. Five current-month borrowing-base, factor, or collateral reports submitted under the transition process.
6. At least three accepted, paid, or cleanly no-go'd reporting outcomes.
7. 40,000-100,000 PLN collected from current-month customer fees before meaningful seller payout.
8. Rejection log for customers with broken books, unresolved lender disputes, fraud concerns, insolvency, missing authority, legal/accounting opinion needs, or support demands outside reporting.

6-month POC:
Month 1: source 20 possible sellers, diligence 3, sign one option/earnout LOI, and transfer secure vault/process access for a small subset.
Month 2: reauthorize 5-8 customers, submit first current reporting periods, collect transferred payments, and document no-go cases.
Months 3-4: stabilize 8-15 customers; process 30-50 facility periods; keep report-cycle delivery under agreed turnaround; close the first seller with mostly earnout economics only if churn and support burden are acceptable.
Months 5-6: source a second seller; operate 15-30 active borrower-reporting customers; process 80-140 facility periods; collect 250,000-650,000 PLN revenue; keep gross margin above 55% after accountant/ABL reviewer costs; keep churn below 15%; keep emergency deficiency cases below 25% of reporting periods.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to solo fractional controllers, finance-ops consultants, ABL/factor reporting specialists, specialist bookkeepers, distressed accountants, small ABL brokers with admin tails, ERP/accounting migration consultants, and fractional CFOs. Message: "If you have monthly borrowing-base, factor, or collateral-reporting clients you want to retire from, I can option the book, keep your customers' lender templates and reporting calendar intact, pay mostly on retained revenue, and let you keep higher-value CFO work."

Customer handoff message: "The reporting calendar, lender templates, workpapers, and review path stay intact. You reauthorize only after seeing the transition plan. The borrower remains responsible for truth and certification; we operate the reporting pack and exception tracker."

Economics:
Charge existing customers 5,000-12,000 PLN per month for recurring borrowing-base, factor, or collateral reporting, with higher fees for weekly facilities or inventory-heavy borrowers. Pay the seller a modest option fee plus 15-30% of retained gross revenue for 12-24 months, not a large upfront purchase. A book with 12 customers at 7,000 PLN monthly average produces about 84,000 PLN monthly revenue before contractor/reviewer costs. Margins depend on clean workpapers, repeatable templates, low emergency cleanup, and customer certification discipline.

Copy risk:
Accountants, controllers, fractional CFOs, ABL brokers, factors, lenders, and ERP consultants can perform borrower reporting. The defense is acquired control over a specific live book: seller endorsement, customer reauthorization, payment direction, lender templates, accepted-report history, workpaper vault, reporting calendar, and current-month successful submissions. Competitors can copy the service category but cannot copy the transferred customer book without seller and customer consent.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the seller option, named customer list, seller endorsement, customer reauthorizations, current invoices, payment-direction transfer, workpaper vault, lender/factor templates, accepted-report archive, and first submitted facility periods, a competitor cannot recreate that exact book without re-winning each customer and rebuilding the reporting history. The controlled object is a current payment stream plus accepted lender-reporting workflow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is an acquired borrower-reporting book with current payment direction, not a new consulting offer. The output is recurring submitted borrowing-base, factoring, or collateral-reporting packs using inherited lender templates and customer-approved workpapers. It is not a dashboard, lender broker, generic AR audit, accounting firm, marketplace, report database, template library, or CFO advisory practice.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as lender, factor, broker, accountant of record, auditor, lawyer, valuation expert, collateral agent, collection agency, or CFO of record. The borrower remains responsible for truth, certification, accounting judgment, eligibility claims, inventory valuation, collateral title, and facility compliance. The seller and customer certify transition facts. The startup operates reporting workflow, tie-out support, exception tracking, and evidence organization only. Reject fraud concerns, insolvency, lender disputes, legal/covenant issues, broken accounting records, inventory title problems, valuation disputes, and customers expecting turnaround advice or loan negotiation.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, KSeF invoice release, customer true-up recovery, unapplied cash cleanup, factor reserve release, or the prior BorrowingBase Draw-Release Desk. The controlled object is a transferred recurring borrower-reporting book with customer-approved payment direction and accepted lender/factor templates, not a one-off payment-release case.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Clean sellers may be rare: the book must be small enough for an earnout option but organized enough to transfer safely.
Customers may treat the handoff as a reason to bring reporting in-house, move it to their accountant, or ask the lender/factor for help.
The best sellers may keep the profitable customers and offer only messy, low-margin, high-risk accounts.
Borrowing-base reporting can hide serious accounting, credit, inventory, fraud, covenant, or lender-dispute problems.
Sensitive AR, inventory, debtor, lender, bank, and facility data creates trust friction.
The Warsaw founder has no default credibility with lenders or distressed borrowers unless the seller handoff and reviewer bench are strong.
Support creep can turn monthly reporting into CFO advisory, ERP cleanup, collections, lender negotiation, field-exam prep, or turnaround consulting.
Facility templates vary enough that the first book may not generalize to a second seller.
Part-time execution may be strained if multiple customers have same-week reporting deadlines.
Seller earnout economics may be unattractive if churn risk, liability fear, or customer relationship value is high.
