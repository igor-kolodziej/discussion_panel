Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EUDR DDS Import Release Desk.

One-sentence thesis:
EU/CEE importers of covered EUDR commodities will pay for a named purchase-order or shipment release file that turns supplier geolocation, legality, traceability, and due-diligence-statement evidence into a buyer/broker-usable DDS reference pack before a 2027 shipment, contract, or invoice is blocked.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE importers, distributors, roasters, food/ingredient companies, timber/furniture suppliers, rubber-component importers, and their customs brokers or freight forwarders, but only when a named covered commodity order or shipment is at risk. The first beachhead is medium importers of coffee, cocoa, wood/timber-derived goods, and rubber components that sell into EU retailers, manufacturers, or distributors who are already asking for EUDR reference numbers or source evidence.

Acute trigger:
A retailer, manufacturer, distributor, freight forwarder, customs broker, trade-finance bank, or internal compliance team will not accept a 2027 purchase order, shipment file, downstream handoff, or invoice without EUDR due-diligence-statement reference numbers, supplier authority, plot/geolocation evidence, chain-of-custody data, country-legality evidence, and a clear submit / missing-data / no-go decision for the named order.

Control point:
The startup only accepts live named PO/shipment cases where the importer/operator signs a case authorization, the exporter or supplier signs a data-release mandate for the named origin lane or batch, and the buyer/broker request is in writing. The controlled asset is the shipment-specific EUDR evidence file: supplier authority, order and customs documents, geolocation files, legality evidence, risk-screen notes, due-diligence-statement reference handoff, buyer/broker response wording, and a no-go record if the file cannot honestly support the shipment.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, the founder must have two prepaid cases above 8,000 PLN each, each tied to a named PO or shipment; signed importer/operator authorization; signed supplier/exporter data-release authority; the exact buyer/broker request; commodity/order/shipment documents; a completed evidence index; a submitted or operator-approved due-diligence-statement reference handoff where available; and one written buyer, broker, bank, or customer acceptance, rework request, or clean no-go decision. No supplier-data mandate means no accepted case.

6-month POC:
Close 10-15 paid named-order or named-shipment cases across 3-5 importers or broker channels. At least six cases must produce buyer/broker/customer progress, accepted reference handoff, invoice/order release, or documented clean no-go before the customer wastes shipment money. At least four cases must involve nontrivial supplier-data or geolocation/traceability gaps, not simple checklist retrieval. Build a private case-memory library by commodity, origin-lane, missing evidence type, buyer wording, broker wording, and accepted/rejected reference handoff pattern.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with a list of 60 Polish/CEE importers, roasters, timber/furniture importers, rubber-component importers, freight forwarders, customs brokers, and trade accountants handling coffee, cocoa, wood-derived goods, and rubber. Outreach is not generic EUDR readiness. It asks for one live buyer/broker/customer request tied to a named 2027 order or shipment and offers a fixed-scope 5-business-day triage plus 10-business-day release file. In parallel, ask customs brokers and commodity accountants for overflow cases where their client has supplier data but cannot turn it into a buyer-ready file.

Economics:
Initial pricing is 3,000-5,000 PLN for paid triage and 8,000-25,000 PLN for the full named-order or named-shipment release file, with no free pilots. Reviewer costs stay below 25-35% by using one trade/compliance reviewer and one GIS/remote-sensing reviewer only for flagged sections. Gross margin target is 60%+ after reviewers. The fee is justified only when a named shipment, PO, contract, invoice, or buyer relationship is worth at least 100,000 PLN or strategically important.

Copy risk:
EUDR platforms, certification schemes, commodity traders, customs brokers, law firms, consultants, GIS vendors, and ERP providers can copy generic readiness work. The narrow defense is not generic EUDR knowledge. It is case-level memory from live buyer/broker requests, supplier-data release wording, commodity/origin gap patterns, accepted/rejected DDS reference handoffs, and a disciplined refusal policy that keeps the product tied to named commercial blockers.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Incumbents can sell generic EUDR prep, but they cannot retroactively control the exact supplier data-release mandate, buyer request, origin-lane evidence file, and order/shipment authorization once the founder has signed those for a specific case. The startup wins only inside that specific file: it has the importer authority, supplier authority, exact documents, buyer/broker wording, and case history before a platform or consultant can generalize the workflow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell readiness reports, software, vendor listings, commodity sourcing, marketplace matching, legal opinions, certification, or general EUDR advice. It accepts only a named commercial blocker and returns a usable release artifact for that blocker: evidence index, supplier authority trail, DDS reference handoff, buyer/broker response wording, missing-data/rework list, or clean no-go. The POC is measured by paid cases and order/shipment/invoice progress, not by downloads, dashboards, leads, or advisory hours.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The importer/operator remains responsible for EUDR due diligence, legal conclusions, Information System submission, supplier selection, declarations, and commercial decisions. The startup does not certify deforestation-free status, provide legal advice, guarantee customs or buyer acceptance, act as importer of record, trade commodities, provide customs representation, or make false claims. Country-legality questions, disputed product scope, sanctions, customs classification, contracts, and contested supplier claims are routed to qualified counsel or rejected. GIS and remote-sensing reviewers provide issue-spotting only, not certification.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from CBAM Import Continuity Desk because the controlled proof object is EUDR supplier/geolocation/legality evidence and due-diligence-statement reference handoff for coffee, cocoa, wood, rubber, and related commodity orders, not CBAM embedded-emissions reporting or declarant continuity for steel, aluminium, cement, fertilizers, electricity, or hydrogen. It is distinct from GreenTender EPREL because it is not public-tender product labeling. It is distinct from REDBlocked because it is not Amazon connected-device compliance. It is distinct from DORA because it is not ICT supplier-risk evidence for regulated-finance renewals.

Strongest anticipated objections:
EUDR may still be too early for urgent paid demand before late 2026. Many importers will use existing EUDR platforms, certification schemes, customs brokers, consultants, large traders, or internal compliance teams. Hard cases depend on supplier data that the founder cannot force. Legal responsibility and country-legality assessment remain outside scope. Downstream obligations have been simplified, reducing some pain. Simple cases may be low-value reference-number collection, while hard cases may require changing suppliers rather than packaging evidence. Commodity relationships and origin data are trust-heavy for an unknown part-time founder.
