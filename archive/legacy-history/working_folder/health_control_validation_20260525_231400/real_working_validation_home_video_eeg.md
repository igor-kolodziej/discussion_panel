## Zero To One Summary

Zero To One agreed that this is better than a generic EEG clinic or equipment purchase because the proposed control point is an acquired micro-operator demand surface: phone/listing/referral queue/bookings/equipment/technician workflow/reading neurologist relationship.

It still scored only 75 because the control point is fragile and medically entangled. The core problem is that a non-clinician founder may not be able to safely and profitably transfer the trust, clinical delivery, technician capacity, and medical responsibility inside a 2-month under-100k PLN POC.

## Strongest Objections

1. Seller trust may not transfer.
   - Referral doctors and parents may trust the individual technician-owner, not the phone number, Google profile, or website.
   - The deal would need 60-90 days of seller visibility, referral introductions, patient rebooking support, technician continuation, and interpreting-neurologist continuation before close.

2. It may legally look like medical activity.
   - If the startup contracts with parents, controls workflow, holds equipment, and bills for EEG services, counsel must determine whether it must be a registered medical entity or operate through a registered medical partner.

3. Technician and reader capacity are the real bottleneck.
   - Buying a device does not create pediatric EEG execution capacity.
   - Scarce work includes electrode placement, child cooperation, sleep capture, artifacts, video synchronization, and trusted interpretation.

4. Pediatric trust is fragile.
   - A messy handoff, delayed report, confusing consent, or unclear medical responsibility can kill the brand quickly.
   - A faceless logistics brand is weak; seller/clinician endorsement is mandatory.

5. Margins are not automatically strong.
   - Home/sleep/video EEG prices can be higher than standard EEG, but costs include skilled technician time, travel, failed sleep capture, cancellations, consumables, interpretation, insurance, legal/GDPR, and admin.

6. Owner earnings may be weak for a semi-operator.
   - A 20-30 test/month book may support a technician-owner, but may be thin when the founder pays contractors and coordinates everything.

7. Competitors can copy home EEG service.
   - Defensibility depends only on acquired referral loop and smooth execution, not on offering home EEG itself.

## Pivot Implications

Do not advance EEG micro-lab acquisition unless a real signed option already includes:

- 30+ active bookings;
- 2-3 retained referral doctors/clinics;
- one technician under continuation contract;
- one interpreting neurologist signed;
- legal memo on medical-activity registration/partnering and medical-record custody;
- 10-15 paid tests completed with contribution margin above 35-40%.

Next candidate: Stoma/Urology NFZ Micro-Shop Buyout, because it has stronger recurring transaction-flow custody and less dependence on a non-clinician founder owning medical delivery.
