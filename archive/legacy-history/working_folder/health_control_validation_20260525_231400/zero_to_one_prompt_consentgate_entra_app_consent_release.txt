Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ConsentGate Entra App-Consent Release

One-sentence thesis:
A 5-7 business day Microsoft Entra app-consent release sprint for small B2B SaaS vendors whose enterprise buyer cannot approve a Microsoft OAuth/OIDC/Graph integration because the app shows as unverified, lacks publisher-domain or verified-publisher evidence, requests overly broad Graph scopes, has unclear admin-consent wording, or fails the buyer's tenant app-consent policy.

Exact buyer:
20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors, workflow automation vendors, analytics products, document-processing tools, security/compliance tools, HR/ops tools outside high-risk decisioning, and vertical SaaS vendors that integrate with Microsoft 365, Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, Entra ID, or Microsoft Graph and have a named enterprise buyer, pilot customer, renewal, or deployment blocked by admin consent. First channel partners are SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, Microsoft 365 MSPs, Entra/identity consultants, and SaaS implementation partners who see consent blockers but do not want to own a narrow app-registration cleanup sprint.

Acute trigger:
The enterprise buyer's Microsoft tenant blocks or delays the SaaS integration: the consent screen shows unverified publisher; the app lacks a verified publisher badge or configured publisher domain; user consent is disabled for unverified apps; the requested Graph permissions require admin consent; the permissions appear too broad for the use case; the buyer security team asks for least-privilege mapping, app ID/publisher evidence, redirect URI and domain evidence, data-access explanation, or admin-consent packet; or a pilot/renewal cannot start until the buyer's Microsoft admin approves the app.

Control point:
Signed live consent-release case with client authorization, app registration access or screen-share authority, Microsoft Entra tenant/app metadata, publisher-domain status, Partner Center/PartnerID readiness where applicable, requested Graph delegated/application permissions, redirect URI list, consent-screen screenshots, buyer admin/security request, buyer deadline, app data-flow notes, current product scope, test tenant or demo account, least-privilege permission map, excessive-scope reduction plan, admin-consent packet, and approved no-go rules. The startup controls the app-consent issue map, technical correction checklist, buyer-admin evidence room, permission rationale, and release/no-go packet for that active enterprise deployment.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three partner channels or direct SaaS vendors sign paid routing terms; six live enterprise app-consent blocker cases are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 12,000-45,000 PLN each; every case has a named buyer, buyer tenant/admin blocker, current app registration, requested Microsoft Graph/OAuth/OIDC permissions, enterprise deployment deadline, and client authorization; at least four cases produce verified publisher/domain progress, reduced risky scopes, buyer-admin packet acceptance, admin-consent review progress, admin consent granted, pilot deployment unblocked, or clean no-go because the product needs deeper engineering/security remediation; one partner routes a second case; gross margin stays above 60% after Microsoft identity/appsec reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Deliver 25-50 app-consent release sprints; collect 400,000-1,200,000 PLN in direct, wholesale, and urgent sprint fees; document 20+ buyer-admin progress outcomes, 8 admin-consent grants or pilot unblocks, 8 excessive-scope reductions, 8 verified-publisher/domain fixes, and 8 clean no-go/remediation decisions; build a private taxonomy of consent-screen defects, Graph permission objections, publisher-domain issues, Partner Center verification blockers, app-registration misconfigurations, redirect URI/domain mismatches, least-privilege wording, buyer-admin follow-up patterns, and accepted evidence packet formats; keep turnaround under seven business days for evidence-ready apps; decline apps that need broad product re-architecture or want to bypass tenant security policy.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, Microsoft 365 MSPs, Entra/identity consultants, and SaaS implementation partners serving B2B SaaS vendors. Pitch only live blockers: "When an enterprise buyer's Microsoft admin cannot approve your client's app because the publisher, domain, Graph permissions, consent wording, or app-registration evidence is unclear, route the case to me. I produce a buyer-admin consent release packet and fix bounded app-registration/publisher-domain issues in 5-7 business days. I do not bypass tenant policy, sell Microsoft consulting broadly, or request permissions the app does not need."

Economics:
Charge 12,000-18,000 PLN for a narrow evidence-ready consent packet and permission-rationale sprint, 20,000-35,000 PLN for app-registration cleanup plus buyer-admin packet, and 35,000-60,000 PLN for urgent enterprise-deal blockers involving multiple app registrations, several Graph scopes, or verified-publisher/Partner Center work. Partners can wholesale or direct-bill. The SaaS vendor pays because the fee is small relative to a delayed enterprise pilot, blocked renewal, stalled deployment, or security-review failure involving a 100,000+ PLN equivalent deal.

Copy risk:
Microsoft partners, Entra consultants, identity architects, appsec firms, SOC2 consultants, MSPs, SaaS implementation agencies, and competent SaaS developers can copy parts. The defense is live buyer deadlines, narrow consent-release specialization, repeated permission-rationale memory, fast partner-routed delivery, accepted buyer-admin evidence formats, and strict refusal to become generic Microsoft 365 or SaaS development consulting.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the SaaS vendor or partner signs the case, grants authorized access or screen-share control over the app registration, provides the buyer-admin request, consent blocker, app ID, permission list, publisher/domain state, and payment guarantee, the startup controls that active consent-release packet for the named buyer deadline. A Microsoft partner or appsec firm can offer an alternative later, but it must displace an in-flight app-registration cleanup and buyer-admin evidence room already tied to the customer's blocked deployment.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live enterprise Microsoft app-consent blockers with a named buyer, app registration, permission list, and deployment deadline. The output is an operational release artifact: corrected publisher/domain/app-registration checklist, least-privilege Graph permission map, consent-screen evidence, buyer-admin response packet, scope-reduction notes, verified-publisher/domain progress, and release/no-go decision. It is not generic Microsoft consulting, app development, SOC2 evidence, legal compliance, GRC dashboard, template database, marketplace, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not bypass tenant security controls, impersonate admins, request unnecessary permissions, provide legal advice, guarantee buyer admin approval, certify security, perform full penetration testing, implement broad product architecture, submit false publisher information, or act as Microsoft, Partner Center, tenant admin, DPO, security officer, or software owner of record. SaaS vendors, Microsoft, Partner Center, enterprise buyers, tenant admins, lawyers, DPOs, security teams, Microsoft partners, and product engineers remain responsible for final admin consent, publisher verification, legal/privacy conclusions, product security, and deployment decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because it does not handle regulated-finance ICT third-party risk, DORA clauses, bank questionnaires, exit plans, or supplier-risk evidence. It is not RAGProbe because it does not test AI security or RAG/agent behavior. It is not AppExchange Security Retest Lane because the platform is Microsoft Entra/OAuth/Graph admin consent, not Salesforce marketplace security review. It is not REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CyberBind, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Microsoft partners, MSPs, Entra consultants, appsec firms, and competent SaaS developers may already solve this. Some blockers require real product engineering, security architecture, OAuth flow changes, data-minimization work, or Partner Center verification outside a quick sprint. Enterprise tenant admins may still reject the app even after cleanup. Verified-publisher requirements can depend on the vendor's Microsoft partner account, domain, tenant, and authority rather than the founder. Graph permission reduction may break product features. The unknown founder needs a credible Microsoft identity/appsec reviewer before SaaS vendors trust app-registration access. The market may be too narrow unless many enterprise deals actually stall on consent warnings rather than broader security review.
