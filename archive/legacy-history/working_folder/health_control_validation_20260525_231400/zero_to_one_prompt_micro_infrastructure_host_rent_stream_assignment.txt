Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Micro-Infrastructure Host Rent Stream Assignment

One-sentence thesis:
Small landlords and site owners hosting parcel lockers, ATMs, vending banks, telecom cabinets, microcells, EV chargers, or similar installed machines will sell or lockbox already-paying host-rent streams when the founder secures payer acknowledgement and first redirected payment before capital is committed.

Exact buyer:
The first economic counterparty is the property owner or site host with an existing installed machine contract and a non-disputed monthly or quarterly host-rent, commission, or revenue-share stream. The later buyer can be the startup itself holding the stream, a small yield buyer, or an operator-approved assignee, but the POC can work without reselling the stream.

Acute trigger:
The property owner is selling the site, closing a business, cleaning up small receivables, needs short-term cash, dislikes tracking tiny operator payments, or has inherited a lease/host contract after acquiring a property. The rent stream already exists, but it is too small and awkward for banks, funds, or standard factoring providers.

Control point:
A counsel-reviewed assignment, purchase option, or lockbox mandate over a named host-rent stream from an existing machine/site contract, plus owner authority, operator contract, payment history, operator statement or remittance evidence, assignment notice, operator acknowledgement or accepted payment-direction change, and first redirected payment or scheduled remittance before the founder commits purchase capital.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Ten host contracts screened; three signed owner mandates or purchase options over non-disputed streams; two operator acknowledgement or accepted payment-direction paths; one first redirected rent/commission/revenue-share payment; and no upfront purchase larger than a small diligence deposit before payer acceptance.

6-month POC:
Source 50-100 existing host contracts from property managers, small landlords, retail strips, fuel stations, gyms, schools, car washes, storage sites, industrial sites, and local commercial-property brokers. Accept only fixed or highly predictable streams above 1,000 PLN per month with clear owner authority, clean payment history, no maintenance dispute, no site dispute, and a creditworthy operator. Close 3-8 acknowledged assignments or lockbox mandates, keep capital exposure below 100,000 PLN, collect first redirected payments, and prove a 20-35% gross spread over expected collected cash or a 10-20% success fee where no purchase capital is used.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to property managers, small landlords, retail-site owners, car-wash/fuel-station owners, gyms, schools, storage operators, and commercial-property brokers: "If you host a parcel locker, ATM, vending bank, telecom cabinet, EV charger, or similar machine and want to cash out or clean up that rent stream, I buy or administer only after the operator acknowledges the payment direction." Ask for the operator contract, last 6-12 months of remittances, site authority, and assignment/notice terms.

Economics:
For fixed rent streams, buy 12-30 months of expected payments at a 15-35% discount only after operator acknowledgement, or charge 10-20% of redirected payments for a lockbox/success-fee structure without upfront capital. Minimum accepted stream is 1,000 PLN per month or 12,000 PLN per year. Reject small vending commissions unless bundled. Target 70%+ gross margin on success-fee files and a 20-35% gross spread on purchased streams after counsel review, diligence, payment administration, and failed-file costs.

Copy risk:
Factoring firms, small receivables buyers, operators, landlords, property managers, and local brokers can copy the general model. The defense is case-level control: signed owner mandate or purchase option, operator contract evidence, operator acknowledgement, and accepted payment direction on a specific host-rent stream before another buyer sees it.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the property owner signs the assignment or option and the operator acknowledges the payment direction, the founder controls that exact rent stream for the agreed period. Competitors can pursue future landlords or other machines, but they cannot collect the already-acknowledged stream without replacing the signed assignment and operator payment instruction.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell analytics, listings, introductions, software, rent benchmarking, site acquisition, or machine placement. The unit is an existing named host-rent stream that is assigned, purchased, or lockboxed, with operator acknowledgement and first redirected cash as the proof.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No disputed rents, no debt collection, no consumer lending, no deposit-taking, no securities offering, no fractionalized sale to retail investors, no operator contract breach, no site-placement brokerage, no maintenance responsibility, and no promises about machine uptime or future site performance. Counsel reviews assignment and purchase forms. The operator remains provider of record for the machine, maintenance, safety, service, and customer operations.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack Safety, or HeatQuiet. It is closest in structure to assigned-cashflow ideas, but the control point is different: existing small machine-site host rents with operator-acknowledged payment direction, not AP exceptions, platform recovery, securities payouts, regulatory evidence, import continuity, physical heritage suites, or municipal payment routing.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Operators may prohibit assignment, ignore notices, or require full novation. Many host rents may be too small to justify diligence. Property owners may not sell at a meaningful discount. Revenue-share streams can be volatile and depend on footfall, machine uptime, maintenance, or operator discretion. Payer acknowledgement may be slow. Legal structure may drift into regulated lending, factoring, debt collection, or investment-product territory. The best streams may already be known to operators and property managers. A few assigned streams do not prove scalable acquisition. Part-time execution may be hard if every contract has different assignment, notice, tax, and payment terms.
