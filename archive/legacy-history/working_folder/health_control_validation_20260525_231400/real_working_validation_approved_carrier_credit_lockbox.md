## Strongest Objections

1. The stranded-value pool may be too small. Approved credits may be automatically offset, already reconciled, too small, expired, non-transferable, or subject to carrier/platform rules.

2. Assignment may be blocked by carrier/platform terms. Polish receivables assignment can be allowed generally, but contract restrictions, statutory limits, and obligation nature can block it. The structure must be credit-type-specific.

3. Payment direction and lockbox may be operationally unrealistic if carriers pay only the contracted account holder, apply account offsets, or refuse third-party instructions.

4. Merchant ownership will be messy. The shipment payer, carrier account holder, merchant, insurer, and beneficiary can differ, and the 3PL may already have reimbursed or retained the recovery.

5. 3PLs may fear exposing settlement mistakes or failed merchant pass-throughs.

6. Carrier acknowledgements may not be final. "Approved" can still mean pending credit note, offset-only, duplicate review, set-off against unpaid invoices, or pending account data.

7. A 20-35% success fee may feel high for already-approved money unless the items are old, scattered, near expiry, merchant-owned, or repeatedly ignored.

8. The work can become bespoke reconciliation labor with low repeatability across carrier portals, merchant contracts, COD files, credit notes, and settlement ledgers.

9. Incumbents can absorb the process. Parcel-audit vendors, 3PL finance teams, accounting firms, and ecommerce ops consultants can copy once value is proven.

10. The idea may be too narrow after excluding speculative claims; the largest recovery pools may be in unfiled claims, overcharges, rate errors, duplicate labels, returns, or SLA failures.

## Interpretation For Next Rounds

The debtor-acknowledged receivable control point is not sufficient if the pool is small or irregular. A future payment-control candidate needs acknowledged amounts that are larger, more repeatable, less platform-dependent, and less likely to be auto-reconciled internally.
