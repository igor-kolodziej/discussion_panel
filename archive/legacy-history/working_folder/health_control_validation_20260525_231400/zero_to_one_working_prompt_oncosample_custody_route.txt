Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, patient-data businesses, and prescription-drug businesses without asset/payment-flow control.

Scored idea:

OncoSample Molecular Custody Route Buyout buys or options a small specimen-courier/lab-runner route, pathology office handoff book, or clinical-trial sample logistics micro-operator serving oncology, pathology, molecular diagnostics, and CRO/central-lab workflows in Warsaw/Mazowieckie.

It is not a lab, not a diagnostic provider, not a genetic interpretation service, not a patient-recruitment agency, not a medical-data marketplace, and not a generic courier marketplace. The founder does not order tests, interpret results, recruit patients, advertise medical outcomes, sell prescription medicines, handle investigational products, or claim clinical provider status. Licensed labs, clinical sites, investigators, pathologists, oncologists, sponsors, and CROs remain responsible for medical decisions, testing, reporting, patient consent, trial protocol compliance, and specimen ownership. The startup controls the operational handoff layer: route, kits, SOPs, chain-of-custody, pickup slots, receiving appointments, and paid transfer flow.

Buyer:
- CRO trial operations teams needing local biomarker/specimen reliability;
- molecular/genetic labs receiving FFPE slides/blocks, blood, ctDNA kits, or PAXgene/Streck tubes;
- oncology and pathology sites with slow or rejected send-out workflows;
- private oncology clinics and trial sites that lose screening windows when sample transfer fails.

Acute trigger:
For oncology/genetics and trial-screening workflows, a patient may qualify for biomarker-driven treatment or a trial only if FFPE material, slides, blood, plasma, ctDNA, or other specimens move quickly with correct packaging, forms, chain-of-custody, and lab intake coordination. Wrong kit, wrong tube, missing consent/referral, bad temperature condition, courier delay, or incomplete accessioning can force redraw/re-cut/rejection and lose days.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is the route and custody book:
- active site/lab pickup contacts and receiving contacts;
- seller handoff and phone/domain/mailbox/WhatsApp continuity;
- route timing notes and validated pickup windows;
- site-specific SOPs, requisition checklists, accessioning requirements, and rejection-prevention notes;
- UN3373/exempt-specimen packaging workflow and trained courier partner capacity where required;
- chain-of-custody logs and scan events;
- kit inventory for FFPE slide/block transport, blood/ctDNA tubes where supplied by labs, secondary containers, absorbents, labels, temperature loggers, and tamper-evident bags;
- reserved receiving slots or named accessioning contacts at partner labs;
- paid CRO/lab/site transfer SLAs and per-transfer invoice rights.

The hard control is not a dashboard. It is custody over a named route/book plus paid specimen handoff flow, documented by signed site/lab SOPs and completed paid transfers.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option over a retiring/overloaded specimen courier, lab runner, pathology office admin, or sample-logistics micro-operator route;
- 10,000-20,000 PLN legal/GDPR/insurance/sample-custody review;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN packaging/kit inventory, scanner/labels, temperature loggers, secure storage box, and SOP tooling;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN trained courier partner reserve and route float;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN contractor/admin support for accessioning checklists and customer handoff.

Required POC proof:
1. Signed route/book option or revenue-share handoff with at least 2 active site/lab relationships.
2. Two signed site SOPs and one receiving molecular/pathology lab or CRO/central-lab pilot agreement.
3. Written courier/transport capability for relevant categories, including UN3373/P650 handling where applicable.
4. Insurance/legal review covering custody, loss/delay liability, data minimization, and no-clinical-role boundaries.
5. 30 paid transfers or 15 urgent transfers plus one monthly SLA deposit within 60 days.
6. At least 25,000 PLN collected or prepaid before final close.
7. Median Warsaw/Mazowieckie pickup-to-lab time under 24 hours for urgent transfers.
8. Rejection rate under 5% for logistics/documentation reasons.
9. No test ordering, no diagnosis, no genetic interpretation, no patient recruitment, no patient-data monetization.

Economics:
- 450-900 PLN per urgent specimen transfer/custody event;
- 2,500-8,000 PLN monthly SLA for a site/lab/CRO corridor;
- kit restock and temperature-logger fees;
- rejection-prevention/accessioning checklist fee;
- after POC, route density and recurring trial-screening corridors drive margin.

Gross-margin logic:
Variable courier/packaging/admin cost can be 120-300 PLN per transfer if route density is local. Contribution margin can be 50-65% on urgent transfers and higher on monthly SLA deposits if rejection prevention and accessioning checklists are standardized. Payback can occur after roughly 40-60 paid transfers or fewer if a CRO/lab SLA is prepaid.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The founder is not selling a sample-tracking dashboard or genetic interpretation. The asset is an acquired/optioned route/customer handoff plus kit inventory, SOPs, courier capacity, chain-of-custody logs, receiving slots, and paid transfer flow. The POC only passes if real specimens are moved and paid under signed SOPs with low rejection.

Copy risk:
Generic couriers can move packages, and large labs can build their own send-out logistics. The defensible wedge is the exact oncology/pathology site route, receiving lab contacts, accessioning/rejection-prevention checklist, seller handoff, local urgent pickup windows, kit custody, and paid CRO/lab SLA before close.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- Do not run a lab, order tests, interpret results, or make clinical claims.
- Do not handle investigational products or prescription medicines.
- Do not touch patient recruitment.
- Use site/lab-controlled consent and requisition flows; minimize patient identifiers in startup systems.
- Use legal/insurance review before accepting custody.
- Use trained courier/transport partners for relevant categories and packaging rules.
- Reject specimens/workflows that require a licensed lab, hospital employee, protocol-specific central-lab courier, or sponsor-only kit custody.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Public hospital/pathology procurement can be slow.
- Existing couriers, central labs, CRO vendors, or large diagnostic networks can copy parts of the workflow.
- Some specimens cannot be handled by an outside startup under protocol, sponsor, hospital, or lab terms.
- Chain-of-custody failure, loss, delay, or temperature excursion can create liability.
- The route seller profile may be rare.
- If the acquired route has only contacts and no active paid relationships, the idea fails.
