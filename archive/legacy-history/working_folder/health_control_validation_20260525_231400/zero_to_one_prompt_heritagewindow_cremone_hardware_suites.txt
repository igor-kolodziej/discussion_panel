Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
HeritageWindow Cremone Hardware Suites

One-sentence thesis:
Control complete matched pre-war window-hardware suites before they are fragmented during window replacement, gut renovation, demolition, or estate clearance, then sell project-ready original cremone/espagnolette handles, rods, stays, latches, pulls, hinges, backplates, and visible fastener sets to historic-window restorers, premium joiners, interior architects, boutique developers, and HNW renovation owners who need coherent original hardware across many restored windows.

Exact buyer:
Historic-window restoration firms, premium joinery shops, interior architects, heritage renovation contractors, boutique developers, and HNW owners restoring pre-war apartments, townhouses, boutique hotels, offices, or villas in Warsaw, Lodz, Krakow, Poznan, Wroclaw, Gdansk, and similar cities.

Best first buyers are working on named premium projects where original windows survive or are being rebuilt, but the visible window hardware is missing, mismatched, overpainted beyond ordinary cleanup, replaced with modern/plastic pieces, or split across singles.

Acute trigger:
During pre-war apartment or townhouse renovation, original windows may survive or be rebuilt, but the visible hardware is missing, mismatched, overpainted, replaced with modern handles, or split into decorative singles by salvage channels. A premium restoration wants a coherent run of original brass, bronze, nickel, or steel window hardware across multiple rooms: cremone/espagnolette handles and rods, window stays, latches, pulls, hinges, escutcheons, backplates, and matched fasteners where recoverable.

Control point:
Title or written first-refusal/pickup rights over a complete matched window-hardware suite from one property before it is fragmented, scrapped, sold as singles, or lost during window replacement:
- cremone/espagnolette handles and rods;
- latches, stays, pulls, hinges, backplates, escutcheons, and visible fasteners where recoverable;
- source address/provenance note;
- window count, room map, dimensions, photos, condition notes, and numbered bagging;
- removal/handover document from owner, contractor, demolition crew, estate seller, or window-replacement firm;
- restorer/joiner fit notes;
- buyer deposit before restoration or resale.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three controlled complete window-hardware suites from real properties, each documented before removal with photos/video, window counts, and seller handover.
2. At least 80 individual pieces numbered, bagged, and condition-cataloged.
3. Three buyer deposits from architects, window restorers, joiners, boutique developers, or premium owners for full suites or named projects, not decorative singles.
4. One restoration/metalwork partner willing to quote cleaning, de-painting, repair, replating, rod adjustment, and missing-screw matching.
5. One suite sold or reserved at 50%+ expected gross margin after pickup, cataloging, partner work, and seller payout.
6. At least two first-refusal agreements with renovation crews, window-replacement firms, demolition crews, estate-clearance operators, or premium property managers.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a visual buying guide for pre-war cremone/espagnolette and window-stay hardware; recruit one restorer/metalwork partner and one window joiner/restorer for fit feedback; contact 40 renovation crews, window-replacement firms, demolition crews, estate-clearance operators, premium property managers, architects, and heritage contractors.

Month 2: secure three titled or first-refusal suites before fragmentation. Photograph, number, bag, catalog, and condition-grade at least 80 pieces. Publish a private buyer catalog to 30 architects, window restorers, joiners, boutique developers, and premium renovation owners.

Month 3: collect three buyer deposits for full suites or named projects. Deliver one raw suite and one restoration quote. Document whether buyers value matched suite coherence enough to pay more than decorative single-piece salvage prices.

Months 4-6: control 8-15 suites, sell or reserve 5+ complete suites, collect 80,000-250,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 50%, build a first-refusal network with at least five removal/renovation sources, and build a private matching catalog by city, period, metal, style, rod length, window count, and condition.

First acquisition mechanism:
Supply pitch:
"Before you throw away or split old window hardware during replacement or renovation, send photos of the full set. I pay quickly for complete suites, handle pickup and cataloging, and I care about keeping room/window sets together rather than buying decorative singles."

Buyer pitch:
"If your pre-war windows survive or are being rebuilt but the hardware is missing or mismatched, I can supply a complete original suite from one property: numbered cremone handles, rods, stays, latches, hinges, pulls, backplates, and fit notes. I sell full project sets, not antique-shop singles."

Economics:
Pay 1,000-5,000 PLN per property/suite depending on count and quality, or a per-piece minimum where needed. Sell raw cataloged suites for 6,000-18,000 PLN and restored/refit-ready suites for 15,000-45,000 PLN through restorers, joiners, and architects. Logistics are compact: numbered bags, parts bins, photo catalog, and partner restoration rather than bulky inventory. Deposits should cover pickup, cataloging, and most restoration work before final delivery.

Copy risk:
Architectural salvage dealers, antique shops, window restorers, demolition crews, joiners, and metal restorers can sell individual pieces or copy the idea. The defense is first-refusal supply relationships, pre-fragmentation capture of complete suites, room/window maps, numbered condition catalogs, buyer deposit memory, and a reputation for full project-ready runs rather than decorative singles.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder has title or first-refusal rights over a complete suite from one property, numbered catalog custody, source documentation, and a buyer deposit for a named project, a competitor cannot recreate that exact matched set from scattered singles. The controlled unit is the coherent property-level hardware suite, not a generic antique handle.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business physically controls or options complete window-hardware suites, catalogs them, and sells titled project-ready sets with buyer deposits. It is not renovation management, design advice, restoration service, a salvage marketplace, a decorative catalog, a dashboard, or a broker introduction. Partner restoration exists only to make controlled suites usable for named projects.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify structural window performance, safety, conservation approval, fire escape suitability, child safety, burglary resistance, or code compliance. Window restorers, joiners, architects, conservators, and owners decide installation, safety, conservation, and fit. The startup sells original hardware suites with provenance, condition notes, measurements, and restoration/fit partner quotes. It declines stolen, protected, disputed, or unclear-title material and requires handover documentation.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not HeritageDoor Suites. HeritageDoor controls complete internal door leaves, frames/casings where recoverable, hinges, handles, escutcheons, locks, and trim, and sells the matched door rhythm itself. HeritageWindow controls compact original window operating hardware for surviving or rebuilt windows, excluding door leaves and interior-door suites. The buyer trigger is window hardware mismatch during historic-window restoration, not missing matched internal doors.

It is not HeritageRadiator, StormTile, hotel FF&E, or other bulky salvage because the units are compact, high-value, numbered, and deposit-matched before restoration. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Demand may be too small; many renovations accept reproduction hardware, modern hardware, or mixed originals.
- Window restorers and architects may already source hardware from salvage dealers or metalworkers.
- Fit is harder than it looks: rod length, backset, sash geometry, hinge side, screw pattern, metal fatigue, corrosion, overpaint, and missing pieces can break a "matched" set.
- Conservation or owner approval may be needed for protected properties.
- Some hardware may be stolen, unclearly owned, or removed from protected buildings without permission.
- Buyers may want restored/refit-ready hardware, turning the business into restoration project management.
- First-refusal supply depends on crews remembering to send photos before disposal.
- Complete suites may be rare; lots may often be too incomplete for premium projects.
- This is adjacent to HeritageDoor and must prove that window-hardware suites have independent buyer urgency, not just decorative salvage interest.
