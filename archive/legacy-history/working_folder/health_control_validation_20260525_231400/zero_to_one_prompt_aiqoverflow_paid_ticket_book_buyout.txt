Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AIQOverflow Paid Ticket-Book Buyout.

One-sentence thesis:
Small SOC2, ISO27001, privacy, fractional-CISO, and vendor-risk consultants are starting to hold live enterprise AI-questionnaire tickets for AI-enabled SaaS clients, but many do not want to staff a specialist AI evidence bench; the wedge is buying or operating an already-paid, customer-approved ticket queue with partner approval and payment direction before seller payout.

Exact buyer:
Seller/acquisition target: a small SOC2, ISO27001, privacy, fractional-CISO, security-questionnaire, or vendor-risk consultancy with 3-20 AI-enabled B2B SaaS clients and a live queue of enterprise buyer AI/security/privacy questionnaire sections it has already sold, scoped, or committed to deliver but does not want to staff.

Economic customer: AI-enabled B2B SaaS vendors with ordinary embedded AI features, selling to enterprise buyers, where a named renewal, procurement, pilot, expansion, or security review is blocked by live AI questionnaire sections.

Acute trigger:
Enterprise buyers ask AI-specific questions on top of security and privacy questionnaires: model providers, no-training terms, data retention, prompt/log handling, RAG source controls, human review, evals, hallucination/error handling, model-change control, incident process, opt-out, subprocessors, and customer-data boundaries. The partner consultant is trusted by the SaaS vendor but does not want to build a specialist AI-questionnaire bench for occasional bursts.

Control point:
The startup does not cold-sell AI packs. It acquires or options a current ticket book: signed partner/seller option or subcontract assignment over named tickets, customer-approved white-label or named delivery authority, exact buyer questionnaire wording and deadline, evidence-room access under the partner's trust path, partner final approval of every answer, direct payment direction or partner-guaranteed payment before seller payout, and delivery history from the acquired queue.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One signed option or subcontract assignment with a SOC2/fCISO/privacy partner; at least 8 named live AI-questionnaire tickets with buyer deadlines and deal values above 100,000 PLN equivalent; at least 60,000 PLN cleared to the startup or escrowed/partner-guaranteed before seller payout; at least 5 delivered ticket outcomes such as buyer-progress response, narrowed follow-up, partner-approved no-go, or remediation/legal referral; at least 2 tickets from the same partner repeat or expand after the first delivery; no accepted case in healthcare, HR, credit, biometric, law-enforcement, autonomous adverse decisioning, or formal high-risk AI validation.

6-month POC:
3 acquired or optioned partner ticket books, or one partner book plus two repeat partners; 35-60 paid AI-questionnaire tickets; 350,000-900,000 PLN collected revenue; 20+ buyer-progress, narrowed-follow-up, or clean no-go outcomes; 5+ vendors return for a second buyer questionnaire or renewal; 3+ partner/seller channels send repeat tickets; gross margin above 65% after reviewer, contractor, partner margin, secure workflow, and seller earnout; fewer than 25% of inbound tickets rejected after screen because of legal, high-risk, or remediation scope.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound is to consultants, not SaaS vendors: SOC2 and ISO27001 consultants, fractional CISOs serving AI-enabled SaaS, privacy consultants who answer buyer questionnaires but avoid AI governance staffing, vendor-risk questionnaire specialists, and boutique security consultancies with AI-enabled SaaS clients.

Offer: "If your SaaS clients are sending you live enterprise AI questionnaire sections you do not want to staff, I buy or revenue-share the current ticket queue. You keep the client relationship and final approval; I handle evidence mapping, draft answer pack, no-go routing, and buyer-ready artifact under your brand. Seller payout comes only from cleared customer cash or partner-guaranteed tickets."

Economics:
8,000-18,000 PLN per AI-questionnaire ticket; 20,000-45,000 PLN for multi-buyer or multi-product sprint; partner margin or seller earnout of 20-35% of collected revenue; optional monthly evidence refresh only after repeat tickets prove demand. Costs are founder intake, evidence mapping, draft pack, partner coordination, occasional AI security/governance reviewer, occasional security/privacy reviewer, secure document workflow, deletion log, and audit trail. Payment is tied to current tickets already sold or guaranteed by the partner, not speculative future referrals.

Copy risk:
High at the category level. GRC platforms, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, privacy firms, AI governance consultants, and questionnaire tools can all add AI-questionnaire workflows. The narrower asset is the exact paid ticket queue, partner trust path, client authorization, payment direction, and case memory from buyer-specific AI questionnaire wording and accepted/no-go outcomes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy future workflows, but not the current queue once the startup controls named tickets, partner-approved delivery authority, customer authorization, payment direction or partner-guaranteed cash, exact buyer deadlines, live evidence rooms, and seller warm handoff. A competitor must displace an active delivery owner inside a deadline-bound buyer review, not win a cold template sale.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is an acquired paid ticket book with client authorization, partner final approval, direct payment direction, and current buyer deadlines. The startup does not sell generic AI governance advice, dashboards, readiness reports, marketplaces, referrals, or cold questionnaire drafting.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No legal opinions, AI Act classification, DPIA ownership, ISO42001 implementation, model validation, high-risk AI conformity work, security audit, penetration testing, or product remediation. The vendor and its partner approve every answer. Legal/high-risk/regulatory cases are routed out or rejected. Healthcare, HR, credit, biometric, law-enforcement, clinical, insurance underwriting, autonomous adverse decisioning, and patient-data workflows are excluded from the first POC. The output maps existing evidence, gaps, no-go items, and safe wording boundaries; it does not certify AI compliance.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack, REDBlocked, BioSignal, PromoLeak, or a direct AI evidence-pack service. The control point is an acquired, paid, customer-approved ticket queue from an existing trusted consultant. The buyer trigger is enterprise AI questionnaire sections on live SaaS renewals/procurements, not regulated-finance DORA/ICT supplier evidence. The payment event is transferred or guaranteed current ticket cash before seller payout. The proof artifact is partner-approved ticket delivery with buyer-progress/no-go outcomes, not a generic evidence pack or software dashboard.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The seller profile may be rare; partners may resist assignment or white-label dependency because of client control, liability, embarrassment, or client-stealing fears; GRC platforms and questionnaire tools may automate common AI answers; vendors may lack real evidence for no-training, retention, logging, RAG source governance, evals, human review, opt-out, incident process, or model-change control; legal/high-risk scope creep can contaminate the queue; enterprise buyers may still reject answers if the product lacks real controls; sensitive evidence access remains a trust barrier even through partners; the work could become low-margin outsourced questionnaire labor; payment-direction transfer may be slower than a normal subcontract if clients require procurement approval; one ticket book proves a POC, not a durable company, unless repeat partner acquisition and case memory compound.
