Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

RoadAssist Event Pay Rail

One-sentence thesis:

Roadside assistance and towing contractors serving insurers, leasing fleets, mobility clubs, and assistance networks lose cash when event evidence is reconstructed after the job; sell a live evidence-to-invoice rail that captures assistance job ID, dispatch authorization, arrival/departure timestamps, GPS, photos, service action, customer/driver signoff, distance, storage-day proof, and invoice-line mapping before the payor rejects or delays the invoice.

Exact buyer:

Polish and CEE roadside assistance, towing, vehicle recovery, mobile battery/tyre, replacement-car handover, and storage-yard contractors that already perform recurring jobs for insurers, leasing/fleet companies, assistance networks, mobility clubs, OEM roadside programs, or large fleet managers. The first buyer has at least 300 assistance/fleet/insurer jobs per month and can name one or two payors with recent payment holds, evidence rejections, chargebacks, underpayments, storage-day disputes, distance disputes, or repeated correction requests.

Acute trigger:

A current batch of assistance, towing, roadside repair, recovery, replacement-car, or storage invoices is delayed, rejected, underpaid, or chargeback-prone because the payor says event proof is incomplete or inconsistent: missing job ID, weak dispatch authorization, no arrival/departure timestamp, missing scene photos, unclear vehicle plate/VIN, no driver/customer signoff, wrong tow distance, no storage-day proof, no release photo, mismatch between service code and invoice line, late submission, or missing approved-extra-service evidence.

Control point:

Signed contractor mandate over a named payor/job type plus the live event evidence rail for current-week jobs: payor job ID, dispatch authorization, driver mobile intake, timestamped GPS/photo artifacts, required fields, missing-field alerts, invoice-line mapping, packet generation, submission log, payor response log, correction/no-go taxonomy, and fee right for setup, per-event processing, and released/reversed invoice value. The contractor remains service provider of record and the payor remains insurer/assistance/fleet payment owner.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Sign 2 roadside/towing contractors or one contractor group with at least 300 assistance/fleet/insurer jobs per month. Each contractor identifies one or two payors with recent holds, rejections, chargebacks, or evidence disputes. Mandate the rail for 150-250 live events over 30 days. Capture job ID, payor authorization, vehicle plate/VIN where allowed, location, arrival/departure timestamp, photos, service action, driver/customer signoff where available, distance/tow route, storage-day proof if relevant, and invoice-line mapping. Submit 80+ payor-ready event packets or invoice attachments. Obtain at least 3 payor acknowledgements, accepted correction outcomes, payment releases, chargeback reversals, or written no-go rules. Collect 25,000-60,000 PLN from setup, per-event, or release-linked fees.

6-month POC:

8-15 contractors or 3-5 contractor groups. 2,500-8,000 live assistance events captured. 1,500+ payor-ready packets submitted. 150+ correction/payment/accepted-no-go outcomes. 250,000-900,000 PLN in invoice value processed through the rail. 150,000-450,000 PLN in fees collected. At least 3 repeat payor-specific playbooks such as one insurer, one assistance network, and one fleet/leasing group. Gross margin above 65% after dispatcher QA contractors, translation where needed, storage, and tooling. Demonstrated reduction in rejection/chargeback rate for at least two buyers compared with their prior month or prior payor batch.

First acquisition mechanism:

Target local towing/roadside contractors and dispatcher groups visible in insurer/assistance supplier lists, Google Maps, roadside forums, fleet-service networks, and local towing associations. Lead with a specific payment question: "Which assistance payor most often delays or rejects your invoices because of photos, timestamps, job IDs, storage-day proof, distance, or signatures?" Offer a paid 30-day payor-specific event rail for one job type and one payor, not a new dispatch system. Build first with mobile forms, WhatsApp intake, timestamping, secure folder storage, dispatcher QA, OCR, packet templates, and manual invoice-line mapping before software.

Economics:

Charge 4,000-9,000 PLN setup per contractor/payor workflow, 8-25 PLN per captured event depending on evidence complexity and volume, and 3-8% release/reversal fee only for previously rejected or held invoice value that is accepted after packet submission. Minimum 2,500 PLN monthly platform/manual-QA fee after pilot. The fee is rational if a contractor has 50,000-300,000 PLN/month in assistance billing and 3-10% of lines are delayed, rejected, disputed, underpaid, or chargeback-prone. Contractor gross margin improves if the rail reduces dispatcher/admin time and turns more current events into first-pass accepted invoice lines.

Copy risk:

Assistance companies, insurers, fleet portals, dispatch software, towing ERPs, GPS fleet systems, and internal dispatcher teams can copy pieces. The defense is not generic mobile forms. It is payor-specific event evidence memory, contractor adoption at the roadside, before/after rejection data, and exact packet formats that map events to invoice lines for several recurring payors.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

For a signed contractor/payor workflow, once the startup is the mandated evidence intake for current-week jobs, it controls the job-ID event packets, timestamped artifacts, invoice-line mapping, missing-field alerts, packet submission history, payor response log, and correction/no-go taxonomy. A dispatch app or insurer can copy the format later, but it cannot reconstruct the same live source evidence after the driver has left the scene or the yard day has passed.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The product is a live payment-evidence rail for named assistance events and payor rules. It does not sell reporting, analytics, generic dispatch, claim brokerage, insurer introductions, legal claims handling, or a marketplace. The output is an invoice-ready event packet tied to a payor job ID and a payment outcome: accepted line, corrected line, paid/released value, chargeback reversal, or documented no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

No personal-injury claims, accident causation, liability adjustment, insurance advice, passenger medical data, police/criminal disputes, or claims handling. The contractor remains service provider of record; assistance/insurer/fleet remains payor and policy owner. No custody of funds, factoring, or payment collection on behalf of the contractor. Reject jobs involving disputed accident liability, bodily injury, fraud suspicion, stolen vehicle, police seizure, sanctions, unclear title/authority, storage/legal disputes, or any request to influence claim coverage. Use minimum necessary vehicle/job data, secure intake, access logs, deletion schedule, and payor-approved submission routes.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This is not TraceFaktura because the buyer is not a municipal animal-care contractor, the payor is not a gmina, and the event object is not an animal-care statutory service. It is not TowFaktura because it does not handle municipal impound/storage payment files; it starts with private insurer/assistance/fleet job IDs and recurring payor portals. It is not winterroad, streetlight, KPO/BDO, warranty/RMA, OEM core credit, DRS, pallet deposit, KSeF, Amazon, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, or BatteryFit. The control point is current live roadside-assistance event evidence before invoice submission.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Assistance networks and insurers may already provide apps, portals, or required evidence workflows. Towing contractors may be low-margin, operationally chaotic, and resistant to another tool at the roadside. Many rejected invoices may be valid because of wrong tariff, unapproved service, missed authorization, non-covered policy, excessive storage, inflated distance, late submission, or contractor process error. Payors may refuse third-party packet formats and require their own portal fields or app. Driver adoption is hard; if drivers skip photos, signatures, timestamps, or required fields, the rail cannot fix the case later. The value per event can be too low unless the contractor has high volume and measurable rejection/chargeback pain. Data protection and fraud controls matter because the workflow touches vehicle plates, location, drivers, job IDs, invoices, and sometimes customer signatures. Large assistance providers can standardize evidence requirements or force their own tools if the problem is large. The startup may be pulled into disputed accident liability, customer complaints, storage/legal conflicts, fraud detection, or insurer claims handling. This can become another field-service app unless every case is tied to accepted invoice lines and payor-specific rules.
