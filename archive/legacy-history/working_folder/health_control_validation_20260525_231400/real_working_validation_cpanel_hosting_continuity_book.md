## Strongest Objections Captured

1. A hosting book can hide more support debt than revenue: hacked WordPress sites, stale PHP, weak backups, broken mailboxes, unclear domain ownership, unpaid invoices, old plugins, unsupported themes, and undocumented DNS/registrar access.
2. Part-time coverage is dangerous because hosting failures are urgent: email down, DNS breakage, domain expiry, SSL expiry, malware, PHP upgrade failures, server disk problems, mailbox quota, or blocked SMTP.
3. Large hosts can undercut on price and automation; customers remain only if seller trust and continuity matter more than cheap hosting.
4. Tiny books may be underpriced. Old 30-50 PLN/month accounts expecting human support are bad assets.
5. Seller spreadsheets may overstate real value; only customer-approved payment transfer and collected cash count.
6. Access transfer is operationally messy across registrars, DNS, cPanel/Plesk, root/server access, mailboxes, SSL, backup storage, billing, support inbox, domain ownership, WordPress admin, and plugin licences.
7. Email support can dominate the book through SPF/DKIM/DMARC, mailbox migration, spam filtering, POP/IMAP, and Microsoft/Google confusion.
8. Security cleanup can destroy margins unless malware and hardening are separately priced.
9. The handoff itself can trigger churn to larger hosts or newer agencies.
10. One book may be side-income rather than a company unless seller sourcing is repeatable.
