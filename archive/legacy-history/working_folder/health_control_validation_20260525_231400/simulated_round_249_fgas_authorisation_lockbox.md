# Simulated Round 249: F-Gas Authorisation Lockbox

Date: 2026-05-30 Europe/Warsaw

## Search Posture

Recent failures punished evidence-only desks, current-cash mandates where a counterparty still controls the outcome, and physical assets with uncertain buyer urgency. This round returns to a harder object: a regulatory-use right that can be transferred, accepted, and consumed through an official portal before a shipment can be released or placed on the EU market.

The candidate is deliberately different from the failed `F-Gas Import Licence Continuity Desk`. That failed idea organized shipment evidence and portal status. This round controls the scarce quota-authorisation transaction itself: quota-holder option, importer deposit, F-gas Portal acceptance path, and shipment-specific authorisation/delegation file.

## Source Checks

- European Commission F-gas FAQ states that quota authorisations are required for importing equipment or products pre-charged with HFCs, and that authorisations and delegated authorisations are valid until used up.
- European Commission equipment/product obligations state that importers of pre-charged products/equipment need F-gas Portal registration and sufficient quota authorisations/delegations prior to release for free circulation.
- Regulation (EU) 2024/573 Article 21 provides for quota transfers, authorisations to use quota, and delegations through the F-gas Portal; transfers/authorisations/delegations are valid only if accepted in the portal.
- Commission quota-transfer guidance says only reference-value holders can transfer quota allocation and transferred quota cannot be transferred a second time.

## Selected Candidate

**F-Gas Authorisation Lockbox**

One-sentence thesis: Importers of heat pumps, AC units, refrigeration equipment, and other non-pharma pre-charged HFC equipment will pay a founder-controlled transaction desk that locks quota-holder authorisations/delegations and importer deposits before a named shipment, production order, or customs release stalls.

### Why It Clears Simulation

The control point is stronger than a normal evidence mandate:

- The startup does not merely explain F-gas rules or compile import documents.
- It signs a quota-holder or authorisation-manager option for a named quantity of tCO2e authorisation/delegation.
- It signs an importer deposit/reservation for a named shipment or purchase order.
- It tracks the official F-gas Portal acceptance trail and escrow/payment sequence.
- It counts only accepted authorisation/delegation, shipment progress, or clean no-go as proof.

The buyer pays because the alternative is a delayed shipment, cancelled seasonal order, demurrage, lost reseller allocation, or inability to place pre-charged HFC equipment on the EU market. The first proof is not interest or a report; it is portal transaction movement, importer deposit, and shipment/procurement progress.

### Internal Cap Review

- Concrete 6-month control proof: passes. Portal accepted authorisation/delegation plus importer deposit is a concrete control event.
- One vendor or consultant copy risk: partially passes. F-gas consultants and quota holders can copy the workflow, but they cannot copy a specific optioned quota authorisation, importer deposit, and accepted portal transaction once controlled.
- CAC/payback/margins/cash conversion: passes if buyer deposits fund the authorisation purchase and case fees are tied to high-value shipments.
- Vague partnerships/goodwill: passes only if quota-holder options and importer deposits are signed; otherwise fails.
- Clinical/legal/patient regulated-data: passes by excluding metered dose inhalers, pharma, medical devices, and patient/health data. Legal/customs conclusions remain out of scope.

Simulated score: **88.6 / 100**

## Kill Conditions

- No quota holder or authorisation manager will sign an option before importer demand is fully secured.
- Importers can reliably buy authorisations directly through existing F-gas consultants or portal matchmaking without timing friction.
- Buyer deposits are unavailable before the founder commits capital.
- Portal acceptance or correction timelines routinely exceed shipment deadlines.
- Cases drift into customs declaration, legal interpretation, F-gas compliance opinions, or importer-of-record activity.
- Average gross margin falls below 55% after referral, reviewer, escrow, and portal-admin costs.
- Fewer than 3 paid importer cases and 1 accepted portal authorisation/delegation occur inside 60 days.
