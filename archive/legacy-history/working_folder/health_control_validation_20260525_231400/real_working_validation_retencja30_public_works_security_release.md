## Reviewer Diagnosis

The evaluator accepted the legal/statutory premise: Polish Public Procurement Law gives a clear 30-day return rule after proper performance and a 15-day rule after warranty/guarantee expiry for retained amounts.

The idea failed because legality was not enough. The reviewer saw this as a sharp recovery service, not a defensible startup. The weak points were the size and findability of the overlooked pool, fee acceptance, repeatability, and defensibility.

## Strongest Objections

- Public records may identify likely targets but often do not show final acceptance, amendments, claims, warranty expiry, or whether money is actually due.
- The best contractors already track large cash securities and guarantees.
- The recoverable pool may concentrate in smaller firms and smaller securities, hurting fee size.
- A 5-9% success fee may look expensive for what becomes a release letter and follow-up.
- Guarantee release is weaker than cash recovery because avoided fees or freed limit feel less tangible.
- The 6-month revenue and file volume target is too aggressive for an unknown part-time founder.
- Mandate control protects one file but is not a category moat.
- Legal edge cases will leak in: defects, setoffs, penalties, claims, insolvency, subcontractor disputes, warranty ambiguity, and FIDIC complexity.
- Authorities may be slow even when the request is valid.
- Bond brokers, accountants, lawyers, and bid consultants can copy the pitch.
