Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

KSeF Unpaid Invoice Release Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:

Polish B2B suppliers now have invoices stuck in buyer accounts-payable queues because KSeF went mandatory in 2026; the startup takes only named unpaid-invoice cases where the buyer has already rejected, held, or conditioned payment on KSeF number, FA(3), QR/visualization, correction, access, PO, or receipt evidence, and gets the invoice accepted, paid, corrected, or cleanly abandoned.

Exact buyer:

Polish SMEs and mid-market B2B suppliers selling goods or services to large Polish buyers, public-sector entities, manufacturing groups, distributors, retail chains, construction groups, logistics firms, and corporate shared-service centers where at least one material invoice is already unpaid because the buyer's AP, procurement, tax, or receiving team has flagged a KSeF-related problem. Best early buyers have 50,000-500,000 PLN invoices, thin cash buffers, limited ERP/KSeF support, and written buyer AP rejection text.

Acute trigger:

A buyer refuses, delays, or conditions payment on a specific invoice because the supplier cannot provide a KSeF number, UPO/acceptance evidence, QR/visualization, two-step access link, FA(3)-compatible corrected invoice, buyer NIP/PO/order mapping, delivery/receipt tie-out, split-payment wording, attachment/reference handling, or a correction path acceptable to the buyer's AP process. The invoice is due or overdue now, and the supplier has buyer-side written evidence that payment will not move until the KSeF issue is resolved.

Control point:

Signed unpaid-invoice release mandate; exact invoice number and value; buyer AP rejection or hold text; original invoice/PDF/XML where available; KSeF number, UPO, rejection/error log, or missing-KSeF diagnosis; PO/order/GRN/WZ/delivery references; buyer NIP and seller NIP; correction history; seller accountant or invoicing-system contact; buyer AP contact; response matrix; and a seller-approved resolution packet that results in AP acceptance, scheduled payment, payment release, corrected invoice submission, buyer withdrawal of the KSeF objection, or written no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Ten screened unpaid-invoice cases and five prepaid release mandates from at least three suppliers. Each accepted case must have a named buyer, invoice value above 50,000 PLN, overdue or at-risk payment, and written AP/KSeF blocker. At least three buyer AP acceptances, scheduled payments, actual payments, corrected KSeF submissions, or clean no-go decisions are obtained. First 45,000-90,000 PLN collected from fixed release fees and small release-linked fees. No case is accepted without seller accountant involvement when tax judgment, correction legality, or VAT treatment is required.

6-month POC:

Handle 80-120 screened invoice blockers, accept 35-55 paid cases, release or resolve 2,000,000-7,000,000 PLN of invoice value, collect 250,000-600,000 PLN in fees, document 25+ AP acceptance/payment/correction/no-go outcomes, build a private issue library by buyer AP wording, KSeF evidence type, FA(3) defect, correction route, ERP/export problem, and response pattern, keep gross margin above 65%, and reject at least 35% of cases where the real issue is commercial dispute, missing delivery, tax/legal uncertainty, fraud, nonexistent service, or buyer cash delay disguised as KSeF.

First acquisition mechanism:

Outbound to Polish suppliers selling to large domestic buyers, especially manufacturing suppliers, construction subcontractors, distributors, logistics providers, facility-service providers, B2B services firms, equipment suppliers, and agencies with invoices above 50,000 PLN. The email asks for one current unpaid invoice where buyer AP has written that KSeF number, UPO, FA(3), QR/visualization, correction, PO reference, NIP, receipt, or access evidence is blocking payment. Secondary channels are accounting offices, ERP implementers, KSeF help desks, factoring brokers, and debt-collection firms that see KSeF exceptions but do not want small urgent AP-resolution work.

Economics:

Charge 4,000-8,000 PLN prepaid for a single invoice release case under 150,000 PLN, 8,000-18,000 PLN for urgent multi-document cases or invoices above 150,000 PLN, and 0.5-1.5% of released or newly scheduled payment value when attribution is clear. A paid no-go is still valuable when the buyer's KSeF objection is false and the real issue is delivery, price, tax, legal, or commercial dispute. No financing, invoice purchase, debt collection, or software build is required.

Copy risk:

Accounting offices, tax advisers, ERP vendors, KSeF software providers, AP automation vendors, shared-service centers, debt collectors, factoring brokers, and buyer AP teams can copy pieces of the workflow. The narrow defense is live unpaid-invoice control: once the seller signs the release mandate, provides the invoice file, AP rejection text, KSeF evidence, accountant contact, correction history, and buyer response thread, another provider must displace an active case under a payment deadline. The longer-term defense is a private library of buyer-specific KSeF rejection wording, acceptable evidence packets, correction routes, and no-go patterns.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

The startup does not own KSeF expertise generally. It controls a specific unpaid invoice case after authorization: invoice value, seller mandate, buyer AP blocker, KSeF/UPO/rejection evidence, correction path, accountant-approved statements, buyer contact thread, and release-linked fee right. Accountants and ERP vendors can solve general KSeF setup, but they do not automatically control the buyer AP thread, the payment-release workflow, or the seller's urgent case mandate for that invoice.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The product is a controlled unpaid-invoice release case. It starts only with a named invoice, written buyer AP blocker, and prepaid mandate. The output is buyer AP acceptance, scheduled payment, actual payment, corrected KSeF submission, withdrawal of the KSeF objection, or written no-go. It is not KSeF readiness consulting, tax advisory, ERP implementation, an invoice dashboard, debt collection, factoring brokerage, or a marketplace for accountants.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

The startup does not provide tax advice, legal advice, VAT opinions, accounting services, debt collection, financing, factoring, invoice purchase, ERP implementation, or official certification of KSeF compliance. The seller remains issuer of record and is responsible for invoice truth, tax treatment, corrections, customer communication, and payment terms. The seller's accountant, tax adviser, ERP vendor, or legal counsel approves correction legality, VAT treatment, and any disputed contractual issue. Cases involving fraud, fake delivery, sanctions, litigation, tax avoidance, or buyer nonpayment unrelated to KSeF are rejected or routed out.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This is not TraceFaktura because TraceFaktura concerned municipal animal-care all-event invoice evidence and a lower-gate payment routing rail. This idea is a Poland-wide 2026 KSeF transition payment-release product for ordinary B2B invoices blocked by structured-invoice acceptance, KSeF number/UPO, FA(3), QR/visualization, correction, and AP matching issues. It is not PromoLeak because it does not recover retailer promotion deductions. It is not DORA, CBAM, Data Act, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, GreenTender, Supplement Stack Safety, or a generic evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Accountants, ERP vendors, tax advisers, and KSeF software providers may already solve many cases. Some buyer AP objections will be excuses for commercial disputes, missing delivery, bad PO matching, buyer cash delay, or disputed work. The strongest pain may be temporary during the 2026 transition. Many SMEs may have small invoices that cannot support case fees. A founder must avoid tax advice and must rely on the seller's accountant for correction legality. Buyer AP teams may ignore an outside coordinator. The offer can decay into low-margin KSeF support unless it stays tied to material unpaid invoices and payment-release outcomes.
