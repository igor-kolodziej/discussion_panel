## Simulated Pass Candidate

### CRA Vulnerability Reporting Workflow Pack - 89

One-sentence thesis: a prepaid product-family case pack for EU/CEE manufacturers of products with digital elements that need an actionable Cyber Resilience Act vulnerability and severe-incident reporting workflow before the 11 September 2026 reporting-obligation date, producing product-family inventory, vulnerability intake, triage roles, buyer-ready evidence, and a dry-run report path without claiming legal compliance.

Buyer: 20-250 person EU/CEE manufacturers and importers of connected or software-enabled products: industrial sensors/controllers, smart-building devices, access-control hardware, small networked appliances, embedded Linux devices, B2B IoT modules, edge gateways, and specialized hardware with firmware/software. Best first buyers have a named product family, EU sales, a distributor/enterprise buyer asking for product-security evidence, or a product owner worried about the September 2026 reporting date.

Acute trigger: a distributor, importer, enterprise buyer, security team, insurer, or board asks how the manufacturer will receive, triage, and report actively exploited vulnerabilities or severe incidents under CRA; or the company realizes September 2026 reporting obligations arrive before the full 2027 regime and has no product-security contact, vulnerability intake, triage owner, product-family inventory, report path, or buyer explanation.

Transferable control point: prepaid product-family case with product list, firmware/software components at a practical level, security contact, vulnerability disclosure/inbox process, triage roles, severity/intake rubric, incident/vulnerability reporting decision tree, ENISA Single Reporting Platform readiness notes, buyer-response packet, and one tabletop or dry-run using a plausible vulnerability scenario. The startup controls the case artifacts and workflow for that named product family.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:

- 3 prepaid product-family cases from at least 2 manufacturers/importers.
- One product-security reviewer or embedded-security contractor on paid per-case terms.
- Product-family inventory and vulnerability intake workflow for at least 10 product families/SKUs.
- Public or buyer-shareable vulnerability contact / disclosure route drafted or implemented for each accepted client.
- At least 3 dry-run scenarios producing role assignments, evidence gaps, and reporting/no-report decision notes.
- At least 2 buyer/progress outcomes: distributor/buyer accepts the response, board signs the process, security team closes the gap, importer receives manufacturer evidence, or a product team assigns owner/budget for remediation.
- First 30,000-90,000 PLN collected in case fees.

First acquisition mechanism: target Polish/CEE connected-product manufacturers, embedded-device OEMs, B2B IoT suppliers, smart-building/access-control vendors, industrial electronics firms, and distributors/importers. Ask only for one named product family with a buyer or internal deadline around CRA reporting, not broad "CRA compliance." Partner outreach goes to product-compliance firms, CE/RED labs, embedded-security testers, ISO/IEC 62443 advisors, and law firms that do not want to staff product-family vulnerability workflow setup.

Gross margin and payback logic: price 8,000-15,000 PLN for a light product-family reporting workflow, 15,000-30,000 PLN for a workflow plus buyer packet and dry-run, and 35,000+ PLN for multi-product batches. Reviewer costs are scoped per case. The buyer pays because the reporting date is near, customer/distributor questions are starting, and full CRA consulting can be too broad or expensive for a small manufacturer.

Copy risk: cyber consultancies, product-security firms, law firms, CE/RED/compliance consultants, SBOM vendors, and internal security teams can do pieces of this. The defense is narrow September-2026 reporting workflow focus, product-family case packaging, fast buyer-response artifacts, CEE manufacturer outreach, and case memory by product type, component pattern, buyer request, and dry-run outcome.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: once the startup controls the prepaid product-family case, product inventory, vulnerability intake route, buyer request, dry-run notes, reviewer findings, and response packet, competitors cannot act on that exact case without the manufacturer switching. The compounding asset is not a template; it is product-category reporting scenarios, buyer wording, triage gaps, and workflow outcomes.

Why this is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: the output is implemented or owner-approved product-family reporting workflow artifacts: vulnerability contact route, triage roles, reporting decision tree, dry-run notes, buyer response, and evidence gaps. It is not a CRA readiness report, legal memo, dashboard, database, marketplace, or brokered expert call.

Reasons this could still fail:

- Buyers may not care until closer to September 2026 or full 2027 obligations.
- Product-security and compliance firms can copy the offer.
- Legal interpretation of CRA scope and reporting obligations is risky.
- Product manufacturers may need deep engineering/SBOM/security work beyond a workflow pack.
- Unknown founder credibility is weak without a named product-security reviewer.
- Some firms may use existing ISO 27001, IEC 62443, CE/RED, or product-security advisors.
- Buyer evidence may not convert into urgent payment without a live procurement or distributor request.
- It can drift into full CRA compliance, conformity assessment, legal advice, or vulnerability management tooling.

Decision: advance this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
