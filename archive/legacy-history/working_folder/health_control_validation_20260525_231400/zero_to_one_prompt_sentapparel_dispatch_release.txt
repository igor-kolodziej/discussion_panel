Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
SENTApparel Dispatch Release Gate

One-sentence thesis:
Polish apparel, footwear, and used-clothing wholesalers need a paid dispatch gate for live shipments after clothing and footwear became SENT-monitored goods in March 2026; the first product controls named shipment queues and releases goods only after the correct PUESC/SENT reference, route, carrier, CN/mass, and update obligations are ready.

Exact buyer:
Polish apparel, footwear, used-clothing, outlet, return-lot, and B2B fashion wholesalers or small fulfillment warehouses that move repeated B2B shipments above SENT clothing/footwear thresholds and do not have mature customs, transport-compliance, or WMS/TMS operations. The first buyer has at least 20 qualifying shipments per month, sells to stores, outlets, wholesale buyers, EU counterparties, or B2B marketplace channels, and has current dispatch friction, carrier refusal, shipment delay, penalty fear, or staff confusion around SENT clothing/footwear obligations.

Acute trigger:
Since 17 March 2026, specified clothing, used clothing, and footwear movements are monitored in SENT above named mass or piece-count thresholds. The buyer has live shipments that cannot safely leave the warehouse until company/representative PUESC permissions, shipment classification, gross mass, route, carrier/vehicle data, SENT reference, geolocation/update responsibility, and closure/update responsibility are clear. The pain is a current dispatch release problem, not abstract compliance readiness.

Control point:
A signed no-release dispatch mandate for named shipment queues: warehouse staff, owner, or dispatch manager agrees that qualifying apparel/footwear loads for selected lanes are routed through the startup before carrier handoff. The startup controls the SENT release checklist, PUESC/SENT authority or customer-granted operator workflow, shipment file, CN/system-code/mass/piece-count evidence, invoice/fulfillment exemption screen, carrier/geolocation handoff, SENT reference/status log, update/closure task list, and dispatch-ready release note for the specific load.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three buyers sign prepaid 60-day dispatch-gate pilots at 4,000-12,000 PLN each plus 150-500 PLN per qualifying shipment after the included quota; each pilot names specific warehouses, lanes, and shipment types; each buyer grants PUESC/SENT operating authority or a supervised customer-side submission workflow; at least 40 live shipments are processed; at least 30 receive a SENT reference or documented no-SENT/no-go decision before carrier handoff; at least two buyers use the gate on repeat shipments; at least 20,000 PLN is collected; no pilot counts unless the buyer agrees that qualifying loads do not leave without the release note.

6-month POC:
Operate 8-15 prepaid dispatch-gate accounts; process 400-1,200 live shipments; collect 180,000-500,000 PLN in setup, monthly, and per-shipment fees; maintain same-day turnaround for evidence-ready loads; document 250+ released shipments, 50+ no-SENT/no-go or correction decisions, and repeated lane patterns; keep gross margin above 60% after a transport-compliance reviewer and backup dispatcher; build a private rules and exception library by product type, CN/system code, lane, carrier handoff, exemption basis, missing-field pattern, PUESC permission issue, and update/closure failure.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of 80 Polish apparel, footwear, used-clothing, outlet, return-lot, and B2B fashion wholesalers from public catalogs, warehouse/job postings, marketplace seller pages, trade directories, import/export listings, and local fashion wholesale clusters. Send a specific dispatch-risk offer: "If your clothing or footwear loads now need SENT handling, I will run your next 10 qualifying shipments through a no-release gate so trucks leave only with the correct reference, route, mass/code evidence, and update/closure responsibilities. Paid pilot only for live dispatches, not training." In parallel, approach small fulfillment warehouses and accountants serving fashion sellers, but only accept cases where the warehouse or seller signs the no-release mandate.

Economics:
Charge 4,000-12,000 PLN for a 60-day pilot covering setup, PUESC/permission workflow, shipment intake template, release log, and the first 10-25 qualifying shipments. After that, charge 150-500 PLN per qualifying shipment or 3,000-15,000 PLN/month for recurring dispatch-gate coverage, depending on volume and urgency. Gross margin should exceed 60% because most work is structured intake, evidence checking, portal workflow, release logging, and exception handling; a transport-compliance reviewer is used for edge cases, not every shipment.

Copy risk:
Transport-compliance consultants, customs brokers, accountants, WMS/TMS vendors, freight forwarders, fulfillment warehouses, and internal dispatch teams can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is controlling live shipment queues before dispatch, building lane/product/carrier exception memory from current loads, becoming the buyer's no-release habit, and owning the release log and repeat patterns before a generic consultant or software vendor is called.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After the buyer signs the no-release dispatch mandate, grants PUESC/SENT authority or supervised submission access, prepays the pilot, and routes named shipment queues through the release gate, the startup controls the active shipment file, release evidence, SENT reference/status log, and carrier handoff for those loads. A consultant or TMS vendor can copy the general idea later, but cannot retroactively control the buyer's current release queue, reference history, lane exception memory, or dispatch habit for the live pilot accounts.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell SENT training, compliance reports, software, a dashboard, monthly audits, brokerage, or introductions. It accepts only live shipment queues where the buyer agrees that qualifying goods do not leave until a release note exists. The core artifact is an operational release gate: shipment intake, PUESC/SENT reference or documented no-SENT/no-go decision, carrier/geolocation handoff, update/closure task list, and release log tied to specific dispatches.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, tax advice, customs classification advice, penalty appeals, transport-law representation, AEO certification, carrier services, warehousing, freight forwarding, or guarantees that an authority will accept a filing. The buyer, carrier, accountant, customs broker, lawyer, tax adviser, and public authorities remain responsible for legal classification, tax treatment, transport obligations, carrier compliance, geolocation, official submissions where required, and appeal decisions. Cases involving disputed classification, suspicious goods, missing invoices, false weights, forged documents, sanctions/customs concerns, penalty disputes, or authority inquiries beyond operational filing support are rejected or escalated.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not CBAM Import Continuity Desk because it is not carbon/importer evidence for CBAM-covered steel or aluminium and does not use a customs-broker branded memo. It is not TraceFaktura because it is not municipal animal-care event invoicing. It is not PromoLeak because it does not recover retailer deductions or promotion leakage. It is not GreenTender because it is not public tender product evidence. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, HeritageDoor, KSeF AP release, CATCH fish import release, EUDR buyer-release evidence, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The problem may normalize quickly after sellers learn PUESC/SENT workflows. Logistics providers, customs brokers, accountants, WMS/TMS vendors, and transport-compliance consultants can copy the process. Many small sellers may be too fragmented or too cheap to pay. Warehouses may not let an outside party control dispatch. Some cases will require legal classification, exemption interpretation, penalty handling, or customs advice outside scope. Per-shipment admin can become low-margin unless the first buyers have repeated B2B loads. Official KAS/PUESC helpdesk materials may reduce willingness to pay for simple cases. The startup must prove that dispatch-gate habit and repeat lane memory create value beyond one-time registration help.
