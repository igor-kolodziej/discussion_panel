## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Consultants with enough paid AI questionnaire tickets to matter may either have no real transferable queue or may keep the profitable work internally.
2. Partners may fear client leakage, embarrassment, liability, or output they cannot stand behind.
3. Many SaaS vendors lack real evidence for no-training terms, retention/logging, RAG source isolation, evals, human review, opt-out, model-change control, incident response, or customer-specific boundaries.
4. GRC and questionnaire tools are automating generic AI answer generation and evidence reuse.
5. Legal and high-risk scope creep remains dangerous, especially around AI Act classification, DPIAs, model validation, health, HR, credit, biometric, law-enforcement, clinical, insurance, and autonomous adverse decisioning use cases.
6. Buyer acceptance remains outside founder control; a polished answer cannot make a weak product enterprise-ready.
7. Ticket economics collapse if each case requires deep product review, legal boundary review, partner meetings, vendor chasing, and custom buyer negotiation.
8. Payment-direction transfer may be harder than subcontracting; partner-guaranteed subcontracting is the easier first structure.
9. Sensitive evidence access requires secure intake, NDAs, deletion policy, and audit trail from day one.
10. The 60-day proof target of eight tickets and 60,000 PLN cleared is aggressive without one unusually strong partner.

- gross margin above 60-65%;
