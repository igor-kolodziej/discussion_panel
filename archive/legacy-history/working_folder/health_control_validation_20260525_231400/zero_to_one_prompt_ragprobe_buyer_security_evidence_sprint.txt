Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RAGProbe Buyer Security Evidence Sprint

One-sentence thesis:
A partner-routed 7-business-day AI security evidence sprint for RAG and agent SaaS vendors whose enterprise buyer asks for prompt-injection, data-leakage, RAG source isolation, tool-permission, logging, or human-review proof before approving a deal or renewal.

Exact buyer:
SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, application-security boutiques, privacy/security questionnaire firms, and AI implementation agencies serving 20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors with non-regulated RAG, AI assistant, knowledge-base, document-processing, support automation, workflow automation, or tool-using agent features. The end SaaS client has a live enterprise buyer questionnaire or security follow-up asking for AI security evidence, not just policy text. The first market excludes credit, insurance, HR, biometrics, clinical, law enforcement, autonomous adverse-decision, and regulated-finance model-risk workflows.

Acute trigger:
The enterprise buyer asks for evidence that the vendor's RAG or agent feature has been tested for prompt injection, cross-tenant/source leakage, sensitive source exposure, unsafe tool calls, excessive permissions, audit-log gaps, missing human review, hallucination/error handling, model-provider retention, or AI incident process. The deal, renewal, pilot expansion, procurement approval, or security review is stalled until the vendor can show bounded test traces and an evidence pack rather than only questionnaire answers.

Control point:
Signed partner-routed test ticket with client authorization, live buyer request, deal value/deadline, product scope, test environment or demo account, synthetic tenant/source setup where needed, RAG corpus/sample documents, tool/action permission map, model/provider settings, logging access, human-review workflow evidence, agreed test limits, no-go/escalation rules, and partner-branded buyer evidence packet. The startup controls the scoped test plan, test traces, evidence room, pass/rework/no-go log, and buyer response for the active procurement event.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three SOC2/fractional-CISO/appsec/AI implementation partners sign routing agreements; eight live RAG/agent security evidence sprints are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 15,000-45,000 PLN each; every sprint has a named buyer, questionnaire or follow-up, deal value/deadline, non-high-risk AI scope, and authorized test environment; at least five sprints produce partner-accepted delivery, buyer-progress outcome, narrowed follow-up, approved lower-risk deployment scope, remediation/no-go decision, or clean out-of-scope escalation; two partners route a second sprint; gross margin stays above 60% after AI security reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Deliver 30-60 partner-routed sprints; collect 600,000-1,800,000 PLN in wholesale, direct-bill, and urgent sprint fees; document 20+ buyer-progress outcomes, 10 remediation/no-go decisions, and 5 accepted lower-risk deployment scopes; get at least five partners to route 5+ sprints; keep turnaround under 7 business days for bounded evidence-ready products; maintain a private taxonomy of prompt-injection patterns, cross-source leakage checks, tool-permission failures, logging gaps, RAG source-isolation defects, human-review defects, buyer follow-up patterns, accepted evidence formats, and no-go triggers.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, appsec boutiques, security-questionnaire firms, and AI implementation agencies that already support AI-enabled B2B SaaS vendors. Pitch: "When a buyer asks for RAG/agent prompt-injection, data-leakage, source-isolation, tool-permission, or logging proof, route the ticket to me white-label. I run bounded tests, produce traces and a buyer-ready evidence packet, and return pass/rework/no-go within seven business days. You keep security/privacy/legal ownership and route remediation or high-risk work separately."

Economics:
Charge 12,000-18,000 PLN wholesale for narrow evidence-ready RAG prompt/source tests, 20,000-35,000 PLN for multi-feature RAG or tool-using agent tests, and 35,000-60,000 PLN for urgent high-value renewal or multi-tenant deployment sprints. Direct-bill SaaS clients 20,000-60,000 PLN where the partner prefers referral. AI security reviewer costs are 5,000-18,000 PLN per sprint depending on scope. The buyer pays because the sprint protects a 100,000+ PLN equivalent deal and produces evidence that a template or policy cannot.

Copy risk:
AI security vendors, application-security firms, penetration testers, red-team providers, GRC platforms, AI governance firms, and internal security teams can copy pieces. The defense is partner-routed live buyer deadlines, fast bounded test recipes for small SaaS RAG/agent products, accepted/rejected buyer evidence memory, white-label workflow, no-go discipline, and a growing trace library of procurement-relevant AI security failures.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the partner signs the routing agreement, the SaaS client authorizes the test, and the startup receives the buyer request, test environment, RAG/source sample, tool-permission map, logging access, and payment guarantee, the startup controls the active test traces and buyer evidence packet for that procurement deadline. A broader appsec firm or AI security platform would need to displace the partner-approved sprint while the buyer review is already waiting.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only partner-routed live buyer blockers requiring RAG/agent security evidence for a specific deal, renewal, or procurement deadline. The output is a procurement evidence artifact with test plan, prompt-injection traces, source-isolation checks, tool-permission tests, log evidence, human-review evidence, pass/rework/no-go findings, and buyer response wording. It is not generic AI security consulting, full red-team service, product remediation, legal compliance, dashboard, database, marketplace, template pack, or brokered intro.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification opinions, DPIAs, high-risk AI conformity assessment, SOC2 certification, ISO 42001 implementation, formal penetration-test certification, model validation, safety certification, product remediation, production incident response, or guarantees of buyer approval. Partners, vendors, lawyers, DPOs, security officers, AI governance specialists, auditors, and enterprise buyers remain responsible for legal compliance, privacy, security, product changes, remediation, and procurement decisions. Cases involving regulated high-risk AI, autonomous adverse decisions, clinical data, credit/insurance/HR decisioning, biometrics, law enforcement, live production sensitive data without proper authorization, or buyer demands for formal certification are declined or routed to qualified firms.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle ICT third-party-risk, Article 28/30 DORA evidence, exit plans, subcontracting, audit rights, or regulated-finance operational resilience. It is not AIGate direct or AIQ Partner Ticket Book because the artifact is bounded RAG/agent security test traces and evidence, not questionnaire answer packaging. It is not the failed AI Model-Risk Renewal Evidence Pack because it excludes regulated-finance model validation and high-risk decisioning. It is not VoiceAI Article 50 because it is not voice-agent deployment transparency. It is not REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BESSGuard, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
AI security and appsec firms are natural incumbents and can offer broader credibility. Some test failures require product remediation, not an evidence packet. Buyers may require formal penetration testing, AI red-team certification, legal AI classification, or model validation outside scope. Sensitive test access, logs, prompts, and source documents create security obligations. Tool-using agents can be risky to test without careful sandboxing. Partner margins may compress if senior AI security reviewers are needed on every sprint. A 7-day sprint can create false assurance unless scope limits are explicit. GRC platforms and AI security tooling may automate common checks over time.
