## Zero To One Summary

The evaluator considered this a good channel-gated commissioning evidence idea, especially if a distributor or warranty team ties the passport to a real economic gate such as warranty fast-track, RMA triage, final margin release, rebate release, installer ranking, replacement eligibility, or preferred-installer status.

The score stayed far below the gate because distributor recognition is the whole business. Without a signed economic gate, the product is only a useful checklist. OEM and distributor portals can also absorb much of the workflow, and BESS commissioning carries serious electrical, fire, grid, firmware, telemetry, and warranty boundaries.

## Strongest Objections

1. Distributor recognition is the whole business; installers will not pay consistently unless the packet unlocks tangible economics.
2. OEMs and distributors may already have commissioning forms, warranty registration flows, monitoring portals, and installer certification programs.
3. BESS commissioning touches fire risk, electrical safety, grid settings, thermal environment, firmware, battery protection, and warranty conditions.
4. Installers may see the packet as bureaucracy or as a tool for distributors to deny warranty.
5. Commercial BESS volume may be too low in the first six months; utility-scale projects already have EPC processes and residential jobs are too small.
6. Reviewer cost can compress margins if each job needs deep electrical/BESS review.
7. The product can drift into commissioning consulting, settings fixes, fire design, EMS configuration, grid-code advice, or engineering liability.
8. Warranty outcomes remain outside founder control.
9. OEMs can copy the fields once known.
10. It can look like a duplicate commissioning-passport concept unless BESS-specific warranty/RMA value is proven.

## Evaluator's Best Beachhead

One commercial BESS distributor or warranty team with 10-50 installer partners and repeated post-commissioning support tickets.

Best first gate:

- priority warranty/RMA triage for submitted packets.

Best first jobs:

- 20-200 kWh systems;
- repeat installer partners;
- distributor-supplied battery/inverter combinations;
- telemetry portal already exists;
- settings/export/firmware evidence can be screenshotted or exported;
- warranty team gives accepted/rework/no-go feedback.

Avoid:

- utility-scale EPC projects;
- purely residential batteries;
- unsafe or disputed installations;
- missing installer authorization;
- fire/code/legal disputes;
- projects where the distributor refuses to define an economic gate.

## POC Pass/Fail Suggested By Evaluator

Continue only if within 60-90 days:

- one distributor signs a written recognition pilot;
- five jobs are prepaid by installers;
- three packets are accepted/fast-tracked or returned with specific rework;
- one installer submits a second job;
- reviewer cost stays below 35% of packet price;
- distributor confirms reduced review/support friction.

Even if that proof succeeds, the evaluator estimated the score rises only to 80-82.

## Lesson

Distributor/warranty gate concepts fail below the current real gate unless the founder already controls a signed economic gate or has unfair channel access. Do not continue commissioning-passport variants unless the control point is materially stronger than a hoped-for recognition pilot.
