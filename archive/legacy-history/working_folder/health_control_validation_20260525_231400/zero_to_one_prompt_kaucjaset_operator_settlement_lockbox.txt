Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
KaucjaSet Operator Settlement Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Control named Polish deposit-return-system retailer settlement batches where a collection point has operator agreements, return-count evidence, consumer refund records, and an operator settlement path, then recover or route operator reimbursement and handling-fee cash under signed payment-direction or mandate terms.

Exact buyer:
Small retail chains, franchisees, convenience-store groups, petrol-station operators, food-service collection points, venue operators, and independent retailers that collect deposit packaging under Poland's deposit-return system and must reconcile consumer deposit refunds, collection counts, operator statements, handling fees, and operator reimbursements. The payor is the deposit-system operator or operator-side settlement process. The first buyer is not a large supermarket chain with internal systems; it is a multi-site retailer or collection point operator whose cash is tied up or mismatched across several locations.

Acute trigger:
Poland's deposit-return system has been live since 1 October 2025 and has been scaling through 2026. Retailers and collection points must handle customer deposit refunds, return counts, operator agreements, packaging eligibility, collection logistics, and settlement with one or more operators. In the transition period, mismatched counts, delayed reimbursements, missing handling fees, portal errors, bag/RVM/POS discrepancies, operator statement gaps, or cash-float stress can create immediate accounting and working-capital pain.

Control point:
A signed settlement mandate or payment-direction agreement over a named operator settlement batch, including retailer authorization, operator agreement copy, site list, return-point IDs, packaging-count logs, bag seal or RVM evidence, POS/refund records, collection pickup evidence, operator portal/statement exports, accepted/rejected line items, handling-fee terms, bank/payment details, and no-go rules for disputed or fraud-risk batches. The startup controls the current settlement file and fee right for that batch. The retailer, operator, accountant, POS/RVM provider, and legal advisers remain responsible for statutory duties, accounting, tax, operator contracts, and system participation.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, the founder signs 10-20 retail sites under one to three owners or franchisees; receives operator agreements, return logs, refund/payment records, and portal/statement access or exports for current settlement batches; obtains written retailer authorization and fee terms; receives written operator acknowledgement, case IDs, corrected settlement statement, payment-direction acceptance, or settlement-status confirmation for at least three batches; and collects at least 25,000 PLN in operator-paid cash, retailer-paid recovery fees, setup fees, or escrowed settlement funds before scaling. No case is counted without a named operator, named site, dated return logs, and a cash settlement consequence.

6-month POC:
Control 80-150 active retail/collection sites across 5-10 owners or franchisees; process 250+ named settlement batches; recover or route 300,000-1,200,000 PLN of operator reimbursements, handling-fee corrections, or delayed deposit cash; collect 100,000-350,000 PLN in setup fees, batch fees, and success fees; document operator-specific mismatch patterns, accepted evidence, rejected evidence, portal issues, RVM/POS count gaps, bag/pickup problems, and payment timing; keep gross margin above 60%; and keep rejected/fraud/no-go batches below 25% of accepted intake.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with retail accountants, franchisee associations, petrol-station/accounting groups, POS/RVM service providers, and convenience-store operators that already hear settlement complaints. The first pitch is concrete: "If operator cash or handling fees are stuck or mismatched for your DRS return points, I take one current settlement batch, map the operator statement to your return/refund records, assemble the accepted proof file, and work only under written site authorization and payment terms. No generic DRS readiness, no VAT advice, no POS implementation."

Economics:
Charge a 1,500-4,000 PLN setup fee per owner or cluster, 200-600 PLN per named settlement batch, and 5-12% of recovered or accelerated operator cash where contractually allowed and where the retailer approves the fee. Larger multi-site batches can be priced at 5,000-20,000 PLN. The buyer pays because deposit refunds and handling fees create cash leakage, working-capital stress, accountant time, and owner frustration across multiple sites. The startup uses the 100,000 PLN budget for secure document handling, accountant/reconciliation support, legal/payment-direction templates, and contractor admin, not for speculative advances.

Copy risk:
Deposit-system operators, retail accountants, POS/RVM vendors, franchise headquarters, retail consultants, and EPR/compliance firms can copy pieces. The defense is not generic DRS knowledge. It is control of exact retailer settlement batches, operator-specific accepted/rejected evidence memory, payment-direction or mandate terms, and repeat owner/franchisee channels before portals and operators fully standardize the messy transition cases.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the retailer signs the settlement mandate, grants access to the current batch evidence, and the operator acknowledges the case or payment direction for that batch, a competitor cannot collect the fee right or route that exact operator payment without displacing the signed mandate. They can handle future batches, but they cannot retroactively control the already-authorized site list, return logs, operator case IDs, settlement statements, and payment path.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell DRS readiness, compliance reports, dashboards, POS software, operator matching, packaging-registration advice, VAT advice, or marketplace introductions. It accepts only named current settlement batches with retailer authorization, operator records, return/refund evidence, and a cash consequence. The proof is operator cash recovered, corrected, or routed, not a report.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not become a deposit-system operator, retailer of record, POS provider, accountant, tax adviser, legal adviser, consumer-refund provider, payment institution, or waste/logistics operator. Operators remain responsible for operator duties and settlements. Retailers remain responsible for consumer refunds, accounting, VAT/tax treatment, site obligations, and operator contracts. Accountants and lawyers handle tax/legal questions. The startup rejects batches involving suspected fake returns, duplicated counts, stolen packaging, missing consumer-refund records, operator-contract breach, unclear site authority, disputed packaging eligibility, or pressure to alter records. Any custody of funds uses a lawful escrow, direct operator-to-retailer payment, or contractually reviewed payment path.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not TraceFaktura because it is not a municipal animal-care invoice ledger; the payor is a deposit-system operator and the artifact is a retailer settlement batch. It is not CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, Data Act, GreenTender, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, PromoLeak, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, 3PL recovery, OTA recovery, PSP reserve release, BDO DPR/EDPR allocation, UCO route aggregation, or a generic evidence desk. The control object is current operator settlement cash for named deposit-return collection points.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Operators may prohibit assignment, third-party payment direction, or outside settlement chasing. Large retailers and franchise systems may solve the problem internally. POS/RVM vendors and accountants may own the evidence and relationship. Batch values may be too small to support fees. The transition pain may fade as operator portals stabilize. Count disputes, fake returns, rejected packaging, missing bag seals, RVM errors, consumer refund gaps, and site fraud can destroy trust. Handling funds could create payment-service, escrow, AML, tax, and accounting complexity. The startup may become a low-margin reconciliation clerk unless it controls repeat multi-site owners and only accepts batches with clear operator cash consequences.
