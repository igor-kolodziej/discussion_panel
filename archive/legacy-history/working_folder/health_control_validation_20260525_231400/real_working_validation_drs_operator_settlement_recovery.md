## Zero To One Summary

The evaluator accepted that this is a real local launch-window cash-recovery wedge. It liked that the idea targets current DRS settlement variance rather than readiness, training, product labels, or generic consulting. It also accepted the signed mandate over named outlet-months, POS/RVM/manual-return evidence, operator statements, pickup notes, bank credits, and filed discrepancy register as a credible control point.

## Strongest Objections Captured

1. The recoverable pool may be smaller than apparent variance.
2. Retailer-side process errors may dominate: POS setup, cashier mistakes, wrong EAN mapping, voucher timing, incomplete pickup notes, or manual-return leakage.
3. Operators control accepted evidence standards, and standards may differ across operators.
4. POS integrators, RVM vendors, accounting bureaus, and retail back-office consultants are natural competitors.
5. Success-fee attribution will be disputed without exact filed dates, baseline statements, acknowledgements, corrected statements, credit memos, bank credits, offsets, or written no-go triggers.
6. The proposed 60-day proof was too aggressive for a part-time founder.
7. Monthly close windows create concentrated execution pressure.
8. Large chains may internalize after launch chaos.
9. Operators may improve portals/support and reduce the need for third-party recovery.
10. Scope creep into POS setup, cash variance, tax/accounting, contract interpretation, or legal disputes is likely.

## Lesson

Even a current statutory settlement system and success-fee recovery mandate are not enough if the recoverable pool is unproven and the natural data owners can absorb the workflow. Future candidates need clearer evidence that the signed mandate itself moves cash or controls a scarce payment stream that incumbents cannot normalize away.
