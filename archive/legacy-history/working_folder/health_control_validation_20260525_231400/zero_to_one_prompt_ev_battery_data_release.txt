Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EV Battery Data Release Desk.

One-sentence thesis:
Used EV dealers, fleet owners, leasing companies, and remarketing channels will pay for VIN-specific battery-data release files that convert owner-authorized connected-vehicle data, independent battery scans, warranty facts, and buyer objections into a trade-in, lease-end, auction, or resale price decision.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE used EV dealers, fleet lessors, SME fleet owners, leasing-return teams, fleet remarketers, auction channels, and independent EV workshops serving dealers. The first beachhead is dealers or fleet remarketers handling 10-80 used EVs at a time where buyer discounts, auction reserves, lease-end charges, or trade-in prices are materially affected by unknown battery state.

Acute trigger:
A used EV sale, fleet disposal, lease-end settlement, auction reserve, trade-in, warranty-transfer decision, or dealer purchase is stalled or discounted because the buyer, lessor, auction, or dealer cannot see credible battery state-of-health, warranty status, charging pattern, fault history, BMS summary, recall status, and supporting OEM/app/connected-product data for the named VINs.

Control point:
The startup accepts only VIN-level or batch-level cases where the lawful vehicle user, owner, lessor, or fleet operator signs a data-access and sharing mandate; the dealer or remarketer signs a paid case; the exact sale, lease-end, auction, trade-in, or purchase decision is documented; and an independent EV workshop or battery reviewer performs the scan/test layer. The controlled artifact is the VIN-specific battery data file: owner mandate, OEM/app/connected-data request trail, diagnostic scan or test summary, warranty/recall status, charging and battery-history evidence where lawfully available, exception log, buyer response wording, and no-go notes.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, close two prepaid dealer or fleet batches above 8,000 PLN each, covering at least 20 VINs total. Each batch must include lawful owner/user/lessor data mandates, VIN list, sale/lease/auction decision context, independent scan/test appointment, OEM/app/connected-data export or request trail, battery/warranty/recall evidence index, and one written buyer, auction, lessor, or dealer response showing price uplift, avoided haircut, accepted evidence, rework request, or clean no-go.

6-month POC:
Process 120-250 VINs across 8-15 paid batches from at least four dealers, fleet owners, leasing teams, or remarketing channels. Produce at least 40 buyer/auction/lease-end responses, including accepted files, price adjustments, avoided discounts, rework requests, or clean no-go outcomes. At least three channels must submit a second batch. Gross margin must stay above 60% after EV workshop scans, battery reviewer time, data-handling costs, and customer support.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of 80 Polish/CEE used EV dealers, fleet remarketers, leasing-return managers, EV-focused workshops, auction operators, and SME fleet owners. Outreach asks for one current batch where battery uncertainty is causing a price haircut, lease-end dispute, auction reserve problem, or buyer due-diligence hold. The first offer is a prepaid 5-VIN pilot for 4,000-8,000 PLN or a 20-VIN batch for 12,000-30,000 PLN, with no free certificates and no promise of price uplift.

Economics:
Pricing starts at 600-2,000 PLN per VIN depending on evidence depth, or 8,000-30,000 PLN per batch. Cost per VIN should remain 150-700 PLN for contractor scan/test time, data handling, reviewer spot checks, and pack assembly. The fee is justified only where battery uncertainty can move resale, auction, lease-end, warranty-transfer, or purchase value by several thousand PLN per car, or where a fleet batch has enough aggregate value to justify a fixed fee.

Copy risk:
Battery-certificate providers, OEM dealers, inspection networks, used-car appraisers, auction houses, leasing companies, and EV workshops can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is not generic battery testing. It is repeat VIN-level case memory across OEM data exports, Data Act request wording, buyer objections, auction/lease-end responses, battery-scan formats, warranty edge cases, and batch pricing outcomes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder has the lawful owner/user mandate, VIN batch, exact sale or lease-end decision, independent scan appointment, OEM/app data-request trail, and buyer/auction/lessor objection for that batch, a competitor cannot control those same VIN-level evidence files before the payment decision. Incumbents can compete for future batches, but not the current signed batch and buyer response loop after the founder controls it.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell generic used-car inspection, a dashboard, a battery certificate marketplace, EV advice, legal claims, or buyer introductions. It accepts only named VINs tied to a current sale, lease-end, auction, trade-in, warranty-transfer, or purchase decision. The output is a buyer-usable payment artifact: data-access trail, diagnostic/battery evidence index, warranty/recall evidence, exception log, buyer response wording, and accepted/rework/no-go status.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify battery safety, guarantee battery state-of-health, make warranty decisions, repair vehicles, access unauthorized vehicle accounts, bypass OEM systems, handle unrelated personal trip/location data, provide legal advice, or represent consumers in disputes. Vehicle owners/users/lessors approve every data request and every data-sharing event. Independent EV workshops perform physical scans/tests. Personal data is minimized, redacted, and excluded unless directly necessary for the documented sale or lease-end decision.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from BatteryFit Retrofit Pack because it does not advise PV installers on home-battery retrofit sales; it handles used EV VIN-level battery evidence tied to resale, lease-end, trade-in, auction, and warranty-transfer payment events. It is distinct from Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk because the buyer, device class, payment event, proof artifact, and acquisition channel differ: used EV dealers/fleets and VIN batches, not compressed-air service companies or industrial energy auditors seeking machine data for service/tender work. It is distinct from HeatQuiet, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, GreenTender, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, Supplement Stack, and all health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Existing battery-certificate vendors, EV workshops, OEM dealers, appraisers, leasing companies, and auction houses may already solve the key buyer concern. Data Act requests may be slow, incomplete, or contested, while OBD/diagnostic scans may be enough without a startup. Dealers may resist paying above ordinary inspection costs unless a batch clearly changes price. Connected-vehicle data can include personal location/trip data and must be minimized. The founder has weak automotive credibility without named EV workshop partners. Some cases will produce bad news rather than price uplift. Fleet remarketers and auctions may internalize the workflow if it proves valuable.
