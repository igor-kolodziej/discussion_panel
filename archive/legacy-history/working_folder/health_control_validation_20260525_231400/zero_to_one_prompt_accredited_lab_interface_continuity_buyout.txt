Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AccreditedLab Interface Continuity Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or option the installed support books of retiring micro-vendors who maintain production instrument-export parsers, certificate-template generators, and LIMS upload scripts for non-clinical ISO/IEC 17025 calibration, metrology, materials, NDT, and manufacturing QC labs, then convert the installed base into prepaid continuity contracts before seller payout.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized non-clinical accredited or accreditation-seeking labs: industrial calibration labs, metrology labs, materials testing labs, NDT labs, heat-treatment and coating labs, manufacturing QC labs, construction-materials labs, and internal factory labs that rely on old instrument-export parsers, Excel/VBA macros, Access databases, serial-port collectors, certificate-template generators, or LIMS upload scripts maintained by a retiring micro-vendor, ex-lab engineer, instrument-service technician, or local lab-IT freelancer.

Acute trigger:
The script owner is retiring, ill, overloaded, closing a side business, or no longer willing to support old lab automation. A Windows upgrade, instrument PC replacement, LIMS change, auditor request, customer certificate deadline, customer test-report format change, or broken parser threatens certificate delivery, customer report delivery, audit evidence, or production release paperwork. The buyer does not need a new LIMS project; it needs continuity over installed scripts and mappings that already work in production.

Control point:
Signed seller option over the support book, including source code, compiled scripts where source is unavailable, parser mappings, sample instrument-export files, validated output examples, customer-specific certificate/report templates, LIMS upload formats, issue history, customer contacts, renewal calendar, support inbox, documentation, passwords or access paths to be rotated, vendor/instrument notes, pricing history, and seller transition support. Each customer must approve the successor support owner, data-processing terms, access rotation, and payment direction before seller payout. The lab remains owner of its measurement process and provider of record for all reports and certificates.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One signed option over a named retiring micro-vendor book covering at least 8 live lab customers and 15 production parsers/templates/scripts. At least 8 customer-approved successor support transfers. At least 35,000 PLN collected in prepaid 6-12 month continuity contracts before seller payout. Clean source/config custody for at least 10 production dependencies, with backups and access rotation completed. One live support ticket resolved inside 60 days without changing measurement meaning, certificate validity, accreditation scope, or customer conformity decisions. Contractor review confirms the inherited scripts are maintainable enough to support under fixed boundaries.

6-month POC:
Acquire or option 1-2 support books covering 15-30 non-clinical lab customers and 40-80 production dependencies. Convert at least 15 labs into prepaid continuity contracts. Collect 150,000-450,000 PLN in continuity retainers, emergency fixes, and bounded upgrade tickets. Resolve 15-30 support tickets with a reusable issue taxonomy by instrument type, export format, parser, certificate template, LIMS target, Windows/runtime issue, and customer approval path. Keep gross margin above 60% after contractor review, secure storage, insurance, legal/data terms, and seller earnout. Prove average base-support time below 90 minutes per lab per month and separate all new custom projects into paid change orders.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target retiring lab-IT freelancers, ex-service engineers, metrology programmers, small LIMS/instrument integration shops, instrument-service technicians, and one-person vendors whose names appear in lab job posts, calibration/metrology forums, Polish industrial lab networks, LinkedIn, old websites, GitHub snippets, local chamber directories, and referrals from ISO 17025 consultants. Opening seller pitch: "I buy or option tiny lab-automation support books where you still get calls about old parsers, macros, certificate templates, or LIMS uploads. You get an earnout only after customers approve transfer and prepaid continuity cash clears, so there is no risky big upfront sale."

Economics:
Customer continuity contract: 6,000-18,000 PLN per lab per year for bounded support over named scripts, templates, parsers, and upload formats. Emergency continuity ticket: 2,000-8,000 PLN. Bounded upgrade or migration ticket: 8,000-35,000 PLN when a lab changes instrument PC, Windows runtime, report template, or LIMS import format. Seller payout is mostly earnout from retained gross margin, with small option fees only. Contractor review and fixes are paid per dependency or ticket. Payback is credible because a broken parser or certificate generator can delay customer reports, audit responses, production release paperwork, or paid testing jobs, while full LIMS replacement is much slower and more expensive.

Copy risk:
LIMS vendors, instrument vendors, lab IT consultants, metrology programmers, freelancers, and industrial automation firms can replace or rebuild pieces. The defense is not generic coding skill. It is acquired custody of exact installed scripts, historical export files, customer-specific templates, issue history, customer approvals, access rotation, and prepaid continuity cash before a competitor can inspect or replace the dependency.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller option is signed and customers approve transfer, the founder controls the inherited source/config bundle, support inbox, issue history, renewal calendar, customer payment direction, and seller handoff. A LIMS vendor or freelancer can compete for a future replacement project, but cannot instantly copy the exact old parser mappings, test files, customer templates, support history, and current payment transfer without the seller and customer approvals already secured.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup is not selling new lab software, a LIMS dashboard, a code marketplace, generic lab consulting, calibration coordination, or audit reports. It buys or options an installed continuity book and converts it into paid support contracts over named production dependencies. The controlled artifact is a transferred support/payment stream tied to actual scripts, mappings, templates, test files, and customer approvals.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No clinical or patient data, no HL7/LIS diagnostic-result delivery, no medical-device interpretation, no calibration certificate issuance, no accreditation claims, no measurement-uncertainty decisions, no conformity-assessment decisions, no method validation claims, and no alteration of historic certificates. The lab remains responsible for its measurement process, method validation, accreditation scope, certificates, reports, uncertainty budgets, and customer conformity decisions. The startup only maintains data extraction, formatting, upload, backup, and continuity under lab approval. Cases involving GMP/pharma CSV, medical diagnostics, patient/specimen data, regulated clinical workflows, unclear IP, unsafe access, or scripts that change measurement meaning are rejected or routed to qualified specialists.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, or the prior clinical Lab Interface Continuity Buyout. This is non-clinical industrial/accredited-lab production script continuity acquired from a retiring support-book owner. The control point is source/config/customer-payment transfer for installed parsers and certificate/report templates, not regulatory evidence response, health diligence, clinical interfaces, generic calibration renewal admin, or new software development.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The right seller may be scarce: clean enough code, enough active labs, clear IP, transferable support history, and willingness to do an earnout may not appear quickly. Customer consent may be hard because labs may treat the handoff as a reason to rebuild, move to a LIMS vendor, or ask the original instrument vendor. Old scripts may be brittle, undocumented, unsafe, or impossible to support profitably. Labs may demand custom development, method changes, validation support, accreditation language, or report-format responsibility outside scope. LIMS and instrument vendors can use the continuity event to sell replacements. A solo founder needs credible contractors from day one. If support exceeds the fixed contract or source ownership is unclear, the book becomes a low-margin custom IT helpdesk.
