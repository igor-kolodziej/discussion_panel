Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality.

Scored idea:

Calibration Renewal-Book Buyout buys or options the recurring calibration reminder, certificate, and renewal-invoice book of a retiring small calibration broker, lab-equipment service coordinator, or micro-service company serving Polish molecular, genetic, IVF, diagnostic, biotech, and research labs.

It is not a lab, not a calibration laboratory, not a medical-device manufacturer, not a LIMS, not a diagnostic provider, not a marketplace, and not a report/dashboard business. The founder does not perform calibration unless separately accredited and qualified. Calibration execution is routed through PCA-accredited ISO/IEC 17025 calibration laboratories or existing qualified calibration partners. The startup controls the customer renewal flow, due-date calendar, certificate archive, phone/domain/mailbox, routing, and prepaid renewal cash.

Target seller:
- retiring or overloaded one-person/small-team calibration broker, lab-equipment service coordinator, equipment-maintenance micro-company, or ex-service engineer;
- serves small private molecular/genetic/IVF/diagnostic/research labs;
- owns or can transfer customer contacts, due-date calendar, certificate archive, reminder workflow, phone/domain/mailbox, renewal templates, blanket POs where applicable, recurring invoice rights, and seller handoff;
- has recurring calibration demand but underprices admin, follow-up, reminders, bundling, route planning, and renewal collection;
- is tired of chasing renewals, scanning certificates, coordinating accredited providers, or answering small lab admin calls.

Buyer:
- private molecular/genetic diagnostic labs;
- IVF labs;
- pathology and diagnostic labs;
- biotech/CRO/research labs;
- small lab groups with recurring equipment calibration obligations and audit/certificate pressure.

Acute trigger:
Labs need current calibration certificates for QA, audits, accreditation, SOP compliance, customer requirements, and reliable equipment operation. Missing due dates or certificate archives creates audit stress, delayed work, repeated admin calls, rushed calibration, or nonconformance risk. The retiring broker/service coordinator already owns the renewal habit and certificate history; without a handoff, labs must rebuild due-date tracking or find a new coordinator.

Control point:
The acquired asset is the recurring renewal workflow:
- due-date calendar for pipettes, balances, weights, thermometers, temperature loggers, incubators, freezers, centrifuges, timers, hygrometers, and other lab equipment;
- certificate archive and prior service history;
- customer contacts and lab equipment lists;
- phone/domain/mailbox and reminder workflow;
- renewal templates, price lists, route/batch notes, supplier notes, and customer-specific requirements;
- blanket POs or recurring invoice rights where applicable;
- seller-mediated customer handoff;
- capacity/routing agreement with accredited calibration providers;
- prepaid renewal/transition cash collected before close.

A competitor can call labs and offer calibration coordination, but cannot instantly copy the due-date calendar, certificate archive, seller trust, equipment lists, customer-specific requirements, renewal timing, phone/domain continuity, and prepaid renewal flow.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option fee to the seller;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN legal/assignment/customer-transfer review;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN certificate/archive cleanup, CRM import, phone/domain/mailbox transfer, reminder workflow, and customer notice;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN operational float for first calibration batch deposits, couriers, route logistics, packaging, and admin;
- closing payment only after prepaid renewal cash clears, with remaining seller consideration structured as earnout from retained customers.

Required POC proof:
1. One signed option/acquisition agreement over a named calibration renewal book, covering due-date calendar, certificate archive, customer list, phone/domain/mailbox, reminder workflow, blanket POs/recurring invoice rights where transferable, supplier notes, and seller transition support.
2. Clean transfer review: no customer-data blocker, no non-transferable contracts that kill the deal, no claim that the founder is an accredited calibration lab unless true, and no certificate alteration.
3. At least 15 customer renewal commitments or prepaid batch commitments before close.
4. At least 25,000 PLN collected prepaid renewal/transition cash before close.
5. At least one signed capacity/routing agreement or written cooperation confirmation with a PCA-accredited ISO/IEC 17025 calibration laboratory or existing qualified calibration provider.
6. One completed calibration batch inside 60 days, with certificates issued by the accredited/qualified provider of record and customer cash collected.
7. Explicit boundary: founder handles renewal administration, routing, reminders, certificate archive, packaging/logistics, and customer service; accredited/qualified providers perform calibration and issue certificates.

Economics:
The business is acquisition-led and renewal-led. The first customers are inherited through seller handoff. The first cash comes from prepaid renewal/batch commitments before closing. Labs pay because calibration due dates recur, missing certificates create audit stress, and switching/rebuilding certificate history is annoying.

Initial pricing logic:
- markup or coordination fee on calibration batches routed to accredited/qualified providers;
- annual certificate archive and due-date management fee;
- paid rush coordination for overdue or audit-triggered calibration;
- bundle discount for multi-instrument batches;
- seller earnout paid only from retained customer gross margin.

Gross-margin logic:
The startup should not own expensive calibration equipment at first. It earns margin from renewal capture, routing, batch aggregation, admin, certificate archive, and customer continuity while accredited/qualified providers perform calibration. Payback comes from prepaid renewals before closing and recurring annual due dates.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The asset is an acquired customer renewal/payment flow: due-date calendar, certificate archive, phone/domain/mailbox, customer equipment lists, seller handoff, recurring invoice rights, and prepaid renewal commitments. The startup is not selling a generic dashboard or marketplace listing; it owns the renewal moment and routes the work through qualified providers.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- Do not claim PCA accreditation or ISO/IEC 17025 calibration status unless the operating entity actually has it.
- Do not alter calibration certificates or misrepresent the provider of record.
- Use PCA-accredited ISO/IEC 17025 providers where accreditation is required or valued.
- Keep the first acquisition away from patient data, diagnostic interpretation, clinical software, or medical-device claims.
- Store only customer/equipment/admin data needed for renewal and certificate archive operations.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Customers may view the broker as replaceable if the due-date/certificate archive is weak.
- The seller may not actually own transferable customer flow, only goodwill.
- Accredited providers may not give good pricing/capacity or may prefer to sell direct.
- Margins may compress if the startup is only a pass-through coordinator.
- Some labs may already use LIMS/QMS/calendar systems for reminders.
- Certificate accuracy and provider-of-record clarity are reputation-critical.
- If customers will not prepay renewals, the acquired book is weak.
