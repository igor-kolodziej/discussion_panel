Created: 2026-05-27

## Pivot From Round 83

BDO e-DPR Allocation Lockbox failed working-chat at `62 / 100`. The real objection was not demand; it was trust, legal/payment custody, fraud sensitivity, and incumbent control in a regulated document market.

Round 84 keeps the hard-control discipline but moves into lower-regulatory micro-operator succession where the founder can control:

- an active customer book;
- a physical address, mailbox, route, account, or administrative custody asset;
- customer-approved payment transfer;
- current-month cash before acquisition close.

## Current Timing Anchor

Poland's e-Doreczenia obligation is active for KRS companies and staged for CEIDG businesses:

- Gov.pl says non-public KRS entities registered before 2025 have e-Doreczenia obligations from 2025-04-01, while CEIDG entities registered before 2025 have a 2026-10-01 deadline unless they make qualifying changes earlier.
- Gov.pl FAQ states that the obligation to have an electronic delivery address applies to every entity in KRS entrepreneurs' register and CEIDG, and that KRS entities registered before 2025 have the obligation from 2025-04-01.
- e-Doreczenia can update business registered/correspondence address details, and companies can change providers through a formal process.

## Lead Candidate: KRS Address + e-Doreczenia Continuity Book Buyout

### Buyer

Existing customers of small Polish registered-office / virtual-office / mail-custody providers:

- micro sp. z o.o. companies;
- foreign-owned Polish companies;
- foundations and associations with business activity;
- dormant-but-active KRS entities;
- small agencies, importers, consultants, and ecommerce sellers using a rented registered office and mail handling address.

### Acute Trigger

The current provider is retiring, losing the lease, closing the coworking/mailbox location, or reducing mail handling while official correspondence has become more digital and more deadline-sensitive. KRS companies already need e-Doreczenia addresses, and CEIDG businesses face the 2026-10-01 deadline unless earlier changes triggered the obligation.

For the customer, failure is not abstract: missed tax, court, ZUS, KRS, city-office, bailiff, or contract mail can create real deadlines, default assumptions, fines, blocked filings, or panic work with accountants/lawyers.

### Transferable Control Point

Control exists only when the founder has:

- a conditional option or acquisition agreement over the provider's active address/mail-custody book;
- lease assignment, sublease, landlord consent, or new equivalent physical address capacity;
- seller-endorsed customer transfer notice;
- signed customer re-consent to the founder/new entity as address/mail-custody provider;
- direct payment transfer to the founder/new entity;
- handoff of mailbox IDs, mail logs, forwarding instructions, scan rules, phone/domain/email, service templates, and unresolved mail cases;
- e-Doreczenia onboarding/notification workflow where the company remains account/controller and the founder acts only under lawful authorisation.

The startup does not become an accountant, lawyer, e-Doreczenia qualified provider, or government-delivery operator.

### Sixty-Day Proof

Within 60 days and under 100,000 PLN:

- contact 80-120 small virtual-office, coworking, secretarial, and accounting-side address providers;
- inspect 5 books and reject any without clean customer contracts, mail logs, address rights, and current payments;
- sign one option with a provider for a small option fee and earnout only on retained customers;
- secure the address lease/sublease/landlord or equivalent address continuity before customer notices;
- seller sends transfer notice to 60-150 active customers;
- at least 40 customers sign new service terms and payment transfer;
- collect at least 25,000 PLN prepaid setup/continuity/first-period cash before meaningful seller close;
- complete 20 live mail/e-Doreczenia onboarding or continuity actions;
- close only on customers with active re-consent, payment, and service boundaries.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Seller pitch:

"You have registered-address clients and mail duties you no longer want. I will not buy the book blind. You introduce only active clients. You get an earnout on customers who re-consent and prepay, and no value is paid for stale names."

Customer pitch:

"Your registered-office/mail provider is changing. We keep the address/mail handoff orderly, move payment and forwarding instructions cleanly, and help you check e-Doreczenia readiness without taking over legal/accounting decisions."

### Economics

Revenue:

- 99-299 PLN/month per active company depending on mail volume, scan frequency, forwarding, and address city;
- 199-799 PLN one-time transfer/onboarding fee;
- 100-500 PLN pass-through or marked-up KRS/CEIDG/e-Doreczenia readiness admin support only where lawful and scoped;
- optional higher-tier 499-899 PLN/month for foreign-owned or high-mail customers needing bilingual forwarding and accountant/lawyer coordination.

The proof must show:

- 25,000+ PLN cash collected before close;
- gross margin above 55% after rent/address cost, mail scanning, forwarding, payment fees, seller earnout, and admin time;
- less than 15 minutes/month average handling time for ordinary low-mail accounts;
- no open-ended legal/accounting support included in base service.

### Copy-Risk Defense

Virtual-office incumbents can market to the same general customer type. They cannot copy a seller-endorsed transfer of a specific address book after customers re-consent, payment is redirected, mail logs are handed over, and the physical address/right is controlled. The book is sticky because changing a registered office can trigger KRS/CEIDG updates, bank/vendor/accountant updates, and missed-correspondence risk.

### Why It Is Not A Service, Report, App, Dashboard, Marketplace, Or Generic Broker

The startup buys or options an operating book only after customer payment transfer. It controls the physical address/mail custody workflow and current customer cash. It does not sell a guide, SaaS dashboard, lead list, e-Doreczenia setup checklist, or legal advice.

### Safety And Legal Boundaries

- Customers remain responsible for KRS/CEIDG, tax, ZUS, court, contractual, and e-Doreczenia decisions.
- Accountants/lawyers handle filings or legal advice.
- The founder handles address/mail custody and administrative continuity under written authorization and DPA where needed.
- The founder does not read legal/tax mail beyond contracted scan/index/forwarding instructions.
- No e-Doreczenia login, provider change, or mailbox administration occurs without lawful company authorization and provider-compliant process.
- KYC/AML-style screening rejects suspicious shell/fraud customers and customers seeking concealment.

### Duplicate-Risk Check

- Not DORA: no ICT supplier evidence pack.
- Not REDBlocked: no Amazon account-health evidence.
- Not BioSignal: no health or investor diligence.
- Not TraceFaktura: no municipal event invoice ledger.
- Not HeritageDoor: no physical salvage inventory.
- Not KSeF/e-Delivery Accounting Book Succession Lockbox: this does not acquire accounting work, bookkeeping liability, invoice workflows, or tax filings. It is a registered-address/mail-custody book with e-Doreczenia readiness as a bounded admin layer.

## Internal Objections Before Real Gate

- This can look like ordinary virtual-office roll-up if the address lease and customer payment transfer are not locked before close.
- MRR may be too low if the book has low-mail dormant companies paying cheap commodity rates.
- e-Doreczenia can reduce physical-mail pain for some customers, although it also creates readiness and notification anxiety.
- The seller pipeline may be thinner than expected; good providers may not sell, and bad providers may have low-quality customers.
- KYC/reputation matters: the founder must avoid becoming a mailbox for suspicious entities.
- Incumbent virtual-office providers can copy the general pitch, though not the exact transferred book.
