Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
PostLock CNC Post-Processor Continuity Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Acquire or option retiring CNC post-processor and machine-macro maintainers only when existing machine-shop customers approve the support/payee transfer and prepay current continuity before seller payout.

Exact buyer:
Small and midsize CNC job shops, toolrooms, precision manufacturers, and industrial suppliers that already rely on custom CAM post-processors, machine macros, DNC setups, or machine-specific NC output maintained by one retiring or overloaded independent programmer.

Acute trigger:
A CAM upgrade, Windows/workstation replacement, controller replacement, machine relocation, urgent repeat job, customer delivery deadline, or retiring maintainer threatens production because the shop's exact post-processor or machine macro is undocumented, unsupported, or controlled by one person.

Control point:
The startup does not sell new CNC programming from scratch. It controls a specific inherited continuity book before close: post/source files, machine/controller map, CAM-version map, tool-library assumptions, issue history, support inbox, customer list, seller endorsement, customer-approved support-owner transfer, and payment-direction transfer for current continuity contracts.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign one seller option covering source/post files, update rights, support history, customer handoff email, and earnout terms; attach a senior CAM/post reviewer on paid diligence terms; get at least eight named shops to approve the handoff and prepay 3,000-8,000 PLN each for 12-month continuity or a fixed compatibility update; collect at least 35,000 PLN before any material seller payout; rotate repository/admin access; document machine/controller/CAM maps; and resolve one bounded compatibility or update ticket without field commissioning.

6-month POC:
Close one acquired book or earnout-managed handoff with 20-60 active machine/post relationships; collect 120,000-300,000 PLN in prepaid continuity and fixed update tickets; keep contribution margin above 60% after reviewer and contractor costs; maintain average support below 90 minutes per active machine per month; resolve at least 10 bounded post/update tickets; reject or separately quote open-ended CAM programming, machine parameter repair, field commissioning, crash recovery, and safety-critical automation work.

First acquisition mechanism:
Find retiring or overloaded independent CAM/post programmers through Polish and CEE CNC forums, CAM reseller alumni, machine-tool service networks, LinkedIn, small machining supplier directories, and direct outreach to one-person post/macro specialists. The first message is not "sell me your company"; it is "if your customers depend on posts you no longer want to support, I will pay an earnout only after your customers approve transfer and prepay continuity."

Economics:
Charge 3,000-8,000 PLN per machine/post per year for continuity, version-control cleanup, source custody, bounded compatibility checks, and seller-backed handoff. Charge 4,000-18,000 PLN for fixed-scope CAM-version, controller, or workstation migration tickets. Pay the seller mostly from retained transferred cash and renewal earnout. Use senior CAM/post contractors only for diligence and scoped tickets. The model works only if one acquired book produces 120,000+ PLN annualized revenue, support stays bounded, and no speculative seller payout happens before customer cash.

Copy risk:
CAM resellers, CNC programmers, machine dealers, and automation shops can compete for new post work. They cannot instantly copy an exact inherited book after the startup controls the source files, machine maps, support history, seller endorsement, customer approvals, and current continuity payments. The advantage is transaction-specific, so the business must repeat the acquisition pattern rather than rely on generic consulting.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The first asset exists only inside the retiring maintainer's source folders, customer history, and trust relationship. A competitor can offer replacement programming, but it cannot use the exact post source, issue history, known machine assumptions, and seller-endorsed handoff unless it also wins that seller and those customer approvals before the startup does. Seller payout happens after customer prepayment, so the founder controls the specific payment stream before spending serious money.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup buys or options a named continuity book and takes custody of source, support history, customer approvals, and current payments. It refuses cold custom post development, marketplace matching, generic CNC consulting, broad factory automation, software dashboards, preventive retainers without a transferred book, and design/manufacturing advice.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify NC code as safe, guarantee machine behavior, operate machines, perform field commissioning, alter safety PLCs, approve toolpaths, take responsibility for fixture/workholding, or repair machine parameters. Customers remain responsible for simulation, dry runs, prove-outs, operator supervision, machine safety, and production acceptance. Cases involving safety interlocks, crash recovery, undocumented machine parameters, regulated aerospace/medical/nuclear output, or missing source rights are rejected or routed to qualified providers under separate customer contracts.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, Data Act, CBAM, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, BioSignal, or TraceFaktura. It is closest to prior connector/book-buyout tests, but the control point is materially different: exact CNC post/macro source custody plus machine-specific support history and customer-approved continuity payments, not ERP/ecommerce connectors, lab interfaces, fiscal devices, printer consumables, or generic software support.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The right seller may be rare. Customer prepayment before seller payout may fail. CAM resellers and local CNC programmers already have trust. Source ownership may be informal. Support can expand into bespoke programming or field commissioning. A bad post can damage parts, tools, fixtures, spindles, or machines. The first book may prove only a small profitable acquisition, not a repeatable company. The unknown founder needs a credible senior CAM/post reviewer from day one.
