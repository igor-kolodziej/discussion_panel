Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CBAM Authorised Declarant Import-Release Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
A fixed-fee, broker-routed import-release desk for Polish/CEE SME importers whose 2026 CBAM-covered goods are blocked or at immediate risk because authorised CBAM declarant status, timely application proof, threshold proof, PUESC/CBAM portal status, or the correct customs declaration document code is missing or unclear.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE SME importers of CBAM-covered steel, aluminium, iron, cement, fertiliser, electricity, hydrogen, or downstream covered goods, plus their customs brokers or indirect customs representatives, where a named shipment, customs-warehouse release, import lane, customer order, or customs declaration is blocked or at immediate risk because the importer is above or near the applicable CBAM mass threshold and lacks a clear authorised-declarant/application/status/declaration-code path. The economic buyer is the importer owner, CFO, logistics lead, customs lead, procurement lead, or broker responsible for preventing stopped imports and customer delivery failure.

Acute trigger:
A customs broker, indirect customs representative, customs desk, PUESC/CBAM portal status, customer, freight forwarder, warehouse operator, or internal logistics team says a CBAM-covered shipment cannot be declared, released for free circulation, moved out of customs warehouse, or scheduled for the next import because authorised CBAM declarant status is missing, a timely application reference is needed, threshold proof is unclear, historical CBAM tonnage has not been reconciled, a CBAM TARIC/document code is rejected or missing, the importer role is unclear, or the broker will not lodge the declaration without a status/release file.

Control point:
Signed limited mandate from importer and broker where needed; named shipment/import-lane file; CBAM-covered CN/TARIC lines; commercial invoices, packing lists, prior import declarations, historical mass ledger, broker declaration draft, PUESC/CBAM portal screenshots/status where authorized, authorised-declarant application trail, threshold proof, customs-representation map, TARIC/document-code decision log, broker/customer response trail, and no-go rules for legal advice, customs representation, emissions calculation, supplier verification, formal CBAM declaration filing, tax/customs disputes, sanctions, false codes, or cases without a named shipment/import lane.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign four prepaid importer/broker cases tied to named shipments, customs-warehouse releases, import lanes, customer orders, or imminent customs declarations; put at least 6,000,000 PLN of shipment value under mandate; collect at least 100,000 PLN in fixed fees; obtain two accepted authorised-declarant application/status, timely-application proof, threshold-proof, customs document-code, broker declaration-progress, shipment-release, or clean no-go outcomes; get one broker or indirect representative to send a second case; and keep a rejection log above 50% for generic CBAM readiness, emissions calculation, supplier data chasing, legal disputes, customs representation issues, and cases without a named import event.

6-month POC:
Handle 20-45 urgent CBAM import-release/status cases across 10-25 importers and 4-8 broker/forwarder channels; put 40,000,000-200,000,000 PLN of shipment/import-lane value under mandate; achieve 15+ application-status, threshold-proof, declaration-code, broker-accepted, release-progress, shipment-release, or clean no-go outcomes; collect 450,000-1,500,000 PLN in fees; build a private case taxonomy by CN/TARIC line, importer role, threshold bucket, mass-ledger issue, application/status state, broker objection, declaration-code path, PUESC/CBAM portal state, customs-representation model, and release outcome; and maintain gross margin above 60% after customs/CBAM reviewer, document QA, and secure workflow costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to Polish/CEE customs brokers, freight forwarders, indirect customs representatives, import accountants, and importers visibly handling repeated CBAM-covered goods. The broker message: "If a client has a named CBAM shipment or import lane stuck because authorised-declarant status, timely application proof, threshold proof, portal status, or declaration code is unclear, I build the release/status file under your process so you can decide whether to lodge, release, correct, or no-go." The importer message: "Send the broker blocker, CN/TARIC lines, prior declarations, and portal status. I do not calculate emissions or give customs/legal advice; I assemble the application/status/threshold/code evidence path that lets your broker move or no-go the import."

Economics:
Charge 15,000-30,000 PLN fixed fee for a single shipment/import-lane release/status file, 30,000-60,000 PLN for urgent multi-shipment or customs-warehouse cases, and retainer pricing only after repeated broker cases prove the workflow. The fee is justified only when shipment value, customer delivery value, or blocked import continuity is materially larger than the case fee. Reviewers are paid for CBAM/customs issue-spotting and boundary checks, not for legal advice, emissions verification, formal declarations, or customs representation.

Copy risk:
Customs brokers, indirect customs representatives, freight forwarders, CBAM consultants, customs lawyers, Big Four advisers, CBAM software vendors, and importer logistics teams can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is narrow urgent-release qualification, Polish/CEE broker routing, repeated threshold/status/document-code case memory, fast evidence-room assembly, and active mandate over the named shipment/import-lane file once the importer and broker provide the current blocker and documents.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the importer signs the mandate, prepays, authorizes broker coordination, provides the shipment/import-lane documents, historical CBAM mass ledger, portal/application/status evidence, broker blocker, declaration draft, and response trail, the startup controls the active release/status evidence room and chronology for that named import event. A customs broker or CBAM consultant can compete for future cases, but cannot control the same evidence room, status trail, and no-go/release chronology without the importer and broker switching mid-blocker and rebuilding the file.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell generic CBAM readiness, a CBAM dashboard, emissions calculation, supplier-emissions collection, annual declaration filing, customs brokerage, legal advice, a marketplace, or a broad import consulting report. It accepts only named import-release/status blockers and returns operational artifacts: authorised-declarant application/status evidence, threshold proof, broker-ready declaration-code file, portal-status chronology, customs-release progress file, shipment-release evidence, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not act as customs broker, indirect customs representative, declarant, lawyer, tax adviser, emissions verifier, supplier-emissions auditor, customs authority, or importer of record. Do not submit false customs codes, provide legal interpretations, calculate verified emissions, file formal CBAM declarations as declarant, advise on sanctions, argue customs disputes, or guarantee release. Importers, brokers, indirect representatives, customs authorities, CBAM authorities, counsel, and official portals remain responsible for declarations, legal status, authorisations, customs representation, emissions data, customs decisions, and release. Reject cases requiring legal dispute work, emissions verification, supplier truth creation, sanctions analysis, false codes, or import events without current documents.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is adjacent to the existing CBAM Import Continuity Desk but materially different. The confirmed CBAM idea is broker-branded exposure triage from existing import documents: CN/TARIC exposure, threshold risk, authorised-declarant readiness, supplier-emissions gaps, default-value exposure, and supplier requests. This idea starts only after a broker/importer has a named shipment, customs-warehouse release, import lane, or declaration blocked by authorised-declarant status, timely application proof, threshold proof, portal state, or document-code issues. The controlled object is the active import-release/status file, and the proof is application/status evidence, declaration-path acceptance, customs-release progress, shipment release, or clean no-go. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, Amazon recovery, or a generic customs evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
This may be too close to the already confirmed CBAM import-continuity wedge. Customs brokers and indirect representatives already control the transaction and may internalize repeatable cases. Some cases require legal or customs-representation decisions outside founder control. If an importer missed the status/application path, the correct answer may be no-go rather than release. CBAM portal rules, declaration codes, thresholds, and authority practice can change. Many blockers may actually be supplier-emissions or declaration-preparation issues, which this desk must reject. The 2026 spike may fade once brokers standardize. An unknown Warsaw solo founder needs broker trust before handling urgent customs files.
