## Why It Passed Simulation Under The Acknowledged-Receivable Version

- It avoids illegal prescription-medicine sale or advertising.
- It does not touch medicines, prescriptions, pharmacy fulfillment, or patient treatment.
- The controlled asset is a specific assigned receivable: a PSP/acquirer reserve, rolling reserve, settlement holdback, or released-but-unpaid balance.
- The founder can prove control in 60 days with assignment, debtor acknowledgment, controlled payout instruction, and collected cash.
- It targets an acute cash pain: licensed high-risk clinics can have legitimate consultation revenue trapped in rolling reserves or settlement holds.

## Control Point

The startup buys or advances against one specific B2B receivable only when all are true:

- seller is a licensed legal clinic or provider, not a gray-market Rx seller;
- underlying transaction is lawful clinic/service revenue, not online sale of prescription medicine;
- medicine seller/provider-of-record remains licensed pharmacy/clinic as legally required;
- receivable is evidenced by PSP/acquirer statement, reserve schedule, settlement report, or written balance confirmation;
- merchant agreement has no anti-assignment blocker or the PSP/acquirer gives written consent/acknowledgment;
- debtor/PSP/acquirer acknowledges the assigned receivable or agrees to payment instruction to a controlled account;
- legal review covers AML, factoring/loan boundary, usury/consumer-credit irrelevance, data minimization, and no involvement in medical decisions.

## Two-Month Proof

- one 30,000-80,000 PLN assigned PSP/acquirer reserve receivable;
- written debtor/PSP/acquirer balance acknowledgment or payment-instruction confirmation;
- legal memo that clinic activity is licensed and receivable assignment is not blocked;
- controlled settlement account or escrow instruction;
- partial or full cash release collected inside 60 days;
- discount/factoring fee sufficient to cover legal review and adverse-selection risk.

## Main Risks

1. PSP terms may prohibit assignment.
2. PSP may refuse acknowledgment or payment-direction changes.
3. The underlying clinic may be gray-market or non-compliant.
4. Adverse selection is high; distressed merchants may have disputes, chargebacks, refunds, fraud flags, or regulatory risk.
5. Factoring/advance structure may require careful legal and tax setup.
6. One small reserve may not prove repeatability.
7. Generic factors could copy if risk becomes obviously profitable, though many will avoid high-risk medical/Rx-adjacent merchants.
