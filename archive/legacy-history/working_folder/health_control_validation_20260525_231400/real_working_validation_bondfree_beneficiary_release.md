## Strongest Objections

1. Clean releases should already be processed by banks, insurers, brokers, or contractor finance teams.
2. Stranded files may require original guarantee return, exact beneficiary wording, signatory verification, fee clearance, amendment history, broker cancellation, or no-demand confirmation.
3. Cross-collateralization can mean one guarantee release does not actually free usable cash or limit.
4. Restored guarantee capacity is less tangible than cash unless it enables a named tender, project, payroll, supplier payment, or financing need.
5. Success fees may feel unfair where the contractor already has the beneficiary release letter.
6. Guarantee brokers are both good sources and natural competitors.
7. The legal/brokerage boundary remains thin; the desk must avoid interpreting guarantee enforceability or arguing entitlement to release.
8. Exposure screened is not decisive proof; only cash/line actually freed and paid success fee matter.
9. Banks and insurers may communicate only through the account owner or specific forms.
10. The pool may be too small because the required failure state is narrow: beneficiary release exists but collateral/limit remains blocked.

## Useful Lesson

Narrowing to beneficiary-signed release reduces legal dispute risk but also removes much of the value creation. Do not continue guarantee-release variants unless there is already a repeat channel with documented high-volume stuck release files and cash-margin values, not just occasional admin cleanup.
