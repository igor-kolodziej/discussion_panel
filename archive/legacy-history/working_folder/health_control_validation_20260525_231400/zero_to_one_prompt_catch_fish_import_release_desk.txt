Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CATCH Fish Import Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A prepaid, broker-routed import-release desk for EU/CEE seafood importers whose current fishery-product consignments are blocked or delayed because paper catch certificates, processing statements, non-manipulation documents, duplicate references, operator records, or importer declarations do not copy cleanly into the compulsory EU CATCH workflow.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE seafood importers, customs brokers, cold-chain forwarders, and seafood wholesalers importing fishery products into the EU where a named consignment, retailer order, customs/border step, or cold-chain slot is blocked by CATCH certificate data, catch-certificate chain, processing statement, non-manipulation document, importer declaration, duplicate-reference issue, or broker/authority follow-up. The strongest first buyer is a seafood-specialist customs broker or cold-chain forwarder with repeated importer clients and live CATCH problem cases.

Acute trigger:
Since 10 January 2026, EU importers or their representatives must use CATCH for fishery-product import documentation. A live shipment can be slowed when paper catch certificates, simplified certificates, processing statements, linked catch certificates, product codes, species, fishing-vessel or trip data, exporter/operator records, validating authority data, non-manipulation documents, or duplicate certificate references do not match the CATCH workflow, broker import file, or importer declaration. The buyer pays because demurrage, cold-chain storage, retailer-order failure, customer delivery delay, and staff firefighting can become more expensive than a focused release file.

Control point:
Signed importer or broker mandate for a named consignment; supplied paper certificates and linked documents; broker-provided CN/HS/import reference data; CATCH draft/status screenshots or case access under the importer/broker account; supplier/exporter contact trail; error log; field-by-field source-vs-CATCH reconciliation; authority/broker response chronology; and release/progress/no-go ledger. The startup controls the current evidence file and filing chronology for that consignment, not the official authority decision.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Two customs brokers or seafood importers sign prepaid CATCH release case agreements. Five live or recently blocked consignments are accepted only with importer/broker authorization, shipment value, cold-chain/demurrage/order consequence, and complete source-document set. At least three cases include CATCH draft/status evidence or broker written issue text. At least two cases produce broker acceptance, authority progress, importer declaration submission, corrected duplicate/reference path, release to next import step, retailer/customer hold lifted, or clean no-go. At least 60,000 PLN is collected in fixed and urgent case fees. A reusable issue taxonomy exists across certificate type, country/authority, species/product-code pattern, processing-statement link, duplicate-reference issue, operator-creation issue, missing supplier field, and no-go cause.

6-month POC:
Operate through 4-8 seafood-specialist customs brokers, cold-chain forwarders, or seafood importer groups; process 25-60 live CATCH release cases; collect 300,000-900,000 PLN in fixed, urgent, and broker-wholesale case fees; document 15+ broker/authority/importer progress, release, customer-hold-lifted, or clean no-go outcomes; prove at least three brokers send five or more paid cases each; keep gross margin above 60% after document operations, translation, seafood compliance reviewer, and secure handling costs; reject cases needing customs classification, veterinary/SPS, IUU legality opinions, suspicious/fraudulent documents, or supplier documents that cannot be obtained.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with seafood-specialist customs brokers, cold-chain forwarders, seafood import accountants, and importers handling frozen fish, tuna, whitefish, shrimp, fishmeal, and processed fish products. Broker pitch: "Send only live CATCH problem consignments. You keep the customs relationship and account access. I turn the paper certificate chain, supplier trail, and CATCH errors into a broker-approved field-by-field release file, correction path, or clean no-go inside three business days." Importer pitch: "I only take named consignments with a broker route, source documents, and a real order/storage consequence."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-15,000 PLN for standard CATCH issue files, 15,000-25,000 PLN for urgent cold-chain or multi-certificate consignments, and 25,000-45,000 PLN for large multi-origin or retailer-blocking batches. Broker wholesale pricing can be 6,000-12,000 PLN per evidence-ready case. Costs are document operations, translation, reviewer checks, and secure handling, not litigation, customs brokerage, or lab testing. The buyer ROI is credible when one consignment is worth 100,000-1,000,000+ PLN and delay creates demurrage, cold storage, missed retail slot, or customer penalty.

Copy risk:
Customs brokers, seafood compliance consultants, freight forwarders, importer staff, and food-law advisers can copy pieces. The defense is narrow current-case control, CATCH transition timing, exact certificate-chain issue memory, broker-routed acquisition, seafood supplier communication patterns, fast no-go discipline, and a private taxonomy of accepted correction paths by country, certificate type, product/species pattern, and CATCH error.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has a prepaid broker/importer mandate, exact consignment file, paper certificate chain, CATCH draft/status evidence, supplier contact trail, broker approval route, and response chronology, another provider cannot act on that specific shipment without the broker or importer switching mid-deadline. Competitors can offer general CATCH help, but they do not control the already-paid release file, evidence chronology, and issue taxonomy for that named consignment.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The controlled unit is a current import-release case tied to a named consignment and CATCH filing path. The output is a broker-approved CATCH release file, correction path, evidence reconciliation, submitted/importer-ready declaration support file, progress ledger, or clean no-go. The startup does not sell training, tender alerts, a software dashboard, a database, a marketplace, generic customs brokerage, or introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as customs broker, importer of record, official representative to authorities, veterinary/SPS adviser, food-safety certifier, IUU legality certifier, or legal adviser. Customs brokers, importers, competent authorities, suppliers, lawyers, veterinary/food specialists, and official representatives remain responsible for declarations, classification, legal sufficiency, import eligibility, official submissions, and authority decisions. The startup uses broker/importer-provided classifications and rejects cases involving suspected forged documents, sanctions issues, non-cooperating flag states, unresolved legality concerns, disputed classification, veterinary/health certificate problems, or pressure to make inconsistent documents appear acceptable.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not CBAM Import Continuity Desk because the controlled artifact is a fishery-product CATCH certificate chain and import-release file under IUU import controls, not embedded carbon emissions, authorised CBAM declarant status, CBAM certificate exposure, or supplier emissions evidence. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, KSeF payment release, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The buyer trigger, control point, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: named seafood consignments with CATCH import documentation progress, not bank supplier evidence, platform product compliance, diligence, salvage, municipal invoice routing, retailer deductions, machine-data access, public tender SKU evidence, or medical data.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Customs brokers may already handle CATCH cases as ordinary import support. The pool of paid problem cases may be small once brokers and importers learn the system after the 2026 transition. Hard cases may depend on foreign suppliers, flag-state authorities, vessel data, paper certificates, or processing statements outside founder control. The work can drift into customs classification, IUU legality advice, veterinary/SPS controls, food-law interpretation, or official representation. Authorities may reject or delay files for reasons outside the startup's control. A founder in Warsaw has weak seafood-specific trust unless broker-routed acquisition is real. If the case output is just data entry, brokers can internalize it quickly. If many accepted cases are suspicious or missing source documents, clean no-go volume may be high and buyer willingness to pay may fall.
