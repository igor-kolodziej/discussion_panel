## Search Frame

Recent failures show that ordinary recovery mandates fail when the recovered pool is vague, low-ticket, lawyer/accountant-owned, or easy for the customer to internalize after seeing a checklist. The remaining plausible recovery shape needs:

- an official portal with numbered monetary debits/credits;
- a current cash deduction or credit memo path, not only an advisory finding;
- enough ticket value to pay for expert review;
- a narrow rule set where case memory compounds;
- a signed mandate over an existing queue before the founder spends labor.

IATA BSP agency debit memos are a candidate because airlines issue ADMs to travel agencies through BSPlink, agents can dispute eligible ADMs, and successful cases can create airline ACMs, reversals, or settlement credits. The pain is ugly and back-office-heavy, not speculative consulting.

Source checks used for timing and mechanics:

- IATA describes BSPlink as the global interface for travel agents and airlines to access the Billing and Settlement Plan and view/dispute financial documents.
- IATA materials describe Agency Debit Memos and Agency Credit Memos being issued in BSPlink.
- IATA materials describe post-billing dispute functionality for debit transactions, with time limits and bilateral resolution mechanics.

## Advanced Candidate

Idea name: BSP ADM Credit Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis: Recover cash for CEE travel agencies by taking a signed mandate over already-issued airline Agency Debit Memos in BSPlink, filing evidence-backed disputes, and getting paid only from ACMs, reversals, settlement credits, or avoided BSP deductions.

Exact buyer:

- IATA-accredited Polish/CEE travel agencies, consolidators, ticketing desks, TMCs, and tour operators with recurring airline ADMs in BSP.
- Best first buyers have 20+ ADMs in the last 12 months, ticketing volume above 10 million PLN equivalent, a lean back office, and visible pain from fare-rule, refund, tax, waiver, schedule-change, card-fee, no-show, reissue, NDC, or interline debit memos.

Acute trigger:

- An airline has issued ADMs through BSPlink, or ADMs have already been deducted from BSP remittance.
- The agency suspects some ADMs are invalid, duplicate, wrongly timed, unsupported, inconsistent with airline policy, or defensible with PNR/ticket/fare/waiver/refund evidence, but lacks time or specialist bandwidth to work the queue.
- The commercial event is current cash: debit memo deduction, pending BSP settlement, ACM/reversal, or airline credit.

Control point:

- Signed recovery mandate for a defined ADM batch.
- Controlled evidence room for ADM numbers, airline, issue date, billing period, ticket numbers, PNRs, fare basis, tax/YQ details, waiver codes, refund/exchange history, GDS logs, airline policy excerpts, prior correspondence, and BSP settlement reports.
- Filed BSPlink disputes or airline escalation records.
- Airline response, accepted dispute, ACM, reversal, settlement credit, or documented no-go.
- Fee right tied only to recovered or avoided cash from the controlled ADM queue.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

1. Five agencies sign ADM recovery mandates covering at least 80 live or post-billing ADMs.
2. At least 150,000 PLN equivalent of ADMs are screened and ranked.
3. At least 60,000 PLN equivalent of ADMs are filed as evidence-backed disputes or airline escalations.
4. One airline ACM, reversal, settlement credit, or cancelled deduction is received.
5. First success-fee invoice is issued only after the agency sees the credit/reversal in BSPlink, BSP settlement, or airline correspondence.
6. Cases without ticketing evidence, time-window viability, authority, or plausible airline-policy basis are rejected.

6-month POC:

- Sign 20 agencies or 2 agency groups/consolidators.
- Screen 1,000+ ADMs across named airlines and issue types.
- File 300+ disputes/escalations where evidence supports the position.
- Produce 300,000-900,000 PLN equivalent in ACMs, reversals, settlement credits, or avoided deductions.
- Collect 60,000-180,000 PLN in success fees and screened-batch fees.
- Build a private case taxonomy by airline, ADM reason, time window, evidence type, outcome, and dispute wording.
- Keep rejected/no-go cases above 30% if needed to avoid weak disputes.

First acquisition mechanism:

- Build a target list of Polish/CEE agencies, TMCs, consolidators, tour operators, and ticketing back-office providers with IATA/BSP exposure.
- Use a narrow outbound message: "Send a redacted export of your last 12 months of airline ADMs. I will screen the queue and take only the debits with a plausible evidence-backed reversal path. You pay from ACMs, reversals, or avoided BSP deductions, not from a generic report."
- Partner outreach to ticketing trainers, GDS consultants, accounting firms serving agencies, agency associations, and retiring ticketing managers who see ADMs but do not want to work old queues.

Economics:

- Screening fee: 1,500-5,000 PLN for a batch only when the agency has enough ADM value and evidence access.
- Success fee: 20-30% of ACMs, reversals, settlement credits, or avoided pending deductions.
- Minimum case threshold: do not pursue individual ADMs below 1,000-2,000 PLN unless bundled by same airline/reason.
- Gross margin target: 65%+ after ticketing reviewer costs.
- Cash conversion is fast when valid disputes reverse current or recent BSP deductions; longer tail cases stay success-fee-only.

Copy risk:

Agency ticketing desks, airline sales reps, GDS consultants, back-office outsourcing firms, IATA/BSP specialists, and large TMC finance teams can copy pieces of the workflow. They cannot copy a signed mandate over a specific ADM queue, the agency's evidence files, filed dispute history, airline response memory, and success-fee right once controlled.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

The startup does not claim a monopoly over all airline debit memo work. It controls named ADM batches for named agencies. Once the agency signs the mandate, provides BSPlink/ticketing evidence, and disputes are filed under that case file, a competitor would need the same agency authorization and evidence access for the same ADMs. Longer term, the defense comes from airline/reason/outcome memory across many controlled queues.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The product is a recovery mandate over numbered monetary debits in an official settlement workflow. The startup is paid from ACMs, reversals, settlement credits, or avoided deductions, not from a dashboard, generic audit, training, marketplace lead, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- The agency remains the IATA/BSP participant and approves all dispute statements.
- The startup does not sell travel, issue tickets, act as an airline agent, hold client funds, provide legal advice, or guarantee reversal.
- Reject fraud, forged waiver, passenger-data overreach, sanctions, chargeback, or airline-security cases.
- Use minimum necessary PNR/ticket evidence, secure storage, and DPA/confidentiality terms.
- Ticketing/GDS specialist reviews edge cases before filing.
- No weak disputes that could damage agency-airline relationships.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

- Not PromoLeak: this is not FMCG/retailer deduction leakage or retail-media recovery; the artifacts are IATA BSP ADMs, PNRs, tickets, fare/refund/waiver evidence, and airline ACMs.
- Not Amazon Vendor Central Recovery: no Amazon 1P deductions, Vendor Central disputes, or retail chargebacks.
- Not OTA hotel settlement recovery: buyer is travel agency/TMC and the payor path is airline/BSP ADM/ACM, not hotel OTA commission or virtual-card settlement.
- Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:

1. Many ADMs may be valid because the agency made a real ticketing, refund, exchange, waiver, or fare-rule error.
2. Agency ticketing desks may already know how to dispute the best cases.
3. Airlines may reject disputes, preserve relationships over strict evidence, or make reversals slow.
4. BSPlink time windows and airline policies may kill old cases.
5. Evidence access includes sensitive passenger, ticketing, payment, and agency-account data.
6. GDS, NDC, interline, tax, fare construction, and refund rules require real ticketing expertise.
7. The best agencies may have good back offices; weak agencies may have messy evidence and low trust.
8. Airline relationships can be damaged by over-aggressive disputes.
9. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if an airline would have credited the agency anyway.
10. One ADM queue is a cash recovery project; the company only compounds if airline/reason/outcome memory repeats across many agencies.
