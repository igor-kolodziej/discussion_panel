Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AI Voice-Agent Revenue-Claim Diligence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
Sell fixed-scope diligence packs to investors, search funds, PE buyers, strategic acquirers, and corporate procurement teams evaluating AI voice-agent startups or contact-center automation claims, using real call tests, transcripts, failure taxonomy, and unit-economics evidence to decide whether claimed automation, margin lift, or replacement value is credible.

Exact buyer:
Seed and Series A investors, search funders, lower-middle-market PE buyers, corporate development teams, BPO/contact-center acquirers, and enterprise procurement teams with a live investment, LOI, acquisition, pilot expansion, or vendor selection decision involving AI voice agents for customer support, appointment booking, collections-adjacent service, inbound sales, insurance intake, telecom support, utilities support, travel/hospitality service, or other call-heavy workflows.

Acute trigger:
A target company or vendor claims that its AI voice agent can replace or deflect human call volume, improve gross margin, reduce average handle time, book appointments, qualify leads, collect structured information, handle accents/languages, disclose AI use properly, integrate with CRM/helpdesk/payment/scheduling systems, and resolve calls at lower cost. The buyer must decide before wiring capital, signing an LOI, closing an acquisition, expanding a pilot, or approving a procurement award.

Control point:
Signed paid diligence mandate for one live decision, plus controlled access to the specific evidence needed for that decision: target deck, dataroom materials, pricing, sample recordings, transcripts, call-flow diagrams, eval results, support tickets, containment/transfer data, integration/tool-call logs where available, customer references, claimed ROI model, pilot results, and buyer-approved synthetic or mystery-call test scripts. The startup controls the diligence file, test plan, transcript annotations, failure taxonomy, and decision memo for that active deal or procurement.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three prepaid live-decision packs at 8,000-35,000 PLN each. Each case must have a named buyer, a live investment/acquisition/procurement/pilot-expansion decision, a claimed automation or margin outcome, and permission to review target materials or run buyer-approved call tests. At least one pack must be used in an IC memo, investment committee discussion, LOI/SPA mark-up, procurement scorecard, pilot expansion decision, valuation adjustment, earnout/holdback request, evidence request, or kill decision. Every pack includes a call-test matrix, transcript/failure annotations, containment and escalation review, disclosure/compliance boundary screen, cost-per-resolved-call model, and strongest unanswered evidence requests.

6-month POC:
Complete 8-15 paid diligence packs, collect 120,000-350,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 65% after optional call-center ops, privacy, or AI-evals reviewers, and prove decision impact in at least five cases. Build a private benchmark library of voice-agent failure modes by use case: latency, interruption handling, hallucinated policy, bad handoff, missing disclosure, accent/language failure, fragile tool calls, wrong CRM updates, unhandled angry callers, edge-case refunds, appointment errors, and economics that fail after human QA and escalation costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Targeted outbound to 60 investors, search funders, PE operating partners, and corporate buyers already looking at AI voice-agent, BPO automation, contact-center software, or AI-enabled services deals. Send a one-page sample teardown using public demos and a precise offer: "If an AI voice-agent target is claiming call replacement or margin lift, I will turn the dataroom, calls, demos, transcripts, pricing, and buyer-approved test calls into a decision pack within 5 business days: credible, overclaimed, missing evidence, terms adjustment, or kill."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-15,000 PLN for a quick live-deal claim screen, 15,000-25,000 PLN for a full call-test and unit-economics pack, and 25,000-35,000 PLN for urgent acquisition/procurement cases with multiple workflows or languages. Reviewer cost is 1,000-6,000 PLN only when call-center operations, privacy, or technical-eval review is needed. Buyers pay because a weak voice-agent claim can distort valuation, procurement choice, headcount plan, pilot expansion, or acquisition underwriting by far more than the diligence fee.

Copy risk:
Technical diligence firms, call-center consultants, AI advisers, procurement consultants, and investors can copy pieces. The defense is narrow live-decision focus, fast voice-specific test artifacts, repeated failure taxonomy, buyer decision language, and a private library of accepted and rejected claims by use case. The company should not compete as a generic AI consultant.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the buyer signs the mandate, shares the target materials, approves test calls, and uses the diligence pack in a live deal or procurement process, the startup controls that specific claim-risk file and decision artifact. Another provider would have to replace the test plan, transcript annotations, failure taxonomy, unit-economics adjustment, unanswered evidence requests, and buyer-ready memo before the decision deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live investment, acquisition, procurement, or pilot-expansion decisions where an AI voice-agent revenue or automation claim is being underwritten. The output is a decision artifact tied to a current capital or procurement event: call-test matrix, transcript annotations, failure taxonomy, unit-economics adjustment, evidence request list, and decision memo. It is not AI consulting, vendor selection software, a dashboard, a marketplace, a generic report, implementation help, call-center outsourcing, or a brokered expert introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, investment advice, procurement authority, privacy counsel, call-center implementation, employment advice, or guarantees about AI performance. It uses buyer-approved test scripts and target-provided or lawfully shared materials. It avoids collecting unnecessary personal data, redacts or uses synthetic calls where possible, stores recordings and transcripts securely, deletes raw call data on schedule, and routes legal/privacy/employment questions to qualified advisers. Regulated collections, healthcare, banking, insurance claims handling, and emergency/safety workflows are excluded unless specialist legal and domain review is contracted and the buyer accepts the added boundary.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack. BioSignal evaluates biosignal, wearable, healthtech, EEG, sleep, fatigue, recovery, longevity, remote-monitoring, and digital-biomarker claims for healthtech investors. AI Voice-Agent Revenue-Claim Diligence evaluates voice-agent automation, call-resolution, handoff, disclosure, integration, and unit-economics claims in live AI/contact-center investment, acquisition, or procurement decisions. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, PromoLeak, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Technical diligence firms, call-center consultants, AI advisers, and sophisticated investors can run their own tests. Target companies may refuse access to recordings, logs, transcripts, or real customer data. Synthetic or mystery-call tests may not represent production distribution. Voice-agent performance can change quickly as models, prompts, and integrations change. Many claims depend on the buyer's workflow quality, knowledge base, escalation rules, CRM setup, and human QA, not only the vendor's product. The founder needs credibility in AI evals and contact-center economics. If the buyer does not have a live decision, the product becomes generic AI commentary. Regulated workflows can create privacy, consent, employment, call-recording, consumer-protection, or sector-law risk.
