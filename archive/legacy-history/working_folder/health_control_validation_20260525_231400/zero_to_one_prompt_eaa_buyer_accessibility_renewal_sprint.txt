Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: EAA Buyer Accessibility Renewal Sprint

One-sentence thesis:
A fixed-scope buyer-unblock sprint for Polish and CEE B2B SaaS and software-product vendors whose bank, insurer, telecom, transport, ecommerce, booking, payment, or fintech customer renewal, procurement, rollout, or release is blocked by a live European Accessibility Act, EN 301 549, WCAG, VPAT/ACR, or accessibility-defect request.

Exact buyer:
20-150 person Polish and CEE B2B SaaS or software-product vendors that supply user-facing digital components, portals, checkout flows, booking flows, mobile apps, self-service tools, document workflows, customer communications, or embedded widgets to EU consumer-service companies now exposed to accessibility obligations and buyer pressure.

The economic buyer is the founder, COO, CTO, product lead, enterprise sales owner, or customer-success owner trying to preserve a live customer renewal, procurement, rollout, strategic logo, or release gate. The first buyer must already have a named customer accessibility request or defect list; generic accessibility curiosity is rejected.

Acute trigger:
A named enterprise customer sends an EAA/accessibility questionnaire, EN 301 549 or VPAT/ACR request, WCAG defect list, procurement hold, renewal condition, release-blocking Jira ticket, product-accessibility addendum, or accessibility issue list tied to a current renewal, procurement, rollout, release, or customer acceptance deadline. The vendor needs a buyer-acceptable response and visible fixes before the customer deadline.

Control point:
Prepaid sprint with the live buyer request, affected user journeys, product/staging access, defect list or questionnaire, vendor authorization, accessibility tester, front-end/dev contractor, answer/evidence owner, before/after artifacts, issue-disposition table, and customer-submitted response. The startup controls the sprint queue and the evidence artifact for that specific renewal blocker.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid live buyer-unblock sprints from at least two vendors.
2. One accessibility specialist/tester and one front-end contractor on paid per-sprint terms.
3. Vendor authorization to review the named product journey and prepare the buyer response/evidence pack.
4. A scoped affected-flow inventory for at least five journeys across the first cases.
5. At least 30 accessibility defects triaged into fixed, customer-accepted workaround, owner-approved remediation plan, out-of-scope legal/product decision, or no-repro.
6. At least two customer-progress outcomes: renewal or procurement moves forward, buyer accepts the issue disposition, release blocker is downgraded, buyer narrows follow-up, or rollout gate reopens.
7. First 40,000-90,000 PLN collected in sprint fees.

6-month POC:
Month 1: recruit one experienced accessibility tester and one front-end contractor, build intake and evidence templates, and contact 60-80 Polish/CEE software vendors with consumer-service customers plus 20 agencies or advisors that see buyer questionnaires.
Month 2: close three prepaid live buyer-unblock sprints, reject generic audit leads, and deliver the first before/after defect and customer-response packages.
Month 3: secure at least two customer-progress outcomes and document why every lost case failed: no live blocker, too legal, too broad, no product access, no budget, or formal-audit-only.
Months 4-6: complete 10-15 paid sprints, process 150+ defects across 20+ named journeys, collect 150,000-350,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 55% after tester and contractor costs, keep average founder handling under 8 hours per sprint after intake, and build case memory by buyer type, request wording, defect pattern, remediation snippet, evidence artifact, and customer outcome.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of Polish and CEE software vendors with public customers in banking, insurance, payments, telecom, transport, ecommerce, booking, and fintech. Outbound asks only for live buyer accessibility blockers, not general audits: "If a current enterprise customer is holding a renewal, procurement, rollout, or release because of EAA, EN 301 549, WCAG, VPAT/ACR, or accessibility defects, we run a fixed-scope sprint that produces buyer-ready issue disposition, before/after evidence, and contractor-backed fixes."

Partner outreach goes to boutique accessibility testers, UX agencies, security/DORA advisors, enterprise-sales consultants, and software houses that see buyer accessibility questionnaires but do not want urgent remediation sprints. Partners keep broader advisory, redesign, audit, and implementation work.

Economics:
Charge 12,000-20,000 PLN for a five-business-day triage and buyer-response sprint, 20,000-35,000 PLN for a ten-business-day remediation sprint with contractor fixes, and 40,000+ PLN for multi-flow urgent renewal sprints. Tester and front-end contractor costs are scoped before kickoff. The buyer pays because the fee is small compared with a delayed or lost 100,000+ PLN renewal, enterprise rollout, procurement approval, or strategic logo.

Copy risk:
Accessibility agencies, UX shops, dev shops, automated accessibility tools, auditors, and in-house product teams can do pieces of the work. The defense is narrow vendor-side live buyer-blocker focus, local Polish/CEE outreach, fast paid sprint packaging, customer-response evidence memory, and combined tester-plus-remediation capacity. It is not defensible as a broad accessibility audit or compliance consultancy; it must specialize in live enterprise buyer blockers with deadlines.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup controls the paid live buyer ticket, affected product journeys, staging access, defect list, tester notes, contractor patch queue, issue-disposition table, buyer response wording, and submitted evidence, competitors cannot work that exact renewal blocker without the vendor switching mid-deadline. Each case adds accepted wording, recurring defect patterns, customer-question variants, remediation snippets, and before/after artifact patterns by buyer type and product flow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The output is not an audit PDF, accessibility score, monitoring dashboard, database, or brokered expert call. The proof is customer-facing progress on a named buyer blocker: fixed issues, before/after test artifacts, buyer-ready ACR/VPAT/EN 301 549 response sections where appropriate, issue-disposition table, staged screenshots or videos, and customer acceptance or narrowed follow-up. The startup sells an urgent renewal sprint, not general compliance readiness.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, certify legal compliance, issue formal conformity declarations, guarantee accessibility conformance, guarantee buyer acceptance, or replace the vendor's product ownership. Formal legal interpretations, declarations, regulatory filings, and customer contract commitments remain with the vendor and its advisors. Cases requiring broad redesign, full product accessibility programs, formal audits, or unresolved legal interpretation are escalated or declined. The sprint covers scoped user journeys, defect triage, remediation support, evidence organization, and buyer-response packaging.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because the trigger is accessibility and EAA/EN 301 549/WCAG buyer acceptance, not DORA/ICT third-party risk. It is not REDBlocked because there is no Amazon/Seller Central, connected-device CE/RED/GPSR listing block, or marketplace reinstatement workflow. It is not BioSignal because there is no investor diligence or health claim-risk memo. It is not HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet. The material distinction is a named enterprise buyer accessibility blocker plus actual remediation and before/after acceptance artifacts for B2B software vendors.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Accessibility agencies and automated tools are abundant.
Some buyer requests require deep product redesign rather than a sprint.
EAA legal interpretation, declarations, or conformance claims can drift into legal/compliance advice.
Unknown founder credibility depends on a named accessibility tester and credible contractor bench.
Buyers may accept only formal audits or established accessibility vendors.
Vendors may not pay until a customer explicitly threatens renewal or procurement.
Defect remediation can sprawl if the affected user journey is not tightly scoped.
Case memory may not compound if every product and customer request is bespoke.
