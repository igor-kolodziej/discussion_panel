Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
SEPA Direct-Debit Collection Rescue Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Rescue live failed or at-risk SEPA direct-debit collection cycles for subscription and membership businesses by taking authorized operational control of the mandate register, migration/re-consent work, bank/PSP batch file, and first successful collection run.

Exact buyer:
EU subscription or membership businesses with 100-5,000 recurring payers and at least 50,000 PLN/EUR equivalent of monthly direct-debit collections, such as gyms, fitness chains, coworking operators, language schools, private clubs, small ISPs, local SaaS, property-service subscriptions, and other non-regulated recurring billers. The first buyer has a current failed batch, provider migration, creditor-ID change, billing-system cutover, or return-code spike threatening the next collection cycle.

Acute trigger:
A recurring SEPA direct-debit batch has failed, is about to fail, or cannot be submitted because the old PSP/bank/billing tool is closing, the creditor identifier or bank account is changing, mandate references do not map, payer notices or re-consents are incomplete, return codes spiked, or a billing-system migration broke the mandate file before the next monthly collection.

Control point:
Signed collection-continuity mandate from the biller; current payer mandate register; old and new creditor identifiers where relevant; payer notice/re-consent plan where required; new PSP/bank onboarding status; return-code ledger; corrected mandate-reference mapping; test file; production debit file; batch-submission acknowledgement; settlement report; and the first successful collection batch through the licensed bank/PSP. The startup never holds customer funds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three signed biller mandates, each tied to a current failed or at-risk SEPA collection cycle. At least 150,000 PLN/EUR equivalent of current monthly collections under rescue. Two corrected mandate registers and collection files accepted by a bank/PSP/billing platform. One successful recollection or migration batch with settlement into the biller's account. 25,000-75,000 PLN collected in setup and current-cash recovery fees, excluding unpaid success-fee receivables.

6-month POC:
Screen 20-40 billers; accept 8-15 rescue mandates; migrate, repair, re-consent, or cleanly no-go 2,000-12,000 payer mandates; submit 1,000,000-5,000,000 PLN/EUR equivalent in repaired collection batches; save, recollect, or prevent failure on 250,000-1,200,000 PLN/EUR equivalent in current collections; collect 120,000-350,000 PLN revenue from setup fees, urgent batch fees, and capped collection-rescue fees; keep gross margin above 65% after payment-ops contractor review, secure file handling, customer communications, and partner fees.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target PSP migration partners, billing-system implementers, accountants serving subscription businesses, gym/franchise operators, coworking operators, and small ISPs. The offer is specific: "If your next SEPA direct-debit run is blocked by a provider switch, creditor-ID change, failed return-code spike, or broken mandate migration, I take one authorized rescue batch from register to submitted file to settlement report. No funds custody, no PSP resale, no debt collection."

Economics:
Charge 4,000-12,000 PLN/EUR setup for accepted live rescue cases plus 3-8% of the first saved or recollected batch value, capped per biller. For urgent batches above 100,000 PLN/EUR, add a rush fee. The biller pays because one missed collection cycle creates immediate cash-flow damage, churn, manual invoicing, support tickets, and reputational issues with payers.

Copy risk:
PSPs, banks, billing platforms, accountants, payment-operations consultants, and internal finance teams can copy the workflow. The defense is temporary but concrete control of the exact current batch, mandate mapping, return-code memory, migration/re-consent scripts, partner playbooks, and repeated rescue cases before the next collection deadline.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder has the biller's signed mandate, current mandate register, return-code ledger, payer communications, corrected batch file, PSP/bank acknowledgement, and deadline calendar, another operator cannot act on that exact collection cycle without the biller switching mid-rescue. The first asset is the current collection batch and mandate file, not a generic checklist.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit of work is a live authorized collection cycle that ends in a submitted debit file, bank/PSP acknowledgement, settlement report, or clean no-go. There is no case without a named failed or at-risk batch, payer mandate data, authorized collection path, and near-term cash movement. The startup does not sell software, a migration report, a PSP directory, or payment-provider introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Licensed banks, PSPs, and billing platforms remain the payment processors and funds handlers. The biller remains merchant/creditor of record and responsible for payer contracts, mandate validity, customer communications, refund decisions, complaints, chargebacks, accounting, and legal compliance. The startup does not hold funds, initiate unauthorized debits, provide legal advice, act as a PSP, debt collector, factoring provider, credit provider, or merchant of record. Reject consumer credit, insurance, gambling, medical/health subscriptions, adult content, charities with donor-sensitive issues, suspected fraud, sanctions risk, high chargeback risk, ambiguous payer authorization, and any case requiring legal interpretation of mandate validity.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central, Retail OTIF, 3PL overbilling, COD remittance, or other recovery mandates; the cash event is a current direct-debit collection batch, not a disputed deduction or platform claim. Not KSeF/AP payment release; no enterprise buyer AP action is required. Not DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, TraceFaktura, HeatQuiet, BioSignal, or HeritageDoor; the buyer, trigger, control point, and proof artifact are payment-stream continuity.

Strongest anticipated objections:
PSPs, banks, billing tools, and accountants are natural owners and can internalize the workflow. Many failed batches are caused by invalid mandates, payer churn, insufficient funds, cancelled authorizations, or bad customer data, not fixable migration defects. Handling payer bank data, mandate references, and customer communications creates GDPR, fraud, and security trust friction. Payer notices and re-consents can take longer than the collection deadline. A part-time unknown founder may be too slow for urgent monthly collection windows. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if the next batch would have recovered naturally. The opportunity may be episodic around migrations rather than recurring enough to build a durable company.
