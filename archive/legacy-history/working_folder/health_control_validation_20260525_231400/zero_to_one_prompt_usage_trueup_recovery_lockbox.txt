Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
API Usage True-Up Recovery Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Recover earned but uninvoiced B2B SaaS/API revenue by taking a signed recovery mandate over named customers whose contracts already define usage overages, minimum commits, or annual true-up fees, then issuing corrected invoices only after usage logs and CFO approval support the claim.

Exact buyer:
20-200 person B2B SaaS, data API, infrastructure API, developer-tool, analytics, workflow, and vertical software vendors with enterprise contracts, usage-based pricing, minimum commits, or overage clauses, but weak billing operations.

Acute trigger:
The vendor has earned usage overage, minimum-commit shortfall, storage/API-volume true-up, extra environment fee, premium feature fee, or annual usage true-up under a signed customer contract, but it was not billed because usage logs, CRM terms, invoicing, customer-success notes, and billing systems do not line up.

Control point:
Signed CFO/founder recovery mandate; contract clause; named customer list; usage/export logs; entitlement or metering evidence; prior invoice history; proposed invoice schedule; customer-notice wording approved by the vendor; corrected invoice; customer acknowledgement, payment, credit note, or clean no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three signed mandates; ten named customer accounts screened; at least 400,000 PLN in candidate underbilled revenue identified; two corrected invoices issued with vendor CFO approval; one customer payment, acknowledged invoice, or scheduled payment; and 30,000-90,000 PLN in fees collected or invoiced.

6-month POC:
Screen 20-40 vendors; review 80-200 customer contracts; review 4,000,000-12,000,000 PLN candidate underbilling; issue or schedule 1,000,000-4,000,000 PLN corrected invoices; convert 400,000-1,500,000 PLN into collected cash or acknowledged AR; collect 200,000-700,000 PLN startup revenue; keep gross margin above 70% after usage-log QA, RevOps support, and contract-boundary review.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target SaaS/API vendors through fractional CFOs, RevOps consultants, SaaS finance communities, usage-billing migration consultants, and founder/CFO outbound. Offer a paid "underbilling recovery screen" only for vendors with signed enterprise contracts, usage logs, and pricing clauses. Do not sell billing software or a general RevOps audit.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-15,000 PLN screening/setup plus 10-20% of collected or customer-acknowledged recovered revenue. Minimum candidate recovery per customer is 25,000 PLN and preferred total mandate value is 150,000+ PLN. The buyer pays because recovered cash and corrected ARR are immediate owner earnings, and the startup is paid only when invoices are issued, acknowledged, or collected.

Copy risk:
RevOps consultants, billing-platform migration firms, accountants, fractional CFOs, customer-success teams, and billing software vendors can copy. The defense is current mandate control, usage-log reconciliation playbooks by billing model, customer-notice patterns that preserve the relationship, and a record of which clauses convert to paid invoices rather than disputes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the vendor signs a mandate, provides contracts and usage evidence, approves the customer notice, and lets the startup prepare or route corrected invoices for named accounts, competitors cannot take that exact recovery file without the vendor restarting a sensitive customer-facing process. Rivals can copy future playbooks, but not the already-authorized invoice queue and customer acknowledgement trail.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a named recovered invoice file, not an audit report or dashboard. If the contract clause, customer, usage ledger, vendor approval, corrected invoice, and payment/acknowledgement path are not present, the startup rejects the case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No legal interpretation, collection agency work, debt collection, factoring, aggressive audit claims, or customer threats. Vendor counsel or CFO approves disputed clauses and customer notices. Reject cases with ambiguous contract language, missing usage truth, customer delivery dispute, product downtime, data-quality defects, privacy issues, or likely churn damage. The startup does not change billing systems, implement metering infrastructure, negotiate contract amendments, or act as customer success.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not PromoLeak because this is vendor-owned SaaS/API revenue recovery, not retailer promotion leakage or deduction recovery. Not Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL overbilling, Retail OTIF, PartnerCenter, MDF, commercial lease deposits, or platform remittance recovery. Not a software app, dashboard, or RevOps consulting retainer; it is current underbilled contract revenue converted into corrected invoice/payment.

Strongest anticipated objections:
RevOps teams, fractional CFOs, billing-platform consultants, accountants, and customer-success leaders can do this internally. Many apparent underbillings will not be collectible because the contract wording is ambiguous, the usage logs are incomplete, the vendor waived fees informally, the customer will dispute the charge, or invoicing the true-up damages retention. Usage-based billing platforms increasingly solve the root problem. Sensitive customer-facing notices require careful CFO/legal approval. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if the vendor planned to invoice anyway. The business can become a generic RevOps audit unless it only accepts named contract-backed cash recovery files.
