Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CylinderDeposit Supplier-Acknowledged Return Credit Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Recover or cash out supplier-acknowledged industrial gas cylinder deposits, returned-cylinder credits, and closed-account balances for multi-site companies after supplier switches, site closures, and branch cleanups, using signed customer mandates and supplier-recognised serial/deposit ledgers.

Exact buyer:
Multi-site industrial SMEs, PE-backed groups, non-clinical labs, food plants, welders, metal fabricators, breweries, hospitality groups, and facility-heavy businesses that used industrial gases across multiple locations, returned cylinders, switched suppliers, closed sites, or inherited messy branch accounts. The strongest first buyer has supplier account statements or return records showing at least 75,000 PLN expected recoverable value from one supplier, or at least 150,000 PLN across a multi-site bundle.

Acute trigger:
Supplier switch, site closure, M&A cleanup, branch wind-down, lab move, plant consolidation, accounting migration, or annual balance-sheet cleanup reveals returned cylinders, unreturned-but-identifiable cylinders, deposit balances, cylinder rental overhang, supplier credit memos, closed-account balances, or unresolved cylinder/deposit schedules. The buyer wants cash, a credit note, an accepted offset, or a clean no-go before the file disappears into old AP/procurement history.

Control point:
Signed customer mandate; account and entity authority; supplier account statement; cylinder serial list where available; delivery and return slips; deposit schedule; branch/site account map; supplier correspondence; supplier acknowledgement of returned items, credit balance, refund path, accepted offset path, missing correction evidence, or no-go reason. The startup controls the named supplier-account file and refund/credit chronology, not the cylinders themselves.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three customer mandates over named supplier accounts or multi-site bundles; two supplier acknowledgements of deposit/credit balances, return correction lists, or accepted offset paths; one refunded cash amount, credit note, or accepted account offset; at least 25,000 PLN collected in fixed and success fees; no physical cylinder handling by the startup.

6-month POC:
Close 12-20 paid supplier-account bundles from accountants, outsourced CFOs, plant-closure advisers, procurement-cleanup consultants, PE portfolio operators, and gas-supplier-switch projects. Put 2,000,000-6,000,000 PLN of candidate acknowledged deposit/credit value into the ledger. Convert at least 800,000-2,000,000 PLN into cash refunds, credit notes, accepted offsets, or written clean no-go outcomes. Build supplier-specific playbooks for required authority letters, serial evidence, deposit schedules, return-slip formats, branch account consolidation, refund forms, and offset rules. Keep gross margin above 65% and reject low-balance or physically handled cases.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start through accountants, outsourced CFOs, PE operating partners, plant-closure advisers, lab/plant relocation vendors, procurement-cleanup consultants, and suppliers-switch projects. The offer is narrow: "If you have supplier statements, deposit schedules, serial lists, or returned-cylinder ledgers after a gas supplier switch or site closure, I cash out acknowledged deposits and credits or produce a clean no-go. I do not touch cylinders or negotiate gas supply."

Economics:
Charge a fixed review fee of 4,000-12,000 PLN per supplier/account bundle plus 15-30% of recovered cash or accepted credits above a minimum. Use higher fixed fees for multi-site bundles with many branch accounts. Reject files below 75,000 PLN expected recoverable value unless bundled into a larger portfolio. Delivery costs are founder reconciliation work, secure document handling, and occasional paid procurement/accounting reviewer support. No inventory capital, no transport, no hazardous-goods exposure, and no cash advance are required.

Copy risk:
Gas suppliers, accountants, procurement consultants, AP recovery firms, plant managers, and outsourced CFOs can copy the checklist. The defense is control over specific signed mandates, branch account maps, serial/deposit ledgers, supplier acknowledgement trails, and refund/credit chronology before any competitor sees the file. Case memory by supplier and account structure improves speed and screening.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the customer signs the mandate, the named supplier-account bundle is in the workroom, the serial/deposit ledger is reconciled, and the supplier has acknowledged the correction, refund, credit, offset, or no-go path, another party cannot take over that exact file without restarting customer authority, evidence intake, branch mapping, and supplier correspondence. Incumbents can copy future workflows, but not the controlled live account-period claim.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
Each accepted file ends with supplier acknowledgement, cash refund, credit note, accepted offset, required correction list, or clean no-go. The startup is not selling advice, a report, software, a database, gas supply introductions, or AP benchmarking. It controls named current-credit files and gets paid from measurable supplier-account outcomes.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not transport, store, inspect, fill, test, certify, rent, sell, or broker gas cylinders. Licensed gas suppliers, customers, and authorised logistics providers remain responsible for cylinder handling, safety, hazardous-goods transport, account terms, rental terms, damage assessment, and supply relationships. The startup does not provide legal advice, debt collection, hazardous-material services, gas procurement, or supplier switching advice. It rejects clinical/medical oxygen, consumer homecare, disputed liability, suspected fraud, missing entity authority, physical pickup requests, and any case requiring pressure tactics or unsafe cylinder handling.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack, REDBlocked Amazon Connected-Device Evidence Rescue Desk, BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack, HeritageDoor Suites, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet. The control point is supplier-acknowledged industrial gas cylinder deposit and return-credit cashout, not regulated-finance evidence, Amazon product evidence, investor healthtech diligence, heritage physical lots, municipal invoice rails, retailer deduction recovery, import continuity, machine-data access, public tender product mapping, supplement review, PV retrofit economics, or heat-pump noise packets.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Clean balances may be too small after rental charges, lost-cylinder fees, damage deductions, contamination, late returns, account debts, minimum charges, branch-level offsets, and contract terms. Supplier statements may not be accurate or may require account-owner-only communication. Gas suppliers, accountants, procurement teams, and AP recovery firms can internalize the workflow. Customers may lack serial lists, return slips, deposit schedules, or entity authority after site closures and mergers. Suppliers may prefer credits against future gas purchases rather than cash refunds. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if the supplier was already going to reconcile the account. The work can collapse into low-margin ticket chasing unless every case starts with visible supplier statements, named account bundles, and high minimum recoverable value.
