## Source Timing Notes

The EUDR obligation set has been postponed and simplified, with large/medium operators generally facing the 30 December 2026 application date and most micro/small operators facing 30 June 2027. The revised approach concentrates due-diligence statement submission on the first operator placing covered goods on the EU market, while downstream actors collect reference numbers in narrower cases.

This creates a near-deadline import and purchase-order continuity problem for covered commodities, especially when EU buyers, forwarders, customs teams, retailers, and distributors start requiring due-diligence statement reference numbers, geolocation evidence, supplier legality files, or no-go decisions before accepting 2027 shipments.

Primary official/context sources checked:

- European Commission / Access2Markets notice on the December 2026 EUDR postponement and simplified obligations.
- European Parliament answer E-003747/2025 on the new application dates and due-diligence statement responsibility.
- Council press material on the targeted EUDR revision.

## Internal Objections Before Real Gate

- This is close to the already-confirmed CBAM Import Continuity Desk. The difference must stay explicit: EUDR proof is supplier/geolocation/legality/DDS reference continuity for covered commodities, not CBAM emissions reporting for steel/aluminium/cement/etc.
- The first operator/importer keeps legal responsibility for the due-diligence statement. The startup cannot be the compliance certifier or legal decision maker.
- EUDR software platforms, certification schemes, large commodity traders, customs brokers, and consultants can copy generic readiness work.
- Supplier data is outside founder control unless the importer/exporter signs a data-release mandate and the case is rejected when data is absent.
- Timeline risk: if enforcement and buyer pressure remain too far away, current willingness to pay could be weak before late 2026.
- Role/classification and country-legality issues can drift into legal advice. These must be routed to counsel or left for operator approval.
