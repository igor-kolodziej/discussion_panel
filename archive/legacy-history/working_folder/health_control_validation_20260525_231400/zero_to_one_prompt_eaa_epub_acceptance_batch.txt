Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EAA EPUB Acceptance Batch

One-sentence thesis:
A prepaid, title-batch remediation lane for small and mid-size EU/CEE publishers, ebook distributors, and library suppliers whose EPUB files, accessibility metadata, or distributor/library acceptance are blocked by European Accessibility Act pressure.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-size Polish, CEE, and EU-adjacent trade publishers, academic publishers, ebook distributors, library suppliers, and digital textbook publishers with named EPUB or ebook title batches that must remain listed, be accepted by a retailer/distributor/library platform, satisfy a procurement framework, or clear a buyer/distributor accessibility review. The economic buyer is the publishing director, production lead, digital distribution manager, rights/operations lead, or CFO responsible for keeping a title batch sellable.

Acute trigger:
A distributor, ebook retailer, library buyer, public-sector framework, procurement customer, aggregator, or internal sales team flags that a title batch has accessibility problems after the European Accessibility Act entered application: EPUBCheck errors, missing or weak accessibility metadata, missing navigation, poor reading order, image-alt gaps, malformed headings/tables, fixed-layout accessibility issues, missing conformance/discoverability data, or a platform/library rejection note that blocks listing, renewal, procurement, or distribution.

Control point:
Signed prepaid batch mandate over specific title files and acceptance tickets: original EPUB/source files, retailer/distributor/library rejection notes or accessibility finding lists, ONIX/accessibility metadata records, EPUBCheck and accessibility checker outputs, title-level remediation queue, publisher authorization to modify files and metadata, named EPUB/accessibility contractor capacity, resubmission package, acceptance or narrowed-defect trail, and explicit no-go rules for titles requiring editorial, legal, rights, translation, image-description, or platform decisions outside the sprint.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign three prepaid publisher or distributor title batches covering at least 45 EPUB titles total; collect at least 35,000 PLN before delivery; secure one EPUB/accessibility specialist and one metadata/ONIX specialist on per-batch terms; run baseline validation for every title; remediate and resubmit at least 30 titles; produce at least 20 accepted titles, narrowed defect lists, distributor/library progress outcomes, or clean no-go decisions; and document reusable issue patterns by title type, platform, metadata field, validation error, remediation step, and acceptance outcome.

6-month POC:
Handle 300-800 titles across 12-25 publisher/distributor batches; collect 180,000-650,000 PLN in batch fees; maintain gross margin above 55% after EPUB/accessibility contractors, metadata specialists, QA, and secure file handling; obtain at least six repeat or referral batches; show that at least 65% of accepted titles move from blocked/at-risk to accepted, narrowed, relisted, procurement-ready, or clean no-go; and build a private acceptance-pattern library across EPUB structure, metadata, platform notes, language, genre, and issue type.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of 120 Polish/CEE/EU publishers, digital production teams, ebook distributors, library suppliers, university presses, and digital textbook publishers with visible ebook catalogs. Outbound only asks for live title batches: "If a distributor, retailer, library, public buyer, or procurement framework is holding or warning a batch because EPUB/accessibility metadata is wrong, send the files and rejection notes. I return remediated files, metadata updates, resubmission notes, and clean no-go decisions in a fixed batch sprint." Also approach ebook conversion shops and accessibility agencies for overflow batches they do not want to staff urgently.

Economics:
Charge 350-700 PLN per straightforward reflowable EPUB title, 800-1,200 PLN for complex illustrated, academic, table-heavy, or fixed-layout titles, and 8,000-45,000 PLN per batch depending on title count and complexity. Publishers pay because a blocked or at-risk batch can stop retailer listing, library supply, procurement acceptance, backlist revenue, or a distributor relationship, and the batch fee is small relative to keeping a catalog sellable. Seller payment is prepaid or milestone-based; no speculative remediation.

Copy risk:
Ebook conversion houses, accessibility agencies, freelancers, publisher production teams, distributors, and metadata vendors can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is narrow live-acceptance positioning, fast fixed-batch packaging, exact title-file custody, repeated acceptance pattern memory, Polish/CEE outreach, contractor bench assembly, and a private library of platform-specific EPUB/accessibility/metadata failure patterns.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the publisher signs the batch, prepays, transfers the title files, rejection notes, metadata records, validation outputs, and resubmission authority, the startup controls that active acceptance queue and delivery schedule. A conversion vendor can compete for future batches, but it cannot remediate the exact in-flight files without the publisher switching mid-deadline and rebuilding the intake, baseline, QA, metadata, and resubmission trail.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The output is not an accessibility audit report, compliance dashboard, metadata database, expert marketplace, training, or brokered introduction. The startup accepts only prepaid title batches with actual files and acceptance pressure, then returns remediated EPUBs, corrected accessibility metadata, validation evidence, resubmission notes, accepted/narrowed platform outcomes, or clean no-go decisions. No files and no acceptance ticket means no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not provide legal opinions, certify EAA compliance, guarantee platform acceptance, rewrite editorial content, translate, create rights-sensitive descriptions without publisher approval, alter copyrighted works outside authorization, advise on exemptions, or make conformance claims for the publisher. Publishers remain responsible for legal/compliance statements, editorial decisions, rights, author approvals, final metadata claims, and public accessibility statements. Reject files requiring deep editorial rewrite, missing rights, unresolved image-description authority, inaccessible source assets, custom reading-system engineering, or open-ended web/app remediation.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from the failed broad EAA buyer accessibility sprint because it is not a web/app/product remediation service; the controlled object is a prepaid batch of exact EPUB files and metadata tied to listing, library, distributor, or procurement acceptance. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender EPREL, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, PPWR Lab-Chain, or any Amazon/platform evidence desk. It also differs from generic document accessibility because the file format, buyer, trigger, artifact, and acceptance trail are ebook-specific.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Ebook conversion vendors and accessibility agencies already exist and may serve this need. Many publishers may have in-house production teams or distributor-provided remediation paths. Some title batches will be low-value backlist with weak willingness to pay. Complex fixed-layout, image-heavy, illustrated, academic, math, or table-heavy books can destroy margins. Metadata-only fixes may be too small, while deep remediation may require editorial and rights decisions outside founder control. Platform acceptance may remain opaque and outside control. The founder needs credible EPUB/accessibility contractor capacity before publishers trust live files. The market may be too narrow in Poland alone, so the first wedge must be EU/CEE from day one.
