## Selected Candidate

**Corporate-Action Residual Payout Assignment**

One-sentence thesis: Corporate and professional holders with already-announced corporate-action cash sitting at an issuer, depositary, trustee, broker, or paying agent will pay for a narrow mandate/assignment desk that gets payor acknowledgement and routes the named payout.

### Why It Clears Simulation

The idea advances because the cash is already payable before the founder enters the workflow. The founder is not creating demand, advising on securities, litigating, trading, brokering a deal, or asking a buyer to believe in a future benefit. The narrow proof object is an accepted mandate or receivable assignment over a named payout.

The strongest beachhead is not broad unclaimed-property recovery. It is B2B holder cleanup around old SPVs, small funds, family offices, founder holding companies, dormant ESOP pools, and companies cleaning post-transaction cap tables. These buyers can have real money stranded by closed brokerage accounts, stale bank details, corporate name changes, expired signatory authority, missing tax forms, cross-border KYC, or old entity paperwork.

The control point is harder than a normal admin service:

- the holder signs a narrow collection mandate or receivable assignment over a specific corporate-action amount;
- the amount is tied to an issuer notice, depositary statement, trustee notice, broker record, or corporate-action event;
- the payor, agent, broker, or depositary acknowledges the mandate, assignment, or permitted payment instruction;
- fees are charged only for acknowledged cases, a paid file fee, or recovered cash;
- no consumer heir-hunting, legal dispute, investment advice, securities trading, or custody of client assets is accepted.

### Internal Cap Review

- Concrete 6-month control proof: passes. A signed mandate/assignment, payor acknowledgement, and first payout are concrete proof.
- One vendor or consultant copy risk: partially passes. Brokers, lawyers, and transfer agents can do the paperwork, but they cannot copy a specific mandate and accepted payment instruction once controlled.
- CAC/payback/margins/cash conversion: passes only if ticket sizes are above 50,000 PLN and acquisition comes through law/accounting/broker cleanup lists, not broad retail search.
- Vague partnerships/goodwill: passes only if first files are introduced by a professional channel with named stranded payouts.
- Regulated authority: passes only with strict boundaries: no securities advice, no litigation, no consumer claims, no regulated custody, and lawyer/notary template review.

Simulated score: **88.2 / 100**

## 6-Month POC

1. Build a professional-channel source list: boutique corporate-law firms, fund administrators, ESOP administrators, M&A accountants, family-office accountants, small brokers, and issuer-service providers.
2. Accept only corporate/professional holders with event evidence and a named payable amount above 50,000 PLN.
3. Use counsel-reviewed mandate and receivable-assignment templates, plus a no-custody payment direction into the holder account or regulated escrow.
4. File acknowledgement requests with the issuer, depositary, trustee, broker, or paying agent.
5. Close or kill files inside 30 days: accepted instruction, requested correction list, or no-go.
6. Target proof: 5 signed mandates, 2 acknowledged payor instructions, 1 cash payout, and 25,000-75,000 PLN collected.

## Kill Conditions

- Paying agents, brokers, or depositaries routinely reject mandate/assignment instructions from a third-party founder.
- Professional channels cannot produce named stranded payouts without the founder doing consumer-style search.
- Cases require substantive legal advice, securities advice, litigation, inheritance work, tax advice, or regulated custody.
- Average ticket size falls below 50,000 PLN or gross margin falls below 60% after counsel/notary/reviewer costs.
- First acknowledgement takes longer than 30 days in most files.
- Buyers prefer their existing broker/law firm once the payout is identified.
- Fraud/KYC risk forces bank-like compliance infrastructure before the first repeatable wedge.
