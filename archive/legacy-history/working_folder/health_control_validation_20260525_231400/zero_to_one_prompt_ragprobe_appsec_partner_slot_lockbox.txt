Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RAGProbe AppSec Partner Slot Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Pre-sell bounded RAG and workflow-agent security evidence modules to appsec boutiques, SOC2 consultants, and fractional CISOs that already own trusted client relationships, then deliver sandbox-only prompt-injection, source-isolation, tool-permission, logging, and human-review evidence annexes for live enterprise buyer blockers.

Exact buyer:
The first buyer is a Polish, CEE, UK, or EU appsec boutique, SOC2 consultant, fractional CISO, security questionnaire firm, or SaaS-security adviser serving B2B SaaS vendors with RAG assistants, enterprise-search tools, document-processing AI, support copilots, workflow copilots, or limited internal agents.

The end economic payor is a B2B SaaS vendor whose enterprise buyer, security team, procurement team, or customer CISO is blocking a PO, renewal, pilot, go-live, security review, or milestone until it sees concrete evidence around prompt-injection handling, tenant/source isolation, retrieval boundaries, tool permissions, model-provider settings, logging, human review, unsafe output handling, or buyer-safe no-go/rework status.

Acute trigger:
A live enterprise buyer request asks for proof that the vendor's RAG or limited agent feature does not leak customer data, retrieve from the wrong tenant/source, accept obvious prompt-injection attacks, call unsafe tools without guardrails, hide model-provider retention settings, or lack logging/human-review evidence. The request is tied to a named commercial event above 100,000 PLN equivalent: procurement approval, renewal, security review, pilot conversion, go-live, contract milestone, or payment hold.

Control point:
The startup does not start with cold vendor trust. It signs prepaid or partner-guaranteed AI-security module slot blocks with appsec/SOC2/fractional-CISO partners that already have client NDAs, client trust, security-review context, and buyer-response ownership.

For each module, control requires:
- signed partner routing or slot agreement;
- prepaid or partner-guaranteed module fee;
- client authorization through the partner;
- exact buyer request or security-review blocker;
- sandbox, staging, or disposable test environment only;
- scoped feature map, source/tenant map, allowed test accounts, and forbidden actions;
- no production destructive actions, no money movement, no live external email sending, no privileged admin actions, and no unsandboxed tool execution;
- partner-approved test plan;
- reproducible test traces, screenshots, logs, and issue disposition;
- buyer-safe annex wording and internal partner notes;
- outcome reporting from the partner: buyer accepted, buyer asked follow-up, product rework/no-go, or formal assurance needed.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three appsec/SOC2/fractional-CISO partners sign paid slot or priority-routing agreements covering at least six modules. At least six modules are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 8,000-22,000 PLN each. At least five modules involve live buyer blockers with a named commercial event above 100,000 PLN equivalent. At least four modules are accepted into partner reports, security-review responses, or buyer evidence rooms. At least two modules produce documented buyer progress, scoped rework, formal-assurance escalation, or paid no-go. At least one partner routes a second module. Gross margin stays above 60% after AI-security reviewer, test setup, insurance/security tooling, and partner margin.

6-month POC:
Deliver 35-50 paid modules through 5-8 partners, with at least 25 buyer-progress, scoped-rework, no-go, or formal-escalation outcomes. Five partners route at least three modules each. Average delivery stays under seven business days for evidence-ready sandbox cases. Reviewer cost stays below 40% of revenue. Fewer than 25% of qualified cases drift into legal AI Act classification, DPIA-only work, formal pentesting, regulated high-risk AI, production incident response, or broad remediation. The private case library covers recurring RAG/agent failure patterns, buyer wording, accepted evidence formats, prompt-injection traces, source-isolation defects, tool-permission gaps, logging gaps, and no-go triggers.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of 80 appsec boutiques, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, and security questionnaire firms serving B2B SaaS vendors. Pitch only a partner-owned module:

"When your SaaS client has a live enterprise buyer asking for RAG or agent security proof, I supply a bounded AI-security annex under your brand. You keep the client, report, and final advice. I handle sandbox test traces, source-isolation checks, tool-permission checks, logging/human-review evidence, rework/no-go tables, and buyer-safe wording. No production testing, no formal pentest claim, no AI Act legal classification, and no regulated high-risk AI."

Offer the first three partners discounted prepaid slot blocks rather than one-off soft referrals: two modules for a fixed upfront price, with partner margin and written outcome reporting required.

Economics:
Charge partners 8,000-12,000 PLN wholesale for narrow RAG/source-isolation annexes, 14,000-22,000 PLN for broader RAG plus limited-agent modules, and 25,000-40,000 PLN for urgent multi-feature evidence-ready packages. Partners resell, bundle, or pass through the work. The end SaaS vendor pays because the fee is small relative to a blocked 100,000-1,000,000 PLN customer deal, renewal, go-live, or enterprise security approval. Delivery uses repeatable test recipes, controlled sandbox setup, and a paid reviewer bench so contribution margin can exceed 60%.

Copy risk:
AI-security firms, appsec boutiques, penetration testers, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, GRC platforms, and AI governance firms can copy pieces. The defense is not a generic checklist. It is control of prepaid partner module slots, repeat trusted partner routing, buyer-wording memory, reusable sandbox-safe test recipes, evidence annex formats that fit partner reports, and a private library of accepted/rejected buyer evidence for RAG and limited-agent systems.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a partner prepays slot capacity, routes a live buyer blocker, obtains client authorization, and locks the startup into the partner's report/evidence workflow, a competitor cannot copy that exact current module without displacing the partner's client workflow during an active security review. A partner or AI-security firm can build a similar module later, but cannot instantly own the already prepaid slot, sandbox access, buyer request, test traces, partner report annex, and outcome reporting for that named case.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup sells a bounded operational evidence module through trusted appsec partners, not a dashboard, marketplace, generic AI governance report, broad penetration test, AI Act advisory, or buyer introduction. Each paid module has a live buyer blocker, scoped sandbox test plan, reproducible traces, issue disposition, partner-branded annex, and outcome record. It refuses generic readiness, template sales, broad AI policy work, and unsandboxed production testing.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification, DPIA opinions, formal penetration-test certification, SOC2 audit opinions, ISO 42001 certification, security certification, model validation, clinical/HR/credit/biometric/law-enforcement review, production incident response, or guaranteed buyer acceptance. It does not test production environments, destructive actions, privileged admin actions, money movement, live external email sending, or regulated high-risk workflows. Partners, client security owners, lawyers, auditors, and appsec firms remain responsible for final security, legal, compliance, remediation, and buyer commitments.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because the buyer trigger is not regulated-finance ICT third-party-risk or DORA Article 28/30 supplier evidence. It is not REDBlocked because it is not Amazon account-health, CE/RED/GPSR product evidence, or connected-device listing rescue. It is not BioSignal because it is not investor diligence or health claims. It is not HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack, or HeatQuiet. It is also materially different from prior failed direct RAGProbe and RAGTrace variants because the control object is prepaid appsec-partner module capacity plus partner-owned client trust and sandbox-only delivery, not a cold-sold direct evidence sprint or a buyer-holdback recovery desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Appsec partners may internalize the module after a few cases. Larger AI-security and pentest firms have stronger credibility for formal assurance. Buyers may reject scoped traces and demand formal pentesting, SOC2 evidence, ISO 42001, DPIA, legal AI Act position, or product remediation. Test failures may require engineering work outside scope. Sensitive prompts, source documents, tenant data, logs, model settings, and tool maps create access friction. Short modules can create false assurance unless the wording is precise. Partner margins can compress if reviewer time is high. Buyer outcomes remain outside startup control. The business fails if partner slots are only informal referrals rather than prepaid or partner-guaranteed current cases.
