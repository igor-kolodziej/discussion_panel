## Lead Candidate: Russia-Origin MTC Customs Release Pack

### Buyer

Primary buyer/channel: Polish/CEE customs brokers, freight forwarders, courier customs teams, and trade-compliance boutiques serving SME importers of iron/steel-containing goods under CN/HS chapters and product classes caught by Russia sanctions origin-proof requirements.

Economic beneficiary: SME importers of steel/iron products, steel-containing components, machinery parts, fasteners, frames, brackets, tools, fittings, and industrial goods whose shipment, customs clearance, customer delivery, or production schedule is delayed because the broker/customs office/carrier asks for proof that the relevant iron/steel inputs do not originate in Russia.

### Acute Trigger

A current shipment, customs declaration, carrier customs query, broker exception, buyer delivery promise, or warehouse release is blocked by a request for mill test certificates, supplier statements, origin of melt/pour/input evidence, product/CN mapping, or documentation showing that listed iron/steel products processed in a third country do not incorporate Russian-origin inputs.

### Transferable Control Point

The startup controls a case only when these are present:

- current written broker/customs/carrier/customer query or hold;
- importer authorization and broker/forwarder route;
- invoice, packing list, CN/HS/product description, supplier contact path, and existing MTCs or origin documents;
- prepaid fixed case fee;
- agreement that the customs broker/importer remains declarant and legally responsible for sanctions/origin statements;
- right to retain anonymized case taxonomy: product type, CN/HS family, evidence requested, MTC gaps, supplier response, customs/broker outcome, and no-go reason.

### 60-Day Proof

1. Two broker/forwarder routing agreements or recurring referral understandings for Russia-origin iron/steel evidence exceptions.
2. Eight prepaid current-shipment cases at 4,000-12,000 PLN each.
3. At least three different importers and at least two brokers/forwarders represented.
4. At least six completed case packs: product/CN scope map, MTC/evidence checklist, supplier RFI, gap/no-go register, broker-ready response file, and escalation boundary note.
5. At least two shipment-progress outcomes: broker accepts file for declaration, carrier/customs query progresses, customs request is satisfied, buyer/customer delivery hold is lifted, or shipment is cleanly killed before demurrage/storage grows.
6. One trade-compliance/customs reviewer on paid per-case terms for flagged cases.
7. 40,000-80,000 PLN collected with >60% gross margin after reviewer and translation costs.

### Economics

Fees:

- 4,000-6,000 PLN for a simple one-supplier, one-shipment MTC gap pack.
- 7,000-12,000 PLN for multi-line/multi-supplier shipment file.
- 15,000-25,000 PLN monthly lane support for repeated importers or broker accounts with 5+ recurring shipments.

The importer pays because the alternative is delayed release, demurrage/storage, missed production/customer delivery, emergency supplier chasing by internal staff, or a failed shipment. The broker pays or resells because a clean file protects the client relationship without turning the broker into a sanctions-origin document factory.

### Copy Risk

Customs brokers, freight forwarders, trade-compliance consultants, sanctions lawyers, and large logistics firms can copy generic checklists. The defense is not legal knowledge. The defense is:

- broker-first current exception routing;
- product/CN evidence patterns by shipment type;
- supplier RFI wording that gets usable MTC/origin responses quickly;
- known no-go patterns;
- accepted/pushed-back broker/customs response files;
- repeat importer lane memory.

### Why Incumbents Cannot Copy Before The Founder Controls The Specific Case

Once a broker or importer routes a current shipment, grants document access, signs the paid case, and starts supplier evidence collection through the startup workflow, a competitor cannot access that shipment file or response clock without the importer switching mid-exception. The exact asset is the live case file: query, docs, supplier trail, MTC gaps, broker wording, and release/no-go outcome.

### Why It Is Not Generic Consulting

The startup does not sell sanctions readiness, legal advice, a dashboard, a supplier database, origin certification, customs declaration filing, or broker introductions. It accepts only current shipment or release-block cases with a written query and prepaid fee. The output is a broker-ready case file: evidence index, supplier RFI, MTC/origin gap register, response draft, and no-go/escalation note.

### Boundaries

The startup does not give legal advice, sanctions opinions, customs classification decisions, origin determinations, certificates, declarations, or clearance guarantees. The importer, broker, customs representative, supplier, and qualified trade/legal advisers remain responsible for declaration, legal interpretation, final origin statements, sanctions compliance, and customs communications. Suspected circumvention, missing evidence, false supplier statements, sanctioned parties, high-risk routes, or requests to fabricate/alter documents are rejected or escalated.

### Duplicate Risk

Closest confirmed idea: CBAM Import Continuity Desk.

Material differences:

- CBAM is carbon-border import continuity, authorised CBAM declarant readiness, emissions/default-value exposure, supplier emissions evidence, and future CBAM certificate cost.
- This idea is Russia-sanctions iron/steel input origin proof for current shipment release, using MTCs/supplier-origin evidence and customs/broker queries.
- The proof artifact is an MTC/origin evidence release file, not a CBAM exposure memo.
- The payment event is current customs/carrier/broker shipment progress or clean no-go under sanctions-origin proof, not CBAM reporting/readiness.

This is still close to CBAM because both use broker-first import documents and steel-related imports. It should be killed if brokers say the same paid CBAM relationship would naturally absorb MTC/sanctions-origin exceptions as a minor add-on rather than a separate paid lane.
