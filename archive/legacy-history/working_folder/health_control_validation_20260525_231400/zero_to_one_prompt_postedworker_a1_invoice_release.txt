Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
PostedWorker A1 Invoice Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A current-payment and site-access release desk for Polish and CEE subcontractors whose EU customer, general contractor, industrial site, or public buyer is blocking worker entry, monthly invoice acceptance, retention release, or framework renewal until named posted-worker A1/social-security and host-country declaration evidence is complete.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE subcontractors with 10-150 workers supplying installation, maintenance, electrical, mechanical, HVAC, industrial outage, fit-out, facade, steel, insulation, scaffolding, machinery, warehouse automation, or specialist construction crews to Germany, France, Belgium, Netherlands, Austria, Nordics, or other EU markets. The first buyer has a named site/customer, written document request, current invoice batch or site-access consequence, and workers already scheduled or already performed work. Exclude temp-work agencies, immigration/work-permit cases, illegal subcontracting, wage-law disputes, employment-status disputes, tax-residence disputes, and cases requiring legal opinions.

Acute trigger:
An EU customer, general contractor, site operator, compliance desk, payroll auditor, or buyer says that named workers cannot enter the site, continue working, receive badges, pass an audit, have monthly invoices accepted, get retention released, or renew a framework unless A1 certificates, posted-worker declarations, employer/worker details, assignment dates, site address, social-security evidence, appointment/representative details, wage/working-time evidence, or host-country portal confirmations are complete and consistent. The subcontractor is not buying general compliance; it is paying because site access or current cash is blocked.

Control point:
Signed release mandate over the named site/customer/invoice blocker; worker list; customer request text; invoice or retention value; site address and dates; employment/social-security status evidence supplied by the employer; A1 application/status trail; issued A1 certificates where available; host-country posted-worker declaration or portal confirmation; customer-specific checklist; missing-document log; payroll/legal escalation list; worker-data minimization and retention rules; customer-ready evidence packet; and payment/site-access outcome tracker. The startup controls the active document-release file, customer checklist, official-document status tracker, and fee right for the named blocker. It does not decide employment status, social-security affiliation, wage compliance, tax position, immigration status, or legality of posting.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Eight paid live blocker cases at 6,000-22,000 PLN each; every case has a written customer/site/invoice request, named workers, named site, current invoice/retention/site-access consequence above 50,000 PLN, employer authorization, and a payroll/accounting/legal escalation owner. At least five cases produce issued A1 certificates, posted-worker portal confirmations, customer acceptance of the packet, site badge/access progress, invoice acceptance, retention release, or paid clean no-go because official documents or legal structure are not available. At least 80,000 PLN is collected within 60 days, and no case counts unless a customer/site/payment outcome is documented.

6-month POC:
Process 45-90 live site/invoice blocker cases across 8-15 subcontractors and 3-5 payroll/accounting/law/referral channels; collect 350,000-1,100,000 PLN in case and batch fees; document 35+ site-access, invoice-acceptance, retention-release, customer checklist, or clean no-go outcomes; build a private taxonomy of customer request wording, country/portal path, A1 evidence gaps, worker-list errors, site-date mismatch patterns, document acceptance formats, no-go triggers, and payment-release outcomes; keep gross margin above 60% after payroll/legal reviewer cost; reject at least 25% of cases that drift into legal, tax, wage, immigration, or fake-document risk.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target Polish subcontractors working abroad through site managers, construction accountants, payroll bureaus, cross-border tax/payroll advisers, industrial-maintenance coordinators, and general contractors' supplier desks. Partner pitch: "When your subcontractor client has workers or invoices blocked by A1/posting document gaps, route the named site file to me. You keep payroll/legal advice. I assemble the customer checklist, A1/status trail, declaration confirmations, worker/site evidence, missing-document log, and customer-ready packet for site access or invoice release." Direct outreach uses public Polish subcontractor references, foreign construction projects, and companies advertising cross-border crews.

Economics:
Charge 6,000-9,000 PLN for triage and missing-document/no-go packet, 10,000-22,000 PLN for one site/customer blocker with up to 20 workers, and 25,000-60,000 PLN for urgent multi-site or monthly-invoice batches. Add 3-7% outcome fee only when a written customer acceptance, invoice release, or retention release above the fixed fee is documented. The subcontractor pays because a 10,000-25,000 PLN packet is rational when a 50,000-500,000 PLN invoice, retention, framework renewal, or crew/site schedule is blocked.

Copy risk:
Payroll bureaus, accountants, employment lawyers, posted-worker consultants, HR outsourcing firms, general contractors, and larger subcontractors can copy pieces. The defense is not legal interpretation. It is fast live-blocker execution, Polish/CEE worker-document reality, country/customer request memory, customer-ready packet formats, official status tracking, referral channels, and no-go discipline around illegal or unresolved employment structures.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the subcontractor signs the mandate, shares the written customer request, worker/site list, invoice or retention value, and authorization, the startup controls the active document-release file, customer checklist response, evidence packet, and fee right for that named site or invoice batch. Payroll firms, lawyers, and accountants can compete generally, but they must displace the already-authorized packet while the customer/site/payment deadline is running.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business refuses general posted-worker compliance, legal opinions, payroll outsourcing, immigration work, labor-law advice, dashboards, marketplaces, and broad HR consulting. It accepts only named site-access or invoice-payment blockers with written customer requests and official-document paths. The deliverable is a release packet: A1/status trail, host-country declaration confirmation, worker/site checklist, missing-document log, escalation notes, customer-ready evidence, and site/payment outcome record.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, employment-law advice, tax advice, social-security affiliation determinations, wage-law compliance opinions, immigration/work-permit advice, payroll processing, worker leasing, representative-of-record services, or guarantees of A1 issuance/customer acceptance. Employers, payroll bureaus, accountants, employment lawyers, tax advisers, host-country representatives, and customers remain responsible for legal compliance, employment status, wage/working-time duties, tax positions, official submissions, worker notices, and customer decisions. Cases involving illegal posting, fake documents, disputed employment status, unpaid wages, tax disputes, immigration/work-permit issues, union/litigation matters, sanctions, or refusal to provide truthful worker records are declined or routed to qualified advisers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not TraceFaktura because it does not handle municipal animal-care all-event invoices. It is not KSeF payment release because the blocker is posted-worker/A1/site-access evidence, not structured invoice acceptance. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, CyberBind, LC discrepancy, Retencja30, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The buyer trigger, control object, and proof artifact are materially different: named cross-border worker/site documents tied to current site access, invoice acceptance, or retention release.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Payroll bureaus, accountants, employment lawyers, and posted-worker specialists may already own the workflow. A1 issuance timing and social-security eligibility are outside startup control. Host-country rules vary and can quickly become legal advice. Subcontractors with weak admin may also have wage, employment-status, tax, or illegal-posting problems that must be rejected. Worker personal data creates GDPR, security, retention, and access-control burden. General contractors may insist on their own portals and may not engage with a third-party coordinator. Case fees may be too high unless invoices or retentions are clearly blocked. Larger subcontractors may already handle this internally. The market may be episodic around inspections, site starts, or invoice disputes rather than recurring. The business can become generic cross-border HR compliance unless live payment/site blockers are enforced.
