Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: Amazon Vendor Central Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
Sign recovery mandates with EU/CEE brands selling 1P to Amazon, extract their live Vendor Central deduction queue, file evidence-backed shortage/chargeback disputes, and collect success fees only from recovered credits.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE brands and distributors with Amazon Vendor Central 1P accounts, recurring PO/invoice/remittance flows, and unexplained or under-disputed shortage claims, chargebacks, price claims, returns, coop/accrual deductions, or compliance fees. The best first buyer is too small for enterprise deduction software but large enough to have 50,000-300,000 PLN of annual Amazon deductions or short-paid invoices.

Acute trigger:
Amazon has already short-paid invoices or deducted cash. Dispute windows and evidence freshness decay. The brand's finance/ops team lacks time to reconcile POs, ASNs, invoices, remittances, POD/BOL, delivery confirmations, chargeback codes, and Vendor Central dispute paths.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate plus delegated Vendor Central/export access, deduction register, evidence folder, dispute authority under client account rules, success-fee right, and live claim-status tracking.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. One signed recovery mandate with success-fee terms and data access authorization.
2. Vendor Central, remittance, PO, invoice, chargeback, shortage, and dispute exports for a defined lookback window.
3. Claim register with at least 50,000-150,000 PLN in candidate deductions.
4. 10-30 evidence-backed disputes filed or reopened.
5. Amazon acknowledgement, case status, credit memo, reversal, or payment evidence for at least one claim.
6. First success-fee invoice or paid recovery fee from recovered or credited cash.

6-month POC:
Month 1: recruit one Amazon Vendor Central operations reviewer, build a deduction-code/evidence checklist, and target 40-60 Polish/CEE brands, e-commerce finance consultants, Amazon ops agencies, and fractional CFOs.
Month 2: sign one recovery mandate, ingest exports, build the claim register, and file the first 10 disputes.
Month 3: record the first Amazon acknowledgement, reversal, credit memo, paid shortage, or documented unrecoverable decision; invoice the first success fee if cash/credit is recovered.
Months 4-6: sign 3-5 recovery mandates, process 300,000-1,000,000 PLN of candidate deductions, recover or credit at least 60,000-150,000 PLN, collect 15,000-45,000 PLN in success fees/setup fees, and build case memory by deduction type, evidence requirement, dispute path, win/loss reason, and client operational root cause.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target Polish/CEE brands that are large enough to be Amazon 1P vendors but not large enough for enterprise deduction software. Use Amazon ops agencies, fractional CFOs, e-commerce finance consultants, retail finance networks, and public brand/vendor lists. Offer a no-recovery/no-success-fee pilot with a small setup fee only if data cleanup is heavy.

Economics:
Charge 20-30% of recovered cash or credited invoices, plus optional 2,000-8,000 PLN setup for messy exports. A single recovered 20,000-50,000 PLN shortage/chargeback queue can justify the first engagement. Founder work is data reconciliation, evidence packet assembly, dispute filing, and status tracking. Capital need is low because the client already owns the claim and Amazon already owes or has deducted the disputed amounts.

Copy risk:
SupplyPike/SPS, Threecolts, Recofoxx, Amazon vendor consultants, AP audit firms, and brand finance teams can do this. The defense is narrow EU/CEE mid-market focus, current mandate/access, case-memory by Amazon deduction type, and immediate first-cash proof. Incumbents cannot copy a specific client's active claim queue after mandate/access is signed.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific claim/payment stream:
Once the brand signs the mandate, grants exports/access, and the desk files current disputes, competitors cannot access or act on those same claims unless the client switches. The desk controls the live queue, evidence trail, claim status, and success-fee right for that mandate period.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business is not selling an audit report, consulting deck, dashboard, or marketplace of auditors. It acts on a signed recovery mandate, files disputes, tracks claim status, and earns from recovered credits/cash. The proof is first recovered cash or credited invoices, not a prettier leakage register.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The desk does not provide legal advice, falsify evidence, impersonate the client, guarantee recovery, or dispute valid deductions dishonestly. The client authorizes access and approves filings under its Vendor Central/account policies. The desk uses client-provided PO, invoice, ASN, POD/BOL, remittance, chargeback, shortage, and delivery evidence; uncertain cases are marked weak or unrecoverable. Tax, accounting, legal, and contractual decisions remain with the client and its advisors.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is closest to PromoLeak Recovery Desk, but materially different in control point and data rail. PromoLeak targets challenger brands losing margin through retailer deductions, promotion mechanics, and modern-trade/document reconciliation. Amazon Vendor Central Recovery Mandate targets the Amazon 1P platform deduction queue, Vendor Central dispute workflows, shortage/chargeback/status tracking, and success-fee recovery from credited Amazon claims. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, or HazmatBlocked.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Amazon Vendor Central recovery is already served by strong incumbents and tools.
Some deductions are valid and unrecoverable.
Vendor Central dispute windows, evidence standards, and opaque denials can reduce win rates.
Brands may be too small for meaningful claim value or too large and already served.
Access/security/legal approval can slow onboarding.
The founder must avoid false evidence, unauthorized account actions, and overpromising recovery.
If the work becomes broad retail finance consulting instead of active Amazon claim recovery, differentiation disappears.
