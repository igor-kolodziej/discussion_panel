Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Kaucja SKU Order Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Unlock Polish beverage retail and distributor orders for named SKUs stuck on deposit-return-system readiness by controlling the operator onboarding file, deposit-mark label proof, SKU/EAN mapping, retailer or distributor acceptance, and released purchase order.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized beverage producers, importers, private-label owners, craft beverage brands, functional drink brands, specialty alcohol-free drink importers, and regional distributors selling beverages in packaging covered by the Polish deposit-return system. The first buyers are those with named SKUs, current retail/distributor demand, and blocked or delayed purchase orders because the buyer, chain, wholesaler, or marketplace wants deposit-system readiness evidence before listing, replenishment, or shipment.

Acute trigger:
A retailer, wholesaler, distributor, or chain buyer will not release a PO, listing, replenishment, or shipment authorization until the beverage SKU has the deposit-system path ready: operator onboarding, correct deposit mark on the label or approved relabel plan, SKU/EAN mapping, packaging type/deposit amount, settlement/reporting route, and buyer-accepted proof. The producer or importer is losing current order value, shelf timing, promotion slots, or distributor trust because deposit-system paperwork, label proof, and buyer acceptance are incomplete.

Control point:
Signed authorization from the producer/importer over a named SKU cluster; current buyer hold or PO condition; operator onboarding/application file; packaging/SKU/EAN/deposit-amount map; label artwork or sticker proof showing the required deposit mark; printer/relabel confirmation where needed; retailer/distributor acceptance trail; shipment or PO-release checklist; and proof that the production provider, importer, distributor, retailer, and deposit-system operator all use the same SKU/package facts. The desk controls the release workflow for exact blocked SKUs, not a generic compliance checklist.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign eight release mandates covering at least 60 beverage SKUs with named blocked or conditional orders; obtain three operator onboarding confirmations or accepted operator files; complete deposit-mark label or relabel proof for at least 30 SKUs; obtain 15 retailer/distributor acceptance emails or portal approvals; release at least 500,000 PLN of purchase-order, listing, replenishment, or shipment value; collect at least 60,000 PLN gross fees; and document no-go reasons for SKUs with wrong packaging scope, missing producer authority, unclear importer responsibility, label rights problems, or no real buyer hold.

6-month POC:
Handle 40-70 producer/importer accounts; process 350-900 SKUs; release 4,000,000-12,000,000 PLN of PO, listing, replenishment, or shipment value; generate 350,000-1,200,000 PLN gross revenue from fixed release fees, urgent batch fees, and small success fees on first released order value; maintain gross margin above 65% after packaging/prepress contractors, operator admin support, translations, and QA; build operator-specific and retailer-specific acceptance playbooks; and keep legal/compliance escalation below 15% of cases by refusing content changes, product-scope disputes, and unclear responsibility cases.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to beverage importers, craft beverage producers, regional distributors, private-label suppliers, label printers, packaging prepress shops, beverage wholesalers, and retail category managers. The first message is SKU-specific: "If a Polish buyer or distributor is holding a listed beverage SKU because deposit-system operator onboarding, label proof, SKU/EAN mapping, or settlement evidence is incomplete, we take a fixed-fee release mandate and only work on named blocked SKUs with current order value." Source initial leads from visible imported beverage brands, label printers seeing urgent deposit-mark changes, distributors with mixed legacy stock, and retailer buyers who need suppliers to clean up blocked SKU packets.

Economics:
Charge 6,000-18,000 PLN per urgent SKU cluster, 800-2,000 PLN per additional SKU in the same packaging/family batch, and 1-3% of first released PO or shipment value where a buyer hold is documented before the mandate. A good pilot account has at least 75,000 PLN of blocked or conditional order value, repeat distribution potential, and enough SKUs that fixed fees are cheaper than losing a listing, promotion window, or shipment. The startup uses contractors for label/prepress QA, translations, and operator admin, but does not print packaging, certify labels, act as legal counsel, or become a deposit-system operator.

Copy risk:
Operators, EPR consultants, packaging agencies, label printers, distributors, and large beverage companies can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is narrow urgency, case memory around operator and retailer acceptance patterns, control over exact blocked SKU files, buyer-hold evidence, SKU/EAN/deposit mappings, label proofs, and retailer/distributor acceptance trails before a generic consultant can reconstruct the release packet.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the producer signs a release mandate for named SKUs and provides the buyer hold, SKU/EAN data, label files, operator route, distributor contact, and current PO/listing condition, competitors can advise on deposit-system compliance generally but cannot instantly control the same release trail or buyer acceptance sequence without redoing the file, obtaining producer authorization, and reopening the retailer/distributor communication. The moat is case-level control of the specific SKU order release, not ownership of the whole deposit-system category.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not sell general deposit-system advice, dashboards, compliance reports, operator introductions, or packaging design. It accepts only named beverage SKU clusters with a current buyer hold or conditional order and gets paid to move that exact SKU packet from blocked to accepted through operator onboarding, label/deposit-mark proof, SKU/EAN mapping, retailer/distributor acceptance, and released PO or shipment authorization. No named SKU block and no current order value means no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not act as a deposit-system operator, legal adviser, tax adviser, waste-management authority, packaging certifier, retailer of record, importer of record, or producer of record. Do not certify legal compliance, packaging recyclability, tax treatment, product classification, alcohol rules, food claims, product safety, or label content. Producers/importers remain responsible for legal obligations, deposit-system membership decisions, label content, reporting, contracts, fees, and market placement. Reject cases involving disputed packaging scope, alcohol licensing questions, food-safety claims, product composition changes, false label history, unclear importer authority, no operator path, no buyer hold, or no current commercial order value.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because the buyer trigger is Polish beverage SKU/order release under the deposit-return system, not regulated-finance ICT supplier evidence. It is distinct from REDBlocked because there is no Amazon connected-device regulatory ticket. It is distinct from CBAM Import Continuity Desk because the controlled event is deposit-system operator/label/SKU acceptance for beverage packaging, not carbon border import reporting. It is distinct from GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, BatteryFit, Data Act, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, and BioSignal Claim-Risk because it is neither tender evidence, battery data, machine-data access, municipal invoice routing, deduction recovery, physical salvage, nor investor diligence.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Deposit-system operators may already onboard producers directly, making the desk unnecessary. Packaging agencies, label printers, EPR consultants, and distributors may provide the same help cheaply. Retailers may accept transitional stock or handle supplier readiness without blocking meaningful orders. The opportunity may be a temporary 2026 transition wave rather than a durable business. Gross fees may be too small if most producers have only one or two SKUs. The business can drift into legal compliance, label approval, food/alcohol rules, or packaging design. Retailer acceptance may be slow and outside the founder's control. Producers may blame the desk for operator delays, printer delays, rejected label proofs, or incorrect packaging classification. The model only works if there are many named blocked SKU orders where speed is worth paying for and the founder can repeatedly produce accepted release packets faster than incumbents.
