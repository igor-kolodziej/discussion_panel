## Strongest Objections

1. Professional ITAD providers, Apple MSPs, leasing companies, refurbishers, and device brokers already know how to wipe, release, grade, and resell devices.
2. If incumbents already handle the clean lots, remaining lots may be messy: missing admin access, ex-employee Apple IDs, weak serial records, unclear title, damaged hardware, low-value old devices, or lease ambiguity.
3. Release sequence errors can create unrecoverable exceptions, especially if devices are released before Activation Lock handling is completed.
4. Data erasure, chain of custody, secure transport, exception handling, and insurance are serious liability points.
5. Refurbisher deposits may not arrive before physical inspection and grading; conditional offers are more realistic than deposits.
6. Per-device pricing is vulnerable if exception rates reach 20-30%.
7. Schools and SMEs may lack purchase invoices, lease records, assignment records, or accurate serial manifests.
8. Buyers may prefer full-service ITAD rather than a narrow release desk.
9. One lot can produce cash but does not prove repeatable channel supply.
10. The topic still has scam-adjacent optics unless positioned strictly as company-owned refresh-lot release, erasure, and resale acceptance.

## Useful Lesson

This is locally executable and cleaner than chasing original owners for refurbishers, but it remains too close to professional ITAD/MSP work. Do not continue Apple-device release variants unless the founder controls a repeated channel or a current payment event that incumbents cannot already own.
