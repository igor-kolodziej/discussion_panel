## Strongest Objections

1. Brokers may see this as work they should already provide, not a paid client add-on. The offer must be technical evidence extraction for the broker, not form-filling or coverage help.

2. MSPs are natural owners of the evidence. MFA screenshots, EDR exports, backup logs, patch data, restore-test records, and admin-access evidence usually sit with the MSP or internal IT team. A strong MSP can do it directly; a weak MSP may delay or resist.

3. Evidence packaging cannot fix missing controls. If there is no MFA, EDR, restore proof, patching discipline, or incident-response plan, better writing will not make the client insurable.

4. Insurance-distribution boundaries are serious. The startup must not advise on coverage, insurer choice, limits, exclusions, policy wording, negotiation, or act as broker of record. Broker compensation or referral flows need legal review.

5. Sensitive security data creates real liability. The startup may touch infrastructure diagrams, admin evidence, EDR exports, backup configuration, vulnerability reports, remote-access details, and incident-response documents. Secure intake, minimization, access logs, retention/deletion policy, NDA, and DPA are table stakes.

6. Underwriters may not value polished PDFs. Many want exact portal answers, yes/no attestations, or broker notes. The product must map exact question text to evidence object, source, owner/MSP attestation, limitations, and remediation notes.

7. Pricing may be squeezed. 5,000-15,000 PLN is plausible only when a contract, binder, renewal, premium, exclusion, financing, or procurement requirement is blocked. Routine cyber-policy questionnaires may not pay.

8. Scope creep into remediation consulting is likely. Clients will ask for MFA setup, IR plan writing, EDR choice, backup adequacy opinions, or answers that make controls look better than they are.

9. The moat is modest. Broker routing, accepted examples, underwriter-question taxonomy, evidence-object library, renewal memory, and speed help, but brokers, MSPs, cyber consultants, and insurers can all build similar checklists.

10. Channel trust is the main weakness. An unknown solo founder has no automatic permission to sit between broker, insured, MSP, and underwriter.

## Interpretation For Next Rounds

This failure rejects broker-routed evidence work as insufficient unless the founder owns something more durable than current-case routing and accepted-response memory. Even with a real deadline and good timing, Zero To One capped it as a copyable expert-service wedge with trust and regulated-distribution issues.

The next search should move farther toward titled scarce assets, assigned acknowledged receivables, direct payment streams, or exact lots with buyer deposits. Evidence-pack variants should be treated as likely subthreshold unless the channel/control mechanism is materially stronger than broker referrals.
