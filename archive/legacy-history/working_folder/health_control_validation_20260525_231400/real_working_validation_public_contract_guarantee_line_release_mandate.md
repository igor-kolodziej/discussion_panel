## Strongest Objections

1. Many guarantees remain active for valid reasons: unresolved acceptance, defects, penalties, claims, warranty periods, missing handover documents, or beneficiary discretion.
2. Broadening beyond beneficiary-acknowledged release improves volume but increases legal/procurement dispute risk.
3. Competent contractor CFOs, procurement admins, surety brokers, and bank relationship managers should already maintain guarantee schedules.
4. Beneficiaries may ignore a third-party coordinator and insist on official contractor channels.
5. Success-fee attribution is weak when an expiry or cancellation might have occurred automatically.
6. Cash deposits and guarantee capacity have different value; released capacity is valuable only if tied to a new tender, covenant, surety renewal, or working-capital need.
7. Public-procurement boundaries are dangerous if the startup drifts into interpreting rules or entitlement.
8. The instrument list is too broad: bid deposits, performance guarantees, warranty guarantees, insurance guarantees, bank guarantees, standby letters of credit, and surety bonds all have different mechanics.
9. The 60-day proof is aggressive without warm broker/CFO channels.
10. Large disputed guarantees are tempting but would turn the business into legal dispute handling.

## Useful Narrowing

The evaluator would start with:

- bid deposits or bid guarantees after lost or expired tender;
- performance guarantees after acceptance;
- warranty guarantees after expiry or clear step-down.

Accept only:

- named guarantee/deposit instrument;
- amount, expiry, beneficiary, bank/insurer/surety number;
- contract/tender file;
- acceptance, lost tender, expiry, no-claim, or step-down proof;
- clear beneficiary and bank/insurer route;
- no active dispute;
- meaningful value.

Reject:

- active defects;
- penalties;
- unresolved acceptance;
- legal disputes;
- insolvency;
- ambiguous beneficiary rights;
- missing instruments;
- premature requests;
- low-value files;
- anything requiring legal/procurement interpretation before a basic release request.

## Lesson

Cash and credit-line release alone is not enough when the evaluator sees the work as normal CFO/broker administration or as drifting into legal/procurement interpretation. The next candidate needs either an unusually mandatory regulatory buyer trigger or an already-controlled current payment stream that an internal team cannot plausibly clean up with routine follow-up.
