Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

Acknowledged True-Up Payment-Direction Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:

Release already-accepted SaaS/API/data-vendor true-up receivables only after the customer or enterprise AP owner has acknowledged the amount, invoice, PO, amendment, usage statement, or payment path; control a signed payment-direction/release file and collect only when current cash moves.

Exact buyer:

B2B SaaS, API, data, infrastructure-software, and usage-priced vendors with 20-300 employees, 1-20 enterprise customers, and named customer-acknowledged true-up receivables above 100,000 PLN equivalent. The economic buyer is the vendor CFO, founder, controller, RevOps lead, or billing owner. The customer/debtor is an enterprise buyer that has already accepted the commercial entitlement through a signed amendment, order form, true-up statement, approved usage report, PO request, invoice approval, AP ticket, procurement acknowledgement, or written payment-date path.

Acute trigger:

The vendor has already earned and commercially agreed the true-up, overage, minimum-commit shortfall, data-volume adjustment, API usage charge, seat/device expansion, or annual usage reconciliation, but cash is stuck because enterprise AP/procurement/vendor-master/PO/legal-entity/tax/payment-cycle steps have not been closed. The vendor does not need underbilling discovery; it needs an acknowledged receivable converted into cash without damaging the customer relationship.

Control point:

Signed vendor mandate for a named receivable; debtor/customer acknowledgement of amount or payment path; contract/order-form/amendment or true-up clause supplied by vendor; customer-approved usage statement or accepted invoice; PO/AP/procurement ticket where available; named debtor legal entity and vendor entity; payment-direction or release instruction approved by the vendor and acknowledged by the customer/AP owner where possible; correspondence chronology; secure evidence room; no-go memo for any disputed entitlement; and success-fee right tied only to actual paid cash or customer-confirmed payment release.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Three vendors sign mandates covering named acknowledged true-up receivables above 100,000 PLN each; every case includes written customer/AP/procurement acknowledgement or signed order/amendment before acceptance; at least one customer/AP owner confirms payment direction, PO release, invoice approval, payment date, or payment-cycle unblock; at least one receivable above 150,000 PLN is paid or scheduled in writing; the startup collects at least 15,000 PLN in setup or success fees; and no accepted case involves disputed usage, surprise backbilling, legal collection, customer insolvency, service-credit disputes, strategic-account pressure, or ambiguous contract interpretation.

6-month POC:

Control 15-30 acknowledged true-up release cases across 8-15 vendors; move 2,000,000-6,000,000 PLN equivalent of customer-acknowledged receivables into paid, scheduled, or clean no-go status; collect 150,000-500,000 PLN in setup and success fees; secure three repeat channels from billing-migration firms, usage-billing consultants, fractional CFOs, RevOps operators, or SaaS finance advisers; keep average intake-to-release packet below five business days for evidence-ready cases; maintain gross margin above 70%; and document win/loss reasons by AP blocker, customer entity issue, PO blocker, usage statement type, and invoice approval path.

First acquisition mechanism:

Start with billing-migration partners, usage-based pricing consultants, fractional SaaS CFOs, RevOps consultants, and enterprise billing implementation firms that discover accepted-but-unpaid true-ups during cleanup. Direct outreach to vendors is specific: "If a customer has already accepted a true-up or overage but the PO, AP, vendor-master, or invoice-release path is stuck, I package the exact acknowledged receivable, payment path, and AP release file and get paid only when cash moves or a clean no-go is documented."

Economics:

Charge 5,000-15,000 PLN prepaid setup for evidence-ready cases above 100,000 PLN, plus 3-8% of paid cash or 1-3% of customer-confirmed scheduled payment where the vendor accepts the attribution. A single 300,000 PLN release can support a 15,000-30,000 PLN fee. The business has low capital needs: secure document handling, templates, one legal/accounting boundary review, and part-time contractors for evidence indexing and AP/procurement chronology.

Copy risk:

CFOs, AR teams, RevOps teams, customer-success teams, billing-platform consultants, fractional CFOs, collections firms, and usage-billing migration partners can copy the workflow. The defense is not generic AR skill; it is the specific controlled release file, debtor/customer acknowledgement, payment-direction or AP release chronology, secure evidence room, partner channels, strict no-go discipline, and accumulated memory of which acknowledged true-up blockers convert to cash without damaging accounts.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

Once the vendor signs the mandate, the customer/AP owner acknowledges the amount or payment path, and the startup controls the named receivable evidence room, payment-release chronology, and fee right for that exact invoice or true-up, a competitor cannot instantly own that specific customer acknowledgement, correspondence trail, AP release file, or payment-direction workflow unless the vendor replaces the startup mid-case. Incumbents can build a similar process for future receivables, but they cannot recreate the already-controlled release file and attribution trail.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The startup does not sell underbilling analysis, a dashboard, RevOps consulting, billing implementation, legal collections, factoring, debt purchase, or a marketplace of AR helpers. It accepts only named, customer-acknowledged receivables and produces an operational payment-release artifact: approved usage statement, invoice/PO/AP packet, payment-direction or release chronology, customer-approved no-go if blocked, and fee only from actual cash movement or customer-confirmed payment scheduling.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

The startup does not interpret contracts as legal advice, sue or threaten customers, buy receivables, lend money, factor invoices, provide tax/VAT/accounting opinions, contact customers without vendor approval, pressure strategic accounts, or handle disputed debts. Vendor CFOs, counsel, accountants, RevOps owners, and customer-success owners remain responsible for commercial decisions, contract interpretation, customer relationship management, invoicing, tax treatment, and whether to pursue payment. Customer AP/procurement remains responsible for payment timing and internal approvals. Reject disputed usage, surprise backbilling, informal waivers, service-credit issues, delivery disputes, customer insolvency, old debt, ambiguous clauses, or cases where collection pressure would risk the account.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This is not PromoLeak because it does not recover retailer deductions, promotions, rebates, retail media, or FMCG trade leakage. It is not Amazon Vendor Central recovery, 3PL overbilling, PartnerCenter incentive release, cloud marketplace remittance recovery, credit-memo cashout, or customer-acknowledged true-up cash release as previously tested because the required control point is stronger: external customer/AP acknowledgement plus a payment-direction or AP release file for a named true-up before acceptance. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Clean cases may already be handled by competent CFO, AR, RevOps, billing, or customer-success teams. Customer acknowledgement may still leave PO, vendor-master, procurement, budget, tax, or legal-entity blockers that the startup cannot control. Enterprise AP teams may ignore outside coordinators. Success-fee attribution may be disputed if payment would have arrived anyway. Sensitive contract, usage, invoice, and customer email data creates trust friction. Channel partners can internalize after seeing the checklist. The pool of large, clean, acknowledged, non-disputed true-ups may be too small. The work can drift into ordinary AR follow-up, billing migration, RevOps consulting, legal collection, or customer-success escalation unless intake rules stay severe.
