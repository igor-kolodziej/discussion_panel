Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

TradeCredit Claim Release Mandate

One-sentence thesis:

Exporters, wholesalers, and B2B distributors that already bought trade-credit insurance can still lose or delay indemnity because the live claim file is incomplete; the wedge is a prepaid, broker-routed claim-evidence release desk for named insured receivables, not insurance brokerage, debt collection, or generic AR recovery.

Exact buyer:

Polish and CEE exporters, wholesalers, and B2B distributors with existing trade-credit insurance and one or more unpaid insured receivables above roughly 250,000 PLN equivalent.

The best first buyer is not an uninsured company looking for cover. It is a policyholder whose credit-insurance broker, export accountant, fractional CFO, or finance lead already has a live debtor default, insolvency, overdue receivable, claim submission, claim deficiency request, or indemnity timing problem.

Acute trigger:

A named insured debtor has not paid, entered insolvency, exceeded an overdue threshold, or triggered a claim route, and the policyholder's cash recovery is blocked or delayed by claim evidence requirements.

Accepted triggers include:

- insurer or broker asks for missing delivery, invoice, contract, reminder, collection, insolvency, credit-limit, or policy-compliance evidence;
- claim portal shows missing items, deficiency status, pending documents, or rejected evidence;
- the policyholder has a claim deadline and scattered documents across ERP, email, warehouse, freight, accounting, broker, and debt-collection folders;
- indemnity timing matters because working capital, bank covenant, replacement order, or supplier payment depends on the insured receivable.

Control point:

The startup accepts only one named insured claim file at a time under a signed administrative mandate from the policyholder.

The controlled file must include:

- trade-credit insurance policy or schedule excerpt;
- insured debtor name and credit-limit evidence;
- invoice and unpaid balance;
- delivery or service-performance proof;
- payment terms and overdue chronology;
- reminder and collection chronology;
- broker or insurer requirement list, portal status, support email, or deficiency text;
- policyholder authorization to assemble the evidence room and route communications through the policyholder, broker, or authorized claim channel.

The startup does not sell or place insurance, advise on coverage, collect debt, finance receivables, hold client funds, threaten debtors, provide legal opinions, or submit anything the policyholder has not approved.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Within 60 days, the founder must produce:

- 2-4 signed claim-file mandates;
- prepaid fixed screen/reconstruction fees;
- at least 1,000,000 PLN equivalent insured receivable face value under file control;
- policyholder-approved evidence rooms for at least two claims;
- at least one insurer or broker acknowledgement, accepted deficiency response, claim status movement, indemnity schedule, partial payment, or written clean no-go;
- one repeat channel that can send a second live claim file.

6-month POC:

The POC is a broker-routed claim evidence desk, not a broad credit-management service.

Targets:

- 12-25 paid claim files;
- 4,000,000-12,000,000 PLN equivalent insured receivable face value reviewed;
- 8-15 insurer/broker progress, accepted-response, indemnity-scheduled, partial-payment, or clean no-go outcomes;
- 3 repeat broker/accountant/CFO/export channels;
- 150,000-500,000 PLN revenue;
- gross margin above 60%;
- at least 35% of leads rejected because the file is not document-fixable, not insured, too small, legally disputed, or broker/insurer-owned enough that the startup adds no value.

First acquisition mechanism:

Start through people already near live insured-receivable problems:

- trade-credit insurance brokers that do not want to spend senior time reconstructing messy claim files;
- export accountants and fractional CFOs serving exporters with insured receivables;
- export associations and chambers where members report debtor defaults;
- debt-collection firms that find policy evidence gaps before claim submission;
- CFO groups for wholesalers and distributors with insured debtor books.

The first outreach asks for a live claim deficiency, not a discovery call:

"If a client already has trade-credit insurance and a named unpaid insured receivable, I turn the claim requirement list, invoices, delivery proof, overdue chronology, broker thread, and source documents into a policyholder-approved claim evidence room in five business days. Fixed prepaid fee. No insurance placement, no debt collection, no coverage opinion."

Economics:

Initial pricing:

- 2,000-4,000 PLN paid triage for one named claim file;
- 8,000-18,000 PLN fixed claim-evidence reconstruction fee for a complete file;
- optional 5-12% success fee only on clearly attributed indemnity uplift, accepted deficiency cure, or accelerated scheduled indemnity where the policyholder and broker agree attribution in writing.

Cost base:

- founder time for evidence indexing, chronology, portal/status ledger, source-document chase, and policyholder-approved response pack;
- part-time trade-credit claim reviewer or broker-side reviewer for boundary checks;
- lawyer only for routed no-go/legal flags, not routine work;
- secure document storage and deletion process.

The fee is credible only when the insured receivable is large enough that faster acceptance, avoided rejection, or a clean no-go is worth the fixed fee.

Copy risk:

Credit-insurance brokers, credit managers, accountants, debt collectors, lawyers, and claims consultants can copy pieces of the workflow.

The copy risk is reduced by accepting only live deficiency files where the policyholder has scattered evidence, the broker wants overflow execution rather than strategy, and the startup controls the exact claim file, evidence index, chronology, and response trail before any rival sees the issue.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

This is not category-level protection. It is file-level control.

After signing the mandate, the founder controls the working copy of the policyholder's named claim evidence room: invoice set, delivery proof, overdue chronology, broker/insurer requirement text, portal/status screenshots, source-document checklist, response draft, and no-go logic. A broker, accountant, or lawyer can still advise the client, but another operator cannot take over that live reconstruction file before the first insurer/broker response unless the policyholder revokes the mandate.

The compounding asset is the private pattern library of claim deficiencies by insurer, debtor event, document type, proof gap, overdue timeline, accepted response, indemnity timing, and clean no-go reason.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

It is not sold as claim advice, insurance placement, debt collection, credit management, a report, or a dashboard.

It activates only when a named insured receivable and claim route already exist. The deliverable is a policyholder-approved claim evidence room, deficiency response package, chronology, insurer/broker status ledger, and accepted/no-go outcome tied to that claim. The startup does not introduce insurers, sell policies, buy claims, finance receivables, collect from debtors, or intermediate payment.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- The insurer, broker, lawyer, debt collector, and policyholder remain responsible for insurance, legal, collection, and coverage decisions.
- The startup does not interpret coverage, decide whether the insurer must pay, or advise on litigation, insolvency filings, sanctions, fraud, assignment, or debtor enforcement.
- The startup rejects claims involving alleged fraud, forged documents, sanctions/export-control issues, active litigation, disputed delivery, insolvency-law strategy, insurance-misrepresentation risk, unclear policyholder authority, or attempts to backdate or alter records.
- The startup handles only policyholder-supplied or broker/insurer-requested evidence and every outbound response is approved by the policyholder or broker.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This differs from PromoLeak because it is not retailer deduction, promotion, marketplace, or distributor leakage recovery. It begins with an existing trade-credit insurance policy and an insured debtor claim.

It differs from DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, and TraceFaktura because the control object is a named insured receivable claim file and possible indemnity movement, not compliance evidence, marketplace reinstatement, import continuity, machine data access, tender evidence, or municipal invoice routing.

It differs from prior generic AR, true-up, carrier credit, and platform recovery attempts because the buyer already has a contracted insurer claim route and the first proof is broker/insurer claim status movement or a clean no-go, not a vague payment chase.

Strongest anticipated objections:

1. Credit-insurance brokers may already handle good claims, leaving only bad or messy files.
2. Insurers may reject claims for valid policy reasons that better evidence cannot fix.
3. The founder must avoid insurance advice, debt collection, legal interpretation, and coverage opinions.
4. Policyholders may not trust an unknown founder with sensitive debtor, policy, invoice, delivery, and collection files.
5. Brokers may see the startup as a competitor or liability risk rather than an overflow partner.
6. Claim evidence may be missing, contradictory, late, or commercially embarrassing.
7. Some unpaid receivables are delivery disputes, quality disputes, sanctions issues, fraud concerns, or buyer counterclaims, not clean insured defaults.
8. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if the insurer was already processing the claim.
9. The market may be smaller than expected after filtering for insured, high-value, document-fixable, broker-routed claims.
10. A few accepted claim files prove useful cashflow, but durable defensibility requires repeat broker/accountant/CFO channels and a private claim-deficiency pattern library.
