Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Qualified-Signature Renewal Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Option or buy the expiring-certificate customer book of a retiring mobile qualified-signature registration agent or e-signature reseller, then transfer only customer-approved renewal payment direction and support routing while the qualified trust service provider remains identity verifier, certificate issuer, and provider of record.

Exact buyer:
Corporate customers, accountants, small construction and tender contractors, board members, HR/payroll offices, exporters, and SME finance/admin teams already using qualified electronic signatures or seals for KSeF, tenders, board filings, tax/payroll filings, contracts, banking administration, or official submissions. The source seller is a small mobile registration agent, local reseller, accountant-adjacent signature desk, or e-signature support operator with an expiring-certificate calendar and current customer invoices.

Acute trigger:
Qualified signature or seal certificates expire soon, and customers need continuity for KSeF, public tenders, board/company filings, payroll/tax work, customer contracts, or official submissions. The retiring reseller/agent is no longer willing to chase renewals, support appointments, route identity steps, manage tokens, or maintain the phone/domain/mailbox that customers call when certificates expire.

Control point:
Signed seller option over customer list, expiring-certificate calendar, renewal history, current invoices, phone/domain/mailbox, customer support scripts, provider/reseller route, and seller endorsement; written QSTP/provider confirmation of the allowed reseller/support route; customer approval for renewal contact, payment direction, and support transfer; current renewal invoices; and no-go list for certificates that must be handled directly by the provider or customer.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign one seller option; verify at least 100 certificates expiring in the next 120 days; obtain at least 40 customer renewal approvals; collect at least 50,000 PLN in current renewal/admin fees before seller payout; complete at least 25 successful renewals, provider-routed appointments, seal/signature reissues, or paid no-go outcomes; and get written provider confirmation that the startup is not acting as a qualified trust service provider.

6-month POC:
Acquire or option 2-4 small renewal books covering 400-1,000 expiring certificates; retain at least 250 paying customer renewals; collect 180,000-600,000 PLN in renewal/admin revenue; maintain gross margin above 45% after provider fees, seller earnout, contractors, support, and token/logistics costs; document renewal conversion by customer type, expiry window, provider, identity-flow type, and support burden; and convert at least 80 customers into annual certificate-calendar/admin subscriptions.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to small mobile registration agents, local e-signature resellers, accountant-adjacent signature desks, tender-support operators, and IT/admin service firms whose owners are retiring, overloaded, or tired of low-margin renewal chasing. The offer is a low-upfront option plus earnout on retained renewals, with customers approving payment transfer before close and the old seller endorsing the handoff.

Economics:
Revenue comes from renewal/admin margins, corporate calendar packages, token/logistics fees, seal/signature support fees, and bulk renewal service fees. Target 250-800 PLN gross margin per individual certificate or seal renewal, higher for corporate batches and annual admin packages. Seller payout is mostly earnout on collected renewals, keeping upfront cash under 100,000 PLN. The provider remains the certificate issuer and identity verifier.

Copy risk:
Qualified trust service providers, larger resellers, accountants, IT firms, and tender-support firms can sell renewals directly, but they do not control the retiring seller's customer list, renewal calendar, phone/domain/mailbox, seller endorsement, and current customer payment approvals before the startup options the book. The defense is book-level control plus current expiry urgency, not secret technology.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs the option and customers approve renewal/payment transfer, the startup controls the current expiry calendar, customer communication path, renewal invoices, support routing, and fee right for those certificates. A QSTP or reseller can market broadly, but must displace an already-endorsed handoff during the customer's renewal window.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business is an acquisition-led renewal-book handoff with signed seller option, customer approvals, current invoices, and provider-routed renewals. It does not issue certificates, verify identity outside the provider process, sell generic e-signature consulting, build a marketplace, or provide a dashboard. The first proof is customer-approved renewal cash and completed provider-routed renewals.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as a qualified trust service provider, certificate issuer, identity verifier, registration authority unless separately authorized by the QSTP, legal adviser, tax adviser, tender adviser, or KSeF compliance consultant. The qualified provider remains responsible for identity verification, certificate issuance, revocation, certificate policies, trust-service terms, and legal validity. The startup handles only customer renewal coordination, calendar/admin support, provider routing, and customer-approved payment transfer within the provider's allowed reseller/support model.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from KRS address/e-delivery continuity, MailDomain, fiscal-device, calibration, and generic IT admin book ideas because the trigger is expiring qualified certificates/seals used in official signing workflows and the provider-of-record remains a QSTP. It is distinct from DORA, PromoLeak, CBAM, REDBlocked, Data Act, TraceFaktura, and recovery mandates because the control object is a warm renewal book and current payment direction, not an evidence pack, claim queue, import file, product listing, or municipal/payment-recovery rail.

Strongest anticipated objections:
QSTPs may sell directly and bypass resellers; provider margin may be too thin; customers may use the renewal event to switch to a larger provider; remote identity flows and provider portals may reduce the need for mobile agents; seller books may be messy, low-ARPU, or not transferable; customers may not approve payment transfer before close; support burden around lost tokens, board changes, foreign directors, revocations, seal/signature type confusion, KSeF authorizations, and tender deadlines can eat margin; the model may be a local cash-flow rollup rather than a defensible startup unless multiple books can be sourced and retained.
