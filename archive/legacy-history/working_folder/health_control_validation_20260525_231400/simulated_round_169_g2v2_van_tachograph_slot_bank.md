## Chosen Candidate

Idea name: G2V2 Van Tachograph Slot Bank

One-sentence thesis: Buy and hold near-deadline approved workshop capacity for smart tachograph version 2 retrofits in 2.5-3.5t international courier vans, then sell VIN-specific installation packages to fleets that cannot legally run cross-border after 1 July 2026 without fitted and calibrated devices.

Exact buyer: CEE and UK/EU small fleet operators with 5-80 vans between 2.5 and 3.5 tonnes used for international hire-and-reward transport or cabotage, especially courier subcontractors serving Germany, Benelux, Ireland/UK, and cross-border ecommerce logistics.

Acute trigger: From 1 July 2026, in-scope light commercial vehicles over 2.5 tonnes used for international goods transport/cabotage must carry smart tachograph version 2. The deadline is near enough that waiting for normal workshop availability can strand revenue-producing vans.

Control point: Signed workshop capacity contracts for exact installation dates, specific tachograph units or supplier allocation where possible, and VIN-tagged customer deposits that reserve those dates. The founder is not selling advice; the controlled asset is dated install capacity and the first proof is a calibrated vehicle.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

- Two to four approved tachograph workshops sign slot reservations for a combined 40-80 installation/calibration appointments before and shortly after 1 July 2026.
- Five fleet customers sign VIN schedules and pay 30-50 percent non-refundable deposits for specific slots.
- First 5-10 vans receive installed/calibrated G2V2 tachographs with workshop certificates.
- Founder issues first invoices for the capacity spread and scheduling package.

6-month POC:

- Start in Poland plus one nearby cross-border corridor, then add a second country only if workshop availability is locked first.
- Sell 120-180 VIN-specific retrofit packages across 15-30 fleets.
- Maintain a live slot ledger with workshop PO, fleet deposit, VIN, vehicle weight, international-use attestation, installation date, calibration certificate, and post-install invoice.
- Add post-deadline emergency pricing for vans stopped from international dispatch because no slot is available.

Economics:

- Customer price: 1,450-2,250 EUR per van depending on urgency, geography, and included installation/calibration.
- Workshop/device cost: 950-1,600 EUR per van.
- Gross margin: 300-650 EUR per van after payment processing and coordinator costs.
- POC target: 140 vans at 430 EUR blended gross margin = 60,200 EUR gross profit.
- Cash conversion: customer deposit before workshop slot is consumed; no large device inventory unless supplier allocation is tied to a paid VIN schedule.
- CAC: outbound to courier subcontractors, transport associations, leasing brokers, TimoCom/load-board communities, Facebook fleet groups, and tachograph workshops with unused non-selling capacity.
- Payback: one paid fleet of 8 vans can cover initial outreach and coordinator costs.

Copy risk:

High from approved workshops and fleet compliance providers. The mitigation is to control specific dated appointments before a fleet shops around. Workshops can sell direct, but many do not aggregate outbound demand across small cross-border fleets or manage multi-workshop scheduling. The POC must prove slot control, not just lead generation.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

Once a workshop signs a date block and a fleet pays a VIN-specific deposit, that appointment capacity is no longer available to late-copying workshops, consultants, or generic fleet advisors. The control is perishable dated capacity, not knowledge about the law. A competitor can copy the method but cannot copy already-reserved install days or prepaid VIN schedules.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The product sold is a paid, VIN-specific retrofit package backed by workshop capacity and resulting calibration certificates. The founder takes payment, holds slots, coordinates installation, and delivers a fitted vehicle. There is no neutral marketplace and no advisory report as the main artifact.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- Approved tachograph workshops remain the installer/calibrator of record.
- The startup does not certify devices, alter calibration records, provide legal transport advice, or represent that a fleet is exempt or in scope.
- Customer must attest international hire-and-reward use, vehicle MAM, and VIN details.
- Exemption questions go to the operator's transport counsel or competent authority.

Duplicate risk:

Not DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, GreenTender, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, Data Act, or HeritageDoor. It has some similarity to scarce-lot/reservation logic, but the controlled asset is dated approved workshop capacity tied to a statutory vehicle-operation deadline, not historic interior inventory or generic equipment resale.

Strongest anticipated objections:

- This may be a one-deadline services arbitrage, not a durable company.
- Workshops are the real control point and can sell directly.
- The founder may be a broker unless contracts actually reserve capacity and customer deposits are collected.
- Demand may be smaller than panic narratives imply because many vans are exempt, not used for hire/reward, or already newly fitted.
- Scheduling, no-shows, device shortages, and geography can destroy margins.
- Liability must stay clearly with approved workshops and fleet operators.
