Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
MCPToolFence Buyer Action Release Sprint

One-sentence thesis:
A 7-business-day buyer-release sprint for B2B SaaS vendors whose enterprise buyer blocks an AI agent, MCP server, or tool-using AI feature until the vendor produces sandboxed proof of tool permissions, scoped credentials, human approval gates, prompt-injection/tool-poisoning tests, audit logs, and no-go boundaries.

Exact buyer:
20-200 person B2B SaaS vendors, AI workflow vendors, devtool vendors, support-automation vendors, internal-knowledge vendors, sales-ops vendors, and AI implementation agencies whose product includes a non-regulated AI agent or MCP/tool integration that can call APIs, query systems, create tickets, update records, read files, write to repositories, trigger workflows, or interact with customer data. The practical channel is appsec boutiques, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, AI implementation partners, and security-questionnaire advisers that already serve these vendors.

Acute trigger:
An enterprise buyer, security team, procurement team, data-protection team, or implementation owner sends a written blocker before go-live, renewal, pilot expansion, production access, procurement approval, or payment. The blocker asks how the AI agent or MCP/tool integration is prevented from taking unsafe actions, overusing tools, leaking data across tenants/sources, following indirect prompt injection, using overbroad credentials, bypassing human approval, hiding tool calls, or acting outside the agreed deployment scope.

Control point:
Signed live buyer-action release ticket with client authorization, exact buyer condition text, deal/go-live/payment consequence, non-high-risk product scope, authorized sandbox or demo tenant, synthetic data where needed, MCP server or tool catalog, API/action list, credential and permission map, allowlist/denylist rules, human approval gates, logging/audit access, prompt-injection and tool-poisoning test plan, no-go/escalation rules, and partner-branded buyer evidence packet. The startup controls the scoped test plan, sandbox traces, permission evidence, audit-log screenshots/exports, pass/rework/no-go log, and buyer response wording for that procurement event.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three appsec, SOC2, fractional-CISO, or AI implementation partners sign routing terms, or six direct vendors prepay live cases. Six buyer-action release sprints are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 20,000-60,000 PLN each. Every sprint has written buyer condition text, a named deal/go-live/renewal/payment consequence, an authorized sandbox, tool catalog, credential/permission map, and logging access. At least four sprints produce a buyer-progress outcome, narrowed production scope, accepted manual-gate condition, remediation/no-go decision, or clean escalation to formal appsec/legal review. At least two cases get a buyer response to the tool-action evidence bundle. Gross margin stays above 60% after AI security reviewer cost.

6-month POC:
Deliver 25-45 buyer-action release sprints; collect 700,000-1,800,000 PLN in direct and partner-routed fees; document 18+ buyer-progress, lower-risk go-live, narrowed-scope, remediation, or no-go outcomes; get five partners to route at least three cases each; keep average delivery under 7 business days for bounded evidence-ready products; maintain a private trace library by tool type, MCP server pattern, credential scope, human gate, prompt-injection route, tool-poisoning route, audit-log format, buyer objection, accepted evidence format, and no-go trigger.

First acquisition mechanism:
Partner outreach to appsec boutiques, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, AI implementation agencies, security-questionnaire firms, and devtool security advisers: "When a buyer blocks an MCP or tool-using AI deployment because it can take actions, route the live condition to me. I run a bounded sandbox sprint, produce tool-permission traces, audit-log evidence, prompt-injection/tool-poisoning checks, and pass/rework/no-go wording. You keep the broader security, legal, SOC2, and remediation work." Direct outreach targets AI vendors publicly launching agents or MCP integrations and asks only for live buyer blockers, not general audits.

Economics:
Charge 20,000-30,000 PLN for a single bounded tool-action evidence sprint, 35,000-60,000 PLN for multi-tool or urgent go-live/payment blockers, and 8,000-15,000 PLN for a clean no-go or retest after remediation. AI security reviewer cost is 6,000-18,000 PLN per case depending on scope. The buyer pays because a 100,000+ PLN equivalent contract, renewal, production rollout, or implementation payment is already blocked by action-safety concerns that a policy answer cannot resolve.

Copy risk:
AI security vendors, appsec firms, red-team providers, penetration testers, SOC2 consultants, GRC platforms, devsecops tools, and internal security teams can copy pieces. The defense is narrow live buyer-action blockers, fast bounded sandbox recipes for MCP/tool-using AI, partner distribution, accepted/rejected buyer evidence memory, trace library by tool/action pattern, and strict no-go boundaries that keep the product from becoming broad AI security consulting.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the partner or vendor signs the live case, pays or guarantees the sprint, and provides buyer condition text, sandbox access, tool catalog, credential map, logging access, and response deadline, the startup controls the active trace bundle and buyer evidence packet for that procurement event. A broader appsec firm or AI security tool can compete later, but cannot instantly replace the already-authorized sandbox tests, case chronology, buyer wording, and partner-approved packet under the current deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live buyer blockers tied to MCP/tool-using AI action safety for a specific deal, renewal, go-live, implementation, or payment event. The output is a buyer-action release artifact: scoped test plan, tool-permission matrix, sandbox traces, prompt-injection/tool-poisoning probes, audit-log evidence, human-approval evidence, pass/rework/no-go findings, and buyer response wording. It is not a generic AI audit, legal memo, SOC2 project, dashboard, database, marketplace, template pack, scanner, or expert introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification, DPIA, SOC2 certification, ISO 42001 implementation, formal penetration-test certification, model validation, safety certification, production incident response, or guarantees of buyer approval. It does not test live production actions that send external email, move money, delete data, alter customer records, execute code, deploy to production, or use privileged admin credentials unless a qualified appsec firm owns the formal engagement. High-risk AI, regulated finance model-risk, credit, insurance, HR decisioning, biometrics, clinical workflows, law enforcement, autonomous adverse decisions, and sensitive production data without written authorization are declined or routed to qualified firms.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle regulated-finance ICT third-party-risk, exit plans, audit rights, subcontracting, or operational resilience evidence. It is not AIQ Partner Ticket Book because the artifact is sandboxed tool-action traces and permission proof, not AI questionnaire answer packaging. It is not RAGProbe Buyer Security Evidence Sprint because the control point is MCP/tool execution, credential scope, human gates, audit logs, and action no-go boundaries rather than RAG source isolation and prompt-injection evidence generally. It is not VoiceAI Article 50, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, REDBlocked, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
AI security and appsec firms are natural incumbents and can offer broader credibility. Enterprise buyers may still demand formal penetration testing, AI red-team certification, SOC2/ISO evidence, legal review, DPIA, or remediation outside scope. Many cases will reveal missing controls that need engineering work rather than an evidence packet. Short sandbox traces can create false assurance unless scope language is strict. Tool-using agents are risky to test because they can trigger real actions, data changes, external messages, code execution, or API writes if sandboxing is weak. Sensitive prompts, logs, credentials, tool maps, and customer data create trust and security burdens. Partners may internalize the playbook after several cases. GRC and AI security tools may automate common checks over time.
