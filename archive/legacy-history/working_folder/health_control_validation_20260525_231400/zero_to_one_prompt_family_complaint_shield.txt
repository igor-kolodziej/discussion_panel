Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Family Complaint Shield for registered private care homes in Poland.

One-sentence thesis:
Registered private care-home owners with a live family complaint, bad public review, inspection stress, refund threat, or occupancy risk will pay for a mandatory 30-day high-risk event rail so the next family/inspection response is built from current structured evidence instead of WhatsApp, staff memory, and scattered notes.

Exact buyer:
Owners or directors of registered private 20-80 bed care homes in Poland, especially homes visible in voivode registers and showing a current urgency trigger: family complaint, bad Google review, recent incident, staff turnover documentation gap, inspection anxiety, refund demand, threatened resident move-out, occupancy pressure, or owner fear that the next family challenge will be reconstructed from memory.

Acute trigger:
The home has already experienced or is actively facing a family complaint, bad review, complaint escalation, refund threat, inspection stress, or retained-bed/occupancy risk. The buyer does not pay for generic care-home CRM. The buyer pays because one bad event narrative can cost refunds, occupancy, owner time, review reputation, family trust, and inspection stress.

Control point:
A paid, signed mandatory event-routing mandate inside one care home's current workflow for named residents, resident groups, or high-risk event classes for 30 days. During the sprint, all agreed high-risk events must be captured through the Family Complaint Shield workflow first or same-shift: falls, family calls/complaints, medication handoff notes, hydration/meal refusal, pressure-sore checks, missed-care allegations, consent/visit notes, incident follow-up, transfer/hospital handoff, and owner response packets. The care home remains the care provider and data controller. The startup acts as workflow operator/processor and packet assembler.

60-day signed/prepaid proof:
One or two registered private care homes sign a prepaid 30-day crisis sprint at 4,000-8,000 PLN each, or one stronger first home pays 6,000-10,000 PLN where the complaint/occupancy trigger is acute.
A data-processing agreement and secure handling workflow are signed before resident data is handled.
The owner/director names the resident(s), resident group, or event classes that must route through the rail.
Staff capture all agreed high-risk events for 30 days.
At least 30 event entries are captured, or fewer if they are directly tied to a live complaint/escalation.
At least two owner-ready family/inspection response packets are produced.
At least one owner-confirmed retained-bed, reduced-refund, reduced-complaint, avoided escalation, faster family response, or inspection-readiness outcome is documented.

6-month proof:
Five to eight paid crisis sprints.
At least three homes retain monthly at 1,500-3,500 PLN.
300+ high-risk event entries captured across homes.
At least 12 family/inspection/owner response packets produced.
At least five owner-confirmed economic or operational outcomes: retained resident, avoided refund, reduced complaint escalation, improved review response, staff handoff improvement, inspection packet acceptance, or avoided owner time sink.
Reusable event-class templates, packet formats, reviewer QA checklist, and staff compliance metrics across homes.
Gross margin above 60% after nurse/care-quality reviewer, optional legal reviewer, secure storage, and manual QA costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list from voivode registers of registered private care homes in Mazowieckie, Wielkopolskie, Slaskie, Lodzkie, and Malopolskie. Filter for 20-80 bed homes, private ownership, visible Google-review pain, recent negative reviews, public complaints, recruitment churn, weak websites, and owner/director contactability. Outbound directly to owners/directors with a narrow crisis offer: "Your next family complaint should not be reconstructed from WhatsApp and memory. For 30 days, we route the high-risk events you name into owner-ready response packets."

Economics:
Initial 30-day crisis sprint: 4,000-8,000 PLN, or 6,000-10,000 PLN for an acute live complaint with named residents/classes.
Retention: 1,500-3,500 PLN/month for continuing high-risk event routing, family response packets, staff compliance metrics, and inspection-readiness packet updates.
Costs: reviewer QA, secure storage, lightweight capture tools, manual packet assembly, and optional care-law response review. Reviewers handle templates and edge cases; the founder does not pay a nurse or lawyer to review every routine entry.
Payback for the home can come from one retained resident, one avoided refund, one de-escalated family complaint, one improved owner response to a negative review, one smoother inspection packet, or reduced owner/staff time reconstructing events.
Founder cash conversion is prepaid sprint first; no inventory, no receivable funding, no public procurement dependency.

Copy risk:
Care-home CRMs, nurse auditors, care-law consultants, and larger care groups can copy templates or incident workflows. The defense is not templates. The defense is owning the live crisis workflow for named homes during a painful 30-day window, building event-class response patterns, staff-compliance benchmarks, and owner-trusted packet memory across repeated complaints. Once the founder is mandated inside a live complaint sprint, a competitor cannot retroactively capture those same event entries or packet outcomes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before control is secured:
The first sale is not a system replacement. It is a crisis sprint sold directly to a home owner who has immediate complaint/review/refund/inspection pressure. A CRM vendor or consultant can build a similar offer later, but they cannot own the named resident/event-class rail once the owner has mandated it for the current 30-day crisis and the staff is already using it for owner response packets.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, marketplace, or generic consultant:
Family Complaint Shield does not sell care-home CRM, reputation management, legal representation, inspection consulting, or generic documentation templates. It controls live high-risk event routing for named residents/classes during a paid crisis sprint and turns those events into owner-ready response packets. The product is the mandatory current-event rail plus packet memory, not after-the-fact advice.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The care home remains the care provider, employer, medical/care decision-maker, and data controller. The startup is a processor/workflow operator under DPA and does not diagnose, prescribe, create care plans, certify care quality, supervise clinical care, provide legal representation, or make resident-treatment decisions. Medication, pressure-sore, fall, nutrition, hospitalization, and clinical decisions remain with qualified care-home staff and licensed professionals. A contracted nurse/care-quality reviewer may review templates and edge cases, and a care-law reviewer may review response wording when needed, but the startup does not claim medical or legal authority. Resident data is handled only for the contracted home workflow and is not resold or reused as a health dataset.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, PromoLeak, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, parcel claims, heritage-door inventory, or TraceFaktura animal-care invoice routing. The closest analogue is TraceFaktura because both use mandatory live event routing, but this idea has a different buyer, acute trigger, event object, and safety boundary: private care-home owners facing family complaints, refund/review/inspection/occupancy risk, and resident high-risk event documentation rather than municipal animal-care contractor invoice evidence.
