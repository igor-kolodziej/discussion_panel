Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RAGModule Paid Annex Queue Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or operate a current paid queue of white-label RAG/agent security annex modules from a small appsec, SOC2, or fractional-CISO partner that has already sold buyer-facing AI security evidence work but does not want to staff the intermittent specialist delivery.

Exact buyer:
Small appsec boutiques, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, privacy/security questionnaire firms, and AI implementation agencies with 5-50 B2B SaaS clients, at least three recent or current paid RAG/agent security evidence annexes, and no desire to hire a permanent AI-security reviewer.

Acute trigger:
The partner has already promised or sold RAG/agent security annexes inside client security reviews, SOC2-style readiness reports, enterprise buyer responses, or appsec reports, but delivery bottlenecks around prompt-injection traces, source-isolation checks, tool-permission tests, logging/human-review evidence, and buyer-safe wording are delaying reports or consuming senior partner time.

Control point:
Signed option or operating agreement over an existing paid annex queue; partner client-authorized tickets; historic accepted annex examples; buyer blocker text; partner report templates; sandbox/test-environment access; RAG/source sample; tool/action map; logging/human-review evidence; reviewer workflow; outcome reporting right; post-handoff partner endorsement; ticket payments or partner guarantee; seller payout tied to accepted annexes and second-ticket routing.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One signed option or operating agreement with a partner that has delivered or sold at least three RAG/agent security annexes; six paid or partner-guaranteed modules transferred into the startup workflow; 90,000-240,000 PLN in module revenue collected, escrowed, or contractually guaranteed; four partner-accepted annexes delivered after handoff; two buyer-progress, narrowed-follow-up, remediation/no-go, or lower-risk deployment outcomes documented; one partner routes a second post-handoff module; reviewer cost stays below 40% of revenue.

6-month POC:
3-5 partner queue options or operating agreements; 30-60 paid transferred modules; 600,000-1,800,000 PLN revenue; 20+ partner-accepted annexes used in buyer responses or reports; 15+ buyer-progress, narrowed-follow-up, remediation/no-go, or lower-risk deployment outcomes; 5 partners route at least five modules each or two acquired partners renew the operating agreement; gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, secure workflow, and seller earnouts.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target appsec/fCISO/SOC2 micro-firms that publicly mention AI security, RAG, agents, or AI questionnaires but do not employ a dedicated AI-security specialist. Offer to acquire or operate their current AI annex queue on a seller earnout: the partner keeps the client relationship and report ownership; the startup takes delivery, reviewer coordination, trace production, and buyer-safe annex drafting.

Economics:
Wholesale module fee is 12,000-18,000 PLN for narrow RAG source-isolation/prompt-injection annexes, 20,000-35,000 PLN for tool-using agent annexes, and 35,000-60,000 PLN for urgent multi-feature buyer deadlines. Seller/partner keeps 25-40% or receives earnout from post-handoff accepted modules. The end SaaS vendor pays because the annex protects a 100,000+ PLN equivalent deal, renewal, pilot expansion, or procurement approval.

Copy risk:
Appsec firms, AI security firms, red-team providers, GRC platforms, and partners themselves can copy. The defense is not a generic playbook; it is acquiring or operating the existing paid queue, accepted annex examples, partner report format, outcome reporting, buyer blocker patterns, and payment rights before the partner hires or internalizes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the general method, but not an already-optioned partner queue, transferred paid module, partner-authorized sandbox, accepted-annex history, outcome-reporting right, and seller earnout/payment trail for those specific buyer deadlines. A larger appsec firm would need to displace the partner-approved operating agreement while the partner has already transferred tickets and report workflow to the startup.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a transferred paid module inside a partner's existing report workflow, delivered with scoped test plan, prompt-injection traces, source-isolation checks, tool-permission tests, logging/human-review evidence, rework/no-go table, buyer-safe annex wording, partner acceptance, and outcome record. It is not generic AI consulting, a marketplace, appsec brokerage, software, questionnaire labor, or a standalone report sold cold to SaaS vendors.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No legal advice, AI Act classification, DPIA, formal penetration-test certification, SOC2 audit opinion, ISO 42001 implementation, model validation, production incident response, or product remediation. Test only authorized sandbox/demo environments with no destructive tools, money movement, external email, privileged admin actions, or live sensitive data unless the partner has a proper lawful testing structure. Decline regulated high-risk AI, autonomous adverse decisions, clinical, HR, credit, insurance, biometric, law-enforcement, and regulated-finance model-risk cases. Partner and SaaS client remain responsible for product changes, security decisions, legal compliance, and final buyer communication.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not RAGProbe direct because it does not cold-sell standalone buyer sprints. Not RAGModule AppSec Overflow Bench because it requires an optioned existing paid queue, accepted annex history, outcome reporting rights, and seller payout tied to transferred modules, not only new partner routing. Not AIQOverflow because the artifact is test-trace annexes for RAG/agent security, not questionnaire answer packaging. Not DORA, REDBlocked, AI Act transparency, VoiceAI Article 50, CMMC, NIS2, or generic cyber evidence packs.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Partners with real paid AI annex demand may keep the work, hire, or internalize after seeing the playbook. The exact seller profile may be rare. Enterprise buyers may require formal penetration testing, AI red-team certification, ISO 42001, SOC2 audit evidence, legal AI Act classification, or full remediation. Sensitive prompts, corpora, logs, tool maps, and source documents create trust and security burdens. Short annex modules can create false assurance if scope wording is loose. Reviewer costs can compress margin. GRC and AI-security tooling may automate common tests. Partner outcome reporting may be incomplete because the partner owns the client relationship.
