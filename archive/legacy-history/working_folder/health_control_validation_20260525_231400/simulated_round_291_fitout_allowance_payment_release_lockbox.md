## Search Frame

Recent rounds showed that ordinary AR, deposits, credits, rebates, platform claims, physical lots, and evidence desks fail unless the founder controls current cash or a specific transaction before incumbents can absorb the workflow. This round tests current cash owed under commercial leases: landlord-funded tenant fit-out allowances and reimbursement contributions where a tenant has already completed or accepted works and the remaining blocker is a documented payment-release file, not lease negotiation or legal entitlement creation.

The candidate is deliberately narrower than lease auditing or construction consulting. It accepts only named, high-value allowance files where the lease/side letter defines the reimbursement, the works are already performed or accepted, the tenant has invoices/proof, and the landlord/property manager has acknowledged a document or process path for payment.

## Simulated Pass Candidate

### Fit-Out Allowance Payment Release Lockbox - 88.4

One-sentence thesis: a signed-mandate payment-release desk for commercial tenants whose landlord-funded fit-out allowance, opening contribution, or relocation incentive is already earned or acknowledged but unpaid because the landlord/property manager requires a clean closeout and reimbursement packet.

Buyer: multi-site retail chains, franchisees, gyms, clinics that are not health-data providers in this workflow, restaurants, showrooms, service tenants, and office tenants with named lease incentives above 100,000 PLN equivalent. The economic buyer is the CFO, founder, franchise owner, expansion lead, or outsourced finance lead that needs landlord cash after opening or fit-out completion.

Acute trigger: the tenant has opened, moved in, or completed landlord-approved works, but the landlord contribution is still unpaid because invoices, payment proof, completion certificates, photos, as-built documents, contractor statements, insurance/permit closeout, VAT data, landlord forms, or board/property-manager approvals are incomplete or not in the required format.

Transferable control point: signed payment-release mandate plus lease/side-letter allowance clause, landlord/property-manager correspondence, invoice and proof-of-payment file, completion/handover evidence, site photo set, defect/punch-list status, responsible tenant contact, landlord packet checklist, submitted reimbursement packet, payment tracker, and success-fee right. The startup controls the file assembly and release path for that named allowance.

Exactly what is signed or prepaid inside 60 days:

- 3 signed mandates for named allowance files above 100,000 PLN each.
- At least 1 landlord/property-manager written acknowledgement of required packet fields, accepted packet submission, scheduled payment, approved reimbursement, credit note, or paid amount.
- 1 first released payment, landlord credit, or accepted payment schedule.
- 1 success-fee invoice or fixed release fee collected.
- Reusable packet matrix across lease clause, invoice evidence, completion proof, landlord forms, rejection reasons, accepted packet wording, and payment timing.

First acquisition mechanism: outbound through tenant reps, fit-out project managers, franchise accountants, retail expansion consultants, outsourced CFOs, and commercial brokers. The ask is not "do you want a lease audit"; it is "send only live, named landlord allowance files where cash is due or acknowledged and your payment is stuck on packet closeout."

Gross margin and payback logic: charge 5,000-12,000 PLN for intake/no-go and 5-12% of released cash, capped per file, or 8,000-25,000 PLN fixed for high-value non-disputed files. A single 150,000-500,000 PLN allowance can justify the fee. The first proof is cash movement, not a report.

Copy risk: tenant reps, lease auditors, lawyers, construction PMs, accountants, outsourced CFOs, and internal expansion teams can copy the workflow. The defense is strict live-file qualification, fast packet assembly, repeat landlord/property-manager packet memory, and partner channels that see stuck files but do not want contingency follow-up.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: once the startup has the signed mandate, exact lease file, landlord thread, invoice proof, closeout packet, submitted reimbursement request, and success-fee right, a competitor cannot work that exact allowance without the tenant switching mid-release. The case memory compounds by landlord, property manager, document checklist, rejection reason, and payment path.

Why this is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: the output is not a lease audit memo or dashboard. The proof is a landlord-accepted reimbursement packet, payment schedule, credit note, released cash, or no-go decision for a named allowance. It is a payment-release lockbox for current cash, not consulting or introductions.

Boundaries and exclusions:

- No lease negotiation, legal entitlement creation, rent dispute, construction claim, litigation, or collection pressure.
- No files with active defects, unresolved completion, disputed works, landlord counterclaims, insolvency, unclear authority, missing lease clause, or unclear tenant entity.
- Counsel, tenant reps, project managers, engineers, and tax advisers remain responsible for legal, technical, construction, tax, and lease interpretations.
- The startup only assembles and tracks the payment-release packet through the tenant's authorized channel.

Duplicate-risk distinction: this is not PromoLeak, commercial lease deposit release, construction retention release, acknowledged SaaS true-up, public guarantee release, or generic lease auditing. The distinct control object is a landlord-funded tenant improvement/opening allowance with a live lease-side payment path, high average line value, and landlord packet acceptance proof.

Reasons this could still fail:

- Tenants may already rely on tenant reps, brokers, lawyers, PMs, or accountants.
- Many apparent allowances are not actually due because conditions precedent, defects, landlord approvals, permits, rent arrears, or invoice rules are unsatisfied.
- Landlords may ignore third-party coordinators and require official tenant communication.
- Success-fee attribution can be disputed if payment was already scheduled.
- Case volume depends on partner channels because public signals for stuck allowance files are weak.
- The product can drift into lease auditing, construction claims, rent disputes, or legal work unless no-go rules are enforced.
