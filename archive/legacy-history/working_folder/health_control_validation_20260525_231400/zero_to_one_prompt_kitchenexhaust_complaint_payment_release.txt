Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
KitchenExhaust Complaint Payment Release

One-sentence thesis:
Resolve or cleanly no-go commercial kitchen exhaust odor, smoke, grease, noise, and rooftop-fan complaint blockers that hold up restaurant openings, landlord acceptance, fit-out final payment, rent continuity, lease negotiations, or contractor payment, using a paid location-specific evidence and response packet routed through qualified HVAC, acoustic, sanitary, or legal reviewers when needed.

Exact buyer:
Polish restaurant operators, food-court tenants, dark-kitchen operators, landlords, shopping-center managers, restaurant fit-out firms, commercial kitchen installers, ventilation/HVAC contractors, property managers, hotel/office landlords with food tenants, and franchise operators with a named location where kitchen exhaust odor, smoke, grease deposits, fan noise, vibration, rooftop discharge, duct routing, filtration, or neighbor/building-community complaints are blocking opening, final acceptance, rent, lease continuity, remedial payment, or contractor invoice release.

Acute trigger:
A restaurant or food tenant cannot open, cannot pass landlord handover, has a fit-out payment withheld, faces rent or lease pressure, receives repeated neighbor/building complaints, gets a landlord or property-manager defect notice, or has a contractor final invoice blocked because someone claims the kitchen exhaust system creates odor, smoke, grease fallout, noise, vibration, unsafe discharge, poor filtration, or nuisance to residents/offices/other tenants.

Control point:
Signed location-release mandate over a named restaurant/food tenant/project; written complaint, defect notice, landlord email, fit-out punch item, withheld-payment note, lease/rent issue, or contractor final-payment blocker; kitchen equipment list; hood/filter/fan/duct/rooftop-discharge documents; photos/videos; operating schedule; maintenance and cleaning records; site access; qualified HVAC/ventilation, acoustic, sanitary, or legal reviewer route where needed; issue map; remedial-scope split; response packet; landlord/contractor/property-manager/authority/community progress outcome or written no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three signed channel partners such as kitchen installers, ventilation contractors, property managers, landlords, fit-out firms, or commercial kitchen service companies; six prepaid location cases; four accepted cases with written complaint/payment blocker and complete source files; two landlord, contractor, property-manager, authority, insurer, or building-community progress outcomes, final-payment releases, opening/acceptance steps, remedial-scope approvals, rent/lease stabilization events, or paid no-go decisions; 50,000-140,000 PLN startup fees collected or invoiced.

6-month POC:
35-75 prepaid location cases; 20-45 accepted evidence/response packets; 12-30 progress outcomes, no-go decisions, remedial-scope approvals, opening/acceptance steps, final-payment releases, rent/lease stabilization events, or contractor payment releases; 350,000-900,000 PLN startup revenue; five repeat channels each sending three or more cases; gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, site visits, document handling, channel commissions, and limited legal/sanitary boundary review.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with commercial kitchen installers, ventilation/HVAC contractors, restaurant fit-out firms, shopping-center property managers, and landlords that see complaint or final-payment blockers but do not want to coordinate evidence, reviewer routing, response wording, and no-go discipline. Ask only for named location cases with written complaint or withheld-payment/opening/acceptance trigger. Avoid cold-selling generic restaurant compliance.

Economics:
Charge 6,000-12,000 PLN for a remote evidence triage and response outline, 12,000-25,000 PLN for a location packet with reviewer routing and landlord/contractor response, and 25,000-45,000 PLN for urgent opening/final-payment cases with site visit coordination and multiple reviewers. Optional success fee of 1-3% of released fit-out final payment, contractor payment, documented rent/lease value protected for 60 days, or approved remedial order, only when the event is documented.

Copy risk:
HVAC engineers, ventilation contractors, acoustic consultants, sanitary consultants, restaurant fit-out firms, property managers, landlords, lawyers, and facility consultants can copy pieces. The defense is a narrow commercial release workflow, repeated complaint/blocker memory, channel routing from installers and landlords, fast issue-split packets, accepted response formats, reviewer bench, and no-go discipline before the case becomes a legal or engineering swamp.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the general workflow, but not an already-signed mandate over a named location, the timestamped complaint/payment/opening blocker, the gathered kitchen exhaust source file, the reviewer route, the landlord/contractor/property-manager response trail, and the success-fee right attached to that specific final-payment, opening, acceptance, rent, lease, remedial-order, or no-go event. The wedge depends on capturing urgent cases through installers, ventilation contractors, landlords, and fit-out firms before the dispute is handed directly to a lawyer or full engineering consultant.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a named location complaint/payment release file converted into landlord acceptance progress, contractor final-payment release, opening/fit-out acceptance step, remedial-scope approval, rent/lease stabilization, authority/insurer response, building-community progress outcome, or written no-go. It is not generic HVAC design, restaurant consulting, acoustic certification, legal defense, sanitation consulting, software, dashboards, databases, marketplace matching, or contractor brokerage.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, nuisance-law opinions, permit advice, sanitary certification, fire-safety engineering, acoustic certification, HVAC design, air-quality certification, official measurements, equipment installation, maintenance service, or remediation guarantees. Qualified HVAC/acoustic/sanitary/legal reviewers and contractors handle professional decisions. Reject cases involving fire-safety hazards, carbon monoxide risk, gas safety, illegal construction, missing permits, mold/health claims, active litigation, harassment, eviction, criminal complaints, dangerous exhaust discharge, full redesign needs, or customers seeking to suppress legitimate neighbor complaints rather than fix or no-go the issue.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not HeatQuiet, because this is commercial kitchen exhaust odor/smoke/grease/noise release for restaurants, landlords, kitchen installers, ventilation contractors, and fit-out payment blockers, not residential heat-pump outdoor-unit acoustic siting or neighbor heat-pump complaint evidence. Not BatteryFit, PV/heat-pump commissioning, GreenTender, DORA, REDBlocked, Data Act, CBAM, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, HeritageDoor, BioSignal, or Supplement Stack Safety.

Strongest anticipated objections:
HVAC and acoustic specialists may already handle serious cases. Landlords, neighbors, property managers, or authorities may not accept a packet if the exhaust system is genuinely wrong. Many cases require actual remediation, duct redesign, filtration upgrade, fan replacement, cleaning, fire-safety correction, legal negotiation, or official measurement. Unknown-founder trust is weak for conflict-heavy tenant/landlord cases. Restaurant owners may be price-sensitive unless opening or payment is clearly blocked. The business can drift into legal nuisance defense or engineering project management. Channel partners may copy the intake and issue map after a few cases. Complaint outcomes can be emotional, political, and outside founder control.
