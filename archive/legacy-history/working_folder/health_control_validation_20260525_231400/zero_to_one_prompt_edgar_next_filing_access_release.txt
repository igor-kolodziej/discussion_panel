Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EDGAR Next Filing-Access Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
For small SEC filers, foreign private issuers, insiders, funds, and filing agents whose real EDGAR submission is blocked by EDGAR Next account administration, Login.gov, delegation, CCC/passphrase, Form ID, or filing-agent authority issues, sell a prepaid access-release workroom that gets the filer or its filing agent to a documented access/delegation outcome and the first accepted EDGAR filing or a clean no-go.

Exact buyer:
Small public companies, foreign private issuers, Section 16 insiders, Rule 144 sellers, Form D fund issuers, small funds/advisers, and EDGAR filing agents or securities-law firms with clients whose EDGAR filing workflow is blocked by post-EDGAR Next access/account-management issues. The strongest buyer is a filing agent, IR firm, law-firm support team, or company secretary that already has client trust and needs overflow resolution for named filings.

Acute trigger:
A named filing is due or imminent, but the normal filer or filing agent cannot submit because the filer is not properly enrolled in EDGAR Next, missed the old streamlined enrollment window, lacks usable account administrators, has unresolved Login.gov identity/access problems, has not delegated authority to the filing agent, has a broken CCC/passphrase/Form ID path, or cannot prove that the intended filing user/delegated entity can submit for the CIK. The trigger must be a specific filing or access blocker, not general compliance cleanup.

Control point:
A signed limited access-resolution mandate tied to a named CIK and named filing, plus the filer/filing-agent authorization needed to inspect EDGAR dashboard status, account administrators, delegated entities, Login.gov/account-admin setup, CCC/passphrase/Form ID status, and filing-agent authority. The controlled artifact is the active access-resolution workroom and evidence chain, not disclosure content.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five prepaid cases with named CIKs and named filings; written mandate from the filer, insider, fund, law firm, or filing agent; dashboard/account-admin/delegation evidence collected; at least three documented access/delegation/Form ID/passphrase/admin outcomes; at least two first accepted EDGAR accessions or a documented clean no-go caused by legal/filer-status facts outside scope; 70,000-150,000 PLN collected.

6-month POC:
Acquire 12-18 prepaid access-release cases through filing agents, IR firms, issuer secretaries, broker affiliate-sale desks, and small securities-law support teams. Complete at least eight objective outcomes: accepted EDGAR filing after access repair, accepted delegation to filing agent, restored account-administrator path, accepted Form ID or amended Form ID path, passphrase/CCC reset outcome, or clean no-go. Convert at least two channel partners into repeat referrers. Reject disclosure drafting, securities-law analysis, XBRL/tagging, exchange compliance disputes, issuer delinquency strategy, sanctions/fraud concerns, and cases without a named filing deadline.

First acquisition mechanism:
Direct outreach to small EDGAR filing agents, investor-relations firms, securities-law paralegal teams, company-secretarial providers, brokers handling affiliate-sale paperwork, and CEE/European issuers with SEC reporting footprints. The first paid hook is not "EDGAR consulting"; it is "we take only named filing-access blockers where your existing counsel or filing agent stays in charge of the filing content and we produce a documented access/delegation outcome before the filing date."

Economics:
Prepaid fixed fee of 15,000-35,000 PLN per access-release case, with optional 5,000-15,000 PLN success add-on for accepted filing/accession or filing-agent delegation accepted before deadline. Contractor help is limited to SEC filing operations specialists and paralegal-style account-administration checklists. Gross margin target is 70%+ because the product is evidence collection, account-admin coordination, status chasing, and access-path resolution, not legal drafting. Payback is immediate on prepaid cases; owner earnings depend on maintaining strict rejection rules and channel-led intake.

Copy risk:
High from EDGAR filing agents, securities lawyers, corporate-secretarial providers, issuer-services vendors, and paralegal contractors. The defense is narrow case selection, repeat overflow from agents that do not want to waste senior staff on account-admin chaos, and a case memory library of EDGAR Next access failure modes, evidence checklists, delegation paths, and clean-no-go scripts.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the service category, but not a signed mandate for a specific CIK, named filing deadline, current EDGAR dashboard state, delegated-entity request, account-admin succession problem, passphrase/CCC/Form ID path, and prepaid access-resolution workroom already in motion. The wedge is time compression: once the founder has the live blocker, access artifacts, named filing, and prepaid mandate, the value is resolving that specific access state quickly rather than owning a generic EDGAR advisory market.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It is a prepaid case-control desk for a binary filing-access outcome. The deliverable is not a memo or dashboard; it is documented EDGAR account access repaired, delegated filing authority accepted, Form ID/passphrase/CCC path resolved, first accepted filing/accession, or clean no-go. It does not introduce clients to lawyers or filing agents; the existing counsel/filing agent remains responsible for filing content and legal obligations.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not give securities-law advice, decide what must be filed, draft disclosure content, certify issuer status, act as securities counsel, act as broker, make legal representations to the SEC, or submit filings unless an authorized filing agent and the filer explicitly make it an administrative user within their normal process. It rejects disclosure drafting, lateness strategy, exchange-listing issues, SEC enforcement/sanctions/fraud matters, XBRL/tagging, issuer-status disputes, and any case where the only value is legal interpretation.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet. The control point is SEC EDGAR account/delegation access for a named filing, not regulated-finance vendor evidence, Amazon product evidence, investor health-claim diligence, scarce physical lots, municipal invoice routing, retailer deductions, import-authorisation continuity, machine-data exit rights, tender packs, supplement-safety claims, battery matching, or heat-pump noise.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Securities lawyers and EDGAR filing agents already own trust and may handle this internally. Unknown Warsaw founder credibility is weak for SEC credentials and issuer access. The EDGAR Next transition may be a temporary spike rather than a durable business. Many official resources are free and filing agents know the process. Legal boundaries are easy to cross under time pressure. The urgent cases may be too few, too fragmented, or too risky. A blocked filing may be caused by delinquency, missing legal authority, bad issuer status, or filer inaction rather than access mechanics. Buyers may only pay if the founder already has EDGAR operations references.
