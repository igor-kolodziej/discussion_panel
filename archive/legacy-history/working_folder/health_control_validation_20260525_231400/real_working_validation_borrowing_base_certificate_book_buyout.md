## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Clean sellers may be rare: the seller must be small enough to accept an earnout but organized enough to transfer templates, workpapers, calendars, credentials, client history, and accepted reports.
2. Sellers may keep clean profitable clients and offer only messy, low-margin, emergency-heavy accounts.
3. Customers may use the handoff to move reporting in-house, to their accountant, to a fractional CFO, or to a lender/factor-recommended provider.
4. Borrowing-base certificates are borrower-certified submissions; borrower remains responsible for truth, eligibility, valuation, receivable validity, collateral title, and covenant compliance.
5. Some clients may be distressed borrowers with bad debt, fraud concerns, covenant issues, lender disputes, ineligible collateral, or broken books.
6. Seller judgment may be the real asset if lender, borrower, exception, and covenant handling is undocumented.
7. Reporting deadlines can cluster around month-end or week-end, straining a part-time founder unless contractors and reviewer coverage are in place.
8. ABL/factor formats vary by lender, reserve logic, ineligibles, debtor concentration, inventory eligibility, and cash application.
9. Gross margin can collapse if every client needs ERP cleanup, cash application, credit memo review, collections support, or emergency lender back-and-forth.
10. Seller earnout may misalign incentives unless payout depends on retained cash, accepted reports, and no material undisclosed defects.
