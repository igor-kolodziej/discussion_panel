## Simulated Pass Candidate

Idea name: TachoArchive Download-Compliance Book Buyout

Simulated score: 88.5 / 100

One-sentence thesis: Acquire or option tiny retiring transport-admin and telematics-installer books that already perform recurring tachograph and driver-card download/archive/reminder work for small EU/CEE cross-border carriers, but close only after customers approve payment transfer and current-month cash is collected.

Buyer: Economic customers are small Polish/CEE cross-border carriers with 3-50 vehicles or driver cards. Acquisition sellers are retiring or overloaded transport accountants, tachograph-data clerks, telematics installers, and fleet-admin micro-providers with active recurring download/archive clients.

Acute trigger: Carriers cannot treat tachograph data as a one-off installation problem. Driver cards, vehicle units, remote-download devices, archive retention, reminder calendars, infringement exports, and inspection-ready DDD file custody repeat monthly or quarterly. The 1 July 2026 LCV tachograph expansion adds a fresh wave of small fleets that need ongoing admin after installation, but the business is not an install-slot arbitrage.

Control point: Seller option plus customer-approved transfer of the live recurring book: vehicle/driver-card register, download calendar, DDD file archive, remote-download portal/admin credentials where lawful, SIM/device inventory if any, reminder templates, customer contracts, support inbox/phone/domain, pricing, issue history, and direct billing/payment direction to the new entity.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof: One seller option; at least 20 customer approvals or 80 vehicle/driver-card units transferred; 30,000 PLN current-month or quarterly renewal cash collected before closing; successful archive custody test for a sample of DDD files; credential rotation and access log; seller endorsement message; no regulatory representation, legal advice, workshop calibration, or tachograph installation work.

6-month POC: Control 2-3 micro-books totaling 200-400 active vehicle/driver-card units; collect 120,000-300,000 PLN gross revenue; keep gross margin above 60%; show less than 20 minutes founder/admin time per vehicle per month after transition; prove two sellers sourced through the same outreach path; prove churn below 15% after customer reconsent.

First acquisition mechanism: Build a list of 150 retiring, small, or overloaded transport accountants, tachograph-data clerks, telematics installers, fleet-admin freelancers, and small software resellers. Ask for a no-cash-upfront option: the founder contacts customers with seller endorsement, only transferred/prepaid customers count toward closing value, and the seller receives a fixed multiple or earnout on retained monthly gross margin.

Economics: Charge 69-149 PLN per vehicle or active driver-card unit per month depending on remote-download complexity, archive volume, and reminder/report scope. A 150-unit book at 99 PLN/month is about 14,850 PLN monthly gross revenue. With portal/software and contractor admin costs below 35-40%, a no-cash-upfront option can pay back seller consideration from retained recurring cash rather than speculative acquisition capital.

Copy risk: Telematics platforms, tachograph workshops, transport accountants, and fleet-compliance firms can offer similar work. The defense is not generic knowledge. It is acquisition of the exact small book, seller endorsement, customer reconsent, historical archive custody, payment direction, and recurring reminder/download calendar before competitors know those accounts are movable.

Why it is not a service/report/app/dashboard/marketplace/broker: The startup does not sell a new dashboard or broker leads. It acquires customer-approved recurring admin/payment books and proves current cash before paying for them.
