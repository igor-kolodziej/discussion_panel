## Zero To One Summary

The fresh evaluator scored the idea `73/100`. It accepted the underlying pain: Google OAuth verification, sensitive/restricted scopes, security-assessment requirements, and Workspace admin controls can block enterprise deployments, renewals, and rollouts.

The score fell because the evaluator viewed the idea as productized expert services around a platform bottleneck the startup does not control. It said the best version is a revenue-blocker release desk for vendors whose enterprise customer is stuck, but the weak version becomes a small consulting shop doing consent-screen cleanup, privacy-policy edits, demo videos, and generic appsec coordination.

## Strongest Objections

1. The business is still a service business despite the "operating queue" framing.
2. Google, assessors, Workspace admins, and customer security teams control final approval or acceptance.
3. Hard cases may not be paperwork problems; they may require product/security remediation, scope reduction, data-handling changes, or architecture changes.
4. Unknown-founder trust is severe because buyers must expose OAuth projects, scopes, customer names, rejection threads, security evidence, architecture, and deployment details.
5. Incumbents can copy the workflow: appsec firms, SOC2 advisers, Workspace MSPs, integration agencies, Google-designated assessors, and customer-trust consultants.
6. The 6-month volume target is too aggressive for a part-time founder when restricted-scope cases can involve assessor coordination, remediation, retesting, and review delays.
7. Restricted-scope pricing can compress because buyers may compare the desk fee to visible assessor package prices.
8. Case memory is useful but may be less reusable than expected because privacy, NDAs, customer-specific architecture, and customer security requirements limit reuse.
9. Workspace admin consent may be a separate customer-side problem where the SaaS vendor has limited leverage.
10. Google timelines can break the urgent-release promise.

## Useful Narrowing

The fresh evaluator's best beachhead is B2B SaaS vendors with 100k-2m EUR ARR, enterprise customers, and necessary Gmail/Drive/Calendar/Docs access where a named deployment or renewal is blocked by a Google reviewer rejection, Workspace admin block, or restricted-scope/security-assessment request.

Suggested first packages:

- Google reviewer-response rescue: 12,000-25,000 PLN.
- Workspace admin go-live packet: 15,000-30,000 PLN.
- Restricted-scope assessor handoff: 35,000-60,000 PLN plus pass-through assessor fees.

It recommended a paid 3,000-5,000 PLN diagnostic ending in accepted case or clean no-go, and fixed-fee pricing rather than success fees.

## Lesson

Working validation liked the platform gate enough to pass at 86, but fresh validation rejected the durability and founder-trust assumptions. Do not keep iterating technical platform-access release desks unless the next control point changes from a scoped expert workflow into a harder payment stream, acquired account/book, exclusive channel, or asset the founder actually controls beyond one live case.
