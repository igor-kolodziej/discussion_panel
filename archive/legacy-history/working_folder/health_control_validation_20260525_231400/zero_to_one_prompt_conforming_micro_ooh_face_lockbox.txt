Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Conforming Micro-OOH Face Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Small brands, franchises, clinics, schools, events, and local OOH agencies will prepay for exact legal street-level advertising faces in constrained Polish city nodes when the founder controls the landlord/permit/use right before campaign capex.

Exact buyer:
Local and regional OOH agencies, franchise marketing managers, private clinics, schools, gyms, local service chains, storage operators, event promoters, property developers, car dealers, and B2B businesses that want a specific street, commuter, school-run, retail-strip, clinic-cluster, industrial-zone, or neighborhood sightline and cannot buy that exact legal micro-location from the major outdoor networks.

Acute trigger:
A campaign, opening, school intake, clinic launch, franchise rollout, event, property sale, seasonal promotion, competitor response, or local hiring push needs visible presence at one exact neighborhood node within days or weeks, but legal outdoor inventory at that node is scarce, informal, fragmented, or controlled by property owners rather than listed in standard OOH packages.

Control point:
A short exclusive option, lease, assignment, or campaign-use agreement over one exact wall, window, fence, pylon, facade, storefront, ground sign, or small-format outdoor face, with owner or property-manager consent, documented authority to use the site for advertising, site dimensions/photos, installation/removal terms, electricity/lighting terms if relevant, and legal/use evidence showing the face is already permitted, on-premise, conforming, or otherwise usable without speculative new-permit risk.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Ten signed site options or campaign-use agreements over specific legal micro-OOH faces; three prepaid buyer insertion orders or deposits tied to named faces, dates, creative size, and installation/removal terms; one posted campaign or paid no-go after legal/site review; and no founder-funded print, install, lighting, or buildout spend before a buyer deposit covers the exposure.

6-month POC:
Source 30-60 existing small legal ad faces from retiring micro-OOH operators, landlords, property managers, local retail strips, clinics, gyms, schools, car washes, storage sites, industrial fences, and service buildings. Sign 10-20 exclusive options or short campaign-use leases only where owner authority and legal/use evidence are documented. Sell exact faces to agencies and local advertisers with photos, dimensions, location context, dates, install path, and no-guarantee legal boundaries. Complete 8-15 paid campaigns, collect 80,000-250,000 PLN revenue, keep 40,000-125,000 PLN gross margin, and prove at least five repeat site renewals or repeat buyers.

First acquisition mechanism:
Walk, call, and map owner-controlled existing ad faces in Warsaw and nearby Polish city nodes: small legal wall signs, shop-window faces, fenced lots, local pylons, facade panels, property-manager signage, and neglected static boards. Approach owners with a simple offer: the founder sells prepaid campaigns for that exact face, handles advertiser coordination and removal, and pays a fixed monthly fee or revenue share only when the site is used. In parallel, pitch local agencies and franchise marketers a short list of exact controlled faces, not a generic inventory marketplace.

Economics:
No speculative digital-screen capex. Start with static or small-format faces. Pay the site owner 25-45% of campaign revenue or a fixed 500-3,000 PLN monthly site fee only for active campaigns. Charge buyers 2,000-12,000 PLN per month per face depending on location, size, visibility, exclusivity, and production needs, with printing/install/removal billed separately or funded from the buyer deposit. Target 45-65% gross margin after owner share, print/install coordination, removal, site checks, legal review, and sales commissions.

Copy risk:
Major OOH networks, local billboard owners, media agencies, landlords, printers, and property managers can copy the general playbook. The defense is exact-site control: signed owner consent, campaign-use option, legal/use evidence, buyer deposit, and live insertion order on a specific face before a competitor can sell that location.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Incumbents can offer other outdoor inventory, but they cannot sell the exact wall, window, fence, facade, pylon, or local sightline once the founder has a signed site option or lease, documented owner authority, legal/use file, and prepaid buyer campaign right for that face. The startup does not own all OOH demand; it controls individual legal faces before a campaign moves.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell media planning advice, a listing database, software, agency strategy, introductions, or speculative ad placements. It controls specific legal micro-OOH faces under written site rights and sells prepaid campaign use of those faces. If the founder does not control the face before sale, the case is rejected.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No illegal signs, traffic-safety conflicts, heritage/building disputes, road-lane obstruction, speculative new-permit development, political ads, alcohol/tobacco/gambling-sensitive campaigns without separate legal review, or claims that a campaign is municipally approved unless documented. The owner or property manager must have authority to grant the site right. Local sign, building, road, and landscape-rule questions are reviewed by a qualified local adviser where needed. The startup coordinates site use and campaign handoff, not legal opinions, urban-planning advice, or media performance guarantees.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack Safety, or HeatQuiet. It is closest in control logic to a scarce asset option, but the asset, buyer trigger, and proof are different: exact legal outdoor advertising faces and prepaid insertion orders, not heritage interiors, regulatory evidence, import continuity, platform recovery, or municipal payment routing.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many outdoor advertising faces may be illegal, nonconforming, nontransferable, politically sensitive, or exposed to city enforcement. Landlords may offer vague consent but not a real campaign-use right. Major OOH networks, local billboard owners, media agencies, and property managers can copy future sites. Buyers may not trust audience claims without measurement. Local advertisers may be low-ticket and churny. Installation, removal, damage, insurance, electricity, weather, vandalism, and creative delays can destroy margin. The model may become fragmented local ad sales rather than a repeatable company. Part-time execution may be hard if campaigns need fast creative, install, removal, and site checks.
