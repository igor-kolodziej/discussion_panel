Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Atlassian DC App Tail Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Option and acquire the support, source, customer-continuity, and payment tail of small Atlassian Data Center Marketplace apps only after existing enterprise customers prepay continuity, because Atlassian's Data Center wind-down leaves installed app dependencies that are too small for major vendors but too risky for customers to let die before migration or retirement.

Exact buyer:
The first payor is an existing Atlassian Data Center customer that depends on one narrow Marketplace app for Jira, Confluence, Bitbucket, Bamboo, or Jira Service Management and cannot immediately move to Cloud because of data residency, compliance, custom workflows, app-data migration, missing cloud parity, migration timing, regulated approval, or operational dependency. The acquisition seller is a small Marketplace partner, micro-ISV, or app vendor that has an installed Data Center customer tail but is deprioritising Data Center after the official wind-down, focusing on Cloud, lacking support capacity, or willing to carve out the Data Center tail on earnout. Exclude apps involving payments, secrets management, privileged security administration, regulated clinical/financial decisioning, copied IP, unclear customer data rights, unresolved security incidents, high-severity unpatched vulnerabilities, fake marketplace history, or heavy bespoke professional services.

Acute trigger:
The customer has a named Data Center instance, installed app dependency, renewal or expansion decision, migration project, audit/security review, internal continuity request, or cloud-readiness gap. The app vendor has reduced Data Center support, no credible cloud migration path, no new customer growth after the Data Center sales wind-down, an app-data export problem, a compatibility/security patch backlog, or customers asking whether support will survive through 2028-2029. The customer does not want a broad Atlassian migration consultant; it wants the exact app it already uses to remain supportable long enough to migrate, export, replace, or retire safely.

Control point:
The startup controls a named app-tail file before acquisition close: signed seller option over the Data Center app tail; source repository and build pipeline review; IP and contractor-rights evidence; app listing/vendor-rights transfer route or support-rights carveout where Marketplace transfer is not possible; license and renewal history; customer install/version map; support inbox and issue taxonomy; security/advisory history; DPA and data-handling terms; seller-endorsed customer continuity notice; customer-approved payee/support-owner transfer; prepaid continuity contracts; first patch, compatibility test, or export-support task under founder control; and seller earnout tied to retained customer cash rather than upfront purchase price.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign one seller option over a named Atlassian Data Center app tail with at least 20 identifiable active Data Center customers and 12-month license/support history. Obtain read-only due diligence over source, build path, support history, customer list, license history, and security history. Get at least 5 named customers to sign 12-month continuity contracts or escrowed purchase orders at 10,000-40,000 PLN each before acquisition close, with customer-approved payee/support transfer and written support boundaries. Collect or escrow at least 80,000 PLN of customer continuity cash. Complete one contractor code/security/support review and one small patch, compatibility test, or data-export-support task under the new operating workflow before meaningful seller payout.

6-month POC:
Close one app-tail acquisition or support-rights carveout. Retain at least 10 paying Data Center customers after the seller-endorsed handoff. Collect 180,000-500,000 PLN in prepaid or invoiced continuity revenue. Ship at least two maintenance, compatibility, security, or export-support releases that benefit multiple customers. Keep recurring support under 1.2 contractor/founder hours per customer per month after the transition month. Keep gross margin above 65% after contractor support, app hosting/build infrastructure, security review, legal/IP review, seller earnout, and marketplace/vendor costs. Build a second seller pipeline with at least 5 qualified small Data Center app vendors, but do not close a second tail until the first support economics prove repeatable.

First acquisition mechanism:
Search Atlassian Marketplace and vendor pages for Data Center apps with visible installed bases, small vendors, no or weak Cloud equivalent, migration/export friction, narrow workflow value, recent support slowdown, low marketing activity, and customers likely to be regulated or migration-constrained. Target apps that are boring but embedded: workflow validators, Confluence macros, reporting add-ons, admin utilities, issue synchronization helpers, export tools, approval/checklist plugins, structured field utilities, or ITSM helpers. The seller pitch is: "Your Data Center tail is shrinking but customers still need continuity. I will option the tail, convert named customers into prepaid continuity, pay you mostly from retained cash, and leave your Cloud roadmap alone."

Economics:
Charge 10,000-40,000 PLN per customer per year for fixed-scope continuity, depending on user count, support load, app criticality, and migration/export needs. Charge separate paid escalation for production incidents, bespoke migration scripts, major version jumps, security emergency work, data export projects, or custom integrations. Seller consideration is a small option fee plus earnout from retained Data Center customer cash, not a large upfront acquisition. The first app should pay for itself from customer prepayments before close. Reject apps unless one reusable patch or support workflow can serve multiple customers and customer prepayments cover acquisition risk.

Copy risk:
Atlassian Solution Partners, app vendors, migration consultants, enterprise admins, systems integrators, and larger Marketplace partners can copy the category. The defense is exact asset control: source, update/build path, support history, license records, customer list, seller endorsement, customer-approved payee/support transfer, and prepaid continuity contracts for one named app. A consultant can offer a migration project, but cannot inherit the specific app tail without replacing the seller option, customer consent, source rights, and payment stream.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The controlled object is one named installed app dependency and its customer-approved continuity cash, not generic Atlassian expertise. Once the seller signs the option, customers sign prepaid continuity, the source/support path is reviewed, and the payee/support-owner transition is accepted, another party cannot take that exact tail without renegotiating seller rights, customer consents, support scope, IP transfer, DPA terms, and payment routing. Incumbents can compete for future migration projects, but they do not own the controlled app-tail file or prepaid continuity book.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell Atlassian migration consulting, app discovery reports, dashboards, marketplace listings, buyer introductions, or generic support outsourcing. It options or acquires a named app tail and payment stream only after existing customers prepay continuity. The proof is app/source/support control, seller-endorsed transfer, customer-paid continuity contracts, retained customers, and shipped maintenance/export-support work under the new owner.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, data-residency advice, regulated compliance certification, security certification, Atlassian migration guarantees, cloud readiness guarantees, or managed Atlassian administration outside the acquired app scope. Atlassian remains the platform and Marketplace authority. Customers remain responsible for their Atlassian instances, backups, access control, migration decisions, cloud approvals, and compliance decisions. Seller must prove IP ownership, contractor assignments, licensing rights, and right to transfer or carve out support. Customer data access is minimized, consented, logged, and covered by DPA/security terms. Reject apps with unclear IP, copied code, unresolved severe vulnerabilities, hidden credential exposure, missing build path, no reusable support surface, heavy bespoke services, or customers unwilling to approve the payee/support transition.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack, REDBlocked Amazon Connected-Device Evidence Rescue Desk, BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack, HeritageDoor Suites, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, OneStack Connector Lockbox, Shopify Ops App Continuity Buyout, or cPanel Hosting Continuity Book. The buyer trigger, asset, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: official Atlassian Data Center wind-down, a named Marketplace app tail, source/support/customer rights, customer-prepaid continuity contracts, and first patch/export-support under new control.

Strongest anticipated objections:
1. The right seller may be rare: clean IP, meaningful Data Center customer list, small enough to sell, but valuable enough for customers to prepay.
2. Customers may prefer known Atlassian Solution Partners, the original vendor, or direct migration to Cloud instead of trusting an unknown Warsaw operator.
3. Atlassian Marketplace transfer, licensing, app-vendor terms, data-processing terms, and customer approvals may be complex or restrictive.
4. App support can become urgent enterprise production support, security patching, version compatibility, migration scripting, customer-data export, or custom Atlassian administration.
5. Data Center is a declining tail, so revenue can decay faster than expected as customers migrate, retire, or replace the app.
6. One app proves a cash-flow acquisition, not a repeatable company, unless seller sourcing and support economics repeat.
7. Customer prepayment before close may be hard because the handoff itself can signal vendor weakness.
8. The 100,000 PLN budget is only safe if seller payout is mostly earnout and customer cash clears before meaningful acquisition consideration.
