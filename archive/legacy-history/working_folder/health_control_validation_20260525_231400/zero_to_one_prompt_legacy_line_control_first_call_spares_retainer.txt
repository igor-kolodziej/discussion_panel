Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: Legacy Line-Control First-Call Spares Retainer

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or option a retiring automation technician's named installed-base emergency account book, convert factories and integrators into paid first-call spare retainers, and acquire only exact tested PLC/HMI/drive modules tied to those named lines.

Exact buyer:
Maintenance managers at Polish manufacturing SMEs with legacy Siemens, Omron, Mitsubishi, Schneider, ABB, Lenze, Beckhoff, or similar production-line controls; also local automation integrators that already support such plants and need reliable emergency stock for named customers.

Acute trigger:
An obsolete or long-lead control module can stop a production line for days. A full retrofit is slower and more expensive than a like-for-like module swap performed by the buyer's own technician or integrator. The buyer pays for first-call access to exact tested modules before the failure happens, or pays a premium during a line-down event.

Control point:
The acquired seller-endorsed first-call phone/mailbox, installed-base/customer support book, line-level BOM and failure notes, customer-approved payment direction, paid emergency-spare retainers/reservations, first-look rights to decommissioned control-panel lots, and titled tested modules selected from those named installed systems.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Seller option/handoff over one named installed-base book from a retiring, overloaded, or succession-seeking automation technician/integrator.
2. Customer reauthorization letters for at least 8 named factories or integrators, approving the new first-call spare contact and payment direction.
3. Paid retainers or reservations from at least 5 customers for exact module families or emergency-stock access.
4. 20,000-40,000 PLN in paid reservations, deposits, emergency-stock retainers, or first emergency orders before large inventory exposure.
5. Titled tested inventory under 60,000 PLN, selected only from the reauthorized installed-base BOMs and failure histories.
6. Serial, photo, firmware/revision notes where observable, test records, title trail, and limited return terms for each stocked module.
7. One paid emergency sale, rental, or reserve-release transaction.

6-month POC:
Month 1: identify 25-40 retiring or overloaded automation technicians, small integrators, plant electricians, machine rebuilders, and decommissioning sources; sign one seller option that includes warm handoff and access to named installed-base notes.
Month 2: reauthorize at least 8 customer accounts, collect paid retainers/reservations from at least 5, and buy only modules tied to those customers' BOMs.
Month 3: complete one emergency sale/rental/reserve release or a paid reserve-stock dispatch drill with buyer acceptance.
Months 4-6: expand to 15-25 reauthorized accounts, 10+ paid retainers/reservations, a tested inventory value below 100,000 PLN, two first-look supply sources, and at least 70,000-120,000 PLN cumulative collected cash from retainers, deposits, rentals, emergency sales, or paid sourcing.

First acquisition mechanism:
Source retiring or overloaded automation technicians, small integrators, machine rebuilders, control-panel decommissioners, and plant electricians. Pay an option or earnout only after customer reauthorizations and prepaid reservations prove that the book is current demand, not a stale lead list. Use contractors for testing, photos, serial logs, and safe handling.

Economics:
Revenue comes from first-call emergency-stock retainers, paid reservations for exact module families, emergency sales, short-term loaner/rental with deposit, consignment spread, and paid sourcing for named buyer requests. The first proof should collect 20,000-40,000 PLN before inventory exposure exceeds 60,000 PLN. Emergency transactions can carry high gross margin because downtime drives willingness to pay, but the model only works if deposits and retainers fund most inventory and dead-stock risk is reserved against.

Copy risk:
Radwell, EU Automation, PLC Center, repair shops, local integrators, OEMs, Allegro/eBay sellers, and global used-automation suppliers can all supply parts. The defense is not broad inventory. The defense is the signed installed-base book, customer payment authorization, seller endorsement, named line BOMs, first-look Polish decommissioning lots, and exact titled modules tied to those customers before a failure occurs.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific account/asset/payment stream:
After the founder controls the seller-endorsed phone/mailbox, customer-approved first-call terms, paid reservations, and exact modules mapped to named lines, a competitor cannot recreate that relationship or reserve those same modules for those same sites. They can only offer substitute parts after the buyer starts searching, usually under worse time pressure and with less context.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business owns or controls the payment stream and physical emergency stock. It does not sell advice, diagnostics, a software dashboard, a parts directory, or generic sourcing introductions. Qualified automation technicians, the customer's own integrator, or another authorized contractor handle installation, programming, safety review, and engineering.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The founder does not perform safety-critical engineering, PLC programming, machine guarding, CE conformity work, electrical installation, or warranty repair unless a qualified provider is formally responsible. The offer is limited to titled used/refurbished parts, documented condition, payment/reservation control, and logistics. All modules require clear title and stolen-property diligence. Buyers or their integrators verify compatibility, install, commission, and accept risk under explicit limited return terms.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed and failed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, or BioSignal because it is not an evidence desk, platform unblock desk, or diligence pack. It is related to the earlier Factory Control-Spares Donor Bank, but the control point materially changes: the prior version centered on titled inventory and first-look supply; this version centers on seller-endorsed named installed-base accounts, customer-approved payment direction, paid first-call retainers/reservations, and only then exact inventory mapped to those customers. It avoids becoming generic industrial-spares inventory by refusing to buy broad stock without prepaid named-site demand.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Incumbents are strong and already trusted by maintenance teams.
Factories may still search known suppliers during emergencies instead of paying retainers.
Compatibility, firmware, revision, software licensing, and testing credibility can kill transactions.
Seller books may be stale, personal, undocumented, or hard to transfer.
Line-down demand is lumpy unless paid retainers convert before failures occur.
Testing contractors, returns, dead stock, and title diligence can consume margin.
The part-time founder may struggle to coordinate seller handoff, customer reauthorization, testing, and urgent logistics fast enough.
