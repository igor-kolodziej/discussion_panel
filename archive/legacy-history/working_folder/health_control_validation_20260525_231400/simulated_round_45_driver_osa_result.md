## Best Truthful Wording

A white-label OSA evidence lane for occupational-medicine clinics handling professional drivers. The startup does not issue fitness decisions. It controls the operational bottleneck: 5-10 validated HSAT devices, booked 48-72h sleep-physician interpretation slots, driver pickup/return logistics, report custody, employer invoicing, and recurring review scheduling. The 60-day proof is one signed occupational-medicine clinic routing all suspected driver OSA cases for 90 days, one named sleep physician contract, 10 prepaid tests, and written confirmation that the clinic used the reports in its own driver medical decision process.

## Why It Failed

1. Authority problem.
   - The startup cannot decide fitness to drive. Licensed occupational/driver-exam doctors and sleep/pulmonary specialists own the legal and medical decision.

2. Control-point weakness.
   - HSAT devices plus physician slots are useful, but copyable by occupational clinics, sleep labs, telemedicine clinics, or fleet medical providers.

3. Channel resistance.
   - Fleets and drivers may avoid a workflow that can remove a driver from service unless the occupational clinic already forces the route.

4. Weak owner-earnings proof.
   - Polish home sleep apnea tests appear commonly priced around 395-650 PLN. Ten paid tests proves workflow, not meaningful owner earnings.

5. Treatment bottleneck.
   - Positive OSA cases may need CPAP setup, adherence proof, and follow-up. Fast testing alone may not solve the employer's real bottleneck.
