Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
KaucjaMark SKU Release Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A prepaid named-SKU release pack for Polish/CEE beverage producers, importers, distributors, and private-label suppliers whose Polish retailer, distributor, marketplace, or operator listing is blocked by deposit-refund-system mark, operator, EAN, artwork, product-master, invoice/deposit, or transition-stock evidence gaps.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE beverage producers, beverage importers, craft breweries, cider/soft-drink/water/juice/functional-beverage brands, private-label beverage suppliers, and distributors whose named SKUs must be accepted by Polish retailers, distributors, marketplaces, or operators under the active Polish deposit-refund system.

Best first buyers have 5-100 beverage SKUs, sell through retailers/distributors rather than only direct-to-consumer, lack a large internal regulatory/retail-master-data team, and have a current written SKU request or rejection.

Acute trigger:
A retailer, distributor, marketplace, private-label customer, operator, packaging supplier, or internal sales team blocks or delays a SKU because deposit-system artifacts are missing, inconsistent, or not retail-ready: operator membership/status, deposit mark/logo, covered packaging classification, EAN/SKU master data, product-card fields, label artwork, transition stock split, deposit value, invoice/deposit line handling, buyer response, or sell-in date.

Control point:
Prepaid named-SKU release case with:
- current written retailer/distributor/operator/marketplace/SKU-master-data request, rejection, or PO/listing condition;
- SKU/EAN/product-card file, packaging type/volume, label/artwork file, packaging supplier or printer contact, operator status/evidence, and existing product master data;
- buyer-approved response packet: SKU/EAN deposit field map, label/artwork evidence, operator/status evidence, transition stock note, known/missing items, and escalation list for legal/accounting/operator approval;
- right to retain anonymized case memory by packaging type, retailer request wording, operator issue, EAN/data-field issue, artwork rejection, and progress outcome.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Five prepaid named-SKU release cases from at least three beverage producers/importers/distributors.
2. At least two current retailer/distributor/marketplace/operator requests represented.
3. Two retail progress outcomes: product master accepted, listing progressed, PO released, corrected artwork approved for print, operator evidence accepted, stock transition plan accepted, or buyer narrows follow-up to a non-DRS issue.
4. 40,000-100,000 PLN collected.
5. One packaging/artwork reviewer and one accounting/legal/operator escalation contact on per-case terms.
6. Case memory across at least 20 SKUs/EANs.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a target list of 120 Polish/CEE beverage producers, importers, craft breweries, beverage distributors, private-label suppliers, and beverage-focused packaging/artwork agencies; build a redacted sample SKU release pack using dummy SKU/EAN/artwork/operator data; recruit one packaging/artwork reviewer and one accounting/legal/operator escalation contact.

Month 2: close five prepaid named-SKU release cases only where a written retailer, distributor, marketplace, operator, or SKU-master-data request exists. Produce SKU/EAN deposit field maps, operator/status evidence summaries, artwork/mark evidence, transition-stock notes, buyer response drafts, and missing-item/escalation registers.

Month 3: secure at least two retail progress outcomes and document all no-go cases: no live SKU blocker, not covered packaging, missing operator decision, legal/accounting question only, retailer issue unrelated to DRS, artwork/printer delay outside scope, or no budget.

Months 4-6: complete 20-35 paid SKU release cases, process 100+ SKUs/EANs, collect 150,000-350,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60%, get one packaging/artwork agency or beverage distributor sending three repeat cases, and build case memory by retailer/distributor/operator wording, packaging type, mark/artwork issue, product-master field issue, transition-stock pattern, and progress outcome.

First acquisition mechanism:
Producer/importer pitch:
"If a Polish retailer, distributor, marketplace, or operator is blocking a beverage SKU because of deposit mark, EAN, operator, artwork, product-card, invoice/deposit, or transition-stock questions, send the exact request and the SKU file. I turn it into a buyer-ready release pack: what is already supported, what is missing, what needs operator/legal/accounting approval, and what response should go back to the buyer."

Partner pitch:
"You keep packaging design, operator onboarding, accounting, ERP, legal, and printing work. I handle the urgent SKU release file when a beverage producer/importer has a live retailer or distributor blocker and needs a clean response pack fast."

Economics:
Charge 6,000-9,000 PLN for one SKU family or simple importer listing blocker, 10,000-20,000 PLN for a multi-SKU retailer release pack, and 3,000-8,000 PLN for annual or new-SKU refresh once prior operator/artwork/master-data files exist. Reviewer/escalation costs are scoped per case. The buyer pays because a retailer listing, sell-in date, private-label order, distributor onboarding, or imported SKU revenue is blocked.

Copy risk:
Deposit-system operators, packaging consultants, artwork agencies, accountants, ERP/EDI consultants, lawyers, and retailer supplier teams can solve pieces. The defense is not legal knowledge; it is current SKU release speed, retail buyer wording memory, operator/artwork/master-data issue taxonomy, Polish/English supplier/importer communication, and repeated progress outcomes by retailer/distributor/packaging type.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a producer/importer routes a live SKU blocker, grants access to SKU/EAN/artwork/operator files, signs the prepaid case, and uses the startup's release pack under a retailer/distributor deadline, another provider must displace an active SKU-release process. The controlled unit is the exact retailer/operator request, SKU file, artwork/mark evidence, product-master data, response wording, and retail progress or pushback outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only current named-SKU release blockers with prepaid fees and specific buyer/operator requests. It does not sell broad deposit-system readiness, legal advice, accounting implementation, operator onboarding, packaging design, ERP setup, a dashboard, a database, or broker introductions. The output is a buyer-ready SKU release pack for a concrete listing, PO, distributor onboarding, marketplace ticket, or sell-in blocker.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not give legal advice, accounting advice, tax advice, operator-of-record services, packaging design certification, retailer guarantee, ERP implementation, or final compliance conclusions. The producer/importer remains responsible for legal classification, operator agreements, deposit-system participation decisions, label approval, accounting treatment, invoices, retailer communications, and product-market decisions. Legal/accounting/operator/packaging/printer questions are escalated or flagged rather than answered as final advice.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak because it is not recovery of retailer deductions, trade promotions, retail media charges, or remittance leakage. It is pre-sale SKU/listing release under the Polish deposit-refund system.

This is not GreenTender EPREL because there is no public lighting tender or EPREL product energy-label evidence. It is not CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, EUDR, KSeF, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Data Act, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, or Supplement Stack Safety.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Deposit-system operators, packaging agencies, accountants, ERP consultants, and retailer supplier teams may already handle these issues.
- Many SKU blockers may be simple data-entry or artwork tasks, too low-value for a specialist fee.
- Some producers may not have an urgent retailer/distributor blocker and will treat DRS as general compliance readiness.
- Operator/legal/accounting questions can exceed the founder's authority.
- Retailer acceptance remains outside founder control.
- The transition pain may fade after the 2025-2026 rollout stabilizes.
- The business may become low-margin master-data cleanup or packaging project management.
- Case memory may not be a deep moat unless the same retailers/distributors/operators repeat.
