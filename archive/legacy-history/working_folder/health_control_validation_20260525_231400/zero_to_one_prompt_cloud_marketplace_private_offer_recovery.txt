Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: Cloud Marketplace Private-Offer Remittance Recovery

One-sentence thesis:
A success-fee recovery mandate for small EU/CEE B2B SaaS vendors whose accepted AWS, Microsoft Azure, or Google Cloud marketplace private offers have payout, fee, tax, discount, refund, metering, customer-commit, or remittance mismatches that can be documented and disputed through marketplace portals.

Exact buyer:
10-150 person B2B SaaS and ISV vendors in Poland, CEE, and EU-adjacent markets with at least one cloud marketplace listing and accepted private offers, but no mature marketplace operations or finance-ops team. Best first buyers have 250,000 PLN+ annual marketplace GMV or one six-figure private offer where payout timing, marketplace fees, tax, customer discount, renewal, metering, or cloud-commit drawdown is not matching the sales contract, offer terms, portal reports, or bank remittance.

Acute trigger:
The seller closed a private offer through AWS Marketplace, Microsoft commercial marketplace, or Google Cloud Marketplace, but expected remittance is short, delayed, misclassified, refunded, taxed or withheld unexpectedly, not tied to the right offer, not consuming the customer's committed cloud spend, or inconsistent across portal exports and bank receipts. The cash is already associated with a closed order, not speculative pipeline.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate, seller authorization, marketplace seller-portal reports, private-offer terms, offer/order IDs, customer PO/contract evidence, payout/remittance statements, bank receipt evidence, tax/fee line items for accountant review, support-case submissions, and first accepted correction, credit, payout trace, or recovered remittance. The startup controls the claim workflow for the named closed private offers.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three signed recovery mandates from SaaS vendors with live private-offer/remittance issues.
2. Portal/report access or exported evidence for at least 10 private offers.
3. At least 500,000 PLN in candidate mismatch value screened, with only documented non-speculative claims accepted.
4. Five marketplace support cases or finance tickets filed with structured evidence.
5. At least one marketplace acknowledgement, corrected report, payout trace, credit memo, or recovered remittance.
6. First 20,000-70,000 PLN collected in setup fees, minimum fees, or success fees.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a target list of 80-120 EU/CEE SaaS vendors with public AWS/Azure/GCP marketplace availability or marketplace private-offer language, build reconciliation templates, and secure one cloud-marketplace operations reviewer plus one accountant for fee/tax boundary review.
Month 2: close three signed mandates with minimum fees, normalize reports for at least 10 private offers, and file the first marketplace finance/support cases.
Month 3: secure at least one accepted correction, payout trace, recovered remittance, corrected report, or documented no-go with clear reason.
Months 4-6: complete 10-15 mandates, screen 5,000,000 PLN+ in private-offer GMV, file 25+ structured marketplace cases, recover or clarify 250,000 PLN+ in payouts/credits/report corrections, collect 120,000-300,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 65% after specialist review, and document every lost claim reason by marketplace, offer type, fee/tax/refund pattern, timing, evidence gap, and support outcome.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target EU/CEE SaaS vendors that publicly list AWS Marketplace, Microsoft commercial marketplace, Azure Marketplace, Google Cloud Marketplace, Microsoft co-sell, AWS Marketplace private offers, Google Cloud marketplace procurement, or enterprise customer cloud-commit language. Outreach goes to founders, CFOs, revenue operations, alliances, and channel leads with a narrow ask: "Do any accepted private offers have payout, fee, tax, discount, refund, metering, or customer-commit mismatch against your contract, portal reports, or bank remittance?"

Economics:
Charge a 4,000-12,000 PLN minimum/setup fee for report normalization and claim register creation, plus 10-20% of recovered credit, corrected remittance, or previously stuck payout. Large private offers make one recovery worth tens of thousands of PLN. Founder work is structured report reconciliation, claim evidence, portal cases, and follow-up; accountants and cloud-marketplace specialists are paid only for edge review.

Copy risk:
Cloud GTM consultants, RevOps contractors, marketplace agencies, accountants, cloud alliances consultants, and larger ISV finance teams can do pieces of the work. The defense is narrow EU/CEE small-ISV focus, recovered-cash pricing, private-offer/remittance case memory, fast report normalization, and refusal to become generic cloud marketplace consulting.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the signed mandate, private-offer IDs, portal exports, bank remittance evidence, customer PO/contract evidence, support cases, and recovery workflow, competitors cannot act on that exact claim without the vendor switching. The asset compounds through mismatch taxonomy by marketplace, offer type, fee/tax/refund pattern, support-case wording, evidence package, and payout outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The output is not a marketplace-readiness report, RevOps dashboard, listing service, database, or brokered intro. The proof is an accepted correction, payout trace, credit, recovered remittance, corrected marketplace report, or documented no-go reason for a closed private offer. The startup is paid for cash recovery and payment clarity tied to already-sold offers.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not give tax advice, legal advice, accounting opinions, or cloud procurement advice. The vendor's accountant approves tax/withholding interpretation and the vendor remains responsible for revenue recognition, tax treatment, customer contract commitments, and marketplace account authority. The startup only normalizes reports, assembles evidence, files authorized support cases, tracks outcomes, and invoices for agreed setup/minimum/success fees.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak because it does not involve retailer trade deductions, promotional leakage, FMCG, retail-media claims, or broad retailer chargebacks. It is not Amazon Vendor Central recovery because the platform, buyer, evidence, and payment event are cloud marketplace private-offer remittances for B2B SaaS vendors, not 1P retail deductions. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The eligible EU/CEE private-offer seller pool may be small.
Larger ISVs already have finance operations, cloud alliances, or marketplace agencies.
Many apparent mismatches may be valid fees, taxes, refunds, timing delays, or contract misunderstandings.
Marketplace support outcomes can be opaque and slow.
Portal access and commercial contract data are sensitive.
Cloud GTM consultants can copy the workflow.
Success-fee cash conversion may lag if corrections take weeks or months.
The idea can drift into generic marketplace listing, co-sell, or RevOps consulting.
