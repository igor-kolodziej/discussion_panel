## Strongest Objections

1. LC discrepancy volume may be too narrow and lumpy: SME exporter, documentary credit, shipped goods, written discrepancy notice, partner receives the call, exporter consents, and fee approval all have to coincide.
2. Partners may like the idea but avoid routing live files because they absorb reputational risk if the case fails under their brand.
3. Banks and applicants still control acceptance, waiver, and payment, so outcome attribution can be weak.
4. Legal boundaries are fragile because urgent LC cases can involve fraud, sanctions, document authenticity, title, buyer refusal, cargo disputes, or legal interpretation.
5. A named documentary-credit reviewer is mandatory, and reviewer availability/cost can break speed and margin.
6. Partners can internalize the checklist after seeing several cases unless the startup compounds reviewer access, speed, and cross-partner case memory.
7. The 60-day proof target is probably too aggressive because live ticket timing cannot be manufactured by the founder.
8. Founder credibility is a serious constraint for an unknown part-time Warsaw solo founder handling high-stakes trade-finance files.

## Useful Lessons

This confirms the LC branch is not strong enough for the final gate even when the working chat accepts it. The working score rewarded partner-routed trust and cash-blocked urgency; the fresh score penalized volume uncertainty, specialist-labor dependence, and weak domination potential.

Do not keep iterating minor LC discrepancy variants. Future rounds should avoid partner-overflow service desks unless the founder can control a current payment stream, assigned claim, signed payment direction, or prepaid recurring queue with stronger visible volume.
