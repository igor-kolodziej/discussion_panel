Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Legal OOH Face Cashbook Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Option or acquire a retiring micro outdoor-advertising operator's exact legal advertising faces, site-owner consents, advertiser contracts, booking calendar, sign structures, and payment direction, paying the seller mainly from transferred current advertiser cash rather than prospecting for new media locations.

Exact buyer:
The economic seller is a retiring or distracted micro outdoor-advertising operator, sign shop, local media seller, property manager, or small billboard owner with 15-80 legal small-format advertising faces on private buildings, fences, retail parks, parking lots, clinics, gyms, schools, sports clubs, hoardings, or local roadside/private sites. The payors are current advertisers, agencies, local service businesses, real-estate developers, clinics, schools, franchisees, and regional brands already paying for those exact faces or booked upcoming campaigns.

Acute trigger:
The seller wants to retire, stop collecting rents and ad invoices, clean up a small non-core route, or dispose of small OOH faces before leases, municipal paperwork, advertiser renewals, maintenance tasks, and artwork files fragment. Advertisers need continuity for current campaigns and booked seasonal placements. Site owners want rent paid and a single accountable operator rather than abandoned signs or unclear ad responsibility.

Control point:
Signed seller option or acquisition agreement; list of every ad face with address, dimensions, photos, site-owner contract or written consent, permit/legality evidence where applicable, rent schedule, advertiser contract, booking calendar, artwork handoff, invoice history, arrears list, maintenance notes, sign-structure ownership, electricity/lighting responsibilities, and site-owner/advertiser-approved payment direction transfer. The startup controls exact ad faces, not a directory of possible locations.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One seller option over at least 15 legal ad faces. Site-owner written consent or assignment path for at least 8 faces. Advertiser payment-direction approvals on at least 8 current or booked placements. First-month advertiser cash collected on at least 30,000 PLN of booked placements before acquisition close. No-payment or removal decision on illegal, non-transferable, unpaid, unsafe, or permit-unclear faces. Seller consideration paid mostly as earnout from retained faces.

6-month POC:
Option or acquire 40-120 legal ad faces across 2-5 micro sellers in one dense region. Transfer 25-70 advertiser placements or booked campaigns. Collect 180,000-600,000 PLN in advertiser revenue, resale, renewal, and urgent replacement-placement revenue. Maintain gross margin above 55% after site rent, print/install contractors, basic maintenance, removals, electricity/lighting, invoicing, and seller earnout. Prove that at least 60% of acquired faces retain either the old advertiser, a booked replacement, or a paid agency/operator reservation within 90 days. Sell or wholesale weak faces to a regional OOH operator rather than carrying dead inventory.

First acquisition mechanism:
Direct outreach to micro billboard owners, local sign shops, ad-route owners, property managers, sports clubs, gym/clinic poster-frame operators, construction-hoarding media sellers, and retiring local advertisers' representatives. Seller pitch: "I buy only the legal, transferable faces with site-owner and advertiser continuity. You get an earnout from retained paid faces, and I remove or leave behind anything illegal or non-transferable." Demand-side pitch to advertisers: "Your existing face and dates stay live; pay the new operator only after the site-owner assignment is confirmed."

Economics:
Revenue comes from current advertiser payments, booking renewals, replacement advertisers, agency/operator wholesale reservations, and occasional resale of clean face packages. Small local faces may rent for 500-3,000 PLN per month; better private roadside, retail-park, hoarding, or facade slots can be higher. Site rent and maintenance are face-specific. The seller is paid 3-8x transferred monthly gross profit as earnout, not upfront for a route list. CAC is embedded in seller handoff and current advertiser payment transfer. The buyer pays because the ad face is already booked, local, visible, and cheaper to keep live than to redesign a local campaign or renegotiate a new OOH site.

Copy risk:
Outdoor-advertising operators, sign shops, local agencies, property managers, OOH brokers, and regional media firms can compete generally. The defense is not OOH sales skill; it is signed control over exact legal faces, site-owner assignments, advertiser contracts, booking calendar, sign structures, and first-month cash before seller payment. Competitors can source other faces, but cannot sell the already-controlled wall, fence, hoarding, pylon, poster frame, or booked campaign without displacing the site-owner and advertiser transfer.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a site owner consents to assignment, advertisers approve payment direction, and the startup controls the booking calendar, artwork file, invoice trail, sign structure, and current cash for a specific face, another OOH operator cannot copy that exact media position without persuading the site owner and advertiser to switch mid-campaign. The asset is the transferred face-level payment stream and site right, not a list of addresses.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup acquires and operates an existing face-level cashbook: legal ad faces, site rights, advertiser contracts, booking calendar, structures, and payment direction. It does not sell a report, dashboard, ad marketplace, directory, campaign advice, or brokered introduction. No site-owner assignment and no advertiser cash transfer means no acquisition.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Reject faces with unclear ownership, missing site-owner authority, municipal/landscape-law problems, road-safety risks, public-space permit uncertainty, unpaid rent disputes, electrical hazards, unsafe structures, counterfeit traffic signage appearance, school/minor-sensitive ad restrictions, tobacco/alcohol/gambling/adult-content issues, or removal orders. Site owners remain responsible for authority to grant rights. Licensed contractors handle installation, removal, electrical, height, lifting, and structural work. The startup does not give legal advice about permits or municipal advertising law; it uses written site-owner documents and qualified local review before paying for any face.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not HeritageDoor because it does not acquire architectural salvage or premium interior suites. It is not GreenTender because there is no public procurement or EPREL evidence. It is not TraceFaktura, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, PromoLeak, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The buyer trigger, control point, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: legal OOH site-right and advertiser payment transfers, not evidence response, recovery claims, municipal invoice routing, machine-data access, or physical heritage inventory.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many small outdoor ad faces may be illegal, non-transferable, under local landscape restrictions, unsafe, ugly, low-traffic, or worth less than claimed. Site-owner consents and advertiser contracts may be personal to the seller and may not assign cleanly. Advertisers may use the handoff to cancel, renegotiate, or move to digital channels. OOH operators, sign shops, local agencies, and property managers can buy the same type of route. Maintenance, printing, installation, electricity, vandalism, storm damage, rent collection, and removals can erode margin. Local ad demand may be too lumpy and sales-heavy for a part-time founder. The business can become a small local media operator, not a scalable startup, unless acquisition discipline keeps seller payment tied to transferred current cash.
