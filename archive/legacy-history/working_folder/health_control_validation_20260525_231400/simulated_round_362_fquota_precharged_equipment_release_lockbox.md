## Current External Timing Check

- Regulation (EU) 2024/573 is in force and governs fluorinated greenhouse gases.
- Article 19 requires HFCs contained in pre-charged refrigeration, air-conditioning, heat-pump equipment, and metered dose inhalers to be accounted for within the HFC quota system before being placed on the market.
- Article 20 makes F-gas Portal registration relevant to quota management, import/export licensing, and customs interconnection through the EU Single Window.
- Article 21 allows a quota holder to authorise another undertaking in the F-gas Portal to use quota for importing pre-charged equipment, and allows one delegation; acceptance in the portal is required.
- Article 23 says customs authorities verify whether the importer in the customs declaration has quota or authorisations to use quota before releasing goods for free circulation.

## Main Simulated Objections

1. Quota holders and established distributors may not deal with an unknown solo founder.
2. Customs brokers, F-gas consultants, and quota holders already sit closer to the data and decision path.
3. The legal boundary is tight: the founder must not unlawfully trade quota, act as a customs broker, provide legal advice, or facilitate illegal imports.
4. Some shipments will be impossible because the importer lacks valid registration, the equipment is prohibited, the refrigerant/CO2e data is wrong, the only representative is missing, or the product falls outside allowed conditions.
5. The importer pool may be narrow and lumpy.
6. Accepted authorisation is not the same as customs release; customs can still reject classification, documentation, declarations, or broader compliance.
7. F-gas Portal rules, correction deadlines, and quota-holder incentives can make case work slow and brittle.
