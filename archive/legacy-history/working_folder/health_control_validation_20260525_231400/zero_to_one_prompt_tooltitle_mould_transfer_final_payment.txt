Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ToolTitle Supplier-Held Mould Transfer & Final-Payment Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
For manufacturers and private-label brands whose dedicated moulds, dies, fixtures, printing cylinders, or tooling sit at a supplier, assemble the title, possession, condition, release, and new-producer acceptance file that lets the buyer move production or release final supplier payment without repurchasing or litigating over the same tooling.

Exact buyer:
Plastic, rubber, die-casting, metal stamping, packaging, consumer-goods, industrial-component, and private-label companies that paid for dedicated production tooling held at suppliers in Poland/CEE, Turkey, or nearby sourcing regions. The first buyer has a named supplier-held tool family tied to at least 150,000 PLN of annual production, duplicated-tooling replacement cost, delayed production transfer, or final payment exposure.

Acute trigger:
The buyer wants to move production, qualify a second supplier, end a supplier relationship, close a project, sell the product line, release final supplier payment, satisfy a buyer/customer audit, or avoid repurchasing a tool, but cannot proceed because ownership proof, supplier possession acknowledgement, serial/photos, condition notes, pickup authorization, no-lien/release letter, or new-producer acceptance file is missing.

Control point:
Signed mandate over named tooling and supplier; purchase orders; tooling invoices; payment records; ownership clauses; project correspondence; supplier storage/location acknowledgement; photos, serials, cavity count, shot count, maintenance/repair notes, and condition declaration; pickup and carrier authorization; new producer acceptance checklist where relevant; final-payment release tracker; and clean no-go record for cases with disputed title, supplier lien, insolvency, quality dispute, or missing proof.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign five tooling-release mandates; reconstruct at least eight named tooling files; obtain five supplier possession acknowledgements, release/no-lien letters, pickup authorizations, new-producer acceptances, final-payment releases, or clean no-go outcomes; document at least 500,000 PLN of production, duplicate-tooling, or final-payment value affected; and collect at least 50,000 PLN in fixed fees.

6-month POC:
Run 25-45 tooling-release files across 12-25 buyers; cover at least 70 named tools or tool families; obtain 25+ supplier acknowledgements, transfer authorizations, new-producer acceptances, final-payment releases, avoided duplicate-tooling decisions, or clean no-go outcomes; collect 350,000-900,000 PLN in fees; maintain gross margin above 65% after engineering/tooling reviewer and legal-template costs; build a private playbook by tool type, supplier country, ownership wording, evidence gap, condition-risk pattern, and accepted release format.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to procurement, operations, product-line, and finance owners at CEE private-label brands, plastic/rubber/metal/packaging buyers, and industrial component companies that source from multiple suppliers or recently announced supplier changes. Secondary channel through tooling engineers, sourcing consultants, product-transfer consultants, contract manufacturers, and M&A advisers who see supplier-held tooling messes before production moves or asset sales.

Economics:
Charge 8,000-18,000 PLN for a title/possession/release reconstruction file and 18,000-45,000 PLN for urgent transfer or new-producer acceptance files. Use fixed prepaid fees as the core model. A small success fee of 3-8% is optional only where final-payment release, avoided duplicate tooling cost, or transfer completion is explicitly attributable. Reject low-value tools unless part of a batch.

Copy risk:
Procurement teams, lawyers, sourcing consultants, tooling engineers, and contract manufacturers can copy pieces. The defense is a focused tooling-release file discipline, supplier-specific release wording, proof checklists, condition-photo standards, new-producer acceptance memory, and account-level control over named tools before a production transfer or final-payment deadline.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the buyer signs the mandate and provides tool purchase records, supplier correspondence, payment proof, ownership clauses, and target outcome, the startup controls that named tooling file, supplier acknowledgement request, transfer/no-go tracker, and fee right. A consultant can compete generally, but cannot resolve that same supplier-held tool without replacing the already-authorized file owner and duplicating the evidence path.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not broker tooling, resell molds, create a marketplace, build software, write generic supplier-switch reports, or provide legal opinions. It accepts only named supplier-held tools with a production-transfer, final-payment, or duplicate-tooling avoidance event and produces a possession acknowledgement, release/no-lien letter, pickup authorization, new-producer acceptance, final-payment release, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not handle active litigation, supplier insolvency, disputed liens, sanctions, fraud, unpaid supplier-debt fights, IP disputes, product-safety certification, engineering certification, tool repair, tool transport as carrier, customs, or legal advice. Do not say the buyer legally owns the tool unless documents support it and counsel signs off where needed. Qualified tooling engineers review condition/transfer practicality; lawyers handle disputed title, liens, release terms, and jurisdiction-specific issues. Reject cases where supplier cooperation or documentary proof is absent.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from HeritageDoor and other physical-lot ideas because the startup does not buy, store, resell, or salvage physical assets. It is distinct from OrphanMold/last-run parts ideas because it does not monetize obsolete molds or sell parts; it resolves supplier-held title/possession files for active production or final-payment events. It is distinct from recovery mandates because the control object is tooling title/possession/release tied to production transfer, not a receivable or platform claim.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many cases will be real supplier disputes over unpaid invoices, liens, quality, ownership, IP, or termination rather than document gaps; suppliers may refuse third-party contact; clean cases may be rare because competent procurement teams already archive ownership clauses and tooling invoices; condition and transferability may require engineering inspection; moving tools can damage them or reveal missing maintenance; new producers may refuse old tools; legal counsel may be required for no-lien/release letters; the founder may become a low-margin project coordinator between procurement, lawyers, engineers, carriers, and suppliers; repeated demand depends on enough buyers having supplier-held tooling messes that are valuable but not already legal disputes.
