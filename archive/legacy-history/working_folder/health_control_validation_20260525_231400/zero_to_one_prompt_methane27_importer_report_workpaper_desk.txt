Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Methane27 Importer Report Workpaper Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A deadline-driven workpaper desk for CEE fossil-energy importers and their customs/energy compliance advisers that turns 2025 import records, supplier responses, origin/producer/exporter information, and Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 Article 27 field requirements into a competent-authority submission support packet before and after the 31 May 2026 methane-regulation importer-report deadline.

Exact buyer:
CEE fossil-energy importers, smaller coal/coke/fuel traders, LNG/natural-gas importers, crude-oil or petroleum-product importers where covered, and customs/energy compliance advisers serving importers with EU methane-regulation importer reporting obligations.

The best first buyer is an importer or adviser with actual 2025 non-EU fossil-fuel import records, a named responsible person, a near-term competent-authority submission or correction need, and missing supplier/origin/producer/exporter evidence.

Acute trigger:
The second annual EU methane-regulation importer report is due by 31 May 2026 for the 2025 reporting period. Importers need to assemble origin, producer/exporter, import volume, supply-chain, monitoring/reporting/verification, and methane-abatement information from internal import records and non-EU suppliers. Competent-authority submission, board/compliance signoff, supplier response gaps, and future public methane transparency profiles create a deadline-driven workpaper need.

Control point:
Importer-authorized report workpaper with:
- importer mandate and named responsible person;
- customs/import records, invoices, contracts, CN/TARIC/cargo identifiers where available;
- producer/exporter/origin mapping;
- Article 27 / Annex IX field checklist;
- supplier RFI templates and response log;
- available producer MRV / abatement / emissions information;
- missing-field and no-go log;
- competent-authority submission support packet for the importer's own submission;
- reviewer issue-spotting from energy/compliance counsel where needed.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid importer or adviser-routed workpaper cases at 15,000-45,000 PLN each.
2. At least one report workpaper used in a competent-authority submission or correction response.
3. At least one non-EU supplier response obtained or a documented clean no-go for unavailable supplier information.
4. One energy/compliance reviewer on paid per-case terms.
5. Repeat field map covering origin, producer/exporter, import volumes, MRV/equivalence, abatement evidence, transport path, missing supplier evidence, and authority follow-up.
6. One adviser or broker channel that can route a second importer case.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 Article 27 / Annex IX field map and supplier RFI template; recruit one energy/compliance reviewer; build a target list of 40 CEE importers/advisers from customs, energy, coal/coke, LNG, gas, oil, commodity, and port-agent networks.

Month 2: close three prepaid live workpaper cases with actual 2025 import records and a named responsible person. Deliver import-record map, supplier RFI pack, missing-field log, draft authority response/workpaper, and board/compliance summary.

Month 3: document whether at least one workpaper is used in a competent-authority submission, correction response, board signoff, adviser file, or supplier follow-up. Record supplier response rates and missing-field causes.

Months 4-6: complete 8-15 paid workpapers or correction responses, collect 150,000-450,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, secure one repeat adviser channel, and build a private field-gap taxonomy by fuel, country, supplier type, importer process, and authority follow-up.

First acquisition mechanism:
Importer/adviser pitch:
"If your 2025 fossil-fuel import records are due in the methane-regulation importer report or a competent authority is asking for corrections, I turn your import files and supplier responses into a structured Article 27 workpaper: field map, missing-evidence log, supplier RFI, reviewer issue-spotting, and submission-support packet. I do not provide legal advice, verify emissions, or submit as the importer."

Partner pitch:
"You keep legal, energy, trading, tax, and customs advice. I handle urgent importer workpapers and supplier-evidence maps for Article 27 reports when your client has records but not an organized submission-support file."

Economics:
Charge 15,000-25,000 PLN for a focused single-fuel/importer workpaper, 25,000-45,000 PLN for multi-supplier/multi-origin importers with reviewer pass and supplier RFI tracking, and 8,000-20,000 PLN for correction-response or annual refresh packs. Reviewer cost is scoped per case. The importer pays because late, incomplete, or disorganized methane reporting can create authority follow-up, board/compliance anxiety, supplier friction, and public transparency-profile risk.

Copy risk:
Energy lawyers, ESG consultants, Big Four firms, commodity advisers, customs brokers, and internal compliance teams can copy pieces. The defense is speed, CEE importer focus, current deadline files, adviser routing, repeat field-gap taxonomy, supplier-response memory, Polish/English workpaper templates, and authority-follow-up memory.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once an importer or adviser assigns a live 2025 reporting file, prepays the workpaper, grants access to import records and supplier contacts, and routes supplier RFIs and authority-response preparation through the startup under the reporting or correction deadline, another provider must displace an active submission-support workflow. The controlled unit is the importer file, deadline, field map, supplier response log, reviewer notes, and authority follow-up trail.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live importer reporting or correction files with actual import records and a named responsible person. The output is a submission-support workpaper: Article 27 field map, import-record table, supplier RFI log, missing-evidence/no-go log, reviewer issue notes, and authority-response pack. It is not ESG consulting, a dashboard, a database, legal advice, emissions verification, commodity brokerage, or generic report writing.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, energy trading advice, sanctions advice, customs filing, emissions verification, methane-intensity certification, supplier audit, MRV certification, competent-authority submission as representative, or assurance. The importer, adviser, counsel, and qualified reviewers remain responsible for legal interpretation, official submission, authority communication, supplier contract terms, and commercial decisions. Cases involving sanctions, disputed origin, fraud, money laundering, restricted counterparties, or unresolved legal status are declined or escalated.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not CBAM Import Continuity Desk. CBAM concerns carbon-border-adjustment goods, customs release/free-circulation continuity, authorised CBAM declarant status, CN/TARIC exposure, supplier emissions for CBAM goods, and certificate-cost exposure. This idea concerns Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 methane reporting for fossil-energy imports, Article 27 importer workpapers, supplier methane/MRV/abatement information, and competent-authority reporting/correction files.

It is not DORA, REDBlocked, Data Act, BioSignal, GreenTender, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- The buyer pool may be small and enterprise-heavy, with legal/energy advisers already controlling the relationship.
- A Warsaw solo founder has weak credibility in fossil-fuel import reporting unless a named energy/compliance reviewer is attached.
- The 31 May 2026 deadline may be too immediate for cold acquisition, and after the deadline the wedge becomes correction/annual-refresh work.
- Supplier methane/MRV/abatement data may be unavailable, inconsistent, confidential, or commercially sensitive.
- Many importers may already use internal compliance teams, energy lawyers, Big Four advisers, or commodity consultants.
- The work can drift into legal interpretation, emissions verification, sanctions/origin questions, customs filing, supplier contract negotiation, or ESG assurance.
- Public methane transparency profiles create urgency but may not create enough willingness to pay for smaller traders.
- Case volume in Poland/CEE may be too sparse for repeat owner earnings.
- It is structurally close to CBAM-style import evidence work unless the methane Article 27 field map and fossil-energy importer channel repeat independently.
