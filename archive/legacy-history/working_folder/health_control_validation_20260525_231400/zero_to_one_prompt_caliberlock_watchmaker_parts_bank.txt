Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CaliberLock Watchmaker Parts Bank

One-sentence thesis:
A B2B vintage watchmaker parts bank that options or buys retiring watchmaker parts drawers and estate lots, catalogs exact discontinued movement-caliber parts, donor movements, dials, crowns, stems, wheels, bridges, balances, crystals, pushers, and bracelet/case components, then sells only against professional watchmaker repair deposits or verified active part requests.

Exact buyer:
Professional independent watchmakers, vintage watch restorers, jewelry-shop repair benches, specialist vintage dealers with repair queues, and restoration-focused workshops whose paid repairs are stalled by exact discontinued mechanical watch parts or donor components. The first buyer is a watchmaker with an active client repair, known movement caliber or part reference, owner deposit or approved estimate, and willingness to prepay or reserve an exact part if condition and compatibility check out.

Acute trigger:
A paid vintage watch repair or restoration is stuck because the watchmaker cannot source the exact part through brand service, normal distributors, existing parts sites, forums, eBay, or donor watches quickly enough. Typical blockers include balance complete, staff, setting lever, winding stem, date jumper, chronograph wheel, bridge, rotor, pusher, crown, crystal, dial, handset, case tube, bracelet link, movement screw, or donor movement for a named caliber. The watch owner has already approved repair or diagnosis, so a specific part can release current revenue rather than create speculative inventory.

Control point:
Titled or optioned retiring-watchmaker parts lot with seller bill of sale, provenance notes, drawer/tray photos, brand/caliber labels where available, no-stolen-goods representation, watchmaker reviewer inspection, condition grading, part-reference and movement-caliber catalog, compatibility notes, high-resolution photos, active watchmaker request log, deposit/reservation terms, no-return conditions for as-described vintage parts, and paid order workflow. The startup controls the exact parts and donor components before they are split across hobby marketplaces or sold as unsorted scrap.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Two retiring watchmaker, jewelry repair, or estate sellers sign exclusive option or sale terms over parts drawers/lots; at least one lot is titled and physically controlled; 300+ identifiable parts or donor components are cataloged by brand, movement caliber, part type, condition, and photos; 20 professional watchmakers are contacted with a narrow "send active part requests" offer; 10 paid reservations, deposits, or orders are collected against active repair jobs; 5 parts are shipped or handed off to watchmakers; one watchmaker places a second order; gross margin stays above 55% after lot cost, cataloging, reviewer fees, storage, packing, and returns/writeoffs; no consumer repair work is accepted.

6-month POC:
Control 4-8 retiring-watchmaker or estate lots; catalog 2,000-6,000 identifiable parts/donor components; build a request list from 80-150 professional watchmakers and restoration workshops; close 80-200 paid part orders or deposits; collect 150,000-500,000 PLN in part sales, reservation fees, and lot-sourcing fees; document 30+ repairs unblocked by exact part match; maintain a private cross-reference by brand, caliber, part type, substitute/donor compatibility, condition, buyer, and sale price; keep repair-service revenue below 10% of sales so the business stays a parts bank, not a watch-repair shop.

First acquisition mechanism:
Source supply from retiring watchmakers, small jewelry shops closing repair benches, estate clear-outs, watchmaker families, old tool/parts auctions, collector lots, and CEE repair shops with unsorted drawers. Offer fast cash, estate sorting, and a seller option: "I buy complete watchmaker parts drawers, catalog them, and sell exact pieces to professional watchmakers rather than splitting the lot into hobby scrap." On demand, contact independent watchmakers with a concrete request form: brand, movement caliber, part type/reference, photo of missing/broken part, urgency, repair value, and deposit amount.

Economics:
Buy unsorted or semi-sorted lots for 5,000-40,000 PLN where the seller values speed and bulk sale, not individual listing labor. Sell rare exact parts at 150-2,500 PLN each depending on brand, movement, condition, scarcity, and repair value; donor movements and rare dials/cases/components can price higher when provenance and condition support it. Charge deposits or prepayment before deeper part matching. The buyer pays because one exact part can release a repair invoice, avoid a donor-watch hunt, and keep a client relationship alive.

Copy risk:
Vintage watch parts dealers, eBay sellers, watch forums, brand service centers, retired watchmakers, estate dealers, and specialist restorers can copy pieces. The defense is not a public marketplace; it is controlling complete retiring-watchmaker lots before fragmentation, cataloging them into movement-caliber/request memory, selling against active watchmaker repair demand, and building a private map of which professional buyers need which parts now.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs an exclusive option or bill of sale for a specific parts lot and the startup has cataloged/reserved exact parts for active watchmaker repair requests, other dealers cannot copy those same owned components. They can search for other parts, but they cannot sell a bridge, balance, dial, stem, pusher, or donor movement already under title and reserved for a paid repair queue.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup controls titled or optioned physical parts and sells exact components against active professional repair demand. It does not repair watches, sell generic watch advice, create a hobby marketplace, list other people's inventory, broker introductions, build a dashboard, or write valuation reports. The product is the controlled scarce part plus catalog proof, condition photos, compatibility notes, and deposit-backed handoff.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide watch repair, authenticity certification of whole watches, appraisal, brand authorization, counterfeit goods, stolen goods, warranty guarantees, waterproofing, timing regulation, polishing, or consumer repair advice. Professional watchmakers remain responsible for diagnosis, installation, repair quality, customer communication, originality decisions, and final compatibility. The startup rejects suspicious provenance, counterfeit-branded components, stolen-property risk, restricted brand parts where lawful resale is unclear, and consumer owners seeking direct repair service.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not HeritageDoor because the controlled asset is compact discontinued watch parts and donor components for professional repair queues, not architectural door suites for premium renovations. It is not a generic physical-lot bank like hotel FF&E because the parts are small, high-value, exact-fit, and sold against active repair deposits. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, RAGProbe, CyberBind, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Existing vintage parts dealers, eBay, Chrono24/forum sellers, brand service channels, and watchmaker networks already supply many parts. Seller sourcing may be hard because good retiring watchmakers know the value of their drawers or already have dealer relationships. The founder lacks horology credibility and needs a paid professional watchmaker reviewer immediately. Cataloging unsorted lots is slow, expert-heavy, and error-prone. Condition, originality, compatibility, counterfeit, and provenance mistakes can destroy trust. Many parts will be low-value or slow-moving. Professional watchmakers may prefer known suppliers and may not prepay until trust is built. The business may become a profitable specialist inventory desk rather than a scalable or highly defensible company.
