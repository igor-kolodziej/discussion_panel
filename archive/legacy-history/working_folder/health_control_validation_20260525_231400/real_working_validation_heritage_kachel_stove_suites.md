## Zero To One Summary

The evaluator treated the idea as a physical-asset control play and agreed with the core logic: a complete, documented tiled-stove suite from one property is more valuable than random loose tiles. The wedge is strongest when the founder controls an intact, photographed, numbered suite before demolition and has a buyer who values provenance, completeness, and original visual rhythm.

The score stayed far below the gate because repeatable demand is uncertain, logistics and breakage are serious, functional rebuild depends on qualified stove fitters and building/chimney safety, and reproduction/new tiled stoves are viable substitutes.

## Strongest Objections

1. Demand is narrower than doors because a stove is usually one focal object, not a repeated architectural element across many rooms.
2. Cheap supply does not equal valuable inventory; many old tiled stoves are low-value unless rare, complete, attractive, and buyer-matched.
3. Dismantling, weight, dust, soot, storage, palletizing, and breakage can kill margin.
4. Functional rebuild is outside startup control because chimney, fire safety, ventilation, building rules, conservation approval, and stove-fitter judgment matter.
5. Stove fitters, antique dealers, and heritage renovators may already see the best supply.
6. Taste risk is high across color, glaze, relief pattern, period, dimensions, crown/plinth style, and room fit.
7. Missing or damaged pieces can push a premium suite into a low-value parts lot.
8. Storage can become a graveyard if deposits do not precede removal.
9. Conservation and title risk require strict written source notes.
10. The business can drift into generic salvage if it buys loose tiles or incomplete stoves.

## Evaluator's Best Move

The evaluator recommended a stricter 60-day proof:

- control 2 complete suites, not a broader pile of partial opportunities;
- get 2 buyer deposits before dismantling or restoration spend;
- have one stove fitter inspect and quote each suite;
- sell or reserve one suite at 45%+ gross margin after dismantling, transport, cleaning, storage, breakage allowance, and seller payout;
- reject anything incomplete, common, damaged, overpainted beyond recovery, unclear-title, or functional-rebuild-dependent.

## Lesson

Not every coherent heritage suite inherits HeritageDoor's strength. The next physical-control test needs broader premium demand, less safety/building dependency, and easier inspection/placement before buyer deposit.
