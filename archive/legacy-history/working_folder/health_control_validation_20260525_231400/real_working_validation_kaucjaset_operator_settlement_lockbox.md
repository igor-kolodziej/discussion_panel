## Zero To One Verdict

Zero To One considered the idea materially better than a generic compliance desk because it targets a cash-flow artifact: named DRS settlement batches with refunds, handling fees, counts, operator statements, and evidence. It also gave credit for the Poland-local wedge, because the system is live, fragmented, and document-heavy.

The validation still failed decisively because the business risks becoming low-margin reconciliation labor unless it focuses only on multi-site owners with recurring high-value batches and operator-specific accepted-evidence memory.

## Strongest Objections

1. Batch values may be too small. Individual disputed DRS settlements may not justify setup or success fees unless one owner controls many high-return sites.
2. Operators may refuse third-party payment direction or assignment. If operators settle only with the contracted retailer through their own portal, the startup becomes an adviser rather than a controlled payment-flow business.
3. The market may standardize quickly as DRS portals, RVM integrations, POS exports, and accounting routines mature through 2026.
4. RVM-heavy collection may reduce messy manual evidence. The best niche would need mixed manual/RVM, franchise, shared collection, or multi-operator reconciliation pain.
5. Accountants and POS/RVM vendors may already own the trusted data and retailer relationship.
6. Handling funds creates avoidable payment, legal, AML, tax, and accounting complexity. The cleaner model is operator-to-retailer payment plus startup invoice.
7. Fraud and weak evidence risk is high: fake returns, duplicate counts, missing seals, POS/refund gaps, and rejected packaging can destroy trust.
8. Franchise headquarters can internalize repeat patterns with templates and accountant instructions.

## Lesson

The control point is not strong enough for the gate. A signed mandate over DRS batches gives permission to help and may produce recoverable cash, but it does not reliably control the operator payment path. The business also fails the owner-earnings bar unless each mandate controls many sites and recurring high-value mismatches.

Do not continue with broad DRS settlement variants. Only revisit this domain if the control object is a formally accepted operator payment instruction or assigned receivable over large multi-site settlement batches, with minimum monthly cash value already visible before intake.
