Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality.

Scored idea:

Stoma/Urology NFZ Micro-Shop Buyout is a profitable niche platform/acquisition idea for Poland's recurring reimbursed medical-device flow. It does not create a generic caregiver marketplace or delivery concierge. It options or acquires the legal entity / enterprise of a small NFZ-contracted medical shop that already realizes e-zlecenia for stoma, urology, continence-adjacent, catheter, urine-bag, and related medical-device supplies, then modernizes monthly fulfillment and caregiver ordering.

The target buyer/user:
- stoma, urostomy, catheter, urine-bag, and related urology patients, many elderly but also 50-60/pre-elderly after cancer, neurological disease, surgery, or urinary retention;
- caregivers/adult children who manage repeat monthly orders;
- hospital discharge coordinators, stoma nurses, urology clinics, and small medical shops that need continuity for patients.

The payer / transaction substrate:
- authorized clinicians issue e-zlecenia for reimbursed medical devices;
- patients realize those orders through NFZ-contracted shops/pharmacies/medical-device points;
- monthly/recurring products can create repeat order flow, with patient top-up/co-pay and premium-product choices where lawful.

Hidden insight:
The scarce asset is not advice about reimbursed products. The scarce asset is a small contracted transaction node with a recurring patient book: phone number, local trust, eZWM/NFZ workflow, supplier terms, SKU history, reordering calendar, caregiver contact path, and owner handoff. Large online shops can compete on price, but they cannot instantly copy a signed local book/phone/company transition with patient re-authorizations.

Control point:
The founder signs a conditional option/acquisition agreement over:
- the legal entity or enterprise of a small NFZ-contracted medical shop where transferable after legal review;
- phone number, domain/website, Google profile/local listings, and shop brand where transferable;
- eZWM/NFZ realization process, staff handoff, supplier accounts/terms, reorder calendar, active patient book, SKU history, and starter inventory;
- patient/caregiver re-authorizations for monthly delivery/contact, with clear patient choice and consent;
- hospital/stoma nurse/urology referral introductions from the seller.

The startup does not prescribe, issue e-zlecenia, or make medical decisions. Authorized clinicians issue orders; the acquired/contracted shop realizes them according to NFZ rules. The startup owns transaction execution, patient/caregiver reorder workflow, inventory, delivery, billing/admin, and acquired customer-book continuity.

2-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 60,000-80,000 PLN upfront option/acquisition budget for a small shop/company/book, with meaningful earnout tied to retained monthly revenue rather than full cash purchase upfront;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN for legal, accounting, NFZ-contract/enterprise-transfer diligence, GDPR/patient-transfer notices, and patient authorization language;
- 10,000-20,000 PLN starter inventory / working capital for key stoma/urology SKUs and courier packaging;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN for lightweight reorder CRM, phone routing, caregiver portal/forms, and delivery workflow.

Required POC proof:
1. One binding option/acquisition agreement under 80,000 PLN upfront plus earnout for a small NFZ-contracted medical shop / enterprise, covering phone/domain/book/eZWM process/inventory/supplier handoff where legally transferable.
2. At least 70-100 active recurring stoma/urology/medical-device patients verified in the seller's book.
3. At least 40 patient/caregiver re-authorizations or new service agreements for monthly fulfillment/contact.
4. At least 25-40 shipped monthly reimbursed orders through the acquired channel.
5. First NFZ settlement or clear written accountant/NFZ-process proof that the orders are realized correctly through the contracted shop.
6. GDPR/medical-data transition notices and patient-choice process reviewed by counsel.

Economics:
The purchase only closes if the book has recurring monthly order volume. Stoma and urology supplies can support meaningful monthly revenue per active patient, and the local shop often under-monetizes reorder automation, private accessories, premium product guidance, and delivery convenience. Acquisition-led CAC is the core advantage. Gross margin comes from supplier spread, patient co-pay/top-up where applicable, private accessories, delivery efficiency, and retained recurring order flow.

Why this is not a generic service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The foundational asset is the acquired or optioned NFZ-contracted transaction node plus active patient book, not software. If a competitor wants the same book, they cannot buy one SaaS tool or run ads; they must win or acquire another shop/book and transfer patient trust.

Main risks acknowledged:
- NFZ contract / eZWM process transfer may be legally or administratively harder than expected; if the legal entity cannot be acquired or the transaction rights cannot transfer, the idea weakens.
- Patient data is health data and must be handled with strict GDPR and patient-choice language.
- Some patients may switch to large online incumbents if price/service is better.
- Margins on reimbursed commodity supplies may be lower than expected after delivery, returns, support, working capital, and stockouts.
- A small book may be operationally messy or contain low-value/low-retention patients.
- Hospital/stoma nurse referral flow may not transfer despite seller endorsement.
- This should be killed if it becomes only a delivery concierge without company/book/phone/order-flow acquisition.
