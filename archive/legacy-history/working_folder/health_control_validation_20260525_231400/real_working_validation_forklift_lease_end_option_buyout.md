## Strongest Objections

1. Residual values may already be market-priced by leasing companies.
2. Used forklift dealers are structurally better positioned with technicians, buyers, transport, storage, refurbishment, warranty options, and UDT familiarity.
3. Lease-end purchase options may not be transferable, forcing the lessee to buy first and creating VAT, title, invoice, timing, and liability complexity.
4. Battery condition can destroy the economics.
5. UDT and service-documentation gaps can materially reduce resale value.
6. Buyer deposits before title transfer may be hard to obtain.
7. The 7-45 day option window creates execution risk for a part-time solo founder.
8. Lessees may demand too much of the upside once the value is explained.
9. The best opportunities may already go directly to dealers and service firms.
10. The model remains deal-by-deal without a strong compounding asset.

## Useful Learning

The evaluator did not reward control of an exact serial-numbered asset when the broader trade already has visible liquidity, stronger incumbents, and technical trust advantages. Future physical-asset candidates need a more proprietary source event, clearer incumbent blind spot, and a larger locked-in spread than generic lease-end equipment options.
