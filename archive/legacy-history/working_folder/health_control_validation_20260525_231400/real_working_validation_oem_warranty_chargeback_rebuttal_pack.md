## Zero To One Summary

The evaluator treated the idea as a commercially serious rebuttal/recovery niche with real money at stake, a clear buyer, a concrete trigger, and urgent pain when an OEM or Tier 1 warranty debit/reserve can wipe out supplier margin.

It did not score higher because the work is expert-heavy, relationship-sensitive, and often not fixable by packaging alone. Serious cases may require labs, teardown analysis, warranty engineers, commercial negotiation, and legal interpretation. An unknown Warsaw solo founder cannot credibly sell it without named automotive warranty or quality reviewers.

## Strongest Objections

- OEMs or Tier 1s may ignore third-party packaging and only listen to the supplier's quality director, warranty manager, commercial account owner, lab, lawyer, or recognized engineering expert.
- Serious cases require technical investigation: teardown analysis, lab testing, metallurgical or electronics failure analysis, FMEA review, root-cause engineering, field-return sampling, warranty part return review, design/process-change analysis, and customer-specific warranty procedure knowledge.
- Legal and commercial interpretation can creep in through warranty allocation, limitation of liability, field-action clauses, serial population, notice deadlines, contribution formulas, and settlement precedent.
- Suppliers may not have the needed evidence: serial trace, lot genealogy, shipment history, test logs, production records, deviation records, returned-part analysis, customer usage facts, or defensible technical explanation.
- Internal teams and incumbents already handle this: quality directors, warranty managers, commercial leads, plant managers, IATF consultants, automotive lawyers, and warranty consultants.
- Success-fee attribution is messy because later reductions may be credited to negotiation, expected OEM movement, delay rather than reversal, partial pack support, or offsetting future debits.
- Case cycles can stretch beyond a seven-business-day pack into ongoing RFIs, teardown reviews, meetings, revised claims, and negotiations, hurting margins.
- Data sensitivity is high: customer claims, warranty exposure, pricing-sensitive correspondence, serial/lot data, quality records, test records, failure descriptions, contract excerpts, and field-action context.

## Evaluator's Better Wedge

Seven-day OEM warranty debit rebuttal pack for CEE suppliers with a live six-figure customer claim and existing lot/test/quality evidence.

Accept only cases where:

- written OEM/Tier 1/customer claim exists;
- exposure is above 150,000 PLN, preferably above 300,000 PLN;
- supplier has internal quality and commercial owner assigned;
- serial/lot or shipment population exists;
- customer deadline or withholding/reserve exists;
- reviewer capacity is locked before quoting;
- legal/liability interpretation is excluded or counsel-routed.

## Lesson

Do not advance expert-heavy industrial rebuttal packs unless the founder controls reviewer credibility, evidence availability, and a buyer channel before validation. Current cash exposure alone is not enough when the buyer's main concern is recognized technical/legal authority rather than packet structure.
