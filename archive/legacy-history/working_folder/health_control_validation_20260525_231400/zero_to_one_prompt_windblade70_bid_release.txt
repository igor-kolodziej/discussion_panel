Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
WindBlade70 Bid Release Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A fixed-fee live-bid evidence pack for EU/CEE wind EPCs, repowering developers, turbine representatives, municipal-energy contractors, and procurement advisers whose public wind tender after 30 June 2026 is blocked by the new EU requirement to demonstrate at least 70% wind-turbine blade recyclability.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized EU/CEE wind project developers, repowering developers, EPCs, turbine resellers/representatives, municipal-energy contractors, public-utility contractors, and bid/procurement advisers supporting onshore or offshore wind public procurement bids. The first buyer must have a named public tender, named proposed turbine model or shortlist, a bid deadline inside 30 days, and written tender language asking for blade recyclability, environmental sustainability, EN 45555-style evidence, recycling route evidence, or confirmation against Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/718 / Regulation (EU) 2024/1735.

Acute trigger:
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/718 applies to covered public procurement procedures launched on or after 30 June 2026 and requires wind turbine blades of onshore and offshore wind technologies to have a recyclability rate of at least 70%, demonstrated at the latest upon contract completion. A bidder, EPC, turbine representative, or bid adviser sees a live tender clause and cannot tell quickly whether the proposed turbine/blade package has enough manufacturer evidence, recyclability calculation support, recycling-technology route, supplier wording, and clarification language to bid without disqualification, later contract-performance risk, or a forced model substitution.

Control point:
After the buyer signs the mandate and pays, the startup controls the current bid evidence file for that named tender: tender clause extract, bid deadline, turbine/blade model map, OEM/manufacturer evidence request, received blade recyclability documents, evidence-gap tracker, reviewer issue list, clarification/RFI draft, bid/no-bid memo, accepted no-go language, and delivery log. The startup does not control the contracting authority or the OEM, but it controls the time-sensitive evidence room and response artifact that the bid team uses before submission.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, secure three prepaid live-bid cases at 18,000-45,000 PLN each; obtain written mandates, tender clauses, proposed turbine/blade model lists, and OEM evidence trails for each; attach at least one qualified blade/LCA/recycling reviewer on a paid per-case basis; deliver three bid packs; produce at least one tender clarification/progress outcome, one model substitution or clean no-go decision, and one OEM evidence response trail; collect at least 50,000 PLN gross revenue with gross margin above 60%.

6-month POC:
Complete 10-18 paid bid packs, collect 180,000-550,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, build a private tender-language-to-blade-model evidence library, get at least two repeat channels such as bid advisers, municipal-energy procurement consultants, wind developers, or turbine representatives, and show that at least five packs changed a real bid action: bid submitted, clarification accepted, turbine model substituted, evidence gap closed, no-go decision made, or contract-performance clause narrowed.

First acquisition mechanism:
Monitor EU/CEE public procurement portals and utility/municipal tender feeds for onshore/offshore wind, repowering, public-energy, public-utility, port, water, district-energy, and public infrastructure wind tenders launched after 30 June 2026. For each live tender, identify likely bidders, turbine representatives, EPCs, and bid advisers, then send evidence-led outbound: "This tender appears to trigger the new 70% blade-recyclability requirement. If you send the proposed turbine/blade shortlist and OEM documents, I return a 72-hour bid evidence pack: model evidence matrix, missing OEM proof, reviewer issue list, clarification text, and bid/no-bid risks. I do not certify recyclability or give legal tender advice."

Economics:
Charge 18,000-28,000 PLN for a single-model urgent bid pack, 28,000-45,000 PLN for multi-model or multi-lot tenders, and 8,000-15,000 PLN for a fast no-go or clarification-only screen. Reviewer cost is 2,000-8,000 PLN per case, only when technical issue-spotting is needed. The buyer pays because a few tens of thousands of PLN is small relative to a public wind bid, bid-preparation spend, model substitution cost, disqualification risk, or later contract-performance dispute. Capital needs are low: tender monitoring, secure evidence room, reviewer retainers, and bid-pack templates.

Copy risk:
Wind consultants, turbine OEM bid teams, procurement lawyers, engineering firms, LCA advisers, bid consultants, and recycling specialists can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is narrow timing, exact tender-led outbound, repeated clause/model/OEM evidence memory, fast bid-pack execution, reviewer bench, and a private library of accepted and rejected blade-recyclability evidence by turbine/blade family and tender wording.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the general idea later, but once the buyer signs a mandate for a live bid, shares the tender and proposed model list, pays the case fee, and routes OEM/reviewer questions through the evidence room, the startup controls that specific deadline file: evidence tracker, OEM request wording, reviewer issue list, clarification draft, gap/no-go memo, and delivery trail. A lawyer, OEM, or engineering consultant can still advise the buyer, but it must displace an already-running bid evidence file before the submission deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The deliverable is a bid-release artifact for one named public procurement procedure and one proposed turbine/blade package: tender requirement extraction, blade evidence matrix, OEM evidence request, missing-proof log, reviewer issue list, clarification/RFI draft, bid/no-bid recommendation, and clean no-go language. It is not a general sustainability report, tender-alert dashboard, blade database, recycling marketplace, bidder introduction, legal tender representation, or engineering certification.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify blade recyclability, verify hidden BOM data, provide legal tender advice, provide engineering certification, issue conformity declarations, represent the bidder before the contracting authority, approve recycling technologies, design wind projects, or guarantee bid acceptance. OEMs/manufacturers remain the source of product truth. Qualified blade/LCA/recycling reviewers provide issue-spotting only. The bidder, counsel, OEM, engineer, and contracting authority remain responsible for legal, technical, procurement, and contract-performance decisions. Reject cases with no named tender, no proposed turbine/blade model, no OEM evidence route, alleged fraud, disputed product identity, legal appeal, sanctions/export-control issue, or safety-critical engineering claim.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs because it does not involve lighting distributors, LED tenders, EPREL energy labels, product information sheets, or SKU/lighting catalog matching. It is not CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, or Supplement Stack Safety. The distinct control point is a live public wind procurement bid file tied to the new EU 70% blade-recyclability requirement and a specific turbine/blade evidence package.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Live public wind tenders with this clause may be sparse in the first six months. Major OEMs and sophisticated EPCs may handle the evidence internally. Smaller bidders may not have access to blade BOM/recyclability data. OEMs may be slow or unwilling to provide useful evidence. Contracting authorities may treat the 70% proof as a contract-performance clause due at completion rather than a bid-stage blocker. Procurement lawyers, wind consultants, LCA firms, and OEM bid teams have stronger credibility. The founder needs a qualified reviewer bench immediately. Some cases will require legal interpretation, engineering certification, or OEM-only claims outside scope. If the buyer pool is only large enterprises with existing bid teams, this becomes a small overflow service rather than a strong startup.
