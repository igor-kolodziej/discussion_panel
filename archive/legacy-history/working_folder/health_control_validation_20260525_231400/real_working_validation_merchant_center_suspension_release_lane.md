## Zero To One Summary

Zero To One judged this as one of the stronger rescue-desk ideas because it has a painful revenue trigger, clear agency channel, prepaid case economics, low capital needs, and repeatable case-memory potential.

It still failed because Google controls the final reinstatement decision, many suspended merchants are bad-fit or unsafe, agencies can copy surface workflow, and cases can sprawl into generic site/feed/PPC cleanup.

## Strongest Objections

- Google outcomes are opaque and not fully controllable; the desk can improve evidence quality and appeal discipline but cannot control reinstatement.
- Many suspended merchants are bad-fit: counterfeit, fake discounts, hidden identity, dropshipping with weak fulfillment truth, misleading claims, banned/restricted products, or policy evasion.
- Agencies may route one case, learn the checklist, and then keep the work in-house.
- Suspensions can become generic feed, website, policy-page, CRO, PPC, or development cleanup unless tightly scoped.
- Suspended merchants may distrust upfront fees after prior failed consultants.
- Account access creates security and liability risk.
- Success bonuses can create bad incentives if not limited to clean cases.
- Case memory may not compound deeply enough because Google workflows and enforcement shift.
- Larger agencies may already have specialists.
- Ethical pressure will be constant; one bad case can damage the agency channel.

## Evaluator's Best Version

Launch narrowly as an agency-routed Merchant Center suspension release desk for live blocked Shopping accounts.

Only accept cases with:

- live Merchant Center suspension/restriction;
- meaningful blocked revenue;
- agency or merchant authorization;
- access to Merchant Center, feed, site/CMS, policy pages, and business identity evidence;
- no counterfeit/fraud/illegal product issue;
- no account-evasion request;
- prepaid fee;
- merchant approval before appeal submission.

The evaluator said the score could rise to `82-85` only after live proof: 2 agency routing agreements, 12+ prepaid cases, 5+ successful reinstatements/restriction removals, one agency sending 3+ cases, >60% gross margin, fewer than 30% rejected after triage, and average case time below 6-10 hours.

## Search Implication

Platform rescue lanes need either real proof of agency-routed outcomes or a less discretionary control point. Future candidates should target formal payment/regulatory exceptions where the buyer has a defined obligation, clear test cases, and measurable release criteria.
