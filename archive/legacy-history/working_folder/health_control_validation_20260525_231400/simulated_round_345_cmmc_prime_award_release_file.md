## Selected Idea

**CMMC Prime Award Release File**

### One-Sentence Thesis

CMMC is now entering DoD contracts, and some Polish/CEE defense, aerospace, electronics, software, and dual-use suppliers will have a named prime/subcontract award, PO, or supplier onboarding blocked by CMMC Level 1/2 evidence, SPRS/affirmation status, or CUI/FCI scoping; the wedge is a prime-routed award-release file, not broad cybersecurity consulting.

### Exact Buyer

Polish/CEE suppliers with a named U.S. defense prime, U.S. subcontractor, NATO-adjacent prime, or DoD-funded program buyer asking for CMMC/DFARS/NIST 800-171 evidence before awarding or activating a subcontract, PO, supplier onboarding, production order, or payment milestone.

Best initial buyer types:

- precision manufacturing and aerospace component suppliers;
- electronics and embedded systems suppliers;
- defense software/tooling subcontractors;
- engineering services firms supporting U.S. primes;
- dual-use industrial suppliers with FCI/CUI-adjacent requests.

### Acute Trigger

A written prime, subcontractor, buyer, or procurement note says the award, PO, onboarding, payment milestone, or supplier approval is blocked or conditional until the supplier provides one or more:

- required CMMC level evidence;
- Level 1 or Level 2 self-assessment evidence;
- SPRS score/status or annual affirmation evidence where applicable;
- CUI/FCI scoping and system-boundary statement;
- SSP/POA&M evidence where appropriate;
- subcontractor flowdown response;
- DFARS 252.204-7012/7020/7021/7025 evidence response;
- prime portal cybersecurity questionnaire response tied to the contract.

### Control Point

The startup controls one named award-release file under supplier mandate:

- exact prime/buyer request;
- solicitation, subcontract, PO, supplier-portal, or flowdown clause excerpt;
- named award/order/program and commercial consequence;
- CMMC level requested;
- CUI/FCI scope statement;
- current assessment and affirmation evidence where applicable;
- SSP/POA&M status where appropriate;
- evidence index;
- response chronology;
- reviewer-backed issue split: answerable now, remediation needed, C3PAO/RPO/QP route, counsel/export-control route, or no-go.

### 60-Day Signed/Titled/Assigned/Prepaid Proof

Within 60 days:

- one retained CMMC/NIST 800-171 reviewer with relevant credentials or prime-facing experience;
- two referral sources: U.S. govcon consultant, defense exporter adviser, prime supplier-development contact, aerospace/defense quality consultant, or CEE defense association contact;
- two to three prepaid supplier mandates at 18,000-45,000 PLN each;
- at least two submitted award-release files;
- at least one hard outcome: prime acknowledgement, accepted questionnaire, accepted self-assessment evidence, award/onboarding progress, payment milestone progress, or written no-go accepted by the supplier/prime.

### 6-Month POC

- 10-16 paid files;
- 5+ prime/buyer acknowledgements, accepted evidence responses, onboarding progress, award progress, payment progress, or accepted no-go outcomes;
- 3 repeat/referral sources;
- 250,000-550,000 PLN revenue;
- 55%+ gross margin after reviewer payments;
- private library of prime request wording, accepted evidence structures, no-go cases, reviewer flags, and CEE foreign-supplier scoping issues.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

1. Build a list of 80 Polish/CEE defense, aerospace, electronics, software, and dual-use suppliers with public U.S. prime, NATO, aerospace, or defense references.
2. Build a partner list of 30 govcon consultants, CMMC advisers, defense export advisers, aerospace quality consultants, and supplier-development people who see CEE suppliers but do not want urgent evidence collation.
3. Send a narrow offer: "If a named prime/subcontract award, PO, onboarding, or payment milestone is blocked by CMMC/DFARS evidence, we turn your existing facts into a reviewer-backed award-release file. No certification claim, no security implementation, no legal/export advice."
4. Accept only live written blockers tied to named commercial consequences.

### Economics

- 5,000-8,000 PLN paid triage.
- 18,000-45,000 PLN award-release file.
- 45,000-90,000 PLN urgent multi-entity or multi-prime sprint only if evidence exists and reviewer capacity is locked.
- Reviewer cost: 4,000-14,000 PLN depending on scope.

Value capture is tied to preserving or unlocking a named defense/aerospace subcontract, PO, onboarding approval, or payment milestone.

### Copy Risk

CMMC consultants, RPOs, C3PAOs, MSPs, MSSPs, govcon advisers, primes, law firms, and aerospace quality consultants can copy parts of the workflow.

The defense is not CMMC knowledge. It is controlled live award files for CEE/foreign suppliers, strict no-go discipline, and repeated case memory across prime request wording and foreign-supplier scoping issues.

### Why Incumbents Cannot Copy Before Control

They can copy a generic CMMC checklist, but they cannot work the specific file before the founder controls:

- the signed supplier mandate;
- exact prime/buyer request;
- award/PO/onboarding/payment consequence;
- current supplier evidence;
- reviewer-backed response matrix;
- no-go boundary;
- submission chronology.

### Why It Is Not A Service, Report, App, Dashboard, Database, Marketplace, Or Generic Broker

It is a named award-release file for a live procurement/payment blocker. It is not CMMC readiness consulting, managed security, a certification promise, a dashboard, a marketplace of assessors, or a generic questionnaire service.

The deliverable is a submission-ready file tied to one prime/buyer request and one commercial consequence.

### Boundaries And Legal/Provider Limits

- No CMMC certification.
- No representation as C3PAO, RPO, RPA, lawyer, export-control counsel, or contracting officer.
- No submission of false SPRS score, false annual affirmation, false system boundary, or invented evidence.
- No handling of CUI unless a qualified, authorized structure is explicitly set up.
- No security implementation except through separate qualified providers.
- No export-control, ITAR, sanctions, facility-clearance, FOCI, or legal advice.
- Reject cases involving classified information, unresolved export-control issues, suspected false statements, fraud, sanctions, or requests to backdate controls.

### Duplicate Risk Against Existing Confirmed Ideas

Not DORA: this is U.S. defense-prime CMMC/DFARS award release for CEE suppliers, not EU regulated-finance vendor renewal evidence.

Not SAM/NCAGE award release: entity registration alone is excluded unless the live blocker is CMMC/DFARS evidence tied to a named award or PO.

Not VEX/SBOM, CyberBind, or RAGTrace: this is not generic vulnerability, insurance, or AI security evidence; it is tied to a current defense procurement clause and award/onboarding consequence.

Not REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BioSignal, or HeritageDoor.

### Strongest Anticipated Objections

1. CMMC consultants, RPOs, C3PAOs, MSPs, MSSPs, and govcon advisers already exist.
2. Many blockers require actual security remediation, not response packaging.
3. Unknown Warsaw founder credibility is weak without a named CMMC/NIST reviewer.
4. CUI, export control, ITAR, sanctions, FOCI, and classified-work boundaries can appear quickly.
5. Prime acceptance and contract award remain outside control.
6. The CEE foreign-supplier niche may be small and hard to source.
7. Primes may prefer direct CMMC consultant relationships or internal supplier-development teams.
8. A one-file mandate protects the case but not the category.
