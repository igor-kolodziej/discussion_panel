## Source Checks

- Polish Public Procurement Law Article 453 requires return of performance security within 30 days after contract performance is accepted as duly performed.
- The contracting authority may retain up to 30 percent of the security for warranty/defect claims, and that portion is returned no later than the 15th day after the warranty/defect-liability period expires.
- Public tender documents routinely show 70 percent return after final acceptance and 30 percent return after warranty/guarantee expiry.

## Chosen Candidate

Idea name: Retencja30 Public-Works Security Release Mandate

One-sentence thesis: Recover due-but-unreturned Polish public-works performance securities for small contractors by using public contract records, final-acceptance/warranty dates, and signed mandates to obtain cash refunds or bank/insurance guarantee releases from contracting authorities.

Exact buyer: Polish construction, road, installation, landscaping, maintenance, IT-public-sector, and facility contractors with 10-250 employees that perform repeated public contracts and have cash performance securities, bank guarantees, or insurance guarantees retained after final acceptance or warranty/defect-liability expiry.

Acute trigger: A security return date has already passed or is within 45 days: 70 percent should return after accepted performance, or the retained 30 percent should return after warranty/defect-liability expiry. The contractor's cash, bank guarantee line, insurance guarantee limit, or collateral is still tied up and can block bids, bonding capacity, payroll, or working capital.

Control point: A signed release mandate for specific contracts/security instruments, public tender/contract records, final acceptance protocol, warranty/defect-liability expiry evidence, bank/insurance guarantee copy or cash-deposit proof, authority contact, contractor bank account, and a dated release-request package. The startup controls the assigned release queue and correspondence until the authority pays or releases the guarantee.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

- Five contractors sign mandates covering at least 20 contract securities.
- At least 2 million PLN equivalent in cash securities, guarantee exposure, or collateral is assigned for release review.
- Three contracting authorities issue release letters, refund confirmations, or guarantee-return/cancellation approvals.
- At least two cash refunds or bank/insurance guarantee releases complete.
- First success fees are invoiced after cash movement or guarantee cancellation.

6-month POC:

- Build a public-record target list from BZP/TED/local tender portals: contractors with repeated public works and contracts where acceptance or warranty-return dates are likely due.
- Process 120-200 assigned contract-security files for 25-40 contractors.
- Release 8-15 million PLN equivalent in cash securities, guarantee lines, or collateral.
- Generate 300,000-900,000 PLN revenue from setup fees, release fees, and contractor portfolio sweeps.
- Build a private database of authority-specific release wording, required attachments, delay reasons, payment timing, and contractor recurrence.

Economics:

- Intake/setup fee: 1,500-4,000 PLN per contractor portfolio, credited against success fees for first release.
- Success fee for cash returned: 5-9 percent of recovered cash security.
- Fixed guarantee-release fee: 1,500-5,000 PLN per released guarantee or 5-15 percent of verified annual fee/collateral savings where cash is not returned.
- Portfolio sweep: 8,000-20,000 PLN upfront for contractors with 10+ public contracts, plus release fees.
- Gross margin target: 70-85 percent because work is document/correspondence heavy, with lawyer review only for disputed or contested cases.
- Buyer payback is immediate: a 100,000 PLN cash security refund or freed guarantee line can justify the fee.

Copy risk:

Contractors, accountants, lawyers, bid consultants, bond brokers, and public-procurement advisors can do the work. The wedge is proactive public-record acquisition plus mandate control over overlooked release dates. Many small contractors manage current bids and site delivery better than old security release calendars; banks/insurers benefit from guarantees remaining outstanding; lawyers are too expensive for undisputed administrative release cases.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

Once the contractor signs a contract-specific release mandate and the startup files the release request with the public authority, the startup controls the correspondence chronology, evidence packet, deadline follow-up, and success-fee right for that security. A competitor can copy the process later but cannot take the already-assigned release queue, authority contact trail, or success-fee position.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The deliverable is cash returned or guarantee/collateral released on named public contracts. The product is a mandate-backed release action with payment on completed release, not a dashboard, report, procurement advisory, marketplace, or introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- The startup handles only undisputed administrative release requests where contract performance, acceptance, and warranty/defect-liability dates can be documented.
- It does not litigate, provide legal opinions, represent in disputes, negotiate contested claims, or challenge authority setoffs.
- Disputed, penalized, defective, setoff, insolvency, or litigation cases are declined or referred to counsel under a separate client engagement.
- Contractor remains party to the public contract and recipient of returned cash.

Duplicate risk:

Not TraceFaktura or other municipal invoice rails: this is not better invoice documentation or municipal service settlement. It is a statutory/contractual security release after accepted public contracts or warranty expiry. Not PromoLeak or retailer deductions. Not CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, HeritageDoor, or BatteryFit.

Strongest anticipated objections:

- Competent contractors already track large securities and bank guarantees.
- The remaining unreturned queue may be small, disputed, or low value.
- Public authorities may return securities automatically, weakening pain.
- Legal boundaries can become messy if the authority claims defects, penalties, setoffs, or missing documents.
- Acquisition from public records may be noisy because acceptance and warranty dates are not always public.
- Success-fee economics may look expensive for a simple request letter.
- The business may be a cash-flow service rather than a defensible company unless public-record targeting and authority-specific release memory compound.
