## Lead Candidate: Cloud Marketplace Remittance Recovery Mandate

### Why It Advances

- It is cash recovery, not a dashboard, audit report, marketplace setup service, or GTM advisory.
- The first proof is a signed mandate over a named payout discrepancy plus marketplace reports, offer/order IDs, support-case chronology, and first recovered payment, credit, released payout, or clean no-go.
- The buyer has current cash pain: private-offer payout, channel-private-offer split, marketplace payout, tax/withholding line, billing schedule, bank trace, or held/abandoned payout is not matching expected revenue.
- The founder does not move customer funds, underwrite tax positions, create marketplace offers, negotiate buyer contracts, or handle legal/accounting decisions. The client remains account owner; the startup organizes and pursues a named remittance case.
- The niche is small enough to map manually through cloud GTM agencies, SaaS CFOs, marketplace operations freelancers, and ISVs already using AWS/Azure/GCP marketplaces.

### Internal Objections

- Visible recoverable discrepancies may be rare; many apparent gaps are normal billing cycles, valid fee/tax treatment, buyer nonpayment, offer terms, channel shares, or report timing.
- Cloud marketplace operations consultants, cloud alliances teams, accountants, and platform support teams are natural substitutes.
- Seller-console access is sensitive and may block an unknown founder.
- Success-fee cash conversion may be slow if AWS/Microsoft/Google support queues take weeks or months.
- The founder's control is case-specific rather than durable; repeated channel supply must be proven.
- If buyers mostly need marketplace ops cleanup, this becomes cloud-GTM consulting and should be killed.

It should be killed if Zero To One finds that the discrepancy pool is too rare, incumbents already own the workflow, or support-case discretion makes recovered cash too unreliable.
