Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

Google OAuth Restricted-Scope Go-Live Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:

B2B SaaS, AI, workflow, email, document, backup, and integration vendors with Google API integrations will pay for a tightly scoped go-live release desk when a named enterprise customer deployment, renewal, procurement step, or rollout is blocked by Google OAuth verification, restricted scopes, security-assessment requirements, Workspace admin app controls, or a Google reviewer rejection.

Exact buyer:

Small and mid-sized B2B software vendors selling Google Workspace, Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Sheets, Docs, identity, backup, DLP, document automation, sales/email productivity, AI document workflow, RevOps, or workflow-automation integrations to enterprise customers. The buyer is the vendor's founder, CTO, security owner, customer-trust owner, or revenue owner when a named customer or cohort cannot deploy because the app is not trusted, verified, approved, or scoped acceptably.

Acute trigger:

A named enterprise customer, reseller, procurement team, customer security team, Workspace administrator, or internal rollout owner blocks go-live, expansion, renewal, or broader user rollout because of one or more of these live issues: unverified-app warning, OAuth consent screen rejection, sensitive-scope review, restricted-scope verification, request for security assessment or Letter of Assessment, rejected Google verification submission, overbroad Gmail/Drive/Calendar/Sheets scopes, Workspace third-party app access blocked or not trusted, missing admin-consent evidence, or Google project/configuration changes requiring re-verification.

Control point:

Signed vendor mandate; named customer/deployment/renewal blocker; Google Cloud project and OAuth client inventory; exact requested scopes; current Google review/rejection/submission thread; consent-screen configuration; homepage/privacy/terms/data-deletion evidence; use-case-to-scope map; demo-video script and proof checklist; data-use and retention answers; scope-minimization decision log; reviewer-response log; Workspace admin app-access packet; customer-facing deployment/trust packet; and security-assessment route through a Google-designated or otherwise qualified assessor when restricted-scope rules require it.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Three prepaid live cases with named customer or deployment blockers, not generic readiness work. Every case has a signed mandate, exact OAuth project, scope inventory, Google review/rejection thread or Workspace admin block, customer deadline, and prepaid fixed fee. At least one case results in a Google reviewer response accepted/narrowed, sensitive-scope or brand verification progress, successful scope reduction and resubmission, Workspace admin trust/allowlist progress, or a paid clean no-go. At least one restricted-scope case has an assessor-ready package and scheduled or completed security-assessment handoff. Target 60,000-180,000 PLN collected.

6-month POC:

Process 15-30 prepaid live Google OAuth or Workspace admin-consent release cases. Collect 350,000-900,000 PLN in fees and pass-through-managed assessment spend where applicable. Produce 8-15 objective outcomes: verified or re-verified OAuth app, accepted or narrowed Google reviewer response, approved sensitive-scope submission, restricted-scope security-assessment package accepted for assessor review, Letter-of-Assessment handoff, scope-reduction resubmission, Workspace admin trust/allowlist progress, customer security/procurement go-live progress, or clean no-go. Build a private case library by API, scope, app type, rejection wording, homepage/privacy/data-deletion gap, demo-video defect, scope-minimization route, assessor question, Workspace admin objection, customer wording, and outcome. Maintain gross margin above 60% excluding pass-through assessor fees.

First acquisition mechanism:

Do not sell to every developer trying to verify an app. Target SOC2 consultants, customer-trust advisers, appsec boutiques, Google Workspace MSPs, SaaS dev agencies, RevOps/integration agencies, and AI implementation partners with a precise offer: "Send only vendors with a named customer deployment or renewal blocked by Google OAuth verification, restricted scopes, security assessment, or Workspace admin app controls. I package the Google submission, reviewer-response trail, scope-minimization evidence, demo proof, admin-consent packet, and no-go decision without pretending to be Google, a security assessor, or legal counsel." In parallel, search public support/forum signals and outbound to B2B SaaS vendors whose product visibly depends on Gmail, Drive, Calendar, or Workspace APIs.

Economics:

Standard sensitive-scope or Workspace admin-consent release case: 12,000-25,000 PLN fixed fee. Urgent customer go-live or renewal case: 25,000-40,000 PLN. Restricted-scope prep and assessor handoff: 35,000-80,000 PLN fixed fee plus pass-through assessor/security-review costs. Optional success fee only when a named customer go-live, rollout, renewal, or procurement milestone moves after the case. Fees are rational because a single enterprise customer, renewal, or rollout can be worth far more than the release fee, and Google verification/security-assessment delays can block the vendor's revenue plan.

Copy risk:

Appsec firms, Google-designated security assessors, SOC2 consultants, Workspace MSPs, SaaS dev shops, customer-trust advisers, and internal engineering teams can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is early control of live go-live blockers, exact Google rejection wording, repeat scope/minimization patterns, reviewer-response memory, demo-video evidence patterns, Workspace admin objection memory, assessor handoff templates, and partner-routed cases before broad firms treat this narrow release queue as a product.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

Once the vendor signs the mandate, grants approved project visibility, provides the Google review thread, names the blocked customer deployment, approves scope-minimization decisions, and starts the submission/admin-consent workroom, a competitor cannot copy that specific case without the vendor switching mid-review and rebuilding context. Incumbents can copy the category later, but not the controlled live Google project, rejection trail, customer deadline, prepared submission evidence, reviewer-response chronology, and paid release file already in motion.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

It is an operating queue for live Google OAuth and Workspace deployment blockers. The output is not a report, dashboard, app, training, generic security questionnaire, or brokered introduction. Each case ends with a platform or customer action record: submitted verification package, accepted/narrowed Google reviewer response, scope reduction and resubmission, security-assessment handoff or Letter-of-Assessment evidence, Workspace admin trust/allowlist progress, customer go-live/procurement progress, or documented clean no-go when the vendor's app/data handling cannot support the requested scopes.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

The startup is not Google, a Google-designated assessor unless separately qualified, a QSA, a law firm, a privacy counsel, a formal security auditor, a certification body, a cloud provider, an MSP of record, or the app developer of record. It does not guarantee Google approval, customer acceptance, Workspace admin trust, security-assessment pass, legal compliance, privacy compliance, SOC2/ISO compliance, or production security. It does not collect or inspect production user data unless explicitly approved and unnecessary data is avoided. Vendor counsel, security leadership, developers, Google-designated assessors, Workspace administrators, and customer security teams remain responsible for their decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet. The control point is a live Google OAuth/Workspace platform access case tied to a customer go-live or renewal, not regulated-finance supplier evidence, Amazon connected-device evidence, investor biosignal diligence, heritage inventory, municipal event invoicing, retailer deduction recovery, import continuity, industrial machine-data access, public lighting tender evidence, supplement safety review, PV retrofit economics, or heat-pump noise evidence.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Google approval and Workspace admin trust remain outside the startup's control. Serious restricted-scope cases may require a real security assessment by a Google-designated third party, and assessor cost/timing may dominate the case. Many vendors can solve simple consent-screen, homepage, privacy-policy, or demo-video issues internally. Appsec firms, SOC2 advisers, Workspace MSPs, and dev agencies can copy the process. Some apps genuinely over-request scopes, mishandle data, or need product/security remediation before any release packet is honest. The 60-day proof is vulnerable to Google review timelines. Unknown-founder trust is weak because OAuth projects, scope maps, customer names, app architecture, and security evidence are sensitive. The work can drift into broad appsec, privacy, legal, product remediation, enterprise customer-success, SOC2/ISO, or customer security questionnaire labor unless entry rules stay strict.
