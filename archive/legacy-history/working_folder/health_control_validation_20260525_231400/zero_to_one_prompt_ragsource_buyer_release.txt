Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RAGSource Buyer Release Sprint

One-sentence thesis:
A partner-routed 5-business-day source-isolation evidence sprint for read-only RAG and enterprise-search SaaS vendors whose enterprise buyer has written that PO approval, renewal, pilot expansion, or production deployment is blocked until the vendor shows bounded evidence that retrieval stays inside the right tenant, workspace, connector, permission set, and source class.

Exact buyer:
SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, application-security boutiques, security-questionnaire firms, and AI implementation partners serving 20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors with read-only RAG, enterprise search, knowledge-base assistant, document-search, support-copilot, or customer-document retrieval features. The end SaaS vendor has a live enterprise buyer condition about source isolation, cross-tenant leakage, connector permissions, retrieval boundaries, sensitive-source exposure, logging, or human review. The first market excludes tool-using agents, production write actions, payments, external email sending, code execution, privileged admin actions, regulated finance model-risk, credit, insurance, HR decisioning, biometrics, clinical workflows, law enforcement, and autonomous adverse decisions.

Acute trigger:
An enterprise buyer, security reviewer, or procurement team writes that the vendor cannot close the PO, renew, expand a pilot, or enter production until it provides evidence that the RAG feature does not retrieve the wrong tenant's data, wrong workspace, unauthorized connector content, hidden source class, excluded documents, stale/deleted files, or unreviewed sensitive snippets. The buyer asks for proof traces, test cases, permission explanation, logs, or human-review workflow rather than only policy language.

Control point:
Signed partner-routed test ticket with vendor authorization, written buyer condition, commercial deadline, read-only RAG scope, sandbox or demo environment, synthetic or redacted tenant/source setup, source manifest, connector/permission map, retrieval test plan, logging access, human-review evidence, no-go/escalation rules, and buyer-safe evidence packet. The startup controls the scoped test plan, test prompts, source-isolation traces, pass/rework/no-go log, evidence room, and buyer response packet for that procurement deadline.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three SOC2/fractional-CISO/appsec/AI implementation partners sign routing agreements; six live RAGSource sprints are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 18,000-45,000 PLN each; every sprint has a written buyer condition, named commercial deadline, read-only RAG scope, authorized sandbox or demo environment, source manifest, connector/permission map, and approved scope limits; at least four sprints produce partner-accepted delivery, buyer-progress outcome, narrowed follow-up, approved lower-risk deployment scope, remediation/no-go decision, or clean out-of-scope escalation; two partners route a second sprint; gross margin stays above 65% after AI-security reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Deliver 25-45 partner-routed RAGSource sprints; collect 600,000-1,500,000 PLN in wholesale, direct-bill, and urgent sprint fees; document 18+ buyer-progress outcomes, 8+ remediation/no-go decisions, and 5 accepted lower-risk deployment scopes; get at least five partners to route 4+ sprints each; keep turnaround under 5 business days for bounded evidence-ready products; maintain a private taxonomy of source-isolation failures, connector-permission gaps, tenant-boundary bugs, deleted/stale-source leakage, logging defects, buyer follow-up patterns, accepted evidence formats, and no-go triggers.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, appsec boutiques, security-questionnaire firms, and AI implementation agencies that already support AI-enabled B2B SaaS vendors. Pitch: "When a buyer blocks a read-only RAG deployment over source isolation or cross-tenant retrieval concerns, route the ticket to me white-label. I run bounded source-isolation tests in an authorized sandbox, produce buyer-safe traces and pass/rework/no-go findings in five business days, and return the packet for your approval."

Economics:
Charge 18,000-28,000 PLN wholesale for narrow source-isolation tests on one RAG feature and one connector/source class; 28,000-45,000 PLN for multi-tenant, multi-connector, or urgent buyer-release sprints; and 45,000-70,000 PLN for direct-bill urgent cases tied to a large renewal or production deployment. Reviewer cost should stay below 35% of revenue after test recipes and evidence templates stabilize. The buyer pays because the sprint protects a 100,000+ PLN commercial event and produces evidence a policy template cannot.

Copy risk:
AI-security firms, application-security boutiques, penetration testers, GRC vendors, AI governance consultants, SOC2 consultants, and internal security teams can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is partner-routed live buyer deadlines, narrow read-only RAG source-isolation recipes, accepted/rejected buyer evidence memory, fast five-business-day delivery, no-go discipline, and a growing trace library of procurement-relevant RAG retrieval failures.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the partner signs the ticket, the SaaS vendor authorizes the sandbox, the buyer condition is attached, and the startup receives the source manifest, connector/permission map, test environment, and prepaid or partner-guaranteed fee, the startup controls the active test traces and buyer evidence room for that procurement deadline. A broader appsec firm can copy the category later, but it must displace the partner-approved sprint while the buyer review is already waiting.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only written buyer-release conditions for read-only RAG source isolation. The output is not a generic report or questionnaire answer; it is a bounded evidence artifact containing source manifest, test plan, test prompts, retrieval traces, permission observations, logging/human-review evidence, pass/rework/no-go findings, and buyer response wording for a specific PO, renewal, pilot expansion, or production deployment deadline. It is not broad AI security consulting, full red-team work, penetration testing, product remediation, legal compliance, dashboard, marketplace, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification opinions, DPIAs, SOC2 certification, ISO 42001 implementation, formal penetration-test certification, model validation, production incident response, product remediation, or guarantees of buyer approval. It does not test tool-using agents, production writes, payments, external emails, code execution, privileged admin actions, or regulated high-risk AI workflows in the first product. Partners, vendors, lawyers, DPOs, security officers, AI governance specialists, auditors, and enterprise buyers remain responsible for legal compliance, privacy, security, product changes, remediation, and procurement decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle ICT third-party-risk, Article 28/30 DORA evidence, exit plans, subcontracting, audit rights, or regulated-finance operational resilience. It is not AIQ Partner Ticket Book because it does not package questionnaire answers. It is narrower than the prior RAGProbe buyer security sprint because it excludes tool-using agents, broad AI-security assurance, production tests, and generic buyer evidence; it only handles read-only RAG source-isolation release conditions. It is not AppExchange security retest, VoiceAI Article 50, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BESSGuard, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
AI-security and appsec firms are natural incumbents and have stronger credibility. Partners may internalize the recipes after several tickets. Enterprise buyers may still demand formal penetration testing, approved-vendor security review, SOC2/ISO evidence, legal AI Act analysis, or product remediation. Sensitive source manifests, logs, prompts, connector maps, and sandbox data create security obligations. Test failures often require engineering fixes rather than an evidence packet. A five-business-day sprint can create false assurance unless scope limits are explicit. The first market is narrower than broad AI security because it requires a written buyer condition, read-only RAG feature, usable sandbox, source manifest, and partner willingness to route.
