Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: KasaSerwis Authorized Fiscal Device Book Buyout.

One-sentence thesis:
Acquire or option a retiring/overloaded authorized Polish fiscal-cash-register service book, controlling device serials, review dates, service history, customer consents, customer-approved payment transfer, technician/provider-of-record handoff, and first current-month inspection/repair cash before closing.

Exact buyer / customer:
Small retailers, restaurants, service firms, salons, fuel/repair shops, market stalls, local chains, and other Polish SMEs with fiscal cash registers or fiscal printers that need mandatory technical inspections, fiscalization support, repairs, paper/consumables, device replacement, and online cash-register support.

Source sellers:
Authorized fiscal-service shops, POS dealers, and registered service technicians nearing retirement, overloaded, or lacking succession.

Acute trigger:
Fiscal devices require periodic technical review at least every 2 years by an authorized/service-qualified party. Late or failed review creates tax-risk anxiety, downtime risk, and disruption for small merchants. Many service books have real review calendars, support calls, and customer histories, but small local operators may lack succession or current capacity.

Transferable control point:
- Seller option or earnout over the fiscal-service customer/device book, support phone/domain/email, service records, customer notes, parts/consumables inventory, and manufacturer/dealer relationships where transferable.
- Device-level register: taxpayer/customer, device serial, model, fiscalization date, last and next technical review, service history, warranty/repair status, online-reporting notes, contact, and pricing.
- Written customer consent and payment-transfer approval for current-month reviews, repairs, consumables, or device replacement before close.
- Authorized service technician/provider-of-record agreement and manufacturer authorization path; the founder does not perform regulated service unless properly authorized.
- Current-month cash collected into the founder/new entity or lockbox before seller payout.
- Legal/accounting review for customer transfer, service authorization, data handling, VAT, liability, seller non-circumvention, and whether the transaction is an asset/book option or entity purchase.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:
- Conditional seller option or book purchase/earnout agreement tied to retained customers and transferred current-month cash.
- Device-level appendix listing the fiscal devices, due review dates, customer contacts, service history, and active obligations.
- Customer reauthorization forms or contract addenda approving new billing/payment direction and service-continuity contact.
- Technician/provider-of-record handoff agreement with scope, pricing, response times, liability allocation, and customer-facing continuity terms.
- First batch of current-month paid inspections, repairs, consumables, or replacement orders billed by the founder/new entity or lockbox before final seller payout.

6-month proof:
1. Identify 30 authorized fiscal-service sellers or POS dealers with retiring, overloaded, or succession-limited operators.
2. Inspect 3 seller books with device-level records, 12-month revenue, margin history, support burden, customer concentration, and technician/manufacturer status.
3. Sign one conditional option/earnout under 100,000 PLN total upfront exposure, with no close unless customer-approved payment transfer happens.
4. Get 30+ customer reauthorizations or 50+ devices moved into a transferred current service calendar.
5. Complete 20 paid inspections, repairs, consumables orders, or replacement orders under new billing with an authorized technician/provider.
6. Collect 10,000-25,000 PLN current-month revenue before or at close.
7. Prove 35-50% gross margin after technician share, seller earnout, parts, travel, call handling, accounting, and admin.
8. Document repeatability: source another two seller books and compare device density, customer retention, handoff load, and cash conversion.

First acquisition mechanism:
Seller pitch: "Your fiscal-device book has review dates, support history, and recurring service cash. I can option or buy it with a seller earnout if customers approve billing transfer and the authorized technician handoff stays clean. You get value from the book without forcing a full shop sale."

Customer pitch: "Your existing fiscal-register service continuity remains intact: same authorized service standard, clearer review calendar, current contact, and no device/tax chaos during the seller's handoff. The authorized technician/provider remains responsible for regulated work."

Economics:
- Typical technical reviews are often priced around 200-400 PLN per device, with additional revenue from repairs, consumables, paper, accessories, fiscalization support, online-device support, and replacement devices.
- Target acquired book: 200-700 active devices across 80-300 customers, with 80,000-300,000 PLN annual service/device gross revenue. Because the founder has a 100,000 PLN cap, the deal is structured as a low-down-payment option or earnout rather than a full cash acquisition.
- Down payment target: 0-50,000 PLN, with total upfront exposure including working capital below 100,000 PLN.
- Seller earnout tied to retained customers, transferred billing, and collected revenue.
- Target monthly owner contribution: 8,000-20,000 PLN after handoff if the device book is geographically dense and support load is controlled.
- First-deal payback comes from current-month reviews/repairs/consumables plus the recurring device-review calendar.
- No close unless current-month customer-approved direct billing proves cash conversion.

Copy risk:
Other authorized fiscal-service shops exist, but they cannot copy a signed seller handoff, customer-approved payment transfer, device-specific review calendar, and service history before the current reviews are billed. The defensible asset is the specific device/customer/payment book and continuity relationship, not generic fiscal-register servicing knowledge.

Why incumbents cannot copy before control is secured:
The founder is not trying to out-market incumbents. The founder targets small retiring or overloaded operators where the specific customer/device book, support number, service memory, and payment direction are transferred under option. An incumbent could call the same market, but cannot claim the same device history, seller introduction, authorized handoff, and customer-approved billing change once those are signed.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business controls an existing book of fiscal devices, customer approvals, payment direction, service records, and authorized-provider continuity. Software may help track review dates, but the acquisition value is the transferred device/payment book and current-month cash, not a dashboard or lead marketplace.

Boundaries and legal/provider-of-record limits:
The founder does not perform regulated fiscal-device service unless personally qualified and authorized. Authorized technicians/providers handle technical reviews, fiscalization, and repairs. Manufacturer authorization, service-technician registration, and any required official records remain with properly authorized parties. Customers remain responsible for truthful device use and tax compliance. Legal/accounting review covers data handling, liability, VAT, asset vs entity transfer, customer consents, and seller non-circumvention.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack: the buyer is not a bank vendor facing DORA renewal evidence, and the control point is an acquired fiscal-device service/payment book rather than regulatory documentation.

This is not REDBlocked Amazon Connected-Device Evidence Rescue Desk: the control point is not marketplace evidence routing; it is customer-approved direct billing and authorized service continuity for Polish fiscal devices.

This is not BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack: there is no health-claim diligence or investor validation-service sale.

This is not TraceFaktura All-Event Routing Pilot: it is not municipal event evidence routing; it is a private SME fiscal-device service book.

This is not HeritageDoor Suites: it is not a hospitality renovation/suite asset play.

This is not a generic calibration renewal book: fiscal cash registers have specific authorized service rules, device serial history, taxpayer review obligations, fiscalization/online-reporting context, direct merchant payment, and a visible pattern of small POS/fiscal-service operators with transferable books.

Strongest likely objections to test:
- Whether customer consent and service-record transfer are legally and practically clean.
- Whether manufacturer/service authorization is transferable enough for continuity.
- Whether retiring operators will sell a partial book under 100,000 PLN rather than sell the whole business.
- Whether support calls and travel time consume the margin.
- Whether merchants are too price-sensitive or disorganized to sign billing transfer before close.
- Whether existing POS/fiscal-service dealers can preempt the best books.
