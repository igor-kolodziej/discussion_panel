Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: 3PL Overbilling Credit Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
A signed credit-recovery mandate for ecommerce brands whose outsourced 3PL invoices for storage, pick-pack, packaging, returns, kitting, special handling, pallet/bin counts, receiving, disposal, minimums, or project labor do not match contract/rate-card terms and WMS/order/return evidence.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE ecommerce, DTC, marketplace, subscription, beauty, apparel, supplement, food, accessories, and specialty retail brands using outsourced 3PLs or prep centers. Best first buyers have 3,000-50,000 monthly orders, multiple invoice line types, a contract or rate card, WMS/customer portal exports, and a finance or operations owner who suspects invoice leakage but lacks time to reconcile.

Acute trigger:
Monthly 3PL invoices are high, the brand is cash-constrained, margin is under pressure, or a founder/CFO is preparing for contract renewal, 3PL switch, funding diligence, or seasonal inventory ramp. The specific cash event is already-invoiced charges where storage counts, pick-pack units, return counts, packaging charges, SKU handling, kitting, receiving, disposal, minimums, or project labor can be compared against WMS/order/return/storage evidence.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate, contract/rate card, WMS/order/return/storage exports, 3PL invoices, prior credits, email/work-order evidence, approved claim list, 3PL claim submission, and first credit memo, corrected invoice, offset, or cash refund. The startup controls the evidence ledger and claim workflow for named invoices.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three signed recovery mandates with ecommerce brands.
2. Twelve months of invoices, rate cards, and WMS/order/return/storage exports for at least two brands.
3. At least 300,000 PLN in screened candidate overbilling, with speculative claims rejected.
4. Five structured claim packages submitted to 3PLs.
5. At least one credit memo, corrected invoice, offset, or cash refund.
6. First 20,000-60,000 PLN collected in minimum/setup and success fees.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build reconciliation templates for storage, pick-pack, returns, receiving, kitting, packaging, disposal, and project labor; recruit one ecommerce ops contractor; contact 80 ecommerce brands plus 30 ecommerce accountants, fractional CFOs, operations agencies, and 3PL-switch consultants.
Month 2: close three signed mandates with paid screens, normalize invoices/rate cards/WMS exports, and submit the first claim packages.
Month 3: secure the first credit memo, corrected invoice, offset, cash refund, or documented no-go with line-item reasons.
Months 4-6: complete 10-15 mandates, screen 2,000,000 PLN+ in 3PL spend, submit 30+ structured claim packages, recover or correct 250,000 PLN+ in credits/offsets/refunds, collect 120,000-300,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 65% after contractor cost, and document every lost claim reason by 3PL, charge type, rate-card clause, WMS export issue, evidence gap, relationship risk, and credit outcome.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target Shopify/marketplace/DTC brands, ecommerce accountants, fractional CFOs, operations agencies, 3PL-switch consultants, and brand communities. The outreach ask is not "audit your logistics" but "send your latest 3PL invoice, rate card, and WMS export; if there is no claimable mismatch, we stop after a paid screen."

Economics:
Charge a 3,000-8,000 PLN paid screen/minimum for invoice/rate-card/WMS normalization, plus 15-25% of recovered credits, corrected invoices, offsets, or cash refunds. A single month of storage or pick-pack overbilling can be worth 10,000-100,000 PLN for a growing brand. Founder work is structured reconciliation and claim packaging; ecommerce ops contractors are paid only for edge cases.

Copy risk:
Ecommerce ops consultants, 3PL-switch advisors, freight-audit firms, brand accountants, spend tools, and 3PLs themselves can copy the workflow. The defense is narrow evidence-led recovery, fast claim packaging, accepted/rejected line-item memory by 3PL/rate-card pattern, and direct minimum-fee economics so failed screens do not consume founder time.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the signed mandate, invoices, rate card, WMS/order/return/storage exports, claim register, and 3PL submissions, competitors cannot pursue that specific credit without the brand switching. The case-memory asset compounds by 3PL, charge type, rate-card clause, WMS export structure, accepted evidence, and credit outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The output is not a logistics audit report, dashboard, database, broker intro, or 3PL-switch recommendation. The proof is a credit memo, corrected invoice, offset, or cash refund for named invoice lines. No credit, no success fee.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, accounting opinions, contract litigation, debt collection, or 3PL selection advice. Brands approve all claims before submission and remain responsible for commercial relationship decisions. Claims involving disputed services, strategic contract renegotiation, warehouse termination, legal threats, or unclear data are referred to the brand's counsel, accountant, or operations advisor. The startup only normalizes evidence, prepares claim packages, submits authorized requests, tracks outcomes, and invoices agreed setup/minimum/success fees.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak because it does not involve retailer trade promotions, promotional deductions, retail-media leakage, scanbacks, MDF, or broad retailer chargebacks. It is not Amazon Vendor Central recovery because the payer/counterparty is the brand's outsourced fulfillment provider and the proof is WMS/order/return evidence against 3PL invoices, not Amazon 1P deductions. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet.

Strongest anticipated objections:
3PL contracts and invoices vary heavily.
Some charges are valid but poorly understood by the brand.
3PLs may resist credits or use relationship leverage.
Brands may fear damaging the 3PL relationship before peak season or a warehouse move.
WMS/order exports may be messy or inaccessible.
Large brands may already reconcile internally.
Small brands may not have enough leakage to justify fees.
Consultants, accountants, or 3PL-switch advisors can copy the workflow.
