Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality.

Scored idea:

Lab Interface Continuity Buyout buys or options tiny Polish lab-interface MSP/vendor maintenance books that already support live production interfaces for private molecular, IVF, pathology, and diagnostic labs. It is not a LIMS, not a lab, not a diagnostic provider, not medical AI, not a clinical decision system, not a data marketplace, and not a custom integration agency. It does not perform tests, interpret results, diagnose, prescribe, sell patient data, or build net-new bespoke integrations during the proof.

Target seller:
- one-person or small-team lab-interface vendor, freelancer, ex-lab IT person, retiring integrator, or overloaded micro-MSP;
- already maintains live LIS/LIMS, HL7/ASTM, instrument, billing, and result-delivery integrations for several labs;
- has recurring maintenance invoices, renewal rights, or active support obligations rather than only old project files;
- is tired of support, underpricing maintenance, retiring, overloaded, or lacking a successor;
- can assign or transfer source code, interface mappings, runbooks, deployment notes, test files, support mailbox, issue history, renewal calendar, customer contacts, processor terms / DPAs where applicable, and customer-approved admin handoff.

Buyer:
- private molecular diagnostic labs;
- IVF labs;
- pathology labs;
- private diagnostic lab groups;
- labs where an existing interface failure delays sample processing, instrument import/export, result delivery, billing, physician portal uploads, or private customer reporting.

Acute trigger:
The current maintainer is exiting, slow to respond, or unable to keep up with LIS/LIMS upgrades, instrument firmware/output changes, HL7/ASTM mapping changes, billing/portal changes, OS/security updates, SFTP/API certificate renewals, or customer security requirements. The lab does not want a new LIMS project; it wants the exact working production interface kept alive safely.

Control point:
The acquired asset is the paid production maintenance book, not general technical skill:
- source code, scripts, mappings, test files, repositories, and deployment notes;
- support mailbox and ticket history;
- runbooks, known edge cases, customer-specific connection notes, and rollback instructions;
- renewal calendar, recurring invoices, blanket POs, or signed support obligations;
- customer contacts and seller-mediated handoff;
- customer-approved admin transfer;
- processor terms / DPAs where applicable;
- deployment/update credentials, SFTP/API certificate renewal paths, release paths, and access logs;
- one retiring or overloaded seller's signed transition support.

A competitor can build a new interface, but cannot instantly copy the exact production code, mappings, credentials, issue history, seller trust, customer-approved admin access, and prepaid continuity contracts already embedded in the labs.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option fee to the seller;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN legal/IP/DPA/contract-assignment review;
- 15,000-35,000 PLN technical diligence, credential rotation, backups, access logging, test harness, documentation, and one live-ticket fix;
- 0-35,000 PLN closing payment only after prepaid customer continuity cash clears, with remaining seller consideration structured as earnout from retained customers.

Required POC proof:
1. One signed conditional acquisition/option agreement with a named retiring or overloaded micro-vendor covering source code, mappings, runbooks, support mailbox, issue history, renewal calendar, customer contacts, customer-approved admin transfer, and seller transition support.
2. Clean IP/assignment/DPA review: no obvious lab-owned code, contractor ownership blocker, prior-employer issue, unlicensed library issue, transfer prohibition, or processor-term blocker that kills the deal.
3. Five lab customers approve admin transfer and sign new or novated continuity contracts before close.
4. At least 25,000 PLN prepaid three-month support/transition cash clears from those existing customers before close. This prevents buying dead scripts or imaginary goodwill.
5. Credentials are rotated, access logs established, backups taken, rollback procedure documented, and data-minimizing processor/security terms updated where applicable.
6. One live production interface ticket is resolved under the new owner without selling a net-new bespoke integration.
7. Support history shows mostly reusable continuity maintenance rather than custom lab IT chaos. Kill if the book averages more than about 8 support hours per lab per month or requires rewrite-level work.
8. Explicit clinical boundary: only transport, mapping, uptime, audit, format compatibility, release maintenance, and emergency production support. Exclude clinical interpretation, diagnostic logic, result-content modification, QC interpretation, medical-device claims, or IVDR/MDR software functionality.

Economics:
The business is acquisition-led, not ad-led. The seller brings the first customers. Labs prepay because the interface is already embedded and failure would disrupt revenue, result delivery, billing, portal uploads, instrument import/export, or physician/customer reporting.

Initial pricing logic:
- 1,500-5,000 PLN per lab per month for continuity/security/compatibility support depending on interface criticality and number of flows;
- emergency support fee for production breakage outside scope;
- paid transition package for credential rotation, backups, access logs, and documentation;
- strict exclusion of LIMS migrations, new instrument onboarding, workflow redesign, historical data cleanup, server remediation, broad cybersecurity work, and bespoke new integrations unless separately quoted after the POC.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The foundational asset is acquired custody of a paid live production interface maintenance book: recurring invoices/renewal rights, source code, mappings, credentials, runbooks, support history, admin access, named customers, and prepaid continuity contracts. Service work exists only to maintain the acquired asset. If a competitor wants the same accounts, it must replace functioning inherited production interfaces and overcome the seller-endorsed continuity transfer.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- Health/genetic data are GDPR special-category data, so processor agreements, access logging, credential rotation, least privilege, breach procedure, backup/restore, and data minimization are mandatory.
- The startup should avoid storing patient data where possible; it should maintain transport/interfaces and access production data only as needed for support under customer-approved processor terms.
- The interfaces must stay in transport, mapping, uptime, audit, and compatibility. If a connector interprets, transforms clinically meaningful logic, changes diagnostic output, calculates diagnostic values, influences medical decisions, or becomes medical/IVD software, that interface is excluded from the first acquisition.
- The startup does not sell patient data, create clinical reports, recommend treatments, operate tests, or market diagnostic claims.

Main risks acknowledged:
- The exact seller profile may be scarce.
- IP, DPAs, contracts, or admin rights may be non-transferable.
- Support history may reveal bespoke chaos rather than reusable interface maintenance.
- LIMS vendors and larger health IT integrators can compete, but the wedge is continuity of already-working production interfaces, not a better LIMS.
- Five customers may refuse to prepay if they do not perceive continuity risk.
- Gross margins collapse if the business becomes lab IT consulting.
- Security liability is real because production interfaces may touch sensitive health/genetic data.
- Liability caps, cyber/professional insurance, and incident terms need legal review before operating live customer systems.
