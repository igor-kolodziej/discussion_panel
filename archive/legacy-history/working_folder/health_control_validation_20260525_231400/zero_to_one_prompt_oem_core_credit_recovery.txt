Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
OEM Core Credit Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
A prepaid plus success-fee recovery mandate for truck, agricultural, construction-equipment, industrial, HVAC, and remanufactured-parts businesses whose refundable core charges, exchange deposits, advance-replacement deposits, or returned-part credits have already been charged by OEMs or suppliers but not credited after cores were returned.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE truck repair chains, agricultural machinery dealers, construction-equipment service firms, fleet maintenance workshops, industrial repair depots, hydraulic service companies, HVAC compressor wholesalers, parts distributors, remanufacturers, and multi-branch service businesses with supplier statements showing material open core charges, exchange deposits, remanufactured-part deposits, or advance-replacement charges. The economic buyer is the owner, CFO, parts director, service director, branch operator, AP lead, or working-capital owner responsible for recovering supplier credits already earned or writing off the leakage.

Acute trigger:
Supplier or OEM statements show 100,000+ PLN of open core, exchange, reman, or advance-replacement charges; AP has paid the invoice or the charge is sitting on account; parts/service teams believe the old units were returned; credits are missing because RMA numbers, core tags, job cards, serials, shipment proof, condition notes, supplier portal claims, branch records, or credit memo matching are fragmented across locations; and the buyer faces margin leakage, supplier account noise, write-offs, or working-capital pressure.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate over named supplier accounts and core-credit queues; supplier statements; AP invoice lines; part numbers; core charge lines; RMA or return authorization numbers; core tags or labels; job cards; branch return logs; photos or serial numbers where available; shipment proof or pickup notes; supplier portal claim access where authorized; supplier policy and time-window rules; credit memo register; claim submission log; and no-go rules for damaged, late, missing, untagged, undocumented, hazardous-handling, warranty-adjudication, legal-dispute, or physical-return cases outside the documentation recovery scope.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign 4-6 mandates from repair chains, dealers, distributors, service depots, or remanufacturing customers; identify at least 1,500,000 PLN in open supplier-statement core, exchange, reman, or advance-replacement credit value; file documented claims or reconciliation packets for at least 500,000 PLN of that value; obtain at least 250,000 PLN in supplier-acknowledged credits, credit memos, account offsets, refunds, or clean no-go decisions; collect at least 80,000 PLN in prepaid ledger-rebuild fees and success fees; and keep a rejection log above 50% for stale claims, missing shipment proof, condition disputes, low value, hazardous/regulated handling, physical-return work, warranty adjudication, legal disputes, or supplier policy exclusions.

6-month POC:
Handle 25-60 supplier-account recovery mandates across 12-30 customers; put 10,000,000-35,000,000 PLN of open core/exchange/advance-replacement value under review; file 4,000,000-15,000,000 PLN of documented supplier claims; recover or no-go 2,000,000-8,000,000 PLN; collect 450,000-1,500,000 PLN in fees; build a private recovery taxonomy by supplier, OEM, part family, part number, core policy, return path, RMA trail, branch record, proof type, age bucket, denial reason, credit memo matching pattern, and recovery outcome; source repeat channels from parts distributors, dealer groups, fractional CFOs, aftermarket consultants, branch controllers, and inventory/accounting cleanup firms; and maintain gross margin above 60% after parts-ledger contractors, supplier-portal operators, document QA, and secure workflow costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to truck, agri, construction-equipment, hydraulic, HVAC, and industrial repair businesses with multiple branches and visible reman/exchange parts activity. The ask is only for live account leakage: "If your OEM or supplier statements still show open core, exchange, reman, or advance-replacement charges after parts were returned, send the supplier statement and branch return trail. I rebuild the returned-core ledger, file documented supplier claims, match credit memos, and return recovered credit, refund, account offset, or clean no-go." Partner outreach goes to dealer-group accountants, inventory cleanup firms, aftermarket consultants, fractional CFOs, and parts ERP implementers that see unreconciled core ledgers but do not want to chase supplier portals and branch records.

Economics:
Charge 8,000-20,000 PLN prepaid to rebuild a supplier core-credit ledger and claim packet, then 15-30% success fee on supplier-acknowledged credits, credit memos, refunds, or account offsets. For large accounts, charge 30,000+ PLN prepaid plus a lower success percentage. The fee is justified only where open core/exchange value is at least 100,000 PLN and where likely recoverable credits are materially larger than the rebuild cost. Contractors are paid for ledger reconstruction, supplier portal work, and document QA, not for physical logistics, warranty adjudication, or legal disputes.

Copy risk:
Parts departments, AP teams, branch controllers, dealer-system consultants, inventory cleanup firms, aftermarket consultants, supplier reps, and recovery auditors can perform parts of this workflow. The defense is narrow returned-core credit specialization, multi-branch evidence reconstruction, supplier/OEM policy memory, part-family recovery taxonomy, claim-file control, and active mandate over the named supplier account and returned-core ledger.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the buyer signs the recovery mandate, prepays, grants supplier-statement visibility, provides AP invoice lines, branch return logs, RMA/core tag evidence, shipment proofs, portal access where authorized, and credit memo history, the startup controls the active returned-core reconciliation file and supplier claim chronology for that named account. A consultant can compete for future accounts, but cannot control the same supplier claim file, reconstructed core ledger, and credit-memo matching trail without the buyer switching mid-recovery and rebuilding the evidence chain.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell generic AP cleanup, inventory consulting, warranty filing, parts brokerage, a dashboard, a database, or introductions to supplier reps. It accepts only signed recovery mandates over named supplier core-credit queues and produces monetary artifacts: documented supplier claims, accepted return-credit packets, supplier acknowledgements, credit memos, account offsets, refunds, matched credit registers, or clean no-go decisions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not perform physical returns, transport parts, inspect cores, certify condition, handle hazardous goods, adjudicate warranty, provide legal advice, pressure disputed debtors, alter supplier records without authorization, fabricate return evidence, or pursue cases involving damaged/missing cores, fake tags, unresolved delivery facts, supplier litigation, warranty disputes, safety recalls, export-control issues, or regulated physical handling. Buyers remain responsible for return truth, condition facts, branch records, supplier relationships, inventory records, tax/accounting treatment, and final write-off decisions. Suppliers/OEMs remain responsible for credit approval.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, retailer OTIF chargebacks, warranty labor reimbursement, freight invoice audit, supplier rebate recovery, MDF claims, credit memo cleanup, unapplied cash, factor reserve release, SupplierPortal invoice release, SAM/NCAGE, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, or a generic evidence desk. The controlled object is a named supplier account's returned-core/exchange-deposit credit queue, and the proof is supplier acknowledgement, credit memo, refund, account offset, or clean no-go.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Large dealers and distributors may already have parts, AP, and branch controllers responsible for core tracking. Supplier time windows and condition rules may make many old claims unrecoverable. Missing RMA numbers, tags, serials, shipment proofs, or branch logs can destroy the case. Suppliers may deny credits based on condition, lateness, packaging, incomplete returns, policy exclusions, or prior write-offs. The first data rebuild can be labor-heavy. Some categories may involve hazardous or regulated physical handling that the startup must not touch. The market may be narrower than it appears unless multi-branch repair businesses have large enough open core ledgers. Copy risk is real because an experienced parts/AP consultant can replicate the workflow for future customers.
