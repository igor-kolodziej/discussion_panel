Scored idea:

Alcohol Interlock Lease + Calibration Renewal Book Buyout buys or options a small authorized alcohol-interlock installer/service shop's installed-device lease book, calibration renewal calendar, device inventory, phone/domain/mailbox, installer handoff, and renewal payment flow. The buyer is a driver or fleet/clinic/legal channel that must keep a vehicle's alcohol interlock installed, functioning, and documented. The startup does not give legal advice, addiction treatment, or court advice; it controls device/service continuity and renewal administration.

This is addiction-adjacent but not a prescription-drug business. The hard asset is installed device title/lease plus mandatory/contractual calibration/service flow.

Buyer / payer:
- individual drivers using alcohol interlocks after driving-rights restrictions;
- fleets, transport companies, taxi/ride operators, and employers that require alcohol interlock control;
- lawyers/driver schools/occupational channels that refer clients needing installation/service;
- retiring small installer/service owner who wants succession and earnout;
- device manufacturer/importer that wants local service coverage.

Acute trigger:
An interlock user needs installation, periodic calibration/service, documentation, or device swap to keep the vehicle lawful/usable under the relevant restriction or contract. Missed calibration, device failure, expired documentation, or lost installer support can immobilize the vehicle or create legal/employment risk.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is:
- installed device lease/service contracts and serial-number register;
- title to leased devices or clear manufacturer/importer lease rights;
- calibration/service due-date calendar and certificate/archive history;
- phone/domain/mailbox/Google profile and old-owner handoff;
- trained/authorized installer or manufacturer/importer service authorization, or signed manufacturer/importer service path;
- spare devices, mouthpieces/sensors/accessories, and service tools where legally transferable;
- lawyer/fleet/referrer relationships;
- prepaid renewal/calibration/service fees and deposits;
- no legal advice and no alcohol-treatment claims.

The hard control is: installed serial-number book + device title/lease rights + calibration due dates + authorized service path + prepaid renewals.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option over one small installer/service book;
- 20,000-50,000 PLN device inventory, spare units, service tools, or lease deposits, only after due-date and payment proof;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN legal/road-traffic/device/liability/manufacturer-contract review;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN installer training/handoff, phone/mailbox/CRM, certificate archive, and booking workflow;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN courier/mobile service, accessories, and insurance reserve.

Required proof:
1. Signed option/handoff over one installer/service book with at least 40 installed or recently serviced alcohol-interlock customers/devices.
2. Verified serial-number register, device title/lease status, calibration due calendar, and last 12 months of paid renewals/services.
3. Written manufacturer/importer/authorized-service path or trained installer transition agreement.
4. 15 prepaid renewal/calibration/service appointments or deposits before final close.
5. 5 new installations, lease renewals, or device swaps paid in 60 days.
6. 25,000+ PLN collected/prepaid across renewals, deposits, installs, or fleet service contracts.
7. Gross margin above 35% after device cost/depreciation, service labor, certification/admin, travel, accessories, seller earnout, warranty, and liability reserve.
8. No legal advice, no court-outcome claims, no addiction-treatment claims, no misrepresentation of device/legal status.

Economics:
Revenue comes from installation fees, leased-device monthly/quarterly fees, calibration/service renewal fees, replacement sensor/mouthpiece/accessory margin, emergency swap/repair fees, and fleet retainers for scheduled calibration and certificate archive.

Payback logic:
The founder does not buy a generic device catalogue. The founder options an installed base with known due dates and prepaid renewals, proves current-month service/payment flow, then closes with earnout. Recurring renewal/calendar control can pay back faster than speculative spare inventory because the customer's need is time-bound and documented.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
This is an installed-device and renewal-book acquisition. It owns or controls serial-numbered devices, lease/service contracts, due dates, installer handoff, and payment flow. It is not a dashboard, legal advice service, addiction treatment service, or marketplace.

Copy risk:
Manufacturers, importers, and other installers can compete. The wedge is the specific installed book, due-date calendar, device title/lease rights, phone trust, certificate history, and prepaid renewals. If only leads transfer, the score falls below 80.

Regulatory and operating guardrails:
- Legal counsel must verify current Polish alcohol-interlock eligibility, installation, documentation, calibration, and service requirements before closing.
- Startup gives no legal advice, no court-outcome claims, and no alcohol-treatment claims.
- Manufacturer/importer authorization and trained installer continuity must be confirmed before serving customers.
- Device title/lease rights, warranty, calibration tools, service records, and certificate/archive history must be auditable.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Need legal confirmation of current Polish alcohol-interlock rules, installer authorization, and documentation requirements.
- Manufacturer/importer may control service authorization and bypass the founder.
- The installed base may be too small or lumpy.
- Device ownership/lease title may be unclear.
- Calibration/service obligations may require specific certified tools or personnel.
- Customers may switch installers if not locked by device/service structure.
