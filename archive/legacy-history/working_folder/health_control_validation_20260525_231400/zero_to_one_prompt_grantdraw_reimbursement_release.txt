Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
GrantDraw Reimbursement Release Mandate.

One-sentence thesis:
Polish SMEs, NGOs, associations, public-project beneficiaries, and grant consultants will pay for a narrow payment-release mandate when grant money has already been awarded, costs have already been incurred, and reimbursement is blocked by a specific payment-request file rather than by a new grant application or generic advisory need.

Exact buyer:
The buyer is a Polish or CEE beneficiary of an already-awarded public, EU, regional, innovation, energy, export, training, agricultural, fisheries, environmental, or SME grant, plus grant consultancies and accountants that manage beneficiary settlement work. The first buyer must have a signed grant agreement, incurred or paid costs, an upcoming or rejected payment request, authority correspondence, and enough expected reimbursement to justify a 5,000-20,000 PLN fixed fee.

Acute trigger:
The beneficiary has incurred grant-eligible costs or completed a stage and needs cash reimbursement, an intermediate payment, advance settlement, final balance, or payment-request correction. The trigger is a written authority query, rejection, correction list, missing attachment request, payment-request deadline, cash-flow pressure after paying suppliers, delayed final balance, or grant consultant overflow on a payment file. The startup rejects grant applications, scoring appeals, speculative eligibility work, and broad readiness.

Control point:
The startup controls the named payment-release file: signed beneficiary mandate, grant agreement, reimbursement schedule, cost ledger, invoices, payment confirmations, supplier contracts, procurement trail where relevant, deliverable evidence, asset/serial/photo evidence, payroll or participant evidence only if safely in scope, authority correspondence, missing-attachment register, payment-request portal task list, submission checklist, beneficiary approval log, accountant/grant-consultant reviewer notes, and accepted/no-go split.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign at least five paid mandates over named payment-request files, each with a signed grant agreement, incurred cost ledger, expected reimbursement above 50,000 PLN, authority correspondence or deadline, and a prepaid fixed fee of at least 5,000 PLN. At least three cases must obtain authority acknowledgement, correction-list closure, portal submission acceptance, formal no-go, or payment-request status progress. At least one case must produce an accepted payment request, approved reimbursement instruction, disbursement, or first success-linked fee.

6-month POC:
Close 20-40 payment-release mandates with 200,000-600,000 PLN revenue, at least 60% gross margin after grant/accounting/procurement reviewer costs, and at least 12 measurable outcomes: accepted payment request, correction list closed, reimbursement approved, disbursement received, advance settled, final balance released, or a formal no-go that prevents wasted filings. At least five cases must come through grant consultants, accountants, equipment vendors, or beneficiary referrals, and at least three channels must send a second file.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with grant consultants, accountants, equipment vendors, training providers, energy contractors, and SME beneficiaries who already have awarded projects and payment-request pressure. The channel pitch is: “You keep the grant application, accounting, and long advisory relationship; I take the ugly payment-request evidence assembly and correction loop only when money is already awarded and costs are incurred.” Direct outreach targets beneficiaries visible in public award lists, contractor case studies, grant program participant lists, and equipment/project announcements, but only with the post-award payment-release offer.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-8,000 PLN for payment-file triage and correction-list mapping, 10,000-20,000 PLN for a full payment-request release sprint, and 2-6% success-linked fee only where allowed and only on incremental disbursed funds or released final balance. The startup should prefer fixed fees because public-fund success fees can be sensitive. The buyer pays because reimbursement delays create real working-capital pressure after suppliers, payroll, contractors, or equipment vendors have already been paid.

Copy risk:
Grant consultants, accountants, auditors, project managers, procurement advisers, equipment vendors, and beneficiary finance teams can copy parts of the process. The defense is a narrow operating dataset of payment-request correction patterns: required attachments, authority wording, common invoice/payment-proof defects, asset evidence, procurement-support boundaries, deliverable proof formats, no-go triggers, and channel trust from consultants that do not want low-margin correction-loop work.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the beneficiary signs the mandate, pays the fixed fee, and grants access to the named payment-request file, authority correspondence, cost ledger, invoices, proofs, attachments, portal task list, and reviewer route, a competitor cannot control that exact correction loop or payment-request submission before the current deadline unless the beneficiary terminates the mandate. Incumbents can compete for future grant work, but not the specific payment-release file already under paid control.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell grant writing, funding search, application scoring, lobbying, legal appeals, a dashboard, a consultant marketplace, accounting software, or generic EU funds advice. It accepts only post-award payment-release files with incurred costs and produces a specific operational artifact: payment-request evidence index, correction-list closure map, missing-attachment register, portal-ready packet, beneficiary approval log, reviewer boundary notes, and accepted/no-go split tied to a formal payment-request workflow.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No grant application writing, no legal advice, no tax advice, no procurement-law opinion, no audit opinion, no false eligibility claims, no document backdating, no fabricated deliverables, no authority lobbying, no public-funds representation beyond authorized beneficiary submissions, no payroll processing, and no promise of reimbursement. Accountants, auditors, grant lawyers, procurement advisers, tax advisers, project managers, and the beneficiary remain responsible for official accounting, eligibility judgments, legal interpretation, procurement compliance, truthful submissions, portal authorization, and final authority communication.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from TraceFaktura because it is not a live municipal event-evidence rail and does not create source events before invoicing; it is a post-award reimbursement-release mandate for grant payment requests. It is distinct from PromoLeak because it does not recover retail deductions, rebates, promotions, or commercial leakage. It is distinct from CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, GreenTender EPREL, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack, and health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Grant consultants, accountants, and project managers may already own this workflow. Many delayed payment requests are delayed because of genuine eligibility, procurement, VAT, deliverable, state-aid, conflict-of-interest, or audit problems that cannot be fixed by packaging. Public authorities control acceptance and payment timing. Program rules vary, so a generic desk can become low-margin procedural archaeology. Beneficiaries may resist sharing sensitive financial, supplier, payroll, and project data with an unknown founder. Success fees on public money may be inappropriate or restricted. The business only works if the first cases are post-award, incurred-cost, current-payment files with clear authority correspondence and enough reimbursement value to support a fixed fee.
