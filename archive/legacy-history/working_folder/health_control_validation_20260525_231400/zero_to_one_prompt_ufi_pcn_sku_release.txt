Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
UFI/PCN SKU Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Release blocked EU hazardous-mixture SKUs by assembling the responsible entity's formula/SDS/label file, routing qualified CLP review, generating or validating UFI/PCN submission evidence, and delivering buyer-facing proof only for live order, listing, or private-label blockers.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE importers, private-label sellers, distributors, ecommerce brands, cleaning-product sellers, automotive-chemical importers, hobby/resin/adhesive sellers, fragrance/candle brands, refill-product brands, and industrial-mixture suppliers with hazardous mixtures sold into EU retail, marketplace, distributor, Horeca, or private-label channels.

Acute trigger:
A retailer, distributor, marketplace, private-label customer, 3PL, customs-adjacent broker, or chain buyer blocks a named SKU because the UFI is missing from the label/SDS, the Poison Centre Notification proof is missing or inconsistent, the label UFI does not match formula/SDS records, the responsible legal entity is unclear, or a formula/label change requires updated evidence before shipment/listing.

Control point:
Signed SKU release mandate; named SKU/EAN/order/listing; buyer blocker text; responsible legal entity confirmation; supplier/formulator formula or mixture-in-mixture evidence; current SDS and label artwork; qualified CLP/SDS reviewer route; UFI generation or validation trail; PCN submission or update evidence where the responsible entity submits; label/SDS alignment pack; buyer-facing release packet; accepted listing/order release, shipment approval, or written no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Six prepaid SKU release cases; four complete formula/SDS/label/reviewer files accepted for processing; four UFI/PCN submission/update evidence packets prepared with the responsible entity; two retailer, distributor, marketplace, 3PL, or private-label releases, or written acceptance/no-go outcomes; 40,000-120,000 PLN collected; at least 40% of inbound cases rejected because source formula, responsible-entity authority, qualified review, or legal classification boundary is missing.

6-month POC:
40-90 paid SKU release cases; 25-60 complete UFI/PCN/label/SDS files processed; 15-35 buyer/listing/order releases or clean no-go outcomes; 250,000-800,000 PLN startup revenue; three repeat channels such as packaging agencies, SDS/CLP consultants, marketplace agencies, private-label buyers, or import accountants; gross margin above 60% after qualified CLP/SDS reviewer costs, secure formula handling, translations, and channel commissions.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target packaging-label agencies, SDS translators, small CLP consultants, marketplace agencies, private-label brokers, import accountants, and distributors that see urgent SKU blockers but do not want to operate the UFI/PCN evidence workflow. Direct outreach only to brands with written buyer/listing blocker text and named SKUs.

Economics:
Charge 6,000-12,000 PLN for a simple evidence-ready SKU, 12,000-25,000 PLN for multi-country or mixture-in-mixture cases, and urgent premiums for order value above 150,000 PLN. The buyer pays because a live SKU, listing, private-label order, or shipment is blocked and the output is accepted evidence or a clean no-go, not a generic compliance memo.

Copy risk:
CLP/SDS consultants, chemical compliance firms, packaging agencies, labs, marketplace agencies, and large distributors can copy. The defense is current SKU mandate control, repeated accepted/rejected blocker memory, fast packaging/label/SDS/PCN coordination, trusted reviewer bench, and channel relationships with agencies that do not want to own the full workflow.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the workflow generally, but not an already-prepaid mandate over a named blocked SKU, the buyer blocker text, the source formula/SDS/label file, the responsible-entity submission trail, the reviewer coordination, and the buyer-facing release packet for that current order/listing deadline. The wedge depends on controlling urgent SKU files through agency and importer channels before the buyer routes the same blocker to a broad compliance firm.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a named SKU release file tied to buyer blocker text and containing formula/SDS/label/UFI/PCN evidence, qualified review trail, responsible-entity submission proof, buyer-facing packet, and order/listing release or no-go. It is not generic chemical compliance consulting, SDS translation, a dashboard, database, marketplace, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not classify mixtures, certify safety, provide legal advice, act as importer/downstream user/responsible entity, submit false information, or place products on the market. The responsible legal entity and qualified CLP/SDS reviewer own classification, PCN submission, label approval, and legal marketability. Reject biocides, pesticides, pharmaceuticals, controlled substances, medical products, explosives, weaponized chemicals, high-risk vape/e-liquid cases, missing formula/source data, suspicious supplier documents, or requests to bypass classification. Reject cases where a lab test, legal opinion, reformulation, supplier disclosure, or responsible-entity change is required before useful evidence can exist.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not REDBlocked, GPSR responsible person, MoCRA cosmetics, EUDAMED, PPWR/PFAS, EPRBlocked, DRS/KaucjaMark, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, or Amazon button-cell release. The distinctive control point is EU hazardous-mixture UFI/PCN/label/SDS evidence for a named blocked SKU, with qualified CLP review and responsible-entity submission proof.

Strongest anticipated objections:
CLP/SDS consultants and chemical compliance firms already do this. Missing formula or mixture-in-mixture source data cannot be invented. Many blockers require real classification, lab testing, legal interpretation, supplier disclosure, reformulation, or responsible-entity decisions outside scope. Retailers and marketplaces may have inconsistent templates and may still reject. Small sellers may not pay enough per SKU. Handling formulas and toxicological details creates confidentiality and liability burden. The workflow may normalize once brands clean their UFI/PCN portfolios. The business can drift into generic chemical compliance unless it accepts only live commercial blockers with source data and qualified review.
