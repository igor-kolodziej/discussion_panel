Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality.

Scored idea:

NeuroSpare is a neurodiagnostic continuity-inventory company for Poland/CEE. It is the EEG/EMG/PSG version of an obsolete PLC/HMI line-down donor bank. It does not perform EEG/EMG/PSG tests, diagnose, interpret results, prescribe, handle patients, or sell medical advice. It owns and controls hard-to-source legacy neurodiagnostic hardware and emergency loaner/resale flow for labs whose scheduled tests are blocked by device failure.

Buyer:
- private EEG/EMG/neurophysiology labs;
- sleep labs running PSG/HST workflows;
- neurology clinics and small hospitals;
- biomedical service firms that need exact old parts/loaners;
- research or occupational/clinical sites using older neurodiagnostic systems.

Acute trigger:
A lab loses paid test capacity because a legacy headbox, amplifier, stimulator, PSG input module, photic stimulator, cable loom, isolation/power component, workstation, software dongle, camera/sync accessory, or proprietary connector fails. OEM/service lead times may be slow, official parts may be expensive or discontinued, and buying a new system may be overkill when scheduled paid tests are blocked by one exact component.

Control point:
NeuroSpare signs first-look or consignment rights with closing, retiring, upgrading, or underutilized EEG/EMG/PSG labs and biomedical-service shops. It then owns titled, tested donor inventory:
- legacy EEG/EMG/PSG headboxes, amplifiers, stimulators, modules, photic stimulators, cameras, power/isolation units, cables, caps, workstations, dongles, and accessories;
- named system compatibility records for still-used Natus/Xltek/Dantec/Nicolet, Nihon Kohden, Cadwell, Micromed, Compumedics/Embla, Elmiko/Brainbox, and similar installed-base systems;
- QA/test records, serial-number traceability, CE/documentation file where available, and electrical-safety/biomed check records;
- emergency loaner/resale/rental terms for known labs.

The key control point is not "used equipment resale." It is locked access to exact legacy lots plus compatibility/QA records and emergency loaner/resale flow. A competitor can buy generic used equipment, but cannot instantly copy already-titled exact components, first-look rights over closing/upgrading lab lots, local compatibility maps, and standby buyer agreements.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 40,000-70,000 PLN to buy 2-3 high-confidence donor systems/lots or exact modules from used-equipment sellers, retiring labs, or upgrading clinics;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN for biomedical/electrical-safety checks, documentation, test benches, cleaning, serial records, and QA;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN for legal/MDR distributor compliance memo: CE status, Article 14 distributor obligations, Article 23 parts/components boundaries, traceability, complaint handling, no unauthorized refurbishment/relabeling, rental/resale terms;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN for targeted outbound and standby contracts with labs and biomed firms.

Required POC proof:
1. Two signed first-look or consignment agreements over named closing/upgrading/retiring lab or biomed-service inventory lots.
2. Title or exclusive control over 2-3 donor systems/lots under the 100,000 PLN budget.
3. QA/electrical-safety/biomed check records and serial/compatibility file for each sellable or rentable component.
4. Three standby buyer agreements with private labs, sleep labs, neurophysiology rooms, or biomed service firms naming target systems/SKUs and emergency response terms.
5. One paid emergency deposit, rental, resale, or loaner transaction from a lab whose scheduled work was blocked or at risk because of device/component failure.

Economics:
The business buys donor lots for hundreds to low-thousands PLN per useful component, or tens of thousands PLN for full older systems. Urgent replacements can be sold/rented at a high markup because the buyer is comparing against cancelled paid test slots, slow OEM lead time, a service call, or new-system capex. Revenue types:
- emergency loaner fee;
- rush resale of a tested component;
- refundable deposit plus daily/weekly rental;
- annual standby/priority access retainer for labs with unsupported systems;
- consignment margin on locked closing-lab lots;
- paid compatibility/availability check for biomed firms.

The target first beachhead is not all medical equipment. It is old but still-used neurodiagnostic systems where one exact part can stop revenue. The first inventory must be purchased from a compatibility heat map: installed base, failure-prone parts, software/dongle dependencies, and which Polish/CEE labs still use each generation.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The foundational asset is title or exclusive control over scarce physical inventory plus signed first-look rights over future lots. Service work exists only to test, document, and route the inventory. If another company wants the same emergency part, it cannot buy one SaaS subscription or copy a checklist; it must find and secure equivalent titled obsolete hardware before the line-down buyer needs it.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Demand frequency may be too low; one emergency transaction proves pain but not recurring earnings.
- Wrong inventory kills payback; the founder must buy against a compatibility heat map, not random used listings.
- MDR/distributor compliance matters: traceability, CE status, documentation, complaint handling, no unauthorized refurbishment/relabeling, and clear parts/component boundaries.
- Software licenses, dongles, service passwords, old Windows workstations, drivers, and proprietary accessories may be more important than hardware.
- Authorized distributors and service firms can serve supported systems; NeuroSpare wins only on obsolete exact-SKU availability and emergency speed.
- Some labs may prefer OEM service, replacement capex, or internal redundancy.
- Biomedical testing must be credible; unsafe equipment would create liability.
- The founder must avoid implying clinical endorsement or medical performance beyond tested hardware continuity and compatibility.
