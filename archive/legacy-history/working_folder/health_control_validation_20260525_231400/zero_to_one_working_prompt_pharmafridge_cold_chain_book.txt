Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, and any idea that depends on prescription-drug sale or advertising.

Scored idea:

PharmaFridge Cold-Chain Compliance Book Buyout acquires or options the recurring cold-chain calibration, temperature-monitoring, certificate, and excursion-documentation renewal book of a tiny Polish pharmacy cold-chain service broker, pharmacy-equipment reseller, calibration coordinator, or temperature-monitoring micro-operator.

It does not sell, advertise, prescribe, reserve, or deliver prescription medicines. Licensed pharmacies and clinics remain provider/seller of record. The startup never markets branded prescription products to patients. It sells only B2B cold-chain compliance continuity to pharmacies and licensed clinics that already lawfully store refrigerated medicines.

The acquired asset is not a generic calibration business, dashboard, report, or marketplace. It is the operating control point around pharmacy cold storage: customer list, fridge/device IDs, installed logger/probe list, calibration due-date calendar, certificate archive, min/max reading workflow, temperature-excursion documentation templates, support phone/domain/mailbox, customer-specific storage notes, recurring invoice rights, customer-approved admin handoff, and prepaid renewal cash.

Buyer:
- independent pharmacies;
- small pharmacy groups;
- private clinics storing refrigerated medicines;
- IVF/fertility clinics and biologics clinics;
- clinics/pharmacies storing insulin, vaccines, fertility medicines, GLP-1 pens, and other cold-chain stock.

Acute trigger:
Poland's 2025 pharmacy-rule update creates new or sharpened operational requirements around pharmacy cold storage, calibrated temperature-measurement equipment, min/max readings, and handling/documenting temperature excursions, with key timing cited by the source set as March 3, 2026. Pharmacies and clinics face audit, nonconformance, product-loss, and workflow risk if fridges, loggers, calibration certificates, min/max records, and excursion documentation are not ready.

Control point:
The acquired asset is the pharmacy cold-chain renewal flow:
- customer list and seller-endorsed handoff;
- support phone/domain/mailbox;
- fridge IDs, device IDs, logger/probe serials, and installed customer equipment list;
- calibration due-date calendar and certificate archive;
- min/max reading workflow and excursion-documentation templates;
- customer-specific storage notes and recurring renewal templates;
- recurring invoice rights or prepaid compliance-renewal commitments where transferable;
- partner capacity with accredited calibration/provider partners;
- optional owned starter inventory of calibrated/min-max loggers, probes, labels, SOP packs, replacement kits, and courier-safe packaging.

A competitor can sell a logger or offer calibration, but cannot instantly copy the exact installed device list, certificate history, renewal timing, phone/domain continuity, seller trust, customer-specific notes, and prepaid compliance-renewal flow.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option fee to the seller;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN legal/customer-transfer/no-Rx-advertising review;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN certificate/archive cleanup, CRM import, customer notices, phone/domain/mailbox transfer, and renewal workflow;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN starter inventory or deposits for calibrated/min-max loggers, probes, labels, replacement kits, and documentation packs;
- closing payment only after prepaid customer renewal cash clears, with remaining seller consideration structured as earnout from retained gross margin.

Required POC proof:
1. One signed conditional acquisition option under 100,000 PLN over a named pharmacy cold-chain renewal book, covering customer list, fridge/device IDs, logger/probe serials, due-date calendar, certificate archive, support phone/domain/mailbox, templates, customer-specific notes, recurring invoice rights where transferable, and seller transition support.
2. Clean transfer/no-Rx-advertising review: no sale, reservation, delivery, or public advertising of prescription medicines; licensed pharmacies/clinics remain seller/provider of record.
3. One signed capacity/routing agreement or written cooperation confirmation with an accredited calibration/provider partner.
4. Twenty pharmacy or clinic customers prepay compliance renewal, logger/certificate update, or excursion-documentation package before close.
5. At least 35,000 PLN prepaid renewal/transition cash clears before final close.
6. One calibration/certificate batch is completed through the qualified provider.
7. One temperature-excursion documentation workflow case is closed for a customer without touching medicine sale, prescribing, clinical advice, or patient data.
8. Public-facing wording avoids branded prescription-product advertising; B2B targeting can mention regulated refrigerated medicine storage categories only in a compliance context.

Economics:
The business is acquisition-led, not ad-led. The first customers are inherited through seller handoff. The first cash comes from prepaid compliance renewals before closing. Pharmacies and clinics pay because cold-chain failures can create audit problems, product-loss risk, nonconformance, and urgent admin work.

Initial pricing logic:
- annual cold-chain compliance renewal package per pharmacy/fridge;
- calibration certificate routing fee through qualified providers;
- logger/probe replacement or upgrade bundle;
- temperature-excursion documentation pack and urgent support fee;
- certificate archive / due-date management fee;
- seller earnout only from retained customer gross margin.

Gross-margin logic:
The startup should not become a full calibration laboratory at first. It earns margin from renewal capture, logger/probe kit margin, certificate/archive continuity, reminder/admin flow, documentation support, and batch routing through accredited/qualified providers. Payback comes from prepaid renewals before closing and recurring annual due dates.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The asset is an acquired customer renewal/payment flow: installed device list, fridge IDs, certificate archive, due-date calendar, support phone/domain/mailbox, seller handoff, recurring invoice rights, customer-specific notes, and prepaid renewal commitments. The startup is not selling a generic report or marketplace listing; it owns the renewal moment and routes specific cold-chain compliance work through qualified providers.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- Do not sell, advertise, prescribe, reserve, or deliver prescription medicines.
- Do not imply pharmacy or clinical provider status.
- Licensed pharmacies and clinics remain seller/provider of record for medicines.
- Do not claim PCA accreditation or calibration-lab status unless the operating entity actually has it.
- Certificates must be issued by the qualified provider of record and not altered.
- Store only customer/equipment/admin data needed for cold-chain compliance operations.
- Avoid patient data entirely.

Main risks acknowledged:
- If built from cold outreach instead of acquired book, copy risk rises and score should fall.
- If the regulation timing or required scope is overstated, urgency weakens.
- Pharmacy budgets may be small and price-sensitive.
- Calibration/device providers can bypass the startup.
- If customer prepayment fails, the book is weak.
- If the seller has only contacts but no device IDs/certificate archive/due dates, the acquisition is not a control point.
- If the startup mentions branded prescription medicines in public marketing, legal risk rises.
- Margins can compress if the business becomes low-margin pass-through calibration coordination.
