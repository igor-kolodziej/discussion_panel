Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
HeritageLight Matched Fixture Suites

One-sentence thesis:
A controlled-inventory business that buys or options complete matched antique lighting fixture suites from one property before renovations, hotel remodels, estate clear-outs, or demolition split them into individual pieces, then sells documented full-room, corridor, staircase, lobby, restaurant, or hotel-suite lighting sets to premium heritage renovations with buyer deposits before rewiring or restoration spend.

Exact buyer:
Premium interior architects, boutique hotel owners, restaurateurs, cocktail-bar operators, HNW apartment/villa owners, boutique developers, premium office designers, heritage renovation firms, lighting restorers, and project managers in Poland/CEE and nearby EU markets who want authentic repeated-room lighting continuity rather than one decorative antique lamp. The first buyer wants a coherent suite: matched sconces, pendants, chandeliers, corridor lights, stair lights, lobby fixtures, shades, glass, brass/bronze bodies, backplates, arms, canopies, chains, mounting hardware, and provenance from one property.

Acute trigger:
A pre-war apartment, villa, hotel, restaurant, cinema, theater, office, institution, staircase, or historic commercial interior is being gutted, modernized, inherited, sold, or cleared. A coherent run of original fixtures is about to be split by antique dealers, sold as pairs, stripped for parts, rewired piecemeal, lost during demolition, or discarded as outdated electrical material. On the demand side, a premium renovation wants authentic repeated visual rhythm across many rooms or a hospitality project, but reproduction lighting or mismatched antique singles lack the same provenance, patina, proportions, material consistency, and story.

Control point:
Written first-refusal, option, or purchase agreement over a complete matched lighting suite before removal; clear title path from the owner; site photos/video showing fixtures in place; fixture count and room/corridor/staircase map; numbered removal labels; titled possession of sconces, chandeliers, pendants, shades, globes, backplates, chains, arms, mounts, canopies, screws, finials, and matching parts where recoverable; condition notes for glass cracks, missing shades, plating loss, corrosion, bent arms, prior rewiring, missing sockets, paint, damage, and completeness; de-energized sale status; buyer reservation deposits for full suites rather than single fixtures; and handoff to buyer-selected electrician/restorer for rewiring and code compliance.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three written first-refusal or pickup agreements with renovation crews, hotel clearout managers, estate clearers, demolition firms, antique dealers, building administrators, boutique developers, or lighting restorers; two complete matched lighting suites titled or optioned with clear source, site photos, fixture map, removal labels, condition notes, and completeness records; one qualified lighting restorer or electrician willing to inspect and quote buyer handoffs on paid terms; three buyer deposits of 2,000-10,000 PLN for full suites from architects, hotels, restaurants, HNW project managers, office designers, or restoration firms; one paid handoff or reservation contract; no rewiring, plating, restoration, or speculative international shipping started before buyer deposit.

6-month POC:
Control 8-20 complete matched lighting suites; secure 8-15 buyer deposits; complete 5-10 paid handoffs; collect 150,000-800,000 PLN in raw-suite sales, reservation deposits, and restorer/electrician handoff fees; maintain gross margin above 50% after acquisition, careful removal, transport, cleaning, labeling, condition recording, storage, insurance, missing-part allowance, and breakage allowance; build a private catalog by city, building type, period, fixture count, fixture type, material, shade/glass completeness, finish, condition, room-map, restoration path, buyer type, deposit conversion, and sale outcome; get at least five architects, hotel designers, lighting restorers, or hospitality buyers to ask for future suites before specifying reproduction.

First acquisition mechanism:
Supply side: build a list of 200 renovation crews, estate clearers, hotel-remodel contractors, demolition firms, antique lighting dealers, lighting restorers, building administrators, boutique developers, theater/cinema clearout contacts, and old-property managers in Warsaw, Lodz, Krakow, Poznan, Wroclaw, Gdansk, Sopot, Katowice, and historic towns. Pitch: "Call before a complete run of original sconces, pendants, chandeliers, or corridor lights is split. I pay for full matched suites, document them in place, label removals, and handle pickup."

Demand side: build a list of 150 interior architects, boutique hotel designers, restaurant/cocktail-bar groups, HNW project managers, premium office designers, lighting restorers, and heritage renovation firms. Pitch only controlled suites: "Reserve a complete matched lighting suite from one property, with site photos, fixture map, count, condition notes, and electrician/restorer handoff. Full-suite deposits only; no single-lamp sourcing."

Economics:
Acquire or option complete suites for 2,000-25,000 PLN depending on count, period, material, shade completeness, building provenance, condition, access, and seller urgency. Spend 1,000-8,000 PLN on careful removal, transport, basic cleaning, labeling, condition recording, packing, storage, insurance, and breakage allowance. Sell raw documented suites for 12,000-80,000 PLN and rare hotel/lobby/theater Art Deco, Secession, or brass/bronze suites for 60,000-180,000+ PLN through buyer-selected restorers/electricians. The startup captures spread by preserving the complete repeated suite before antique and demolition markets fragment it into pairs and singles.

Copy risk:
Antique lighting dealers, architectural salvage yards, lighting restorers, interior architects, boutique developers, and demolition-connected resellers can copy pieces. The defense is upstream first-look density, fast cash/pickup reliability, full-suite documentation discipline, in-place provenance photos, numbered removal maps, buyer deposits before restoration, compact controlled inventory, and gatekeeper memory of which suite types sell. Once a suite is optioned, documented, titled, and reserved, competitors can offer reproduction or other antique singles but cannot sell that exact matched property-level suite.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After the startup signs the option or pickup agreement, documents the fixtures in place, maps and labels the suite, controls title or possession, and takes a full-suite buyer deposit, an antique dealer or restorer cannot recreate that exact original property-level lighting run. They can source other sconces, pairs, chandeliers, or reproductions, but not the controlled suite's provenance, count, matching finish, room rhythm, condition map, and reservation contract.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup controls titled or optioned physical fixture suites and sells full, documented lighting sets with deposits. It does not sell interior design, lighting design, electrical work, restoration, installation, software, a salvage marketplace, generic sourcing, a database, or brokered introductions. Cases without full-suite control, title path, site documentation, and deposit discipline are rejected.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify electrical safety, provide electrical installation, rewire fixtures as provider of record, guarantee code compliance, provide conservation-law advice, provide building permissions, authenticate museum-grade antiques, or guarantee suitability for energized use. Fixtures are sold as de-energized/restoration-needed unless a qualified electrician/restorer separately certifies work. Qualified electricians, lighting restorers, architects, conservation officers, owners, and contractors remain responsible for rewiring, grounding, mounting, fire/electrical compliance, approvals, and final installation. Material with unclear title, disputed ownership, protected heritage restrictions, suspected theft, unsafe removal conditions, or incomplete provenance is rejected.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not HeritageDoor Suites because it does not control internal doors, frames, casings, hinges, handles, locks, or door-suite rhythm. The controlled unit is a complete matched antique lighting fixture suite from one property, sold as repeated visual lighting continuity for hospitality, HNW, office, or heritage interiors. It is not HeritageWindow, HeritageKachel, HeritageRadiator, HeritageApothecary, StormTile, hotel FF&E, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, BESSGuard, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Reproduction lighting is a strong substitute and can be safer, warrantied, perfectly sized, and code-compliant. Antique lighting dealers and restorers may already control the best supply. Complete suites may be rare because sellers split valuable pairs, lose shades, damage glass, or remove only decorative pieces. Electrical safety, rewiring, grounding, mounting, and fire/code compliance must be tightly outside scope. Buyer deposits may be hard before inspection by a restorer/electrician. Taste risk is high across period, finish, size, shade style, patina, brightness, and project fit. Missing shades, broken globes, bent arms, prior bad rewiring, corrosion, overpaint, or nonstandard sockets can destroy value. The business can become generic antique lighting dealing unless it only controls complete property-level suites with deposits. The 100,000 PLN budget can still be trapped if several beautiful suites sit unsold.
