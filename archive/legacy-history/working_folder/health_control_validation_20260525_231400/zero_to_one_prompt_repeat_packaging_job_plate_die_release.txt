Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RepeatPack Current-Job Plate/Die Release Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Become the paid release operator for current repeat packaging jobs where brands or suppliers cannot print on time because an old broker, retiring prepress operator, failed printer, plate house, or freelancer controls the exact approved production assets: source files, proofs, color targets, plates, dies, sleeves, barcode/version history, approval emails, and job notes.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE food, beverage, household, beauty, industrial, ecommerce, and private-label suppliers with current repeat packaging print runs at risk; packaging converters and print managers that need customer-authorized asset handoff from a previous broker/printer/prepress source; small CPG brands whose retailer, export, seasonal, or production delivery depends on reusing exact packaging assets rather than rebuilding artwork from scratch. Exclude pharma, medical, supplement, hazardous-chemical, child-safety, and high-regulatory label-content cases in the first wedge.

Acute trigger:
A current repeat packaging run is blocked or at risk because the buyer cannot access the exact print-ready files, fonts, linked images, dielines, barcode/version trail, approved proof, color target, plate, rotary die, sleeve, cutter file, job ticket, substrate notes, or printer approval history controlled by a retiring broker, failed printer, old prepress operator, plate house, or freelancer. The commercial consequence is missed retailer delivery, delayed private-label launch, lost seasonal stock, export shipment delay, line stoppage, customer penalty, or urgent recreation cost.

Control point:
Customer authorization plus custody or authorized transfer of the exact production-memory assets for a named current job: source packages, proofs, color references, barcode/version history, dielines, plates, rotary dies, sleeves, cutter/plate IDs, approval emails, print notes, prior printer job tickets, asset-location records, and handoff permissions. The startup controls the release file and physical/digital asset path before seller payout, and it only accepts cases tied to a current PO, shipment deadline, print booking, or customer delivery event.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three signed seller/source agreements from retiring brokers, prepress operators, old printers, plate houses, or packaging freelancers covering current-job asset release terms; six prepaid customer-authorized live repeat-job release cases; custody or authorized transfer of at least 80 SKUs/files/proofs/plates/dies across those cases; one packaging prepress/color contractor on paid per-case terms; three printer-accepted release packets or physical/digital asset handoffs; two print jobs released, rescheduled successfully, or converted into paid no-go/recreation decisions; 60,000-180,000 PLN collected; gross margin above 55% after seller payouts, contractor review, secure file storage, courier/asset handling, and rejection reserve.

6-month POC:
Handle 30-60 current repeat-job release cases across 12-25 brands, suppliers, converters, or print managers; custody or transfer 400-1,200 SKU/file/plate/die assets; complete 20+ printer-accepted release handoffs, successful repeat print releases, rescheduled runs, or paid no-go decisions; collect 400,000-1,200,000 PLN; keep regulated-label-content review out of scope; show at least five source partners or seller channels each sending a second case; document accepted asset packages by printer type, packaging format, file condition, plate/die state, and reason for no-go.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start on the supply side with retiring packaging brokers, flexo/prepress freelancers, small failed or selling print shops, plate houses, color/proofing operators, and print managers who still control customer assets but do not want ongoing job support. Seller pitch: "I pay only when a customer authorizes a current job release and cash is collected; no speculative archive cleanup." Demand outreach goes through packaging converters, food/beauty/private-label founders, print managers, and operations managers: "If a repeat packaging job is blocked by missing old files, proofs, plates, dies, or approval history, I recover and hand off the exact assets to your chosen printer or produce a paid no-go/recreation file."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-18,000 PLN for a narrow current-job asset release, 18,000-35,000 PLN for multi-SKU urgent releases, 2,000-5,000 PLN for paid no-go/recreation decisions, and optional seller/source payouts only after customer payment or released-job acceptance. Contractor review is scoped per case. The buyer pays because the fee is small relative to missed retailer delivery, an idle packaging line, a lost seasonal window, emergency artwork recreation, color/proof failure, or packaging shortage. No broad archive cleanup is performed without a current paid job.

Copy risk:
Printers, plate houses, prepress shops, packaging agencies, print brokers, color consultants, and in-house packaging teams can copy pieces of the workflow. The defense is transaction-level control over exact assets, seller/source release agreements, customer authorization, secure custody, printer-accepted handoff formats, and repeated case memory by packaging format, printer type, proof condition, file defect, plate/die state, and no-go reason.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the seller/source release agreement, customer authorization, current job trigger, files/proofs/plates/dies in custody or under controlled transfer, and prepaid release fee, a competitor cannot instantly access the same asset set or seller handoff without replacing the controlled transaction. Printers can recreate future artwork, but they cannot use the already-controlled approved proof, plate, die, color target, or production notes unless the customer/source switches.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is a controlled current-job release of exact production assets. The output is a printer-accepted handoff, a released/rescheduled repeat print job, or a paid no-go/recreation decision for named SKUs. It is not a design service, print brokerage, archive database, compliance report, dashboard, generic marketplace, or introduction to printers. No current print job, no customer authorization, and no controlled production assets means no work.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not approve label content, claims, allergens, ingredients, nutrition, recycling marks, safety warnings, food-contact status, product compliance, trademark rights, or regulatory wording. Customer and its advisers remain responsible for label legality and final approval. Printers and prepress contractors remain responsible for technical print preparation and press acceptance. The startup rejects unclear ownership, stolen files, disputed IP, counterfeit products, regulated/safety-sensitive labels, and cases needing legal label review. File custody, personal data, trade secrets, and IP terms are handled under written customer/source authorization.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not HeritageDoor, because this controls current packaging-production assets and print-job release, not pre-war interior doors or physical heritage suites. Not PromoLeak, because it does not recover retailer trade-spend deductions or promo leakage. Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, or TraceFaktura. Compared with prior failed PackPlate/RepeatPack book-buyout variants, this is narrower: it accepts only customer-authorized current repeat jobs with live PO/shipment/print deadlines and exact asset custody, not broad continuity books, print brokerage, or reorder catalog operations.

Strongest anticipated objections:
File ownership may be unclear across brand, printer, agency, broker, designer, freelancer, and plate house; printers and prepress shops are natural incumbents; many assets may be incomplete, obsolete, missing fonts/links, damaged, overprinted, wrong version, or unusable by the new printer; customers may recreate artwork instead of paying; physical plates/dies may not fit the target press; regulated label-content mistakes could create liability if boundaries slip; seller/source supply may be episodic; one successful release may teach customers and printers to internalize the process; the business can drift into custom design, print management, label compliance, or archive cleanup if case intake is not strict.
