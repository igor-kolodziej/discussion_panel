Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include strongest objections/caveats.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist; unknown solo founder with contractors; max 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC; part-time start. Cap below 85 if there is no concrete control point securable within 2 months. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Penalize generic services, dashboards, marketplaces, and businesses without asset/payment-flow control.

Idea: Obsolete PLC/HMI Line-Down Donor Bank.

The startup buys, tests, photographs, serializes, and holds titled inventory of discontinued/long-lead PLCs, HMIs, I/O modules, drives, encoder cards, power supplies, communication modules, operator panels, and control-system spares used in Polish factories. It signs first-look/consignment rights with retiring automation technicians, machine rebuilders, plant decommissioners, bankrupt machine shops, and industrial electricians.

Buyer: maintenance managers at Polish factories using legacy Siemens/Omron/Lenze/ABB/Schneider/Mitsubishi systems; automation integrators; service technicians; machine rebuilders; SMEs whose production line is down and cannot wait for retrofit/OEM lead time.

Acute trigger: a line is down because an obsolete PLC/HMI/drive/module failed. OEM replacement is discontinued or slow, retrofit takes weeks, and production losses are high. The buyer pays a premium for a known-good exact module with serial/test record, fast courier, return terms, and qualified installation by their own technician/integrator.

Control point: specific titled tested modules, serial/photos/firmware notes, compatibility notes, first-look supply rights to Polish decommissioning lots, emergency phone/mailbox workflow, and paid reservation/deposit agreements from factories/integrators. The startup does not perform safety-critical engineering; qualified contractors handle installation/controls work.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN:
1. signed first-look/consignment or option agreements with at least 2 supply sources;
2. titled inspected inventory lot under 65,000 PLN, 50+ modules/SKUs, no stolen/questionable origin;
3. test records/photos/serial logs for at least 80% of inventory;
4. 20 buyer interviews naming exact top SKUs and installed systems;
5. 5 factories/integrators sign paid reservations, deposits, or emergency-supply agreements before final close;
6. 30,000+ PLN in paid deposits, preorders, rentals, or sales within 60 days;
7. gross margin above 45% after acquisition cost allocation, testing, dead-stock reserve, warranty/returns, courier, and seller earnout;
8. limited warranty/return terms and no safety-engineering claims.

Economics: emergency sale, short-term rental/loaner with deposit, reserved stock retainers, consignment margin, and paid sourcing only for specific buyer requests. One or two emergency transactions can pay for a meaningful part of the first lot if SKUs are scarce and verified.

Why not a marketplace/service: it owns or controls physical parts, serial/test records, first-look supply rights, and emergency orders.

Copy risk: Radwell/PLC Center/used-automation dealers and integrators exist. The wedge is local title to exact modules, first-look Polish lots, fast test/ship workflow, and paid reservations before inventory purchase. If the founder only lists parts online, score should fall below 80.

Main risks acknowledged: wrong inventory can strand capital; used-part incumbents exist; firmware/revision mismatch can kill transactions; testing/returns must be rigorous; line-down demand is lumpy; founder needs automation technician contractors; clear title and no-stolen-property diligence are mandatory.
