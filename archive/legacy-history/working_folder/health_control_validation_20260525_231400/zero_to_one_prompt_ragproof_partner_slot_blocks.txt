Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RAGProof Partner Slot Blocks

One-sentence thesis:
Sell prepaid white-label RAG and agent security evidence slot blocks to SOC2, fractional-CISO, and appsec partners whose B2B SaaS clients have live enterprise buyer blockers around prompt injection, cross-source leakage, unsafe tool calls, logging, and human-review proof.

Exact buyer:
SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, appsec boutiques, security-questionnaire firms, and AI implementation agencies that already serve 20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors with RAG assistants, enterprise search, customer-support copilots, document-processing RAG, internal knowledge-base assistants, workflow agents, or limited tool-using AI features. The partner, not the unknown founder, owns the client relationship and sells the module as its AI/RAG security evidence overflow lane.

Acute trigger:
The partner's SaaS client has a live enterprise buyer, procurement, security, or renewal request asking for evidence around prompt injection, RAG source isolation, cross-tenant/source leakage, tool/action permissions, sensitive document exposure, model-provider retention, logging, incident handling, or human review. The partner can answer policies and general security questions but lacks a repeatable 5-7 business day module that creates bounded test traces and buyer-safe evidence for the AI feature.

Control point:
Prepaid partner slot block covering three to six RAG/agent evidence modules; partner master terms; client authorization path; live buyer request; product scope; test/sandbox environment; synthetic tenant/source setup if needed; RAG corpus/sample documents; tool/action permission map; model/provider settings; log access; human-review workflow evidence; agreed test limits; no-go and remediation routing rules; partner-branded packet format; and pass/rework/no-go ledger. The startup controls prepaid delivery capacity and the active trace/evidence artifact for specific buyer blockers, while the partner controls the client relationship and any formal security/legal position.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Two partners prepay slot blocks of at least three modules each at 45,000-90,000 PLN per partner; at least six live buyer blockers are allocated to the paid slots; each accepted slot has client authorization, a named buyer request, non-high-risk AI scope, bounded sandbox/test access, and partner signoff on scope limits; at least four modules produce partner-accepted test traces, buyer evidence packets, rework decisions, lower-risk deployment scope, or clean no-go; at least one partner buys a second block or extends the first block; and gross margin stays above 60% after AI security reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Sell 10-20 partner slot blocks across 5-8 partners; deliver 40-80 modules; collect 700,000-2,000,000 PLN in partner-paid slot and urgent module fees; document 25+ buyer-progress outcomes, 15+ remediation/no-go decisions, and 8+ accepted lower-risk deployment scopes; get at least three partners to buy multiple blocks; keep delivery under seven business days for evidence-ready products; maintain a private taxonomy of prompt-injection patterns, source-isolation checks, cross-tenant leakage tests, tool-permission failures, logging gaps, human-review defects, buyer follow-up patterns, accepted packet formats, and no-go triggers.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target SOC2 firms, fractional CISOs, appsec boutiques, security-questionnaire firms, and AI implementation agencies that already get AI-security questions from SaaS clients but do not have a repeatable RAG/agent test module. Pitch: "Buy a three-case AI/RAG buyer-evidence block. When your SaaS client receives a live buyer request for prompt-injection, source-isolation, data-leakage, tool-permission, logging, or human-review proof, I run the bounded tests under your brand, produce traces and buyer-safe evidence, and route remediation/no-go back to you. You keep the client, security position, and remediation work."

Economics:
Charge partners 45,000-90,000 PLN for a three-module block, 80,000-150,000 PLN for a six-module block, and 20,000-45,000 PLN for urgent single modules after the first block. AI security reviewer cost is 5,000-15,000 PLN per module depending on scope. The partner pays because a live buyer blocker protects a 100,000+ PLN deal/renewal for its client, and the partner can mark up the module while keeping remediation, governance, and advisory work.

Copy risk:
AI security vendors, appsec firms, penetration testers, red-team providers, GRC platforms, AI governance firms, and internal security teams can copy pieces. The defense is prepaid partner capacity, buyer-blocker specialization, fast bounded RAG/agent test recipes, accepted/rejected buyer evidence memory, partner-branded workflow, no-go discipline, and a growing trace library focused on procurement-relevant AI security failures. The startup does not need to win end-client trust cold because partners already own that trust.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a partner prepays a slot block and allocates live buyer blockers into the block, the startup controls the paid delivery capacity, test traces, packet format, no-go/rework ledger, and module delivery schedule for those exact procurement deadlines. A competing appsec firm or AI security vendor can pitch future work, but cannot collect the already prepaid partner slot cash or replace the active module without the partner breaching or moving the block.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup sells prepaid delivery capacity for a narrow technical artifact: bounded RAG/agent security tests with trace evidence, source-isolation checks, tool-permission checks, log evidence, human-review evidence, and pass/rework/no-go findings tied to a live buyer blocker. It does not sell generic AI consulting, broad red-team work, remediation, formal penetration-test certification, legal compliance, a dashboard, a database, a marketplace, a questionnaire-answering service, or brokered expert introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification opinions, DPIAs, high-risk AI conformity assessment, SOC2 certification, ISO 42001 implementation, formal penetration-test certification, model validation, production incident response, product remediation, or guarantees of buyer approval. Partners, vendors, lawyers, DPOs, security officers, AI governance specialists, auditors, and enterprise buyers remain responsible for legal compliance, privacy, security, procurement decisions, customer statements, and remediation. Decline regulated high-risk AI, credit/insurance/HR decisioning, clinical workflows, biometrics, law enforcement, autonomous adverse decisions, live production sensitive data without proper authorization, destructive tool actions, external-email/payment/tool calls without sandboxing, and buyer demands for formal certification.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle ICT third-party-risk, Article 28/30 DORA evidence, exit plans, subcontracting, audit rights, or regulated-finance operational resilience. It is not AIGate direct, AIQ Partner Ticket Book, AI Model-Risk Renewal Evidence Pack, or VoiceAI Article 50 because the paid artifact is a prepaid partner slot block for bounded RAG/agent test traces, not questionnaire answer packaging, regulated model validation, or deployment transparency. It is not REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, merchant-acquirer residuals, Shopify app buyout, FlexMW, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Appsec partners may not prepay slot blocks before proof. AI security and appsec firms can internalize the module once demand is clear. A 5-7 day module may create false assurance if the scope wording is not narrow. Many buyer blockers require formal penetration testing, remediation, AI governance, privacy/legal work, or customer-specific certification outside scope. Sensitive test access, logs, prompts, and source documents create security obligations. Tool-using agents can be dangerous to test without strict sandboxing. Reviewer cost can compress margin. GRC and AI security tools may automate common checks. Partner channels may keep the highest-value work in-house after one or two cases.
