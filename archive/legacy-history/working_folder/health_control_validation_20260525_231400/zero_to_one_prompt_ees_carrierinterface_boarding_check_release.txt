Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EES CarrierInterface Boarding-Check Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Small international coach, ferry, and niche air carriers now have to run EU Entry/Exit System Carrier Interface checks before transporting passengers to the Schengen area, and the urgent wedge is a paid live-route onboarding desk that gets named carriers through official web/mobile/API workflows, staff handoff, dry runs, and first departure checks without becoming immigration counsel or travel IT integration.

Exact buyer:
Polish, CEE, Balkan, UK-route, ferry-subcontractor, charter, and niche air/sea/international coach operators that transport third-country nationals to the Schengen area and lack mature travel-technology or compliance teams. The best first buyers run repeated international routes, have dispatch/check-in staff, and face operational risk from missed or inconsistent Carrier Interface checks. Secondary channels are international coach ticketing agencies, tour operators, ferry ground handlers, and coach associations that have multiple small carriers asking what to do.

Acute trigger:
eu-LISA made the Carrier Interface available from 9 January 2026, and from 10 April 2026 air, sea, and international coach operators are required to carry out pre-departure verifications before transporting passengers to the Schengen area under EES. A small carrier with departures, staff turnover, multiple routes, subcontracted trips, or no internal IT owner risks boarding delays, missed procedures, passenger disputes, customer-service failures, and carrier-liability anxiety if the checks are not embedded into dispatch and check-in workflow.

Control point:
Signed carrier onboarding mandate for a named legal operator and named routes; carrier-authorized COBST/Carrier Interface onboarding workflow; staff roster and route schedule; choice of web portal, mobile app, or API path; carrier-owned device/account access; departure-day checklist; escalation/no-go rules; passenger-notice wording approved by the carrier; check-log template; route dry-run record; and first live departure batch with completed pre-departure verification logs. The startup controls the active onboarding case, route workflow, dry-run evidence, staff handoff, and first live operational proof, while the carrier remains the responsible transport operator.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three carriers or carrier-channel partners sign paid onboarding mandates at 8,000-28,000 PLN each; at least two carriers complete official Carrier Interface/COBST onboarding or confirm existing access; five named routes are mapped; 20 dispatch/check-in staff are trained through carrier-approved scripts; three route dry runs are completed; at least two live departure batches produce completed check logs and carrier sign-off; one carrier pays a second route or monthly support fee; no passenger eligibility advice, immigration-law advice, or carrier-liability legal advice is provided by the startup.

6-month POC:
Onboard 15-30 small carriers or routed carrier cases; cover 40-80 named routes; process or supervise 200+ departure batches through carrier-owned workflows; collect 250,000-700,000 PLN in onboarding, route-batch, and monthly support fees; document error categories, route/staff friction, official-interface questions, channel source, staff retraining needs, and repeat route usage; convert at least five carriers into monthly continuity support or second-route fees; keep delivery mostly remote except optional paid departure-day support through a local contractor.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of small international coach operators, ferry subcontractors, charter carriers, cross-border tour operators, and coach-ticketing agencies serving routes into the Schengen area from Poland, CEE, the Balkans, the UK, Ukraine-adjacent routes, and non-Schengen neighbors. Outreach message: "If your dispatch or check-in team now has to use the EES Carrier Interface before Schengen departures, I set up your official workflow, dry-run it on named routes, train staff, and support your first live departures. Fixed fee per operator or route; no immigration advice and no passenger-data resale."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-14,000 PLN for one operator with one access path and up to two routes; 18,000-28,000 PLN for multi-route carriers, ferry subcontractors, or channel-routed cases with staff training and departure-day support; 2,000-6,000 PLN per additional route or staff refresh; 1,500-5,000 PLN per month for post-launch support, log review, route changes, and staff turnover. Gross margin can exceed 60% if the founder uses official materials, standardized checklists, remote training, and route dry-run templates, with contractors used only for departure-day support or multilingual staff training.

Copy risk:
Official support, Frontex/eu-LISA guidance, travel IT vendors, airline systems, immigration consultants, ticketing agencies, transport associations, and internal carrier operations teams can copy parts. The defense is narrow: active small-carrier route cases, local-language staff workflow, channel relationships with coach/tour/ferry operators, accepted check-log formats, error memory from repeated departures, and speed during the live 2026 operational transition.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a carrier signs a paid mandate, authorizes the onboarding workflow, gives route schedules, assigns staff, completes dry runs, and runs the first live departure checks through the startup's handoff process, another consultant or IT vendor cannot instantly control that specific carrier's staff adoption, route checklist, dry-run record, check-log format, and departure support window. Large travel IT vendors can serve enterprise integrations, but the startup controls the immediate small-carrier operational case before a broader system project is justified.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only named carrier onboarding cases tied to live routes and first departure checks. The proof is official-access workflow, trained staff, dry-run records, live departure check logs, and carrier sign-off. It is not a generic compliance report, training course, software dashboard, travel marketplace, passenger helpdesk, carrier-fine appeal service, or brokered introduction to a travel IT vendor.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide immigration advice, visa advice, legal advice, carrier-liability advice, passenger eligibility decisions, denied-boarding claim handling, government representation, API software development beyond scoped configuration support, or guarantees that a passenger may travel. Carriers remain transport operator, data controller where applicable, staff employer, and decision-maker. The startup does not retain unnecessary passenger data, does not access travel history, uses carrier-owned accounts/devices, and escalates legal, data-protection, passenger-dispute, and government-interface questions to the carrier's counsel, DPO, official support channels, or travel-technology provider.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, or any Amazon/platform evidence desk. The buyer is a transport carrier, the trigger is mandatory EES Carrier Interface pre-departure checks, the control point is carrier-authorized route onboarding and live departure check workflow, and the proof artifact is completed route checks and staff handoff rather than a compliance memo, import-risk packet, evidence response dossier, recovered cash file, or controlled physical asset.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Carriers can use official eu-LISA/Frontex guidance and support directly. Large airlines, ferry lines, and mature coach operators may already have travel IT vendors or compliance teams. The opportunity may be a transition spike rather than a durable company. Small carriers may resist paying if they see the web portal or mobile app as self-serve. Ticket sizes may be too low unless the buyer has repeated routes and staff complexity. Mistakes can create passenger disputes, boarding delays, reputational damage, or legal escalation. The startup must avoid collecting unnecessary passenger data or making travel eligibility decisions. Channel partners may internalize the workflow after a few carriers. Some operators may procrastinate until the first failure rather than prepay for onboarding.
