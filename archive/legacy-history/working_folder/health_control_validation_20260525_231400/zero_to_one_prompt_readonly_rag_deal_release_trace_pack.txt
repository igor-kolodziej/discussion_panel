Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ReadOnlyRAG Deal-Release Trace Pack

One-sentence thesis:
B2B SaaS vendors with read-only RAG features lose enterprise deals when buyers ask for proof around prompt injection, data leakage, tenant/source isolation, logging, and human review; the startup sells a fixed-scope trace pack for live buyer blockers, excluding tool-using agents and formal assurance claims.

Exact buyer:
20-300 person B2B SaaS vendors, AI search vendors, knowledge-base vendors, document-processing SaaS vendors, internal-assistant vendors, support-copilot vendors, and workflow software vendors whose read-only RAG features are being reviewed by enterprise buyers, security teams, procurement, SOC2 consultants, appsec partners, privacy advisers, or fractional CISOs.

Acute trigger:
A named enterprise buyer, renewal customer, security team, procurement team, SOC2/customer-trust partner, appsec partner, or privacy reviewer asks for concrete proof that the vendor's read-only RAG feature resists prompt injection, does not leak unrelated sources or tenants, respects connector/source permissions, logs relevant retrieval and answer events, and has human-review or escalation boundaries. The buyer request is blocking procurement, renewal, go-live, rollout, security exception approval, or payment milestone.

Control point:
The startup accepts only prepaid live buyer blockers for read-only RAG systems. Control requires the buyer request or questionnaire section, vendor-approved sandbox or staging access, synthetic or approved test corpus, source/connector permission map, model/provider settings where relevant, logging screenshots or exports, agreed test scope, partner/vendor approval path, trace packet, rework/no-go table, and buyer-safe response wording. It excludes autonomous agents with write actions, payments, emails, destructive API calls, privileged admin actions, code execution, production side effects, regulated high-risk workflows, legal AI Act classification, formal penetration testing, certification, and open-ended remediation.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, close six prepaid trace packs from at least three partners or direct vendors; collect at least 90,000 PLN; deliver at least five read-only RAG trace packets; document at least three buyer-progress, partner-accepted, rework, or clean no-go outcomes; keep delivery under seven business days for evidence-ready cases; attach one senior appsec/AI-security reviewer for flagged outputs; and reject every case involving tool-using agents, write actions, regulated high-risk decisions, formal assurance demands, or missing sandbox/test authority.

6-month POC:
Deliver 30-50 read-only RAG trace packs; collect 450,000-1,200,000 PLN revenue; document 20+ buyer-progress, partner-accepted, rework, or clean no-go outcomes; get five partners to route at least five cases each; keep gross margin above 65%; keep average delivery under seven business days; maintain a reusable accepted/rejected trace memory; and keep fewer than 20% of accepted cases drifting into legal, formal pentest, model-validation, high-risk AI, or remediation work.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with SOC2 consultants, appsec boutiques, fractional CISOs, privacy consultants, and customer-trust advisers serving AI-enabled SaaS vendors. The partner offer is: "Send only live buyer RAG security blockers. We produce a read-only trace packet in 5-7 business days under your approval path. No tool-agent tests, no legal AI classification, no formal pentest claims, no remediation sprawl." Direct outreach targets vendors that publicly ship RAG/search/assistant features and sell to regulated or security-conscious enterprise buyers.

Economics:
Charge 12,000-30,000 PLN per fixed-scope pack depending on deadline, source count, tenant/source-permission complexity, and reviewer load. Partner margin can be 20-30% where partner owns the client. Costs are founder test orchestration, trace formatting, secure evidence room, one appsec/AI-security reviewer on flagged outputs, and optional contractor help for repeatable test harness work. The product dies if every case needs custom engineering or senior reviewer hours above 40% of fee.

Copy risk:
Appsec firms, AI-security vendors, pentesters, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, GRC platforms, customer-trust platforms, and AI governance consultants can copy pieces. The narrow advantage is speed and case memory for read-only RAG buyer blockers: accepted/rejected trace patterns, source-isolation failure modes, connector-permission evidence, logging examples, buyer-safe wording, no-go/rework taxonomy, and partner trust from repeated live cases.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup controls only the prepaid live case after the vendor or partner supplies the exact buyer request, sandbox/test authority, approved test corpus, source-permission map, scope, approval path, and fee terms. An appsec firm can copy future work, but it cannot instantly own the current evidence room, trace packet, rework/no-go record, partner approval path, and buyer-deadline response once the prepaid case is active.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell generic AI readiness, questionnaire outsourcing, dashboards, marketplaces, broad appsec consulting, legal AI governance, pentesting, or remediation. It accepts only a named read-only RAG buyer blocker and produces concrete test traces, source-isolation findings, logging/human-review evidence, a buyer-safe response packet, and rework/no-go boundary.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify security, provide formal penetration testing, guarantee buyer acceptance, make legal AI Act/DPIA/high-risk determinations, handle production secrets without approved workflow, perform destructive testing, test write-action agents, approve model safety, or do remediation inside the fixed pack. Vendors and their partners remain responsible for final statements, product fixes, legal review, security architecture, production access decisions, customer communications, and buyer acceptance.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, CO2TollClass, or payment-release/recovery mandates. It is closest to earlier AI/RAG evidence branches, but materially narrower than the prior 84-scoring RAGProbe: only read-only RAG, no tool-using agents, no production side effects, no formal assurance, no legal classification, and no broad AI questionnaire labor.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Appsec firms and AI-security vendors can internalize the work. Buyers may demand formal penetration testing, SOC2/ISO evidence, DPIA, AI Act classification, or remediation rather than a trace packet. Vendors may lack real controls or logs. Sensitive corpora, prompts, connector maps, and logs create trust friction. A short sprint can create false assurance. Partner margins can compress economics. The first cases may depend on warm partner channels. The product is weaker if it becomes broad AI security consulting or generic questionnaire support.
