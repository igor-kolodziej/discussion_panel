Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DroneEW Claim-Risk Diligence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
An investor/allocator-paid live-deal diligence memo for EU/CEE defense-tech investors, accelerators, grant programs, venture studios, and strategic buyers evaluating drone, counter-drone, autonomy, electronic-warfare-resilience, GNSS-denied navigation, battlefield-validation, and low-cost production claims before funding, selecting, procuring, or acquiring.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE defense-tech investors, dual-use funds, venture studios, accelerators, grant programs, corporate venture arms, family offices, and strategic buyers evaluating drone, counter-drone, autonomy, electronic-warfare-resilience, GNSS-denied navigation, battlefield validation, and low-cost production claims.

Best first buyers see repeated drone/dual-use dealflow, lack a standardized claim-risk screen, and have live decisions where overclaimed technical or production evidence can waste capital, grant money, procurement time, or strategic attention.

Acute trigger:
A live investment, grant, acceleration, procurement, or acquisition decision involves a startup claiming one or more of:
- EW/jamming resilience;
- GNSS-denied navigation;
- autonomy beyond demos;
- swarm coordination;
- battlefield-proven validation;
- high sortie survival;
- low-cost/high-volume production;
- component/supply-chain resilience;
- anti-drone detection/interception performance;
- edge-AI payload capability.

The buyer needs to decide whether to proceed, reject, require conditions, change valuation, ask for a technical test, or request a named evidence item before committing capital or program resources.

Control point:
Investor/allocator-paid live-deal memo with:
- current deal/grant/procurement candidate;
- buyer authorization and NDA/data-room access where available;
- founder deck, demo footage, test notes, BOM/supplier list if shared, production plan, flight logs/telemetry snippets if shared, customer/pilot evidence, and public claims;
- UAV/autonomy/EW reviewer on paid per-case terms for flagged sections;
- fixed output: claim-risk matrix, evidence-missing list, test-request list, production-scale risk, component/supply-chain risk, export/legal escalation flags, and decision use.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid live-deal memos at 6,000-20,000 PLN each.
2. At least two buyer types represented: fund, accelerator, grant program, corporate venture, strategic buyer, or venture studio.
3. One named UAV/autonomy/EW reviewer on paid per-case terms.
4. At least one memo used in a real decision workflow: reject, proceed with condition, request test, change valuation, delay, technical milestone, grant condition, or procurement next-step.
5. Private taxonomy across 15+ claims: EW resilience, GNSS denial, autonomy, production scale, BOM risk, battlefield proof, operator burden, maintenance burden, and evidence missing.

6-month POC:
Month 1: recruit one UAV/autonomy/EW reviewer; build two public sample claim-risk teardowns using only public drone/counter-drone claims; create an evidence request checklist for investors; build a target list of 60 EU/CEE defense-tech funds, dual-use investors, accelerators, grant programs, venture studios, corporate venture teams, and strategic buyers.

Month 2: close three prepaid live-deal memos, each tied to a real investment, grant, accelerator, procurement, or strategic decision. Deliver claim-risk matrix, missing-evidence list, test-request list, production-scale risk, component/supply-chain risk, export/legal escalation flags, and decision-use recommendation.

Month 3: document whether at least one memo changes a real decision: no-go, valuation change, conditional proceed, grant milestone, test request, delayed decision, procurement next step, or deeper expert review.

Months 4-6: complete 10-20 paid memos, collect 100,000-300,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer cost, sign one retainer or repeated screening arrangement, and build a private claim-risk taxonomy across EW, GNSS, autonomy, production, component, battlefield-proof, operator-burden, and maintenance claims.

First acquisition mechanism:
Buyer pitch:
"If a drone, counter-drone, autonomy, or EW-resilience startup is in your current IC, grant, accelerator, procurement, or acquisition pipeline, send the deck and the claims you are underwriting. I return a decision memo that separates plausible, unsupported, overclaimed, missing-evidence, test-needed, production-risk, supply-chain-risk, and legal/export-escalation claims. It is not legal advice, military certification, or classified assessment."

Partner/reviewer pitch:
"You keep the high-value technical review, testing, operational advice, and legal/export-control work. I package live-deal claim-risk memos for investors and grant programs that need a fast first screen before deeper diligence."

Economics:
Charge 6,000-10,000 PLN for a fast public/deck/data-room claim screen, 12,000-20,000 PLN for a live-deal memo with reviewer pass and evidence-request list, and 25,000-50,000 PLN/month for repeated screening during an accelerator, grant, or fund pipeline. Reviewer cost is 1,500-6,000 PLN per case depending on sensitivity and depth. The buyer pays because one bad drone/EW claim can waste an investment memo, grant slot, procurement process, or months of strategic attention.

Copy risk:
Defense advisers, ex-military operators, UAV engineers, EW specialists, technical diligence firms, and grant reviewers can copy pieces. The defense is a repeatable investor-facing claim taxonomy, CEE drone-market focus, fast deal memo format, reviewer bench, and private memory of which claims consistently lack evidence.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once an investor, accelerator, grant program, venture studio, or strategic buyer assigns a live deal, pays for the memo, and routes the deck/data-room/public claims through the startup workflow under a decision deadline, another adviser must displace an active diligence process. The controlled unit is the live decision case, buyer context, claim set, evidence files, reviewer notes, and decision outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live deal/grant/procurement/acquisition candidates with a paying allocator and a decision deadline. The output is an IC/procurement/grant decision artifact: claim-risk matrix, missing-evidence list, test-request list, production-risk flags, and decision-use recommendation. It is not a drone consultancy, market report, expert-call broker, database, dashboard, marketplace, or generic technical due diligence shop.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, export-control advice, military certification, battlefield validation, weapons-performance certification, procurement guarantee, classified assessment, investment advice, or formal safety approval. It does not request classified data or controlled technical data. Legal/export-control, classified, weapons-safety, military testing, procurement, and operational validation questions are escalated to qualified advisers. The buyer remains responsible for investment, grant, procurement, acquisition, and legal decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack. BioSignal evaluates healthtech wearable/EEG/digital-biomarker claims for healthtech investors. This evaluates defense/dual-use drone and EW-resilience claims for defense-tech investors, accelerators, grant programs, and strategic buyers.

It is not DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Defense investors and grant programs may already use trusted ex-military, UAV, EW, or technical advisers.
- Unknown founder credibility is weak without a named UAV/autonomy/EW reviewer.
- Sensitive data, export-control issues, weapons claims, and classified-adjacent material create trust and legal boundaries.
- Some claims cannot be assessed without real field tests, classified context, operational trials, or access to customer data.
- Dealflow may be relationship-driven and hard to access through cold outreach.
- The memo may become generic expert diligence unless it uses a repeatable claim taxonomy.
- Buyer willingness to pay is uncertain if investors expect informal expert calls for free.
- It may be too close structurally to BioSignal unless the defense/drone claim taxonomy and buyer channels repeat.
