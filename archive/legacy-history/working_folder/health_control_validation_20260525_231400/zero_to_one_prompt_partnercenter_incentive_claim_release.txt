Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
PartnerCenter Incentive Claim Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A cash-recovery and claim-release desk for Polish/CEE Microsoft cloud partners whose already-earned or already-allocated Partner Center incentive, co-op/MDF, marketplace, rebate, CPOR/PAL, or private-offer payout money is blocked, rejected, miscounted, near expiry, or stuck in support-case limbo.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized Polish/CEE Microsoft CSPs, systems integrators, MSPs, ISVs, Azure/Security/Modern Work partners, marketplace sellers, and channel resellers with active Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program incentives, co-op/MDF allocations, marketplace rewards, marketplace private-offer payouts, CSP rebate lines, customer-association incentives, or vendor-funded campaign claims. Best first buyers have 30,000-300,000 PLN equivalent in visible unpaid, rejected, expiring, or mismatched incentive value and no dedicated partner-operations team.

Acute trigger:
The partner sees earned or allocated money inside Microsoft Partner Center, incentive statements, co-op/MDF balances, marketplace payout reports, private-offer statements, customer-association records, or distributor/vendor correspondence, but cash is delayed, rejected, short-paid, expiring, or frozen because proof-of-execution files, claim forms, customer associations, partner IDs, marketplace offer records, tax/bank profile routing, support-case evidence, statement reconciliation, or program-period documentation do not line up.

Control point:
Signed monetary claim mandate over a named current claim queue with limited Partner Center/export access, incentive statements, co-op/MDF balance screenshots, claim IDs, support-case IDs, partner IDs, customer-association evidence, marketplace/private-offer reports, proof-of-execution files, campaign invoices, distributor/vendor correspondence, recovery fee terms, exclusion log, and case-status ledger. The startup controls the current claim packet and support-case workflow for named incentive money, not the partner's Microsoft relationship or program eligibility.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five partners sign paid claim mandates; at least 300,000 PLN equivalent in visible unpaid, rejected, expiring, or mismatched incentive value is placed into the case ledger; ten specific claims or payout lines are reconstructed and submitted, corrected, escalated, or cleanly no-go'd; at least three claims produce official movement such as accepted proof of execution, reinstated eligibility, corrected customer association, Microsoft/distributor support acknowledgement, corrected statement, paid incentive, or documented no-go; at least one partner pays a success fee or second fixed claim-batch fee; gross margin remains above 60% after contractor partner-ops review.

6-month POC:
Handle 40-80 named claim lines across 12-20 Microsoft partners; place 2,000,000-5,000,000 PLN equivalent of visible incentive value through the ledger; collect 200,000-600,000 PLN in fixed and success fees; document 800,000+ PLN equivalent in paid, reinstated, corrected, accepted, or cleanly no-go'd incentive value; get at least four partners to submit a second batch; keep average first-pass reconstruction under five business days for evidence-ready claims; build a private taxonomy of Partner Center rejection codes, proof-of-execution gaps, customer-association mismatches, marketplace payout mismatches, partner-ID issues, period cutoffs, support-case wording, and distributor escalation patterns.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of Polish/CEE Microsoft partners from Microsoft partner directories, LinkedIn partner roles, cloud communities, CSP/ISV events, public case studies, and marketplace listings. Outreach is only about current cash: "If Partner Center shows incentive, co-op, marketplace, or rebate money that is unpaid, rejected, expiring, or mismatched, send the statement, claim IDs, support-case history, POE files, and payout report. I reconstruct the claim packet, submit/escalate the case, or give a clean no-go. No generic partner-ops audit."

Economics:
Charge 4,000-10,000 PLN fixed setup for evidence reconstruction where at least 30,000 PLN equivalent of visible claim value exists, plus 5-12% of recovered, reinstated, or newly paid incentive value. For larger partner queues, charge 12,000-25,000 PLN fixed plus 4-8% success fee. Contractor review costs are 1,000-4,000 PLN per messy batch from experienced Microsoft partner-ops specialists. The buyer pays because the money is already visible, expiring, rejected, or short-paid, and the fee is tied to cash/credit movement or a clear no-go before the claim window closes.

Copy risk:
Microsoft distributors, partner-development managers, partner-ops consultants, partner marketing agencies, accountants, and experienced CSP operations teams can copy pieces. The defense is narrow current-money focus, CEE partner-language support, claim-status taxonomy, support-case wording memory, proof-of-execution pattern library, and signed control over named claim queues before a general consultant sees the money.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the partner signs the claim mandate, prepays or agrees the success fee, and shares the named incentive statements, co-op/MDF balance, claim IDs, support cases, customer-association evidence, marketplace reports, POE files, and payout deadline, the startup controls the active claim packet and escalation ledger for that money. A distributor, consultant, or accountant would have to displace the already-built claim file, case wording, support chronology, and fee agreement while the claim deadline is running.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only named Microsoft partner-program monetary claims with visible unpaid, rejected, expiring, short-paid, or mismatched incentive value. The output is a claim-release artifact used in an active Partner Center or distributor workflow: reconstructed claim packet, POE index, statement mismatch table, customer-association correction file, support-case chronology, escalation wording, recovered/paid/corrected/no-go ledger, and success-fee invoice. It is not generic Microsoft partner consulting, FinOps, SaaS, a dashboard, a marketplace, partner marketing, or brokered introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide tax advice, legal advice, accounting opinions, Microsoft program eligibility opinions, licensing advice, marketplace legal interpretation, bank-profile setup, identity verification, fund custody, or guarantees of Microsoft payment. Partners, accountants, legal advisers, Microsoft, distributors, customers, and marketplace operators remain responsible for eligibility, tax/bank profile, contractual compliance, licensing, customer consent, and payment decisions. Cases involving fraud, false proof of execution, fake customer association, disputed resale authority, sanctions, suspicious tax/bank profile, or pressure to manufacture evidence are declined or routed to qualified advisers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak because it does not recover retailer deductions, trade-promotion leakage, retail-media charges, or FMCG/customer remittances. It is not Amazon Vendor Central recovery because there is no Amazon shortage, chargeback, co-op accrual, Vendor Central remittance, or ASIN/platform dispute. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The distinct control object is Microsoft partner-program incentive money inside Partner Center and related distributor/support-case workflows.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Microsoft distributors, partner managers, and experienced partner-operations consultants may already help partners with incentives and co-op claims. Some stuck payouts may be caused by tax, bank profile, eligibility, licensing, customer consent, or program rules that cannot be fixed by better claim packaging. Partner Center access is sensitive and may create security and trust friction. Microsoft support outcomes can be opaque and slow. Many partners may have too little visible claim value to justify fees. The process may become generic Microsoft partner-ops consulting unless the acceptance rule stays limited to named current monetary claims with visible value and a support/claim path.
