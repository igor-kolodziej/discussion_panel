## Strongest Objections Captured

1. The addressable market is smaller than the headline because actual SENT scope depends on direction, goods, thresholds, invoice status, AEO status, postal exclusions, procedure 42, and other carve-outs.
2. Ordinary domestic B2B wholesale dispatches with proper VAT invoices may often be outside the paid pain zone.
3. Free public support reduces willingness to pay for simple cases: PUESC/KAS materials, technical helpdesk, and dedicated clothing/footwear support channels are available.
4. The pain may normalize after 3-9 months as firms learn the workflow, set templates, assign PUESC permissions, or route work to forwarders/accountants.
5. Forwarders and customs brokers are natural incumbents because they already sit close to the shipment.
6. Warehouses may resist a no-release mandate because an outside party blocking trucks can interfere with delivery deadlines.
7. Classification and exemption boundaries are dangerous and require buyer-provided classification or pre-approved rules from an accountant/customs adviser.
8. Per-shipment pricing of 150-500 PLN is too low for exception-heavy loads with missing invoices, uncertain CN codes, mass errors, route changes, carrier changes, driver issues, geolocation problems, or urgent dispatches.
9. The most confused sellers may also be too cheap or chaotic to pay reliably.
10. The moat is mostly operational habit, not legal, technical, or regulatory defensibility.

## Lesson

New regulation and live dispatch pressure are not enough when the paid workflow can normalize quickly and natural transport/accounting incumbents can absorb it. Round 230 should return to harder control: owned payment streams, titled assets with buyer deposits, assigned current cash, or a live case/account where the founder controls something more durable than operating habit.
