Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Scored idea:

Obsolete PLC/HMI Line-Down Donor Bank buys, tests, photographs, serializes, and holds titled inventory of discontinued/long-lead PLCs, HMIs, I/O modules, drives, encoder cards, power supplies, communication modules, operator panels, and control-system spares used in Polish factories. It signs first-look/consignment rights with retiring automation technicians, machine rebuilders, plant decommissioners, bankrupt machine shops, and industrial electricians.

The startup sells or rents exact tested donor modules during line-down events where a retrofit would take weeks and a single compatible part can restart production. The business does not perform safety-critical engineering without qualified contractors; it controls asset title, test records, compatibility notes, seller handoff, and emergency transaction flow.

Buyer / payer:
- maintenance managers at Polish factories running legacy Siemens/Omron/Lenze/ABB/Schneider/Mitsubishi systems;
- automation integrators and service technicians who need exact replacement modules quickly;
- machine rebuilders and plant electricians with old installed bases;
- decommissioners and retiring technicians who want cash/consignment for old stock;
- SMEs whose production line is down and cannot wait for retrofit or OEM lead time.

Acute trigger:
A line is down because an obsolete PLC/HMI/drive/module failed. OEM replacement is discontinued, official lead time is slow, a full retrofit is too expensive or takes weeks, and the factory is losing production every shift. The buyer will pay a premium for a known-good exact module, fast courier, return rights, and proof that the part has been tested.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is:
- titled exact-SKU donor inventory with serial numbers, photos, firmware/version notes, and test records;
- test bench and contractor automation technician workflow;
- first-look or consignment rights from plant decommissioners, bankrupt shops, machine rebuilders, and retiring automation service technicians;
- compatibility notes by machine/PLC family and known firmware/revision constraints;
- emergency phone/domain/mailbox and response workflow;
- paid reservation/deposit agreements from factories/integrators for the top failure-prone SKUs;
- supplier/seller history and acquisition pipeline before buyers can search the same lot.

The hard control is: specific titled tested modules + first-look supply rights + emergency buyer deposits/orders.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN option/consignment agreements with 2-3 decommissioners/technicians/machine shops;
- 35,000-65,000 PLN first titled donor inventory lot, bought only after buyer interviews and deposits;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN test bench, technician verification, photos, packaging, labels, and inventory system;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN legal/terms/limited warranty/returns/liability review;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN courier/emergency logistics and damaged/dead-stock reserve.

Required proof:
1. Signed first-look/consignment or option agreements with at least 2 supply sources.
2. Titled inspected inventory lot under 65,000 PLN, with 50+ modules/SKUs and no stolen/questionable origin.
3. Test records/photos/serial logs for at least 80% of acquired inventory.
4. 20 buyer interviews naming exact top SKUs and installed systems.
5. 5 factories/integrators sign paid reservation, deposit, or emergency-supply agreement before final close.
6. 30,000+ PLN in paid deposits, preorders, rentals, or sales within 60 days.
7. Gross margin above 45% after acquisition cost allocation, testing, dead-stock reserve, warranty/return allowance, courier, and seller earnout.
8. No safety-engineering claims beyond "tested donor part"; qualified contractors handle installation and controls work.

Economics:
Revenue comes from emergency sale of tested donor PLC/HMI/drive/modules, short-term rental/loaner with deposit for factories that need time to retrofit, reserved stock retainers for integrators/factories, consignment margin from decommissioning sources, and optional paid search/sourcing fee only when tied to a specific paid buyer request.

Payback logic:
The founder does not buy random obsolete electronics. The founder signs supply rights, interviews buyers for exact SKU pain, collects deposits/reservations, buys the lot, tests it, then sells/rents into urgent line-down demand. One or two emergency transactions can pay for a meaningful part of the first lot if the SKUs are scarce and verified.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The business owns or controls physical parts, serial/test records, first-look supply rights, and emergency orders. It is not a dashboard, lead-gen site, repair consultancy, or marketplace wrapper.

Copy risk:
Large used-automation dealers and integrators exist. The wedge is local title to exact modules, first-look rights to specific Polish decommissioning lots, fast test/ship workflow, and paid reservations from factories/integrators before inventory purchase. If the founder only lists parts online, the score falls below 80.

Regulatory and operating guardrails:
- Verify clear title and avoid stolen/questionable-origin parts.
- Use limited warranty/return terms and no safety-engineering claims.
- Qualified automation contractors handle testing and installation/controls work.
- Document firmware/version/accessories and known compatibility exclusions.
- Do not promise certification, safety validation, or engineering signoff unless performed by qualified professionals.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Wrong inventory can strand capital.
- Used-part incumbents already exist.
- Firmware/software/revision mismatch can kill transactions.
- Testing and warranty/returns must be rigorous.
- Line-down demand is lumpy unless supply and buyer networks compound.
- Founder needs automation technician contractors for testing and compatibility.
