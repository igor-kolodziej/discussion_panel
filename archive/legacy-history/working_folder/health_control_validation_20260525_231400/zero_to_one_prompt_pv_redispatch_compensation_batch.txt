Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ReDispatchCash PV Compensation Batch Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Polish PV owners affected by PSE non-market redispatch orders need a fast, batchable way to turn named curtailment dates into complete compensation applications, portal acknowledgements, deficiency responses, and collected cash without hiring an energy-law project for every small SPV.

Exact buyer:
Polish PV farm SPVs, small RES portfolio owners, and O&M/accounting administrators managing 1-20 MW PV installations affected by PSE non-market redispatch orders.

Acute trigger:
PSE publishes a non-market redispatch notice for PV installations, the owner has matching curtailment or reduction events in SCADA, meter, OSD, operator, or internal production records, and compensation applications must be submitted through WOZE or the PSE emergency/bulk route before the work becomes a delayed receivable.

Control point:
A case is accepted only after the startup has a signed owner or authorized-manager mandate for named installations and event dates, WOZE or application-signing access path, supplied SCADA/export/meter data, installed-capacity data, owner and SPV details, PSE/OSD instruction evidence, bank/tax invoicing details, a prepaid batch fee, and a success-fee entitlement tied to collected compensation. The owner remains claimant and legal/tax decision-maker.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Six signed claimant mandates across at least three PV owners or O&M/accounting administrators; twenty named installation/date compensation applications assembled; ten applications submitted through WOZE or accepted emergency/bulk route; two PSE/WOZE acknowledgements, accepted import confirmations, deficiency notices narrowed to owner data, or payment-progress outcomes; 40,000-90,000 PLN collected from setup fees plus documented success-fee receivables; one energy-settlement reviewer on paid per-case terms.

6-month POC:
Twenty to forty signed claimant mandates or administrator-routed batches; 120-250 installation/date applications assembled; 70+ applications submitted; 25+ acknowledgements, deficiency narrowings, payment-progress outcomes, or collected-compensation events; 250,000-650,000 PLN collected from setup fees and success fees; at least two repeat O&M/accounting/asset-manager channels; gross margin above 60% after reviewer and data-cleanup costs; fewer than 35% of accepted cases killed by missing owner data or legal/technical eligibility disputes.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list from PSE PV redispatch notice dates and target small PV portfolio owners, O&M firms, RES accountants, and asset managers whose portfolios are too small for a large law-firm project but large enough that multiple installation/date claims matter. Outreach offers a fixed-fee triage of named PSE event dates against owner data, then a prepaid batch application package with success fee only on collected compensation.

Economics:
3,000-5,000 PLN triage for one owner or installation group and event-date eligibility map; 6,000-12,000 PLN prepaid batch setup for up to 10 installation/date applications; 600-1,200 PLN per additional installation/date application; 6-12% success fee on collected compensation with caps or declining percentages for larger portfolios. The owner pays because compensation is current cash linked to official curtailment events. Administrators pay or resell because repeated event dates and many SPVs create a batch workload that is too operational for lawyers and too specific for generic accountants.

Copy risk:
O&M firms, asset managers, energy lawyers, RES accountants, and larger portfolio owners can copy the workflow. The defense is signed mandates over current compensation queues, reusable installation/date workpapers, WOZE application memory, no-go patterns, reviewer checklists, and fast batching for small portfolios that are under-served by higher-end energy advisers.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After a PV owner or authorized administrator signs a mandate and supplies named installation/date data, WOZE or application-signing route, SCADA/meter exports, SPV details, and fee terms, the exact claim queue is locked inside that owner's current compensation file. A competitor can copy the general process, but cannot access or submit that owner/date batch without the claimant switching mid-claim and duplicating data cleanup, reviewer checks, application assembly, and deficiency-response history.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is a controlled compensation-application batch tied to named PV installations and PSE redispatch dates. The proof artifact is submitted WoR applications, WOZE/PSE acknowledgements, accepted import confirmations, deficiency responses, payment-progress outcomes, and collected compensation. There is no marketplace, no software-first dashboard, and no generic advice memo.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal opinions, tax advice, grid-code opinions, engineering reconstruction, market-price disputes, eligibility certification, or guarantees of PSE acceptance or payment. The PV owner remains claimant, declarant, recipient of funds, and decision-maker. Disputed eligibility, missing meter truth, unclear ownership, false data pressure, tax treatment, or grid/legal interpretation is escalated to the owner's counsel, accountant, energy adviser, O&M provider, or rejected.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack Safety, or HeatQuiet. It is closest in shape to prior recovery-mandate and payment-rail attempts, but the control point is materially different: official PSE PV redispatch compensation applications for named installation/date events, not retailer deductions, platform credits, factoring, KSeF invoice acceptance, municipal invoice evidence, product compliance evidence, or generic chargeback recovery.

Strongest anticipated objections:
O&M firms and asset managers may already handle compensation applications for their portfolios. Energy lawyers and RES accountants may be more trusted for edge cases. PSE timing and acceptance are outside founder control. Small PV owners may not have clean SCADA, meter, instruction, or SPV data. Some cases may require legal or technical eligibility decisions outside scope. Public PSE forms may make the workflow look like admin rather than a defensible company. Portfolio owners may resist success fees if they believe the application is routine. Case volume may be seasonal or event-driven. A part-time unknown founder needs a credible energy-settlement reviewer and tight rejection rules to avoid becoming a low-margin claims administrator.
