Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CashTransit Float Return Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Multi-site cash businesses with closed sites, cashless conversions, CIT provider switches, smart-safe shutdowns, franchise handoffs, or insolvency cleanup will pay to recover provider-held cash balances, change-fund residues, cassette deposits, settled overages, and residual cash-management balances when the startup controls the mandate, site ledger, provider acknowledgement, and refund instruction.

Exact buyer:
Corporate retailers, fuel-station groups, franchise administrators, hospitality groups, parking operators, amusement/cash-terminal operators, cash-heavy service chains, insolvency administrators, and retail finance consultants managing 10+ former or switching cash sites. The first buyer must have a named cash-in-transit, smart-safe, bank cash-processing, or cash-management provider and at least 75,000 PLN of expected recoverable balances across a site group.

Acute trigger:
A store estate closes, a franchisee exits, a cashless conversion retires cash drawers, a retailer changes CIT or smart-safe provider, a cash-management account is terminated, or an insolvency administrator cleans up former branches. Finance knows there may be returned cash, cassette deposits, change-fund residues, sealed-bag exceptions, smart-safe balances, or settled overages, but the site files, provider statements, bank credits, closure dates, cassette IDs, safe IDs, carrier case references, and refund instructions are scattered.

Control point:
Signed corporate mandate over named sites and provider accounts; site-level cash/safe/CIT/change-fund/cassette/deposit-bag ledger; prior carrier or smart-safe statements; provider contact path; bank refund instruction approved by the corporate finance owner; case log; deficiency tracker; accepted refund instruction, provider acknowledgement, account credit, returned balance, or clean no-go. The startup never touches cash or holds funds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five paid mandates covering at least 40 closed, switched, or cashless-converted sites and at least 750,000 PLN of candidate provider-held balances. At least three cash-management providers acknowledge a balance, refund path, deficiency list, account credit, or no-go reason. At least one actual refund, account credit, or offset is completed. The startup collects 40,000-120,000 PLN in fixed, setup, or success fees, excluding unpaid success-fee receivables.

6-month POC:
Handle 15-30 mandates across 150-400 former or switching sites. Put 3,000,000-12,000,000 PLN of candidate provider-held balances under review. Achieve 20+ provider acknowledgements, accepted refund instructions, deficiency lists, account credits, returned balances, offsets, or clean no-go outcomes. Collect 250,000-800,000 PLN revenue. Keep gross margin above 65%, average clean-file provider response under 30 days, and at least 40% of screened sites rejected or no-go'd because the balance is too small, disputed, already reconciled, fraud-sensitive, or outside scope.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with Polish/CEE retail finance consultants, insolvency administrators, franchise accountants, cash-office contractors, store-closure project managers, and multi-site operators visibly closing stores, switching CIT providers, or converting to cashless operations. The opening ask is not "let us audit your cash." It is: "Send one closed-site or provider-switch balance tail where a cash-management provider statement, smart-safe ledger, or CIT case trail exists but refund ownership and site reconciliation are unfinished. We take a fixed paid file, return cash to your company account, or produce a clean no-go."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-18,000 PLN for a small site group, 20,000-45,000 PLN for a multi-site closure/switch file, or 8-18% of returned balances where allowed and clearly attributed. Costs are founder operations, ledger reconstruction, secure file handling, finance-owner calls, provider correspondence, contractor reconciliation support, and legal/accounting template review. The buyer pays because even 100,000-500,000 PLN of returned cash justifies a fixed cleanup file when the alternative is stale finance work across many former sites.

Copy risk:
CIT providers, smart-safe vendors, retail accountants, insolvency administrators, auditors, and cash-office teams can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is the specific controlled file: signed mandate, named site list, provider account IDs, provider statement trail, finance-owner refund instruction, case chronology, accepted provider path, and repeat taxonomy by provider and closure/switch scenario.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the corporate mandate, provider account list, site ledgers, historical statements, refund instruction, and case correspondence for a named closure or provider-switch group, another provider or consultant cannot recover that same cash without displacing the already-authorized file owner. Future copy risk remains real, but the first proof is transaction-specific control over current provider-held balances.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell software, benchmark cash operations, broker CIT contracts, or introduce providers. It controls current refund files over named provider-held balances and gets cash returned, account credited, or no-go'd. The output is cash movement or provider-acknowledged no-go, not a report.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not handle physical cash, transport cash, operate safes, act as a cash-in-transit provider, hold client funds, provide accounting opinions, perform audits, investigate theft, provide AML or fraud remediation, adjust insurance claims, collect debts, or give legal advice. Corporate finance owners approve refund bank details and representations. Licensed cash carriers, banks, smart-safe providers, accountants, auditors, insurers, lawyers, and insolvency officers remain responsible for their regulated roles. Reject suspected theft/fraud, AML/sanctions concerns, employee misconduct, insurance claims, police matters, disputed shortages, litigation, unclear corporate authority, consumer funds, low-value single-store files, and cases without provider statements or a credible provider contact path.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central, Retail OTIF, 3PL overbilling, OTA recovery, COD courier remittance, pallet deposit recovery, PSP reserve release, KSeF/AP payment release, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack, or any marketplace evidence desk. The buyer is a multi-site cash business, the trigger is cash-site closure or cash-provider switch, the control point is provider-held cash balance acknowledgement and refund instruction, and the payment event is returned cash or account credit from a cash-management provider.

Strongest anticipated objections:
CIT providers and retail finance teams may already reconcile these balances as ordinary account closure work. Provider-held balances may be much smaller than assumed after normal credits, shortages, fees, refunds, bank deposits, and write-offs. Many apparent balances may be disputed shortages, theft, employee issues, stale cash-office errors, or fraud-sensitive files outside scope. Providers may ignore a third-party coordinator and deal only with the retailer's existing treasury contact. Sensitive site cash data creates trust friction for an unknown founder. Success-fee attribution may be weak if a refund was already scheduled. Chain closures and provider switches may be episodic. Insolvency files can be slow and legal-heavy. The 6-month revenue target depends on finding multi-site files above 75,000 PLN, not scattered single-store exceptions.
