Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
SwiftBridge CSP Attestation Evidence Sprint.

One-sentence thesis:
Smaller SWIFT users will pay for an assessor-ready evidence sprint when annual KYC-Security Attestation, independent-assessment support, new-user go-live, or correspondent/counterparty visibility is at risk, because failure to submit or support the attestation can create network, supervisor, and relationship consequences.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE smaller SWIFT users and SWIFT-connected organizations: payment institutions, EMIs, small banks, credit unions/cooperative banks, securities brokers, treasury centers, financial-market utilities, corporate treasury teams with direct SWIFT connectivity, and service-bureau customers that lack a large internal SWIFT CSP program. The economic buyer is the CISO, COO, compliance owner, SWIFT officer, audit lead, head of payments, or CFO responsible for annual CSP evidence and KYC-SA submission readiness.

Acute trigger:
The buyer is inside the July-December annual SWIFT KYC-SA attestation cycle, preparing for a new SWIFT go-live, responding to an independent-assessor evidence request, addressing prior-year mandatory-control gaps, or facing a correspondent/counterparty/security-due-diligence request that asks about SWIFT CSP posture. The buyer has scattered control evidence and a deadline; it does not need generic cyber strategy.

Control point:
The startup accepts only live attestation or assessor/counterparty request files. The controlled artifact is the assessor-ready SWIFT CSP evidence room: signed scope and confidentiality mandate, SWIFT user/BIC and environment scope, architecture/scoping notes, control-by-control evidence index, owner map, missing-evidence register, screenshots/export checklist, assessor-request response tracker, safe draft wording for customer review, independent-assessor handoff pack, management approval log, and no-go/remediation split.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign at least two paid SWIFT users or one user plus one certified-assessor/referral partner. Each case must include a live KYC-SA, independent-assessment, new-joiner, or counterparty evidence trigger and a prepaid fee of at least 15,000 PLN. At least one case must produce an assessor-ready evidence room, assessor acknowledgement, accepted evidence response, KYC-SA submission readiness decision, or documented no-go/remediation split. One certified SWIFT CSP assessor, cyber reviewer, or experienced SWIFT security practitioner must be contracted for boundary review before any customer-facing pack is delivered.

6-month POC:
Close 8-15 paid SWIFT CSP evidence sprints during the attestation cycle, with 250,000-700,000 PLN revenue and at least four cases sourced by assessors, payment consultants, service bureaus, or financial-sector cyber partners. At least six cases must produce measurable outcomes: assessor-ready evidence room delivered, evidence request closed, KYC-SA readiness confirmed by the user, assessor follow-up narrowed, counterparty response accepted, or clean no-go/remediation route documented. Gross margin must stay above 55% after reviewer, secure-workspace, translation, and founder time.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with certified SWIFT CSP assessors, financial-sector cyber boutiques, service bureaus, payment consultants, treasury consultants, and smaller SWIFT users in Poland/CEE. The partner pitch is: "You keep the independent assessment and control judgment; I run the evidence room, owner chasing, control-index assembly, and assessor-ready pack for smaller clients under your review." The direct buyer pitch is: "If your KYC-SA or assessor request is live and scattered across IT, audit, payments, and vendors, I turn it into an assessor-ready evidence pack without claiming to assess or attest for you."

Economics:
Charge 15,000-25,000 PLN for scoped evidence-room rescue, 30,000-60,000 PLN for full control-by-control assessor-ready sprint, and 8,000-15,000 PLN for annual evidence refresh or counterparty response. The value is protecting SWIFT go-live, annual attestation readiness, correspondent/counterparty trust, and avoiding deadline panic. Costs are reviewer fees, secure document handling, evidence indexing, translation, and project coordination. The startup does not perform independent assessment, certify controls, submit attestations on behalf of the user, or implement cybersecurity controls.

Copy risk:
Certified assessors, Big Four firms, cyber consultancies, banks, internal security teams, and GRC platforms can copy the workflow. The defense is narrow execution memory for smaller SWIFT users: accepted evidence patterns, owner-chasing checklists, assessor request wording, KYC-SA support artifacts, environment-scope templates, and partner trust from assessors who want evidence prep handled without assigning senior staff to document chasing.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder controls a paid live attestation case with user authorization, SWIFT user scope, assessor or counterparty request text, evidence owners, secure evidence room, reviewer route, and delivery deadline, a competitor cannot control that exact evidence file before the current submission or assessment window unless the user terminates the mandate. Incumbents can compete for future CSP work, but not the specific live evidence sprint already under paid control.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell generic cybersecurity consulting, GRC software, a dashboard, auditor introductions, independent assessment, attestation, legal advice, or remediation. It accepts only live SWIFT CSP attestation or evidence-request files and produces a specific control artifact: assessor-ready evidence room, evidence index, owner map, missing-evidence register, request tracker, management-approval log, reviewer boundary notes, and handoff pack for the user and its independent assessor.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No independent SWIFT CSP assessment, no certification, no attestation submission authority, no statement that controls are compliant, no legal advice, no payment-network advice, no cybersecurity implementation, no penetration testing, no audit opinion, no supervisory communication, and no remediation delivery unless separately contracted to qualified providers. The SWIFT user remains responsible for truthful attestation, control implementation, management approval, KYC-SA submission, assessor selection, and regulator/counterparty communications. Certified assessors, auditors, lawyers, cyber firms, SWIFT service bureaus, and internal control owners remain responsible for formal assessment, control judgment, legal interpretation, and technical remediation.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because the buyer is the SWIFT user preparing annual CSP/KYC-SA or counterparty evidence, not a software vendor answering regulated-finance procurement questionnaires. The control object is the SWIFT CSP attestation evidence room and assessor handoff, not DORA ICT third-party-risk supplier renewal evidence. It is distinct from CyberBind, PCI4 TPSP, NIS2/UKSC, AIQ, RAGProbe, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, Supplement Stack, and health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Certified SWIFT CSP assessors and financial-sector cyber firms already own this trust layer. Many SWIFT users may be mature enough to use assessors directly. If controls are missing, the case becomes remediation rather than evidence assembly. Sensitive SWIFT architecture and security evidence create a high trust barrier for an unknown part-time founder. The Poland/CEE buyer pool may be small after filtering for users small enough to need help but valuable enough to pay. Partners may internalize the evidence-prep checklist. The business may look like another cyber evidence pack unless the first cases are live KYC-SA or assessor-request files with paid fees and assessor acknowledgement.
