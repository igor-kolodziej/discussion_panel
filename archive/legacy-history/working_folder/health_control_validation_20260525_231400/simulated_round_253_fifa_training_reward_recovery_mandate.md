Source checks used:

- FIFA states that the FIFA Clearing House centralises, processes, and automates training-reward payments between clubs, initially training compensation and solidarity contribution.
- FIFA describes the electronic player passport process: a provisional EPP is generated in TMS, clubs and associations review it, and a final EPP/allocation statement establishes amounts owed to training clubs.
- FIFA says the FIFA Clearing House is a regulated payment institution in France and that payments are based on the final EPP and allocation statement approved by FIFA administration.
- FIFA reported that, two and a half years after launch, the FCH had allocated over USD 500 million to 7,000 clubs and paid USD 300 million in training rewards.

## Lead Candidate

**FIFA Training-Reward Recovery Mandate**

### Internal Simulated Score

88.8 / 100

### Why It Clears Simulation

- The payment rail is official and current: final EPP/allocation statements create concrete owed amounts.
- The founder does not have to argue broad damages or litigate; the first wedge is document/control work around EPP review, registration evidence, FCH onboarding, and payment release.
- Small clubs and academies are structurally under-administered but can have meaningful entitlements when a former player transfers or signs professionally.
- Public transfer/news data and club histories allow targeted outbound, while exact registration evidence remains controlled by the club.
- First proof is a signed mandate, EPP correction/progress, FCH onboarding, allocation statement, or paid reward, not interest.
- The startup does not touch funds; FIFA Clearing House pays clubs directly, and the startup invoices success fees or fixed dossier fees.

### Internal Objections

- Sports lawyers, agents, federations, and club administrators can handle strong cases.
- FIFA Clearing House automation may reduce the need for a third-party operator.
- Entitlements are episodic, not monthly recurring.
- Some clubs may lack clean registration records or player passports.
- EPP disputes, sell-on clauses, registration-status questions, and cross-border legal issues can push into sports-law practice.
- Clubs may be low-trust, low-budget, or politically messy.
- Transfer data is incomplete and public monitoring may create many false positives.
- Success-fee attribution must be tied to mandate timestamp, EPP event, allocation statement, or payment.

### Gate Decision

Advance to working Zero To One validation because the simulated score is strictly above 87 and the control point is a signed mandate over a named club/player/FCH payment path with first proof through official EPP/allocation/payment events.
