Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
PalletPool Deposit Cash Direction Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Recover and reconcile stuck pallet, crate, stillage, and returnable-transport-item deposits or credits for CEE distributors where return batches, customer/pool ledgers, pickup notes, and deposit schedules show cash or credit should be released but the account remains unresolved.

Exact buyer:
CEE food, beverage, FMCG, building-materials, packaging, wholesale, and industrial distributors with recurring returnable pallet/crate/stillage accounts across retailers, 3PLs, wholesalers, pool operators, branches, and customers. The first buyer has at least 50,000 PLN visible returnable-asset exposure or 10,000 PLN expected recoverable value in named account periods.

Acute trigger:
Deposit cash, credit notes, or adjusted account balances are stuck after returned pallets, crates, stillages, or other transport items because return counts, branch/site records, pickup notes, damage deductions, customer mappings, pool exports, or account ownership do not match. The buyer wants cash release, credit note, accepted adjusted count, or a clean no-go before month-end, quarter-end, supplier settlement, or customer account close.

Control point:
Signed mandate over named returnable-asset accounts and account periods; deposit schedules; delivery notes; pickup notes; return records; pool/customer account exports; branch/site ledgers; photos where available; carrier/3PL/customer confirmations; damage and rejection logs; payment-direction or credit-note request; debtor/customer/pool acknowledgement tracker; and clean no-go ledger for disputed ownership, missing proof, or damaged/unreturned items.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign six mandates; screen at least 20 return-batch or account-period files; obtain at least 200,000 PLN in debtor/customer/pool acknowledged, credited, paid, adjusted-balance, or clean no-go outcomes; collect at least 30,000 PLN in screened-ledger and success fees; and get at least two buyers to submit a second account period.

6-month POC:
Run 30-60 mandates across 15-35 distributors; process 150-350 account-period or return-batch files; resolve 2,000,000-7,000,000 PLN of deposit/credit value through payments, credit notes, adjusted balances, accepted counts, or clean no-go outcomes; collect 250,000-900,000 PLN in fees; maintain gross margin above 70%; build supplier/customer/pool-specific evidence memory by return item type, branch, account age, proof type, rejection reason, and accepted count format.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to distributor CFOs, operations directors, logistics managers, and finance controllers at food, beverage, FMCG, building-materials, packaging, and wholesale distributors. Triggered lists come from distributors with many branches, retailer deliveries, pallet-heavy products, 3PL handoffs, customer account closures, or public complaints about pallet balances. Partner routes include logistics accountants, pallet consultants, 3PL finance teams, and outsourced bookkeeping firms that see unresolved returnable-asset ledgers.

Economics:
Charge 4,000-12,000 PLN for screened-ledger reconstruction per customer/account period plus 10-20% of recovered, credited, adjusted, or formally acknowledged deposit value generated after the mandate. Reject account periods below 10,000 PLN expected recoverable value unless bundled. Suppliers, customers, or pool operators pay/credit the customer directly; the startup does not handle funds or buy/move pallets.

Copy risk:
Logistics teams, finance teams, pallet brokers, 3PLs, pool operators, and accountants can copy pieces. The defense is mandate-level control over messy return-batch files, debtor-specific accepted evidence memory, branch/account mapping, and a pay-from-recovered-cash model for distributors whose logistics and finance teams have already failed to settle the ledger.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the distributor signs the mandate and provides account exports, return batches, pickup notes, deposit schedules, customer/pool contacts, and authority to submit reconciliation requests, the startup controls that account-period file, debtor acknowledgement path, credit-note request, and fee right. A pallet broker or accountant can offer a generic review, but cannot pursue the same acknowledged account-period claim without replacing the already-authorized reconciliation path.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not trade pallets, move pallets, sell a dashboard, broker pallet purchases, or write generic logistics audits. It accepts only named returnable-asset account periods and produces cash payment, credit note, accepted adjusted balance, accepted count, narrowed follow-up, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not handle litigation, fraud allegations, stolen pallets, criminal loss, active commercial disputes, physical transport, pallet inspection as a certifier, debt collection, tax/accounting advice, or cases where ownership/return proof is absent. Do not take custody of funds or pallets. Cases with real damage, missing items, disputed ownership, or customer refusal requiring legal escalation are rejected or routed to the customer's normal legal/commercial process.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak because it does not involve retailer promotion deductions or trade-spend leakage. It is distinct from 3PL overbilling because the claim object is returnable transport-item deposits and account balances, not fulfillment invoices. It is distinct from closed-site utility refunds because the balances are recurring logistics deposit ledgers, not utility/service account closures. It is distinct from Amazon/vendor/platform recovery and physical-lot ideas because the startup neither uses platform support nor buys inventory.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Counts and ownership may be too messy to prove; deposit values per item may be too small unless account periods are large; customers, pool operators, or 3PLs may reject third-party contact; damage, rejected returns, unreturned assets, theft, branch mistakes, and account offsets can consume the apparent balance; internal logistics teams may claim they already own pallet reconciliation; pallet brokers and pool operators can copy if value is visible; success-fee attribution can be disputed; the work can become low-margin spreadsheet and email chasing unless intake thresholds are strict.
