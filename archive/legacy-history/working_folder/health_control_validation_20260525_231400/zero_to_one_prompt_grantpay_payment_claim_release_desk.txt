Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
GrantPay Payment-Claim Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A current-cash release desk for Polish/CEE SMEs and their accountants/grant advisers with already-awarded EU, PARP, NCBR, FENG, regional, innovation, or energy grants whose reimbursement or payment claim is rejected, corrected, paused, or not ready because invoices, bank proofs, procurement attachments, fixed-asset protocols, deliverables, indicators, publicity proofs, payroll/timesheets, or portal attachments are missing or inconsistent.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE SMEs, grant-funded project owners, accountants, CFOs, and grant advisers with already-awarded grants and a live payment-request, correction, reimbursement, advance-settlement, or final-tranche file.

The best first buyer has incurred eligible costs, a signed grant agreement, a named claim/correction deadline, a payment amount above 100,000 PLN, and enough documents to assemble a defensible file if the evidence is organized quickly.

Acute trigger:
The beneficiary has already spent money or is near a payment claim, but reimbursement/current cash is blocked by a correction request or missing evidence: invoices, bank proofs, procurement comparisons, contracts, fixed-asset protocols, delivery/acceptance documents, photos, publicity markings, timesheets, payroll proofs, deliverables, indicators, VAT/split-payment details, or portal attachments.

Control point:
Signed payment-claim mandate with:
- grant agreement and payment schedule;
- current payment claim/correction request;
- eligible-cost ledger;
- invoice and bank-payment proof;
- procurement/evaluation documents where applicable;
- delivery/acceptance/fixed-asset protocols;
- deliverables, photos, indicators, publicity proofs, timesheets, payroll documents where applicable;
- portal attachment checklist;
- correction response pack;
- no-go/escalation log for legal/accounting/procurement questions.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid payment-claim release cases at 5,000-25,000 PLN each.
2. At least one claim or correction pack used in a submitted payment request/correction response.
3. At least one accepted correction, payment-claim progress, reimbursement release, or clean no-go before wasting more time.
4. One grant-accounting/compliance reviewer on paid per-case terms.
5. Repeat taxonomy across missing invoices, bank proofs, procurement, delivery protocols, indicators, publicity, payroll/timesheets, VAT, and portal attachment gaps.
6. One accountant, grant adviser, or equipment vendor channel that can route a second case.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a strict intake checklist for awarded-grant payment claims and correction requests; recruit one grant-accounting/compliance reviewer; build a target list of 60 accountants, CFO advisers, grant consultants, machinery/equipment vendors, energy retrofit vendors, and SMEs with public awarded-grant signals.

Month 2: close three prepaid live payment-claim cases. Accept only files with a signed grant agreement, incurred eligible costs, current payment/correction deadline, and a claim amount above 100,000 PLN. Deliver a correction pack, attachment index, missing-evidence log, no-go/escalation notes, and portal-ready evidence folders.

Month 3: document whether at least one claim progresses commercially: correction accepted, payment request submitted, reimbursement released, final tranche unblocked, advance settlement accepted, supplier/procurement evidence corrected, or clean no-go issued before further wasted work.

Months 4-6: complete 10-20 paid release cases, collect 100,000-350,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, secure 2-3 repeat accountant/grant-adviser/equipment-vendor channels, and build a private taxonomy of payment-claim correction patterns by grant type and evidence object.

First acquisition mechanism:
Buyer pitch:
"If you already have an awarded grant and a reimbursement/payment claim is stuck on a correction request or missing attachments, send the grant agreement, correction, claim ledger, invoices, bank proofs, procurement docs, and deliverables. I return a portal-ready evidence pack, missing-proof log, no-go/escalation list, and correction response package. I do not apply for grants, provide legal/accounting advice, or promise payment."

Channel pitch:
"You keep grant strategy, accounting, tax, and legal advice. I take the ugly payment-claim evidence file when your client already has awarded money and current cash is blocked by attachments, procurement proof, invoices, bank payments, protocols, indicators, or portal corrections."

Economics:
Charge 5,000-9,000 PLN for a simple payment-claim correction pack, 10,000-18,000 PLN for equipment/procurement-heavy reimbursement files, 18,000-25,000 PLN for multi-cost-category or final-tranche release packs, and optionally 3-7% success fee only on released reimbursement where legally and contractually permitted. Reviewer cost is scoped per case. The buyer pays because a 100,000+ PLN reimbursement, final tranche, or advance settlement is current cash, not abstract compliance.

Copy risk:
Grant consultants, accountants, bookkeepers, project managers, equipment vendors, and internal finance teams can copy pieces. The defense is refusing grant-writing, focusing only on live payment-claim release, fast evidence-folder assembly, accepted correction memory, channel overflow, and a private taxonomy of what actually blocks reimbursement after award.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a beneficiary or adviser assigns a live payment claim, prepays the release desk, grants access to the current correction/payment file, and routes the attachment build through the startup under a deadline, another provider must displace an active cash-release workflow. The controlled unit is the live grant payment claim, evidence room, correction log, reviewer notes, portal attachment map, and reimbursement outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only already-awarded grant files with incurred costs and a live payment/correction/settlement blocker. The output is a payment-claim release artifact: attachment index, evidence folder, correction response pack, missing-proof/no-go log, and portal-ready file map. It is not grant writing, subsidy hunting, application consulting, accounting advice, legal advice, a dashboard, a database, a marketplace, or a brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, tax advice, accounting certification, public-procurement advice, grant eligibility decisions, audit opinions, official beneficiary representation, or a promise of reimbursement. Accountants, grant advisers, lawyers, procurement specialists, auditors, and the beneficiary remain responsible for accounting, legal, tax, procurement, official submission, and commercial decisions. Cases with suspected fraud, false invoices, conflicted procurement, missing underlying eligibility, sanctions, payroll abuse, or materially disputed costs are declined or escalated.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not TraceFaktura because it does not create live municipal animal-care invoice evidence at the operational moment. It handles already-awarded grant payment claims and correction files for SMEs after costs are incurred. It is not PromoLeak because it is not retailer deduction recovery. It is not CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, Data Act, BioSignal, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Grant consultants and accountants may already handle payment claims as normal work.
- Cases clean enough to fix fast may be too easy for the beneficiary/accountant; messy cases may require legal/accounting/procurement advice outside scope.
- Public-procurement errors, eligibility mistakes, payroll/timesheet problems, or missing evidence may be impossible to cure.
- Authorities may reject or delay for reasons outside the founder's control.
- Data access and trust are hard because files include invoices, payroll, bank proofs, procurement, contracts, and internal project data.
- Buyer acquisition is hard unless accountants, grant advisers, or equipment vendors route live cases.
- Success-fee economics may be legally/contractually limited.
- The work can become low-margin document chasing unless the intake filter requires large claims, clear deadlines, and upfront payment.
