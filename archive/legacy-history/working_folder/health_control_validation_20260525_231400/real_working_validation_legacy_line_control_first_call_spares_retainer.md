## Zero To One Judgment

Zero To One called this a commercially sharper improvement over generic obsolete-spares inventory because the sequence is better: acquire the installed-base relationship first, collect retainers/reservations second, and only then buy exact modules tied to named lines.

It still failed well below threshold because incumbents are strong, factories may not prepay before failure, compatibility/testing risk is severe, seller-book trust may not transfer, and a part-time founder is weak for emergency response.

## Strongest Objections Captured

- Incumbents are credible: Radwell, EU Automation, repair houses, OEM distributors, local integrators, Allegro/eBay sellers, and automation parts dealers already provide obsolete and hard-to-find modules.
- Maintenance buyers may refuse retainers and only buy reactively after a failure.
- A nominal part match may still fail because of firmware, hardware revision, memory card, license key, parameter set, protocol, HMI runtime version, OEM lock, missing backup, or safety integration.
- Testing credibility is hard; basic power-on checks are insufficient for many PLC/HMI/drive modules.
- Retiring-technician books may be stale, personal, undocumented, or impossible to transfer without a warm handoff.
- First-call rights may be ignored during a real emergency if factories call everyone at once.
- Inventory can still become dead stock if the customer retrofits, cancels, scraps the line, or the module is incompatible.
- Loaner/rental modules can be damaged by bad wiring, power, installation, contamination, or adjacent failures.
- Emergency dispatch is too time-sensitive for a part-time founder without technician and logistics coverage.
- Safety/liability boundaries are critical and reduce the founder's ability to own the full value chain.

## Useful Narrowing

Zero To One said the best version would be:

- one retiring technician's installed base in one local manufacturing cluster;
- 8-15 named critical lines;
- repeated module families;
- warm seller handoff;
- 5+ prepaid retainers/reservations;
- credible testing partner;
- no safety PLCs, deep CNC modules, robot/servo systems, or untestable stock.

Even with those proofs, it estimated the score would rise only to 76-80. This remains below the threshold.

## Decision

Reject. Do not continue industrial spares or installed-base retainer variants unless the founder controls a payment stream that buyers already pay today, not a hoped-for preventive retainer before failure.
