- Real lab capacity usually requires trusted volume history, technical credibility, retainers, or pre-vetted scopes.
- Agencies own the seller relationship and can bypass the founder after learning the lab path.
- Labs own the testing capability, so the founder controls one case at most, not the future stream.
- Hard cases may require manufacturer-controlled firmware, app/cloud architecture, update policy, vulnerability handling, technical-file changes, or product redesign.
- A lab memo or report may still not unblock Amazon or a retailer if the reviewer asks for different evidence or the account/listing/manufacturer facts mismatch.
- Large incumbents already cover RED/EN 18031 advisory, gap analysis, testing, notified-body support, and conformity paths.
- Margins can collapse after lab fees, agency share, rework, refunds, disputes, and coordination cost.
- Physical samples, firmware/app/cloud scope, lab scheduling, seller urgency, and agency coordination are heavy for a part-time founder.
- "Slot lockbox" positioning can create expectation risk if sellers hear it as faster approval or a higher chance of marketplace acceptance.
- Case memory is useful but not a moat until enough accepted/rejected cases accumulate.
