Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
LiftLog Data Act Maintenance Tender File

One-sentence thesis:
Use owner-authorized EU Data Act access requests to turn connected-lift fault logs, service history, remote-monitoring exports, and maintenance-contract evidence into a tender-ready file that lets Polish property managers rebid or renegotiate lift maintenance with independent UDT-qualified lift firms.

Exact buyer:
Polish housing cooperatives, residential community managers, office/retail property managers, facility owners, and independent UDT-qualified lift maintenance firms supporting those owners. The first paid buyer is the property manager or owner with a live maintenance renewal, price increase, recurring breakdown pattern, or rebid, often co-sponsored by an independent lift firm that wants enough data to bid responsibly.

Acute trigger:
The current OEM or incumbent maintenance provider raises the maintenance price, delivers poor uptime, creates repeated callbacks, withholds remote-monitoring/fault history, or approaches renewal while an independent UDT-qualified lift firm says it cannot price or take over responsibly without historical fault/service data and device documentation.

Control point:
The startup controls the owner-authorized data-access and tender-file workflow: owner mandate, current maintenance contract, lift asset list, UDT/device documentation available from the owner, incumbent/OEM contact path, portal screenshots or exports where available, Data Act access/share request, response log, fault/service-history export, callback chronology, maintenance-scope matrix, tender questions, independent-firm data room, and no-go notes. The startup does not control or touch lift operation, repair, inspection, modernization, safety decisions, or UDT filings.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three prepaid building files at 8,000-25,000 PLN each; each has a signed owner/property-manager mandate, a live maintenance renewal/rebid/price increase or repeated-callback issue, at least 4 connected lifts or a maintenance contract above 80,000 PLN/year, the current maintenance contract and asset list, an issued Data Act access/share request to the data holder or remote-monitoring provider, a response or portal/export evidence, a normalized fault/callback/service-history file, and at least one independent UDT-qualified lift firm using the file to submit a bid, decline with clear technical no-go reasons, or support the owner in price/scope renegotiation.

6-month POC:
Complete 8-15 prepaid building files covering at least 50 lifts; obtain at least 5 data-holder responses or portal/export packets; get at least 4 independent-firm bids, clean no-go decisions, or incumbent price/scope concessions; keep gross margin above 60% after legal/data reviewer and lift-domain reviewer costs; build a reusable taxonomy of data-holder response patterns, fault-log fields, tender questions, no-go triggers, and owner decision outcomes.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start through independent UDT-qualified lift maintenance firms, building technical managers, housing-cooperative administrators, property-management firms, and lift consultants who already hear complaints about OEM lock-in, renewal pricing, unavailable fault history, or risky takeovers. The pitch: "If the owner wants to rebid maintenance but lacks remote-monitoring and fault-history data, I handle the authorization, data request, export normalization, and tender file. You keep all maintenance, safety, inspection, and repair work."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-12,000 PLN for a small building file, 12,000-18,000 PLN for multi-building or mixed-OEM files, and 18,000-25,000 PLN for urgent renewal/rebid files with many lifts or poor export quality. Reviewer cost is 1,000-5,000 PLN per file for Data Act/legal-boundary review and lift-domain issue spotting. The buyer pays because a maintenance contract above 80,000-300,000+ PLN/year can be rebid or renegotiated, and because taking over lifts without fault/service history creates operational risk for the owner and independent firm.

Copy risk:
OEMs, incumbent maintainers, lift consultants, property-management firms, and larger independent lift companies can copy parts of the workflow. The defensible wedge is narrow: owner mandates, real data-holder response memory, normalized field mappings, tender-question libraries, independent-firm no-go/bid patterns, and repeat relationships with property managers and independent UDT-qualified lift firms. Copy risk is high if the output is only a generic checklist.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the owner signs the mandate, shares the maintenance contract, lift asset list, complaint/callback history, portal access evidence, and authorizes the Data Act request, the startup controls the active rebid file and response log for that building. A different adviser or lift firm must displace the data request, normalized export, fault/callback chronology, tender questions, and independent-firm bid/no-go workflow under a renewal deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live lift maintenance renewal, rebid, price-increase, or repeated-callback cases with owner authorization and connected-product data access. The output is a tender/rebid artifact used in an active maintenance decision: access request, response log, fault/service-history normalization, callback chronology, maintenance-scope matrix, data room, tender questions, and bid/no-go evidence. It is not generic facility consulting, lift maintenance, lift safety review, UDT inspection, software, dashboard, brokered introduction, or repair advice.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
UDT-qualified lift contractors, inspectors, owners, and competent parties remain responsible for maintenance, repair, inspection, modernization, safe operation, emergency response, UDT obligations, technical decisions, and building safety. The startup does not touch lift control systems, retrieve data by unsafe access, interpret logs as safety conclusions, recommend repairs, provide UDT/legal opinions, represent the owner before UDT, or guarantee that a data holder must provide every requested field. Cases involving active safety hazards, accidents, illegal access, credential sharing, tampering, or urgent rescue/repair are declined or routed to qualified parties.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is adjacent to the confirmed Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk because both use EU Data Act access rights. It is materially different in buyer, trigger, economics, and proof artifact: lift maintenance renewal/rebid files for property managers and independent UDT-qualified lift firms, not compressor telemetry energy-savings memos for manufacturing SMEs and compressed-air service partners. It is also not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, or any health/Rx route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
OEMs or incumbent maintainers may resist data requests, provide incomplete exports, or argue that fields are unavailable, safety-sensitive, trade-secret protected, or outside Data Act scope. Independent lift firms may already inspect enough to bid, making the file unnecessary. Property managers and housing cooperatives can be slow, political, and price-sensitive. UDT and safety boundaries are serious; any drift into maintenance judgment or repair recommendations is dangerous. Some older lifts may not be meaningfully connected. A single rebid file may be too episodic unless repeat channels form. The idea may be seen as a duplicate or narrow vertical variant of the existing Data Act machine-data liberation theme.
