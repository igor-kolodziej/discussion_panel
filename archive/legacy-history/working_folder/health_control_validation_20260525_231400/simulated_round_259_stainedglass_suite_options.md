## Internal Gate Review

### Concrete 6-month control proof

Pass. The first proof is not a list, report, or marketplace. It is signed control over named physical suites plus buyer cash:

- seller option or title-transfer agreement over a complete suite;
- building/owner authority and removal permission;
- measured panel schedule with photos, dimensions, damage map, provenance notes, and conservation/removal plan;
- buyer deposit or escrowed conditional purchase before removal;
- no speculative storage-heavy acquisition.

### CAC, payback, margin, cash conversion

Pass, if kept narrow. Target economics:

- 3,000-7,000 PLN paid scouting/measurement fee where the buyer asks for a hunt;
- 20,000-80,000 PLN buyer deposit or conditional purchase for a coherent suite;
- 15-35% spread or success fee on completed transfers;
- first six-month target: 2 closed suites, 3 optioned suites, 2 buyer deposits, 60,000-180,000 PLN gross margin.

### Copy risk

Conditional pass. Salvage dealers, stained-glass restorers, architects, and antique dealers can copy the category, but they cannot copy a specific optioned suite once the founder controls it. The only durable wedge is rapid control of coherent suites before demolition/renovation fragmentation, plus buyer-deposit discipline and a growing buyer request file.

### Founder constraints

Pass. The founder does not restore, certify, install, conserve, or speculate. Contractors handle removal, crating, transport, condition review, and restoration estimates. The founder controls only sourcing, options, deposits, packet discipline, and no-go rules.

### Duplicate-risk distinction

Materially close to HeritageDoor Suites, but not a duplicate if kept to:

- decorative stained/etched/transom glass, not doors;
- buyer trigger is visual feature/restoration design freeze, not a missing door suite needed for apartment completion;
- payment event is buyer deposit before conservation-safe removal;
- proof artifact is a measured glass condition/provenance/removal map, not a door-hardware/fit schedule.

### Simulated strongest objections

1. It may be too close to HeritageDoor and could be only another heritage-salvage vertical.
2. Stained-glass restorers and salvage dealers already see many opportunities and have more trust.
3. Conservation restrictions, owner authority, condominium rules, or monument status can block removal.
4. Condition risk is high: cracks, missing pieces, lead came fatigue, bowing, paint loss, prior bad repairs, and poor fit.
5. Buyer deposits may be hard before inspection and restoration cost estimates.
6. The category may not produce enough repeatable volume in Warsaw/CEE.
7. Fragile transport, crating, insurance, and restoration estimates can eat margin.
8. Simple decor buyers may choose new custom glass instead of salvage.
