Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CAPEACH Electronic Refund Enrollment Rail

One-sentence thesis:
IEEPA refund money can be delayed even after an importer or customs broker has a refund path if the importer lacks ACE importer sub-account access, valid Form 5106 contact data, ACH refund authorization, bank information, refund-report monitoring, or an accountable owner for the electronic refund rail; sell a narrow setup-and-proof rail that converts broker/importer-ready refund batches into traceable electronic refund readiness without giving customs or legal advice.

Exact buyer:
CEE/EU brands, distributors, manufacturers, and ecommerce importers that acted as U.S. importer of record for IEEPA-duty entries and expect material refunds, plus small and mid-sized U.S. customs brokers serving such importers but lacking back-office capacity for importer ACE/ACH setup follow-up and refund-status reconciliation. The first buyer has at least USD 50,000 expected refund value or a broker-routed cluster of importers, and already has a broker, trade adviser, or internal owner for CAPE eligibility and filing.

Acute trigger:
The buyer has paid IEEPA duties and has a broker/importer path for CAPE or other CBP refund handling, but electronic refund payment can be delayed because ACE Portal access, importer sub-account permissions, Form 5106 email/contact data, ACH refund authorization, U.S. bank/refund-recipient data, refund monitoring reports, or internal owner assignment is missing, stale, or unverified. The commercial pain is not tariff theory; it is avoidable delay in receiving otherwise payable electronic refunds.

Control point:
Signed refund-readiness mandate over named importer-of-record accounts and refund-rail artifacts: importer authority, ACE access checklist, importer sub-account status, Form 5106 contact/email status, ACH refund authorization status, bank-data confirmation record, broker/IOR filing path, CAPE declaration ID/status if available, REV refund-monitoring exports or screenshots, exception log, refund-date or refund-readiness confirmation, no-go memo, and fee right. The startup does not file customs declarations unless the authorized importer or licensed broker does so, does not provide tariff/legal opinions, and does not hold refund proceeds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Sign six IOR or broker-routed refund-readiness mandates covering named importer accounts and expected refund pools. Complete four paid refund-readiness setup files. Produce at least three objective ACE/ACH outcomes such as importer sub-account access confirmed, Form 5106 contact/email corrected, ACH refund authorization completed, bank data confirmed, or refund-recipient record corrected by the authorized party. Produce two CAPE declaration or refund-status traces through ACE reports, broker exports, or importer screenshots. Produce at least one electronic refund payment, refund date, accepted refund-monitoring status, or written refund-readiness confirmation tied to a before-state defect. Collect 60,000-160,000 PLN equivalent in fixed setup fees and clearly attributed success fees.

6-month POC:
Handle 25-60 refund-readiness files across 8-20 importers or 3-6 broker-routed importer clusters. Complete 15-35 ACE/ACH setup, correction, confirmation, or monitoring outcomes. Produce 10-25 refund-status traces, accepted declaration traces, payment-date records, actual refund confirmations, or clean no-go memos. Collect 250,000-700,000 PLN in fixed and success fees. Maintain gross margin above 65% after secure intake, admin reviewers, broker/trade-compliance boundary review, and data cleanup. Build a private playbook for ACE access defects, Form 5106 contact defects, ACH refund authorization problems, bank-data mismatches, importer/broker responsibility splits, report-monitoring workflows, and refund-status reconciliation.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start through small and mid-sized U.S. customs brokers, importer accountants, trade-compliance freelancers, CEE/EU export advisers, and CEE/EU companies with public U.S. importer-of-record exposure. The first offer is: "If the broker or importer already owns CAPE eligibility and filing, I make sure the electronic refund rail is ready: ACE access, ACH refund enrollment, contact data, refund reports, exception log, and payment trace. No customs opinions, no protest strategy, no refund custody."

Economics:
Charge USD 1,500-4,000 for a single IOR refund-readiness sprint, USD 500-1,500 for additional broker-routed importer files using the same workflow, and 1-3% of refunds only when a documented before-state refund-rail defect is fixed and an after-state refund, payment date, accepted refund-monitoring status, or written readiness confirmation is documented. Fixed fees dominate because refund entitlement belongs to the importer and CBP may pay valid refunds once proper electronic setup exists. Costs are secure intake, admin review, status reconciliation, broker coordination, and occasional trade-compliance boundary review.

Copy risk:
Customs brokers, trade lawyers, freight forwarders, Big Four customs teams, importer trade-compliance teams, and refund-recovery firms can copy the general workflow. The startup's edge is narrow operational control over exact importer refund-rail files during a live refund wave, plus CEE/EU importer discovery and broker-routed overflow execution. This is not a long-term moat unless refund-status memory and broker relationships expand into repeated customs-refund readiness operations.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
For each signed file, the founder controls the specific refund-readiness trail: named importer account, authorized contact path, ACE/ACH checklist, before-state defect, bank/contact correction evidence, broker or importer filing path, refund-monitoring reports, exception log, and fee trigger. A broker or adviser can copy the process for future files, but displacing a paid file already moving through ACE/ACH confirmation and refund-status monitoring requires the importer or broker to rebuild the same trail and responsibility split.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell a customs advisory memo, tariff-refund opinion, eligibility screen, protest strategy, software dashboard, broker introduction, or generic refund database. It accepts only named refund-readiness files where an importer or broker already owns the refund path. The output is an operationally completed electronic refund rail: ACE/ACH setup or correction, refund report trace, exception/no-go log, and payment/readiness evidence for that exact importer account.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No customs classification, tariff eligibility opinion, protest preservation, drawback advice, reconciliation advice, court-claim strategy, importer-of-record dispute, surety dispute, refund-rights opinion, legal advice, tax advice, banking advice, customs declaration filing by the startup, or custody of CBP refund proceeds. Licensed customs brokers, trade counsel, importers, banks, and accountants remain responsible for filing, legal/customs interpretation, bank account ownership, tax treatment, and refund-recipient decisions. Reject unclear importer authority, disputed refund rights, final-liquidation strategy, entries requiring legal judgment, missing broker/importer filing path, suspected fraud, sanctions, insolvency, or any case asking the startup to receive government funds.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, KSeF, LC discrepancy, PV compensation, or a platform evidence desk. It is adjacent to the previously tested CAPE IEEPA Refund Batch Desk, but the control point is materially narrower: electronic refund enrollment and refund-readiness after the broker/importer refund path exists, not entry eligibility, CAPE declaration preparation, customs screening, or tariff recovery strategy.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Customs brokers may already handle ACE/ACH enrollment and refund monitoring for serious importers. The best importers may have internal trade-compliance teams, while messy cases may involve legal eligibility, protest, drawback, reconciliation, or importer-rights issues that must be rejected. A narrow refund-enrollment sprint may feel too simple for high fixed fees unless refunds are large and delays are acute. The opportunity may be a temporary IEEPA refund wave rather than a durable company. Public CBP instructions reduce perceived complexity. Importers may distrust an unknown Warsaw founder with ACE, banking, customs, and refund-status data. Brokers may use the founder only once, then assign junior staff or automate the workflow. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if CBP would have paid after normal setup anyway. CEE/EU importer acquisition may be slow because many rely entirely on U.S. brokers. The business can become low-margin admin chasing if cases are accepted without a clean broker/importer owner, specific before-state defect, and objective refund-readiness outcome.
