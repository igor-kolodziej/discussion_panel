Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
BorrowingBase Draw-Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Inventory- and receivables-backed SMEs often have current borrowing availability blocked because lender borrowing-base certificates, AR aging, inventory schedules, lockbox cash, reserves, credits, ineligibles, and support schedules do not tie out; the startup accepts only named lender deficiency cases and produces a lender-ready draw-release or clean no-go file.

Exact buyer:
Primary buyer: 20-300 person distributors, importers, manufacturers, wholesalers, ecommerce wholesalers, B2B suppliers, and light industrial companies using asset-based lending, receivables finance, inventory finance, factoring, or hybrid collateral-backed working-capital facilities.

Best first buyer: a CFO, controller, founder, turnaround operator, or finance lead with a named current draw, availability, reserve release, overadvance cure, first-certificate, or monthly borrowing-base submission blocked by a lender, factor, or collateral-monitoring team.

Channel buyer: fractional CFOs, turnaround advisers, ABL brokers, factoring brokers, distressed accountants, ERP/accounting migration consultants, lender workout advisers, and lenders that want borrower-side cleanup without owning the borrower's reporting mess.

Acute trigger:
The borrower needs working-capital availability now, but the lender rejects or delays the borrowing-base certificate because AR aging, inventory listing, cash application, credit memos, reserves, concentration calculations, ineligible accounts, debtor exclusions, inventory locations, availability formula, lockbox receipts, or backup schedules do not match the lender's format. The consequence is a blocked draw, reduced availability, reserve hold, delayed supplier payment, missed payroll buffer, covenant stress, quarter-end cash squeeze, or inability to buy inventory.

Control point:
The startup controls the borrower-signed mandate, exact lender deficiency request, facility reporting calendar, lender certificate template, AR aging, inventory export, sales/credit memo schedule, cash/lockbox proof, reserve and ineligible schedules, support folder, exception tracker, submitted corrected certificate or no-go note, and lender response trail for a named facility period.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Four prepaid borrower mandates with written lender/factor deficiency requests or blocked availability emails.
2. At least one channel partner that has seen repeated borrowing-base certificate failures.
3. 20,000,000 PLN equivalent collateral value screened across AR and inventory.
4. Four lender-ready draw-release or no-go files delivered.
5. Two measurable outcomes: accepted certificate, incremental availability, draw release, reserve reduction, narrowed lender exceptions, or clean no-go accepted by borrower.
6. 50,000-140,000 PLN collected from fixed fees and first monthly-report retainers.
7. Rejection log showing at least 40% of inbound cases rejected for true credit risk, legal/covenant dispute, missing books, insolvent debtor concentration, inventory title problems, lender discretion, fraud concern, or requests for accounting/legal opinions.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build lender-format intake checklist, secure data room, AR/inventory/cash tie-out templates, no-go rules, and reviewer bench for accounting and ABL issue spotting.
Month 2: complete four prepaid release/no-go cases and one channel relationship.
Months 3-4: process 12-20 paid facility periods across 8-12 borrowers; document repeated lender deficiency patterns by facility type; convert at least four borrowers to monthly certificate support.
Months 5-6: process 35-60 paid facility periods; collect 350,000-900,000 PLN revenue; produce 15+ accepted certificates, draw releases, reserve reductions, narrowed exceptions, or lender-accepted no-go outcomes; maintain gross margin above 60% after accounting/ABL reviewers; build a lender-format memory by factor/ABL provider.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start through fractional CFOs, turnaround advisers, distressed accountants, ABL/factoring brokers, ERP migration consultants, and borrower-side finance operators. Message: "If a lender/factor has rejected or delayed your borrowing-base certificate and current availability is blocked, send the deficiency text plus AR, inventory, cash, and reserve exports. I return the lender-ready tie-out file or a clean no-go in five business days. Fixed fee; no legal opinion, no accounting signoff, no lender brokerage."

Direct outreach is limited to companies already signaling ABL/factoring/working-capital stress, recent ERP/accounting migration, fast inventory growth, seasonal cash squeeze, or lender-reporting gaps.

Economics:
Charge 8,000-25,000 PLN for a five-business-day draw-release or no-go sprint, depending on facility complexity and urgency. Monthly recurring certificate support is 5,000-12,000 PLN per borrower after a successful first sprint. A capped success component can apply only to documented incremental availability, reserve release, or draw release, but fixed fees remain primary. One accepted certificate can unlock hundreds of thousands or millions of PLN of current liquidity, making the fee credible when the blocker is real.

Copy risk:
CFOs, controllers, accountants, fractional CFOs, ABL brokers, factoring brokers, lender relationship managers, field examiners, ERP consultants, and turnaround advisers can copy pieces. The defense is fast narrow execution on lender-specific deficiency text, lender-format memory, repeated AR/inventory/cash tie-out exceptions, monthly recurring certificate calendars, and channel partners that prefer to route cleanup instead of doing low-status spreadsheet reconstruction.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the category, but they cannot own a specific borrower mandate, lender deficiency email, certificate template, AR/inventory/cash exports, exception tracker, support folder, submitted certificate, lender response trail, and monthly reporting calendar once that facility period is routed to the startup. The startup controls a named availability case and can turn it into recurring facility-period work before a competitor re-enters.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The output is not a dashboard, financing broker introduction, generic AR audit, accounting cleanup report, or lender advisory memo. It is a lender-ready borrowing-base draw-release or no-go file for a named facility period: corrected certificate, support schedules, exception tracker, evidence index, availability bridge, submitted pack, lender response trail, and monthly continuation calendar.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as lender, factor, broker, accountant of record, auditor, lawyer, valuation expert, collateral agent, or collection agency. It does not decide legal eligibility, covenant compliance, lien perfection, debtor creditworthiness, inventory valuation policy, true-sale treatment, fraud issues, or accounting judgments. The borrower, lender, accountant, counsel, and facility documents remain authoritative. The startup maps borrower-provided facts to the lender's requested format, flags unresolved issues, and rejects cases where the real blocker is credit risk, insolvency, fraud, legal dispute, missing records, or lender discretion.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, KSeF invoice release, customer true-up recovery, unapplied cash cleanup, or factor reserve release. The controlled object is a recurring lender borrowing-base certificate and current collateral availability, not a customer invoice payment, marketplace evidence packet, public grant file, or one-off recovery mandate.

Strongest anticipated objections:
CFOs and controllers should already own borrowing-base reporting if the facility is important.
Lenders, factors, ABL brokers, accountants, and field examiners already sit close to the workflow.
Many blocked certificates are not tie-out problems; they reflect real credit risk, bad debt, customer concentration, ineligible inventory, missing title, covenant breach, weak records, fraud concern, or lender discretion.
Borrowers in cash stress may be poor payers or may misrepresent collateral facts.
Sensitive AR, inventory, bank, lender, debtor, and facility data creates trust and confidentiality friction.
Success-fee attribution can be disputed if the lender would have released availability after ordinary back-and-forth.
Part-time execution may be strained when payroll, supplier payment, or inventory buys are waiting.
The startup can drift into accounting cleanup, lender negotiation, turnaround consulting, collections, ERP cleanup, or loan brokerage.
Lender formats vary enough that standardization may be slower than expected.
The first cases may come from messy borrowers whose books are too broken to solve within five business days.
