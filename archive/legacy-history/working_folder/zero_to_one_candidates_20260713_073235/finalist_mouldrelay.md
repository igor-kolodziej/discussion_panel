# Zero-to-One Candidate: MouldRelay Dormant-Tool Reorder Rights

## One-Sentence Thesis

Acquire title or exclusive production rights to dormant but qualified injection moulds from retiring Polish converters, pair each tool with its historical process file and seller-authorised customer reorder right, and exercise the option only after a legacy-component buyer prepays a new production run.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build the target database, analyse reorder economics, automate quoting, and create a tool/process registry. A contract tooling engineer and an established moulding subcontractor perform inspection and production. The founder does not need to operate a factory during validation.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

Industrial OEMs and maintenance buyers occasionally need 500–20,000 units of a legacy plastic component after the original converter retires or refuses small runs. Recreating a mould can cost tens of thousands of PLN, qualification takes time, and the old tool's process settings are often scattered. Buyers currently over-order, reverse engineer, persuade the retiring converter, or redesign the assembly.

## Product Wedge

Obtain a 90-day option over ten named moulds plus CAD, steel/material record, maintenance history, validated process window, sample parts, and seller-approved contact to the historical buyer. Exercise one tool only when the buyer prepays a production run covering relocation, inspection, setup, material, and margin.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The value is not scrap steel; it is the qualified bundle of physical tool, process history, sample truth, and an identified recurring buyer. Retiring converters view intermittent jobs as nuisance work, while broad moulders will not catalogue tiny legacy demand. A portfolio can centralise storage, inspection, small-run scheduling, and quoting.

## Underused Momentum

EU repair and ecodesign policy raises the value of repairable components and discourages premature obsolescence. Poland combines a large SME manufacturing base with lower-cost toolrooms and converters. These are directional signals, not proof that a specific tool has repeat demand.

## Beachhead Market

Non-regulated, non-safety-critical industrial plastic components from one retiring Wielkopolska converter: guards, knobs, cable guides, enclosures, handles, and machine interfaces where the historical buyer still operates the parent product.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Charge a 2,000–5,000 PLN annual tool-custody/availability fee plus 35–55% gross margin on production runs. Target 20,000–80,000 PLN revenue per active tool/year. A 100-tool active portfolio at 45,000 PLN average revenue produces 4.5m PLN revenue; a scaled 1,500-tool portfolio produces 67.5m PLN. Storage, insurance, maintenance, material, and subcontract production are included in a target 48% gross margin and 20% operating margin. All figures are hypotheses.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten come from seller-authorised historical buyer contact attached to optioned tools. First 100 come from five converter exits, exact part-number SEO, maintenance distributors, and cross-selling to OEM groups. No tool is bought merely because it may someday be useful.

## Primary Control Point

Title or an exclusive field/territory production licence to the named mould, possession/custody, complete process file, golden sample, and an assigned or seller-authorised reorder route. A tool without clean IP and customer rights is Tier-3 scrap and must be rejected.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Build a list of 60 retiring converters from trade directories, used-equipment listings, KRS/CEIDG signals, and direct owner outreach. Pay no more than 5,000 PLN for a ten-tool option. Spend up to 10,000 PLN on tooling/IP review and obtain buyer quotes before relocation. Require one prepaid 20,000+ PLN run and 40%+ expected contribution within 180 days. An unknown founder can offer retirement monetisation without forcing the converter to run nuisance batches, but owner cooperation remains unproven.

## Distribution Advantage

Historical buyers are attached to the first tools; exact part numbers and samples create later inbound. The portfolio may offer one procurement account across many legacy components. The copy risk is severe: a buyer can redraw the part or a converter can reproduce a simple tool.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with one converter's noncritical legacy tools, then add adjacent tool portfolios, central storage, validated process recipes, and recurring custody contracts. Expand from injection moulds to dies and jigs only after the same buyer channel and unit economics repeat. The company wins only if transferring a qualified tool is materially faster and cheaper than redesign.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Local toolrooms, short logistics, Polish buyer/seller documentation, and post-1989 SME succession create a practical entry. There is no formal barrier; title, qualification history, exact samples, and accumulated buyer routes must supply the defence.

## Compounding Advantage

Every active tool adds custody revenue, process data, material/supplier demand, buyer relationships, and scheduling density. A portfolio can batch materials and negotiate better small-run economics. Inactive tools, however, create storage cost rather than advantage.

The model fails if active-tool utilisation is too low or one customer owns the design and can move demand without the tool.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash while the founder remains employed and limit optional distributions to 5%; the strong case reinvests 100%. Capital funds only optioned tools with buyer evidence, storage, inspection, and prepaid-run working capital.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–60: 60 seller targets, 30 approaches, five buyer interviews, two tool-room quotes. Days 61–90: sign one ten-tool option for <=5,000 PLN. Days 91–150: inspect files and contact historical buyers with seller permission. By day 180: one prepaid run of >=20,000 PLN, 40%+ contribution, clean tool/IP title, and a repeat-demand indication. Total pre-proof spend <=28,000 PLN alongside employment.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- 6,000 PLN discovery and travel.
- Up to 28,000 PLN total after a credible option, including engineer/legal review.
- Up to 60,000 PLN only after a prepaid run and successful first delivery.
- Up to 100,000 PLN only after three independent buyers and two reorder events.
- Family capital excluded until repeat utilisation and positive contribution are documented.

## Critical Dependency Map

Clean tool/IP title, tool condition, historical buyer authority, qualified substitute moulder, and repeat reorder demand are all required. The option must expose them sequentially; if two or more require speculative cooperation after payment, do not exercise.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

At least 50 active tools, five independent buyers, two repeated runs, positive fully loaded contribution, a repeatable seller-sourcing signal, and 12 months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

An expired option leaves the tool registry, diligence checklist, buyer interviews, and subcontractor quotes. No physical tool is warehoused without paid demand.

## Critical Evidence

EU policy supports repairability and on-demand spares; the DIRECTSPARE project documents long-tail inventory problems. Evidence does not establish tool-level willingness to pay.

## Critical Hypotheses

Owners will assign tool and customer rights; historical buyers will prepay; tools remain qualified; annual utilisation supports the economics; design/IP ownership is clean.

## Kill Criteria

No option after 60 targets; no historical-buyer prepayment within 180 days; unclear IP/title; relocation/repair exceeds 50% of the first two years' expected gross profit; active-tool utilisation below 30%; or fewer than 500 plausibly acquirable active tools after two seller funnels.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Founder 15–20 hours/week; contract tooling engineer; transaction/IP lawyer; insured storage provider; two qualified subcontract moulders. Pre-proof 28,000 PLN; 12-month founder cap 100,000 PLN.
