# Zero-to-One Evaluation: HaulGraph PL v1

## Diagnosis

HaulGraph correctly narrows the claim to rejecting bad corridor assumptions before a trusted survey or permit process. Its founder-owned normalization code, timestamped source history, swept-path tests and purchased benchmark labels are more concrete than a generic route-planning dashboard, and the first asset does not require authority or customer permission. The company shape fails on the wealth/control combination: the conservative case clears €10 million by only €0.53 million while assuming 300 European accounts at €20,000 each, 35% operating margin, 78% founder ownership and portable country ingestion. The brief itself cites a feature-rich incumbent at $400/month and has not evidenced either the serviceable buyer denominator or a repeatable reason to pay four or more times generic software pricing. Without those assumptions, the asset is a useful Polish preflight service/data feature, not yet a €10 million-founder company.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Official Polish evidence supports open national geometry, current-disruption and bridge-limitation sources, individualized abnormal-load routing, multi-manager consent and a substantial permit flow. Competitor material supports existing heavy-haul routing, bridge/turn constraints, survey workflows and low public software prices. No evidence establishes current local-road completeness, commercial caching for every source, the initial benchmark accuracy, insurance availability, buyer willingness to pay 8,000–15,000 PLN per report, a €20,000 annual account value, 300 reachable EU accounts or declining marginal cost of country ingestion. No panel or chatbot verdict was used.

## Overall Score

**66/100.** Weighted points are 132 across 20 weight units; `10 × 132 / 20 = 66`. This is advisory only and is not a validation score.

## Control Point

**Tier 2, weak.** The founder can directly own the normalization pipeline, source snapshots where terms permit, test harness, purchased benchmark labels and correction history. These assets are real and do not depend on customer contributions. The raw facts remain public, and the first benchmark is purchasable by any rival. Strongest copy risk: Octave, RoadScope, a Polish survey firm or a TMS vendor adds a Poland data module through ordinary GIS hiring/data procurement, then bundles it into an already trusted workflow before HaulGraph develops enough historical corrections or switching cost.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The conservative result is €10.53 million in year 14—only 5.3% above the gate—while combining 300 accounts, €20,000 blended annual value, 35% operating margin, 78% ownership, 7× EBITDA and only €1.2 million net obligations. The brief has no denominator showing that 300 is non-heroic and cites a generic incumbent at no more than $4,800/year. Country-by-country source/legal/QA work further pressures margin and dilution. A small miss in any one assumption breaks the non-compensating gate.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** Two corridor cells, a small paid professional benchmark, explicit unknown states and two unrelated >=8,000 PLN prepayments can be attempted within 180 days and 34,000 PLN while the founder remains employed. The product can be built without authority access or customer data, and the first report can have positive contribution. The main commercial risk is that buyers see it as inseparable from their trusted route survey.

## Expansion Check

The Polish rejection-preflight beachhead is coherent, but profitable multi-country expansion is not a direct adjacency. Each jurisdiction adds source licensing, road-manager structure, data completeness, language, benchmark and liability work. The same schema helps, yet the resulting company risks becoming a country-by-country data-integration service. The standalone wealth thesis fails if the product must be bundled into an incumbent survey/permit stack to gain trust.

## Fatal Objections

1. The conservative founder-wealth case is not resilient and depends on unverified premium pricing plus 300 EU accounts.
2. The core inputs are public and a trusted incumbent can reproduce the normalization/benchmark through ordinary hiring and data work.
3. The buyer may not fund a separate artifact that explicitly cannot replace its existing survey or permit workflow.
4. Old LiDAR, missing local bridge capacity and current-condition changes may leave too much of the target network as `unknown`.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

An uncertainty-honest, timestamped public-data layer can economically falsify obviously bad abnormal-load corridors before high-trust survey and authority work begins.

## Recommendation

**kill**

Do not cosmetically raise prices, add countries or rename the report. The same public-data/preflight shape does not presently carry a resilient founder-wealth path or a hard-to-copy control point. Preserve the source/versioning and rejection-benchmark method for a future candidate with a controlled transaction or proprietary sensor feed.
