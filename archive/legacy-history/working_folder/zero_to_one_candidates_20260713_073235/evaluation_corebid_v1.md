# Zero-to-One Evaluation: CoreBid PL

## Diagnosis

CoreBid is a disciplined, demand-first physical-trading proposal, and that materially distinguishes it from speculative obsolete-spare or exact-match inventory pools: it does not buy until two buyers publish overlapping SKU demand, and it can test a complete cash loop cheaply. That difference improves reachability, but it does not create a durable control point: the proposed bid books are not take-or-pay commitments, the workshop “first-call” agreements remain rejectable and non-exclusive, and large core-bank, remanufacturing, distributor, broker, collector, and logistics incumbents already possess more supply relationships and acceptance data. The written “conservative” founder-wealth bridge is not conservative: 275,000 cores per year is 9.5% of a top-down *potential-value* denominator that is not established as accessible annual flow, and it requires 9.2 eligible cores per workshop per month despite the candidate defining qualifying supply as two per month and its operating threshold as three per month. The Reachability Gate passes narrowly, but the Founder Wealth Gate fails; the exact business shape should not proceed to an independent panel.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Sourced evidence

- The April 2025 Polish aftermarket study supports a large *potential* core-value pool. It does not, as presented, establish annual transacting core count, the share not already captured by deposit/core-bank programs, the share within CoreBid's 30–50 eligible families, or accessible contribution after incumbent prices.
- BORG's disclosed throughput above two million remanufactured units, range above 13,700 part numbers, Polish production, and core storage above one million units establish real demand but also demonstrate incumbent scale, operating knowledge, and inventory advantage.
- CoremanNet's matching, inspection, credit, pickup, 18-month return, and core-bank processes show that the proposed pain and functions are already addressed inside an integrated parts-distribution relationship for at least some workshops.
- Premier Components' public purchasing position supports the possibility of a buyer conversation. It does not prove that an unknown entrant can obtain a signed floor-price book, overlapping second-buyer coverage, objective post-delivery acceptance, or payment within 30 days.
- Poland's tax authority states that wholesale or retail sellers of motor-vehicle parts cannot use the ordinary small-business VAT exemption. It also states that used-goods margin VAT is conditional on the type of seller from whom the goods were purchased. The candidate's 350/650 PLN arithmetic is therefore not decision-grade until it specifies whether each figure is gross or net, who invoices whom, input-VAT recovery, margin-scheme eligibility, and cross-border treatment. [Polish Ministry of Finance: VAT exemptions](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/en/value-added-tax/vat-exemptions/), [Polish Ministry of Finance: VAT guidance](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/podatkowa-wspolpraca-miedzynarodowa/dpi-digital-platform-information)
- GIOŚ has publicly described suitable used parts intended for reuse or remanufacturing as non-waste in an industry discussion, but that is not a transaction-specific safe harbour. EU waste law defines “preparing for re-use” as a recovery operation once a product/component has become waste. The source, condition, holder intent, rejection path, storage, and transport facts therefore matter; the candidate correctly treats a written Polish opinion as a pre-inventory dependency. [GIOŚ](https://www.gov.pl/web/gios/spotkanie-w-gios-dotyczace-regeneracji-czesci-samochodowych-z-przedstawicielami-branzy), [EU Waste Framework Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02008L0098-20240218)
- The December 2025 EU vehicle-circularity agreement supports long-run remanufacturing direction, but it remained provisional and was expected to apply only after formal adoption and a later application date. It does not create present demand, supply rights, or a regulatory moat for CoreBid. [Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/12/circular-economy-council-and-parliament-strike-deal-on-rules-for-vehicle-circularity-and-management-of-end-of-life-vehicles/)

### Candidate hypotheses, not evidence

- Two unrelated buyers will provide contract-grade, overlapping bid books to an unknown small supplier. A signed or dated price book still is not controlled demand unless it includes a binding purchase quantity or take-or-pay obligation, durable price validity, narrow auditable downgrade rights, and creditworthy payment terms.
- At least 40 of 80 targeted workshops possess eligible cores not economically committed to a distributor deposit/core-bank flow.
- The workshop owns each removed part or has the vehicle owner's authority to transfer title. A workshop provenance declaration alone does not cure a missing customer title or an existing deposit/return obligation.
- Average buyer payment is 650 PLN, full direct cost is 500 PLN, complete rejection is at most 15%, purchase-value loss is at most 10%, and realised contribution is at least 100–150 PLN across every acquired core.
- Eight of 20 workshops repeat inside 180 days, later cohorts reach at least three eligible cores per month, first-call retention becomes meaningful, and acquisition payback remains below three months.
- Cross-buyer acceptance data yields a pricing or loss advantage large enough to survive incumbent price and pickup response.
- Inventory turns below 30 days and short-duration debt remains available without guarantees, material dilution, restrictive covenants, or a margin-erasing financing cost.

### Consequential evidence gaps

1. No executed buyer price book, purchase obligation, paid batch, credit evidence, or objective rejection history exists.
2. No bottom-up denominator shows annual eligible removals by category, workshop, and route; exclusion rates for matching deposits, customer-owned parts, existing collectors, uneconomic freight, and product/waste treatment are unknown.
3. The 275,000-core scenario conflicts with the stated supplier cadence. At three cores per active workshop per month, 2,500 workshops yield only 90,000 cores per year. Reaching 275,000 requires about 9.2 per month from every active workshop, before churn and seasonality.
4. VAT-inclusive versus VAT-exclusive prices, margin-scheme eligibility, workshop invoice practice, input-VAT recovery, intra-EU supply treatment, and disposal cost for rejects are unresolved.
5. The lawful chain of title from vehicle owner to workshop to CoreBid, including the effect of distributor core deposits and insurer/fleet/customer contracts, is unresolved.
6. No blind batch establishes the weighted acceptance, downgrade, fraud, logistics, labour, storage, and financing losses.
7. No evidence shows a small entrant can retain 78% founder ownership while financing 18m PLN of gross working capital and carrying only 12m PLN net debt at the value date.

## Overall Score

**53/100.** Weighted points are **105 out of 200** (`10 × 105 / 20 = 52.5`, rounded to 53). This is an advisory score, not a simulated-panel or validation score. Strong test design and a credible small pilot cannot compensate for failed founder-wealth economics and a copyable control point.

## Control Point

**Current tier: Tier 3. Attainable within 12 months: weak Tier 2, not Tier 1.**

At present, the candidate owns no buyer commitment, inventory, supply right, acceptance history, or route density. Two standing buyer bid books would narrow speculative demand, but unless they bind minimum purchase volume and price they remain revocable procurement terms rather than controlled transaction flow. Owned inventory is genuine title but temporary and risky; the proposed workshop first-call right is non-exclusive, lets the workshop reject every quote, and arises only after successful transactions. A paid repeat cohort, enforceable title records, stable two-buyer terms, and a realised acceptance graph could create a repeatably accessible Tier-2 operating position. It would still not be Tier 1 because no scarce supply, exclusive buyer flow, or mandatory workflow is controlled.

**Strongest copy risk:** an integrated core-bank, remanufacturer, parts distributor, broker, or local collector can issue the same closed wanted list, use its existing returns/pickup route, match the workshop price, and internalize the data with one procurement/process change. Its installed buyer/supplier relationships and acceptance history likely let it respond faster than CoreBid can compound. The candidate therefore belongs below 80 absent contrary paid evidence.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.**

The scenario table is mechanically complete and properly avoids double counting: it states volume, buyers and workshops, price, revenue, gross and operating margins, working capital, net debt, valuation, ownership, reinvestment, and timing. Its conservative arithmetic also works as written: 13.6m PLN operating profit at 5× gives 68.2m PLN enterprise value; subtracting 12m PLN net debt and applying 78% ownership gives about 43.8m PLN, or €10.2m at 4.30 PLN/€.

The bridge nevertheless fails credibility for five independent reasons:

1. **Heroic denominator:** 275,000 annual cores is about 9.5% of a 2.9m-unit count imputed from a 1.8bn PLN *potential-value* estimate. The source is not shown to be current accessible annual flow, and an average resale price cannot safely convert heterogeneous category value into unit count.
2. **Internal throughput mismatch:** 2,500 workshops must average 110 cores annually, or 9.2 monthly. The candidate qualifies workshops at two eligible removals monthly and sets an operating threshold at three monthly. At the latter rate, its stated supplier base supports 90,000 annual cores, only 33% of the wealth-case requirement.
3. **Thin threshold clearance:** €10.2m is only about 2% above the gate. A modest miss in 8% operating margin, 5× multiple, 78% ownership, 12m PLN net debt, or the 4.30 exchange assumption puts the founder below €10m.
4. **Capital and margin exposure:** the case requires an 18m PLN facility, rapid inventory turns, low rejection, and 8% operating margin in a physical brokerage business facing buyer power and route/inspection cost. No lender term, financing price, or loss history supports that combination.
5. **Incumbent response:** scale is not occurring in an empty market. Integrated core-bank and returns networks can use existing sales accounts, credits, pickup, and acceptance infrastructure to compress CoreBid's spread before it reaches national leadership.

Expected and strong cases are upside scenarios, not independent evidence that repairs the conservative case. Ninety-five percent reinvestment while employed is correctly stated, but reinvestment cannot manufacture eligible supply, defensibility, or profitable volume.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS, narrowly.**

The candidate can attempt real paid proof without a complete product: obtain two overlapping bid books and legal/accounting advice before inventory, acquire 20–35 prechecked cores, receive cleared payment from buyer A, repeat with unrelated buyer B, and recycle cash into a third batch. The stated maximum original capital at risk is 39,000 PLN, no warehouse/vehicle/software build is required, rejected inventory is conservatively treated as scrap, and specialist inspection plus consolidated pickup makes a 15–20-hour weekly founder schedule possible alongside employment.

The pass is narrow because the loop has four independent preconditions: two buyers must provide usable terms, workshops must have transferable eligible cores outside superior deposit returns, counsel must approve the exact product/waste/title/VAT flow, and blind-batch spread must survive rejection and freight. Those are ordinary commercial and professional counterparties rather than unspecified strategic partnerships, and each can be tested sequentially before the next capital tranche. The gate passes because failure is cheap and fast, not because demand is already controlled. A mere price book, workshop interview, or unpaid lot would not count as paid evidence.

## Expansion Check

The first corridor and closed SKU list are appropriately narrow. Category and route expansion are legible, and a Polish collection base near large EU remanufacturing markets is operationally sensible. However, the asymmetry runs against the entrant: broad incumbents can move inward using installed workshop accounts, parts sales, return credits, warehouses, technical acceptance, and pickup routes, while CoreBid must build each local supply cohort, fund inventory, and prove legal/logistics handling. CEE expansion also changes freight, VAT, waste/product, title, buyer, and working-capital conditions; it is not automatic reuse of one Polish asset. Local execution can support a profitable small broker, but the candidate has not shown that it supports durable national or CEE profit retention at the scale required for founder wealth.

## Fatal Objections

1. **Founder-wealth volume is unsupported and internally inconsistent.** The stated 2,500-workshop base does not generate 275,000 cores at the candidate's own three-per-month threshold, and the 9.5% share is derived from an upper-potential value figure rather than accessible annual transactions.
2. **The control point is prospective and copyable.** Bid books are not take-or-pay demand; first-call agreements are non-exclusive; the data graph begins far behind incumbent systems; route density is an operating scale effect available to established collectors.
3. **Workshop supply rights are not established.** The vehicle owner, insurer, fleet, distributor deposit account, or repair contract may control the removed part. A seller declaration does not by itself prove title or extinguish a return obligation.
4. **Product/waste and VAT treatment can reverse the economics.** Eligible and rejected cores may follow different legal regimes; mandatory active VAT registration, gross-versus-net pricing, margin-scheme eligibility, input recovery, cross-border supply, and lawful reject disposal are unresolved.
5. **Rejection, fraud, and post-delivery buyer discretion are economically central.** A 45 PLN reserve and 15% rejection ceiling have no blind-batch evidence, while condition grading and downgrade disputes occur after CoreBid has already paid the workshop.
6. **Working-capital scaling is assumed rather than secured.** The company pays suppliers before buyer acceptance and ultimately needs 18–55m PLN facilities; financing cost, guarantees, covenants, concentration, and obsolete-stock risk can consume margin or founder ownership.
7. **Incumbents can respond using bundled economics.** A core-bank or distributor can subsidize pickup through parts sales and deposit credits, while a standalone intermediary must earn its full cost from the core spread.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

**Demand should be documented before physical inventory is purchased, and profitability should be measured across every acquired unit after rejection, freight, handling, fraud, financing, and time—not only accepted units.** This is a strong capital-control principle. It does not, by itself, make automotive-core aggregation defensible or large enough for the founder-wealth gate.

## Recommendation

**kill**

Kill the exact CoreBid physical core-aggregation shape for this run. The cheap paid-loop experiment is credible, but it would validate a small trading spread, not the non-heroic founder-wealth and durable-control conditions required here. The candidate overlaps the rejected inventory/core/spare-pool family in its eventual economics even though its buyer-first discipline makes it safer than speculative stock: without binding takeoff, exclusive supply, or an already controlled transaction rail, scaling still means repeatedly sourcing, inspecting, financing, and routing copyable physical inventory against better-positioned incumbents.
