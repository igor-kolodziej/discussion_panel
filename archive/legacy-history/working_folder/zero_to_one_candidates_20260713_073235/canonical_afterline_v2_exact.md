# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**Afterline Paid Transition Mandate** is a seller-paid continuity-and-acquisition rail for owner-led Polish/CEE niche machine makers with a dated retirement or product-line shutdown. The exiting owner prepays 12,000–20,000 PLN for a fixed 60-day transition mandate. At signature, the owner deposits the technical archive and grants Afterline exclusive transition authority, temporary commercial-use rights, seller-authorised installed-base communication, and a call option over specified tooling, spares, mark, files, and transferable contracts.

The mandate is not succession advice and cannot end as a report. Its contracted output is a verified rights/data escrow, active-installed-base schedule, three fixed continuity offers, and either a demand-gated aftermarket transfer or a documented return of the archive. The prepaid fee is credited against Afterline's closing consideration. Afterline exercises the option only when at least three unrelated machine owners provide deposits, purchase orders, or prepaid plans totalling at least 25,000 PLN and one first delivery shows at least 45% contribution margin.

## One-Sentence Thesis

A retiring niche machine founder will pay to preserve customer continuity and residual aftermarket value, allowing an unknown buyer to secure technical truth, exclusive transition rights, and installed-base distribution before risking acquisition capital.

## Founder Fit

The founder is a Warsaw data scientist with neuroscience, finance, business-analysis, FMCG, and AI leverage. He can source owners from public records, automate archive ingestion and parts matching, model installed-base economics, and standardise mandates and quotes. His parents' seven-person renewable-installation company provides practical familiarity with field service and owner-led operations but is not a required channel. A transaction/IP lawyer and a vertical-specific machinery engineer cover scarce early expertise; order-paid technicians handle fulfilment. Founder time remains 15–25 hours per week and at most five hours per day.

## Customer And Pain

The first paying customer is a 55+ owner of a Polish non-regulated niche machine OEM, or a small OEM discontinuing one product line, with 100–1,500 active machines, incomplete succession, repeat aftermarket activity, and a dated exit. Abandoning the line destroys residual value, damages the founder's reputation, consumes remaining staff time, and leaves customers exposed. General brokers prefer whole-company deals; service companies do not package a transferable rights-and-customer asset.

The second customer is a factory operating the machine. It faces downtime, undocumented substitutions, technician loss, and pressure to replace a serviceable asset. It currently hoards parts, calls the founder, commissions reverse engineering, or replaces the machine.

## Product/Project/Idea Wedge

The seller prepays a 12,000–20,000 PLN fixed mandate. Before work begins, the contract must grant:

- exclusive transition authority and a non-appointment/non-licensing covenant;
- archive escrow/custody and temporary rights to analyse, quote, and fulfil the explicitly permitted first orders;
- compliant seller-authorised communication to the installed base;
- a call option over the scheduled IP/files, tooling, spares, mark, and transferable contracts;
- liability, insurance, warranty, conformity, data-use, closing-credit, and return provisions.

Afterline verifies at least 30 active machines, creates an installed-base risk/offer table, and sells continuity plans at 8,000–25,000 PLN per site/year, parts at 45–70% target gross margin, and planned retrofits at 25–40%. A paid mandate without custody and exclusivity is prohibited.

## Commercial Insight Or Market Gap

The first Afterline proposal failed because it asked an unknown founder to win an unpaid option, complete legal and technical diligence, obtain customer cash, and deliver before anyone paid him. The improved commercial insight is that the exiting owner is also a budget owner. A fixed paid transition mandate can finance the trust-building and rights-packaging stage while immediately creating a contracted control point. The scarce asset remains the bundle of technical truth and installed-base distribution, not loose spare parts or consulting hours.

## Underused Momentum

The European Commission's 22–23 June 2026 business-transfer guidance calls SME owner retirement without successors a pressing demographic challenge potentially affecting millions of firms and encourages better transfer preparation and matchmaking. More than 60% of European firms are family businesses. EU repair and ecodesign policy also increases the strategic importance of repair information and spare continuity. These are timing signals; owner willingness to prepay remains a hypothesis.

## Beachhead Market

Start with one machinery vertical in Wielkopolska/Mazovia—non-regulated food, woodworking, building-products, or light-industrial equipment—where targets share technicians, suppliers, customers, and liability patterns. The first seller must have a dated exit, 100–1,500 verified active machines, repeat service invoices, clean assignable rights, and no safety-critical scope requiring authority Afterline cannot own or contract.

## Primary Control Point

The initial Tier-1 control point is the **executed and prepaid exclusive transition mandate**: archive custody, temporary commercial-use rights, installed-base communication, seller exclusivity covenant, and call option over named aftermarket assets. At closing it converts into an irrevocable assignment or exclusive licence. If the owner may appoint another agent, revoke access, withhold an essential right, or license the package again, the control point collapses to Tier 3 and the mandate is not accepted.

### Cold Start & Resource Bootstrapping

1. Build 100 targets with visible exit/discontinuation signals from CE plates, trade catalogues, KRS/CEIDG, used-machine listings, and public restructuring records.
2. Interview 20 owners with the exact 12,000–20,000 PLN prepaid mandate. Spend no more than 5,000 PLN before seeing budget evidence.
3. Send 80 personalised owner letters; require four serious calls and two conditional data-room invitations.
4. Sell one signed prepaid mandate by day 120. Cap founder cash before that payment at 18,000 PLN.
5. The seller deposits the archive and signs temporary rights before work. The fee funds up to 12,000 PLN of lawyer/engineer review.
6. By day 180, verify 30 active machines and obtain three unrelated customer deposits/POs/prepayments totalling at least 25,000 PLN. Do not close without them.

An unknown founder is selling a bounded, paid exit product with a closing credit and auditable earn-out, not asking for an unpaid speculative acquisition. The signed mandate itself proves seller budget, trust, exclusivity, and resource custody.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture

The mandate must deliver at 55%+ contribution after allocated lawyer and engineer cost. If the transfer closes, seller consideration is 10–20% cash plus a capped 15–25% of realised gross profit for three to five years; earn-outs are operating/acquisition cash obligations and are explicitly deducted in margin and terminal liability schedules.

A mature line with 300 active machines targets 2.5–4.5m PLN annual revenue, 58–65% gross margin, and 20–28% operating margin after technicians, warranty reserve, earn-out expense, and central overhead. Reject a line below 35% delivered contribution, 75% plan renewal, or 30-month acquisition-cash payback. All commercial figures are hypotheses until paid mandates, orders, and renewals exist.

### Distribution Moat

The first seller pays and contractually transfers the first channel: seller-authorised access to existing machine owners. Future seller acquisition uses delivered transition references inside one machinery vertical, while exact model/part-number pages, used-machine dealers, and cross-line factory ownership reduce customer CAC. The strongest copy risk is a better-capitalised consolidator offering owners lower fees or more cash. Afterline must win with small-deal speed, the prepaid closing credit, standard rights escrow, and vertical operating synergies.

## Competitive Advantage And Expansion Path

1. Complete one paid mandate and transfer one rights-clean line.
2. Require a second unrelated paid mandate before calling seller acquisition repeatable.
3. Stay in one vertical until two lines share at least two of technicians, suppliers, customers, and documentation systems.
4. Build a cross-line parts/failure graph, contractor density, archive standard, and model-specific inbound channel.
5. Expand from Poland into Czechia, Slovakia, and the Baltics only after 75%+ plan renewal and 20%+ line operating margin.

Broad service firms can hire technicians, but they cannot recreate an active exclusive mandate or an already assigned archive, mark, tooling set, contracts, and customer history. Customer maintenance spend remains contestable, so Afterline must win on authorised speed, exact technical truth, and availability rather than claiming a legal monopoly over every order.

## Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers many founder-led post-1989 SMEs, local-language owner/customer trust, dense light manufacturing, and lower field-service cost. The defence is not regulation. It is early access to small vertical product-line transitions that global consolidators ignore, followed by rights custody, seller references, exact-model search demand, and local technician/supplier reuse.

## Why This Could Compound

Each paid mandate finances diligence and adds a seller reference even before acquisition. Each transferred line adds customers, technical files, supplier history, parts demand, cash flow, and exact-model search traffic. Vertical restriction prevents unrelated complexity: shared technicians, suppliers, factories, and liability templates must be measured before line two. Standard documentation and product-line operators replace founder memory.

## Reinvestment And Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution while the founder is employed; optional founder distributions are capped at 5%. Strong reinvests 100%. After transition, distributions require warranty reserves, debt covenants, and liquidity for two mandates. Seller mandate fees finance diligence; customer cash and capped earn-outs finance acquisition; founder capital is not used for unproved goodwill.

## Reachability And 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–45: 20 owner interviews with exact price/contract; kill if fewer than three show real budget or none offers conditional data-room access.
- Days 46–90: 80 personalised approaches, four serious calls, two scoped data rooms, reusable lawyer/engineer-reviewed mandate.
- Days 91–120: one 12,000–20,000 PLN prepaid exclusive mandate; founder pre-payment spend <=18,000 PLN.
- Days 121–150: seller deposits archive; deliver rights/active-base schedule and three offers at 55%+ mandate contribution.
- Days 151–180: three unrelated installed-base deposits/POs/prepayments totalling >=25,000 PLN and one first fulfilment plan. No acquisition close without these.

The paid seller mandate is the Reachability Gate evidence. Customer commitments are the next capital-release gate. All work remains compatible with employment through batching and contractors.

## Capital Tranches And Maximum Loss

1. Up to 5,000 PLN for 20 owner interviews and contract testing.
2. Up to 18,000 PLN total before a paid seller mandate; stop without one by day 120.
3. Up to 40,000 PLN total only after mandate payment and archive custody, to deliver the mandate and first customer proof.
4. Up to 70,000 PLN only after successful mandate delivery at 55%+ contribution and three customer commitments totalling 25,000 PLN.
5. Up to 100,000 PLN only after safe first fulfilment, positive contribution, and repeat/renewal evidence.
6. Family 100,000 PLN excluded until two paid mandates, one closed line, multiple independent paying machine owners, and a documented scaling case.

Maximum founder cash loss before paid proof is 18,000 PLN; before installed-base proof it is 40,000 PLN. Contracts exclude uncapped inherited liabilities.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Remain employed until two unrelated paid seller mandates, at least one closed line, six independent paying machine owners, six months of repeat/renewal demand, positive contribution after contractors/warranty/earn-outs, a repeatable owner channel, and 12 months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery And Reusable Assets

If an owner will not pay, stop before building the business. If a paid mandate does not transfer, return the archive under contract and retain only non-confidential diligence templates, target evidence, and learned price objections. Conditional machine-owner deposits are refunded if rights or fulfilment fail. No informal partnership substitutes for custody or exclusivity.

## Validation Plan

The first 120 days validate the new buyer, budget, trust, and contracted resource. Days 121–180 validate archive quality, installed-base activity, and customer commitment. Months 7–12 validate safe first delivery, 45%+ order contribution, 75%+ plan renewal intent, and a second paid seller mandate inside the same vertical. Only two paid mandates and one completed transfer permit the company to claim a repeatable acquisition channel.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than three of 20 dated-exit owners show real 12,000–20,000 PLN budget, or none offers conditional data-room access.
- No prepaid exclusive mandate after 80 qualified approaches or by day 120.
- Mandate lacks archive custody, temporary commercial-use rights, installed-base communication, exclusivity covenant, call option, or liability allocation.
- Mandate contribution below 55%.
- Fewer than three unrelated machine-owner commitments or less than 25,000 PLN committed by day 180.
- First fulfilled order below 45% contribution; plan renewal intent below 75%; acquisition cash payback above 30 months.
- Essential safety/regulatory work requires authority Afterline cannot contractually control.
- No second paid mandate in the same vertical within 12 months, or the first two lines fail to share at least two operating assets.
- Net-of-liability founder wealth model falls below 43m PLN under real debt, earn-out, margin, dilution, and ownership data.

## Budget And Time

Pre-mandate: <=18,000 PLN. Total before machine-owner commitments: <=40,000 PLN. Twelve-month founder capital: <=100,000 PLN. Founder 15–25 hours/week; lawyer and engineer funded largely by the prepaid mandate; technicians paid by orders. No platform, warehouse, full-time hire, or acquisition closing before the relevant cash gate.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

1. Choose one machinery vertical and write exclusions.
2. Build 100 dated-exit/discontinuation targets with owner and installed-base evidence.
3. Draft a two-page paid exclusive mandate and full rights/liability schedule with a lawyer and engineer.
4. Interview 20 owners using the exact 12,000–20,000 PLN price and contract, recording budget evidence rather than politeness.
5. Prepare archive template, 30-machine verification sample, and three installed-base offers.
6. Kill the pivot on day 45 if the price/data-room test fails.

## Yearly Execution Plan

- Year 1: one prepaid mandate by day 120; three machine-owner commitments by day 180; one safe delivery; one transfer; second paid mandate in the same vertical by month 12.
- Year 2: two to four additional paid mandates/lines sharing technicians and suppliers; hire first product-line operator; prove renewal and 20%+ line operating margin.
- Year 3: four to six lines; model/part-number inbound; secured order-backed working capital; apply the full-time transition gate.
- Years 4–6: eight to twelve CEE lines with documented vertical synergies and retained-earnings acquisition.
- Years 7–11: 14+ lines, 80m PLN expected-scenario revenue, 24% operating margin after earn-outs, and 82% founder ownership.
