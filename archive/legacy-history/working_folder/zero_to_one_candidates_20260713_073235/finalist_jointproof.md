# Zero-to-One Candidate: JointProof

## One-Sentence Thesis

JointProof gives PV installers a founder-owned, connector-specific, de-energized proof-load and four-wire resistance check before mating each field termination, creating a recorded production-quality step between “used the right tool” and a rooftop joint expected to survive decades.

## Founder Fit

The founder's parents operate a seven-person renewable-installation company, giving direct access to technicians, installation sequences, cable scrap, a workshop and a lawful internal alpha environment. That access can establish the manual baseline and create known-good/deliberately flawed samples; it cannot count as the paid customer or scale moat.

The founder fits experimental design, signal processing, anomaly thresholds, calibration data, firmware and structured field evidence. A contracted electrical/product-safety engineer designs isolation, force control and measurement; a connector/solar specialist reviews recipes. The founder remains at 15–20 hours/week and does not claim electrical-certification authority.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is a Polish commercial/industrial PV, battery or O&M installer making hundreds of field DC terminations per month. Its owner/quality lead wants fewer hidden high-resistance joints, callbacks, thermal events and warranty disputes without slowing the roof crew or buying factory harness equipment.

Current practice uses connector-specific crimp/assembly tools, visual inspection, insertion feedback and a manual tug where prescribed. These steps are necessary. The misunderstood gap is that correct tool/process does not create an individual recorded electrical/mechanical result. However, a new verifier is valuable only if it detects meaningful assembly defects more reliably than the existing process and does not damage the connector seal/contact.

## Product Wedge

A portable de-energized fixture for one exact cable/connector recipe:

- operator selects/scans connector and cable recipe;
- replaceable vendor-specific temporary test-mate cassette receives the assembled connector;
- controlled axial proof load checks mechanical retention below the qualified non-damaging limit;
- a Kelvin/four-wire path measures connection resistance through the test mate and subtracts cassette baseline;
- geometry/insertion sensors check seating/retention;
- device returns pass/fail/rework with calibration status and optional physical tag;
- no energised array, insulation-resistance or universal mixed-brand claim.

Start with one widely used connector family and two cable cross-sections. The POC is a quality-control tool, not proof of 25-year reliability, connector compatibility or regulatory acceptance.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The dangerous economic object is the individual field-made joint, yet most assurance attaches to the tool, component family and process. A fast, connector-specific end-of-termination measurement could let installers catch missing strands, poor strip/crimp/insertion or retention before the connector becomes part of a live array.

The product is commercially useful only if blind samples show separable measurement distributions, repeat field use stays below a 20–30-second cycle, and detected defects change rework. A database, report or generic milliohm meter is not the product; the calibrated mechanical/electrical fixture and recipe are.

## Underused Momentum

- Stäubli explicitly emphasizes connector-specific qualified tools and correct field assembly, confirming that termination quality matters and that mixed/incorrect tooling is unsafe.
- The PVEL/HelioVolta PV connector safety guide documents mismatch and resistance risks. It does not validate JointProof.
- Fluke already sells PV connector tools/accessories and can be a formidable entrant.
- EU building/solar policy supports continued installation volume, while installer labour shortages increase the value of fast quality steps. Neither creates willingness to pay by itself.

## Beachhead Market

Polish C&I rooftop installers making at least 500 field DC terminations/month under a quality owner who tracks rework/callbacks. One connector family, two cable sizes, de-energized pre-mating use and ten external jobs. Exclude residential hobby use, mixed-brand mating, already-energized troubleshooting and any connector whose manufacturer instructions make the test unsafe/meaningless.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

POC: two unrelated installers each prepay 4,000–6,000 PLN for a ten-job field pilot covering at least 200 terminations. Production hypothesis: €4,000–€6,000 device; €400–€700/year calibration/software; €80–€200 per replacement test-mate cassette depending on rated cycles.

At scale target 55–65% device gross margin and 65–75% calibration/cassette gross margin; 22–28% operating margin after engineering, liability, support, warranty, sales and founder replacement. Reject the business if fully loaded production cost exceeds 45% of price, annual service/cassette attachment is below 60%, warranty/returns exceed 5% of device revenue, or external pilots will pay less than a €2,500-equivalent production price.

The device does not receive a success fee for “prevented fires.” Value must be shown through time per termination, defect/rework rate, reduced destructive sampling/callback investigation, quality records and willingness to standardize the step.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten: 40 unrelated Polish C&I installers and O&M firms from public directories/tender/project references. Each sees an unedited blind-sample benchmark and runs its own field-made samples. Two prepaid pilots are the gate; five production deposits and repeat use across 1,000 terminations prove initial product demand.

First 100: direct installer outbound, electrical/PV quality trainers, independent labs and distributors only after the product has a safety/conformity dossier. Distribution partners are optional resellers, not a foundational resource. A free demo fleet is capped; customer deposits and live comparison drive adoption.

## Primary Control Point

The founder can own without permission:

- circuit/mechanical design, source code, firmware and calibration algorithms;
- test-load geometry and fixture/tooling;
- replaceable connector/cable-specific test-mate cassettes and rated-cycle history;
- blind good/bad specimen bank and measured recipe thresholds;
- calibration/service workflow, device fleet history and customer-authorised aggregated failure labels;
- trademark, design filings and any patentable non-obvious fixture/compensation method.

This is Tier 2 until performance/tooling/field data create switching cost. No connector trademark approval, OEM endorsement or exclusive distributor is assumed. The product must never imply that owning a public connector sample creates rights over the connector system.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. With the family workshop, document current termination cycle/error/rework and create known-good and seeded defects under engineer supervision.
2. Spend <=30,000 PLN on force gauge/actuator, four-wire electronics, test mates, calibration standards, enclosure and engineer review for an owned bench alpha.
3. Run blinded repeated tests across operator, temperature, cable lot and cassette wear; pre-register acceptance/repeatability thresholds.
4. Obtain IP/FTO, connector-instruction, safety and conformity review. Reject any destructive or instruction-conflicting test.
5. Show results to 40 unrelated installers. By day 180 require two independent prepaid 4,000–6,000 PLN pilots.
6. After payments, spend only the gated remainder up to 100,000 PLN on rugged prototype, fixtures, calibration, safety/EMC pre-compliance and the first paid ten-job deliveries.

Why the founder can secure it: he need not obtain a licence, partner or customer data to build the alpha; commodity components and contract engineering are replaceable vendors. The family company provides an internal baseline but external installers pay only after seeing blind performance and using their own terminations.

## Distribution Advantage

Outbound can target firms by documented PV project scale and lead with a measurable result: “On blinded terminations, this 25-second step found X seeded defects without changing qualified good samples.” This is stronger than generic safety messaging. Each installed device embeds a recipe/calibration/cassette workflow, while customer-authorised outcome labels improve thresholds and next-connector prioritization.

The distribution advantage disappears if OEM tool bundles include equivalent measurement or if installers rely only on connector-maker warranty rules. No influencer, homeowner marketing or status channel is assumed.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Prove one connector/two cable recipes and ten paid jobs.
2. Sell first 25 devices with calibration/cassette attachment.
3. Qualify second connector family only after independent engineering and field evidence.
4. Expand to battery/EV/industrial DC preassembly QA where de-energized joints share the measurement need.
5. Build a focused termination-verification product family and calibration/cassette network.

The strongest copy risk is Stäubli, Fluke or a general test-equipment maker adding controlled pull and milliohm measurement with established trust/distribution. JointProof must move faster through independent cross-family recipes, compact workflow, field labels and a price appropriate for mid-sized installers.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland provides the founder's test access, lower-cost engineering/manufacturing, a large installer base and EU product access. Connector-specific instructions and EU conformity/safety requirements apply across the beachhead. There is no regulatory barrier to foreign entrants. Defence must come from owned design, verified field performance, price, recipes and calibration network.

## Compounding Advantage

Every device adds cassette wear/zero drift, force/resistance distributions, operator/time patterns, rework decisions and later callback outcomes when customers permit. Those data improve limits, calibration intervals, fixture design and which recipes to add. Recurring cassettes/calibration fund quality engineering; more validated recipes make one device useful across more crews and adjacent DC markets.

## Critical Evidence

- Connector-specific tools and correct assembly are established needs.
- Connector resistance/mismatch can create safety/reliability risk.
- PV test-equipment incumbents already have trust/distribution.
- No cited source proves an external proof-load/resistance fixture predicts field reliability or that manufacturers permit it.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Good/bad terminations form stable separable force/resistance/geometry distributions.
- The proof load and temporary test mate do not damage or contaminate the connector.
- Installers will insert a <=30-second step and pay €4k–€6k/device.
- Connector-specific cassettes can be qualified economically without OEM cooperation.
- Field labels and recurring calibration/cassettes create speed/switching advantages before incumbents copy.

## Kill Criteria

- Blind repeatability/defect sensitivity misses pre-registered threshold.
- Proof load/test mate changes seal/contact or conflicts with instructions.
- Cycle exceeds 30 seconds or catches no meaningful defects beyond normal process.
- No two external payments after 40 qualified installer approaches by day 180.
- Production device price ceiling <€2,500 or gross margin path <55%.
- Conformity/liability requires >100,000 PLN before successful delivery.
- Each connector family needs uneconomic bespoke engineering.
- No five independent buyers/1,000 paid field terminations/repeat use after first delivery.
- Conservative ownership/margin/installed-service bridge no longer reaches €10m.
