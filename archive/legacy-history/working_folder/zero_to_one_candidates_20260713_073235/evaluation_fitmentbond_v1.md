# Zero-to-One Evaluation: FitmentBond

## Diagnosis

FitmentBond removes PlatformRoot's inventory, merchandise COGS, supplier-credit and refund-liquidity burden, but it does not repair the two central weaknesses: there is no counted premium compatibility-decision market and the decision corpus is nonexclusive. The offer also has a value paradox: at 1,400–1,800 PLN it excludes the part price and the success of the repair, while CNC and distributors already provide model/serial-based compatibility help alongside the parts transaction; broadening the credit enough to change behaviour would increase professional, contractual and potentially insurance-like exposure. A three-customer paid falsification test is possible within 30,000 PLN, but the expected founder-wealth case requires 25,000 annual high-price decisions and software-like margins from a liability-bearing, serial-specific technical operation. The exact revision is not panel-ready because the Founder Wealth Gate fails; the recommendation is to kill this standalone business shape.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Sourced or directly verifiable evidence

- UDT requires enterprise certification for businesses actually installing, servicing, repairing or decommissioning relevant stationary F-gas equipment and requires certified personnel, procedures and records for those activities. FitmentBond says it performs none of those physical acts, so an F-gas enterprise certificate is not clearly required for the certificate-only role; this is not a legal conclusion, and a contracted certified expert does not automatically confer technical authority or certification on FitmentBond. [UDT enterprise certification](https://www.udt.gov.pl/uslugi-udt/szwo-i-f-gazy/certyfikat-dla-przedsiebiorcow)
- The UDT population of more than 16,000 certified Polish F-gas enterprises is a broad outbound ceiling spanning refrigeration, air conditioning and heat pumps. It is not a count of firms repairing post-warranty private-label hydronic heat pumps or buying high-value compatibility decisions.
- The cited 745,000 Polish heat-pump stock is likewise a broad installed-stock ceiling. No current source in the completed research counts the post-warranty private-label hydronic subset, its annual high-value replacement-part events, wrong-part frequency or events for which a paid compatibility opinion is the preferred remedy.
- Public CNC examples range from a 1,400 PLN controller to compressors at 4,410 PLN and 13,021 PLN. Thus a 1,400–1,800 PLN certificate can equal the price of a controller and consume roughly 32–41% of the 4,410 PLN compressor price. The examples prove that high-value parts exist, not the transaction distribution.
- CNC already says it verifies heat-pump part compatibility from model, serial number and nameplate information. Bluon's PartsConnect identifies correct OEM and replacement parts and connects users to existing distributors. Neither source proves complete coverage of the Polish private-label niche, but both establish a close incumbent substitute and a short copy path. [CNC compatibility process](https://cncpompyciepla.pl/czesci-do-pomp-ciepla/) and [Bluon PartsConnect](https://www.bluon.com/partsconnect)
- Public Polish SME liability wording illustrates the coverage problem rather than solving it. Warta's wording recognises product/service liability but excludes pure financial loss and, in the relevant extension, activity involving design, advice, control and opinions; removal/reinstallation protection requires specific extensions and conditions. No binding quotation covers FitmentBond's exact decision certificate and fixed credit. [Warta SME policy wording](https://www.warta.pl/documents/oferta_dla_firmy/WARTA_OWU_Ekstrabiznes_Plus_C7905_int.pdf)
- EPREL and KEYMARK can seed brand/model or some OEM/private-label relations, but they do not provide the serial/date/revision/BOM/connector/firmware/outcome corpus proposed here. UDT service records are held by individual firms, not exposed as a public commercial-use dataset.

### Candidate claims or hypotheses, not evidence

- Three unrelated firms will pay at least 1,200 PLN, and the market will sustain 1,400–1,800 PLN, for a certificate that does not cover the part price, diagnosis, repair result, property damage or consequential loss.
- A written legal opinion will classify the fixed service-error credit as an ordinary service warranty or contractual remedy rather than a regulated insurance product, and a suitable professional-liability policy will be available within the proof budget. The fixed credit is triggered by FitmentBond's own error, which may support a contractual-liability interpretation, but the public record is insufficient to adopt that interpretation.
- A case can be verified for no more than 300 PLN, the average risk reserve can remain 160 PLN, and accepted-case error can stay below 3% while the company chooses precisely the difficult cases that free supplier support could not resolve.
- There are 25,000 annual premium decisions available at the expected price and enough reachable buyers to produce them without heroic penetration. No account count, events per account, country allocation, capture rate or mature CAC supports this volume.
- Signed contributor terms will give FitmentBond durable commercial rights to serial/revision/evidence/outcome records. The contributor's authority to share every underlying record, the GDPR treatment of indirectly identifiable serial/photo/site information, source licences and cross-border rights remain record-specific. Contractual rights would be nonexclusive and would not prevent independent collection of the underlying technical facts.
- One hundred paid outcomes, 30 reusable mappings and 80% of accepted cases resolved without fresh expert work by month 12 will create a material speed/error advantage. These are kill targets, not currently controlled assets.

### Prior reviewer context

The pivot document itself labels FitmentBond the closest paid-proof route but rejects it as a standalone founder-wealth business. That is an internal reviewer conclusion, not market evidence. No PlatformRoot score, panel score or chatbot verdict is imported into this evaluation.

## Overall Score

**42/100.** The weighted points total 83.0 across 20.0 total weight, producing a weighted average of 4.15/10 and 41.5/100 before rounding. This is an advisory evaluator score only; it is not a simulated-panel, working-chat, fresh-chat or validation score.

## Control Point

**Current control point: Tier 3. Potential month-12 asset: Tier 2 only if the stated targets are actually met.** FitmentBond presently controls no unrelated paid decision stream, exclusive mapping, outcome corpus, distribution channel or liability facility. Public identifiers, family vocabulary, hired expertise, legal work and contributor terms are obtainable inputs, but they do not exclude competitors.

The month-12 corpus could become a useful Tier 2 operating asset if 150 unrelated paid certificates yield 100 closed outcomes, 30 repeatedly used provenance-backed mappings, enforceable commercialisation rights and at least 80% resolution without fresh outside expertise. Even then, it is nonexclusive: a payer can use other services, facts can be independently observed, and data rights are constrained by the source record, contributor authority, confidentiality and privacy. It is not Tier 1.

**Strongest copy risk:** CNC or another Polish distributor can add a documented capped compatibility promise to the serial/model verification it already performs, using its inventory, supplier access, technical staff and installer trust. Bluon can add the same promise to PartsConnect after localising the relevant platform families. Either rival needs only the high-frequency families, not the whole aspirational corpus; this is a routine workflow, pricing and underwriting change rather than reconstruction of a scarce asset.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.** The scenario table observes the required reinvestment rules, avoids double-counting, states ownership and uses ordinary 5–6× operating-profit multiples. The conservative case nevertheless produces only about **€3.7 million** of founder equity in years 8–10, far below the €10 million requirement.

The expected case reaches about €12.6 million only by processing **25,000 certificates per year at €400 each**. If all events came from Poland, that is 3.36% of the entire cited 745,000-unit heat-pump stock every year before narrowing to post-warranty, hydronic, private-label, high-value, revision-sensitive failures, before excluding cases with OEM-only software or authority, and before competitor leakage. It also equals 1.56 certificates per year for every firm in the broad 16,000-company F-gas ceiling; because only a subset is eligible, actual events per target firm and required capture would be much higher. If the case instead relies on CEE/EU expansion, it supplies no enumerated eligible stock, account list, country mix, events per account, capture share or acquisition economics.

At 25,000 certificates, the business must complete roughly 100 liability-bearing serial-specific decisions per 250-day work year. Even at the month-12 target of 80% resolution without fresh external expertise, 5,000 annual cases still need fresh expert work and the remaining 20,000 require accountable intake and quality control. The stated 75% gross and 36% operating margins have no bound insurance premium, specialist labour curve, claim administration, adverse-selection sensitivity, customer support or audit cost stack. A 160 PLN risk reserve per 1,600 PLN case can fund only a limited frequency/severity combination, and no observed error rate supports it.

The founder's 70% expected ownership is not inherently implausible, but capital and dilution after proof are not specified for the technical, insurance, QA and cross-border operation. More importantly, the 5× multiple cannot compensate for an unproved event pool and a control point that incumbents can copy through one process change. The strong case repeats the same assumptions at greater scale. Founder wealth therefore fails independently of Reachability.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS narrowly for a falsification test, not for the business model.** The exact plan caps pre-proof cash at 30,000 PLN, below the 40,000 PLN limit, and can remain compatible with the corporate job for three cases. It uses a directly enumerable UDT list, requires three unrelated payers, buys no parts, needs no OEM/importer/distributor agreement and excludes the family company from payment evidence. A founder-led test with a contracted F-gas/electronics expert, counsel and a broker is operationally possible within five hours per day if case acceptance is narrow.

The release sequence is mandatory. Before accepting the first payment, FitmentBond needs: (1) a written Polish opinion on contractual-warranty versus insurance/insurance-distribution treatment; (2) confirmation of whether the exact compatibility-opinion activity triggers any professional or enterprise certification requirement; (3) a binding professional-liability quotation or policy explicitly covering technical compatibility opinions and the fixed service-error credit, rather than a generic broker indication; (4) an expert protocol with named accountable sign-off; and (5) contributor/privacy terms that the submitting firm has authority to grant. The public UDT scope suggests that merely issuing an opinion is distinct from physically repairing the equipment, but that inference is not sufficient for launch.

Commercial reach remains weak rather than absent. The offer asks an unrelated firm to pay 1,200–1,800 PLN to an unknown provider even though the capped credit excludes the part, diagnosis and successful repair and local suppliers provide compatibility help with the transaction. The likely first buyers are cases that incumbent channels could not resolve, creating adverse selection precisely where evidence and error risk are worst. Stop if legal/coverage/expert prerequisites exceed the 30,000 PLN cap, if fewer than three unrelated firms pay by day 180, if no unrelated payer repeats, or if customers require part-price or repair-result coverage. A pass here means the falsification experiment is reachable; it does not justify paneling because the Founder Wealth Gate fails.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is narrow enough to test: two or three expensive revision-sensitive assemblies within one repeated post-warranty private-label family. Its economic size is not established. A candidate that solves only rare stalled cases risks becoming high-skill technical consulting; a candidate that addresses common cases faces free supplier support and the fastest incumbent response.

Expansion to more platform families and EU hydronic markets increases the corpus, but it also multiplies evidence provenance, firmware and connector revisions, specialist protocols, privacy/source permissions, liability territories and outbound channels. FitmentBond expands one family and one liability-bearing decision at a time. CNC, a distributor or Bluon moves inward with existing technical data, inventory, workflow placement and trust, so the expansion is not asymmetric.

Polish language, a public prospect list and the family installer's lawful field vocabulary provide a useful testing advantage. They do not constitute a local adaptation defence against CNC, Polish distributors or a localised Bluon workflow.

## Fatal Objections

1. **No exact annual event denominator supports 25,000 premium decisions.** Broad heat-pump stock and F-gas-company counts are ceilings, not the targeted failure/compatibility transaction pool; no events-per-account or capture model exists.
2. **The price and coverage work against each other.** The four-figure certificate omits the part price and repair outcome, while the capped labour/freight credit is often only modestly larger than the fee. Broadening coverage to create decisive value recreates insurance, liability and capital exposure.
3. **Demand is adversely selected.** Straightforward cases go to free OEM, supplier or distributor support; the cases willing to pay an unknown provider are likely the least documented and most error-prone, undermining the 300 PLN verification cost and 3% error ceiling.
4. **The control point is nonexclusive and copyable through one process change.** CNC, other distributors or Bluon can combine superior existing data and trust with the same documented promise without reproducing the whole corpus.
5. **Professional and insurance boundaries are unresolved.** No legal opinion or bound policy covers the exact technical opinion and service-error credit; public SME terms show relevant advice, opinion and pure-financial-loss exclusions.
6. **The margin model hides accountable technical operations.** Twenty-five thousand annual serial-specific decisions require expert escalation, QA, claims, audit and support at a scale inconsistent with the unsupported 75% gross/36% operating margins.
7. **Data commercialisation rights are incomplete.** Payer terms alone do not establish rights to every underlying manufacturer document, photo, serial, site/customer datum or technician contribution, and the rights do not make the technical facts exclusive.
8. **Founder fit is partial at the decisive trust layer.** The founder can build the evidence system and run a disciplined test, but lacks the existing technical authority, installer trust and insurance relationship on which the paid promise depends.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is that a compatibility record is commercially more useful when it is serial/revision-specific, provenance-linked, attached to a paid accepted-or-declined decision and closed with installation outcome, callback and error data. That insight should be preserved as an operating asset where a business already controls trusted technical or parts flow. It does not make FitmentBond a defensible standalone founder-wealth vehicle under the exact assumptions evaluated here.

## Recommendation

**kill**

The exact shape is trapped between a narrow promise that does not support its four-figure price and a broader promise that recreates the liability and regulatory burden it was designed to remove. Lower pricing makes the already-unproved 25,000-event wealth requirement worse; stronger coverage increases capital and classification risk; and the nonexclusive corpus does not slow an incumbent with existing data and distribution. Do not panel the exact revision.
