# Zero-to-One Candidate: HoseWitness

## One-Sentence Thesis

HoseWitness is a bench-top production-screening system that lets hydraulic-hose assemblers sell a documented “clean at cap” assembly by rinsing each finished mineral-oil-compatible hose through a sealed disposable membrane cassette, imaging the captured debris, and issuing a traceable pass/fail record—without claiming that the result is an ISO particle analysis.

## Founder Fit

The founder's useful advantages are analytical product design, applied AI access, data handling, process discipline, and the ability to build a narrow evidence-backed workflow while retaining corporate employment. The first product does not require the founder to invent a new sensor or filtration chemistry: it combines controlled fluid handling, a repeatable disposable cassette, standard optics, image analysis, and a production record.

The founder's material gaps are equally important:

- no established hydraulic-industry reputation or privileged distributor access;
- no demonstrated experience designing fluid-power equipment, selecting compatible seals and materials, or managing industrial product liability;
- no existing route into hose workshops or OEM quality teams;
- no right to assert that an optical membrane image is equivalent to an ISO cleanliness result.

Those gaps are addressed through ordinary paid inputs rather than speculative partnerships: a contract fluid-power engineer reviews the circuit and compatibility envelope; an independent contamination laboratory supplies reference measurements; a product engineer builds the low-pressure fixture; and two unrelated workshops are recruited through direct outbound for paid comparison pilots. The founder must not count a family company, friendly test, free trial, or contractor opinion as external demand proof.

This is executable alongside employment during the first 180 days at roughly 15–20 founder hours per week. It becomes a poor founder fit if customers require a founder with formal laboratory authority, if selling depends on personal status rather than a blind comparison, or if the physical product demands continuous field service before recurring consumable revenue is proved.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first customer is not the average emergency-hose counter. It is a stationary hose workshop or small hose-production cell making repeated, mineral-oil-compatible assemblies for contamination-sensitive mobile machinery, hydraulic test rigs, industrial maintenance, rebuilders, and small equipment manufacturers. Its buyer is the workshop owner, quality manager, or production manager who can price a documented-clean assembly or who already bears rework, warranty, commissioning, and customer-audit costs.

The end customer's real job is not “count particles for scientific interest.” It is: **avoid installing a new hose that introduces debris into an expensive hydraulic system, and have defensible evidence that the finished assembly was clean and sealed when it left the workshop.**

The current workflow commonly combines some of the following:

- cut, skive, crimp, visually inspect, and cap;
- blow a foam projectile through the hose, sometimes with an exit-verification counter;
- clean a batch, assume the cleaning method stayed effective, and retain no per-assembly evidence;
- send occasional samples to a laboratory or run a manual patch test;
- rely on supplier reputation, operator habit, or a signed production sheet.

Parker's hose handbook says most hose contamination is introduced during production, especially cutting and skiving, and recommends cleaning and plugging assemblies before use. Parker and Ultra Clean already sell projectile-cleaning and sealing workflows. Ultra Clean's verification equipment verifies that a projectile exited and logs cleaning cycles; it does not, on the public product description, quantify the residual debris left in the finished assembly. This creates a narrow opening between “the cleaning action occurred” and “this particular capped assembly produced a low-debris screen.”

The pain is commercially strong only where one of these conditions is present:

- a customer specification or audit asks for documented cleanliness;
- contamination-related commissioning or warranty incidents are expensive enough to support a premium SKU;
- the workshop wants to differentiate from local competitors with evidence rather than a generic “cleaned” promise;
- the same production cell makes enough relevant hoses to spread the reader cost over at least several thousand annual screens.

The proposal must not assume that every hose needs this record. Commodity emergency repairs, incompatible fluids, low-volume counters, and customers unwilling to pay for documented cleanliness are outside the first market.

## Product Wedge

The first product is a restricted production-screening workflow for straight or gently curved, mineral-oil-compatible hydraulic-hose assemblies within a validated diameter and length envelope. The provisional starting envelope is 1/4–1 inch internal diameter and up to 2 metres long; the final range is set by testing, not marketing.

The workflow is:

1. The operator connects the newly crimped assembly to keyed adapters in a guarded, low-pressure bench loop.
2. A metered quantity of filtered, low-viscosity mineral test oil passes through the hose using a fixed quick-rinse recipe.
3. The effluent flows through a sealed, serialized disposable membrane cassette.
4. The cassette moves into a controlled-light optical dock. The system images the membrane and scores debris count, covered area, size bands, and suspicious metal- or rubber-like morphology against the validated recipe.
5. The software returns pass, fail, or “manual/reference review required,” records the assembly identifier, recipe, blank check, operator, cassette lot, image, and timestamp, and prints a label.
6. The hose is subjected to a validated filtered-air purge, capped immediately, and sealed. The test record refers to the state at capping.

The product claim is deliberately narrow:

> HoseWitness is a per-assembly production screen correlated to a purchased reference method within a defined hose and fluid envelope. It is not an accredited laboratory analysis, does not itself assign an ISO cleanliness code, and does not prove that every contaminant has been removed.

ISO/TS 18409:2018 describes two methods for collecting fluid samples from hoses and hose assemblies for solid-particle contamination. The standard states that the methods are recommended for statistical validation of other cleaning methods suited to systematic batch processing, while analysis of the collected samples is outside its scope. HoseWitness uses that distinction honestly: external reference analysis validates the commercial screen, while the commercial product focuses on quick, repeatable production decisions.

The initial offer is not a complete factory platform. It is a paid 100-hose comparison pilot followed, if successful, by a reader, starter adapter kit, cassette packs, annual verification service, and production-record software.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Hydraulic-hose cleanliness is usually sold as a cleaning process, a filtration product, a laboratory test, or a broad quality promise. The commercially under-served moment is after the finished assembly has been crimped and cleaned but before it is capped: the customer wants evidence tied to the exact item that will enter the machine.

The insight is that a workshop does not need an expensive universal laboratory if it can create a narrow, validated premium production class. A repeatable screen can be more valuable than a richer laboratory result when it is:

- fast enough for the production bench;
- attached to the individual assembly;
- priced below the premium or avoided-risk value;
- explicit about its application envelope;
- periodically correlated to an independent reference rather than supported only by the vendor's algorithm.

This changes behavior only if the workshop can monetize or contract around the result. The pilot must therefore test two linked hypotheses: whether the screen tracks meaningful contamination, and whether the workshop can obtain a purchase order, specification acceptance, or at least a stated paid premium for the documented-clean SKU.

### Incumbent comparison

| Existing option | What it proves well | Gap HoseWitness tests | Copy or substitution risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ultra Clean projectile systems and exit verification | A cleaning projectile was fired and exited; cleaning-cycle data can be logged | Residual debris captured from the specific finished hose | Very high: Ultra Clean could add a capture cassette or partner with an imaging vendor |
| Parker pellet flushing, caps, and workshop network | Established cleaning and sealing workflow with trusted distribution | Item-level residual-debris evidence | Very high: Parker can bundle a similar screen if customer demand is visible |
| HYDAC ContaminationTest Unit | Technical-cleanliness extraction for components | A cheaper, hose-specific production station and disposable workflow | High: existing contamination-equipment engineering and customer trust |
| Donaldson or MP Filtri patch/imaging kits | Visual or approximate fluid-contamination assessment | Keyed hose recipe, sealed cassette, item record, cycle-time discipline | High: components are available and the basic optical concept is not novel |
| Occasional external laboratory analysis | Independent, richer reference result | Routine per-assembly economics and speed | Low as direct substitute for every hose, but essential as the reference method |
| Visual inspection or signed checklist | Cheap process evidence | Physical output from the exact hose | Easy status quo; strongest substitution if customers do not value the result |

HoseWitness is therefore not defensible merely because it has a camera or AI classifier. It earns a position only if the disposable geometry, validated extraction recipes, blanks and calibration discipline, adapter system, installed workflow, and result corpus compound faster than an incumbent can reproduce them. Until that is demonstrated, the concept should be treated as an owner-buildable but commercially unproved physical control point.

## Underused Momentum

Several existing forces make the test timely without constituting proof of demand:

- modern hydraulic components use tighter clearances and vendors explicitly warn that solid contamination causes failures;
- hose makers already spend money on projectile cleaning, sealing, filtration, cleanliness testing, and production traceability;
- low-cost controlled optics and edge image analysis make a consistent membrane screen cheaper than it was when inspection depended on a skilled microscope operator;
- machine builders and maintenance customers increasingly expect item-level digital records rather than an undocumented workshop habit;
- large hose-service networks already provide dense branch lists from which a focused founder can identify potential high-volume benches without privileged introductions.

The underused commercial layer is a purchasable, per-assembly evidence product between cleaning equipment and laboratory testing. None of these trends excuses the need to prove compatibility, screen validity, paid demand, and the scale denominator.

## Beachhead Market

The initial beachhead is **Polish and nearby Central European stationary hose-production benches making at least 2,500 mineral-oil-compatible, cleanliness-sensitive assemblies per year for industrial machinery, mobile-equipment rebuilders, hydraulic test systems, and small OEM programmes.**

This is narrower than “all hydraulic workshops.” A prospect qualifies only if:

- it has a repeatable bench process rather than mostly emergency field work;
- at least one customer segment can pay for or require documented cleanliness;
- the validated hose and fluid envelope covers a meaningful share of its volume;
- a quality or production owner can authorize a paid comparison pilot;
- the workshop can isolate a premium documented-clean SKU while evidence is accumulated.

The first geographic ring is Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, and eastern Germany, where travel is manageable and direct support can be provided without a national service network. English, Polish, and later German documentation are sufficient. No regulatory exclusivity or “Poland barrier” is claimed.

Public branch networks establish that this is a real, distributed workflow, but they do not establish the eligible denominator. Parker states that more than 1,000 ParkerStores each have a hose-assembly workshop; Hydroscand lists more than 240 hose-service branches; and Pirtek lists more than 200 service centres across Europe. That is an observable floor of more than 1,440 named stationary locations across three networks, before Fluiconnecto, independents, and OEM production cells. Many will be ineligible, duplicated with customer groups, too low-volume, or inaccessible. The larger scale model is conditional on constructing and auditing an account-level list of at least 5,000 eligible high-volume benches or production cells; it is not inferred from a generic global workshop count.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

The proposed commercial model combines a reader sale, recurring disposable cassettes, and annual verification. Prices are hypotheses to be tested in paid pilots and procurement conversations.

| Item | Proposed net price | Direct cost assumption at early scale | Gross contribution | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bench reader, pump/control module, starter adapters, optics, training | €6,500 | €3,400 | €3,100 / 48% | Contract-built; no custom high-pressure pump in the first version |
| Serialized membrane cassette, including allocated fluid/filter/cap/label consumables | €5.00 each | €2.00 | €3.00 / 60% | Cassette cost must fall through tooling and volume; shipping is included in the cost assumption |
| Annual verification, software record export, and support | €900 per reader | €300 | €600 / 67% | Software cannot be the only reason to retain the system |
| Paid comparison pilot | 6,000–9,000 PLN | 3,000–5,500 PLN delivery cost | Positive but modest | Includes 100 production screens and a blinded reference subset; exact laboratory quote required before sale |

At 2,500 screened assemblies per year, a workshop would spend roughly €12,500 on cassettes plus €900 annual verification, after the initial reader purchase. Four minutes per test would also consume about 167 operator hours. That expense is irrational for a commodity hose. The economic case requires the workshop to earn or protect at least roughly €15–€25 per documented assembly through a premium SKU, customer contract, avoided rework, warranty protection, or retained account. For example, a €15 premium produces €37,500 of annual incremental revenue at 2,500 units; after €13,400 of HoseWitness consumables/service and an internally costed €4–€6 of labour per assembly, a positive workshop contribution remains. Both the premium and labour figure must be observed, not assumed.

The vendor's first-year gross contribution from a typical 2,500-test customer is approximately €11,200: €3,100 from the reader, €7,500 from cassettes, and €600 from annual service. Direct customer-acquisition spend must stay below €3,000 per installed site, and on-site commissioning must fall below one engineer-day after the first ten installations. Those limits create room for direct sales before distributor economics are considered.

The company does not initially finance readers from its own balance sheet. A monthly lease can be offered only through a standard equipment-finance provider or after recurring cash flow exists; a financing partnership is not required for the first sale.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

### First ten

The first ten are acquired through account-level outbound, not advertising or an unspecified distributor agreement:

1. Build a list of 150 stationary workshops and production cells from public ParkerStore, Hydroscand, Pirtek, Fluiconnecto, independent distributor, machine-builder, and hydraulic-rebuilder directories in Poland and the first geographic ring.
2. Qualify by phone for annual relevant hose volume, cleaning method, costly contamination incidents, customer specifications, and authority to buy a 6,000–9,000 PLN comparison pilot.
3. Offer a fixed-scope “100 assemblies: process proof versus physical output” pilot. The workshop keeps all images and reference results even if it does not buy the reader.
4. Require a non-refundable payment or budget-backed purchase order before on-site testing. A free design-partner letter is not paid proof.
5. Convert the two initial pilots only after the blinded reference comparison and production-time test pass.
6. Use the first two independent case records to sell eight more sites in the same use case. Customer names may remain private; the method, blinded error rates, cycle time, and economics must be publishable in anonymized form.

If 150 well-qualified accounts do not produce two paid pilots, the founder should stop or change the customer and offer rather than build a better reader.

### First hundred

The first 100 installed customers are reached through a named-account sales motion organized by machinery cluster and customer specification:

- 40 high-volume independent or chain workshops serving premium industrial/mobile machinery;
- 30 OEM or contract hose-production cells with item-level QA requirements;
- 20 rebuilders and hydraulic-service centres handling contamination-sensitive systems;
- 10 reference-driven accounts reached through laboratory, engineer, or customer referral—but contracted directly, without relying on a channel partnership.

At least 70 of the first 100 must reorder cassettes within nine months, and at least 50 must screen 2,500 or more assemblies on a trailing annualized basis. A lower-use reader base does not support the wealth model. A distributor may later reduce acquisition cost, but the plan is viable only if the founder can sell the first 10 and establish repeatable direct economics independently.

## Primary Control Point

The primary control point is the **owned production-screening consumable and its validated operating system**:

- sealed cassette geometry and flow path designed to capture a reproducible fraction of debris without handling the membrane;
- keyed adapters, blank-check procedure, flow/volume recipe, purge, and immediate-capping sequence for each validated hose class;
- controlled optical dock, calibration target, firmware, and pass/fail logic;
- an owner-created physical specimen bank containing deliberately seeded and independently measured reference hoses, blanks, membranes, and failure modes;
- cassette lot traceability and the per-assembly evidence record;
- accumulated field correlation between screen output, reference measurements, hose recipes, and customer acceptance.

The founder can secure the initial version within 12 months because the critical components can be purchased, the geometry can be designed and tooled under contract, and the reference corpus can be created from founder-owned specimens and bought laboratory services. No customer must donate data or grant exclusive access for the first usable asset to exist.

Control-point classification at generation is **Tier 2: an owner-buildable physical consumable and workflow whose commercial power is not yet proved.** It can move toward Tier 1 only if the cassette/recipe system is materially more repeatable and economical than generic patch testing, customers reorder consumables, and the resulting installed workflow and corpus make substitution costly. The strongest copy risk is an established cleaning or filtration supplier adding a disposable capture membrane and reader through one product-development cycle. Patents, if available, are supporting friction rather than the thesis.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

The cold start requires five resources, each with a specific acquisition mechanism and proof:

| Required resource | How it is secured | Why an unknown founder can secure it | Proof before serious capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safe, repeatable low-pressure extraction circuit | Fixed-fee design review and build by a contract fluid-power/product engineer using catalog components | This is purchased engineering, not privileged access; the first envelope is deliberately narrow | Pressure/flow logs, blank runs, spill and fault tests, materials list, and written design review |
| Disposable membrane cassette | Machined/printed alpha units, followed only after proof by a small pilot tool or contract-converted cassette | Prototype manufacturers sell this work to unknown companies | Replicate captures, leak tests, handling comparison, preliminary costed manufacturing quote, owned drawings and tooling terms |
| Reference truth | Purchase a defined set of particle/patch analyses from an independent contamination laboratory | Laboratory analysis is a paid service; no exclusivity is needed | Blinded report on at least 30 assemblies/conditions plus blank controls |
| Initial corpus | Buy hose, fittings, oil, rubber/wire debris standards, and create controlled clean-to-dirty specimens | The founder owns the specimens and images; no customer data is necessary | Versioned image set, contamination ladder, repeatability and inter-operator results |
| Paid customer access | Directly prospect 150 named production benches and sell two fixed-scope comparison pilots | The offer does not ask for trust in a certification claim; it asks the customer to compare its own output and keep the evidence | Two unrelated non-refundable payments or budget-backed purchase orders, successful delivery, and one conversion/reorder signal |

The founder should design the first proof around mineral-oil-compatible hoses only. Water-glycol, phosphate-ester, food, oxygen, gas, brake-fluid, aerospace, and other safety- or compatibility-sensitive applications are excluded until independently engineered and validated. The test fluid, seals, purge, waste handling, and any residual oil must be accepted in writing by each pilot customer for the selected assemblies.

The founder is not allowed to treat a clean-looking membrane as validation. Before sale of a production reader, a blinded set must show that the screen can distinguish the agreed high-contamination condition from acceptable output and that blanks do not dominate the signal. The reference method may be a purchased gravimetric, microscopic, or particle-count/patch analysis appropriate to the experiment; its exact interpretation must be specified by the laboratory.

## Distribution Advantage

The first distribution advantage is asymmetric specificity, not reach. Broad contamination-equipment suppliers sell across many fluids and components; HoseWitness can concentrate on one hose envelope, one production moment, one premium SKU, and one economic claim. This permits a smaller founder to publish useful benchmark evidence, adapter instructions, cycle-time data, and a purchase calculator for a narrowly defined buyer.

The repeatable acquisition asset is a structured account map containing:

- production location and bench count;
- annual eligible volume and hose envelope;
- installed cleaning method;
- customer/OEM cleanliness requirement;
- buyer, quality owner, and trigger event;
- paid-pilot result, observed premium, and reorder behaviour.

Direct sales is the initial route because it proves whether customer economics work without sharing margin or waiting for a partner. Later distribution options include hydraulic distributors, cleaning-equipment sellers, laboratories, and private-label cassette supply. They are acceleration paths, not existential assumptions. No scale forecast counts partner revenue before a signed contract and demonstrated sell-through.

The moat strengthens if a machine builder begins naming a HoseWitness result profile—or an equivalent validated profile—in supplier requirements. The company must not represent such acceptance as independent certification or assume that an OEM will adopt it.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

The initial advantage is a faster, item-linked production decision than outsourced analysis and richer physical evidence than a projectile-exit log. The product can become difficult to replace through five compounding layers:

1. **Validated recipes:** hose length, diameter, reinforcement, flow, rinse volume, blank, purge, and threshold profiles that have independent reference correlation.
2. **Consumable consistency:** lot-controlled cassette flow and optical background that reduce the variation of manual filters.
3. **Installed workflow:** adapters, operator training, labels, customer records, and downstream specifications that make switching more than a reader purchase.
4. **Failure corpus:** rubber, wire, dust, machining, fluid, and handling signatures tied to corrective actions.
5. **Distribution evidence:** a named account map and proof that workshops can price documented cleanliness.

Expansion is sequential:

- **Wedge:** mineral-oil-compatible hoses within a narrow size/length envelope at stationary high-volume benches.
- **Recipe expansion:** longer and larger hoses only after reference validation and acceptable extraction repeatability.
- **Production-cell integration:** barcode/API connection, automated cassette handling, and line-level statistics for OEM hose cells.
- **Clean-screen-seal cell:** integrate or interoperate with an existing cleaning step so the operator receives one traceable result and caps immediately.
- **Adjacent components:** rigid tubes and simple manifolds only where the same cassette and optical control point survives; do not become a generic cleanliness consultancy.
- **Additional fluids:** separate material, safety, purge, and reference programmes; no universal-fluid marketing.

The concept should be killed rather than expanded if customers buy only occasional consultant-led tests, if every new hose family requires bespoke founder labour, or if the cassette can be replaced by a generic filter plus phone photograph without losing meaningful repeatability.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

The local advantage is operational rather than legal:

- Central Europe contains dense machinery, maintenance, rebuilding, and hose-service activity within founder-drivable distance;
- Polish engineering, prototyping, optics integration, and pilot tooling can be purchased at costs compatible with the 100,000 PLN validation ceiling;
- an unknown founder can target fragmented independent workshops and smaller OEM cells that are less attractive to a global equipment vendor's enterprise sales force;
- multilingual EU documentation and local commissioning can make the first ten accounts easier to serve than from a remote global product organization.

There is no formal barrier preventing Parker, Ultra Clean, HYDAC, Donaldson, or MP Filtri from entering. The defense must be a lead in recipes, installed workflow, account knowledge, and evidence—not “made in Poland.”

The commercial unit must also comply with applicable EU product rules. Depending on the final design and placing-on-market date, review must cover the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, applicable from 20 January 2027, the EMC Directive, RoHS, and potentially the Low Voltage and Pressure Equipment Directives. A low-pressure architecture may reduce pressure-equipment burden but does not decide legal scope. A qualified conformity adviser must document which rules apply before commercial shipment.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid installation can strengthen the next one in ways that do not require ownership of confidential customer production data:

- cassette volume reduces unit cost and improves lot control;
- more validated recipes expand the sellable hose envelope;
- reference comparisons improve thresholds and reveal false reassurance;
- operator and blank failures improve fixture poka-yoke and training;
- anonymized economic evidence makes the premium SKU easier to buy;
- installed labels and customer specifications create workflow switching costs;
- the account map reveals which industries and trigger events support high utilization.

Customer data is opt-in and contractually scoped. The core system must work from the founder-owned specimen bank and purchased reference results. Otherwise, the company would face the same prospective-data weakness as inspection ideas that have no usable asset before customer cooperation.

The strongest compounding loop is:

> more paid high-volume benches → more cassette volume and reference cases → lower cost and better recipe confidence → stronger workshop economics and buyer trust → more paid high-volume benches.

That loop fails if utilization is low, if results do not correlate across sites, or if incumbents bundle an equivalent screen at negligible incremental price.

## Founder Wealth Model

These are conditional operating scenarios, not probabilities or valuation promises. They use one observable public network floor and a separate hard denominator test. Before capital beyond initial proof, the company must construct an account-level list of at least 5,000 eligible high-volume stationary benches or production cells across reachable and later export markets. “Eligible” means a plausible 2,500+ annual screen volume and a documented-cleanliness buyer. Public branch totals alone do not satisfy this test.

All scenarios avoid double-counting: founder wealth is calculated from founder-owned equity value after net financial obligations. Optional employment-period distributions are excluded. Retained earnings that support company value are not also counted as personal cash.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

The first compounding engine is cassette gross profit, not consulting hours. Cash is allocated in this order:

1. maintain cassette stock, warranty reserve, reference checks, and customer support;
2. reduce cassette cost and variation through owned tooling, lot QA, and dual-source manufacturing;
3. validate the next high-volume hose recipe using founder-owned specimens and independent analysis;
4. build account-level direct distribution in one adjacent machinery cluster;
5. automate assembly, optical QA, and production-cell integration only after sustained utilization;
6. add sales and field service only when the preceding cohort pays back within 12 months.

During the corporate-employment phase, 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution is retained and reinvested in the conservative and expected cases. The founder may take no more than 5%, and those optional payments are not included in wealth. The strong scenario assumes 100% reinvestment during scale-up.

Capital efficiency depends on selling cassette packs in advance, avoiding self-financed reader leases, and using contract manufacturing before a factory is justified. A custom automated production line, large international inventory, or a national service team is prohibited until multiple cohorts reorder with viable contribution economics.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

The reachable outcome is two unrelated paid 100-hose comparison pilots, successful initial delivery, and one purchase/reorder or budget-backed continuation within 180 days while founder cash at risk stays below 40,000 PLN.

### Days 1–30: falsify the physics cheaply

- Pay a fluid-power engineer to define the narrow hose/fluid envelope, circuit, adapters, purge, spill controls, and test-fluid compatibility.
- Obtain written quotes and sample requirements from at least two independent contamination-analysis providers; buy a small reference set.
- Assemble an open-loop alpha rig from catalog components and machined/printed cassette bodies.
- Run blanks and seeded debris ladders across at least three hose constructions.
- Interview 20 qualified production/quality buyers about volume, current cleaning, customer specifications, labour, premium, and purchase authority. Do not pitch a broad platform.

### Days 31–60: prove repeatability and define the paid offer

- Complete at least 100 internal runs including blanks, clean repeats, deliberately contaminated conditions, adapter changes, and two operators.
- Freeze an alpha cassette, controlled-light imaging setup, and one extraction recipe.
- Commission a blinded reference comparison on at least 30 assemblies/conditions.
- Require, provisionally, at least 95% detection of the pre-agreed high-contamination condition, no more than 10% false rejects at the commercial threshold, rank correlation of at least 0.8 with the selected reference measure, and blank signal below 20% of the pass/fail threshold. The laboratory and engineer may replace these with more appropriate pre-registered measures before the blind is opened.
- Set the fixed pilot scope, compatibility exclusions, liability terms, and 6,000–9,000 PLN price.

### Days 61–100: obtain external paid proof

- Prospect at least 150 named accounts; speak with at least 30 qualified decision-makers.
- Secure two unrelated, non-refundable paid pilots or budget-backed purchase orders. Neither can be a family company, employer-affiliated favour, contractor, or reseller speculation.
- Confirm that each workshop has at least 2,500 eligible annual assemblies and can identify the downstream buyer or avoided cost for documented cleanliness.
- Build two customer-specific adapter sets only after payment.

### Days 101–150: deliver and test value capture

- Screen 100 real production assemblies at each site, with customer-approved test fluid and a blinded reference subset.
- Measure total operator cycle time, blank/failure rate, added contamination, purge/residue, intervention time, and result consistency.
- Ask the workshop to quote or sell a documented-clean SKU to at least one downstream customer, or obtain a written budget/specification decision explaining the economic value.
- Deliver the report and records even if the product fails; no selective removal of bad comparisons.

### Days 151–180: require a commercial continuation

- Obtain at least one paid reader/starter-cassette order, paid extended pilot, or cassette reorder from an independent customer.
- Confirm positive pilot contribution after travel, laboratory work, cassettes, and contractor time.
- Produce a costed beta design, compliance plan, and 12-month resource-control record.
- Stop before beta tooling if physics, paid demand, utilization, or workshop value capture is not proven.

This plan does not require leaving employment, building the complete product, signing a distributor, obtaining a certification, or risking most founder capital.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder may leave corporate employment only when all of the following are true:

- at least ten independent paying customers, with no single customer providing more than 25% of trailing revenue;
- at least six customers have reordered cassettes or are on contracted recurring purchase schedules;
- trailing six-month contribution economics are positive after cassette production, shipping, warranty, travel, contractor support, and attributable acquisition cost;
- a named-account channel produces qualified opportunities and paid customers at a repeatable rate, with cash CAC below €3,000 per installed site or payback below 12 months;
- at least five installed sites are annualizing at 2,500+ screens and customer-level labour/value economics remain positive;
- the cassette and reader have second-source or documented continuity plans and an agreed product-conformity path;
- the founder has at least 12 months of personal living runway outside company working capital;
- the next 12 months of business cash need is covered by receipts, committed purchase orders, retained cash, or financing that does not endanger personal solvency.

A high advisory or validation score cannot waive these gates. One prestigious logo, a grant, an unsigned distributor conversation, or a large free trial does not qualify.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If HoseWitness fails, the founder should stop cleanly rather than turn it into generic hydraulic consulting. Reusable assets include:

- a documented corpus of hose-debris images, blanks, materials, and reference results owned by the company;
- safe low-pressure fluid-handling, controlled-optics, cassette, and fixture design knowledge;
- a qualified account map of high-volume hose-production locations and customer specifications;
- evidence on which buyers will pay for item-level cleanliness records;
- contract-manufacturing quotes, compliance scoping, and a tested industrial outbound process;
- potentially reusable optical inspection software and lot-control methods for another owner-controlled consumable.

Possible recovery routes, only after evidence, are licensing the cassette/optics workflow to an established cleaning-equipment company, selling the dataset and fixtures as a validation asset, or applying the owned consumable to a different component-cleanliness niche. These are salvage options, not assumed wealth paths.

The failure cost remains bounded because the founder does not buy a factory, carry large inventory, leave employment, or rely on a multi-year certification programme during proof.

## Critical Evidence

Current evidence supports the existence of the workflow and the gap, but not yet the product's performance or market demand:

- ISO/TS 18409:2018 defines fluid-sample collection methods for assessing solid-particle contamination in hoses and hose assemblies and distinguishes sample collection from analysis and systematic batch cleaning validation.
- Parker's hose technical material identifies cutting and skiving as major contamination-entry points and recommends cleaning and plugging assemblies before use.
- Parker and Ultra Clean publicly offer projectile/pellet cleaning, exit verification, and sealing products, demonstrating existing spend and the direct incumbent threat.
- HYDAC markets a stationary ContaminationTest Unit for technical cleanliness of components; Donaldson and MP Filtri sell patch-test or patch-imaging equipment, demonstrating that membrane-based contamination review is established rather than magical.
- Named public service networks establish more than 1,440 stationary workshop locations across ParkerStore, Hydroscand, and Pirtek, but do not establish how many have adequate eligible volume or willingness to pay.
- No source yet proves that a sub-five-minute HoseWitness cassette screen will correlate adequately with reference results, that a mineral-oil rinse and purge is acceptable to target customers, or that customers will pay €5 per screen plus labour. Those are the first decision-critical experiments.

## Critical Hypotheses

1. A fixed quick-rinse recipe and cassette can produce a repeatable signal from the selected hose envelope despite length, geometry, and operator variation.
2. The signal can identify a pre-agreed high-contamination condition with acceptable false-pass and false-reject rates when compared blindly with a purchased reference analysis.
3. Blank contamination from the rig, adapters, oil, cassette, and environment stays materially below the commercial threshold.
4. The test fluid and filtered-air purge do not create unacceptable compatibility, residue, warranty, or safety risk for the first mineral-oil applications.
5. Median operator cycle time is four minutes or less and the 95th percentile is below five minutes after training.
6. At least two unrelated workshops will pay 6,000–9,000 PLN for a 100-hose comparison before the production product exists.
7. A qualifying workshop can charge at least a €15 documented-clean premium, retain a customer specification, or identify avoided costs that support a €5 cassette and labour.
8. At least 70% of installed early customers reorder cassettes within nine months and high-value sites annualize at 2,500+ screens.
9. Cassette landed direct cost can fall to €2.00 or less with modest tooling and repeatable lot quality.
10. The founder can enumerate and substantiate at least 5,000 eligible high-volume benches or production cells before significant scale capital; a generic global branch estimate is insufficient.
11. The recipe/cassette/workflow system creates more customer value and switching cost than a generic filter holder and phone image.
12. Incumbents do not bundle an adequate equivalent so cheaply and credibly that a focused entrant cannot retain the beachhead.

## Kill Criteria

Kill HoseWitness, rather than cosmetically revising it, if any of these occur:

- the blinded reference comparison fails the pre-registered discrimination and repeatability criteria after one technically justified recipe revision;
- two or more engineers/laboratories conclude that the proposed production screen cannot support a stable interpretation across the narrow hose envelope;
- blanks exceed 20% of the pass/fail threshold or field blank drift cannot be controlled by the cassette and operating procedure;
- the test introduces debris, leakage, damage, or a residue/purge risk that target customers will not accept;
- median trained cycle time exceeds five minutes and customers will not pay a premium sufficient to cover the labour;
- fewer than two unrelated customers pay after 150 named, qualified accounts are approached and at least 30 decision-maker conversations occur;
- no paid customer can identify an end buyer, customer specification, or avoided cost supporting at least a €15 premium or equivalent value;
- pilot variable economics are negative and cannot plausibly reach a €5 cassette price with at least 60% cassette gross margin;
- early readers are used fewer than 1,000 times per year and there is no identifiable higher-volume segment;
- fewer than 70% of the first 20 installations reorder within nine months;
- an incumbent releases a bundled equivalent that removes the measurable workflow advantage before HoseWitness has meaningful recipe, specification, or installed-base lock-in;
- the system remains a consultant-operated test with no repeatable consumable workflow;
- commercial beta requires unresolved pressure, machinery, fluid-compatibility, waste, or product-liability exposure outside the founder's capital envelope;
- an account-level denominator study cannot substantiate at least 5,000 eligible sites/cells or the recalculated conservative founder-equity value falls below €12 million before personal-liability adjustment;
- the model requires multiple speculative partnerships, formal laboratory authority, or indefinite founder labour to sell.

- Founder remains product and commercial owner.
- One part-time contract product engineer supports beta and manufacturing transfer.
- One fluid-power adviser remains available for controlled design reviews, not routine delivery.
