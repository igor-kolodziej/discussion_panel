# Zero-to-One Evaluation: PlatformRoot

Advisory evaluation only. This is not an independent panel score, chatbot score, validation result, or confirmation. PlatformRoot is evaluated exactly as written; no revised business has been designed here.

## Diagnosis

PlatformRoot identifies a real and costly repair failure mode and improves on a generic parts catalog by combining cross-badge ancestry, revision evidence, field outcomes, a prepaid basket, and a wrong-fit refund. The family installer gives the founder an unusually useful learning seed, while the three-unrelated-firm rule correctly prevents that relationship from masquerading as customer proof. However, the conservative wealth case requires 2,000 mature accounts to generate 40,000 annual high-value baskets from a beachhead whose actual installer count, private-label installed base, major-repair incidence, basket distribution, and repeat frequency have not been measured; the cited 16,000-company F-gas register is a broad prospecting universe, not this denominator. Paid proof is narrowly reachable, but the founder-wealth gate fails and the proposed graph is currently a prospective Tier 2 asset facing much larger incumbent data, inventory, workflow, and distribution positions.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Sourced evidence

- UDT exposes a public register of certified enterprises covering stationary refrigeration, air-conditioning, and heat-pump work. Its cited 2026 bulletin reports more than 16,000 certified enterprises, but the category combines several trades and does not identify which firms independently repair post-warranty hydronic air-to-water heat pumps, which work on private-label units, or which have enough eligible cases to buy 20 baskets per year. The register supports enumerated outbound, not the founder-wealth denominator. [UDT registers](https://www.udt.gov.pl/uslugi-udt/szwo-i-f-gazy/rejestry) and [UDT bulletin](https://www.udt.gov.pl/images/kampania/biuletyn/pdf/INSPEKTOR_OZE_WCAG.pdf)
- EHPA reported about 745,000 Polish heat pumps in May 2025 and said only about 5% of Polish homes had one. It also reported a 35% fall in 2024 sales, which supports a meaningful installed stock but not a growing flow of high-value post-warranty repair baskets. [EHPA Poland market note](https://ehpa.org/news-and-resources/press-releases/steady-heat-pump-subsidy-scheme-key-to-cleaner-polish-air/)
- The JRC country fiche reports 207,992 Polish heat-pump sales in 2022. This supports the candidate's large aging cohort, but neither the JRC nor EHPA source gives the private-label share, failure rate, repair-versus-replacement rate, part value, independent-service share, or basket frequency. [JRC Poland heat-pump fiche](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC137131/JRC137131_020.pdf)
- Bluon currently claims technical documents, BOMs, specifications, and cross-references across more than 20 million model numbers and 200 brands, plus an AI trained on 135,000 verified service-call solutions. It sells contractor, FSM, API, distributor-search, and compatible-parts-routing products. These are company claims rather than independently audited coverage, but they demonstrate an incumbent product and data shape extremely close to PlatformRoot's expansion path. [Bluon](https://www.bluon.com/)
- Bluon's April 2026 PartsConnect announcement says its database had grown to more than 30 million model numbers across more than 240 OEMs and that contractors can identify compatible replacements and buy from local distributors. The release is US-oriented and does not prove coverage of Polish private-label hydronic pumps, but it shows that compatible-part identification tied to supplier availability is already a funded incumbent workflow, not an empty category. [PartsConnect announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bluon-launches-partsconnect-enabling-hvac-contractors-to-find-and-buy-compatible-replacements-sold-by-their-local-distributor-302739588.html)
- CNC Pompy Ciepła advertises about 1,000 listed parts, thousands of original components across major brands, direct ordering from manufacturers, nationwide/cross-border delivery, and help selecting parts for older or less common units. These are CNC's own claims, but they establish a local technical-sales and inventory competitor whose phone support and installed trust must be beaten. [CNC parts offer](https://cncpompyciepla.pl/czesci-do-pomp-ciepla/)
- EU product-compliance guidance says a distributor must know applicable conformity, labeling, traceability, documentation, and safety requirements; an importer must verify conformity and technical documentation; and a seller that markets a product under its own name can assume manufacturer responsibilities. Product obligations therefore vary materially between an original EU-sourced part, a third-country import, and a PlatformRoot-branded or substantially modified equivalent. [EU product-compliance guidance](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-requirements/compliance/index_en.htm) and [EU importer/distributor guidance](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/goods/ce-marking/importers-and-distributors_en)
- Directive (EU) 2024/2853 applies to products placed on the market after 8 December 2026. It includes importers in the liable-operator chain, can treat a substantial modifier as a manufacturer, and can expose a distributor when an EU liable operator cannot be identified. National contractual, tort, conformity, and existing product-liability rules also remain relevant; a B2B disclaimer cannot be assumed to cap every third-party injury or property claim. [Directive (EU) 2024/2853](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/2024-11-18/eng)
- EU guidance recognizes possible copyright and sui-generis protection for an original or substantially invested-in database. That may help PlatformRoot protect its compiled graph, but it does not automatically grant a right to reproduce OEM manuals, BOMs, firmware, supplier databases, photos, or confidential service materials used as inputs. [EU database-protection guidance](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/running-business/intellectual-property/database-protection/index_en.htm)

### Arithmetic that is internally correct

- The conservative case's 40,000 orders at €1,260 produce €50.4 million principal revenue.
- At 20% merchandise gross margin on a €1,200 basket and 80% fee gross margin on €60, gross profit is €288 per order and €11.52 million total, or 22.9% of reported revenue.
- €5.6 million operating profit implies €140 per order and an 11.1% operating margin. After the stated gross profit, only €148 per order remains for acquisition amortization, unresolved-case expertise, data operations, payment costs, shipping not included in COGS, returns, insurance, compliance, finance, support, and overhead.
- A 4.0x operating-profit value gives €22.4 million, and 70% founder ownership gives about €15.68 million. The model does not double-count distributions and correctly assumes no distribution while employed.

### Decision-critical hypotheses, not evidence

- The family records contain at least 30 relevant post-warranty/private-label cases, at least three repeated platform families, and enough lawful source material to create ten saleable evidence dossiers.
- Independent firms experience an urgent enough gap to pay 750 PLN for fitment determination and prepay an average €1,200 parts basket from an unknown merchant rather than call an established parts house, OEM network, or specialist service center.
- A broad UDT list can be filtered cheaply enough to reach 100 relevant prospects, and three unrelated firms will pay, install, report the result, and produce one repeat order within 180 days.
- A mature account generates at least 20 eligible baskets each year. No source currently measures the eligible post-warranty/private-label case count per firm, average major-parts incidence, repair-versus-replacement choice, or attainable order share.
- Merchandise gross margin is 20%, the fitment fee retains 80% gross margin, CAC remains below €300, wrong-fit returns remain below 5%, and the whole model sustains 11.1% operating margin after technical review, liability cover, refunds, cross-border compliance, inventory, and financing.
- At least 70% of accepted cases become resolvable without fresh paid expert work after only 250 closed cases, and 100 reusable outcome-confirmed mappings can be created from those cases without unsafe extrapolation across serial, firmware, connector, or supplier revisions.
- Normal professional/public purchasing channels permit reliable resale, appropriate warranty/return rights, and enough gross margin without an OEM, distributor, or wholesaler contract.
- Twenty-five unrelated firms will accept terms that lawfully let PlatformRoot retain and commercialize de-identified case/outcome data, and those firms actually control the rights they purport to grant.
- PlatformRoot can document the legal provenance of every graph element and avoid reproducing protected manuals, supplier-database content, firmware, confidential technical material, trade secrets, and personal data without permission.

### Denominator and working-capital stress test

- Two thousand accounts equal 12.5% of the entire cited 16,000-company Polish F-gas population. The true eligible denominator is smaller because the register also includes refrigeration and air-conditioning businesses, OEM-authorized work, firms that do not repair hydronic heat pumps, firms without private-label exposure, and dormant or low-volume operators. The conservative case also says Poland/CEE but supplies no equivalent CEE account count.
- Forty thousand annual baskets equal about 5.4% of Poland's entire cited 745,000-unit heat-pump stock each year. Because PlatformRoot excludes in-warranty units and focuses on a private-label subset with high-value parts needs, its required share of the actually eligible annual repair flow is necessarily much higher. Against the 207,992-unit 2022 cohort alone, 40,000 baskets would equal about 19.2% per year. These ratios are not proof of impossibility; they show why the current scenario cannot be called conservative without failure-frequency and basket-value data.
- Conservative annual merchandise COGS is about €38.4 million. Fifteen days of that COGS is about €1.58 million and 30 days about €3.16 million, before VAT timing, refund reserves, slow stock, chargebacks, and cross-border inventory. The stated €2 million working-capital line covers only about 19 days of merchandise COGS. Full customer prepayment could make cash conversion attractive, but the model does not state customer settlement timing, supplier terms, inventory days, return recovery, or whether prepayment remains acceptable at scale.
- If 5% of orders receive a full €1,260 wrong-fit refund, expected gross refund outflow is €63 per completed order before any part return, supplier credit, or resale recovery—about 22% of the stated €288 gross profit. The economics therefore depend on both the return rate and the recoverable value/timing of returned parts, not the rate alone.

### Resource, liability, and operating gaps

- The proposed month-12 resource—1,000 nodes, 100 outcome mappings, and 250 cases from 25 firms—does not yet exist. It is an operational milestone, not present control.
- A contributor's agreement can cover the contributor's own case/outcome data, but it cannot by itself grant rights in third-party OEM documentation, supplier catalogs, installer employers' records, or homeowner personal data. “De-identified” is not a sufficient data-rights specification when serial numbers, installation details, dates, photographs, or addresses can re-identify a unit or household.
- The merchant-of-record role is economically useful because it captures transaction margin and lets PlatformRoot guarantee fitment. It also places PlatformRoot in the product supply chain. Each SKU needs a documented manufacturer/importer/distributor role, conformity/traceability file, recall route, warranty and returns position, permitted installation context, and cross-border treatment. A genuine OEM part already placed on the EU market is a different risk class from a third-country or “validated equivalent” component.
- The 4,000 PLN legal-review line is a sensible first gate, but the candidate does not include a bound product/professional-liability insurance quote, deductible, exclusions for equivalent parts or technical advice, or a maximum uninsured loss. “Insured shipping” is not product or professional indemnity cover.
- The founder's data skills fit the graph and falsification design. The founder does not personally supply F-gas/electronics authority, parts-quality assurance, product compliance, warehousing, return inspection, or cross-border logistics. Three cases can be coordinated part-time; 250 first-year cases and later 40,000 annual orders require a technical/operations team whose cost and hiring path are absent from the margin model.

## Overall Score

**58/100.** Weighted points are 116.5 across 20.0 total weight, giving a weighted average of 5.825/10 and `10 × 5.825 = 58.25`, rounded to 58. The candidate earns credit for a real insight, a narrow beachhead, a direct paid-proof design, and unusually strong falsification discipline. It is held down by the failed wealth gate, current absence of the proposed control asset, incumbent data/distribution reach, unproved repeat density, merchant liability, and unmodeled cash conversion.

## Control Point

**Current: Tier 3. Attainable by month 12: Tier 2, not Tier 1.** Today the founder controls only potential access to family records that the company may lawfully share. There is no proven ancestry graph, outcome mapping, unrelated contributor agreement, transaction history, reserved inventory, supplier right, exclusive dataset, or routed repair flow. Expert labor, public/professional parts, legal review, software, and outbound lists are purchasable inputs.

If the candidate actually reaches 1,000 provenance-linked nodes, 100 outcome-confirmed mappings, 250 closed cases, rights from 25 unrelated firms, and 70% evidence reuse, it would control a nonexclusive but provable data/transaction asset with compounding operational value—credible Tier 2. It remains below Tier 1 because contributor data rights are nonexclusive, physical part supply is uncontrolled, platform ancestry facts can be independently reconstructed, and no right prevents firms or suppliers from giving the same outcomes to a rival. Database protection can deter wholesale extraction of PlatformRoot's compilation; it does not make the underlying facts exclusive.

**Strongest copy risk:** Bluon can localize its much larger equipment/service corpus and PartsConnect routing for European hydronic/private-label units, while CNC, an OEM portal, or a regional distributor can add structured case intake, revision capture, outcome logging, and a fitment guarantee to an inventory and trust position it already owns. The strongest local copy does not need to recreate the entire graph before competing: a specialist can cover the same three high-frequency platform families, use existing phone support and stock, and neutralize the initial behavior advantage.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.** The candidate does many required things correctly: it gives conservative, expected, and strong scenarios; states customers/orders, price, revenue, gross and operating margins, capital, ownership, dilution, reinvestment, value, timing, and milestones; uses 95% reinvestment in conservative/expected and 100% in strong; assumes no distribution while employed; and avoids double-counting retained earnings.

The failure is credibility of the conservative bridge, not arithmetic. Two thousand mature accounts are 12.5% of the entire broad Polish F-gas-company universe before excluding irrelevant trades and low-volume/non-independent operators. Each must then buy 20 eligible €1,260 baskets annually, producing 40,000 high-value repairs—5.4% of the entire cited Polish heat-pump stock each year despite the candidate excluding in-warranty and non-private-label cases. No bottom-up count establishes the number of eligible accounts, units, failures, major-part baskets, repair decisions, or orders PlatformRoot can capture across Poland/CEE.

The operating model is equally assumption-stacked. €288 gross profit must support a €140 operating profit after technical work, acquisition, refunds, QA, product/professional liability cover, data rights, cross-border compliance, logistics, finance, and overhead. A 5% full-refund rate represents €63 expected refund outflow per order before recovery. At 40,000 orders, €38.4 million annual merchandise COGS means a €2 million line covers only about 19 days before buffers; prepayment may solve this, but no cash-conversion evidence shows that customers, suppliers, returns, VAT, and inventory synchronize that way at scale. The claim that 70% of cases become evidence-resolvable after 250 cases is also not evidence that 40,000 orders can avoid expert labor scaling with revenue.

A 4x operating-profit multiple is not the main problem, and €15.68 million founder equity has numerical headroom above €10 million. The problem is that the €5.6 million profit being multiplied has not been connected to a counted serviceable market or demonstrated operating system. Expected and strong scenarios scale the same unresolved account-frequency, margin, funding, data, and incumbent-response assumptions. Until the record audit, independent paid cases, repeat behavior, case-frequency distribution, supplier economics, return recovery, legal/insurance cost, and a country-by-country account denominator exist, the €10 million path is not credible enough to pass.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS narrowly—for paid proof only, not for the scalable business.** The plan caps pre-proof founder cash at 39,000 PLN, starts with a 4,000 PLN data-rights/product-liability review, avoids inventory and an OEM/wholesaler agreement, uses ordinary channels only after customer prepayment, and asks three unrelated firms to pay 750 PLN, prepay a basket, install it, and report the outcome. The family installer supplies vocabulary, lawful records, and alpha learning but is explicitly excluded from the three paid proofs. Three cases plus one repeat can be coordinated alongside corporate employment using the contracted F-gas/electronics expert and are materially stronger evidence than interviews or family use.

The pass is fragile. The public registry is broad, an unknown founder must earn trust for a safety-relevant fitment determination, and a firm may prefer free supplier/OEM support. The legal review must occur before any sale and must establish, for each first-test SKU and data source: PlatformRoot's manufacturer/importer/distributor role; conformity, traceability, instructions, recall, warranty, and return obligations; whether an “equivalent” can lawfully and safely be recommended for the documented revision; what technical/data content may be retained and commercialized; and what liability remains despite B2B terms. A bound product/professional-indemnity quote and deductible are missing, so the 39,000 PLN figure currently caps planned cash spend rather than every contingent loss.

This gate fails before the first basket if counsel cannot define a low-risk EU-sourced SKU path, liability cannot be insured or contractually/operationally bounded to the founder's limit, the family audit lacks repeated platform families, normal channels do not permit resale with adequate documentation/returns, or no independent expert will sign off within budget. Even if three firms pay, the result proves only that a narrow paid repair transaction can happen; it does not prove 20 annual baskets per mature account, 1,000 nodes, 100 mappings, 11.1% operating margin, or the wealth path.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is strong in shape: three repeated post-warranty private-label hydronic platform families are narrow enough to learn and broad enough to create reuse if the family audit confirms them. The initial UDT route is direct and does not require status or a speculative partnership. The missing fact is whether that beachhead contains enough recurring high-value cases; broad certification and installed-stock statistics do not answer it.

Expansion to more brands and CEE/EU countries is legible but not automatically asymmetric. Each country requires a fresh qualified-company denominator, language/technical support, supplier and return economics, VAT/compliance handling, liability cover, and local case outcomes. Broad competitors move inward with existing technical data, distributor integrations, inventory, OEM documentation, and contractor trust; PlatformRoot moves outward by collecting cases one platform and country at a time. Poland supplies a learning and speed advantage, not a durable local barrier.

The graph becomes valuable only if closed transactions produce revision/outcome facts that competitors do not already hold and if those facts materially reduce handling time, wrong fits, callbacks, and CAC. If incumbents already solve the same private-label cases through inventory plus expert phone support, or can localize their data through one distributor/FSM integration, the niche does not compound fast enough.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The conservative founder-wealth denominator is absent.** The 16,000-company F-gas register is a broad outreach list, not a count of independent private-label hydronic repair firms capable of 20 high-value annual orders. Forty thousand annual baskets are not supported by failure, basket, repair, or capture data.
2. **The control point is prospective and nonexclusive.** PlatformRoot has no unrelated outcome data or transaction flow today. Even the month-12 graph is Tier 2 because contributors, suppliers, and facts are not exclusive.
3. **Incumbents begin with vastly stronger assets.** Bluon already claims tens of millions of model records, 135,000 solved calls, FSM/API distribution, distributor search, and compatible-part routing; CNC claims local technical selection and large original-parts inventory. PlatformRoot's exact Polish/private-label gap has not been shown to remain unsolved.
4. **Merchant-of-record economics and downside are incomplete.** The model omits a cash-conversion schedule, return recovery, insurance quote, product/professional-liability cost, per-SKU conformity/traceability work, and cross-border compliance. The €2 million line may be thin at €38.4 million annual COGS unless prepayment truly persists.
5. **Data ownership is overstated unless provenance is controlled.** Installer consent does not automatically clear OEM manuals, BOMs, firmware, supplier databases, trade secrets, homeowner data, or materials the installer did not own. A database right protects the compilation only under its conditions; it does not cleanse input rights.
6. **The early service may not productize.** At the stated 70% reuse target, 30% of 40,000 annual cases still require 12,000 fresh determinations. Technical labor, QA, exceptions, and liability review can scale with orders and destroy the 11.1% operating margin.
7. **The proof is too small for the wealth inference.** Three installed baskets and one repeat are good reachability evidence but cannot establish 2,000 accounts, 20 annual baskets, <5% wrong fits, €300 CAC, stable 20% product margin, or year-10 ownership/value.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is that the valuable post-warranty repair asset is not another cross-brand catalog: it is a provenance-linked platform-ancestry and revision graph built from closed transactions, installation outcomes, callbacks, returns, and supersessions, with the seller economically accountable for fitment. The family installer can seed the vocabulary and first dossiers without being counted as market proof, while unrelated prepaid baskets create both revenue evidence and the first lawful outcome records. This insight is worth preserving if the record audit and paid cases show repeated, high-value mappings.

## Recommendation

**research further**

Do not enter an independent panel yet because the Founder Wealth Gate fails. The exact candidate has decision-critical facts that can be resolved without redesign: audit the lawful family records; count platform-family and high-value case density; obtain written data-rights/product-role advice plus a bound liability-insurance quote; secure documented supplier/resale/return terms for the first SKUs; and run the stated three-unrelated-firm paid test with actual gross profit, handling time, return recovery, and repeat behavior. Re-evaluate the exact candidate after those facts. If the audit does not support repeated mappings or if paid cases cannot plausibly bridge to the required annual account frequency, discard the shape rather than adjusting the forecast.
