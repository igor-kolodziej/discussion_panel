# Round 10 — Installer-Seed Asset Directions

**Role:** candidate generation and research only. No advisory score, panel, validation or chatbot verdict is present here.

**Scope constraint:** the parents' seven-person renewable installer is used only as a lawful source of field vocabulary, existing records that the company may actually share, technician access and first-party experiments. It is not counted as a paying customer, market proof, free labour, capital, an OEM relationship or a privileged distribution channel. Paid proof requires three unrelated customers.

## Decision Frame

This pass tested whether the family installer can seed a resource that compounds across unrelated renewable-service companies. It rejected any shape whose lasting advantage is merely a dashboard, service scheduling, monitoring, generic procurement, installer leads, a local warehouse, an OEM authorisation or one supplier agreement.

Two official denominators keep the exploration grounded:

- UDT reported in 2026 that more than **16,000 Polish enterprises** had received F-gas certificates for stationary refrigeration, air-conditioning and heat-pump work by 2025. The UDT registry exposes named certified enterprises, so the initial professional population can be enumerated rather than inferred from an installed-base headline.
- UDT had issued **6,733 photovoltaic-installer certificates** by April 2025. URE separately counted more than 1.6 million Polish RES micro-installations at the end of 2025, 99.9% photovoltaic, but this installed-stock figure is context rather than the acquisition denominator.

The market is large enough to matter but already well served in obvious forms. Poland had about 745,000 heat pumps in 2025; the 2022 cohort alone was about 208,000 units. Daikin, Mitsubishi Electric and other OEMs have partner portals, technical support and parts ordering. CNC Pompy Ciepła calls its store one of Poland's largest original heat-pump-parts inventories. Bluon is the decisive foreign threat: it claims technical data and cross-references across more than 20 million model numbers and 200 brands, an AI trained on 135,000 successfully resolved service calls, and PartsConnect supplier routing launched in April 2026. Therefore, **a generic cross-brand manual, fault-code, photo-recognition, fitment or parts-search product is not a candidate**.

## Six Researched Directions

### 1. PlatformRoot — white-label heat-pump repair transaction rail

**Status: finalist-worthy, conditional on a fast paid falsification test.**

**Customer truth.** An independent F-gas-certified firm facing a failed, post-warranty air-to-water heat pump does not want another knowledge portal. It wants to invoice a correct repair without spending hours tracing a rebadged platform, ordering a wrong board or sensor, discovering a firmware/connector revision on site, and paying for a second visit. The worst cases are private-label or importer-led models whose badge is less durable than their underlying OEM platform.

**Wedge.** PlatformRoot accepts a nameplate, board/part photographs, fault evidence and measurements for a post-warranty unit. It returns and sells one prepaid, evidence-backed repair basket: exact OEM or validated equivalent part, supersession/revision constraints, required consumables and a short fitment procedure. PlatformRoot is merchant of record and refunds the basket when its documented fitment determination is wrong. It does **not** promise that replacing the selected part will cure a technician's incorrect diagnosis; diagnostic and fitment responsibilities remain separately documented.

**Narrow beachhead.** Polish independent service firms repairing post-warranty hydronic air-to-water units installed during the 2020–2022 surge, beginning with three OEM/private-label platform families actually present in the family installer's records. In-warranty units are excluded: Daikin, Panasonic, Vaillant and Viessmann warranty terms route work through authorised networks and/or original parts.

**Commercial insight.** A badge/model catalog is copyable; a versioned **platform-ancestry graph** is not the same asset. The useful record links badge and model, underlying OEM platform, serial/date range, board and firmware revision, connectors, part supersessions, the evidence used, the basket shipped, installation outcome, callback and return reason. Rebadging creates fragmented labels around a smaller number of physical platforms. A Polish Midea/Noxa/MDV regional service centre explicitly says many products from different manufacturers use Midea components and technology, supporting the existence of the alias problem without proving every claimed interchange.

**Underused momentum.** Poland's 2022 installation surge is moving toward ordinary three-to-five-year warranty expiry. UOKiK/KAS stopped 463 non-compliant heat pumps at the border in 2025 and UOKiK's published product checks show a fragmented importer set. The European Commission's December 2025 draft revision for central heaters, including hydronic heat pumps, proposed spare-parts availability and delivery/price information. These forces improve part discoverability but do not supply cross-label version fitment or field outcome data.

**Initial acquisition mechanism.** After auditing only records the family company can lawfully share, select three repeated platform families and create ten evidence dossiers. Contact 100 geographically dispersed companies from the named UDT registry with one offer: “send one stalled post-warranty case; pay 750 PLN for an evidence-backed fitment determination and prepay the part basket only after accepting the dossier.” A sale counts only when an unrelated firm pays, installs the basket and reports the outcome. The family installer is alpha site zero, never one of the three proofs.

**180-day paid proof and pre-proof loss.** Maximum 39,000 PLN:

- 4,000 PLN — data-rights, product-liability and sale-terms review;
- 6,000 PLN — record extraction, model and evidence schema, secure case intake;
- 12,000 PLN — donor/failed parts, public-source purchases and bench evidence for the first platform families;
- 8,000 PLN — contracted F-gas/electronics expert review, independent of family labour;
- 6,000 PLN — insured shipping, wrong-fit refund reserve and direct outreach;
- 3,000 PLN — contingency.

Required by day 180: three unrelated paying firms; three installed baskets; at least two correct-fit, no-second-order outcomes; positive contribution after shipping, expert review and expected returns; and one repeat order or paid second case from any of the three. No inventory warehouse, OEM deal or wholesaler integration is required: initial baskets can be bought through normal professional/public channels after customer prepayment.

**Owned/contracted resource by month 12, under 100,000 PLN total founder capital.** A version-controlled graph with at least 1,000 evidence-linked badge/platform/part/revision nodes, 100 outcome-confirmed mappings and 250 closed cases from at least 25 unrelated service firms; signed terms assign PlatformRoot the right to retain and commercialise de-identified case and outcome data. At least 70% of accepted cases must be resolvable from already held evidence rather than fresh paid expert work. The resource is the graph plus outcome rights and transaction history—not a supplier relationship. It cannot be reproduced with one vendor contract because it spans competing badges, importers, suppliers, serial revisions and field outcomes.

**Unit economics hypotheses to test, not facts.** Mature average basket €1,200; fitment fee €60; 20% merchandise gross margin; 80% fee gross margin; €288 blended gross profit per completed order; CAC below €300 per contractor; at least 20 annual orders per mature account; wrong-fit returns below 5%; contribution payback within three orders. The first paid tests should use actual gross profit, not GMV, to decide.

**Bottom-up professional denominator.** The first outbound list is the named UDT registry of more than 16,000 Polish F-gas-certified enterprises. Expansion requires separately enumerated public certification/company registries in each CEE/EU country; the EHPA's estimate of 433,000 direct and indirect European heat-pump jobs is context, not a substitute for that account list.

**Founder-wealth path.** The calculations treat PlatformRoot as principal on the parts sale, count no personal distributions in company value, and do not double-count retained earnings.

The conservative model clears the €10m founder-equity gate with about 57% headroom at a 4× operating-profit multiple, but it is **not yet evidence-backed**. Its decision-critical assumption is that 2,000 accounts can each produce 20 eligible, high-value baskets annually. That must be measured from the family alpha and three independent paid cases before the direction deserves evaluation.

**Why broad competitors cannot be ignored.** Bluon already has far more model and service-call data than this founder; Bluon localising PartsConnect for European hydronic heat pumps is the strongest copy risk. OEMs own their in-warranty data and parts flows. CNC and specialist service centres own local inventory and technical experience. PlatformRoot survives only if the white-label/private-label ancestry and revision problem is material, its closed outcomes are locally superior, and installers prefer a cross-brand guaranteed basket over a free phone call to a supply house.

**Kill criteria before serious capital.** Kill if (a) the family records contain fewer than 30 relevant post-warranty/white-label service cases or no repeated platform family; (b) fewer than three of 100 unrelated registry firms pay by day 180; (c) two paid baskets require OEM-only software/authorisation; (d) wrong-fit rate exceeds 5% after 30 orders; (e) median gross profit per order is below €180 or repeat intent below 30%; (f) the first 100 cases show fewer than 25 reusable mappings; or (g) Bluon/CNC already resolves the same Polish private-label cases with equal evidence and transaction convenience.

### 2. BoardRoot — exchange repair for control boards across rebadged heat pumps

**Status: rejected.** The proposed resource was a board-revision/failure-mode corpus plus an exchange-core inventory. The family installer could supply failed-board examples, and prepaid unrelated repair orders could fit 40,000 PLN. However, this repeats the rejected obsolete-spare/core-pool pattern: each board family requires electronics expertise, firmware/version handling, safety testing and physical cores. RGB Elektronika and European Network Services already advertise multi-brand component-level inverter/electronics repair, while CNC holds original heat-pump parts. Scale therefore remains a repair shop plus long-tail stock, not a high-revenue transaction graph. The defensible information belongs as evidence inside PlatformRoot, not as a standalone remanufacturing company.

### 3. PVRoot — inverter ancestry, repair and replacement routing

**Status: rejected.** Poland offers a named 6,733-person certified-installer population and 1.6m micro-installations, so the denominator is real. The family firm could seed nameplates and RMA histories. But SecondSol already claims 250,000 PV spare parts plus testing, inverter repair, repowering and fulfilment; ENS calls itself a European market leader in inverter repair; RGB, FixVolt and other Polish firms repair Huawei, Sofar, Growatt, SolarEdge, Solis and Deye units. Replacement is often simpler than component fitment, and firmware/cloud coupling creates manufacturer dependencies. A new graph would sit between established inventory and repair networks with no protected transaction.

### 4. R290 SafeCase — job-specific propane safety evidence capsule

**Status: rejected.** The direct payer would be an F-gas firm installing or maintaining an R290 monobloc; the product would package site clearance, ignition-source evidence, technician steps and a signed record. Regulation (EU) 2024/573 and the new certification regime create real urgency. Yet SKILLSAFE EU has already published and is trialling industry-wide R290 lifecycle guidance, while Prozon trains technicians and Montero, Feldi and proSERWIS24 already digitise service records. A job capsule is readily added to those products or an OEM app. It may be a useful feature, but even high adoption at modest per-job pricing lacks a conservative founder-wealth path and the control point can be copied through one workflow release.

### 5. FailureStock — independent failure and parts-demand intelligence for wholesalers

**Status: reserve research lead, not finalist-worthy.** The proposed buyer is a Polish/CEE parts wholesaler paying for SKU-level stocking recommendations derived from cross-brand closed repairs. It avoids taking inventory and has high theoretical software margins. The fatal cold-start issue is buyer asymmetry: the family installer supplies too little cross-market data, installers do not naturally benefit from sharing it, and a wholesaler will not prepay a consequential stocking decision from one unknown contractor's history. Bluon's 135,000 solved service calls and distributor product make the data disadvantage explicit. Reconsider only after PlatformRoot independently owns at least 10,000 closed transactions; then this becomes a derivative product, not the initial business.

### 6. WarrantyLine — multi-brand warranty-safe repair and reimbursement workflow

**Status: rejected.** Installers do suffer from fragmented warranty rules, but the authority is not ownable. Panasonic states that heat-pump warranties are issued only by currently accredited installation firms; Vaillant requires authorised partners for covered parts and service; Daikin routes factory-program units to its factory service and partner portals. Any product promising “warranty-safe” third-party repair would still need serial approval, original parts and brand authority case by case. A documentation layer is copied by OEM/field-service software; a claims-recovery layer becomes partner-dependent managed service. PlatformRoot therefore excludes in-warranty work rather than pretending to control it.

## Funnel Decision

Only **PlatformRoot** is finalist-worthy from this installer-seed pass, and it is deliberately narrow: post-warranty white-label/private-label hydronic heat-pump repair baskets, not generic HVAC search, monitoring, lead generation, a warehouse or an installer service network. Its live advantage is a cross-badge platform-ancestry and field-outcome graph tied to transactions. Its fatal unresolved fact is whether the relevant repair frequency, basket value and repeated mappings exist at enough density for 2,000 accounts × 20 annual orders without Bluon, OEM portals or local parts specialists matching the result.

The first move is not more desk research. It is a bounded record audit followed by three unrelated paid stalled-case tests. Until those facts exist, the wealth model remains a labelled hypothesis.

### 1. Post-warranty white/private-label hydronic installed stock and repair events

**What public evidence establishes.**

- NFOŚiGW reports **904,670 requested heat sources** in the Clean Air programme from 19 September 2018 through 30 April 2026, of which **29.2% were air heat pumps**. The arithmetic is about **264,164 requested air-heat-pump sources**. This is neither a completed-installation count nor a hydronic, private-label, warranty-expired or repair-event count.
- UOKiK/KAS inspected 34 imported heat-pump models in 2025 and prevented 786 non-compliant units from entering the Polish market. The 2024 operation examined 564 imported units across 36 models and stopped 463. These controls demonstrate importer/model fragmentation and continuing import flow, but do not reveal national installed stock, badge/OEM ancestry, warranty status or repair frequency.
- A Polish parliamentary committee transcript contains an industry participant's statement that Europe had 17 own-brand heat-pump importers and Poland seven, including brands such as KAISAI and Cooper&Hunter. This is testimony, not an audited installed-unit or market-share dataset, and cannot supply the denominator.
- UDT requires certified firms to document the device name, model, serial number and the type of installation, service or repair activity. The raw facts therefore can exist across firm records, but UDT does not publish a brand/model/serial-level national repair-event aggregate.
- The only public longitudinal repair evidence found is not population-matched. A Swiss federal monitoring project for 61 small heat pumps reported average repair cost of CHF 74.50 per year and 99.75% availability, with only two exceptional repair-cost years. These are older, quality-selected Swiss systems—not Polish/CEE private-label units—but they are adverse evidence against assuming frequent high-value failures without a matched dataset.

**Exact fact availability.** No public current bottom-up count was found for (a) post-warranty white/private-label hydronic air-to-water heat pumps installed in Poland or CEE, or (b) repair events and part-replacement events for that stock. Broad stock, subsidy, F-gas-company and import-control counts cannot substitute. Consequently, public evidence does not establish that 40,000 eligible annual baskets exist.

### 3. Badge-to-OEM-platform mapping availability and legal/data-right/FTO boundaries

**Public mapping layers exist, but they stop before PlatformRoot's proposed control point.**

- EPREL has a public product system and API access. Its supplier guidance explicitly says the displayed brand/trademark can differ from the supplier or original manufacturer, because a supplier may not be the original manufacturer and may place products under different brands. Public EPREL data is product-information-sheet and label data; compliance technical documentation is restricted to market-surveillance authorities. EPREL does not provide a public serial/date/revision-to-BOM/fitment graph.
- Heat Pump KEYMARK has a public certificate/model database and API. Its Annex G defines an OBL/private-label company as one relabelling a valid OEM-certified product. The rules permit an amended same certificate, a separate certificate, disclosure of the original certificate, and a separate certificate where it is **not obvious to the market** that the certificate holder is not the manufacturer. KEYMARK can therefore seed some OEM/OBL links, while its own rules show why public ancestry is incomplete by design.
- UDT service documentation can contain model and serial fields, but the records are held by the servicing enterprise. A firm must have a lawful basis and contractual right to supply case information; the existence of a mandatory field is not a public reuse licence.

**Legal/data-right boundary visible from public law.**

- Article 3 of Directive (EU) 2016/943 treats independent discovery and observation, study, disassembly or testing of a public or lawfully possessed product as lawful acquisition when the acquirer is free of a valid duty restricting it. The same Directive treats unauthorised access/copying, breach of confidentiality and breach of a contractual use restriction as unlawful. This permits a provenance-controlled own-testing route, not blanket extraction from OEM, portal, supplier or employer files.
- Database structure can be protected by copyright; a database's content can receive an EU sui-generis right where substantial investment was made in obtaining, verifying or presenting it. Building a new structure does not clear rights in copied source material or substantial extraction from another database.
- EDPB states that pseudonymised data remains personal data; only properly anonymised data falls outside GDPR. A serial number, installation address, date, technician identity and photographs can enable indirect identification when combined. “De-identified” in commercial terms therefore cannot be assumed to mean anonymous under GDPR.

**Exact fact availability.** Public EPREL/KEYMARK and manufacturer materials can seed brand, model, supplier and some certificate/OEM relations. No public current source supplies the required serial/date/revision-to-part/supersession/connector/firmware mapping for the Polish/CEE private-label beachhead. No blanket public FTO conclusion is available. Each input requires source-level provenance, permission/contract review, privacy analysis and a record of whether the mapping was independently observed, licensed or contributed under explicit commercialisation rights.

### 4. Wrong-part/return rate, liability and insurability

**Returns and fitment evidence.** No public current benchmark was found for the wrong-part, wrong-revision, fitment-return or second-order rate for heat-pump replacement parts in Poland/CEE. Bluon markets 99.9% compatibility confidence and “crush wrong part issues,” but publishes no independently audited realised return rate for European hydronic/private-label transactions. CNC says it checks compatibility from model, serial and nameplate data; it does not publish its realised error or return rate. Daikin's public 2024 price-list terms say special-order accessories, devices and spare parts are not returnable, illustrating that a merchant's customer refund promise may not be matched by upstream return rights.

**Liability is attached to more than the physical SKU.**

- The EU General Product Safety Regulation requires traceability and online-offer identification/safety information, and puts specific duties on distributors and other economic operators. PlatformRoot as merchant of record cannot treat itself as a neutral search index.
- Directive (EU) 2024/2853 expands the product-liability framework and covers components, importers, distributors in specified circumstances and economic operators making substantial modifications. Ordinary repairs without substantial modification are distinguished, but the actual role depends on what is sold, imported, relabelled, modified or recommended. Member States apply the Directive to products placed on the market after 8 December 2026.
- Warta's SME wording effective from 22 June 2025 shows that product/public liability insurance is offered for products placed on the market and services performed. However, its base exclusions include costs of finding, removing, dismantling or replacing a defective product and pure financial loss; optional clause 11A can extend cover to specified removal/reinstallation losses from defective components. The separate pure-financial-loss clause excludes loss caused by a product/service and excludes design, advice, control and opinion activity. A fitment guarantee plus technical dossier therefore cannot be assumed covered by a standard product-OC policy.

**Exact fact availability.** Public policy wording establishes that relevant covers and endorsements exist, but no public source proves a Polish insurer will bind PlatformRoot's exact combination of merchant-of-record sale, cross-brand equivalence recommendation, wrong-fit refund, CEE territory, serial/revision data and technical instruction. No quote, premium, deductible, limit, recall cover or advice/professional-liability endorsement was found. Insurability remains unavailable until written underwriting terms cover both the product sale and the fitment/advice promise.

### 5. Merchant-of-record cash cycle and financing €38.4m annual merchandise COGS

**Public supplier terms do not prove unsecured scale credit.**

- HVAC Polska accepts transfer, pro-forma or electronic payment and begins transfer/pro-forma fulfilment after cleared funds.
- Sofiterm's public terms require full payment before release unless trade credit has been granted; trade credit requires separate arrangements and security such as a bank or insurance guarantee.
- HAKOM says deferred payment is individually agreed for established B2B partners.
- Carrier Poland's general terms make payment due immediately after delivery/service unless the offer/order confirmation specifies otherwise.
- Thermosilesia exposes online B2B ordering and individual commercial conditions but no public net-day limit for a new unknown merchant.
- Daikin's e-parts material shows that registered installers/dealers can see their prices, receive an additional ordering discount and ship an order to the indicated address. Mitsubishi offers registered contractors B2B spare-parts delivery. These prove professional ordering/direct-address fulfilment mechanisms, not PlatformRoot eligibility, drop-ship title allocation, customer-specific packing, return matching, or supplier credit.

**Fixed-scenario working-capital arithmetic.** The €38.4m figure is the scenario's annual **merchandise** COGS. It is €105,205 per calendar day on a 365-day basis. The fee's separately implied direct cost is €0.48m; it is not included in the €38.4m table.

|---:|---:|

| 15 days | €1,578,082 |
| 19 days | €1,998,904 |
| 30 days | €3,156,164 |

A €2m line therefore funds only about **19 days** of merchandise COGS before processor reserves, VAT timing, freight, refunds, chargebacks or safety stock. It cannot by itself support a normal 30–60-day gross operating cycle.

**What a zero/negative merchandise cycle would require.** Customer-cleared prepayment before every supplier order, no owned inventory, direct-to-installer delivery, and supplier payment no earlier than customer cash can make the merchandise purchase self-funded transaction by transaction. Public supplier terms show that prepayment purchasing is operationally possible. They do **not** prove that 2,000 service firms will prepay all 40,000 annual baskets, that suppliers will ship neutrally to each installer, or that payment providers will release funds before the supplier must be paid. Polish tax guidance also states that VAT becomes due on a prepayment when it is received for the amount received, so gross customer cash is not all available working capital.

**Refund-liquidity sensitivity, without changing the fixed scenario.** At a 5% full-refund incidence, 2,000 orders create **€2.52m of gross customer refund outflow**. The table below treats the unrecovered share of that gross refund as an incremental sensitivity; it is not an observed return rate and does not change the fixed order or price assumptions.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|

Even at 100% eventual recovery, refund timing can consume liquidity. The public record contains no committed supplier-credit, drop-ship, processor-reserve or receivables-facility term sheet for this candidate. Financing €38.4m without heroic capital is therefore publicly unproven; the precise proof would be contracted cash-cycle terms, not a general statement that supplier credit will be available.
