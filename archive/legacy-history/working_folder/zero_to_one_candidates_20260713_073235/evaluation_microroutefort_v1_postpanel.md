# Advisory Evaluation — MicroRouteFort v1 After Simulated Panel

## Scope

This advisory evaluation applies to the exact SHA-256-frozen candidate `4a9d50855db8f53a4f54ffbe33e212416fc2ff786485b0c847c1de79c4832a0e` together with the complete objections in `panel_microroutefort_v1_judge_g.md` and `panel_microroutefort_v1_judge_h.md`. It diagnoses and routes; it is not a gate score and makes no validation claim.

## Evidence Classification

- **Established external context:** an EU secure-parking shortage, a specified Bronze standard, and policy support for upgrading existing facilities.
- **Candidate hypotheses:** a compliant underused 20-bay host exists at the proposed lease cost; the host will grant assignable 10–15-year control; conversion will fit 60,000 PLN; two fleets will prepay; the site will hold 25%+ fully loaded EBITDA.
- **Unevidenced scale assumptions:** 35 sites averaging 80 bays; 65% founder ownership after roughly €30m cumulative site capital; only €9m net obligations; 30% portfolio EBITDA; 7× exit value.

## Hard Gates

### Reachability Gate — narrow pass

The candidate seeks real deposits from two independent fleets inside 180 days, caps pre-proof loss at 30,000 PLN, buys no hardware before payment, and remains part-time. The sequential host, audit, quote, insurance, and customer dependencies make this fragile, but the staged test is legitimate.

### Founder Wealth Gate — fail

Both independent judges reached the same fatal conclusion. The conservative scenario reaches only €10.5m, a 5% cushion, while simultaneously assuming 35 controlled sites, 30% EBITDA, 65% founder ownership, a 7× multiple, and modest liabilities after large external capital needs. The relevant supply is not the headline European bay shortage but the far smaller pool of fully serviced, legally usable, separable yards willing to sign long assignable leases at workable economics. No evidence or financing bridge supports that pool.

## Control Point And Copy Risk

An executed long lease plus service rights is Tier 1 for one named parcel. It does not control the conversion method or corridor. Every new site resets host sourcing, legal use, service economics, certification, capital, and demand. Existing yard operators, fuel networks, parking chains, and better-capitalised aggregators can copy the playbook at alternative nearby parcels and may avoid the lease/service margin layer.

## Shared Fatal Objections

1. **The founder-wealth bridge is not conservative.** This independently fails the non-compensating gate.
2. **Scalable host supply is unproved and may be internally contradictory.** A fully serviced corridor yard with spare capacity may already monetise it or demand more rent.
3. **Site-level control does not become portfolio domination.** Repeating the asset does not strengthen control over the next host enough to make expansion asymmetric.
4. **The first-site cash stack has almost no delivery cushion.** At maximum pre-proof and conversion spend, little remains for deposits, insurance, certification gaps, or working capital.

## Recommendation

**Kill this business shape.** Two independent judges identified the same fatal founder-wealth and portfolio-repeatability objection. Under the run rules, do not cosmetically revise pricing, bay count, valuation, or the lease bundle. Preserve only the abstract insight that a valuable business should control a scarce resource and paid demand before capital expenditure. Return to the generator for a different economic shape whose first controlled resource can compound globally or across transactions without reacquiring a location-specific monopoly each time.
