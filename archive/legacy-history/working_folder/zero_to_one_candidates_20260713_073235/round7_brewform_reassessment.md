# Round 7 BrewForm Reassessment: Reject the Business Shape

Candidate-generator funnel decision only — not scored, evaluated, pivoted, panelled, chatbot-reviewed, confirmed, or validated. Research date: 2026-07-13.

## Decision

**Reject BrewForm and do not create a finalist brief.**

The exact proposed rail—secure brewery yeast feedstock, dry and standardize it, qualify it as a pet-food/feed ingredient, and sell recurring bulk orders—is already an established European and global commodity/specialty-ingredient chain. Poland is not an unserved regional wedge: Translis publicly states 7,000 t/year of dry brewer's-yeast production for European markets; AgroYeast already processes brewing/distilling by-products into feed; and Foodcom offers spray-dried brewer's yeast in 25 kg bags and big bags with a five-tonne minimum order. TBESBY separately advertises 3,000 t/year, certified-brewery sourcing, drum drying, milling, bulk packaging, and pet-food use.

The proposed foundational resource is therefore not scarce. Brewery supply rights are incumbent raw-material procurement, pet-food SKU approval is ordinary vendor qualification, and repeat call-offs are non-exclusive purchasing rather than an assigned reorder channel. An unknown founder with no dryer, application laboratory, certifications, or customer formulation history would enter between lower-cost bulk manufacturers/traders and technically stronger yeast specialists.

## Exact Thesis Tested

BrewForm proposed to:

1. contract exclusive or first-refusal rights to wet surplus yeast from regional breweries;
2. use toll processing to stabilize, inactivate, dry, mill, test, and pack it;
3. obtain buyer-approved pet-food ingredient codes and annual call-off orders; and
4. treat the reserved feedstock plus approved-SKU reorder loop as the distribution/control asset.

The thesis would have been materially different from commodity trading only if the founder could obtain feedstock unavailable to current processors, produce a specification incumbents could not economically match, and receive buyer call-offs that were exclusive or costly to requalify away. Current direct evidence supports none of those conditions.

## Why the Proposed Distribution Asset Is Not a Control Point

### Reserved regional feedstock is ordinary procurement

Translis and AgroYeast already operate in Poland, Leiber explicitly sources brewery raw materials, and TBESBY describes the same certified-brewery intake. There is no evidence that meaningful clean wet-yeast volume is stranded, uncontracted, or available under exclusive rights on terms better than current processors can match. A new founder would likely need to outbid an incumbent or accept lower-quality/more variable streams.

Even a signed brewery contract would control only one replaceable input source. It would not prevent buyers from purchasing equivalent dry yeast from Translis, Foodcom, Leiber, TBESBY, IQI, or imports.

### Approved pet-food SKU is table stakes, not assigned distribution

An ingredient code proves that a buyer may purchase a material; it does not give the supplier exclusive access to the formulation, payment flow, or reorder. Pet-food manufacturers can qualify multiple sources for resilience and price leverage. Current specialist suppliers already offer specifications, certifications, application support, samples, and functional variants. No evidence shows that a mid-market buyer would grant a minimum exclusive call-off to an unknown toll-processed supplier.

### Toll processing does not create an owned advantage

A toll dryer, lab, packer, and haulier are purchasable vendors. The founder would not own the process equipment, specialized yeast know-how, protected formulation, or manufacturing economics. Incumbents can use the same vendors or their own plants. Under 100,000 PLN, the founder could perhaps produce qualification batches, but could not create a processing asset comparable to Translis, Leiber, or major yeast companies.

### Regionality does not change buyer economics enough

The product is dry, shelf-stable, and already sold in bulk bags across Europe. Foodcom lists a two-year shelf life, while Translis explicitly serves European markets. Freight savings from Poland are therefore a price input, not a defensible access advantage. Local collection may help wet-feedstock cost, but local manufacturers already possess that advantage.

## Customer Gap Reconstructed After Incumbent Research

Pet-food formulators want consistent microbiology, moisture, protein, colour, odour, palatability, traceability, certification, supply resilience, application support, and competitive delivered price. Existing suppliers already address those jobs at three levels:

- bulk dry brewer's yeast from local/regional manufacturers and traders;
- pet-food-specific ingredient sourcing and formulation support from IQI and similar channels; and
- differentiated extracts, hydrolysates, cell-wall fractions, beta-glucans, MOS, and validated microbial solutions from Leiber, Ohly, Lallemand, Lesaffre, Angel, Alltech, and others.

BrewForm would not fill a missing job. It would offer a new supplier of an existing material, with less scale and less technical authority. “Regional,” “upcycled,” “traceable,” and “approved SKU” are already visible incumbent claims or capabilities.

## Denominator and Wealth-Bridge Failure

The original direction used two broad denominators:

- Eurostat's 34.7bn litres of EU beer production in 2024; and
- FEDIAF's roughly 8.6m tonnes of annual European pet-food sales.

Neither establishes recoverable wet-yeast tonnes, pet-food-grade yield, brewer's-yeast inclusion, eligible formulations, customer switching, or newcomer share. Direct incumbent research makes the proposed conservative model less credible, not more.

The written conservative shape required **12,000 dry tonnes/year at €2,000/t**, 32% gross margin, 14% operating margin, and 70% founder ownership to reach about €13.1m of founder equity. That annual newcomer volume would exceed Translis's published 7,000 t/year capacity and TBESBY's published 3,000 t/year capacity combined, while BrewForm would own no dryer and have no protected customer channel. It also assumed €640/t gross contribution without current market prices, wet-to-dry yield, energy cost, tolling quotes, rejection losses, certification overhead, working-capital cost, or broker/distributor margin.

A source-backed brewer's-yeast pet-food denominator was not found. The 8.6m-tonne whole pet-food market cannot substitute for the ingredient category. Without an eligible-volume denominator and a reason for buyers to replace established sources, 12,000 tonnes is heroic share arithmetic rather than a conservative path. The founder-wealth requirement is therefore not supportable for this business shape.

## Reachability Is Insufficient to Rescue the Direction

A founder might spend up to 40,000 PLN on samples, lab work, toll-dried batches, and buyer outreach within 180 days. A buyer might even pay for a small lot. That would prove only that brewer's yeast is purchasable—already established by current suppliers—not that the founder controls feedstock, buyer reorders, margin, or scalable distribution.

The proposed 12-month resource threshold also fails structurally:

- a 2,000-wet-tonne brewery right is not scarce if equivalent dry product is widely available;
- two buyer ingredient codes do not assign the reorder channel;
- 300 dry tonnes of call-offs would still be ordinary supply orders exposed to price rebids and dual sourcing; and
- no founder-owned asset inside 100,000 PLN prevents an incumbent, trader, brewery, or buyer from internalizing the same route.

Reachable paid proof cannot compensate for a commodity control point and unsupported wealth bridge.

## Kill Conditions Already Met

1. **Exact chain already exists:** multiple current suppliers source brewing by-products, dry and standardize yeast, and sell it for feed and pet food.
2. **Local gap is absent:** at least two Polish processors and a Polish bulk trader already serve the product family.
3. **Scale incumbents are present:** Translis states 7,000 t/year and TBESBY 3,000 t/year; Leiber has specialized processes, pet-food products, international partners, and Asahi backing.
4. **Buyer access is established elsewhere:** IQI and major yeast/feed suppliers already sell directly into pet-food formulation and functional-ingredient budgets.
5. **The resource is replicable:** supply contracts, toll drying, laboratory tests, certification, and SKU qualification are purchasable or routine incumbent capabilities.
6. **The wealth denominator is missing:** beer volume and total pet-food tonnage do not establish the addressable dried-brewer's-yeast category.
7. **The volume case is heroic:** 12,000 t/year would require a newcomer without owned processing to exceed the combined published output of two visible specialist producers.
8. **Margin is unproved:** the original €640/t gross contribution lacks current price, yield, energy, rejection, tolling, logistics, certification, financing, and channel evidence.
9. **Differentiation trends upward:** major suppliers offer extracts, hydrolysates, cell-wall fractions, beta-glucans, MOS, postbiotics, and application evidence; plain dried yeast sits at the commodity end.

## Re-entry Rule

Do not return to dried brewer's yeast through different brewery contracts, a new regional label, pet-food wording, revised tonnage, sustainability claims, toll-processor quotes, or higher assumed margins.

The family may be reconsidered only if the founder already controls a materially non-substitutable input or protected process, has an independently demonstrated functional advantage over current yeast products, and holds a binding exclusive buyer formulation/offtake position large enough to support a bottom-up founder-wealth case. None is present here.
