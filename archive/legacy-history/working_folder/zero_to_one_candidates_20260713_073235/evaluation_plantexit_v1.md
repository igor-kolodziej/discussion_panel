# Zero-to-One Evaluation: PlantExit Exclusive MRO Liquidation Mandates

## Diagnosis

PlantExit can reach a paid transaction cheaply because a signed sale mandate avoids buying inventory, and exact-model matching may recover more value than scrap. It remains an auction/broker business whose supply exclusivity resets with every closure, while incumbent auction houses and MRO dealers already own trust, traffic, and seller relationships. The data graph is useful only after many mandates and is not a first-year control point. The founder-wealth model relies on an aggressive mandate cadence and distributions, so the shape should be killed rather than panelled.

## Evidence and Assumptions

KRZ is a real public sourcing channel and EU sources support reuse/long-tail inventory. Seller exclusivity, 18–30% take rates, condition trust, buyer conversion, 55–64% gross margins, and repeat mandate density are hypotheses. No mandate or buyer transaction exists.

## Overall Score

**59/100.** Weighted points are `117.5 ÷ 20 = 5.875`; multiplying by 10 gives 59 after rounding. Advisory only.

## Control Point

**Tier 2 during a signed mandate, Tier 3 as a company-level moat.** The company controls a named inventory's transaction flow for 90 days but owns no durable supply. Strongest copy risk: an established auction house signs the next mandate and uses its existing bidder base plus the same cataloguing workflow.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The scenarios include required fields, but the conservative path reaches €10m only by combining a modest brokerage valuation with 25m PLN of cumulative post-employment distributions, and it assumes 80 mandates per year. Expected and strong paths require 150–300 mandates annually without a controlled repeatable seller channel. This is heroic distribution, not a credible non-compensating gate pass.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** One exclusive mandate and three paid sales totalling 60,000 PLN GMV can be attempted within 180 days and 25,000 PLN while employed, with inspection contracted. Paid proof would not solve the durable-supply problem.

## Expansion Check

The corridor niche is practical, and buyer data may improve matching. Yet auction/MRO incumbents can move inward through a routine workflow change, while each seller must be won separately. Local inspection is an execution advantage, not an asymmetry.

## Fatal Objections

1. Episodic supply and expiring exclusivity prevent a durable control point.
2. Incumbent auction houses can copy serialised cataloguing and already own trust/distribution.
3. The founder-wealth path depends on heroic annual mandate volume.
4. Disputes, condition verification, and returns can erase the proposed commission margin.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Avoid inventory risk by requiring an exclusive seller mandate and buyer payment before custody.

## Recommendation

kill
