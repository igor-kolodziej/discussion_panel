# Round Six — Direct Distribution, Pre-Sold Products, and Controlled Flows

Research date: 2026-07-13. Candidate generation only — not evaluated, scored, panelled, or validated.

All prices, margins, conversion rates, eligible-account filters, and valuation multiples below are explicit hypotheses. Each direction starts with a resource the founder can create and own, or buy from ordinary vendors, without a strategic partner. Customer purchases are evidence, not control-point prerequisites.

## 1. QuietPane EU — Risk-Mapped Removable Acoustic Window Inserts

### Commercial insight and inside-view pain

More than 112 million Europeans are exposed to transport noise above Environmental Noise Directive reporting thresholds, yet the resident's actual choice is usually “tolerate it or replace the windows.” Renters, conservation-building occupants, and owners with otherwise serviceable tilt-turn windows do not want a disruptive façade project. They want a reversible bedroom-level result, a price before a survey visit, and evidence that the reduction is audible in *their* room.

The underused distribution asset is the combination of official strategic noise maps, building footprints, and ordinary unaddressed mail. It can identify affected façades before residents search for a solution; it does not require resident names or an installer referral.

### Product and exact paid event

- A made-to-measure, tool-free secondary insert: clear acoustic-grade panel, compression perimeter, removable safety retainers, and a phone-guided measurement jig for common European tilt-turn window reveals.
- The first product promise is narrow: one bedroom window, removable from inside, with a measured before/after reduction target. No claim that every room or low-frequency source will achieve the same result.
- **Paid event by day 90:** two unrelated Warsaw residents in buildings shown on official noise maps each pay a 30% deposit of 900–1,500 PLN for one insert, conditional only on fit and a stated measured-reduction floor. Family and friends do not count.

### Cold start and resource bootstrap

1. Build three panel/frame prototypes from cut acrylic or laminated safety glazing, standard gasket extrusion, and CNC-cut corner parts bought from ordinary Polish vendors.
2. Rent calibrated sound equipment for before/after tests; compare at least two cavity depths and asymmetric panel thicknesses.
3. Convert GIOŚ/Warsaw noise layers plus public address points into a 1,000-building anonymous mail test. The mailer offers a free phone measurement and a fixed-price deposit, not an acoustic-consulting engagement.
4. Spend before external paid proof: 8,000 PLN prototypes and jigs; 5,000 PLN testing/rental; 5,000 PLN landing page, measurement tool, and product liability/legal work; 4,000 PLN direct mail; 3,000 PLN contingency. **Maximum 25,000 PLN.**
5. Within 12 months and 100,000 PLN, own the parametric frame design, measurement workflow, cutting files, jigs, test protocol, fit/error history, domain/brand, and first paid performance records. Production remains with ordinary cut-panel and extrusion vendors until repeat demand.

### Control point and copy risk

- **Control-tier hypothesis: Tier 2 at POC.** The founder owns a usable product system and performance/fit corpus, but not an exclusive material or channel. Repeated measurements by window type, a low-remake quote engine, registered designs, and address-level CAC history can make the system harder to copy operationally.
- **Strongest copy risk:** Indow, European secondary-glazing specialists, or a large Polish window manufacturer can reproduce the product concept. The defendable claim must become lower measurement error, predictable acoustic results on European tilt-turn geometries, and much cheaper geo-targeted acquisition—not “secondary glazing is new.”

### Conservative founder-wealth bridge

- **Denominator:** EEA reports 112 million exposed people. At 2.25 people per household that is about 49.8 million affected households. Applying a deliberately severe 10% suitability/willingness filter gives a **hypothesised 5.0 million eligible homes**.
- **Year 11 volume:** 28,000 homes/year, or 0.56% of that filtered denominator; average order €1,100 for two to three panels.
- **Economics:** €30.8 million revenue; 52% gross margin; 18% operating margin = €5.54 million operating profit. Hypothesised six-times operating-profit enterprise value = €33.3 million; less €2.0 million working-capital debt = €31.3 million equity value.
- **Capital/ownership:** up to €2.0 million cumulative tooling, working capital, and market-entry capital, funded after proof by deposits, retained cash, debt, and at most 20 percentage points of dilution. Founder retains 65% after a technical/operations co-founder pool and later financing.
- **Founder wealth and timing:** about €20.3 million founder-owned equity in year 11; no founder distributions are added. While employed, 95% of after-tax distributable cash is reinvested and optional distributions stay at or below 5%.

### Fatal dependencies

- Blind tests must show a useful reduction on already-double-glazed tilt-turn windows; road low-frequency noise may defeat the value proposition.
- Customer self-measurement and parcel shipping must not create remake/breakage economics above roughly 8% of revenue.
- Anonymous risk-mapped mail must produce paid deposits at a CAC materially below window-replacement lead generation.
- The panel must not obstruct emergency egress, ventilation, condensation management, or safe handling.

## 2. RadonBlock Kit — Measured-Result Radon Mitigation for Standard House Types

### Commercial insight and inside-view pain

The customer journey breaks after detection. A homeowner receives an alarming long-duration radon result, learns that a map cannot diagnose the building, and then encounters an opaque bespoke trade: uncertain fan sizing, unclear pipe route, no fixed SKU, and no consistent post-remediation proof. The desired purchase is not a monitor or advice; it is a bounded-price path from a verified high result to a verified lower result.

### Product and exact paid event

- Three standard sub-slab-depressurisation kits for common detached-house configurations: sealed suction collar, appropriately rated fan, pressure/alarm module, labelled pipe pack, controller, commissioning manometer, and independent before/after detector pair.
- The product includes a pre-install pressure-field test. If the house fails the standard-kit envelope, the deposit is returned rather than selling a false certainty.
- **Paid event by day 150:** two unrelated homeowners with accredited annual-average results above the Polish/EU reference level each pay a 2,000 PLN product deposit toward a hypothesised 7,500–9,000 PLN installed kit. Installation is bought from an ordinary licensed electrician/core-drilling contractor at posted rates; no exclusive installer agreement is assumed.

### Cold start and resource bootstrap

1. Buy off-the-shelf fans, pressure sensors, detectors, pipework, seals, and an alarm; create the collar, controller enclosure, commissioning workflow, and installation template.
2. Begin only in Polish counties identified by the national radon plan. Mail an anonymous “measure first” offer; the official source explicitly warns that maps cannot replace dosimetric measurement.
3. Spend before external paid proof: 13,000 PLN prototype/consumables; 7,000 PLN measurement and pressure-field equipment; 7,000 PLN safety, insurance, and specialist review; 7,000 PLN targeted mail and paid search; 5,000 PLN contingency. **Maximum 39,000 PLN.**
4. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, own the collar/controller design, firmware, install and commissioning pack, diagnostic decision rules, quote engine, paid-result records, brand, and product documentation. Commodity fans, detectors, and contractors remain replaceable vendors.

### Control point and copy risk

- **Control-tier hypothesis: Tier 2.** The founder owns the standardized product/diagnostic system and result history, but the physical components and mitigation principle are available to incumbents.
- **Strongest copy risk:** HVAC and radon-remediation firms can assemble similar components. The compounding edge would have to be a much lower misquote rate by building type, verified outcomes, faster direct distribution after a positive test, and a product that ordinary licensed trades can install consistently.

### Conservative founder-wealth bridge

- **Denominator:** the EU had 202 million households in 2024 and 51.7% of residents lived in houses, implying roughly 104 million house-dwelling households. A **hypothesised 2%** combination of above-threshold measurement and suitable building type yields about **2.1 million eligible homes**; this filter must be replaced with country measurement data before expansion.
- **Year 12 volume:** 12,000 kits/year, 0.57% of the filtered denominator, at €1,900 average; plus 40,000 installed systems buying €120/year monitoring, alarm, or test consumables.
- **Economics:** €27.6 million revenue; 50% gross margin; 20% operating margin = €5.52 million operating profit. At six times operating profit, less €2.0 million net obligations, equity value is €31.1 million.
- **Capital/ownership:** approximately €3.0 million cumulative certification, inventory, country launch, and support capital after paid proof; deposits, retained cash and debt fund most of it, with founder ownership at 60% after team equity and dilution.
- **Founder wealth and timing:** approximately €18.7 million equity in year 12; distributions are excluded. Reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash while the founder remains employed.

### Fatal dependencies

- House-to-house slab permeability and pipe-route variation may make a standard kit fictional rather than a product.
- Homeowners may pay for a detector but not for remediation, especially where the home-sale or insurance system does not force action.
- Measurement accreditation, electrical/building rules, and lung-cancer-related liability may require a service network that destroys part-time reachability.
- Verified reduction must remain stable across seasons; a quick post-test cannot substitute for the mandated long-duration result.

## 3. FloodMinute — Self-Testable Aperture Barrier Packs Sold Before the Next Warning

### Commercial insight and inside-view pain

People in a mapped flood zone do not primarily lack forecasts; they lack a protection product they can fit in minutes, store in a closet, and test on a dry day. Sandbags arrive late and create labour; fitted demountable systems often begin with a site visit and a four-figure quote. A ground-floor resident or shop owner wants a pre-sized, labelled pack for the exact door and airbrick openings, with an honest water-height limit and a repeatable seal test.

### Product and exact paid event

- A pre-sold two-opening pack: telescoping aluminium doorway barrier, replaceable compression/bladder gasket, airbrick caps, storage rack, water-height gauge, and a low-volume self-test channel that proves seal continuity without flooding the premises.
- Initial beachhead: ground-floor shops and detached homes on mapped Polish river-flood streets with standard inward/outward doorway reveals; basements and whole-building protection are excluded.
- **Paid event by day 120:** two unrelated mapped-risk shops or homeowners each pay a 30% deposit of 1,500 PLN toward a 4,500–6,000 PLN pack after a photo/measurement qualification. Delivery follows a witnessed static-water prototype test; the deposit is returned if the opening is outside the standard envelope.

### Cold start and resource bootstrap

1. Buy aluminium extrusions, gaskets, fasteners, airbrick caps, and cut parts from ordinary vendors; create the telescoping lock, self-test channel, measurement jig, and labelled deployment sequence.
2. Use Wody Polskie vector flood-risk layers plus public building/address data to send unaddressed mail to named ground-floor properties. The offer is a standard product and demonstration, not a risk report.
3. Spend before paid proof: 14,000 PLN prototypes and a rented water-test rig; 7,000 PLN independent engineering/test review; 5,000 PLN product-liability and instructions; 4,000 PLN mail/demo acquisition; 2,000 PLN contingency. **Maximum 32,000 PLN.**
4. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, own the locking/gasket design, tooling and jigs, tested product envelope, opening-fit library, deployment/error records, brand, and first paid inventory. No insurer, municipality, or emergency-service endorsement is assumed.

### Control point and copy risk

- **Control-tier hypothesis: Tier 2.** The tested product, measurement workflow, address-level acquisition data, and opening-fit catalogue are founder-owned, but metalwork is readily purchasable.
- **Strongest copy risk:** established demountable-barrier makers and door companies can copy a telescoping pack. The only plausible durable edge is a dense fit library, lower installed price/remake rate, credible self-testing, and reaching precisely mapped properties before disaster-season search auctions become expensive.

### Conservative founder-wealth bridge

- **Denominator:** EEA/Climate-ADAPT estimates 52 million Europeans live in potential flood-prone areas. At 2.25 people per household that is about 23 million households. Applying a 10% ground-floor, suitable-opening, ability-to-pay filter gives a **hypothesised 2.3 million eligible homes**, before adding SMEs.
- **Year 10 volume:** 25,000 household packs plus 5,000 small-business packs, together about 1.3% of the filtered home denominator, at €900 blended price = €27.0 million revenue.
- **Economics:** 48% gross margin; 18% operating margin = €4.86 million. At six times operating profit, less €2.0 million working-capital/product-liability obligations, equity value is €27.2 million.
- **Capital/ownership:** about €2.5 million cumulative test, tooling, inventory, claims reserve, and EU distribution capital after proof; founder retains 65% after team and financing dilution.
- **Founder wealth and timing:** about €17.7 million founder-owned equity by year 10, excluding distributions. Reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash while employed.

### Fatal dependencies

- A doorway barrier can create false safety if water enters through walls, drains, floors, or an unprotected second opening; the qualified product envelope must be commercially large.
- Any recognized performance certification may exceed the first-cycle time/capital cap or require destructive full-scale tests.
- Warning-time installation, storage discipline, gasket ageing, and user error may make a self-test feature insufficient for product-liability risk.
- Mapped exposure does not prove property-level willingness to pay; flood-memory recency may dominate conversion.

## 4. SiteMeter Wallet — Temporary-Power Ports That Charge the Consuming Trade

### Commercial insight and inside-view pain

On a multi-trade construction site, temporary electricity is usually a general-site overhead. The general contractor pays the main bill while high-draw subcontractors connect tools, heaters, drying equipment, and cabins with little price signal. Monitoring products show consumption, but the commercial action is weak unless the port itself identifies the trade, enforces a limit, and posts the charge against that subcontractor's prepaid site balance or contract retention.

### Product and exact paid event

- An eight-port temporary distribution board built around already certified protective devices and MID meters. Each keyed/RFID port is assigned to a subcontractor, enforces current/time limits, and posts internal cost allocation through an ordinary payment service provider or the GC's retention account.
- It is not an energy retailer: the GC remains the utility customer and uses the device for contractually agreed internal cost allocation. That legal framing is a hypothesis requiring Polish review.
- **Paid event by day 120:** two unrelated small general contractors each pay a 4,000 PLN deposit toward a hypothesised 12,000–15,000 PLN board for one live site, contingent on electrical conformity sign-off and an agreed internal-charge schedule.

### Cold start and resource bootstrap

1. Assemble a non-production demonstrator using purchased MID meters, breakers, industrial sockets, contactors, RFID readers, and a certified enclosure; founder-created assets are controller firmware, allocation logic, commissioning pack, and tamper record.
2. Recruit buyers directly from public permit/tender notices and contractor registers; demonstrate with portable loads at the contractor's office before asking for a deposit.
3. Spend before paid proof: 18,000 PLN parts/enclosure; 8,000 PLN electrician and conformity design; 5,000 PLN firmware/payment prototype; 5,000 PLN legal/insurance; 3,500 PLN demos and contingency. **Maximum 39,500 PLN.**
4. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, own the controller/firmware, board design and documentation, allocation/payment software, tamper history, and installed transaction records. Meters, enclosures, PSP, and assembly remain ordinary vendors.

### Control point and copy risk

- **Control-tier hypothesis: Tier 2 at POC; potentially Tier 1 for a contracted installed payment route.** The first board is owned and buildable, but the commercial control exists only when site contracts route internal charges through it.
- **Strongest copy risk:** temporary-power contractors, distribution-board makers, and monitoring vendors such as ReMoni can add RFID allocation and billing. Installed board estate, subcontractor contract templates, and payment history must create switching cost faster than they copy.

### Conservative founder-wealth bridge

- **Denominator:** Eurostat counts about 960,000 EU construction-of-buildings enterprises. A hypothesised 5% run multi-trade projects large enough to care, giving **48,000 eligible GCs**.
- **Year 10 installed base:** 2,500 GC customers, 5.2% of that denominator, averaging four active boards = 10,000 boards/80,000 metered ports. Sell 2,500 new/replacement boards/year at €3,500 (€8.75 million) plus €120 per active port/year (€9.6 million), for €18.35 million revenue.
- **Economics:** 55% blended gross margin; 24% operating margin = €4.40 million. At six times operating profit, less €2.0 million obligations, equity value is €24.4 million.
- **Capital/ownership:** about €3.0 million cumulative certification, inventory finance, support, and market-entry capital after paid proof; founder retains 60% after technical team and financing dilution.
- **Founder wealth and timing:** about €14.6 million founder equity in year 10, excluding distributions; 95% of after-tax distributable cash is reinvested during employment.

### Fatal dependencies

- Temporary electricity may be too small a cost line for GCs to create subcontractor conflict or change contracts.
- Polish/EU rules may treat cost allocation as regulated resale of electricity, or require additional certified-system approval beyond purchased MID components.
- Subcontractors may bypass ports, share credentials, or resist prepaid balances; enforcement cannot depend on constant site supervision.
- The board must survive outdoor abuse and electrical liability while remaining cheap enough to beat a normal temporary board plus spreadsheet allocation.

## 5. SkipShare Gate — Per-Trade Weigh-and-Charge Access for Construction Waste

### Commercial insight and inside-view pain

Polish law now requires source separation of at least six construction/demolition waste fractions, with potential penalties from 1,000 to 1 million PLN. On a live project, however, the main contractor sees a contaminated or overweight skip after the fact and cannot identify which trade caused the charge. Posters and waste software do not change the moment of disposal. The economically useful product is a physical gate that identifies the user, weighs each deposit, records the fraction, and posts the cost to the responsible subcontractor.

### Product and exact paid event

- A weatherproof retrofit chute/gate for standard site skips: RFID/keypad access, bag-level load cell, timestamped contamination photo, fraction selector, and internal charge allocation. It does not classify waste with AI or replace legal BDO records.
- **Paid event by day 120:** two unrelated general contractors each pay a 2,500 PLN deposit toward a hypothesised 18,000–22,000 PLN gate for a six-week live-site use, with the purchase contingent on a defined weigh-error and worker-use threshold.

### Cold start and resource bootstrap

1. Build a workshop rig from ordinary load cells, industrial access components, weather enclosure, camera, and fabricated chute; create the controller, user/charge workflow, and tamper/bypass evidence.
2. Reach buyers directly from building permits/tender awards and waste-cost roles in named GCs. Sell avoided contamination/overweight charges and enforceable subcontractor allocation, not a compliance dashboard.
3. Spend before paid proof: 19,000 PLN rig and fabricated chute; 6,000 PLN calibration/weather testing; 5,000 PLN legal, safety, and insurance; 4,000 PLN acquisition/site demos; 4,000 PLN contingency. **Maximum 38,000 PLN.**
4. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, own the gate design, firmware, calibration process, fraction/charge workflow, field-use and bypass history, and first routed charges. Skip suppliers and waste haulers are not required partners; the gate is bought by the GC and fits hired skips inside a defined dimensional envelope.

### Control point and copy risk

- **Control-tier hypothesis: Tier 2.** The physical gate and software are founder-controlled, but industrial weighing/access hardware is commodity. A signed project contract that makes the gate's record the subcontractor charge source would control a real transaction flow for that site.
- **Strongest copy risk:** Gillard already sells compactor access control with RFID and integrated weighing. It or a skip/compactor OEM can add the construction contract-allocation layer; the wedge must prove that a portable bag-level gate works on ordinary hired skips and that per-trade charging changes behaviour.

### Conservative founder-wealth bridge

- **Denominator:** Eurostat's 960,000 EU building-construction enterprises. A hypothesised 10% run sites with enough trades/waste spend, giving **96,000 eligible firms**.
- **Year 11 volume/base:** 3,000 gates/year at €4,500 and 10,000 active gates at €900/year for calibration, access, and charge routing. Annual hardware volume equals 3.1% of the filtered denominator; revenue is €22.5 million.
- **Economics:** 50% gross margin; 18% operating margin = €4.05 million. At six times operating profit, less €2.5 million working-capital/product-liability obligations, equity value is €21.8 million.
- **Capital/ownership:** about €3.5 million cumulative ruggedisation, certification, tooling, inventory, and country support capital after paid proof; founder retains 60% after team equity and financing.
- **Founder wealth and timing:** approximately €13.1 million founder equity by year 11; no distributions are added, and 95% of distributable after-tax cash is reinvested during employment.

### Fatal dependencies

- Workers can throw waste around the gate, share credentials, choose the wrong fraction, or move the device; a product that needs supervision is not scalable.
- Bag-level measurements may not reconcile with the hauler's legally relevant whole-skip/weighbridge charge; trade disputes could make the record commercially unusable.
- If charges are based on measured mass, legal-metrology requirements may expand certification beyond the budget.
- Gillard's existing access-and-weigh product shows that core hardware is not novel; only portability, contract routing, and behaviour economics can justify entry.

## Generator Takeaway

- **QuietPane EU** has the cleanest combination of a customer-visible result, pre-sold founder-created supply, and address-level asymmetric distribution. Its hard problem is not product existence; it is building a European tilt-turn fit/performance system fast enough that incumbent secondary-glazing firms cannot reduce it to a price contest.
- **RadonBlock** has a strong moment of urgency after a positive measurement, but standardisation and health/building liability are existential hypotheses.
- **FloodMinute** has unusually direct public-risk-map distribution and a high-loss customer problem, but whole-building leakage and certification can make a doorway product misleading.
- **SiteMeter Wallet** and **SkipShare Gate** control a commercial action rather than merely report data, yet both are exposed to fast incumbent copying and must prove that the allocated cost is large enough to change contractor behaviour.
