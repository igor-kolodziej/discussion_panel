## Direction 1 — CareChoice Book

### One-Sentence Thesis

Acquire the currently offered Berlin company with more than 1,000 monthly reimbursed care-consumables customers, preserve its statutory payer and reorder rail through a conditional share transfer, and grow it into the most transparent, individually configurable caregiver-managed supply account in Germany—without buying additional books.

### Customer Point of View

The real user is usually an adult child or other informal caregiver managing supplies for a person cared for at home. They do not want “a free box.” They want the correct gloves, bed pads, disinfectant and protective products to arrive without repeated forms, surplus products, silent substitutions, insurer disputes, or another call-centre task. The trade-off they already tolerate is accepting a generic provider workflow because the Pflegekasse reimburses eligible consumables.

The commercial opening is unusually concrete. GKV contract guidance says providers must not portray €42 as an automatic flat entitlement, impose a minimum order, or force a preconfigured bundle; the insured person must be able to configure the order to actual need and see telephone advice. A provider that can document preference, fit, permitted quantity, substitution consent, pause and delivery history may reduce churn, complaints and denied claims while complying better than a “max out the box” seller. This is a retention and claims-economics wedge, not a new reimbursement claim.

### Exact Live Acquisition Target

Nexxt-change listing **B-NA-A-4505**, published 23 March 2026, offers a Berlin business supplying consumable care aids with:

- a stable subscription customer base of **more than 1,000 customers**;
- **€250,000–€500,000** last annual revenue;
- **one to five employees**;
- an indicated **€50,000–€250,000** price range; and
- an online-commerce/health-services classification.

The listing is direct evidence that a relevant cash-producing entity is for sale. It is not evidence of EBITDA, churn, claim acceptance, supplier terms, lawful transfer, exact price, seller financing, or payer-contract continuity. Those are closing conditions.

The statutory revenue ceiling reconciles to the listing: 1,000 customers × €42 × 12 = €504,000 maximum annual reimbursement. This does not prove that all customers order or collect the maximum, but it makes the published scale internally plausible.

### Product Wedge

Keep the existing eligible product catalogue and delivery operation. Replace any fixed-box interface with a caregiver account that shows:

- the eligible individual, Pflegegrad and payer approval state;
- each allowed consumable, exact quantity/value and current monthly total;
- brand/substitution preference and allergy or size notes without medical advice;
- one-click pause, address change and transparent under-€42 order;
- a plain-language reason for every rejected or reduced claim; and
- a delivery and consent record exportable by the caregiver.

No diagnosis, new device, care recommendation or insurer partnership is needed. The company remains a contracted supplier of existing eligible consumables.

### Beachhead

The first market is not all elderly Germans. It is the acquired 1,000+ households and the next 300 adult caregivers who are actively searching online for an individually configurable §40 SGB XI supply account. The initial objective is to retain and improve the existing book, then prove a direct-search acquisition cohort. No care-service referral agreement is assumed.

### Primary Control Point

Control exists only if the founder acquires the **legal entity and operating rail together**:

1. insurer/Pflegekasse provider and billing contracts that remain effective after the change of control;
2. lawful customer contracts, consents, approval records and recurring-order permissions;
3. historical claim, denial, reorder, pause, product and gross-margin data;
4. brand, domain, phone number, payment/billing identifiers and customer-service records;
5. assignable supplier terms and at least two eligible supply sources for critical categories; and
6. retained operating staff for the transition.

A spreadsheet of names, a domain, or an asset purchase without written payer continuity has no control value. The preferred transaction is a 100% share purchase because the contracting legal entity remains the same, subject to counsel confirming all change-of-control and notification duties.

### Acquisition and Resource Bootstrap

The founder should make one conditional offer, not browse indefinitely:

1. **Days 1–21; ≤4,000 PLN:** obtain NDA data, 24 months of monthly customer, claim, cash, product, supplier, churn and complaint cohorts; reconcile revenue to insurer settlements and tax filings before commissioning broad diligence.
2. **Days 22–45; cumulative ≤12,000 PLN:** a German regulatory/privacy/transaction lawyer performs a narrow red-flag review of change-of-control provisions, provider status, customer-consent basis, seller liabilities and data-transfer position. The founder reconstructs contribution by active customer from source exports, with a fractional accountant checking the reconciliation.
3. **Days 46–75; cumulative ≤18,000 PLN:** sign a short exclusivity/option agreement costing no more than 2,000 PLN, creditable against closing, only if the seller accepts a maximum 20,000 PLN cash-at-closing structure with the balance as a non-recourse seller note paid from a fixed share of collected gross margin.
4. **Days 76–120:** sign a share purchase with escrow, seller transition, working-capital floor, claims warranties, supplier continuity and a price adjustment tied to verified active customers. Closing cash is capped at 20,000 PLN, so the cumulative founder cash at risk remains at or below 38,000 PLN until paid proof and below 100,000 PLN in total.
5. **Paid proof:** release the proof tranche only after the successor-controlled company receives one normal Pflegekasse settlement covering at least 50 previously active customers and ten new or reactivated customers, with positive item-level contribution and no contract suspension.

This is reachable only if the seller finances most of even the low end of the published price. At the 9 July 2026 NBP rate, the €50,000 low end is about 215,345 PLN. No seller-note willingness is published. **The first broker question is therefore whether a ≤20,000 PLN cash close plus 36–60-month receipts-capped note is acceptable. A “no” kills the direction immediately.** No personal guarantee outside the acquired company and no family capital are allowed.

### Unit Economics and Value Capture

Evidence:

- the statutory maximum is €42 per eligible customer per month from 1 January 2025;
- the target reports €250k–€500k revenue and >1,000 customers; and
- 4.89 million of Germany's 5.69 million people in need of care were cared for at home in 2023.

Decision-critical hypotheses to test from the data room:

- average collected revenue: at least **€36/customer/month**;
- product, pick/pack, shipping, claim and service gross margin: at least **40%**;
- monthly involuntary plus voluntary customer loss: at most **2.0%**, shown separately for death/ineligibility and provider switching;
- cash conversion: at most **60 days** from shipment to payer cash;
- direct-search fully loaded CAC: at most **€140**;
- twelve-month contribution after customer support: at least **1.5× CAC**; and
- claim denial/rework: below **4% of submitted value**.

At €38 average monthly collected revenue and 42% gross margin, annual gross profit is about €191 per active customer before central operating costs. The business cannot buy growth at €200–€300 CAC and still compound safely. Ten to thirty directly acquired search customers are enough to test this before opening spend.

### Cold Start and Distribution

The acquisition solves the first-customer cold start: the company already has more than 1,000 subscribers, staff, cash receipts and claims history. New-customer acquisition is deliberately limited to a founder-controlled channel during proof:

- exact-intent German search pages for `Pflegehilfsmittel beantragen`, `Pflegebox individuell`, `Pflegebox wechseln` and insurer-specific process questions;
- paid search only where each keyword/ad cohort is linked to approval, first shipment, collected reimbursement, churn and contribution; and
- compliant customer-initiated sharing, with no cash gift or representation that the €42 ceiling is an automatic entitlement.

The first repeatability gate is 30 new independent customers from search within 90 days at ≤€140 collected-CAC and forecast twelve-month contribution/CAC ≥1.5. Ambulant-care agencies, hospitals, insurers and charities are not required partners.

### Compounding Advantage and Copy Risk

The strongest asset is the acquired live book plus payer-accepted order history. With every month, the company can improve product-size fit, substitution decisions, shipment timing, claim completeness, churn prediction and caregiver self-service. That can raise retention and lower support/denial cost across a growing cohort.

This is not a monopoly. Competitors can copy the interface, and GKV rules already require individual configuration. Customers can switch providers. The strongest copy risk is an established provider using a larger purchase book and paid-search budget to offer the same transparent configuration at a lower cost. The business survives only if its claims history and caregiver workflow produce materially lower CAC, churn or service cost than that response. The existing book is Tier-1 control over present cash flow, not over the category.

### Founder Fit and Part-Time Operation

The founder's data background fits cohort economics, claims reconciliation, acquisition attribution, inventory forecasting and self-service automation. It does not qualify him to interpret German care law, advise patients, or run fulfilment personally. The existing German operating team must remain through transition, with a fractional German healthcare counsel and accountant. The founder works on data, controls and capital allocation for at most five hours per day. German-language customer service and fulfilment stay with employees/contractors.

He does not leave employment until there are at least six months of successor-controlled settlements, multiple acquisition cohorts with positive contribution, no unresolved provider-contract threat, operating cash after seller-note payments, a competent German operator, and twelve months of personal runway.

### Capital Tranches

- **Before paid proof:** maximum 40,000 PLN including diligence, counsel, option and closing cash. If the seller requires more before the first successor-controlled settlement, stop.
- **After first paid proof:** release up to 60,000 PLN remaining founder capital only after 50 legacy and ten new/reactivated claims settle with positive contribution and contracts remain effective.
- **Supplier or receivables finance:** only after three normal payer cycles and positive cash conversion; no personal cross-collateral beyond the acquired company.
- **Family 100,000 PLN:** excluded until at least 300 independently acquired or referred customers show repeat monthly demand, positive cohort contribution and stable payer settlement.

### Critical Dependencies and Kill Criteria

Kill the direction if any one of the following occurs:

1. seller rejects ≤20,000 PLN cash at closing plus a non-recourse receipts-capped note;
2. legal entity/provider contracts, customer permissions or insurer approvals do not survive the ownership change in writing;
3. fewer than 900 of the stated customers paid in at least two of the last three months;
4. verified normalized gross margin is below 40% or operating contribution before owner pay below 12%;
5. monthly provider-switching loss excluding death/ineligibility is above 2%;
6. payer cash conversion exceeds 60 days without non-recourse financing priced inside contribution;
7. more than 4% of submitted value is denied/reworked or compliance files show systematic over-ordering, fixed-box, minimum-order, consent or marketing violations;
8. top supplier, payer or traffic source represents more than 35% of gross profit without a tested substitute;
9. the retained German operator or legally required competence cannot be secured inside the total 100,000 PLN founder-cash cap; or
10. the 30-customer direct-search cohort costs more than €140 per collected customer or forecasts less than 1.5× twelve-month contribution/CAC.

- **Reachability screen:** conditional. It is compatible with 180 days, 40,000 PLN pre-proof risk and part-time work only if the seller accepts the low-cash, non-recourse note and payer/provider contracts remain effective. There is no public evidence of either condition.
- **Strongest unresolved hypothesis:** a seller asking €50k–€250k will accept approximately €4.6k cash at closing and finance the balance from collected gross margin while leaving the legal entity, staff and payer rail intact.
- **Generator decision:** this is the only direction in this search worth one advisory evaluation **after** the broker answers the seller-finance and change-of-control questions. It is not ready for an independent panel.

## Direction 2 — SafeWeave DACH

### Commercial Insight

Psychiatric, disability-care, geriatric and justice institutions do not primarily want “technical textiles.” They need bedding and garments that resist tearing, weaponisation, ligature formation or rapid destruction while remaining tolerable for the patient and defensible in procurement. A specialist supplier with known products, trained staff, institutional reorder history and exclusive rights can be more valuable than a new distributor agreement.

### Exact Target Evidence

Nexxt-change listing **A-26040002 / reference 2683570**, published 10 April 2026, offers 100% of a DACH specialist wholesaler founded in 2008. Its one-page broker profile says it is profitable, has one to five experienced staff, €350k–€500k annual revenue, a grown institutional customer base, digitised infrastructure and exclusive distribution rights. The sale is a succession process by bid; price, EBITDA, accounts, SKU economics, supplier identity and right term are not disclosed.

### Why It Does Not Advance Yet

The direct payer and current control are credible after a share transfer, but the financing and scale case are not. Germany alone has 16,505 care homes, and Europe has substantial hospital and justice populations; those are only upper bounds. The proposal would need at least 2,500 institutions spending an average €8,000 annually to produce €20m revenue. At hypothesised 45% gross and 17% operating margin, 7× operating profit and 75% ownership would imply €17.9m founder equity. Nothing published shows 2,500 eligible high-use institutions, €8,000 annual spend, expansion rights beyond DACH, 45% margin, a price inside seller-financed reach, or that the rights survive ownership change.

One bounded broker enquiry may ask for price, EBITDA, customer/reorder cohort, exclusive-right wording/term/change-of-control, supplier concentration and seller-note appetite. Unless those facts arrive together, this remains a specialist dealership whose core supplier can terminate or whose product can be substituted. **Research further only; not finalist-worthy on current evidence.**

## Direction 4 — RingTest Anchor

The commercial truth is sound: laboratories need external evidence that their measurements remain valid. PCA's current public portal lists **1,293 testing laboratories** and only **12 accredited proficiency-testing providers** in Poland. PCA states that accredited PT is widely used to demonstrate laboratory competence, and EPTIS lists thousands of regularly operated schemes worldwide. Accreditation can remain with an unchanged legal entity after a change of ownership subject to advance notification and assessment, as UKAS explicitly documents for accredited conformity-assessment bodies; Polish treatment would need PCA confirmation.

The asset would be a small accredited provider entity, its ISO/IEC 17043 scope, scheme methods, participant contracts, technical managers, sample-production route and historical results. The first paid proof would be an existing participant renewal after share transfer.

No named private provider is publicly offered for sale, no price or seller-financing route exists, and each new analyte needs technical competence, homogeneous/stable samples, statistics and possibly scope extension. The count asymmetry is not acquisition evidence. **Rejected at generation stage until a willing named seller with transferable technical staff and current participant revenue exists.**

## Decision-Critical Research Addendum

_Public-source pass completed 13 July 2026. This addendum answers the five evidence gaps raised in `evaluation_carechoice_book_v1.md`; it does not change the CareChoice Book candidate, score it, pivot it, panel it, or validate it. No seller, broker, payer, provider or authority was contacted._

### Decision Result

The exact Nexxt listing **B-NA-A-4505 does not publicly prove either prerequisite on which the acquisition wedge depends**:

1. it does not disclose a share-capable legal form or say that company shares are for sale; and
2. it does not disclose seller-finance willingness, let alone the **greater-than-90% seller financing** required at the bottom of the asking band.

The listing therefore cannot, on public evidence, support the proposed share-transfer-plus-seller-note transaction. Absence of a published term is not proof that the seller would refuse it, but it is not gate-usable evidence. Under the current no-contact scope, the exact candidate should be returned for re-evaluation and closed unless later-authorized private diligence produces both facts.

### 1. What The Exact Listing Does And Does Not Disclose

The current public listing, dated 23 March 2026, states only that a Berlin company supplies consumable care aids, has more than 1,000 subscription customers, up to five employees, €250,000–€500,000 last annual revenue, and an asking-price band above €50,000 through €250,000. Its field **“Inseratstyp: Privatperson/Firma” identifies the type of advertiser, not the target's legal form**.

The public record contains no target name, register number, GmbH/UG/AG/GbR/Einzelunternehmen designation, “share deal,” “asset deal,” “Anteilsverkauf,” or other transaction-form statement. ### 2. GKV/Pflegekasse Continuity: Explicit Rules Versus Inference

The current GKV-Spitzenverband contract page states that a provider needs a corresponding § 78 SGB XI/§ 127 SGB V contract and, subject to the pharmacy exception, prequalification before it may supply consumable care aids. The public P50 individual-provider contract also ties supply rights to the **provider**, a valid Institutionskennzeichen (IK), the necessary eligibility, and prior cost-coverage approval for the insured person. It requires notification of changes affecting the contract or supply authorization.

The clearest transfer rule is explicit: when one provider takes over the care cases of another provider, the acquiring provider must submit **a new cost-coverage application for every case**. Accordingly:

- **Asset deal or transfer to a different provider:** the reimbursement book does not simply move as a payment rail. The receiving provider must already qualify and contract, and each taken-over case needs a new application. This creates customer/payer re-papering and attrition risk.
- **Change of owner of a sole proprietorship:** GKV prequalification guidance expressly treats this as a material actual/legal change that must be reported to the prequalification body. It is not the unchanged-provider share-transfer shape assumed by the candidate.
- **Pure share purchase of a corporation:** German IHK guidance says that, in a share deal, the GmbH/legal entity remains in existence and its contracts, employees and debts remain with it. That supports a **general-law inference** that the same legal provider can retain its IK, contract participation and existing approvals if the legal entity, name, site, qualified responsible person, prequalification scope and supply operation truly remain unchanged.

That last point is not an explicit GKV/Pflegekasse guarantee. The GKV public material found does not say that every shareholder change is automatically continuity-safe, and the ARGE IK record for a registered company includes its managing directors/shareholders. Changes to IK data are made in writing. A change of managing director, qualified responsible person, name, legal form, site or scope can trigger notification and evidence review even if shares rather than assets are purchased. The target's actual contract may also contain change provisions not visible in the listing.

**Proven:** a different provider taking over cases must make new case-by-case cost-coverage applications; provider contract, eligibility/prequalification and IK are required; material changes must be notified.  
**Conditionally supported but not target-proven:** continuity under a pure share purchase where the identical corporate provider and all eligibility conditions remain intact.  
**Unavailable:** the target's legal form, IK, prequalification certificate, contract/accession, change provisions, responsible person and written payer/GKV continuity treatment.

### 4. Equity And Acquisition-Debt Structure Actually Required

Using the same 9 July 2026 NBP reference rate of **4.3069 PLN/EUR** and the candidate's existing caps:

- the 18,000 PLN diligence/option budget is approximately **€4,179** of transaction cost;
- the maximum 20,000 PLN purchase consideration paid by the founder is approximately **€4,644**; the creditable option is part of, not additional to, that purchase consideration;
- the total pre-proof founder cash remains 38,000 PLN, approximately **€8,823**, but only 20,000 PLN reduces the purchase price;
- at exactly €50,000, the minimum seller note would be about **€45,356, or 90.71%** of price; because the advertised lower bound is “over €50,000,” the actual required amount and percentage are slightly higher;
- at €250,000, the note would be about **€245,356, or 98.14%** of price.

Even at the artificial €50,000 floor, the note's minimum principal payment is about **€1,260/month for 36 months** or **€756/month for 60 months** at zero interest. At an illustrative 6% annual rate those payments become about €1,380 and €877. The listing supplies no EBITDA, cash balance, working-capital requirement, tax liabilities, claims reserves, debt, or permissible debt-service amount, so affordability cannot be established from revenue.

The candidate therefore requires all of the following—not merely a generic acquisition loan:

1. a purchase of 100% of a share-capable target whose provider identity remains unchanged;
2. 20,000 PLN total founder purchase consideration, with the separately budgeted 18,000 PLN covering transaction work;
3. a seller note for more than 90.71% of even the theoretical lowest price, paid only from verified collected receipts or gross contribution, without a personal guarantee outside the acquired business;
4. an agreed working-capital floor left in the target at closing, so the seller does not extract the cash needed to buy inventory, fund payroll, absorb denials and bridge payer settlement;
5. price adjustment/escrow for invalid customers, denials and undisclosed liabilities; and
6. no outside equity at closing unless ownership is recalculated explicitly.

A seller note is debt, not dilution. If the founder owns 100% at closing and later issues only the stated 12% to outside investors, founder ownership is **88%, not 78%**. A 78% terminal stake is internally coherent only if a further 10% employee/management pool is actually issued in addition to that 12%. Terminal founder value must equal founder ownership multiplied by **equity value after subtracting outstanding seller-note and other net debt**; retained cash or distributions cannot also be counted inside the same enterprise value.

**Proven:** the minimum financing percentages and payments implied by the candidate's own cash cap and the published price band.  
**Required but unavailable:** seller acceptance, note tenor/rate/covenants, target free cash flow, working-capital floor, liability adjustment, debt capacity, and whether a 10% pool is intended.

### 5. Corrected CAC And Contribution Arithmetic

The earlier €140 CAC rule is arithmetically incompatible with the same €38 monthly collection and 42% gross-margin assumptions:

```text
12-month gross profit before variable support
= €38 × 12 × 42%
= €191.52

Maximum CAC at a 1.5× 12-month contribution/CAC hurdle
= (€191.52 − 12-month variable support/rework/returns cost) ÷ 1.5
```

Even with zero post-gross-profit support cost, the maximum CAC is therefore **€127.68**, not €140. Sensitivities before churn are €111.68 with €2 per active account per month of additional variable cost and €95.68 with €4.

The acquisition test should use collected, churn-adjusted contribution rather than assume twelve full months. At 2% monthly churn, an acquired account has approximately **10.764 expected active months** in its first year. On the same €38 collection and 42% gross-margin assumptions:

| Additional variable support/rework cost | Churn-adjusted first-year contribution | Maximum CAC at 1.5× |
|---:|---:|---:|
| €0/account/month | €171.79 | €114.53 |
| €2/account/month | €150.27 | €100.18 |
| €4/account/month | €128.74 | €85.83 |

The corrected, candidate-consistent kill rule is:

> CAC must be no greater than churn-adjusted, collected first-year contribution after all variable fulfilment, support, rework, returns and claim-loss costs, divided by 1.5.

The €140 threshold is superseded for re-evaluation. The listing provides none of the cohort, cost or claims data needed to choose among the sensitivities, so the revised formula is proven arithmetic, not proof of viable CAC.
