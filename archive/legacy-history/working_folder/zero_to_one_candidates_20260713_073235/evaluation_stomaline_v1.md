# Zero-to-One Evaluation: StomaLine Workwear

Advisory evaluation only. This is not a simulated-panel score, real-chat score, validation result, or confirmation. StomaLine is judged exactly as written and is not rewritten here.

## Diagnosis

StomaLine starts from a credible human truth: some people with a stoma want ordinary-looking work trousers that do not cross, compress, or visually expose the pouch, and fixed-height garments cannot fit every stoma position. The exact business is nevertheless much less novel than the candidate claims: Lydda Wear already sells personalised semi-classic ostomy trousers, sends three trial garments, adjusts waist height, retains the customer's sizing for repeat orders, and prices casual trousers at €59, versus StomaLine's proposed €220 first-trouser price. The cited pain studies do not establish willingness to pay for coordinate-fit outerwear, while Colostomy UK says most ostomates can find suitable clothing in ordinary retail and lists multiple specialist clothing substitutes. Reachable deposits are possible within the 40,000 PLN cap, but the Founder Wealth Gate fails because 75,000 annual active customers, €320 annual spend, 63% gross margin, 18% operating margin, and 82% ownership are stacked on an unmeasured employed/premium-outerwear segment and a control point that incumbents or ordinary tailoring operations can reproduce.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Evidence map

It does not test coordinate-fit trousers, price sensitivity, purchase frequency, returns, or whether outerwear performs better than ordinary trousers plus support underwear. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31275585/) |

German and UK figures sit inside the European geography and cannot be added again. Temporary/permanent definitions, stoma type, age, income, work status, body geometry, and geographic serviceability remain unresolved. |

Semi-custom international apparel can carry gross margin, but the exact margin requires the bespoke workload and returns to collapse much more than has been demonstrated. |
| Direct search/community acquisition can scale | **Unsupported hypothesis** | There is no keyword volume, click cost, qualified-visit conversion, deposit conversion, community permission, referral rate, or channel-level CAC. The incumbent and association pages already occupy high-intent search space. Public-community participation is not the same as owned distribution and can be restricted when commercial. |
| Measurement-to-pattern data becomes a control point | **Prospective and unproved** | Base blocks, grading rules, pattern files, and customer fit profiles can be owned. However, one patternmaker/CMT contract, ordinary apparel CAD, and the same measurement fields let an incumbent reproduce the process. Twenty to thirty orders and three pattern families do not establish a statistically superior corpus, switching cost, or exclusive access. |
| The offer can remain non-medical | **Plausible but not automatic** | Fit, discretion, and comfort claims can plausibly keep the garment in ordinary consumer-product territory if the company avoids leakage, hernia, compression, treatment, and appliance-performance claims. The garment still needs ordinary product-safety work under the EU General Product Safety Regulation, and the proposed stoma coordinates/pouch-family history are health-related personal data requiring a documented lawful basis, minimisation, security, retention, and explicit handling. [EU GPSR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/2026-05-29/eng) and [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) |

### Candidate hypotheses, not evidence

- Three repeatable pattern families cover at least 70% of paid orders without turning the company into bespoke tailoring.
- Remote self-measurement and a guided photo check produce an alteration/fit-return rate below 15% after one iteration across varying body shapes, scars, stoma positions, pouch systems, posture, and changes in weight.
- At least 24 people place €60–€100 deposits, complete measurement, pay the €190–€240 balance, and accept the stated fit envelope within 180 days.
- A customer who needs one work trouser spends more than €320 in twelve months and repeats often enough to make fit-profile switching costs commercially important.
- Mature CAC falls below €45 without influencer, association, hospital, stoma-nurse, manufacturer, or established-retailer distribution.
- The company can preserve 63% gross margin and 18% operating margin while supporting remote measurement, cross-border shipping, alterations, returns, remakes, technical pattern exceptions, multilingual care, privacy, and a high-empathy customer experience.
- A corpus based on coordinates and pouch families predicts fit better than simpler high/low-waist and body-measurement rules and improves fast enough to outrun Lydda Wear, SIIL, other adaptive brands, and ordinary tailors.
- Seventy-five thousand annual active customers are reachable from the employed, outerwear-constrained, premium-paying segment rather than from the full ostomy installed base.

### Consequential missing facts

1. A non-overlapping, bottom-up denominator for permanent-stoma adults who are employed or frequently buy smart-casual outerwear, have an unresolved waistband/pouch fit problem, buy online in serviceable countries, and will pay at least €190.
2. Twenty or more observed purchase decisions against explicit substitutes: ordinary high-waisted/stretch trousers, existing support underwear, Lydda Wear's €59–€90 trousers, local alterations, and other adaptive jeans/trousers.
3. Direct competitor purchase tests and teardown: true delivered price, fit process, material/construction quality, lead time, return/alteration policy, customer satisfaction, and where coordinate fields materially improve the outcome.
4. Contract quotes for patternmaking, CMT, two fabrics, sample rounds, batch sizes, remakes, alterations, and international shipping, plus gross margin after expected refunds and labor.
5. Keyword volumes, CPC, qualified landing-page conversion, deposit-to-final-payment conversion, refund rate, and referral/repeat rate without relying on community administrators or trusted medical intermediaries.
6. Measured minutes of patternmaker/customer-service labor on first, repeat, and exception orders. The proposed 45-minute repeat-order kill condition is far too high for the scale economics.
7. Proof that the coordinate/pouch-family corpus reduces return/remake cost or increases conversion/repeat purchase relative to simpler size and waist-height rules.
8. Exact privacy, photo-handling, product-safety, liability, claims, and cross-border return opinions for the released product and marketing language.

## Overall Score

**38/100.** Weighted points are **75.5 out of 200** (`10 × 75.5 / 20 = 37.75`, rounded to 38). This is an advisory evaluation score only. The failed Founder Wealth Gate independently prohibits `pursue`.

## Control Point

**Current tier: Tier 3. Proposed 12-month asset: still Tier 3, with a possible later path to Tier 2 only after evidence not present.**

The founder currently controls no patterns, production capacity, orders, customer fit profiles, distribution, exclusive pouch data, or validated fit algorithm. Signed CMT/patternmaking terms and ownership of production files are good operating hygiene, not scarcity: patternmakers, apparel CAD, fabric, and sewing capacity are purchasable. Three pattern families covering perhaps 20–30 orders would create useful internal knowledge but not a meaningful barrier. Tier 2 would require a substantially larger paid cohort and evidence that accumulated coordinates materially reduce CAC, remakes, or fit failures in a way that simple measurement options cannot reproduce; the exact candidate has not shown this is attainable inside 12 months.

**Strongest copy risk:** Lydda Wear already demonstrates the same commercial skeleton—specialist semi-classic trousers, remote trial garments, adjustable waist height, many fabrics, and stored customer sizing—at €59–€90. It or SIIL can add stoma-side/height and pouch-family fields by hiring one technical patternmaker or changing an internal process. An ordinary tailor can handle high-value exceptions locally, while mainstream high-waisted/stretch trousers plus specialist underwear solve the majority case. StomaLine therefore faces both incumbent feature copy and low-tech substitution before its corpus becomes large.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.**

The candidate states customers, pricing, revenue, gross and operating margin, capital, ownership, reinvestment, valuation, and timing, and it does not double-count distributions. The arithmetic is internally correct: 75,000 customers × €320 = €24m revenue; 18% operating margin produces €4.32m operating profit; 5× gives €21.6m business value; 82% ownership gives about €17.7m founder equity. Expected and strong cases similarly compute from their assumptions.

The bridge to those inputs is not conservative:

1. **The denominator is wrong for the buying decision.** The cited 1.4–1.7m U.S./European installed base includes retired, unemployed, temporary-stoma, lower-income, non-online, ordinary-clothing-satisfied, and medically/body-shape ineligible people. The candidate needs employed or socially active adults with a persistent trouser problem who buy premium coordinate-fit outerwear. That number is not estimated.
2. **The raw share understates actual penetration.** Seventy-five thousand is only 4.4%–5.3% of the raw installed base but could be a very large share of the true premium workwear segment. Colostomy UK's statement that most users find ordinary clothing directly weakens the conversion from installed base to eligible buyer.
3. **Annual spend is unproved and price-anchored downward.** €320 implies roughly 1.3–1.45 garments per year at the proposed price. Lydda Wear sells a comparable specialist trouser for €59–€90, while high-waisted ordinary trousers and alterations may cost far less than €220. No repeat cohort supports annual spend.
4. **Margin assumptions require the bespoke work to disappear.** Sixty-three percent gross margin and 18% operating margin at €24m require remote measurements, pattern exceptions, CMT, cross-border returns, remakes, fabric complexity, support, and privacy/liability work to standardise. The candidate itself admits that bespoke patternmaking kills the model, but supplies no proof that three pattern families will cover 70% or that first/second fit converges.
5. **The direct distribution burden is enormous.** At 75,000 active annual customers and €45 target CAC, annual acquisition/retention spending and qualified traffic are substantial. There is no evidence for exact-intent search volume, conversion, organic ranking, community reach, referral, or retention. The founder has no privileged medical or community channel.
6. **The control point does not justify premium valuation durability.** A 5× operating-profit multiple is not inherently heroic for a durable brand, but the saved profile and pattern corpus do not yet create a durable brand, proprietary supply, or switching cost. The closest competitor already stores customer measurements.
7. **Ownership and capital are not the main problem.** Reinvestment and customer-funded inventory can preserve ownership, but €1.5m cumulative growth capital and 82% founder ownership only matter after the missing demand/distribution bridge. They cannot compensate for an unsupported customer denominator.

At the exact conservative economics, €10m of founder equity would require roughly €2.44m of operating profit, €13.55m revenue, and about **42,350 annual active customers** before considering liabilities or a multiple discount. That is still a global niche-apparel distribution achievement drawn from an unmeasured eligible segment. A non-heroic sensitivity—30,000 customers × €280 spend × 15% operating margin × 5× × 80% ownership—produces about **€5.0m** founder equity, well below the gate. Expected (140,000) and strong (300,000) customers scale the same unproved WTP, repeat, acquisition, and margin mechanism; they do not repair the conservative case.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS, narrowly, for deposit/preorder evidence only.**

The candidate can plausibly keep corporate employment and obtain at least one €60–€100 deposit within 180 days while spending no more than 40,000 PLN. Patternmaking, 8–12 prototypes, a storefront, direct photography, and a small acquisition test are ordinary contracted build steps rather than a scientific breakthrough. The offer does not require a hospital, association, pouch manufacturer, retailer, influencer, or speculative partnership, and made-to-order deposits can fund the first batch.

The pass is fragile:

- The budget spends the entire permitted 40,000 PLN before broad proof and leaves no schedule/cash buffer; an overrun must stop the project rather than draw further founder capital.
- A single deposit can prove some payment intent, but it does not prove final payment after measurement, acceptable fit, repeat purchase, positive contribution, or scalable acquisition. The candidate's stronger 24–30-deposit and first-delivery tests remain necessary before further capital.
- Direct acquisition is not evidenced. If communities prohibit commercial posts and exact-intent search volume is thin, the founder has no controlled audience to replace them.
- The unknown founder lacks lived apparel and ostomy credibility. Finished samples, transparent limits, a technically credible patternmaker, privacy discipline, and a no-clinical-claims boundary must carry the trust burden.
- Remote measurement, photo review, returns, and alterations can consume more than the founder's part-time capacity before repeat rules are stable.
- The gate should be treated as failed if deposits require medical efficacy language, if product/legal review pushes the item into a medical-device/accessory route, if first-batch quotes exceed the cap, or if early deposits are mostly refunded after measurement exclusions.

No further spending from the founder's original 100,000 PLN is justified until multiple independent final-paying deliveries show positive contribution after acquisition, remakes, alterations, returns, and support. Family capital remains excluded.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead has a recognizable customer behavior but not a quantified market: employed adults who already alter, size around, conceal, or avoid trousers. English-speaking Europe plus Germany and Poland is operationally broad for a first silhouette because it adds languages, returns, fit expectations, VAT/consumer law, and customer support before a single local cohort is dominated.

The saved profile gives a logical path to jeans, formal trousers, skirts, loungewear, and outer layers. It is not asymmetric expansion yet. Every new silhouette and fabric changes drape, expansion volume, waistband geometry, grading, manufacturing, and returns. Adaptive brands can move inward using their existing audiences and product teams, while StomaLine must acquire each customer without trusted-category distribution. Polish manufacturing can lower cost and speed iteration, but Italy already has a direct offer and the supply advantage is not exclusive. The candidate can potentially become a useful specialist apparel brand; it has not shown a path to profitable niche domination or to expanding outward faster than established adaptive/apparel sellers can move inward.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The closest direct competitor invalidates the claimed gap and control point.** Lydda Wear already offers personalised semi-classic ostomy trousers, remote size trials, adjustable waist height, many fabrics, and saved sizing for repeat orders at €59–€90.
2. **Founder Wealth depends on an unmeasured denominator.** Seventy-five thousand active annual customers are drawn from a raw installed base rather than the permanent, employed/socially active, premium-outerwear-paying, unresolved-fit segment.
3. **Willingness to pay is unsupported and faces a 2.4×–3.7× direct price gap.** The pain studies do not test trousers or payment; ordinary clothing, existing support garments, specialist trousers, and local alterations are credible substitutes.
4. **Annual repeat spending is asserted.** No evidence supports €320 per year, second-garment behavior, category expansion, or low churn after a customer solves the immediate fit need.
5. **The data/control point is prospective and easy to copy.** Coordinates, pouch family, base blocks, digital patterns, and fit histories are useful but not exclusive; one patternmaker/process change lets an incumbent add them.
6. **Custom labor and returns can destroy the margin.** Heterogeneous stoma positions, scars, pouch types, body shapes, posture, self-measurement errors, fabric behavior, and weight change can keep each order bespoke.
7. **Direct distribution is not demonstrated.** Search volume, CPC, community permission, referral, conversion, and CAC are missing, and the founder lacks a trusted medical/community relationship or established apparel brand.
8. **Founder fit is weak for the wedge's hardest work.** The founder's data skills help only after enough correct fit outcomes exist; early success depends on technical patternmaking, intimate customer understanding, apparel operations, merchandising, and high-empathy support.
9. **The non-medical boundary narrows useful claims.** Avoiding leakage, hernia, compression, and appliance-performance claims is prudent, but it also prevents the company from using the strongest clinical pain evidence as direct product efficacy evidence.
10. **The conservative case is not conservative.** International scale, premium pricing, high apparel gross/operating margins, low CAC, repeat purchases, and 82% ownership must all hold simultaneously for 9–11 years.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Some people do not want to look or feel like medical-device users; they want ordinary-looking clothing whose structure silently accommodates the only body/appliance coordinates that matter. Separating fit/discretion from clinical support is a real behavioral insight. It is not enough to support the exact DTC trouser business, its proposed price, its data moat, or its founder-wealth scale.

## Recommendation

**kill**

Kill the exact StomaLine coordinate-fit DTC workwear shape for this run. It may support a thoughtful small specialist apparel business, and a paid deposit is reachable, but the direct incumbent, price anchor, unmeasured premium segment, custom-fit operations, unproved annual repeat, weak founder fit, and one-process-change copy risk make the €10m founder-wealth path non-credible. A cosmetic change in silhouette, fabric, coordinate fields, wording, or customer-count arithmetic would not repair these structural objections.
