# Zero-to-One Candidate: FieldWire Cell

Candidate generation only — not evaluated, scored, panelled, chatbot-reviewed, confirmed, or validated. Research date: 2026-07-13. All prices, performance, buyer behaviour, margins, volumes, valuations, service attachment, and market-size implications not explicitly supported by a linked source are hypotheses to test.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Sell a founder-owned, compact wire-preparation cell that turns a small contractor's mixed terminal schedule into labelled, ferruled, verified job packs at a target price near €12,000—low enough to repay from skilled labour saved, yet capable of compounding through wire recipes, job-format importers, cassette exchange, and installed-machine service data.

## Founder Fit

The founder can own the scheduler, data model, experiment design, vision checks, process records, pricing model, and outbound system. His parents' renewable-installation company can provide a workshop, technicians, real but non-qualifying internal wiring schedules, scrap cable, and a controlled alpha site. This access removes the first workflow-data and test-site cold start; it does not count as paid proof, a customer, or a durable moat.

The founder does not yet have machine-design, crimp-process, or conformity expertise. Those are bounded gaps rather than presumed competencies: use a contract mechatronics engineer, a qualified electrician/panel builder, and an independent machinery-safety adviser for quoted packages with written IP assignment. The founder keeps the corporate job and limits his own work to 15–20 hours a week during proof. Repetitive fabrication, wiring, and testing are contracted or performed by technicians rather than becoming indefinite founder labour.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The initial customer is a 10–50-person Polish control-panel, HVAC-controls, renewable-energy, battery, or building-automation contractor preparing at least 1,000 single-core internal-panel wires per week in many small batches. It builds repeated systems, but its mix is too variable and its volume too low to resemble an automotive harness factory.

The buyer's desired outcome is not “automation.” It is tomorrow morning's complete, correctly grouped job pack without using a senior electrician to interpret the terminal list, measure, cut, strip, ferrule, label, check, and sort every wire. The current workaround is a bench of separate manual or semi-automatic tools, handwritten or spreadsheet lists, and a second check before wiring. The tolerated trade-off is low capital cost in exchange for skilled labour, late-evening preparation, and occasional rework.

The decision-relevant pain is paid skilled minutes per **accepted** job pack, not raw machine throughput. The hypothesis is that the target shops currently spend 15–25 paid hours per week on this work and value loaded skilled labour at 80–140 PLN/hour. Those figures must be replaced with each buyer's observed baseline; they are not established market facts.

## Product Wedge

FieldWire Cell is a guarded workshop machine for a deliberately narrow first envelope: flexible single-core copper control and switchboard wire, initially 0.5–6 mm², using a short approved list of wire, ferrule, and label families. It:

1. imports a CSV/XLSX terminal schedule or accepts manual line entry;
2. selects one of a limited number of loaded wire feeds;
3. measures, cuts, and strips both ends;
4. applies approved wire-end ferrules;
5. prints or applies both-end identifiers;
6. checks the process record, label identity, length tolerance, and end-to-end continuity/resistance against the configured recipe; and
7. ejects wires into job/terminal-group bins in installation sequence.

The alpha may require supervised cassette changes and some manual transfer between guarded modules. The commercial beta must report operator-attention time and every exception honestly; “automatic” never excludes setup, replenishment, changeover, jams, inspection, or rework from the benchmark.

The first product excludes third-party PV DC connector assemblies, high-voltage cable, shielded/multicore cable, customer-specific crimp contacts, and safety claims about the completed panel. Those categories would multiply tooling, validation, and liability before the core labour-saving claim is proved.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Rittal already proves that a digital job can drive automated cutting, stripping, ferruling, labelling, and order picking; Komax and Schleuniger already provide broad cut/strip and modular wire-processing platforms. Therefore this is not a technology-white-space thesis.

The narrower commercial hypothesis is that these product families leave an under-served economic band: contractors with genuinely mixed work and enough weekly wire volume to waste skilled hours, but too little volume, floor space, process-engineering capacity, or capital to justify an industrial wire terminal. FieldWire wins only if an unknown buyer can see its own schedule run, obtain a sub-24-month payback at roughly €12,000, and operate it without a wire-processing specialist.

The product is invalid if written incumbent quotes show that Rittal, Komax, Schleuniger, or another established supplier already offers the same end-to-end job-pack outcome at a comparable all-in price, footprint, changeover burden, and payback. “Cheaper than incumbents” is a hypothesis, not a fact.

## Underused Momentum

The EU's electrification and building-renovation agenda is expanding the volume and variety of panel and control work, while the JRC and CINEA describe installer/construction skills pressure. This is relevant timing, not automatic demand for this machine. The more direct momentum is that mature dereelers, cut/strip modules, ferrule presses, label printers, low-cost motion control, cameras, and AI-assisted job-format parsing can now be cash-purchased and integrated without an OEM licence.

EU machinery rules also create a common product-safety frame for eventual cross-border sales. Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027. Conformity is a cost and market-access requirement, not a moat by itself.

## Beachhead Market

Begin with Polish small panel builders and HVAC/building-controls contractors within a two-hour drive of Ostrów Wielkopolski or Warsaw that:

- process at least 1,000 eligible wires per week;
- use repeatable ferrule and label families;
- produce many batches of 5–50 rather than one long homogeneous run;
- can export or clean a terminal schedule; and
- have an owner or production manager who can approve a 52,000 PLN-equivalent machine from a measured payback case.

Renewable installers are useful alpha users, but the beachhead is not “all electricians.” Shops below the volume threshold will not save enough labour; large industrial harness plants already have stronger equipment and procurement relationships.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

The commercial hypothesis is a direct sale or 24–36-month equipment lease at a target list price of €12,000 (about 52,000 PLN for planning), plus an optional €1,200/year care-and-cassette plan. The recurring plan includes annual calibration/inspection, firmware and job-import updates, remote diagnosis, a pre-calibrated exchange cassette/wear-parts allowance, and approved label/ferrule feed packs. Commodity wire and ferrules remain available separately; recurring value must come from uptime and validated convenience, not an artificial consumables lock-in.

### Buyer labour benchmark

Before any external deposit, run an unedited benchmark on one real schedule containing at least 100 accepted wires, at least 20 line items, a median line batch of no more than five, at least four lengths, and at least three eligible wire/ferrule recipes.

The manual control includes schedule interpretation, spool retrieval, measuring, cutting, stripping, ferruling, both-end labelling, second-person checking, bundling, scrap, and rework. The FieldWire arm includes file cleanup, setup, spool/cassette changes, machine-attention time, exception handling, inspection, rework, and bundling. Report both elapsed time and paid hands-on skilled minutes.

Minimum hypotheses required before deposits:

- at least 35% fewer paid skilled minutes per accepted job pack;
- at least 25% shorter elapsed completion time;
- zero wrong-label, missing-wire, wrong-job-bin, or out-of-tolerance-length critical errors in the 100-wire demonstration;
- no worse ferrule/strip acceptance than the buyer's manual control; and
- less than 1% total rework across the first 1,000 externally produced wires.

At 1,000 wires/week, a buyer baseline of 15–25 hours/week, and a 35% labour reduction, annual savings would be 252–420 hours over 48 working weeks. At the unverified 80–140 PLN loaded-hour range, that equals roughly 20,000–59,000 PLN/year before scrap, rework, or scheduling benefits. The target 52,000 PLN machine therefore implies an 11–31-month labour-only payback. Only buyers whose measured case is at or below 24 months enter the initial beachhead.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

The founder creates a named account list from public business directories, association member pages, Google Maps, trade-fair exhibitor lists, and company websites; no distributor, celebrity, or privileged introduction is required.

**First two:** contact 40 unrelated panel shops, HVAC-control builders, renewable installers, and building-automation contractors. Invite qualified prospects to bring one live schedule to the family workshop for a side-by-side run. Require two unrelated companies to each pay a non-refundable 5,000 PLN deposit, credited against a 15,000 PLN four-week guarded beta pilot, only after seeing their own schedule processed. The family business cannot pay either deposit.

**Customers three to ten:** expand to a 200-account Poland/CEE list; publish the full benchmark method, an uncut run, eligible-recipe envelope, and buyer ROI calculator; hold monthly bring-your-own-schedule demo days. Convert only measured sub-24-month-payback accounts. Hypothesis: one founder plus a part-time technical salesperson can close eight more customers within 12–18 months after successful delivery.

**Customers eleven to 100:** reuse verified schedules as format importers and anonymized proof cases, run direct demonstration days in regional industrial centres, and hire one application salesperson only after ten paid installations. Leasing and rental-to-buy may be cash-purchased from ordinary finance providers after direct unit economics exist, but no financing partnership is existential. A reseller channel is optional after 30 machines; it is not the cold-start assumption.

Hypotheses to test are a 90-day median sales cycle after an on-site/live demo, €2,500 blended CAC, at least 20% demo-to-paid-pilot conversion among volume-qualified shops, and at least 50% pilot-to-purchase conversion.

## Primary Control Point

Within 12 months and 100,000 PLN, the founder must own—not merely access—the following resource bundle:

- the integrated alpha/beta machine, guarded fixture, and test rig;
- mechanical CAD, electrical drawings, bill of materials, toolhead and cassette interfaces;
- scheduler, job data model, firmware, vision/verification routines, and source repositories;
- approved wire/ferrule/strip/label recipes with acceptance tests;
- the mixed-job benchmark corpus and exception/changeover logs; and
- the machinery risk file, verification records, user instructions, and supplier traceability.

Every contractor signs foreground-IP assignment, confidentiality, source-file handover, editable-format delivery, and non-exclusive background-component terms before work starts. Purchased modules must be replaceable from at least two vendors or treated as a kill dependency. The family workshop grants test access but owns none of the product IP.

The machine alone is copyable. The intended compounding control point is the combination of job-format importers, recipe/changeover data, cassette interfaces, field-failure history, installed service relationships, and a low-cost integration architecture. This remains vulnerable to one incumbent product-line extension and must earn speed and switching costs before it deserves to be called durable.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

The bootstrap uses direct access without pretending the related family company is market validation:

1. Freeze the first eligible wire/ferrule envelope from actual internal jobs and record the manual benchmark.
2. Cash-purchase ordinary modules and fabrication; no exclusive supplier or equipment-maker licence is required.
3. Assemble a founder-owned supervised alpha in the family workshop for at most 38,000 PLN.
4. Obtain a machinery-safety/conformity route and capped quote before inviting paid deposits.
5. Demonstrate at least 100 mixed wires unedited, then let external prospects run their own schedules in the controlled workshop.
6. Collect two unrelated 5,000 PLN deposits by day 180.
7. Spend further founder capital only to integrate the existing alpha into one guarded beta; deliver that same beta sequentially to both paying pilot customers within 12 months.
8. Build no second sale unit from founder cash. After the first successful pilot, require a 50% purchase-order prepayment, positive first-unit contribution economics, and then use the customer's payment to finance that unit's bill of material.

This path produces technical performance, paid demand, a deliverable beta, and founder-owned IP before serious scale capital. It does not require an OEM, distributor, insurer, certification-body, or family-company purchase commitment.

## Distribution Advantage

The initial acquisition mechanism is a measurable “bring tomorrow's schedule” demonstration, not a generic machinery pitch. The buyer sees its own mix, labour, errors, and payback rather than accepting the founder's authority. This is suitable for an unknown founder and minimizes status-based trust.

If it works, every installed shop supplies new supported schedule formats, recipe edge cases, before/after economics, and a local reference. A searchable compatibility library and remote pre-qualification let later salespeople reject poor-fit prospects before travel. Direct sales preserve the learning loop; distributors are introduced only when the product and service burden are already bounded.

The strongest copy risk is Rittal, Komax/Schleuniger, or a low-cost Asian integrator combining existing modules with simple job-pack software and using an established service network. FieldWire must reach a lower all-in price, faster mixed-job setup, and sufficient installed-data/service density before that response.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

**Phase 1:** dominate one narrow use case—single-core, ferruled, labelled internal-panel wire in low-volume mixed job packs—rather than claiming universal wire processing.

**Phase 2:** add verified recipe cassettes, schedule importers for common electrical-design exports, remote diagnosis, and job-sequence sorting. Sell the care-and-cassette plan on measured uptime.

**Phase 3:** expand from Polish panel/HVAC-control shops into CEE prefabricated building services, switchgear subcontractors, industrial-maintenance workshops, and service-van/depot cells. Each adjacent envelope receives separate technical and conformity validation.

**Phase 4:** offer a modular machine family with additional conductor ranges, contact-specific toolheads, and line balancing for multi-cell shops. High-voltage, PV connectors, and safety-critical harnesses remain separate future products, never silent scope creep.

The expansion is asymmetric only if the low-cost architecture, installed format/recipe library, and service economics let FieldWire move outward faster than broad industrial vendors can move down-market. If buyers simply compare machine specifications and price, the company is a thin-margin integrator and should be killed.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland provides a low-cost workshop, family-company alpha access, Polish-language support, and direct access to fragmented electrical and controls contractors. It is also a hard test: lower Polish labour costs make the ROI hurdle stricter than in Western Europe. A machine that repays from Polish skilled labour can later have a stronger labour-saving case in higher-cost EU markets, though that is a hypothesis.

The product is designed from the start for Polish/English instructions, EU technical documentation, serviceable commodity components, and the machinery rules applicable to 2027 placement on the market. Local execution is an initial cost and learning advantage, not a legal barrier. Rittal, Komax, and Schleuniger already serve Europe; “made in Poland” alone is no defence.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid installation can add:

- a validated schedule importer and mapping rules;
- observed changeover and operator-attention data;
- wire/ferrule/label recipes and exception signatures;
- mean-time-between-failure, wear, jam, and warranty evidence;
- cassette demand and preventive-service timing;
- a quantified customer labour case; and
- a reference within a narrow contractor cluster.

These assets lower qualification, setup, service, and sales cost for the next similar shop. Recurring cassette exchange and care plans create a relationship after the machine sale, but no network effect is assumed. If customers do not share usable operational data or choose only commodity consumables and break/fix service, the compounding case weakens materially.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

During corporate employment, the conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution; optional founder distributions never exceed 5%. The strong case reinvests 100%. Founder wealth is measured through equity after liabilities, not by adding the same retained cash again.

The first 100,000 PLN creates the owned beta and proof assets. Customer deposits and 50% order prepayments fund sale-unit materials. Retained contribution funds test fixtures, cassettes, spares, and the first application engineer. Equipment/working-capital debt, grants, or outside growth equity are considered only after multiple independent installations, recurring demand, positive unit contribution, bounded warranty costs, and a documented direct-sales channel. Family capital is excluded from paid proof and from the first delivery plan.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

**Days 1–30:** time and film at least three family-company manual schedules; interview ten unrelated shops without treating interviews as demand proof; freeze the first four wire/ferrule recipes; obtain fixed quotes for used/commodity modules, fabrication, safety advice, and IP-assigned contract engineering.

**Days 31–75:** build the founder-owned supervised alpha and guarded benchmark fixture. Implement a minimal scheduler/importer. Complete the 100-wire internal benchmark and publish all setup, attention, scrap, and exception time.

**Days 76–120:** have the machinery-safety adviser review hazards and the beta conformity route. Correct critical risks. Contact 40 unrelated volume-qualified shops from the public account list; invite at least eight to a controlled bring-your-own-schedule demonstration.

**Days 121–180:** run at least four unrelated live schedules. Obtain two independent, non-refundable 5,000 PLN deposits, each credited against a 15,000 PLN four-week beta pilot. A deposit counts only when the prospect's observed labour case implies <=24-month payback and the buyer is not related to the family company. Founder cash at risk stops at 38,000 PLN until both payments arrive.

**Months 7–12 after proof:** spend no more than another 57,000 PLN to turn the owned alpha into one guarded, documented beta; complete the required conformity route before market placement or buyer-site use; deliver the same beta sequentially to both paying buyers. Collect each pilot balance only after agreed installation/acceptance conditions. Founder cash committed in the full POC/POV cycle remains <=95,000 PLN.

Paid proof is a demand signal, not permission for broad production. A second unit requires successful first delivery, positive direct contribution, an acceptable service burden, and a 50% external purchase-order prepayment.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder may leave the corporate job only after all of the following exist:

- at least ten independent paying customers, not merely deposits from two pilots;
- at least five accepted production machines or contracted recurring rentals;
- positive machine contribution after commissioning, warranty, freight, and support;
- care/cassette attachment of at least 35% or another demonstrated recurring service signal;
- a repeatable qualified-demo acquisition channel with measured CAC and sales cycle;
- a trained technician/application engineer so operation does not depend on the founder every day; and
- at least 12 months of personal runway outside the original business capital.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If buyer demand fails, stop before beta integration and retain/resell the purchased modules, printer, camera, controls, and frame components. Preserve the benchmark dataset, process maps, schedule parser, supplier quotes, safety map, customer objections, and editable designs. These can support another bounded workshop-automation product or a paid wire-preparation service experiment, but a manual service is not automatically a scalable fallback.

If the machine works but recurring cassettes do not, sell the machine with third-party consumables and paid annual maintenance; revise the wealth model downward. If Poland's labour economics are too weak but verified productivity is strong, test higher-wage EU buyers without spending on localization until a foreign deposit exists. If incumbents already close the low-cost gap, stop rather than becoming their reseller or a generic integration consultancy.

## Critical Evidence

- Rittal officially offers the directly overlapping automated functions—cutting, stripping, ferruling, labelling, and project-specific output—and supports up to 36 wire types over 0.5–6 mm² depending on variant.
- Rittal's own marketing says wiring preparation can generate substantial time savings and gives a 2.5-year payback example at 300 enclosures/year. That establishes incumbent seriousness, not FieldWire performance.
- Komax's product range spans programmable stripping, broad cut/strip capability, peripherals, and panel-builder automation; the Kappa 340 handles variants up to 16 mm outside diameter.
- Schleuniger's EcoStrip 9380 supports a broad conductor range and modular marking/handling peripherals.
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 establishes machinery health, safety, technical-documentation, declaration, and CE obligations and applies from 20 January 2027. EMC, electrical-voltage, and hazardous-substance rules may also apply depending on final architecture.
- EU electrification and skills-pressure sources support timing context but do not establish a buyer, price, or product gap.

No independent evidence yet establishes FieldWire's technical performance, lower all-in price, customer payback, deposits, conformity cost, gross margin, service attachment, CAC, or scale volume.

## Critical Hypotheses

1. At least 40 reachable Polish/CEE shops meet the weekly volume and mixed-job profile.
2. Their measured manual workflow consumes 15–25 paid hours/week and supports <=24-month payback at about 52,000 PLN.
3. A 38,000 PLN supervised alpha can reduce paid skilled minutes by >=35% without worse quality.
4. Safe ferrule, label, length, job-bin, and electrical verification can be integrated without industrial-line cost.
5. Two unrelated buyers will each pay a 5,000 PLN deposit after their own schedule is demonstrated.
6. A guarded, documented beta and one sequential two-customer delivery can be completed for <=95,000 PLN cumulative founder capital.
7. A €12,000 production machine can retain >=40% gross margin after commissioning and warranty.
8. At least 35% of the installed base will buy €1,100–1,300/year of real care/cassette value.
9. Direct acquisition can reach <=€2,500 blended CAC and <=120-day post-demo sales cycles.
10. European annual demand can eventually support the conservative 1,000-machine/year wealth case without heroic market share; no bottom-up market count yet proves this.
11. Founder ownership can remain >=60% in the conservative case after the capital, inventory, service, and talent needed to scale.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than ten of the first 40 targeted shops meet the 1,000-wire/week and mixed-batch profile.
- Written incumbent configurations show the same end-to-end job-pack result at <=1.8× FieldWire's target all-in price with materially lower buyer risk.
- The internal or external benchmark saves <35% paid skilled minutes, saves <25% elapsed time, or increases accepted-wire defects/rework.
- Changeovers, jams, label/ferrule replenishment, or manual transfers consume >20% of total job time at the target mix.
- No two unrelated 5,000 PLN deposits after 40 qualified outreaches and at least eight own-schedule demos by day 180.
- Either qualifying buyer's measured labour-only payback exceeds 24 months at the proposed price.
- Product-safety/conformity work pushes the owned beta above 95,000 PLN cumulative founder capital or prevents compliant first delivery within 12 months.
- An existential module has only one practical supplier or contractor IP cannot be fully assigned.
- First delivered-unit machine gross margin is <40%, warranty/field support exceeds 8% of first-year revenue, or the beta requires continuous founder attendance.
- Direct CAC remains >€4,000 or median post-proof sales cycle >120 days after ten customers.
- Care/cassette attachment is <35% after 30 installations and no alternative recurring service value exists.
- The conservative 1,000-machine/year path cannot be supported by a bottom-up addressable-account count before scale capital is raised.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

**Founder:** 15–20 hours/week for product specification, scheduler/data, experiment design, account selection, demos, finance, and supplier control.

**Contract mechatronics engineer:** bounded architecture, integration, editable CAD/electrical files, test plan, and IP assignment; hypothesis 80–120 hours across alpha/beta.

**Qualified electrician/panel builder:** recipe definition, ferrule/strip acceptance, controlled benchmarks, and user instructions; family technicians may support alpha but cannot certify demand.

**Independent machinery-safety adviser/test laboratory:** applicability map, risk assessment review, standards list, verification/pre-compliance, technical-file and declaration route. Exact standards and test costs require a written quote; anticipated topics include guards/interlocks, blade/crimp pinch points, dereeler entanglement, emergency stop, stored energy, electrical safety, EMC, noise, maintenance access, and software-related safety functions.

**Part-time fabricator/technician:** frame, guards, wiring, assembly, test fixtures, and later field commissioning.

Pre-proof founder cash: <=38,000 PLN. Full 12-month founder POC/POV cash: <=95,000 PLN. Founder time remains part-time until the transition gate. No co-founder is required before proof; recruit an engineering co-founder only if the alpha reveals that the integration is core and cannot be safely owned through assigned contract work.
