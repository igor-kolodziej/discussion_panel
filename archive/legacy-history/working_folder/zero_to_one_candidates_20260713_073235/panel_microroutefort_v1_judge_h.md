## Diagnosis

This is a disciplined, capital-gated way to test whether an existing serviced yard can be converted more cheaply than building a truck park, and the asset-level lease can create genuine local inventory control. The weakness is that almost every profit-critical input remains a hypothesis: existence of a suitable underused host, the host's willingness to grant a long assignable lease at the modeled cost, a Bronze conversion within 60,000 PLN, and fleet willingness to prepay an unknown founder. The first-site test is reachable enough to run part-time, but the claimed founder-wealth outcome depends on a capital-heavy 35-site roll-up with narrow valuation headroom, unproven repeatability, and optimistic retention of ownership. It is a credible research program, not yet a credible €10 million founder-wealth business.

## Evidence and Assumptions

**Structurally specified rather than evidenced:** a 120-day option, 10–15-year assignable lease, service SLA, independent pre-audit, fixed integrator quote, certification path, two fleet deposits, and site finance are all requirements in the plan; none is an executed fact.

**Commercial hypotheses:** 65% utilisation, €16–20 nightly pricing, 120,000 PLN annual host lease/service cost, 125,000 PLN first-site EBITDA, 25%+ fully loaded EBITDA, fleet block renewal, and lane-specific outbound converting from 40 fleet approaches.

**Consequential missing proof:** qualified host count after screening, landlord economics, a complete opening-cash reconciliation, certification and insurance quotes, signed fleet budget ownership, actual CAC and sales cycle, recurring rather than trial demand, central operating costs, second-site financing terms, and dilution at portfolio scale.

The candidate is unusually explicit about kill criteria and correctly separates official market context from company proof. That increases test quality, but documentation quality is not market evidence.

## Control Point

**Tier 1 at the individual-site level, conditional on execution.** A signed 10–15-year assignable lease over the exact bay section, enforceable service/access rights, and contracted/prepaid fleet inventory would directly control scarce location-specific inventory and the transaction flow. Before those contracts exist, the candidate owns no control point; the concept itself is not Tier 1. Even after execution, the protection is narrow: it controls one named site rather than the regional category or host-sourcing channel.

**Strongest copy risk:** an existing fuel, logistics, hospitality, or parking operator already controlling a serviced yard can hire the same security/certification integrator and sell directly to fleets, while a better-capitalised aggregator can lock up comparable nearby hosts. The long lease prevents copying the exact bays after signature, but not replicating the model at the same corridor node. Early route/yield data and 90-night fleet blocks are too shallow to stop that entry.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.** The scenario table is arithmetically explicit and does not double-count distributions, but it does not establish a credible conservative path to at least €10m net founder wealth.

The stated conservative result is €10.5m at year 15, only 5% above the threshold. It requires 35 sites and 2,800 bays, 65% utilisation, 30% EBITDA after scaling, 65% founder ownership despite roughly €30m of cumulative capital and 80% external funding, a 7× EBITDA enterprise value, and only €9m of net debt/lease/other obligations. There is no bottom-up bridge showing how first-site retained cash, site debt covenants, infrastructure-equity claims, central team costs, lease liabilities, and dilution produce those figures. A modest miss in margin, utilisation, valuation, financing cost, ownership, or obligations removes the entire cushion.

The expected and strong cases do not cure this because they assume still faster site acquisition, more external capital, and high portfolio margins before host repeatability or financeability is evidenced. The candidate may support a profitable asset business, but the €10m claim remains a leveraged roll-up hypothesis rather than a conservative founder-wealth path.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS, narrowly and only as a paid-proof test.** The plan caps founder cash exposure at 30,000 PLN before proof, keeps the founder employed at 15–20 hours per week, and defines acceptable proof as two unrelated fleet deposits totaling at least 15,000 PLN for at least 12 bays across 90 nights. It does not require land purchase, greenfield construction, family capital, or completed hardware before customer money.

The pass is fragile. Within 180 days an unknown founder must locate a rare fully serviced host, secure documented HGV rights and a low-cost option, obtain legal/insurance comfort, pass a pre-audit, secure a fixed conversion quote, and persuade two unrelated fleets to place money with an unopened site. These are sequentially gated rather than blindly funded, so failure remains cheap, but their compound uncertainty materially reduces reachability.

The later opening budget is also too tight at the permitted maximums: 30,000 PLN pre-proof spend plus a 60,000 PLN upgrade leaves only 10,000 PLN of the original 100,000 PLN for certification gaps, lease/security deposits, insurance, and working capital. The 15,000 PLN customer deposits are conditionally refundable and should not be treated as loss-absorbing capital. Thus the gate passes for paid evidence, not for assured delivery within the total capital cap.

## Expansion Check

The 20-bay node is a coherent beachhead, and following the same fleets to adjacent nodes is the right direction of expansion. However, the proposed moat compounds only if signed fleet demand materially lowers host acquisition and finance costs; that is not yet demonstrated. Competitors need not copy the exact leased site: they can secure an alternative serviced yard or convert inventory they already control. Poland offers sourcing and operating advantages, but no regulatory or proprietary barrier prevents inward entry by established parking, fuel, logistics-property, or fleet-service operators.

## Strongest Objections and Fatality

1. **The founder-wealth bridge is not conservative. — Fatal to the €10m thesis and therefore fatal to a `pursue` verdict.** It is not fatal to testing whether one site can be profitable.
2. **The required host may be an economic contradiction. — Potentially fatal.** A near-corridor yard that already has legal HGV access, paving, showers, food, power, internet, drainage, lighting, 24/7 service, and 20 separable underused bays may already monetise the capacity or demand materially more than the modeled host cost. Failure after 200 screens is correctly a kill condition.
3. **The first-site capital stack has almost no opening cushion. — Potentially fatal to delivery under the original 100,000 PLN cap.** At the accepted maximum conversion quote, refundable deposits do not solve the working-capital shortfall.
4. **Fleet willingness to prepay and renew is unproved. — Fatal if the two independent deposits are not obtained; not yet fatal because it can be tested within the loss cap.** Short initial blocks also do not establish annual occupancy, retention, or repeatable CAC.
5. **Site-specific leases do not create portfolio-level domination. — Fatal to durable high-multiple economics if comparable hosts remain accessible to incumbents.** It is not fatal to a locally profitable single asset.
6. **The minimum people stack omits substantial portfolio sales, landlord sourcing, and central operating cost. — Not fatal to the 180-day test, but potentially fatal to the stated margins and founder ownership at scale.** Founder labor may remain a bottleneck until several sites can support replacements.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is the refusal to build expensive services and civil infrastructure: only attempt certification where a host already supplies the complete mandatory service bundle and legal HGV capacity. This creates a fast, falsifiable contrast with greenfield parking and is worth preserving as the central test assumption.

## Final Verdict

**RESEARCH FURTHER.** Do not treat the candidate as qualified or validated, and do not commit the post-proof 100,000 PLN merely because deposits arrive. The low-cost 180-day test is worth running because it can cheaply answer the host, quote, and willingness-to-pay questions, but failure of the Founder Wealth Gate prohibits `pursue`. The exact candidate should advance only as a bounded evidence-gathering experiment under its stated kill criteria; its current score is **58/100**.
