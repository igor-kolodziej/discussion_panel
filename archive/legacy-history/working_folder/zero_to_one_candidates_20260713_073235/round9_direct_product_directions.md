# Round 9 — Direct Physical-Product and Packaged-Rights Directions

**Date:** 2026-07-13  
**Role of this artifact:** Candidate generation and current-source research only. It does not score, evaluate, pivot, panel-test, or validate any idea. Any retained direction must still enter the separate evaluator workflow and both validation gates.  
**Search boundary:** Direct consumer or professional physical products and packaged rights; established technical performance; deposits or preorders without privileged distribution; a resource/control point that an unknown founder can create or contractually secure inside 12 months and 100,000 PLN; paid proof inside 180 days and 40,000 PLN while employed. Rejected families from earlier rounds were excluded.

## Funnel Summary

Six commercially plausible directions were researched. Two are worth drafting as full finalist briefs:

1. **ChairArmor Flight Kit** — fold-flat, model-specific sacrificial armor for the fragile protrusions on complex power wheelchairs. This is the stronger direction. It attacks a severe loss-of-autonomy risk without requiring airline or wheelchair-manufacturer permission, and it can turn exact compatibility and test records into a controlled asset.
2. **StomaLine Workwear** — ordinary-looking trousers and workwear patterned from the wearer's waist, hip, stoma coordinates, and pouch family. The behavioral gap is more specific than “adaptive clothing”: people want to look ordinary at work without compressing a pouch or advertising a medical condition.

The other four directions are not recommended for finalist drafting in their current form. Exact or close substitutes already exist, the control point is weak, or direct paid proof conflicts with the real buying channel.

## Direction 1 — ChairArmor Flight Kit

### Specific behavioral truth

A complex power-wheelchair user is not merely afraid of repair cost. The chair is body-configured infrastructure for work, toileting, pressure management, communication, and independent movement. A reimbursement after damage does not restore the lost weeks of autonomy. Yet the user also cannot hand over a huge rigid crate at every airport: the chair still needs to be visible, rollable, and handleable by ground staff.

Current coping behavior includes removing the joystick or headrest, wrapping exposed parts, attaching instructions, using a soft cover, or avoiding air travel. The useful product is therefore not another whole-chair box. It is a compact kit that protects the few model-specific protrusions most likely to be struck, twisted, or used incorrectly.

### Product wedge

**ChairArmor Flight Kit** is a fold-flat set of model-specific sacrificial guards for a supported power-chair configuration:

- a rigid or semi-rigid joystick/control-pod guard;
- footplate and calf-support edge guards;
- a headrest/seat-actuator protection module where applicable;
- highly visible safe-push, lockout, lifting, battery, and tie-down instructions;
- a serialised pre-flight photo and configuration record accessible by QR code;
- one compact carry sleeve that stays with the passenger after the modules are fitted.

The kit must keep the chair visible and rollable. It must not interfere with brakes, freewheel controls, tie-down points, battery access, powered positioning, or the original manufacturer's handling instructions. The initial offer makes no airline-approval, damage-prevention, or regulatory guarantee.

The first release should support **only three exact, named configurations**, selected after interviews and access checks. “Fits most chairs” is a kill condition, not a benefit.

### Evidence for the market gap

The U.S. Department of Transportation tells passengers to provide model, size, weight, battery, and assembly/disassembly information and requires carriers to stow the device safely and return it in the same condition. That process places the information and handling burden partly on the individual traveller: [DOT Assistive Device Guides](https://www.transportation.gov/resources/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/assistive-device-guides/assistive-device-stowage).

DOT data show that U.S. reporting carriers checked 81,367 wheelchairs and scooters in October 2024 and mishandled 954, or 1.17%: [October 2024 Air Travel Consumer Report](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/air-travel-consumer-report-october-2024-numbers). In January 2025 they checked 56,664 and mishandled 722, or 1.27%: [March 2025 ATCR, January data](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2025-05/March%202025%20ATCR%20January%20Data%20revised%205-9-2025.pdf). In May 2025 they checked 82,383 and mishandled 911, or 1.11%: [July 2025 ATCR](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2025-08/July%202025%20ATCR.pdf). These are checked journeys, not unique customers, so they are useful as risk-frequency evidence and an acquisition pool, not as a customer denominator.

The FAA publishes specific battery handling rules, which reinforces why the kit cannot obstruct or reinterpret the chair's original battery instructions: [FAA Wheelchairs and Mobility Devices](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/wheelchairs-mobility-devices). WHO estimates that 80 million people globally are likely to require a wheelchair, although this broad figure is not the serviceable market for a premium flight kit: [WHO wheelchair provision guidelines announcement](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-06-2023-who-releases-new-wheelchair-provision-guidelines).

### Existing offers and the remaining opening

- AEGIS Adaptive markets a hard-sided travel case for manual wheelchairs and lists power wheelchairs as a later phase: [AEGIS Adaptive](https://www.aegisadaptive.com/).
- Wheel Handi proposes a full AeroPod carrier for electric wheelchairs and scooters: [Wheel Handi](https://wheelhandi.com/).
- Lith-Tech sells a soft travel bag: [Lith-Tech travel bag](https://lith-tech.com/shop/wheelchair-travel-bag/).
- ComfyGo sells model-related travel-bag and controller-protection accessories: [ComfyGo Phoenix travel bag](https://rivete.store/products/comfygo-phoenix-wheelchair-travel-bag-controller-protection).

These offers establish demand for protection, but they do not close the specific opening: **minimal-volume, multi-brand, exact-fit guards that protect the fragile protrusions while leaving a complex chair visible and operational for airport handling**. This opening is real only if interviews show that full carriers and soft bags are rejected for handling, storage, or fit reasons.

### Beachhead and bottom-up reach

The first customer is a European or U.S. owner of a high-value, complex power chair who expects at least one flight in the next 12 months and whose exact configuration is one of the first three supported models. The initial acquisition unit is not “wheelchair users”; it is a person already searching for air-travel instructions, damage protection, or model-specific travel preparation.

The official U.S. figures imply roughly 56,000–82,000 checked wheelchair/scooter journeys in individual sampled months. They do **not** reveal unique complex power chairs, trip frequency, chair value, or willingness to buy a €1,100 kit. Before a full finalist is panelled, the brief must establish a defensible unique-device denominator through paid search response, owner interviews, model registration data where available, and deposits by configuration. The conservative wealth case below uses 12,000 kits per year globally; that is a falsifiable scaling hypothesis, not a market fact.

### Primary control point and 12-month bootstrap

The initial controlled resource is a **tested compatibility corpus and production package**, not a patent or speculative partnership:

- parametric CAD and production files for three exact chair configurations;
- a structured map of protrusion geometry, clearance, freewheel, battery, tie-down, push, and lift constraints;
- repeatable impact/compression fixtures and pass/fail records;
- owned fit photographs and customer configuration records with explicit consent;
- model-specific BOMs, assembly instructions, serial records, and handling cards;
- signed prototype and small-batch production terms, tooling ownership, and reserved first-batch capacity with Polish fabrication suppliers.

An unknown founder can secure this through paid work with a contract mechanical designer, used/donor components, measured owner chairs, and Polish CNC/thermoforming/sewing suppliers. No airline, insurer, association, or chair-manufacturer agreement is required for the proof cycle. Before serious capital, the proof must be three independently tested configurations, non-interference checks, production quotes, and customer deposits tied to exact models.

The strongest copy risk is that AEGIS or Wheel Handi moves from a full carrier to modular guards, or a manufacturer such as Permobil, Quantum, or Ottobock adds an OEM travel kit. The defense is not the plastic shell: it is faster multi-brand coverage, a growing configuration-and-failure corpus, lower fit error, direct owner acquisition, and a serial travel record. If three configurations do not materially improve paid conversion or fit reliability, the control point is too weak.

### Direct paid-proof mechanism — 180 days, maximum 40,000 PLN

1. Recruit 15–20 owners of candidate chair configurations through direct search, relevant public forums, and direct owner outreach. Do not count interviews as paid proof.
2. Select the first three exact configurations only where two physical measurement opportunities and replaceable/donor subcomponents are available.
3. Produce renders and one functional guard set; publish exact compatibility boundaries and a transparent refund condition.
4. Run bench impact, compression, snag, and non-interference tests. The test protocol must be published before deposits convert to non-refundable orders.
5. Seek **20 refundable €200 deposits** against a target kit price near €1,100. Deposits must name the supported model and configuration. A waiting-list email or “interested” response does not count.
6. Convert deposits only after the configuration passes and a fixed production quote supports positive contribution margin. Refund immediately if the model is not released.

Indicative pre-proof budget: 12,000 PLN contract engineering and CAD; 10,000 PLN prototypes; 5,000 PLN used components, owner measurement travel, and participant compensation; 4,000 PLN test fixtures; 4,000 PLN product-liability/GPSR review and insurance quotes; 3,000 PLN landing pages and intent-specific search; 2,000 PLN contingency. Total: **40,000 PLN**.

Paid-proof kill conditions are fewer than 20 deposits after 150 qualified owner conversations/visits and a tested prototype, a fitted guard that impairs any OEM-defined handling function, an insured landed gross margin below 55%, or configuration variation that makes small-batch production effectively bespoke.

### Unit economics hypothesis

- Target direct price: €1,100 per first-generation kit.
- Target landed product, packing, payment, and expected warranty cost: no more than €418, leaving approximately 62% gross margin before acquisition.
- Target paid acquisition: below €120 per converted customer after deposit validation.
- Target first-order contribution before fixed overhead: approximately €562, or 51% of revenue.
- Inventory is released by supported configuration and funded by converted deposits; no broad model inventory before repeatable demand.

The margin must absorb low-volume fabrication, liability cover, fit errors, and replacements. If it depends on mature high-volume tooling to exceed 55%, it is not reachable under the founder's constraints.

### Founder-wealth scaling hypotheses

These cases value retained earnings only through the business value; distributions are not added to founder wealth. While the founder remains employed, the conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash otherwise available for distribution and cap optional founder distributions at 5%. Timing starts after paid proof.

The conservative case clears €10m founder net worth on plausible margins and ownership, but the 12,000-unit global volume remains the critical denominator to prove. It should be treated as credible only after deposits show model concentration, acquisition cost, and willingness to pay. The company must not assume a high-margin insurance or airline sales channel.

### Expansion path

Add exact configurations only when measurement and test records can be reused. Then extend the same compatibility system to rail/coach transport, vehicle loading, temporary control-pod cases, and model-specific replacement travel parts. The compounding asset is the cross-brand geometry and handling corpus, not a broad catalogue of generic mobility accessories.

### Generator decision

**Draft as a full finalist.** Both the founder-wealth and 180-day reachability cases are supportable as hypotheses with clear falsification tests. The finalist must resolve two facts before panel use: access to the first three complex-chair configurations without buying whole chairs, and a defensible unique-device/willingness-to-pay denominator. It must keep the wedge modular and exact-fit; turning it into a full crate recreates existing offers.

## Direction 2 — StomaLine Workwear

### Specific behavioral truth

Many people living with a stoma do not primarily want “ostomy underwear.” They want to attend work, commute, bend, sit, and dress like peers without the waistband crossing the appliance, the pouch bulging visibly as it fills, or a garment signalling a medical identity. A person may tolerate an imperfect shirt or trouser fit for years because ordinary brands do not ask for the only coordinates that matter: where the stoma sits relative to the waistline and the actual pouch footprint.

The gap is therefore not another support garment. It is **ordinary-looking outerwear whose pattern is generated around the person's stoma coordinates and pouch family**.

### Product wedge

**StomaLine Workwear** begins with one trouser silhouette in two fabrics and a made-to-coordinate pattern system. The buyer supplies waist, hip, rise, body height, stoma side, stoma centre relative to natural waist and centreline, and pouch brand/family. A guided photo/measurement check converts this into:

- a waistband line that avoids the wafer and pouch outlet;
- concealed expansion volume over the filling zone;
- an internal stabilising panel or removable non-medical pocket where useful;
- access for emptying without fully undressing;
- ordinary external styling, with no visible adaptive fastening.

The offer does not claim to prevent leakage, treat a hernia, provide compression, or replace clinical support garments. It sells fit, discretion, comfort, and repeatable everyday clothing.

### Evidence for the market gap

A survey of 1,425 stoma users found that support garments can address appearance, function, and sensation, but poorly fitting garments can create negative experiences: [PubMed — support garments for people with a stoma](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31275585/). A study of leakage and everyday life found that 65% of employed respondents affected by leakage reported an impact on their ability to work: [PubMed — leakage and everyday life](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35333558/). This does not prove that tailored trousers reduce leakage; it supports the importance of discretion and work-compatible management.

Population anchors are substantial but inconsistent and must not be added uncritically. UOAA estimates 725,000–1,000,000 people living with an ostomy in the United States and about 100,000 surgeries annually: [UOAA — What is an Ostomy?](https://www.ostomy.org/what-is-an-ostomy/). Deutsche ILCO reports more than 150,000 people living with a stoma in Germany: [Deutsche ILCO in figures](https://www.ilco.de/verband/die-ilco-in-zahlen). The Dutch Stoma Association refers to roughly 700,000 people in Europe: [Stomazorg in Europa](https://www.stomavereniging.nl/2025/06/stomazorg-in-europa-tijd-voor-zichtbaarheid-en-gelijke-zorg/). Colostomy UK states more than 200,000 people in the UK in its 2026 media pack: [Colostomy UK media pack](https://www.colostomyuk.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Media-Pack-2026.pdf). These sources imply a U.S./European installed base on the order of 1.4–1.7 million before resolving geographic overlap and definitions.

### Existing offers and the remaining opening

- SIIL sells an established range of ostomy underwear and highlights thousands of reviews: [SIIL Classic ostomy underwear](https://www.siilostomy.com/en/ostomy-panties-classic/).
- OstomySecrets sells underwear with a front internal pocket and notes a placement boundary below the bellybutton: [OstomySecrets boxer brief](https://www.ostomysecrets.com/products/boxer-brief-black).
- OstomyPockets sells a removable and adjustable pocket: [OstomyPockets](https://ostomypockets.com/product/).
- CUI Wear allows a left/right pouch choice: [CUI Wear size guide](https://cuiwear.com/pages/size-guide).
- A small custom-clothing offer exists at [Ostomy Bag Holder](https://ostomybagholder.com/).

Competition validates category spending but also exposes the gap: most offers concentrate on underwear, swimwear, support bands, or fixed pocket placement. The proposed wedge is coordinate-driven **ordinary outerwear**, not a better-looking support garment. That distinction must survive customer deposits. If buyers are satisfied by high-waisted ordinary trousers plus existing underwear, the direction should be killed.

### Beachhead and bottom-up reach

Start with employed adults who already wear an ostomy support garment or alter their work trousers, buy online, and need a repeatable smart-casual or office trouser. The initial geography is English-speaking Europe plus Germany/Poland only where service and returns can be supported; manufacturing may be Polish, but the market should not be Poland-only.

The conservative wealth case uses 75,000 active annual customers, approximately 4.4%–5.3% of the cited 1.4–1.7 million U.S./European installed base before demographic and overlap corrections. That share is possible only as an eventual multi-category international brand, not for a single trouser. The first denominator to prove is the number of reachable people who will pay for coordinate-fit outerwear rather than existing adaptive underwear or ordinary alterations.

### Primary control point and 12-month bootstrap

The controlled resource is a **measurement-to-pattern and repeat-fit corpus**:

- owned base blocks and grading rules for stoma coordinates, rise, body shape, and pouch family;
- digital pattern files, construction details, and alteration logic;
- consented measurement, fit, returns, and repeat-order outcomes;
- a fit profile that lets a returning customer reorder multiple garments without remeasurement;
- tooling ownership and signed Polish CMT/patternmaking terms, including first-production slots and rights to production files;
- a mapping of common pouch footprints and expansion zones derived from public specifications and customer measurements, without claiming manufacturer endorsement.

An unknown founder can create this with a contract technical patternmaker, low-volume local sewing, paid fit participants, and made-to-order production. It requires no hospital, association, pouch-manufacturer, retailer, or influencer agreement. Before serious capital, proof is at least three repeatable pattern families covering 70% of early paid orders, an alteration/fit-return rate below 15% after one iteration, and repeat purchase from independent customers.

The strongest copy risk is that SIIL, an adaptive-clothing brand, or a large apparel company adds coordinate options or semi-custom trousers. The defense is the accumulated correlation between coordinates, pouch families, pattern adjustments, alteration outcomes, and repeat orders. If every order remains bespoke patternmaking, the corpus does not compound and the business remains a tailoring service.

### Direct paid-proof mechanism — 180 days, maximum 40,000 PLN

1. Interview 25–30 employed stoma users who currently alter, size around, conceal, or avoid particular trousers. Interviews are learning, not paid proof.
2. Contract a technical patternmaker to produce one work-trouser block, two fabric choices, and coordinate adjustment rules. Make 8–12 fit prototypes across different stoma placements and body shapes.
3. Publish ordinary-looking finished samples, exact measurement instructions, expected fit boundaries, alteration terms, and a non-medical disclaimer.
4. Sell 24–30 paid remote or local measurement appointments with a **€60–€100 deposit** credited to a €190–€240 trouser. A deposit is refunded if the measurement falls outside the released fit envelope.
5. Deliver the first paid batch from a local CMT workshop. Require at least eight independent paid customers to order a second garment or place a second deposit before releasing broader inventory.

Indicative budget: 10,000 PLN technical patternmaking and grading; 8,000 PLN prototypes and paid fit participants; 6,000 PLN first paid-batch production deposits; 4,000 PLN photography, measurement guides, and storefront; 4,000 PLN customer acquisition tests; 3,000 PLN returns/alteration reserve; 2,000 PLN trademark and contractual review; 3,000 PLN contingency. Total: **40,000 PLN**.

Direct acquisition begins with intent-specific search terms around stoma/ostomy trousers, jeans, work clothes, waistband discomfort, and clothing alteration; SEO pages based on pouch/waistline scenarios; public-community participation that follows community rules; and customer referrals. Association listings can be explored later, but the proof case does not depend on any association promoting the product.

Paid-proof kill conditions are fewer than 24 deposits after 200 qualified visits/conversations, fit alterations above 25% after the second pattern iteration, fewer than three independent repeat orders from the first 20 deliveries, landed gross margin below 58%, or a pattern workload above 45 minutes for every repeat order.

### Unit economics hypothesis

- Target first-trouser price: €220; repeat garment and premium-fabric blended order value €250–€280.
- Target landed garment, packaging, payment, and expected alteration/return cost: at most 37% of revenue, leaving about 63% gross margin.
- Target acquisition cost after validation: below €45 on a first order, with a 12-month repeat revenue target above €320.
- Made-to-order batches and deposits fund manufacturing; finished-goods inventory is limited to fabric and standard trims until fit repeatability is proven.

### Founder-wealth scaling hypotheses

These cases do not add cumulative distributions to the value of retained earnings. Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder remains employed and cap optional distributions at 5%; the strong-growth case reinvests 100% during expansion.

| Case | Active customers / volume | Pricing and revenue | Gross / operating margin | Capital and ownership | Reinvestment | Business value and founder wealth | Indicative timing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 75,000 annual active customers | €320 annual spend; €24m revenue | 63% / 18%; €4.32m operating profit | About €1.5m cumulative growth capital, mainly retained cash and customer-funded production; 82% founder ownership | 95% while employed | 5× operating profit = €21.6m value; founder equity ≈ €17.7m | Paid proof month 6; repeat-fit product year 1; 5,000 customers year 3; 30,000 year 6; run-rate year 9–11 |

| Strong-success | 300,000 active customers | €460 annual spend; €138m revenue | 67% / 25%; €34.5m operating profit | About €10m cumulative capital; 65% founder ownership | 100% during the growth phase | 7× = €241.5m value; founder equity ≈ €157m | Repeat wardrobe platform year 3; international categories year 5; run-rate year 7–10 |

The conservative founder-wealth case has valuation and ownership headroom, but its 75,000-customer volume requires an international repeat-apparel brand. It cannot be justified from the installed-base numbers alone. Paid repeat purchase and low-cost direct acquisition are the gating facts.

### Expansion path

Expand from one coordinate-fit work trouser into jeans, formal trousers, skirts, loungewear, and outer layers using the same saved fit profile. Later add post-surgery body-asymmetry patterns only if the existing corpus transfers; do not launch a generic adaptive-fashion catalogue. Each category should increase repeat revenue per stored fit profile and make switching less attractive.

### Generator decision

**Draft as a full finalist, behind ChairArmor.** It has direct deposits, low-capital production, a clear non-medical boundary, and a corpus that can become more than a tailoring service. The finalist must prove that customers will pay for outerwear rather than solve the problem with existing underwear or ordinary alterations, and that three pattern families cover most early buyers. High returns or one-off bespoke work should kill it.

## Direction 3 — AFO-Coordinate Split-Size Footwear

### Insight explored

People using an ankle-foot orthosis often size up, remove insoles, seek low heels and wide openings, or buy mismatched footwear to accommodate one braced foot. NHS guidance reflects these compromises. Bradford Teaching Hospitals says the orthosis must be worn in a shoe and that a shoe may need to be half or a full size larger, with removable inlay and low heel: [Bradford AFO guide](https://digitalpatientinformationhub.bradfordhospitals.nhs.uk/leaflets/adult-ankle-foot-orthoses/). East Lancashire guidance says children may need one or two sizes larger and lists adaptive brands: [ELHT footwear guide](https://elht.nhs.uk/services/orthotics/patient-information/finding-footwear-your-childs-ankle-foot-orthosis). Research also documents footwear problems, discomfort, and non-use: [PMC — AFO user experiences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9617808/).

The proposed product was a single-pair system whose two shoes could differ in size/volume, with brace-coordinate fit and a normal external appearance.

### Why it is not retained

The opening is already substantially served. Billy states that many of its shoes accommodate AFOs and identifies a line designed specifically for them: [Billy Footwear EU FAQ](https://www.billyfootwear.eu/pages/frequently-asked-questions). Friendly Shoes sells wide/deep shoes with removable insoles for AFO use: [Friendly Shoes](https://www.friendlyshoes.co.uk/shoes-for-afos/). Adidas launched an in-store single-shoe service across 22 European countries in 2026: [Adidas single-shoe service](https://www.adidas.co.uk/help/products/in-store-single-shoe-service). Scope lists vendors offering split-size or single shoes: [Scope footwear guidance](https://www.scope.org.uk/advice-and-support/shoes-and-leg-splints).

A coordinate-driven fit guarantee could be better executed, but a serious size/last range, safety and wear testing, returns, and inventory are difficult inside 100,000 PLN. The owned fit corpus is likely to become bespoke and return-heavy before it compounds. A conservative €10m founder-wealth path would require a large, unproven annual-pair denominator. **Do not draft as a finalist unless paid evidence first shows that current AFO shoes and split-size services fail a sharply defined adult configuration.**

## Direction 4 — Flat-Closure / Asymmetry-Coordinate Apparel

### Insight explored

People choosing aesthetic flat closure after mastectomy, or living with unilateral asymmetry, may want clothing shaped for their current body rather than garments designed to simulate breasts. The American Cancer Society recognises aesthetic flat closure as an option: [American Cancer Society](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/breast-cancer/treatment/surgery-for-breast-cancer/mastectomy.html). Living Beyond Breast Cancer describes the choice to go flat: [LBBC](https://www.lbbc.org/about-breast-cancer/treatments/breast-reconstruction/going-flat).

### Why it is not retained

The emotional and fit need is real, but exact-category competitors already exist, including [Flatwear Clothing](https://www.flatwearclothing.com/about-1), [Post Surgery Bras](https://postsurgerybras.com/about/), [Busted Tank](https://www.bustedtank.com/), and established mastectomy apparel from [Amoena](https://www.amoena.com/us-en/about-us/what-is-a-mastectomy-bra/). The founder has no demonstrated lived-experience, fashion-distribution, or trusted-community edge. Measurement and pattern data alone are readily copied, while the addressable group willing to buy coordinate-specific outerwear is not counted. A plausible founder-wealth case would require tens of thousands of recurring international buyers, which current evidence does not support. **Do not draft.**

## Direction 5 — Small-Hand / Female-Fit Technical Gloves

### Insight explored

Many women and smaller workers use PPE designed around larger male dimensions, trading dexterity for nominal protection. Research on safety footwear found substantial sex-related comfort differences: [Applied Ergonomics study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687020302817). Research on women in construction reports poorly fitting PPE: [American Journal of Industrial Medicine](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajim.22624). HSE guidance stresses correct fit and comfort: [HSE PPE guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/coshh/basics/ppe.htm).

### Why it is not retained

The market is established and incumbents already recognise the fit problem. Diadora and IBV developed women-specific safety footwear: [IBV/Diadora](https://www.ibv.org/en/latest-news/diadora-and-ibv-the-perfect-tandem-for-the-design-of-new-safety-footwear-for-women-featured-2/). SeeHerWork sells women's workwear and gloves: [SeeHerWork](https://seeherwork.com/). NITRAS has a women's PPE line: [NITRAS](https://www.nitras-safety.com/en/ppe-women-workwear/). Uvex publishes PPE options for smaller dimensions: [Uvex smaller-dimensions guide](https://static-content.cromwell.co.uk/content/images/brands/uvex/PPE%20for%20smaller%20dimensions.pdf).

Certification, factory minimums, and many task-specific standards weaken a sub-100,000 PLN bootstrap. Employers and distributors are often the payer, which conflicts with direct preorder proof; a direct-consumer glove would still be readily copied by existing brands. The unit volume required for founder wealth would be very large and is not supported by a counted direct audience. **Do not draft.**

## Direction 6 — CPAP Side-Sleep Compatibility Pillow

### Insight explored

Mask fit and leak are common CPAP problems, especially when a side sleeper's pillow pushes the mask or hose. In a large survey, 75.4% of respondents reported mask leaks: [PubMed — InterfaceVent](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33451313/). An American Thoracic Society workshop report discusses mask fit, leak, and adherence: [ATS workshop report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7640631/). The product idea was a pillow insert or adjustable cutout geometry selected by mask family and sleeping side.

### Why it is not retained

Cutout CPAP pillows already exist as straightforward products, such as the [Best in Rest CPAP pillow](https://cpapeuropa.com/products/best-in-rest-memory-foam-cpap-pillow). Mask designs with top-of-head hoses, ordinary pillow-edge positioning, and simple soft goods are substitutes. Compatibility data does not produce a durable control point because geometry is observable and easy to copy. Purchase frequency is low, while a founder-wealth case would require a very large one-time unit base or artificial consumables. **Do not draft.**

# Decision-Critical Research Addendum — ChairArmor Flight Kit

**Research date:** 2026-07-13  
**Scope:** one bounded, current public-evidence pass on the exact ChairArmor candidate. No outreach, scoring, evaluation, pivot, panel, validation, or chatbot interaction was performed. Where the public record does not establish the requested fact, the result below is explicitly **UNAVAILABLE** rather than inferred.

## 1. Unique Annual Complex-Power-Chair Flyers

The strongest current official volume source counts **wheelchair/scooter enplanement events**, not unique people or unique devices. The U.S. Department of Transportation's February 2026 Air Travel Consumer Report records 907,259 wheelchair/scooter enplanements and 9,910 mishandled devices for marketing-carrier networks in calendar 2025. Its alternative operating-carrier view records 869,819 enplanements and 9,657 mishandled devices. Those views must not be added together: [U.S. DOT — February 2026 ATCR](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2026-03/February_2026%20ATCR.pdf). The BTS reporting directive defines the measure as wheelchairs and scooters, manual or powered, placed in an aircraft cargo compartment on each covered domestic nonstop flight: [BTS Technical Directive #30](https://www.bts.gov/topics/airlines-and-airports/number-30-%E2%80%93-technical-directive-mishandled-baggage-effective-jan-1-2019). The latest available official quarter similarly reports 181,165 events in Q1 2026 versus 168,640 in Q1 2025: [U.S. DOT — May 2026 ATCR](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2026-05/May%202026%20ATCR.pdf).

These sources do not identify unique travellers, trip frequency, manual versus powered devices, complex Group 3/4 chairs, exact chair models, geography of the traveller, or self-pay propensity. Dividing events by an assumed number of annual flights would manufacture a denominator. Therefore:

- **Unique annual complex-power-chair flyers: UNAVAILABLE.**
- **Defensible lower bound on unique prospective buyers: UNAVAILABLE.**
- The existing 8,400-unit conservative and 12,000-unit expected cases are scenario requirements, not demonstrated market-volume facts.

## 2. Model Concentration And Configuration Variation

No public, model-level source was found for installed base, annual unit sales, or share of power-chair air journeys. Public HCPCS and transport statistics aggregate multiple device classes, while commercial market reports generally stop at manufacturer-level estimates. Consequently, there is no evidence that Permobil M3 Corpus, Quickie Q700 M, and Quantum Q6 Edge 3 Stretto are the three most concentrated flight configurations.

Official configuration documents do establish substantial variation **within** those families:

- **Permobil M3 Corpus:** current product and order resources expose multiple legrest and footplate systems, joystick/control types, fixed and swing-away mounts, headrests, supports, and model-year order forms: [Permobil M3 Corpus](https://www.permobil.com/en-gb/products/power-wheelchairs/permobil-m3-corpus).
- **Quickie Q700 M:** the manufacturer lists multiple backrest, armrest, legrest, seating-dimension, headrest, control, and mount choices, with current order forms: [Sunrise Medical — Quickie Q700 M](https://www.sunrisemedical.com/power-wheelchairs/quickie/mid-wheel-drive/q700-m).
- **Quantum Q6 Edge 3 Stretto:** the April 2026 order form distinguishes base/seating variants, several fixed and retractable joystick mounts, left/right placement, joystick handles, power-articulating and quick-adjust foot platforms, sizes, and extensions; it also flags configuration-dependent interference: [Quantum — Q6 Edge 3 Stretto order form](https://www.quantumrehab.com/pdf/order-forms/quantum_rehab/us_eof_q6e3stretto_contoured.pdf).

A model-family name is therefore not a testable compatibility definition. Each first-release guard would have to freeze, at minimum, base revision, seat system, controller/joystick and mount SKU, mount side and adjustment, footplate/legrest SKU and adjustment, headrest/actuator envelope, and all tie-down, freewheel, and battery clearances.

- **Model concentration for the proposed first three families: UNAVAILABLE.**
- **Configuration variation: ESTABLISHED and material.**
- A three-family launch cannot be represented as three repeatable SKUs until exact option geometries are frozen and physically tested.

## 5. GPSR, Product Liability, Non-Interference, And Insurability

ChairArmor would be a consumer product under the EU General Product Safety Regulation unless a more specific regime applies. GPSR requires manufacturers to place only safe products on the market, perform internal risk analysis, maintain technical documentation and traceability, provide warnings/instructions, and consider foreseeable effects on other products: [Regulation (EU) 2023/988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj/eng), [European Commission GPSR Q&A](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/safety/consumers/consumers_safety_gate/obligationsForBusinesses/documents/Q%26A.pdf), and [Your Europe — technical documentation](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-requirements/compliance/preparing-technical-documentation/index_en.htm).

The EU Medical Device Regulation defines an accessory by its intended role in enabling or directly assisting a medical device's medical functionality: [Regulation (EU) 2017/745, consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?qid=1781810094174&uri=CELEX%3A02017R0745-20260101). A transport-only guard may fall outside that definition, but public sources do not settle ChairArmor's classification; a documented classification assessment remains necessary.

Relevant air-transport engineering material exists but is not a ready-made guard certification. ANSI/RESNA AT-1:2024 Section 3 covers handling procedures for mobility devices in commercial aircraft: [ANSI/RESNA AT-1:2024 Section 3](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/resna/ansiresna2024s3). RESNA describes Section 4 as containing design, labelling, and test methods plus an air-travel configuration card: [RESNA AT-1 announcement](https://www.resna.org/About/RESNA-News/RESNA-Blog/new-resna-standard-for-assistive-technology-for-air-travel-is-now-available). IATA guidance addresses batteries, joystick removal where designed, and consultation with the passenger: [IATA mobility-aid transport guidance](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/7b3762815ac44a10b83ccf5560c1b308/iata-guidance-on-the-transport-of-mobility-aids-final-feb2023.pdf). No public guard-specific impact, compression, snag, or interference acceptance standard was found. A bench test must therefore use a written hazard analysis, OEM clearance matrix, paid standards, recorded loads and pass/fail thresholds; “tested” cannot mean an undocumented demonstration.

Public Polish SME liability wordings reveal a serious coverage issue. Colonnade's 2025 wording excludes, in the standard form, products installed or used in aircraft and medical devices, with U.S./Canada exposure requiring special treatment: [Colonnade Liability Protect terms](https://www.colonnade.pl/cdn/65b2eb68-cf8e-0106-94e7-7fcbfbaa6c5e/e1d6e913-0440-4238-8ad3-2cf8944b7035/065_OWU_OC_Liability_Protect_EN_2807_2025_z.pdf). Warta's wording includes aircraft/airport-operation and medical-device bodily-injury exclusions: [Warta Ekstrabiznes Plus terms](https://www.warta.pl/documents/oferta_dla_firmy/WARTA_OWU_Ekstrabiznes_Plus_C7906_int_en.pdf). Polish strict product-liability exposure cannot simply be removed by contract: [CMS — product liability in Poland](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/expert-guide-to-product-liability-and-warranty-litigation/poland).

- **Applicable GPSR duties and need for a technical risk file: ESTABLISHED.**
- **MDR/non-MDR classification for this exact intended use: UNAVAILABLE without a formal assessment.**
- **Guard-specific non-interference test protocol and acceptance loads: UNAVAILABLE publicly.**
- **Binding insurability, premium, limits, recall cover, aviation wording, and U.S./Canada cover: UNAVAILABLE.** General off-the-shelf policies may exclude the central risk, so an indicative written specialist quotation is a prerequisite to accepting deposits.

## 7. Repaired 180-Day Paid-Proof Sequence — Same Candidate And Economics

This sequence preserves the exact business shape, €1,100 selling price, €200 deposit, 20-deposit target, and eventual three-configuration first release. It changes only evidence order: deposits are prohibited until one exact-configuration prototype has completed documented testing. Founder-funded exposure remains capped at 40,000 PLN.

### Days 1–30 — Freeze And Secure One Exact Configuration

- Select the first family only after model-demand evidence identifies the most reachable exact configuration; do not assume the three named families are the most concentrated.
- Complete a purchase or signed physical-access agreement for **two examples of the same frozen option geometry**, or secure one whole chair plus the exact duplicate protrusion assemblies needed for repeat-fit checks.
- Record serial/model year, order-sheet options, controller and mount SKU/side, legrest/footplate, seating, headrest, actuator envelopes, tie-down/freewheel/battery clearances, and owner-approved handling boundaries.
- **Cumulative founder-spend ceiling: 9,000 PLN**, including no more than 7,000 PLN for chair/access and 2,000 PLN for documentation and access due diligence.
- **Stop condition:** no exact physical access at the ceiling. No engineering and no deposits.

### Days 31–75 — Engineer And Build; No Deposits

- Produce CAD, bill of materials, reversible attachment design, configuration-specific exclusions, and first functional prototype.
- Complete repeat-fit checks on both exact examples. Document that installation and removal do not alter the chair, load medical/postural components, obstruct controls, move the freewheel mechanism, interfere with securement points, or trap/bend cables.
- **Incremental ceiling: 10,000 PLN; cumulative ceiling: 19,000 PLN** — up to 6,000 PLN engineering/CAD and 4,000 PLN materials/prototype.
- No customer money is requested or accepted.

### Days 76–105 — Documented Test, Classification, And Insurance Check; No Deposits

- Purchase the relevant ANSI/RESNA material and build a written hazard analysis and test matrix using OEM manuals and IATA handling requirements.
- Run recorded compression, impact, snag, retention, repeated-installation, emergency-removal, clearance, and no-damage inspections with declared loads, instrumentation, pass/fail thresholds, results, and failures. Preserve raw evidence and design revisions.
- Obtain a written GPSR/MDR classification memo and assemble the technical-file skeleton, traceability, instructions, warnings, complaint/incident process, and recall logic.
- Submit the exact intended use, countries, sales channel, test record, and U.S./Canada exposure to a product-liability broker; obtain an indicative written quotation or written coverage conditions that do not exclude the product's intended aircraft-transport use.
- **Incremental ceiling: 9,000 PLN; cumulative ceiling: 28,000 PLN** — up to 5,000 PLN for standards, fixtures, and tests and 4,000 PLN for regulatory/insurance work.
- **Stop condition:** material interference or chair damage, unresolved classification, no technically adequate test basis, or no plausible coverage on terms compatible with the €1,100 price. No deposits.

### Days 106–135 — Tested Evidence Then Deposits

- Only after the preceding test report and written risk/coverage conditions exist, publish the tested prototype evidence, exact supported configuration, exclusions, installation video, warranty/claims boundaries, delivery timing, and full €1,100 price.
- Use direct, permissioned owner acquisition and configuration screening. Do not claim protection beyond the documented test envelope.
- Accept up to **20 refundable €200 deposits** only from unrelated owners whose chairs match the exact tested configuration. Keep deposits segregated and unspent until each customer receives the final disclosure and elects to convert.
- **Incremental founder-spend ceiling: 4,000 PLN; cumulative ceiling: 32,000 PLN** for landing page, screening, samples, and direct acquisition.
- Report unique qualified owners reached, consented configuration records, deposit conversions, refusals and reasons, and fully loaded acquisition cost. Do not treat leads, surveys, or wait-list entries as paid proof.

### Days 136–180 — Convert, Micro-Batch, And Deliver

- After the 20-deposit target, request the balance under clear production/refund terms. Use converted customer payments for inventory; do not use segregated unconverted deposits.
- Use at most the remaining **8,000 PLN** of the founder's first-risk tranche for micro-batch fixtures, insured shipment, and contingency. **Maximum cumulative founder-funded exposure: 40,000 PLN.**
- Deliver at least five independent paid kits and record installation time, fit exceptions, damage/near-miss reports, returns/refunds, shipment cost, insured premium allocation, direct acquisition cost, and fully loaded contribution at the unchanged €1,100 price.
- Do not spend on configurations two or three until the first configuration has five independent deliveries, no unresolved safety incident, positive fully loaded contribution, and a documented direct acquisition signal.
- Spending beyond the first 40,000 PLN remains prohibited until paid evidence, successful initial delivery, and plausible positive contribution are demonstrated; later founder-capital and family-capital tranches retain the original release rules.

## Research-Only Conclusion

Public evidence establishes a large number of wheelchair/scooter transport events, material configuration complexity, actual traveller protection behaviour, purchasable used chair families, and live adjacent competitors. Those unavailable facts must not be replaced by journey counts, owner anecdotes, listing prices, or broad market reports. This addendum therefore closes the bounded public-research pass while preserving the unresolved evidence status for the next workflow decision.
