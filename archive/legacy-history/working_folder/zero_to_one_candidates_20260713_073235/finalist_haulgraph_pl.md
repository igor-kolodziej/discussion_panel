# Zero-to-One Candidate: HaulGraph PL

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build a founder-owned, timestamped constraint graph and survey-referenced benchmark from Poland's open LiDAR, terrain, road, bridge and disruption sources, then sell heavy-haul operators a prepaid, uncertainty-labelled corridor preflight that rejects obviously impossible route/vehicle combinations before they spend on trusted field surveys and permit proceedings—without ever claiming to issue, replace or predict an authority approval.

## Founder Fit

The founder's data-science, modelling and AI-leverage strengths fit geospatial normalization, graph construction, versioning, benchmark design, uncertainty calibration and targeted account research. The asset can be built asynchronously and the first fixed-scope reports can be delivered within 15–20 focused hours per week, below the founder's five-hour daily limit. The founder can remain off-camera and use precise, evidence-led outbound rather than constant networking.

The fit is incomplete: the founder is not a bridge engineer, route surveyor, abnormal-load operator, permit specialist or road authority. A contract geospatial engineer, a swept-path/vehicle-dynamics reviewer and an experienced heavy-haul route surveyor therefore review the benchmark and report method under fixed paid scopes. A Polish commercial/insurance lawyer defines the quote-screening limitation and obtains a professional-indemnity quote before the first sale. These people are vendors, not speculative partners, and the company does not scale by selling their hours one-for-one.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is the route-planning, estimating or project-logistics owner at a Polish heavy-haul carrier, crane fleet, wind-component logistics team or industrial-relocation contractor. Before quoting a move, reserving equipment or paying for a full field survey and permit work, this person needs to know:

- which candidate corridors have an obvious height, width, turning, grade, known bridge or current-roadwork conflict;
- which route remains plausible for the stated vehicle/load envelope;
- where public evidence is stale, missing or internally inconsistent; and
- which exact segments now justify a trusted physical survey, structural check or road-manager discussion.

The buyer does **not** need another consumer route map and does not need a startup to pretend it can authorize passage. A bad early route assumption can waste survey/permit spend, make a quote unprofitable, force a late detour or delay a crane/project sequence. The current workaround is manual: combine ordinary maps, prior routes, spreadsheets, calls, current roadworks, operator memory and a professional desktop/field route survey. The survey and authority process remain necessary because local geometry changes, overhead objects are occluded, structural bridge data is incomplete and the legal route depends on the current condition and consent of every relevant road manager.

Willingness to pay exists only if an automated preflight changes a quote or survey scope early enough to save more than its price. The cost of a failed quote, survey or delay has not been verified and is a customer-discovery hypothesis; the brief does not invent a savings figure.

## Product Wedge

The first product is a fixed-scope **Corridor Rejection Preflight**, prepaid at 8,000–15,000 PLN for one defined load/vehicle envelope and origin-destination pair. It returns within ten business days:

1. three candidate corridors or a documented reason fewer are plausible;
2. a machine-generated constraint sheet for clearance, turn envelope, road width, grade, known posted/load restriction and current disruption;
3. source URL or dataset, acquisition/publication date, last retrieval date and coverage class for each consequential constraint;
4. a confidence state of `observed blocker`, `modelled blocker`, `apparently passable within public evidence`, or `unknown—survey/manager decision required`;
5. a swept-path visualization for selected junctions using the buyer's stated vehicle geometry;
6. explicit survey/structural/authority checkpoints; and
7. a signed limitation: **quote-screening preflight, not a permit, structural assessment, field survey, navigation instruction or assurance of passage**.

The product never changes `unknown` into `safe`. It does not submit an application, contact authorities as an agent, calculate bridge structural capacity from unavailable engineering records, or tell a driver to proceed. A buyer may use it to reject a corridor, revise a quote or narrow a subsequent trusted survey. A permit route or professional survey remains the operational decision artifact.

After repeat paid use, the wedge becomes an annual route-desk/API licence: versioned corridor queries, saved vehicle envelopes, change alerts and reproducible evidence packs. Bespoke permit casework is excluded.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The useful decision occurs **before** the trusted route survey and permit process. Existing authority-grade systems and professional surveys focus on producing, documenting or approving a route. HaulGraph PL instead aims to cheaply falsify bad route assumptions while the carrier is still deciding whether and how to quote.

The gap is not lack of maps. It is the absence, if customer discovery confirms it, of a Poland-first constraint layer that joins public 3D geometry, road topology, known bridge limitations and current disruptions; retains source age and missingness; runs the buyer's articulated geometry; and makes the result reproducible enough to compare against a later trusted survey. The commercial claim is therefore narrow: **spend survey and authority attention only on corridors that survive an auditable first-pass rejection test**.

Competitor comparison is deliberately unfavorable to a generic product:

- Octave SafeHaul already combines permit workflows, dimension-based routing, dynamic road/bridge restrictions and bridge evaluation, principally as an integrated solution for agencies, permitting offices, carriers and enforcement. HaulGraph cannot win by recreating those modules. Its proposed wedge is a founder-built Polish evidence layer sold before permit work, without requiring an agency deployment.
- RoadScope publicly markets maps, bridge/turn constraints and professional field-survey-to-permit-ready workflows in North America, with published plans from $79 to $400 per month. This makes a generic heavy-haul mapping subscription a commodity. HaulGraph's higher price is tenable only if the proprietary Polish normalization and benchmark measurably reduce wasted survey scope or bad quotes.
- A trusted route survey sees temporary street furniture, vegetation, wires, local access, construction and structural information that old public LiDAR cannot. HaulGraph complements that survey by prioritizing where it is bought; it does not displace the survey's trust or liability role.

## Underused Momentum

Three existing resources create a low-capital opening:

1. **Open national geometry:** GUGiK states that NMT, NMPT, national LiDAR, orthophotos and BDOT10k are freely downloadable without limitations. Its LiDAR documentation states national ALS coverage at roughly 4 points/m2 and up to 20 points/m2 in cities and says LAZ data can be used freely.
2. **Operational public signals:** GDDKiA publishes a current disruption list and an official list of engineering structures with limited load capacity. Its February 2026 explainer reports roughly 16,000 abnormal-load permits issued in 2025, showing real transaction flow rather than proving a software market.
3. **Fragmentation:** the official geometry, topology, bridge, works and legal-condition sources are useful but not a load-specific, versioned preflight. The founder can assemble the first two corridor cells without customer, authority or platform permission, subject to verifying the terms of each non-GUGiK source.

The same evidence also creates the main caveat. GUGiK's published LiDAR indexes include acquisition years as old as 2011–2020. National coverage is not the same as current clearance truth, and municipal/powiat bridge and road information is not uniform. Freshness must be a product field, not a marketing footnote.

## Beachhead Market

The beachhead is Polish project-cargo carriers and specialist route desks that repeatedly quote wind components, transformers, industrial vessels, large machinery or relocations over 100–300 km corridors involving national plus local roads. The initial geography is two 200 km-class corridor cells selected after ten buyer conversations—likely one north-western port-to-inland industrial/wind corridor and one Tri-City-to-central-Poland corridor, but the actual cells are a hypothesis and are not selected for prestige.

The wedge excludes routine wide-load permits, municipal permit administration, driver navigation and Europe-wide coverage. A candidate account must make enough non-standard route decisions to buy at least two preflights per year, have a named route/estimating owner, and accept that the output is a screening input. Poland alone can prove the product but is unlikely to support the founder-wealth outcome; the wealth case requires sequential CEE/EU data modules.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First ten:** Build a finite list of 60 Polish project-cargo carriers, wind/transformer logistics providers, crane fleets, industrial relocators and route-survey firms from public company sites, project case studies, industry directories and procurement records. The founder sends each route/estimating owner a two-page benchmark, one corridor-specific public-data example and a fixed prepaid scope. The first two customers must be unrelated legal entities and must pay at least 8,000 PLN each. Customers three through ten are won with the same route-change case format, not referrals that depend on one gatekeeper.

**First hundred:** Cover the highest-use Polish corridor cells, then add Czechia, Slovakia, Lithuania and selected German border corridors only after a country-source and commercial-reuse audit. Sell directly to operator route desks and, where they remain independent, to professional survey/permit firms as a triage API. Each country has its own public-source, freshness and authority-boundary record; no distributor is assumed. The first 100 accounts require two hired account executives and local contract data QA, funded from recurring revenue or outside capital after repeat proof.

## Primary Control Point

The raw public maps are not a control point and remain available to competitors. The proposed year-one control point is the founder-owned, legally reusable **Constraint History and Benchmark System**:

1. a normalized road-segment and junction graph joining BDOT10k/road geometry with NMT, NMPT, LiDAR and orthophoto-derived clearance/grade/width features;
2. versioned known bridge/load restrictions and disruptions, with every source and retrieval date retained;
3. vehicle/load envelope models and an articulated swept-path solver with reproducible test cases;
4. a confidence/missingness model that refuses to infer safety from absent data;
5. a benchmark corpus of known blockers and passable controls compared with paid independent survey/engineering labels; and
6. accumulated correction history: which public signals predicted, missed or falsely flagged later survey findings.

The founder owns the normalization code, feature definitions, time-series snapshots where source terms allow storage, solver tests, benchmark labels purchased under assignment, quality metrics and correction history. Public-source facts remain attributed public facts. Customer-specific routes and survey outcomes enter the shared asset only under explicit contractual rights; the initial control point does not depend on customers contributing data.

Within 12 months and 100,000 PLN, the company can prove control by producing two complete corridor cells, source/version manifests, a reproducible benchmark, two paid report artifacts and a documented correction release. It cannot own the underlying Polish public data or prevent Octave, a survey firm or a well-funded TMS vendor from building a similar layer. Defensibility therefore depends on faster multi-source normalization, benchmark depth, historical changes and workflow switching cost, not legal exclusivity.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

The initial asset can be created without a partnership or permit:

- download GUGiK LiDAR/NMT/NMPT, orthophotos and BDOT10k under the published access terms;
- collect GDDKiA's limited-load bridge list, current disruptions and abnormal-load conditions after confirming commercial caching/reuse terms;
- select two corridor cells based on buyer frequency interviews;
- create the graph, clearance/grade features and swept-path tests;
- commission a fixed-price independent route-surveyor/road-engineer benchmark rather than asking for an alliance; and
- spot-check approximately 200 km of public roads from lawful public vantage points, without entering restricted land or representing the work as a legal survey.

The launch benchmark contains at least 40 labelled cases: at least 20 hard geometry/known-restriction blockers and at least 20 passable controls. Ground truth comes from direct measurement where lawful, public engineering facts and a paid independent surveyor's labels. Before sale, the model must have zero serious missed blockers in the small launch set and <=15% false-positive controls; the sample size and uncertainty interval are disclosed. A serious missed blocker is a real obstruction that invalidates the reference vehicle/load but the model calls apparently passable. The continuing kill threshold is >5% serious missed blockers on an expanded blinded set. This benchmark is a screening-quality test, not proof of route safety.

Proof before serious capital is therefore tangible: an autonomously created graph, a benchmark with error classes, a dated corridor report, a fixed professional-indemnity quote and two unrelated customer prepayments. No authority, carrier or survey company must supply the foundational resource.

## Distribution Advantage

The cold-start channel is account-specific outbound to a finite buyer set, not content, an influencer, a trade-show network or a promised association partnership. A prospect receives evidence relevant to its actual job: one public corridor constraint, the benchmark's miss/false-alarm table, the source-age policy and a fixed-price offer. The founder asks for a route envelope and payment, not customer data to build the base product.

Distribution compounds if each paid corridor creates a reusable, non-confidential coverage cell, query pattern and correction class. A growing searchable coverage map lets the company target firms that operate in already-normalized corridors, quote delivery faster and show a nearby benchmark. API integration into a route desk creates switching cost through saved vehicle envelopes, route histories, internal decision records and change alerts. Professional surveyors may later buy the triage layer because it focuses fieldwork, but no exclusive survey relationship is required.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

The initial advantage is Poland-specific evidence density at the **pre-quote rejection** step: faster and cheaper than commissioning a full survey for every speculative corridor, more explicit about missingness than a consumer map, and deployable without a government system sale. It must prove a decision-economic result—fewer surveyed kilometres, an avoided dead corridor or a materially revised quote—not merely a technically accurate map.

Expansion is sequential:

1. two Polish corridor cells and one reference vehicle family;
2. highest-frequency Polish port, wind, transformer and industrial corridors;
3. annual route-desk licences and API access for carriers and independent survey/permit firms;
4. additional vehicle/load geometries and current-change alerts;
5. Czech, Slovak, Baltic and German-border modules only where public-source completeness and reuse pass a country audit; and
6. adjacent users such as wind/transformer OEM project teams, EPC logistics planners and cargo insurers only after the carrier product has repeat transaction evidence.

Octave can copy preflight functionality and trusted survey firms can digitize. HaulGraph's response is not a feature race against their full suites. It is a continuously tested cross-source evidence layer, sold through self-contained API/report artifacts, whose historical miss/correction record becomes expensive to recreate. If buyers insist that this layer must sit inside SafeHaul or their survey provider and will not buy it independently, the standalone company shape fails; an integration feature is not automatically a €10 million-founder business.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland is unusually suitable for the first asset because GUGiK publishes national LiDAR, terrain/surface models, orthophotos and BDOT10k for free download, while GDDKiA publishes route conditions, bridge limitations and current disruptions. The founder can inspect Polish source semantics, work in the local language and visit corridor spot checks cheaply from Warsaw. That is a speed and cost advantage, not a protected right.

The local defence is the Polish normalization history, source-age model, benchmark and route-correction record built before a foreign generalist prioritizes this niche. Each new country is a separate source and liability product, not a superficial map translation. Local execution remains defensible only if country modules can be added at a declining marginal cost and Polish reference evidence shortens sales. If an incumbent can reproduce a country module through one ordinary data-vendor contract with comparable error history, the moat is weak.

## Compounding Advantage

Each normalized corridor adds geometry and source lineage that can answer many future vehicle envelopes. Each completed preflight adds a tested junction, a data-quality observation, an uncertainty pattern and—only where rights permit—a comparison with later survey findings. Each source refresh adds history that a new entrant cannot download retrospectively. Each API customer adds saved workflows and change-alert dependence without being required to donate proprietary data.

The intended loop is: more covered corridors -> more relevant targeted prospects -> more paid preflights -> more independently verified error/correction evidence -> higher trust and faster route-desk adoption -> more recurring cash for country coverage -> more covered corridors. The loop fails if reports remain bespoke analyst work, customers refuse to buy before a trusted survey, or fresh local data cannot be obtained cheaply.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

In conservative and expected-success scenarios, 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution is reinvested while the founder remains employed; any optional founder distributions are capped at 5% and modelled as zero. The strong-success scenario reinvests 100% through the high-growth phase. Reinvestment funds new corridor ingestion, independent benchmark labels, error analysis, source monitoring, insurance, API reliability, account acquisition and country-local data QA.

The founder's original capital buys only the graph/benchmark/paid-proof layer. Customer prepayments fund route-specific delivery. Later country modules are opened only after recurring Polish economics and may use retained cash, grants, debt or equity. Retained cash used in operations is reflected in EBITDA/business value and is not added again as founder cash.

## Critical Dependency Map

1. **Source legality and continuity—partly controlled:** GUGiK access is officially open; commercial caching, attribution and automated access for each bridge/disruption/local source must be documented. Kill the affected module if essential terms are incompatible.
2. **Geometry and structural completeness—uncontrolled facts, controlled disclosure:** old LiDAR, occlusion and missing local bridge capacity are not repaired by modelling. The product returns `unknown`, sends the segment to survey/manager review and is killed if too much of the beachhead remains unknown.
3. **Model accuracy—controlled:** the founder owns the test harness and pays independent label vendors. Release thresholds and a correction log are mandatory.
4. **Freshness—controlled process, imperfect inputs:** every result exposes acquisition/publication/retrieval dates; GDDKiA disruptions refresh at least daily and again at report generation; static source inventories are checked monthly or on official release; old geometry is never described as live.
5. **Authority—explicitly outside control:** GDDKiA states that routes are assessed individually, current road/bridge condition matters and all relevant managers must consent. HaulGraph neither obtains nor predicts that consent.
6. **Liability/trust—commercially testable:** counsel, limitation language and an insurance quote precede sales. Fatal if buyers require safety assurance, permit-grade reliance or uncapped liability from the preflight vendor.
7. **Standalone willingness to pay—commercially testable:** two unrelated prepayments, route/quote change and repeat usage determine whether preflight is separable from the trusted survey.
8. **Founder capacity—controlled:** fixed contractor reviews and risk-based QA keep delivery within 15–20 founder hours/week; bespoke authority calls and manual survey delivery are excluded.

Dependencies are sequenced. No permit, authority data-sharing agreement, exclusive carrier relationship or customer-contributed corpus is required before paid proof.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder keeps the corporate job until all of the following exist: at least five independent paying legal entities; at least three annual licences or contracted recurring route volumes; positive contribution on ten delivered preflights; mature gross margin trending to >=75%; a repeatable acquisition cohort with CAC payback below six months; an expanded blinded benchmark with <=5% serious missed blockers and no concealment of uncertainty; professional-indemnity coverage on acceptable terms; contractor-supported delivery that does not depend on indefinite founder analysis; and at least 12 months of personal runway. One large fleet, one survey partner or one authority project is insufficient.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If customers will not buy preflight separately, stop before country expansion. Reusable assets include the legally sourced normalized Polish corridor graph, provenance/version pipeline, LiDAR/BDOT processing code, swept-path test harness, independent benchmark, source-rights memo, buyer map and measured acquisition record. Where source and contract terms permit, the company can license the constraint/quality layer to an established survey or permit-software vendor; that is a recovery sale, not the base wealth thesis.

The graph and benchmark may also inform a later owned data product for transformer/wind site access or infrastructure-maintenance planning, but only after a new paid problem is established. Customer routes and survey outcomes remain confidential unless the contract explicitly grants reuse.

## Critical Evidence

- GUGiK states that NMT, NMPT, national LiDAR, orthophotos, BDOT10k and basic parcel/building geometry are freely downloadable without limitations. Its English page says opening spatial data is intended to support spatial businesses.
- Geoportal's LiDAR documentation says Poland has national ALS coverage at approximately 4 points/m2 and up to 20 points/m2 in cities, with LAZ available free for any use. It also exposes acquisition-year indexes, including older vintages, proving both availability and freshness risk.
- GDDKiA states that abnormal-load applications are handled individually, vehicle parameters determine the route, all relevant national/regional/county/municipal/concession managers must consent, bridge/road technical condition and current works matter, and a permit is issued only after those route arrangements.
- GDDKiA's February 2026 public explainer reports approximately 16,000 abnormal-load permits issued in 2025 versus more than 15,100 in 2024. This proves activity, not HaulGraph demand or addressable customer count.
- GDDKiA publishes a current road-disruption list and links an official list of engineering structures with limited load capacity.
- Octave SafeHaul already offers integrated OS/OW permitting, vehicle-dimension routing, dynamic restrictions and bridge evaluation. RoadScope already offers North American heavy-haul mapping, bridge/turn constraints and professional survey workflows at publicly listed software prices.

None of this evidence proves the accuracy of a derived HaulGraph layer, current local-road completeness, commercial-reuse terms for every non-GUGiK feed, customer willingness to prepay, avoided cost, annual pricing, insurance availability, retention, European source portability or the serviceable account count.

## Critical Hypotheses

1. At least two unrelated Polish route desks will each prepay 8,000–15,000 PLN within 180 days for quote-screening evidence that explicitly does not replace a survey or permit.
2. At least one paid preflight changes a route, quote or survey scope before authority/field-survey spend.
3. The two initial corridor cells can achieve zero serious missed blockers in the small launch benchmark and <=5% on a later expanded blinded benchmark without classifying most local segments as unknown.
4. Public-source freshness and completeness are sufficient to reject costly bad corridors even though they are insufficient to approve passage.
5. Commercial reuse, caching, attribution and refresh terms permit a profitable production graph; no essential source requires a discretionary authority agreement.
6. Buyers accept limitation language and professional-indemnity coverage is available at a cost compatible with >=55% first-report and >=75% mature gross margin.
7. A Polish route desk will pay €10,000–€25,000 annually, and a high-volume API buyer €30,000–€80,000, because the constraint layer saves more than generic $79–$400/month mapping software.
8. Country ingestion becomes more efficient with shared schema/QA tooling, allowing 300 conservative-case EU accounts without simultaneous authority partnerships.
9. Historical source changes, benchmark corrections and workflow embedding compound faster than Octave, survey firms or TMS vendors can reproduce them.

## Kill Criteria

- Essential source terms prohibit commercial reuse/caching, or a required source exists only through discretionary authority access.
- The initial asset requires private bridge calculations, a permit decision or customer-contributed survey data before it can produce a useful rejection preflight.
- More than 5% of hard blockers are seriously missed on the expanded blinded benchmark, any known miss is hidden, or >40% of target corridor kilometres remain `unknown` after the paid-proof build.
- The product cannot distinguish public facts, founder-derived features and hypotheses in every report, or source acquisition/retrieval dates cannot be reproduced.
- Professional-indemnity cover is unavailable/uneconomic, counsel rejects the limitation, or buyers demand navigation, structural assurance, permit success or uncapped liability.
- Fewer than 12 qualified calls from 60 named accounts, fewer than four proposals, or fewer than two unrelated >=8,000 PLN prepayments by day 180.
- Neither paid buyer changes a route, quote or survey scope; both say the preflight is redundant with their trusted survey/permit provider.
- First-report direct cost exceeds 45% of price, a route requires >12 founder hours after the second delivery, or mature gross margin cannot plausibly reach 75%.
- CAC payback exceeds six months, annual gross revenue retention is <90%, or customers will pay only RoadScope-like generic-map prices for the founder-owned layer.
