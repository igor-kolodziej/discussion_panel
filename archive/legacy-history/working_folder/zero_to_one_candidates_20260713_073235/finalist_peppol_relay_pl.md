# Zero-to-One Candidate: Peppol Relay PL

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build a certified Peppol Access Point and SMP for 50–500 employee Polish and CEE industrial exporters, charging for fast activation of complete buyer-specific order, dispatch, invoice, response, and exception flows—not for commodity invoice transmission—so each customer contract immediately assigns a recurring document route and each activated corridor makes the next exporter cheaper to onboard.

## Founder Fit

This candidate fits the founder's initial constraints better than a hardware, inventory, or authority-dependent business because the first sale is a productised route-readiness engagement that can be delivered alongside corporate employment, with fewer than 40,000 PLN at risk before paid proof. An unknown founder can identify prospects through public company sources and Peppol endpoint/capability data; no celebrity, procurement status, privileged introduction, or speculative distribution partnership is required.

The founder's analytical, software, and latest-AI access can accelerate account research, schema comparison, test generation, mapping documentation, and exception classification. It does not replace EDI experience, information-security operations, or enterprise trust. The minimum credible team therefore includes a part-time AS4/security contractor and a part-time EDI mapping contractor. The founder should not claim 24/7 production reliability personally while retaining corporate employment.

The largest founder-fit risk is domain credibility. Certification supplies technical legitimacy but not the procurement confidence of a known enterprise vendor. The founder must earn trust with a narrow fixed-scope offer, real test messages, transparent incident ownership, and referenceable paid customers rather than broad compliance claims.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The beachhead customer is a 50–500 employee Polish industrial-components or contract-manufacturing exporter with several recurring European buyers. Its finance or EDI team can handle domestic KSeF invoices but still receives orders in a buyer portal or email, exports data from an ERP, manually fixes buyer-specific fields, sends advance shipping notices through another channel, and investigates rejected invoices across several systems.

The customer is not fundamentally buying “Peppol” or “e-invoicing compliance.” It wants these outcomes:

- one accountable route for the buyer's actual order-to-cash document chain;
- fewer rejected documents, portal logins, manual re-keying steps, and payment delays;
- evidence of what was sent, accepted, rejected, corrected, and delivered;
- a fixed implementation scope and a named owner when the counterparty changes a rule;
- the ability to activate another European counterparty without commissioning a fresh bilateral EDI project.

Current workarounds include email and PDF, direct EDI, buyer portals, ERP modules, accounting-suite connectors, the free KSeF API/apps, or an existing Peppol provider. The candidate is irrelevant when invoice-only routing is sufficient. It becomes potentially valuable only where the customer has a live counterparty that supports multiple Peppol business documents or imposes structured-field and response requirements that create recurring exception work.

## Product Wedge

The product is a fixed-scope **Industrial Route Pack** for one exporter, one ERP instance, and one verified counterparty route:

1. verify both endpoints and the counterparty's supported document profiles;
2. map the customer's ERP export/import fields to the required Peppol BIS order, order response, despatch advice, invoice, credit note, and message-level response subset actually used on that route;
3. run test messages and return a signed exception-and-readiness report;
4. reserve a production migration slot conditional on Peppol Relay PL obtaining production certification;
5. after certification, publish or transfer the customer's authorised endpoint, route production documents, monitor failures, and own first-line exception resolution under a bounded SLA.

The initial paid product is a 4,000 PLN non-refundable route-readiness sprint delivered within 30 days. It must include a customer-supplied document sample, real endpoint/capability evidence, test output, and a signed conditional order form for the recurring route. A paid generic “digitalisation workshop” does not count as proof.

The mature pricing hypothesis is:

- 8,000–25,000 PLN one-time activation per ERP/counterparty route, depending on document profiles and mapping complexity;
- approximately €4,800 annual recurring revenue per mature account for managed multi-document routing, included message volume, monitoring, evidence retention, and bounded exception work;
- separately quoted new route or nonstandard ERP work, with no unlimited bespoke customisation hidden in recurring revenue.

These prices are hypotheses. Basic Peppol access is already available far below them; the customer must pay for activation speed and operating accountability, not the certificate.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The commercial insight is that Peppol connectivity itself is becoming cheap, but a mid-market exporter may still lack an owner for the last mile between its ERP, the counterparty's supported profiles, and production exceptions. Large EDI vendors sell broad, credible, customised platforms; cheap Peppol products sell invoice transport. The proposed gap is a narrow industrial-corridor product with a published scope, fast implementation, and recurring exception ownership.

This is deliberately **not**:

- a generic KSeF connector, because KSeF offers free official applications/API access and is already integrated by accounting and ERP vendors;
- a compliance desk or advisory service;
- a public-procurement wedge, because Poland's PEF already provides a free broker and procurement dependence weakens price and reachability;
- a low-cost Peppol mailbox, invoice creator, dashboard, or generic API;
- a claim that certification alone is scarce or defensible.

The market evidence cuts both ways. Peppox advertises managed access for €29 per company per month; B2Brouter advertises €110/year and €300/year web plans with Peppol capabilities; Qvalia describes partner infrastructure at €50/month plus €0.05/message; and PeppolSoft advertises €4,500/year for a 51–200 employee Growth tier including ERP integration. The only supportable €4,800 annual hypothesis is therefore a materially more operational multi-document route, not ordinary sending and receiving.

## Underused Momentum

Poland's KSeF rollout forces finance teams to revisit structured invoicing now: receipt became mandatory from 1 February 2026, with issuance obligations phased through 2026–2027. KSeF is timing momentum, not the product or moat.

Peppol already provides an interoperable four-corner network, standardised BIS profiles, identifiers, PKI, and service-provider agreements. A new company does not need to create a network from zero. The customer can choose and authorise its own Access Point; the foreign counterparty need not sign a new partnership if its endpoint already supports the required document profiles.

Eurostat's 2024 trade-by-enterprise reporting shows that 21.3% of EU goods traders were two-way traders and that manufacturing had the highest two-way-trader share, 43.8%. This supports looking inside recurring industrial trade rather than the whole enterprise population. It does not prove the target denominator.

## Beachhead Market

The initial beachhead is Polish industrial-component and contract-manufacturing exporters with 50–500 employees, an ERP that can expose structured data, and at least one recurring German, Benelux, Nordic, or CEE counterparty whose Peppol endpoint and relevant document profiles can be verified before outreach.

The beachhead is defined by a transaction condition, not by an industry label alone:

- recurring buyer relationship and at least 100 structured commercial documents per month;
- invoice rejection, portal work, bilateral EDI cost, or payment-delay evidence worth at least 40,000 PLN annually;
- counterparty already reachable on Peppol for more than invoice-only exchange, or willing to mandate a documented Peppol profile;
- internal owner in finance, shared services, order management, or EDI with budget authority;
- customer controls selection or migration of its own endpoint.

**Bottom-up denominator requirement:** before downstream evaluation, query Eurostat Trade by Enterprise Characteristics for Polish and adjacent CEE manufacturing exporters/two-way traders by 50–249 and 250+ employee classes, partner-country count, and intra-EU trade. Cross-reference that population with public Peppol Directory/SMP capabilities and direct account research. Statistics Poland reports 26,700 foreign-capital enterprises in 2024, 52.1% of which earned export revenue—about 13,900 firms—but this is only an overbroad proxy, not the eligible denominator.

For the conservative wealth case to avoid heroic share, the research must identify at least 30,000 genuinely eligible accounts across a small number of European corridors without requiring a new foundational approval in each country. The 31–32 million EU enterprise headline is not permitted as a denominator.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Revenue has three layers:

| Layer | Price hypothesis | What the customer receives | Economic requirement |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Route-readiness sprint | 4,000 PLN prepaid | Endpoint/profile verification, field map, test output, exception report, production migration order form | At least 55% contribution margin and delivery in no more than 20 founder/contractor hours |
| Production activation | 8,000–25,000 PLN per route | ERP adapter configuration, testing, endpoint publication/transfer, production cutover | At least 60% gross margin after contractor work |
| Managed route | Average €4,800 ARR per account | Multi-document routing, monitoring, evidence, bounded exception handling, included volume | 72% blended mature gross margin and annual logo churn below 10% |

At the €4,800 ARR hypothesis, an account contributes about €3,456 gross profit at 72% gross margin. A sustainable direct acquisition model requires fully loaded customer acquisition cost below roughly €3,500, payback below 12 months after activation revenue, standard onboarding below 20 hours, and support/exception cost below 15% of recurring revenue. High-volume messages should carry a usage component so Qvalia-like wholesale costs cannot erase margin.

Commodity transport prices are a hard anchor, not a footnote:

- Peppox: €29/month per company using a managed Access Point;
- Qvalia partner example: €50/month plus €0.05 per sent/received message, covering AP/SMP use, routing, validation, monitoring, maintenance, onboarding, and support;
- B2Brouter: €110/year Professional and €300/year Business web plans, including Peppol features;
- PeppolSoft: €4,500/year Growth plan for 51–200 employees, advertised with ERP integration;
- Pepposh: low-volume plans with €0.20 per sent/received invoice beyond an included allowance.

Accordingly, certification and message margin cannot support the business. The unit-economics hypothesis survives only if customers repeatedly pay for multi-document activation, route-specific mappings, and accountable exception operations that cheap products do not deliver in equivalent scope.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

### First 10

Build a list of 150 Polish exporters from public trade-fair exhibitor lists, company sites, registries, and industrial clusters. Retain only accounts for which a named counterparty endpoint and supported Peppol profiles can be checked. For each account, prepare a one-page “route evidence” document showing the existing manual/bilateral flow, the reachable counterparty, relevant document profiles, and an explicit value hypothesis.

Founder-led acquisition sequence:

1. Send 150 highly specific messages to finance/EDI/order-management owners across two corridors.
2. Secure 25 route-diagnostic calls and obtain redacted document samples from at least 10.
3. Close the first three unrelated 4,000 PLN prepaid route-readiness sprints before paying major certification costs.
4. Deliver each sprint inside 30 days and require a conditional recurring order form, not just a satisfaction statement.
5. Reach ten paid sprints, at least six signed production orders, and at least three live recurring routes before treating the wedge as repeatable.

The numbers above are operating targets, not assumed conversion evidence. Failure thresholds appear under Kill Criteria.

### First 100

Stay inside the two best-performing ERP/counterparty corridors. Turn the first mappings, exception taxonomies, security packet, migration checklist, and case evidence into fixed packages. Acquire the next 90 customers through the same direct route-evidence mechanism, customer-permitted introductions to adjacent suppliers, and searchable corridor content based on actual supported profiles. No ERP reseller, accounting firm, or trade association is required for the plan to work; such channels are optional accelerants only after direct sales economics exist.

The 100-customer target is 24–36 months. It requires at least 40% of new activations to reuse an existing adapter/profile package, gross revenue retention above 90%, median sales cycle below 90 days, and a documented acquisition source with sub-12-month payback. If every customer remains a bespoke EDI project, the route to 100 and the mature margins fail.

## Primary Control Point

The formal resource is a signed Peppol Service Provider Agreement, production PKI certificate, certified Access Point status, SMP capability, assigned participant identifiers, and the customer's explicit authority to publish or transfer its receiving endpoint. OpenPeppol's official setup sequence is membership, agreement with the relevant Peppol Authority, test PKI certificate, AS4 implementation, mandatory testbed, production PKI certificate, and certified listing.

The current published small-company AP+SMP fees are €1,800 sign-up, €2,750 annual membership, and €2,500 certification: €7,050 before any national extras, implementation, security, or operating cost. AP-only fees are lower, but AP-only status would weaken direct endpoint control and may introduce SMP dependency.

Once a customer assigns its receiving endpoint, recurring inbound documents for that published identifier route to the provider while the contract remains in force. This is a present, signed routing position. It is not ownership: customers remain free to switch Access Points, outbound sending may be less exclusive, and any certified provider can reach the network. Certification is purchasable and cheap relative to enterprise software budgets. The economic control point must therefore compound from authorised endpoint routes, reusable mappings, migration history, and embedded exception procedures—not the certificate alone.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

The cold start avoids building a complete production platform or asking counterparties to join a new network:

1. **Prove demand first:** sell three prepaid route-readiness sprints using existing endpoint/profile data and a local test harness. Spend no more than 12,000 PLN before the first payments.
2. **Prove delivery:** complete the three maps/tests, document time and contribution margin, and obtain conditional recurring production orders.
3. **Secure the formal resource:** obtain a written Polish Peppol Authority onboarding path; join OpenPeppol; sign the Service Provider Agreement; obtain test PKI; implement the smallest compliant AS4/AP+SMP stack; pass the testbed; obtain production PKI and public certified status.
4. **Secure customer routing:** use explicit customer contracts to publish or transfer endpoints and run live documents. Counterparty adoption is not speculative because outreach is restricted to routes whose opposite endpoint/profile already exists.

The founder can use open-source or hosted AS4 components, as OpenPeppol's setup guide permits different implementation models, but the certificate and customer authority should belong to Peppol Relay PL. Using Qvalia or another provider as the permanent Access Point may accelerate a test, but it converts the foundational resource into a revocable vendor contract and cannot be presented as the end-state control point.

Proof within 12 months and 100,000 PLN must include: written authority requirements; signed provider paperwork; test and production PKI; certified public listing; successful testbed messages; at least three independent paid customers; at least two live customer-authorised endpoints; and positive contribution on initial delivery.

## Distribution Advantage

The initial distribution advantage is **observable route targeting**. Generic providers market “e-invoicing.” Peppol Relay PL contacts only exporters for which it can show a named, reachable counterparty and a concrete supported document path. That reduces education, avoids dependence on mass brand trust, and makes outbound evidence specific.

The approach can compound if every activated route produces, with customer permission:

- another verified counterparty/capability record;
- a reusable ERP-to-BIS mapping component;
- exception patterns for a specific corridor;
- a referenceable implementation time and value case;
- adjacent suppliers or buyers with the same requirements.

This is an execution hypothesis, not protected distribution. Competitors can inspect the same public endpoints and buy the same outbound data. The advantage exists only if the company demonstrates materially faster activation and lower acquisition cost in two corridors.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

### Current competitive set

- **Cheap managed/API products:** Peppox, B2Brouter, Pepposh, Qvalia-powered partners, and similar offers make basic routing a commodity.
- **Enterprise EDI/compliance suites:** Comarch advertises Peppol, KSeF, many formats, purchase orders and ASNs, dedicated project management, 60-country coverage, and more than 130,000 connected entities. ecosio similarly sells a multi-network integration hub, API, monitoring, and broad protocol reach.
- **Polish certified providers:** the official Polish Peppol page currently lists All For One Poland, Anachron Technology Poland, Comarch, EDITEL Polska, and Infinite.
- **ERP/accounting vendors and free state tools:** these can absorb invoice-only KSeF/Peppol use and remove the need for a specialist.

### Likely incumbent response

Low-cost providers can add Polish onboarding, discount transport, or expose the same APIs. Enterprise vendors can package a rapid exporter offer, cross-sell existing EDI customers, and use stronger security credentials. A Polish ERP vendor can make one internal process change and bundle Peppol. The expected response is therefore price pressure on basic access and stronger claims around global compliance, ERP breadth, uptime, and trust.

The candidate can remain differentiated only by proving a narrower economic result: verified counterparty targeting, fixed-scope multi-document activation, migration in weeks rather than quarters, and first-line exception ownership at a predictable price. If PeppolSoft's €4,500 Growth tier or a Comarch quotation delivers equivalent ERP integration and managed exception scope at similar speed, there is no meaningful gap.

### Expansion path

1. two Polish industrial exporter corridors;
2. additional Polish manufacturers using the same ERP/profile packages;
3. Czech, Slovak, and Baltic exporters on the same counterpart corridors;
4. supplier activation around anchor routes;
5. order, response, dispatch, invoice, credit, and status bundles;
6. payment-status and exception-resolution services only when built from permissioned routed events.

The core Peppol resource should not reset country by country, but national extensions, authority conditions, data residency, or local tax mandates may still add work. Expansion is rejected if each country requires a new expensive approval or bespoke compliance team.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland supplies a timely sales trigger through KSeF, a substantial industrial export base, lower initial engineering cost, and finance teams that must bridge domestic clearance with European buyer workflows. Polish-language onboarding and knowledge of common Polish ERP exports can improve activation speed. CEE expansion can reuse regional language, ERP, and corridor knowledge.

The defence is operational, not regulatory exclusivity. Peppol is deliberately interoperable, and foreign Access Points can serve Polish customers. KSeF itself cannot be claimed as a moat. Local adaptation is meaningful only if it produces measurable advantages—shorter activation, fewer rejected documents, lower implementation cost, and a denser reusable map library—before global incumbents prioritise the same segment.

## Compounding Advantage

Potential compounding assets are:

- customer-authorised endpoint relationships and renewal history;
- reusable ERP-to-Peppol mapping components;
- a versioned capability map of counterparties and corridors;
- permissioned exception/resolution data that improves testing and onboarding;
- security, migration, and operating procedures accepted by finance teams;
- corridor references that lower trust and acquisition cost for adjacent exporters.

There is no proprietary Peppol network effect: every Access Point can reach every other Access Point. Endpoint assignments are revocable, standards are public, and data use is constrained by customer contracts and GDPR. The compounding claim is credible only if later customers demonstrably onboard faster and cheaper than the first cohort, and if customers remain because changing providers would require remapping operating procedures—not because of artificial lock-in.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

While the founder retains corporate employment, the conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution into certification, security, reusable adapters, route capability data, incident automation, sales evidence, and customer acquisition. Optional founder distributions are limited to 5%. The strong case may reinvest 100%.

Reinvestment order:

1. production reliability and security evidence;
2. reusable mappings and automated conformance tests;
3. exception automation that reduces support cost per account;
4. repeatable direct acquisition in the initial corridors;
5. local sales/operations capacity only after a corridor has positive contribution economics;
6. country expansion only when the same resource and adapters transfer.

The company should finance early growth with customer payments and retained contribution. Outside equity is justified only after repeatable acquisition and may not be used to disguise negative account economics. Revenue, retained cash, enterprise value, and founder distributions must remain separate in later models.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder may leave corporate employment only after all of the following exist:

- at least ten independent paying customers and at least six contracted recurring production routes;
- multiple live endpoints with positive contribution economics;
- gross margin and support time consistent with the mature model;
- one repeatable acquisition source with sub-12-month CAC payback;
- at least 12 months of personal runway after taxes and liabilities;
- a costed 24/7 operating arrangement that does not depend on the founder being continuously available;
- no single customer above 25% of recurring revenue;
- written confirmation that continued certification/insurance/security obligations fit available cash.

Certification alone, one anchor customer, a grant, or a pipeline does not justify leaving employment.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If certification or pricing fails after the paid-proof phase, the founder should stop rather than operate indefinitely as a generic integration consultant. Reusable assets are the permissioned route maps, BIS test fixtures, ERP field dictionaries, conformance harness, exception taxonomy, security documentation, prospect research, and quantified buyer interviews. These can be licensed or sold as bounded implementation components to an existing certified provider, used to negotiate employment/domain roles, or applied to a future transaction-rail candidate. Customer data and mappings may be reused only where contracts permit.

The first 12,000 PLN tranche is designed so that failure still produces these assets and decision-grade evidence. Later certification spending is released only after customers have paid and initial delivery has positive contribution.

## Kill Criteria

Kill the candidate, rather than turning it into a generic KSeF/compliance desk, if any of these occurs:

1. The Polish Peppol Authority will not provide a written onboarding path compatible with certification inside 12 months and 100,000 PLN.
2. Mandatory national fees, audits, insurance, security evidence, guarantees, implementation, or operating reserves push the initial resource above 100,000 PLN.
3. Fewer than three unrelated customers prepay 4,000 PLN after 150 evidence-led outreaches and at least 25 qualified conversations.
4. Paid buyers accept only a one-off workshop and refuse conditional recurring production orders.
5. The median recurring willingness to pay is below €3,000/year or gross margin cannot exceed 60% after support and infrastructure.
6. Competitor quotations—especially PeppolSoft's €4,500 Growth scope or a Polish enterprise provider—match the route, implementation speed, and exception ownership at equal or lower cost.
