# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Afterline Paid Transition Mandate v2

## Diagnosis

V2 materially changes the failed cold start: the exiting OEM owner is now the first paying buyer, and the prepaid mandate itself delivers archive custody, exclusivity, temporary rights, installed-base communication, and a call option before customer fulfilment. It also confines expansion to one machinery vertical and values founder equity after explicit terminal acquisition liabilities. The unproved owner willingness to pay is now a single cheap falsifiable hypothesis rather than one dependency inside a larger unpaid chain. The candidate is suitable for a new independent panel.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Sourced evidence supports a timely EU/Polish SME succession problem, family-business prevalence, and strategic repair continuity. The v1 panel verdicts are judgment, not market evidence, but they directly informed the structural change. Critical hypotheses remain: dated-exit owners will prepay 12,000–20,000 PLN; a mandate can legally grant the full temporary rights bundle; seller and machine-owner demand will coexist; and two lines in one vertical will share operating assets.

## Overall Score

**82/100.** Weighted points are `163 ÷ 20 = 8.15`; multiplying by 10 gives 82 after rounding. Advisory only; it is not a simulated or real validation score.

## Control Point

**Tier 1 conditional.** A prepaid exclusive mandate directly controls archive custody, temporary commercial use, seller-authorised installed-base communication, non-appointment/non-licensing, and a call option over named aftermarket assets. Strongest copy risk: a better-capitalised consolidator competes for future sellers or independent technicians bypass Afterline on contestable customer spend. The control point falls to Tier 3 if the paid engagement may end as a report or lacks custody/exclusivity.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass.** Conservative, expected, and strong scenarios include lines/sites, pricing, milestones, revenue, gross and operating margin after earn-outs, capital, ownership/dilution, reinvestment, enterprise value, terminal obligations, equity value, distributions, and timing. The conservative path reaches 47.24m PLN/€11.0m after deducting 6m PLN of net debt/seller notes/earn-outs/reserves, with only 5m PLN of separately held post-employment distributions. The expected case reaches 68.9m PLN/€16.0m founder equity after 12m PLN obligations. The decisive weakness is empirical repeatability of 14–15 line transfers, but the arithmetic no longer hides acquisition liabilities.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** The paid proof is one 12,000–20,000 PLN seller mandate by day 120, with no more than 18,000 PLN founder cash at risk. The mandate itself grants the needed resource and funds its lawyer/engineer review. Customer deposits/POs are a later gate before closing, so the first paid proof no longer requires simultaneous seller, rights, customer, delivery, and margin success. Work is compatible with employment; no platform, warehouse, or acquisition is funded first.

## Expansion Check

The buyer/event is narrow and urgent. Expansion is explicitly restricted to one vertical until two paid mandates and two lines show shared technicians, suppliers, customers, or documentation. Competitors can chase future sellers, but cannot copy an active exclusive mandate or already transferred archive. Poland/CEE is an execution and sourcing advantage, not a claimed legal barrier.

## Fatal Objections

None identified in the written v2 plan. The highest-risk hypothesis is seller willingness to prepay for a transition mandate when brokers or buyers often absorb diligence costs. A report-only mandate, unclear temporary rights, or no paid mandate after 80 approaches is fatal and must not be disguised as learning progress.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Make the exiting owner the first paying customer so the paid contract itself secures custody, exclusivity, and the acquisition option before downstream customer proof.
