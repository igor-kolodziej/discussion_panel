# Zero-to-One Evaluation: BatchWindow v1

## Diagnosis

BatchWindow correctly identifies that micro-brand co-packing fails through readiness and capacity risk rather than discovery alone, and its named option plus three deposits is a legitimate paid test. The narrow shelf-stable envelope and staged loss fit the founder. However, plants already offering low MOQs can internalise the same standardisation, marketplaces can add deposits/options, and shared runs may not create enough savings after recipe changes and quality liability. The wealth model is arithmetically complete but assumes a European transaction rail before the first repeatable proprietary advantage exists.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Company sources establish low-MOQ supply and existing discovery/implementation competitors. They do not establish assignable window rights, plant idle dates, brand deposit behaviour, shared-run safety or an 8–10% durable take rate. All run economics, €90m conservative routed value, 35% operating margin and plant/brand acquisition assumptions are hypotheses. No external verdict was used.

## Overall Score

**66/100.** Weighted points are 131.5 across 20 weight units; `10 × 131.5 / 20 = 65.75`, rounded to 66. This is advisory only.

## Control Point

**Tier 2.** A signed named option plus deposits controls one transaction, but the right is short-lived and the method is routine-contract copyable. Strongest copy risk: an existing co-packer or Wonnda/Agilery adds readiness templates, window reservations and escrowed deposits to its installed plant/brand network.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, narrowly as a model; not evidenced.** The scenarios include value, ownership, capital and reinvestment without double-counting. The conservative €13.7m founder value has cushion, but it requires 800 active brands, 45 plants, €90m routed value, 8% fees and 35% operating margin. These are not obviously heroic for Europe, yet there is no evidence that the fee survives direct dealing or that quality labour stays low enough. Any structural revision must preserve a non-direct-dealable control point.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass, narrowly.** Three unrelated deposits against a named plant date are budget-backed proof inside 180 days with <=35,000 PLN loss and continued employment. Sequential plant/technologist/brand dependencies are explicit. Passing deposits does not prove safe execution or repeatability.

## Expansion Check

The first envelope is coherent. Expansion to identical plants/brands can reuse data; expansion to new formats reopens process validation. Poland is a sourcing advantage, not a moat. Incumbents start with stronger distribution and can enter through routine product changes.

## Fatal Objections

1. The control point can be copied by an incumbent through routine contracting and workflow changes.
2. Recipe/changeover/quality variation may eliminate the economic benefit of shared capacity.
3. Direct plant-brand dealing can compress the take rate after the first run.
4. The conservative wealth case assumes a high-margin European rail before retention is proved.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Turn fragmented small buyers into one technically homogeneous, fully funded production event before taking capacity risk.
