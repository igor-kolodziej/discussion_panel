# Zero-to-One Evaluation: FieldWire Cell v1

## Diagnosis

FieldWire has excellent proof discipline: the founder can own the machine/IP bundle, demonstrate the buyer's own mixed schedule, cap pre-payment exposure at 38,000 PLN and fund sale-unit bills of material with customer deposits. It is materially better than a software wrapper or integration consultancy. However, April 2026 official evidence changes the market diagnosis. Rittal's WT L now targets small and medium-sized panel builders with a compact 12-feed machine, the same 0.5–6 mm² envelope, automatic cut/strip/crimp/label functions and an economic claim at 150 cabinets/year. With no verified incumbent price, FieldWire's remaining insight is “similar integrated outcome near €12,000.” That is a price-led entrant thesis against an incumbent with platform engineering, Eplan integration, conformity, manufacturing and service. The conservative wealth model then asks for 1,000 units/year and 6,000 installed units without an eligible-account denominator.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Official Rittal, Komax and Schleuniger material proves that the end-to-end job-driven workflow and modular wire-processing stack exist. Rittal's 2026 WT L announcement directly claims the SME/low-throughput segment and two-square-metre footprint. Public EU statistics show thousands of electrical/control-apparatus enterprises, but do not identify the subset with the specified weekly mixed-wire volume or prove replacement demand. No cited evidence establishes FieldWire's €12,000 build economics, 35% skilled-minute saving, quality, conformity cost, buyer deposits, recurring attachment or 1,000-unit annual market. No panel or chatbot verdict was used.

## Overall Score

**66.5/100.** Weighted points are 133 across 20 weight units; `10 × 133 / 20 = 66.5`. This is advisory only and is not a validation score.

## Control Point

**Tier 2, but readily copyable.** Assigned CAD, firmware, recipes, importers, benchmark data and an installed service record are directly ownable. The alpha does not require a licence or speculative partnership. Strongest copy risk: Rittal lowers WT L pricing/configuration, or Komax/Schleuniger/Asian integrators combine existing modules and job-list software, using established conformity and service to neutralize FieldWire before its installed base compounds.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The conservative result is about €11.1 million in year 14, only 11% above the threshold, while assuming 1,000 machines/year, 6,000 installed units, 19% operating margin, 60% ownership and a 7× multiple. The available 16,500-enterprise statistic is a broad combined industrial category, not the eligible weekly-volume subset. A direct incumbent now targets small/medium firms at 150 cabinets/year. Modest dilution, margin pressure, lower service attachment or a smaller eligible subset breaks the gate.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** The founder can build a supervised alpha from cash-purchased components and assigned contract work, remain employed, expose at most 38,000 PLN before two unrelated 5,000 PLN deposits, and complete one guarded sequential beta within 95,000 PLN after paid proof. Own-schedule demonstrations reduce unknown-founder trust. The gate survives even though the broader company thesis does not.

## Expansion Check

The panel-shop beachhead and adjacent recipes are technically coherent. Yet expansion does not strengthen the control point faster than broad vendors can move down-market: Rittal already offers the same conductor range and workflow with a service/Eplan ecosystem. More recipe support risks increasing integration and conformity burden rather than creating monopoly economics.

## Fatal Objections

1. The newest Rittal product directly targets the candidate's claimed neglected SME/low-throughput beachhead.
2. The advantage is reduced to an unverified price point; no written comparable quote proves the gap.
3. The conservative wealth case requires a large unverified share of the eligible European subset and has little downside resilience.
4. Established machinery vendors can reproduce the integration with existing service, conformity and CAD/workflow distribution.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

“Bring tomorrow's schedule” is a powerful unknown-founder sales mechanism for measurable workshop automation whose owned asset can be built before customer permission.

## Recommendation

**kill**

Do not pivot by shaving scope, changing the conductor range or asserting a still-lower price. Those changes remain inside the same price-led integration contest. Preserve the acquisition mechanism, owned-alpha discipline and customer-funded production logic for a workflow where no incumbent has just launched into the exact beachhead.
