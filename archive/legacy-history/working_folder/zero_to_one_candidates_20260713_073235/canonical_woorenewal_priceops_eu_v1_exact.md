# WooRenewal PriceOps EU v1

## Idea and Thesis

Acquire one WooCommerce bulk-editor plugin with at least 1,000 organic active installations, existing paid billing, and an official transferable update channel, then convert it into PriceOps EU: a bulk price-change workflow for EU merchants and agencies combining maker-checker approval, price history, reference-price calculation, detailed audit, rollback, and evidence export.

Success means 1,000,000 PLN recognized annual revenue by Year 5 with at least 25% operating margin. Revenue excludes VAT, GMV, and pass-through amounts. This replaces the default founder-wealth gate for this run.

## Founder Fit

The Warsaw data-scientist founder can model cohorts, build audit/data workflows, automate support, and use AI with a contract WordPress security engineer. Polish/EU counsel is required to bound product behavior and evidence wording. The first 180 days remain part-time, under five hours daily and 40,000 PLN pre-proof exposure.

## Customer, Pain, and Willingness to Pay

The customer is an EU WooCommerce merchant with at least 1,000 SKUs or an agency managing price changes across at least five stores. The painful event is a bulk price update that publishes unintended prices, erases margin, produces partial changes, or leaves no defensible record of the prior price, approval, and rollback.

EU law requires the prior price in a price-reduction announcement to be the lowest price applied during at least the previous 30 days. Polish enforcement is active. Existing paid bulk editors and Omnibus tools prove spending in the category, but no evidence proves willingness to pay the proposed PriceOps tiers.

## Product Wedge

Acquire a bulk editor that already has organic installations, paid billing, preview or undo, low support, and clean WordPress/Freemius transfer. Preserve existing functionality and pricing initially. Add:

- maker-checker approval for scheduled price changes;
- immutable before/after operation history;
- rollback checkpoints;
- lowest-price calculation for the relevant lookback;
- agency/client evidence exports;
- store-level policies and explicit user roles.

The product does not promise blanket legal compliance. Counsel reviews the workflow and wording; the merchant remains responsible for commercial decisions and country-specific application.

## Beachhead and Distribution

Start with Polish WooCommerce agencies and catalogue-heavy merchants. The acquired installed base supplies the first legacy receipt and the first PriceOps upgrade offers. Additional distribution comes from the WordPress directory, organic search for bulk pricing and price history, documentation, and referrals. No influencer, reseller, affiliate, or platform partnership is required.

The public acquisition precedent is an unnamed 2026 WooCommerce bulk editor offered for USD 6,000 with claimed 1,000+ installs and USD 317 monthly revenue. Seller identity, payer count, renewal, code, security, and availability are unverified.

## Primary Control Point

After acquisition, WooRenewal owns the official update channel, installed base, paid licences, billing, licence server, domain, brand, repository/IP, and support history. Each adopting merchant accumulates local operation history, approvals, rules, and rollback checkpoints.

The control point is limited: merchants can export their data, and competitors already offer history, snapshots, audit, scheduling, undo, lowest-price calculation, or combinations of them. The strongest copy risk is an incumbent bulk editor adding maker-checker approval and evidence export through routine development.

## Competitive Reality

The official WooCommerce Marketplace already lists a bulk editor with detailed operation history, snapshots, rollback, and actor/time audit. Product Editor Pro provides scheduled edits, activity logs, and 50 undo operations. Lavo provides free before/after snapshots and undo. Omnibus and price-history plugins already implement the EU reference-price function, including changes received through APIs and imports.

PriceOps therefore competes through integration and acquired distribution, not unique capability. The proposed features do not currently form a defensible data network, exclusive right, regulatory authorization, or switching cost that a competent incumbent cannot reproduce.

## Cold Start and 180-Day Paid Proof

1. Identify the named plugin and seller behind the public USD 6,000 offer or another target within 28,000 PLN cash.
2. Obtain a primary data room proving owner/update access, at least 100 paid sites or an equivalent credible beta cohort, receipts, renewal, refunds, support, IP, security, and atomic transfer.
3. Close through escrow while total pre-proof exposure remains below 40,000 PLN.
4. Receive one unrelated legacy renewal after transfer.
5. Conduct security and bounded legal review.
6. Offer only the minimum PriceOps beta to the installed base.
7. Require ten paid PriceOps beta upgrades or agency deposits by Day 180; a legacy renewal alone does not validate the pivot.

No seller or customer contact has occurred.

## Reachability and Capital

The public USD 6,000 ask fits the cash envelope, but the seller/product are anonymous and unverified. Sourcing, diligence, security/legal review, acquisition cash, and the beta must together remain below 40,000 PLN before paid PriceOps proof. Further spending up to 100,000 PLN requires successful delivery and positive contribution. Family capital remains excluded.

## Expansion and Compounding

Expand from Polish agencies to EU agencies using the same price-operation engine, then into adjacent controlled catalogue changes such as supplier imports, promotions, and stock. A second plugin acquisition is allowed only when it reaches the same customers and workflow.

Compounding would come from accumulated store policies, history, rollback checkpoints, and WooCommerce compatibility knowledge. Because several competitors already store similar history and audit data, this advantage remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated moat.

## Critical Dependencies and Kill Criteria

- Kill if the named asset cannot be verified or atomically transferred within the cash cap.
- Kill if primary evidence does not show at least 100 paid sites or a credible path to ten paid beta upgrades.
- Kill if fewer than ten paid PriceOps upgrades/deposits arrive by Day 180.
- Kill if merchants will pay only for low-priced display/history utilities rather than the proposed operational tiers.
- Kill if counsel finds the workflow cannot be marketed usefully without individualized legal advice.
- Kill if competitor review confirms that approval, history, rollback, audit, and evidence can be matched through a routine feature release without a specific switching-cost advantage.
- Kill if evidence does not support a path to 1m PLN recognized revenue and at least 25% operating margin by Year 5.
