# Zero-to-One Pivot: Afterline Paid Transition Mandate

## Preserved Insight

The commercially scarce asset is the combination of technical truth and the installed-base distribution route. External cash must arrive before the founder releases acquisition capital.

## Structural Change

Change the first buyer from the installed-base factory to the exiting OEM owner and change the first product from an acquisition option requiring later customer proof to a **paid, exclusive transition mandate** that immediately grants archive custody, temporary commercial-use rights, seller-authorised customer communication, and a call option over the aftermarket assets.

The owner pays 12,000–20,000 PLN upfront for a fixed 60-day continuity conversion because an orderly transition protects reputation, captures earn-out value, and avoids abandoning customers. The fee is credited against Afterline's closing consideration if the line transfers. The mandate is not succession advice: the contracted output is an executed data/rights escrow, a verified installed-base schedule, three customer offers, and either a demand-gated asset transfer or a documented wind-down pack returned to the owner.

This makes the paid mandate itself the 180-day paid proof and the initial control point. Customer prepayments become the second capital-release gate, not a prerequisite for proving that the founder can get paid.

## Target Niche and Buyer

Buyer: a 55+ owner of a Polish non-regulated niche machine OEM or a small OEM discontinuing one product line, with 100–1,500 active machines, incomplete succession, repeat aftermarket activity, and concern about customer abandonment or reputational liability.

Start with owners already signalling retirement, liquidation, product-line discontinuation, or inability to maintain service response. Exclude distressed sellers unable to grant clean rights or pay the mandate.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The event is a dated exit or product-line shutdown. The owner faces customer complaints, lost residual value, pressure on remaining staff, and the risk that years of drawings and service history become worthless. Paying 12,000–20,000 PLN is rational only when the transition can preserve a materially larger earn-out or reduce wind-down cost; this willingness-to-pay is a new hypothesis requiring direct testing.

## Value and Profit Wedge

Fixed 12,000–20,000 PLN prepaid transition mandate with a tightly standardised deliverable and 55%+ contribution after lawyer/engineer allocation. If demand is proven, Afterline exercises the call option using customer prepayments plus a seller earn-out. Later revenue remains continuity plans, parts, and retrofits, but the founder no longer funds the trust-building and rights-packaging stage.

## Domination Wedge

The paid mandate immediately makes Afterline the exclusive transition agent and escrow custodian for the named product line. During the mandate, no other buyer or broker may solicit or transfer the aftermarket package. At closing, the mandate converts into an irrevocable assignment/exclusive licence. The company is not dominant if it only delivers a report or if the owner can appoint another agent.

## Acquisition Channel

Direct written outreach to 80 owners with visible exit/discontinuation signals; listings and CE plates identify machine builders, while KRS/CEIDG, trade-fair history, owner interviews, and succession advisers identify the event. The message sells an orderly, paid continuity conversion with an earn-out—not generic succession consulting or an immediate business sale. The first 20 calls must establish whether owners will pay; if none will, the pivot is killed without customer outreach.

## Primary Control Point

An executed, prepaid, exclusive transition mandate containing:

- archive escrow/custody and defined temporary commercial-use rights;
- seller-authorised installed-base communication and compliant data-use terms;
- exclusive call option over specified tooling, spares, mark, files, and transferable contracts;
- seller non-appointment/non-licensing covenant during the mandate;
- liability, insurance, temporary fulfilment, refund/credit, and exit provisions.

The mandate is Tier 1 during its term only when these rights are enforceable. A paid advisory project without custody and exclusivity is Tier 3 and prohibited.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Spend up to 5,000 PLN to interview 20 target owners and obtain three lawyer/engineer critiques of the mandate.
2. Send 80 event-specific owner letters and require at least four serious calls and two scoped data-room invitations.
3. Sell one 12,000–20,000 PLN prepaid mandate by day 120. Cap sales/legal/travel spend before payment at 18,000 PLN.
4. The seller deposits the archive and signs the option before work starts; the paid fee funds the engineer/lawyer review.
5. During days 121–180, verify 30 active machines and seek three installed-base deposits or POs. No acquisition closes without them.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: the owner is buying a bounded transition product, retains a closing credit and earn-out, and does not have to trust the founder with an unpaid speculative acquisition. The mandate itself proves resource control, budget, and seller trust.

## Expansion Path

First prove the same paid mandate with two unrelated sellers. Then specialise within one machinery vertical so rights schedules, liability exclusions, technicians, parts suppliers, and customer offers reuse. The portfolio may expand only inside that vertical until two lines share at least two of technicians, suppliers, customers, and documentation systems. This prevents a heterogeneous roll-up from masquerading as compounding.

## Minimum People Stack

Founder; one transaction/IP lawyer who signs off the reusable mandate; one vertical-specific machinery engineer; order-paid technicians. Add a product-line operator only after closing. No platform build or full-time hires before one paid mandate and three customer commitments.

## Principal Risk

Exiting owners may refuse to pay for a transfer mandate and may expect the buyer or broker to absorb all diligence cost. If willingness to pay is absent, the new buyer/value-capture structure fails and the original dependency problem returns. A second major risk is that even a paid mandate does not make the eventual line economics or repeat seller supply attractive.

## First Disconfirming Test

Within 45 days, interview 20 owners with a genuine exit/discontinuation signal and present the exact 12,000–20,000 PLN prepaid exclusive mandate. Kill the pivot if fewer than three confirm a real budget or if none will open a data room contingent on payment. By day 120, require one signed prepaid mandate; by day 180, require archive custody plus three independent customer deposits/POs. Total founder cash before seller payment must remain below 18,000 PLN and total pre-proof cash below 40,000 PLN.

## Evidence Preserved

Official June 2026 EU sources support the succession timing problem, family-business prevalence, and need for better transfer mechanisms. The original panel preserves evidence that line-level assigned rights can be Tier 1 and that customer cash is a strong acquisition gate.

## New Hypotheses

- Exiting OEM owners will pay 12,000–20,000 PLN upfront for a bounded exclusive transition mandate.
- The fee can cover rights/technical diligence at 55%+ contribution.
- A paid mandate can lawfully grant temporary commercial-use rights and customer communication before closing.
- Specialising within one vertical will create enough cross-line reuse to support the founder-wealth path.
- Terminal equity value net of seller notes, debt, earn-outs, warranty reserves, and personal liabilities can still exceed 43m PLN.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

The original candidate needed an unknown founder to win an unpaid option, clear legal/technical diligence, obtain customer prepayments, and deliver safely before any external party paid him. The pivot changes the first buyer to the exiting owner, makes paid proof precede customer fulfilment, and secures custody, temporary rights, exclusivity, and the call option in the paid contract. It also restricts expansion to one vertical with measured operational reuse and requires all founder-wealth values to deduct acquisition liabilities. If the owner will not pay, the shape is killed rather than reverting to the original unpaid trust chain.

## Priority

Return this single pivot to the evaluator first. It directly attacks the panel's reachability objection while retaining the line-level Tier 1 control point; the new owner willingness-to-pay hypothesis can be falsified quickly and cheaply.
