# Zero-to-One Candidate: Afterline Installed-Base Succession Buyout

## One-Sentence Thesis

Acquire the complete aftermarket position of retiring or non-core Polish/CEE niche machine builders on low-cash, seller-financed terms, then use the seller-authorised installed-base channel to pre-sell continuity plans, spare parts, and retrofits before closing and compound a portfolio of captive maintenance cash flows.

## Founder Fit

The founder can source and analyse targets asynchronously, build installed-base and unit-economics models, automate documentation, and centralise the data layer. His parents' seven-person renewable-installation company provides relevant exposure to field service, quoting, electrical work, and owner succession, but it is not a required sales partner. A contract machinery engineer and transaction lawyer cover technical and legal diligence. The business avoids influencer distribution and does not require the founder to become the chief technician.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The paying end customer is a Polish/CEE food, packaging, wood, light-manufacturing, or building-products factory operating a maintenance-critical niche machine from a small owner-led OEM. When that OEM retires or drops a line, the factory faces long downtime, undocumented substitutions, technician loss, and pressure to replace a serviceable machine. Current workarounds are calling the founder personally, commissioning one-off reverse engineering, hoarding parts, or replacing the machine. A day of downtime can plausibly exceed a 8,000–25,000 PLN annual continuity fee, but this is a hypothesis to verify per niche.

The asset seller is a 55+ owner, estate, restructuring adviser, or small OEM that wants recurring retirement income and customer continuity without operating a field-service organisation. The owner often values reputation, staff continuity, and an earn-out more than an anonymous scrap sale.

## Product Wedge

For one narrowly defined product line with roughly 100–1,500 active machines, Afterline obtains a 120-day exclusive option covering:

- technical drawings, bills of material, firmware/source rights where applicable, manuals, service history, supplier files, jigs, moulds, fixtures, remaining spares, and the product-line/service mark;
- seller-approved communication to the installed base and assignment of transferable service contracts;
- a clean schedule of liabilities, IP ownership, safety-critical parts, and exclusions;
- a seller-financed closing formula triggered only by prepaid customer demand.

During the option, Afterline offers a prepaid continuity plan, an essential-spares inspection/order, and one high-value retrofit. It closes only when at least three unrelated customers prepay at least 25,000 PLN total and the first delivery shows at least 45% contribution margin.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

General business brokers sell whole companies, service contractors sell labour, and parts dealers sell what is already catalogued. The overlooked asset is the aftermarket position of a narrow installed base: customers, technical truth, brand permission, tooling, supply recipes, and service history together. Separately these assets look too small; assembled and seller-authorised, they create a captive route to customers who already own the machine and cannot switch service providers without replacement or reverse-engineering cost.

## Underused Momentum

The European Commission's June 2026 SME-transfer recommendation explicitly identifies the growing number of owners approaching retirement without a successor and calls for better transfer preparation and matchmaking. More than 60% of European firms are family businesses. At the same time, EU repair and ecodesign policy is increasing the strategic value of repairability, information, and spare-part continuity. These are timing signals, not proof of a specific acquisition.

## Beachhead Market

First target: one Polish owner-led OEM or product-line carveout in non-regulated, maintenance-critical food/packaging/woodworking/light-industrial machinery, with 100–1,500 traceable active machines, a founder approaching exit, repeat spare/service demand, no dominant global OEM support, and no safety case that requires an unowned certification or manufacturer authorisation.

The initial search stays within a two-hour field-service radius of Wielkopolska/Warsaw only for the first proof. The economic niche is the machine family, not the geography.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Hypothesised customer revenue per acquired line:

- continuity plan: 8,000–25,000 PLN per site/year for response assurance, documentation custody, annual review, and priority parts access;
- parts and wear kits: 45–70% gross margin;
- planned retrofits: 25–40% gross margin;
- emergency field service: priced to cover contractor cost but used as a conversion and intelligence channel, not the main profit pool.

A mature line with 300 active machines might produce 2.2–4.0 million PLN annual revenue: 40–70 plans, parts/orders from 20–35% of the base, and 10–20 retrofits. Target blended gross margin is 58–65%; operating margin after technicians, warranty reserve, and central overhead is 18–27%. Kill the line if delivered contribution is below 35%, plan renewal below 75%, or founder/technician hours scale close to revenue after month 12.

Seller consideration is hypothesised as 10–25% cash at close plus 20–35% of acquired-line gross profit for three to five years, capped at an agreed price. No whole-company acquisition is required in the POC.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10: the signed seller sends an Afterline continuity notice to the installed-base list during the option; Afterline follows with a written machine-specific risk review, three fixed offers, and a deadline before the line transfers. The first ten must come from the acquired base, not cold factory prospecting.

First 100: repeat the same owner-sourced channel across three to five product lines, add machine-plate SEO pages and used-machinery dealer/refurbisher referrals, and offer multi-machine plans to factories that own several acquired lines. Distribution compounds because every acquisition transfers an installed channel rather than requiring CAC from zero.

## Primary Control Point

Tier-1-style target control point: an executed exclusive option and then assignment/exclusive licence to the complete aftermarket position for a named product line—technical IP/files, tooling, spares, product/service mark, transferable contracts, service history, and seller-authorised installed-base communication. The option is not enough if any essential right or the customer route remains revocable.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Resource needed: one clean, assignable aftermarket position with an identifiable active installed base.

How to secure it:

1. Build a 100-target list from used-machine CE plates, trade-fair catalogues, KRS/CEIDG age and activity signals, dealer listings, succession advisers, and direct owner outreach.
2. Send a written offer: preserve the founder's name and customer continuity, no full-company sale, low upfront cash, auditable revenue share, and no close unless customers prepay.
3. Complete 10 owner calls, four NDAs/data rooms, two technical screens, and one 90–120 day option.
4. Pay 3,000–8,000 PLN for exclusivity; spend up to 12,000 PLN on legal/IP/contract review and up to 8,000 PLN on an engineer and field sampling.
5. Use only seller-authorised installed-base outreach. Require three independent prepayments totalling at least 25,000 PLN and a first fulfilled order with at least 45% contribution before closing.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: the offer monetises an asset that is often too small for private equity and too specialised for general brokers; it gives the owner a retirement annuity and continuity without demanding that the founder finance the whole company. This remains a hypothesis until an option is signed.

Proof before serious capital: executed option, rights schedule, installed-base evidence, seller co-signed outreach, three prepayments, one delivery, contribution calculation, and a signed closing formula.

## Distribution Advantage

The first channel is transferred, not invented: seller-authorised access to customers who already own the machines. Later acquisitions add new installed bases, search demand by model/part number, dealer referrals, and cross-sell between lines. The strongest copy risk is an industrial-service consolidator offering owners more cash; Afterline must win with small-deal speed, revenue-share alignment, standard rights diligence, and a portfolio that can cross-stock technicians and suppliers.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Dominate support for one narrow machine family by being the only authorised custodian of its technical files, tools, and continuity channel.
2. Acquire adjacent product lines sharing technicians, suppliers, customer plants, or parts-production methods.
3. Build a cross-line compatibility graph, demand forecast, documentation standard, and contractor dispatch system.
4. Expand from Poland into Czechia, Slovakia, and the Baltics through similar owner succession and lower-middle-market fragmentation.
5. Offer portfolio-level uptime plans and selective remanufacturing only after repeated demand supports inventory.

Broad service companies can hire technicians, but they cannot lawfully reproduce an assigned archive, service mark, tooling, installed-base history, and exclusive rights for the same line in one vendor contract.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers a dense manufacturing base, many founder-led SMEs created after 1989, lower field-service costs than Western Europe, and local-language owner/customer trust. The June 2026 EU transfer initiative may improve financing and matchmaking over time. The defence is not regulation; it is being early in small product-line carveouts that global consolidators ignore, then using CEE proximity and acquired rights to expand.

## Compounding Advantage

Each acquired line contributes four reusable assets: installed customers, technical files, procurement history, and cash flow. Centralised parts forecasting reduces inventory; common contractors improve utilisation; machine-plate pages reduce inbound CAC; prior seller references reduce acquisition friction; retained earnings finance the next option. Owner labour is replaced by documented processes, product managers, and a shared technician network.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

During employment, 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution in conservative and expected cases is retained for options, small closing payments, parts working capital, legal diligence, and documentation. Founder distributions are optional and capped at 5%. Strong-success reinvests 100%. After the full-time gate, distributions can increase only after maintaining warranty reserves, debt covenants, and two acquisition tranches of liquidity. Seller earn-outs align cash payment with realised gross profit and prevent the first 100,000 PLN from financing an unproven purchase.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–30: define the target scorecard; identify 100 OEM/product lines; send 40 written owner approaches; interview five maintenance buyers without selling.
- Days 31–60: obtain 10 owner conversations, four NDAs, and two redacted data rooms; hire a lawyer and machinery engineer only for finalists.
- Days 61–90: sign one exclusive option for no more than 8,000 PLN; complete the rights/liability schedule and installed-base sample.
- Days 91–135: seller sends the continuity notice; Afterline offers fixed prepaid plans, parts orders, and one retrofit.
- Days 136–180: require three independent customers and at least 25,000 PLN prepaid, deliver at least one order at 45%+ contribution, and sign the seller-financed closing formula.

This plan is compatible with employment because sourcing and data-room work are asynchronous; owner/customer calls are batched; engineering and field delivery are contracted. Maximum planned pre-proof spend is 34,000 PLN.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

1. **0–8,000 PLN:** target list, basic site, travel, and owner interviews. Stop if no two credible data-room invitations after 40 qualified approaches.
2. **Up to 34,000 PLN total before paid proof:** option fee, legal/IP review, engineer, insurance consultation, and first fulfilment preparation. No acquisition closing.
3. **Up to 65,000 PLN after paid proof:** only after three prepayments, first successful delivery, and 45%+ contribution; fund limited closing cash and parts.
4. **Up to 100,000 PLN founder capital:** only after five independent customers, repeat orders/renewal signal, and positive portfolio contribution.
5. **Family 100,000 PLN excluded:** available only after two acquired lines or one line with six months of repeat demand, positive contribution, clean rights, and a documented scaling case.

Maximum pre-proof loss: 34,000 PLN plus founder time. Contracts must prohibit uncapped inherited liabilities.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder remains employed until at least two acquired lines or equivalent contracted recurring demand produce: six independent paying customers, positive contribution after contractor and warranty costs, six months of repeat/renewal evidence, a repeatable owner-sourcing signal, and at least 12 months of personal runway. A single exciting acquisition does not satisfy the gate.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If the first option expires, the founder retains a target database, diligence checklist, rights schedule, price book, installed-base interview scripts, supplier quotes, and a documented rejection reason. Those assets can support a better target but are not treated as a moat. Any deposits are refunded if the closing condition is not met. No customer is promised support without the legal rights and engineer-approved delivery path.

## Critical Evidence

- June 2026 European Commission guidance identifies retirement without successors as a pressing SME-transfer challenge affecting potentially millions of firms.
- EU policy increasingly values repairability, spare parts, and continuity, though exact industrial scope must be checked per product.
- Poland has an active machinery and SME base and public company/restructuring data useful for sourcing.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Owners will accept an option plus capped gross-profit earn-out instead of a larger immediate purchase price.
- Aftermarket assets and customer communication can be cleanly assigned or exclusively licensed.
- Three installed-base customers will prepay before the transfer closes.
- Portfolio centralisation can sustain 18–27% operating margins without founder labour scaling one-for-one.

## Kill Criteria

- No two qualified owners open a data room after 40 targeted approaches, or no option after 100.
- Option requires more than 8,000 PLN before installed-base verification or more than 40,000 PLN total before customer prepayment.
- Essential IP, tooling title, service mark, contracts, or customer communication cannot be assigned/licensed cleanly.
- Fewer than three independent customers or less than 25,000 PLN prepaid by day 180.
- First fulfilment contribution below 45%, mature-line operating-margin path below 18%, or payback on closing cash above 30 months.
- More than 25% of expected revenue depends on safety-critical work that requires unowned certification or OEM authority.
- No credible path to 12+ lines, 50m+ PLN revenue, and retained founder ownership sufficient for €10m net worth after the first two acquisitions.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Founder: 15–25 hours/week for sourcing, analysis, automation, commercial design, and deal control.
- Transaction/IP lawyer: 8,000–12,000 PLN per first option/rights review.
- Contract machinery engineer: 5,000–8,000 PLN for technical diligence and first safety scope.
- Bookkeeper/tax adviser and insurance broker: 2,000–4,000 PLN.
- Field technicians and fabrication are contracted per order until recurring demand supports hires.
- Pre-proof budget ceiling: 34,000 PLN; 12-month founder-capital ceiling: 100,000 PLN.
