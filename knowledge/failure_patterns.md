# Historical failure patterns

This is a compact retrieval aid, not a blacklist and not a substitute for current research. Generate a fresh, diverse pool before reading it. Then use it to challenge novelty, surface repeated structural weaknesses, and choose what deserves deeper research. A new candidate may revisit a family only when it changes the broken control point or brings new primary evidence.

## Business-shape failures

1. **Useful artifact, no scarce control.** Packs, passports, checklists, scorecards, reference objects, and workflow tools often solved a real task but did not control payment, approval, inventory, rights, source custody, or another hard-to-route-around gate.

2. **The incumbent can bundle the wedge.** OEMs, distributors, accredited labs, platforms, integrators, agencies, and regulated workflow owners already held buyer trust, data, certification, or distribution and could internalize the proposed feature.

3. **Reachable pilot, unsupported wealth path.** Several candidates could plausibly win one paid experiment, but the conservative customer denominator, annual volume, price, gross margin, or founder ownership did not support the target outcome without heroic assumptions.

4. **Services disguised as compounding products.** Custom analysis, integration, fitting, field operations, physical handling, support, or bespoke research made the first sale plausible while limiting repeatability and margins. Manual-first is useful for proof only when the learning becomes a controlled asset or repeatable route.

5. **Trust or authority belongs to another party.** Ideas failed when they required a regulator, bank, distributor, QMS owner, seller, carrier, platform, clinician, insurer, or certification body to grant data, authority, recognition, or workflow access before value could be delivered.

6. **Regulation creates urgency but not defensibility.** Compliance deadlines and evidence requests opened a door, yet generic consulting, established software, law/accounting firms, or the buyer's internal team could still provide the result. The durable mechanism must be separate from the rule itself.

7. **Commodity hardware or reference material is easy to reproduce.** Sensor coupons, phantoms, fixtures, cartridges, connectors, accessories, and small appliances repeatedly lacked proprietary input, trusted calibration, protected design-in, or sufficient repeat consumption.

8. **Rights and acquisition economics are unverified.** Seller-dependent concepts looked attractive only while licence counts, renewal revenue, transferability, support burden, IP ownership, price, or financing remained undisclosed. Public research cannot replace a bounded seller-evidence packet.

9. **A partner gate is asserted rather than contracted.** A passport or evidence product becomes stronger when a distributor, payer, platform, or warranty channel recognizes it economically. Interest, praise, or a generic partnership does not establish that gate.

10. **The deliverable is reproducible by frontier AI.** Reports, research summaries, detectors, prompts, and document preparation need a non-prompt asset—such as controlled source custody, executable rights, routed transaction flow, capital at risk, or scarce capacity—to avoid rapid substitution.

## Process failures that are not market verdicts

- **Advisory scores were sometimes treated as progress signals.** They are diagnoses, not validation and not reusable evidence for revised wording.
- **Thresholds changed across eras.** A historical `APPROVE`, `CONFIRM`, or numeric score records only the original gate. The index preserves exact strings rather than translating them to the current threshold.
- **Audit mechanics consumed runs.** Some v2 runs stopped on telemetry, wrapper parsing, path admission, resource allocation, or auditor semantics before business evaluation. Do not infer that their candidates failed commercially.
- **Registered or partially materialized runs are incomplete.** The presence of agent returns is not proof that a cohort was admitted, frozen, evaluated, or confirmed.
- **A failed exact wording does not ban a market forever.** Revisit only with a materially different payer, control, economics, rights position, distribution route, or new primary evidence; record that difference explicitly.

## Fast post-generation challenge

For each promising fresh candidate, ask:

1. What scarce decision or economic flow does the company control today, and who can route around it?
2. Which incumbent already owns the buyer relationship, source data, authority, certification, or distribution?
3. Does a conservative bottom-up denominator support the outcome after margins, capital, time, and founder dilution?
4. What must a third party grant before the first paid proof, and can the founder obtain it within the stated limits?
5. What compounds after ten customers that an incumbent or a capable AI user cannot reproduce?
6. Which archived idea or failed family is nearest, and what structural difference makes this more than renamed repetition?
