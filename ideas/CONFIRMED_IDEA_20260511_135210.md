# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs** is a revenue-linked compliance evidence service for Polish lighting distributors and importers bidding into public-sector LED and lighting tenders.

Briefly, it works like this:

1. Monitor Polish public tenders from e-Zamowienia/BZP for LED, street-lighting, school, hospital, municipal, and facility lighting purchases.
2. Parse each tender for product requirements: wattage, lumens, CCT, CRI, IP/IK rating, warranty, energy label class, EPREL references, datasheets, declarations, and submission deadlines.
3. Match those requirements against a distributor's public catalog or SKU export plus EPREL product records.
4. Produce a bid-ready evidence pack: matching SKUs, EPREL IDs, label/PIS links, screenshots, spec-match matrix, missing evidence, supplier questions, and a blunt "can bid / cannot bid unless fixed" list.
5. Sell a first paid pack manually, then expand into recurring monitoring that alerts distributors when a new tender fits their catalog but their evidence is incomplete.

This is not legal advice or procurement representation. The product is an evidence-preparation and qualification workflow for commercial bidding teams.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Polish lighting distributors lose or avoid public tenders because matching tender specs to SKUs and EPREL evidence is slow, fragmented, and deadline-driven; a narrow evidence-pack service can prove demand manually and compound into a proprietary tender-spec-to-SKU-to-EPREL graph.

## Founder Fit

The founder can validate this part-time with data scraping, structured parsing, and sales by precise outbound instead of public personal branding. The idea fits a data-scientist profile: the early product is a repeatable data reconciliation workflow, not a capital-heavy operation. It also uses adjacent knowledge from EU energy regulation, procurement, and technical product evidence without requiring inventory, warehousing, certifications, or field installation crews.

The founder should not build a full platform first. The correct first version is a manual service backed by small scripts, spreadsheets, and repeatable templates.

## Customer And Pain

Initial customer:

- Polish lighting distributors, LED importers, and electrical wholesalers with 200-5,000 SKUs.
- They sell to installers, municipalities, schools, hospitals, facility managers, or contractors bidding public tenders.
- Their catalogs are messy: supplier names differ from EPREL registrants, private-label SKUs may not map cleanly, datasheets omit required evidence, and tender requirements arrive under deadline pressure.

Pain:

- Tender teams see opportunities but cannot quickly prove which SKUs satisfy requirements.
- Missing EPREL IDs, labels, product information sheets, or spec evidence can cause disqualification or force conservative no-bids.
- Generic tender-alert tools tell them that a tender exists, but not whether their catalog can win it.
- EPREL compliance services help register products, but they do not turn live Polish tenders into SKU-level bid packs.

## Product Wedge

The wedge is not "compliance monitoring" in general. The wedge is **one tender, one distributor catalog, one bid decision**.

First paid deliverable:

- A 24-72 hour GreenTender Bid Pack for one live or recent public lighting tender.
- Output: eligible SKU shortlist, rejected SKU list, evidence links, EPREL IDs, label/product-sheet evidence, spec-match matrix, missing proof, supplier questions, and recommended bid/no-bid decision.
- Price: 1,000-3,000 PLN per pack depending on SKU count and deadline.

Recurring add-on:

- 500-2,000 PLN/month for monitoring public tenders and sending only tenders that match the distributor's catalog with evidence gaps already identified.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

Most compliance products assume the customer cares about compliance as an abstract risk. Small and mid-sized distributors often do not. They care when a specific tender could generate revenue and a specific missing document could block the bid.

The hidden insight is that **EPREL evidence becomes urgent when it is attached to near-term tender revenue**. The sales conversation should therefore start from a live opportunity, not from a generic warning about EU obligations.

## Underused Momentum

The underused momentum is the combination of:

- EU energy labelling and EPREL infrastructure that already forces structured product evidence into the market.
- Public procurement pressure to consider energy efficiency.
- Publicly visible Polish tender data.
- Distributor catalogs that are already online or exportable but poorly connected to regulatory evidence.
- Municipal and public-sector demand for lower-energy lighting, especially where buyers want evidence without becoming product-data experts.

The commercial capture layer is missing: a tool that turns public tenders plus EPREL/product evidence into a practical bid pack.

## Beachhead Market

Beachhead:

- Polish LED and lighting distributors/importers selling into public-sector tenders.
- Focus on firms too small to have mature internal tender-data infrastructure but large enough to care about recurring tenders.
- Best first targets have public catalogs, named brands/private labels, and visible tender activity or contractor relationships.

Avoid at first:

- Tiny Allegro-only sellers.
- Very large electrical groups with established tender departments.
- Broad construction tender categories where the product evidence problem is less repeatable.

### Cold Start & Resource Bootstrapping 

Initial user acquisition mechanism:

1. Build a list of 40 Polish lighting distributors/importers from public catalogs, trade directories, Google results, Allegro/marketplace brand presence, and tender participation clues.
2. Pull 30 live or recently closed lighting tenders from e-Zamowienia/BZP and public procurement notices.
3. For each target, manually identify 1-3 tenders where their catalog appears plausibly relevant.
4. Send an evidence-led outbound note: "This tender appears close to your SKU range, but these specific evidence gaps may block or slow the bid."
5. Offer a paid 24-72 hour bid pack for the specific tender, not a demo of software.

Foundational resources required for immediate operation:

- Tender source: e-Zamowienia/BZP public tender data and manual portal search.
- Product source: distributor catalog pages, PDFs, XLS/CSV exports, and supplier datasheets.
- Regulatory evidence: EPREL public database, energy labels, product information sheets, and product identifiers.
- Expertise: one lighting-spec reviewer for edge cases and one procurement/compliance reviewer for evidence format. These can be freelance advisors paid per reviewed pack after the first sales calls show demand.
- Templates: reusable spec-match matrix, evidence checklist, supplier question email, and bid/no-bid summary.

No inventory, certification lab, installer network, or large capital purchase is needed before validation.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture 

Manual first pack:

- 2-4 hours of founder work plus optional paid expert review.
- Sell for 1,000-3,000 PLN when tied to a live tender deadline.
- Gross margin should be high once templates exist, because the main cost is structured research and evidence reconciliation.

Repeat pack:

- 1-2 hours after the distributor catalog is normalized.
- Recurring monitoring can be sold for 500-2,000 PLN/month per distributor.

Value capture:

- The fee is small relative to even modest public lighting tenders, where avoiding a no-bid or disqualification can justify a few thousand PLN.
- Pricing should stay tied to tender value, urgency, SKU count, and evidence complexity.

### Distribution Moat 

Distribution starts from proof, not persuasion. Each outbound message contains a specific tender, target SKUs or SKU families, and evidence gaps. That makes the message materially different from generic consulting or SaaS outreach.

The distribution moat compounds through:

- Better target lists of distributors that actually bid public tenders.
- Reusable tender language patterns.
- Supplier response history.
- Catalog normalization for each distributor.
- Trust from being the fastest way to decide whether a tender is worth bidding.

## Monopoly Path

The narrow monopoly path is to own the Polish lighting tender evidence graph:

- Tender requirement language.
- Equivalent spec phrases used by Polish public buyers.
- Distributor SKU aliases and private-label mappings.
- EPREL IDs and supplier registrant names.
- Missing evidence patterns by supplier and product family.
- Win/loss and bid/no-bid outcomes.

Over time, this becomes hard for a generic tender-alert tool, EPREL registration consultant, or ecommerce plugin to copy because the value sits in repeated messy mappings and customer-specific bid workflows, not in one public database lookup.

Expansion path:

1. Public lighting tenders in Poland.
2. Electrical wholesalers and contractors who need evidence from multiple distributors.
3. Other EPREL-relevant public procurement categories: HVAC, pumps, appliances, refrigeration, and possibly tyres where energy labels matter.
4. Supplier-side "tender readiness" reports showing which products are missing evidence for recurring public-sector specs.

## Local Adaptation Defense

This is not a simple foreign-market copy. The defense is local execution:

- Polish tender language and procurement conventions.
- Polish public procurement data sources.
- Local distributor catalogs, aliases, PDFs, and supplier relationships.
- Polish-language bid evidence summaries and supplier questions.
- Familiarity with what Polish buyers actually request in lighting tenders.

Global EPREL tools can show labels or help register products, and generic tender tools can alert on opportunities. They do not appear to solve the local operational problem of mapping a Polish tender to a distributor's actual SKUs and producing bid evidence under deadline.

## Why This Could Compound

The first packs are service-heavy, but each one builds reusable assets:

- A normalized catalog for a distributor.
- A library of tender requirement patterns.
- A map of supplier/brand/EPREL naming mismatches.
- Evidence templates buyers and tender teams trust.
- A record of which tenders were worth bidding and which evidence gaps mattered.

That lets the niche expand outward better than broad competitors can move inward. A broad procurement platform starts with tender alerts. GreenTender starts with the painful last mile: "Can my SKU credibly satisfy this tender today?"

## Validation Plan

30-day validation:

1. Collect 30 live or recently closed Polish public lighting tenders.
2. Build a list of 40 target distributors/importers.
3. For each target, prepare a short custom observation with one tender and one likely catalog/evidence issue.
4. Send direct email and LinkedIn outreach to owners, sales directors, tender specialists, or product managers.
5. Offer a paid bid pack, not a free audit. Free sample can be limited to one SKU and one evidence gap.
6. Track replies, calls, paid packs, time per pack, repeatability of evidence checks, and whether targets already solve this internally.

Demand is easy to verify because the outreach can point to public tenders and public catalog evidence. The founder does not need to ask hypothetical survey questions.

## Kill Criteria

Strict kill condition:

- Kill the idea if fewer than 5 of 40 targets agree to a meeting, request a sample, or ask for SKU review within 30 days.
- Also kill if fewer than 2 targets pay for a bid pack or commit to pay for the next live tender within 30 days.
- Do not build software if the first 10 serious opportunities do not produce reusable tender-spec/SKU/EPREL patterns and instead require bespoke expert work each time.

## Budget And Time

Budget:

- 0-1,000 PLN for initial validation using manual research, spreadsheets, email, and public sources.
- 1,000-3,000 PLN optional expert review budget only after serious customer interest.
- No paid platform build, inventory, certification process, or agency spend before the kill gates are passed.

Time:

- 10-15 hours to build the tender and distributor target list.
- 10-15 hours for first outbound and sample observations.
- 5-10 hours per first paid pack until templates stabilize.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

Week 1:

- Create a spreadsheet of 30 public lighting tenders and 40 distributor/importer targets.
- Define the bid-pack template and evidence checklist.
- Manually complete 3 sample mini-packs from public data.

Week 2:

- Send 40 evidence-led outbound messages.
- Offer paid 24-72 hour packs.
- Interview responders about their current tender evidence workflow and failure cases.

Week 3:

- Deliver first paid or pilot packs.
- Time every step.
- Identify which checks are repeatable and which require expert judgment.

Week 4:

- Try to convert responders into recurring tender monitoring.
- Decide whether to continue based on the kill criteria.
- If continuing, build only the smallest scripts needed for tender parsing, SKU extraction, and EPREL evidence lookup.
