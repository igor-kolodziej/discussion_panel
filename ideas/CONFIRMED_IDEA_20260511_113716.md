# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**PromoLeak Recovery Desk** is a Polish B2B revenue-recovery sprint for challenger health, beauty, supplement, household, and FMCG brands that sell through drugstores, marketplaces, distributors, or modern trade.

The first product is not SaaS and not a dashboard. It is a manual 14-day audit that ingests invoices, retailer remittances, credit notes, promotion agreements, price lists, sell-out files, and retail-media charges to find invalid, duplicated, wrong-window, poorly documented, or commercially unprofitable deductions.

The wedge is recovered or avoided money. Pricing starts as either 15-25% of recovered cash or a 4,000-8,000 PLN fixed audit fee.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Polish challenger FMCG and beauty brands are winning distribution but quietly losing margin through retailer deductions and promotion mechanics that KSeF and enterprise TPM systems do not reconcile for them.

## Founder Fit

- The founder works in FMCG and understands commercial analytics, messy promotion economics, margin pressure, and retailer complexity better than a generic automation founder.
- The work fits a data scientist: document parsing, reconciliation, anomaly detection, deduction taxonomy, causal thinking around promotions, and repeatable analytics.
- It can start part-time, mostly asynchronously, with document review, written outreach, and structured audit delivery.
- It avoids personal-brand content, camera dependency, heavy networking, and large upfront capital.
- It can validate before build: either brands share real commercial documents and leakage is found, or the idea dies quickly.

## Customer And Pain

First customer:

- Polish challenger health, beauty, supplement, household, and FMCG brands with roughly 5-50M PLN revenue.
- They sell through Rossmann, Hebe, Super-Pharm, Allegro, marketplaces, distributors, or modern trade.
- They are too small for heavy enterprise trade-promotion systems but large enough for deductions and promotion leakage to matter.

Pain:

- Retailers, distributors, and marketplaces deduct money through credit notes, remittances, campaign fees, retail-media charges, listing mechanics, rebates, and promotion settlements.
- Founders and finance teams often track gross sales and distribution wins, but do not know whether each retailer relationship is actually profitable after deductions.
- Invalid or duplicated deductions are easy to ignore because recovering them requires matching contracts, invoices, promo windows, sell-out files, and remittance evidence.
- Enterprise TPM tools exist, but they are built for larger organizations and implementation cycles, not small challenger brands needing cash recovery now.

## Product Wedge

Start with a paid recovery sprint:

1. Brand signs a light NDA and shares a narrow document set for one or two retailers or marketplace channels.
2. PromoLeak normalizes invoices, credit notes, remittances, promotion agreements, price lists, sell-out files, and retail-media charges.
3. The audit identifies recoverable claims, avoidable leakage, duplicated charges, wrong-window deductions, unsupported charges, and promotion mechanics that destroy contribution margin.
4. Output is a leakage register, supporting evidence links, dispute drafts, and renegotiation notes.
5. Pricing is success-fee first where possible: 15-25% of recovered cash, or 4,000-8,000 PLN fixed fee for a 14-day audit.

This is deliberately not a reporting product. If the audit cannot find money or change future commercial decisions, it is not worth building.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

The obvious story is that KSeF solves invoice standardization and that trade promotion software solves promotion management.

The hidden issue is different: challenger brands do not mainly need prettier reporting. They need someone to connect the commercial truth across documents that live in separate systems: invoices, credit notes, remittances, price lists, promotion agreements, marketplace files, retailer-media charges, and sell-out reports.

For small brands, the real competitor is not only software. It is resignation: "the retailer deducted it, so we accept it." That resignation can hide tens of thousands of PLN per customer relationship.

## Underused Momentum

- KSeF 2.0 makes Polish invoice access and invoice structure more systematic in 2026, but it does not reconcile deductions, remittances, promotion agreements, and retailer-media charges.
- Retail media is becoming a separate, fast-growing budget category in Poland, adding more retailer-side charges and campaign mechanics for brands to reconcile.
- Challenger beauty, supplement, and FMCG brands increasingly sell through hybrid channels: drugstores, marketplaces, distributors, and ecommerce. That creates more margin leakage points.
- AI document extraction is now good enough to make a manual audit faster, but the defensible asset is the retailer-specific recovery logic and benchmark data, not a generic AI wrapper.

## Beachhead Market

The first beachhead is 5-50M PLN Polish health, beauty, supplement, household, and FMCG challenger brands that recently expanded from DTC/ecommerce into drugstores, marketplaces, distributors, or modern trade.

Why this beachhead:

- They feel cash pressure and margin pressure.
- They have enough document volume to hide leakage.
- They usually lack enterprise-grade revenue-growth-management teams.
- They are reachable through founder, CFO, head of sales, marketplace manager, or finance lead.
- They can say yes to a narrow audit without a large procurement cycle.

## Monopoly Path

The monopoly path is a retailer-specific commercial-recovery graph:

- Deduction taxonomy by retailer, marketplace, distributor, mechanic, and document type.
- Evidence patterns that distinguish valid from invalid or contestable deductions.
- Dispute drafts and escalation playbooks that improve with every audit.
- Benchmarks for promotion mechanics, retail-media charges, rebate structures, and channel profitability.
- Eventually, lightweight software that automates only the repeated reconciliation work after manual recovery has proven demand.

If enough audits are completed in one narrow category, PromoLeak becomes the preferred local reference point for "what this retailer usually deducts, what can be challenged, and which mechanics destroy margin."

## Local Adaptation Defense

This is not a foreign SaaS copy. The local defense comes from:

- Polish KSeF implementation details and Polish invoice formats.
- Polish retailer, marketplace, distributor, and drugstore deduction behavior.
- Polish-language contracts, remittances, credit notes, and commercial email trails.
- Local norms around challenging deductions without damaging retailer relationships.
- A beachhead in Polish challenger brands below the size where SAP, Comarch TPM, or enterprise revenue-growth-management tools are realistic.

Large foreign tools can manage trade promotion workflows, but they are unlikely to start with small Polish brands, local deduction recovery, and success-fee/manual document audits.

## Why This Could Compound

- Every audit builds reusable extraction templates, deduction categories, dispute logic, and retailer-specific benchmarks.
- The first cash-recovery cases create proof that is much stronger than generic analytics claims.
- The service can expand from recovery to prevention: pre-promo margin checks, retailer scorecards, retail-media ROI sanity checks, and channel profitability reviews.
- Over time, the proprietary asset becomes a local commercial dataset: which retailers, mechanics, and document patterns cause leakage for brands at different stages.
- The founder can use AI leverage heavily while keeping the customer promise simple: recovered or avoided money.

## Validation Plan

1. Build a target list of 30 Polish challenger brands in beauty, supplements, household, and FMCG with visible drugstore, marketplace, distributor, or modern-trade exposure.
2. Send direct founder/CFO/head-of-sales outreach with a sharp offer: "I will audit one retailer/channel and show recoverable or preventable leakage, or you should not pay for ongoing work."
3. Target at least 5 serious discovery calls.
4. Convert at least 2 brands into real document-access pilots under NDA or paid audit terms.
5. For each pilot, request only one narrow channel first: invoices, credit notes, remittances, promotion agreement, price list, retail-media charges, and sell-out files for a fixed period.
6. Deliver a leakage register, evidence links, dispute drafts, and promotion mechanics to stop or renegotiate.
7. Measure only hard outcomes: recoverable PLN, avoidable future PLN, accepted disputes, changed promotion terms, paid audit conversion, and willingness to expand to another retailer.

## Kill Criteria

Strict kill condition from independent confirmation:

Kill if, after contacting 30 qualified Polish challenger brands and securing at least 5 serious discovery calls, you cannot get 2 brands to share real commercial documents under NDA or pay for an audit; or if the first 2 document-level audits fail to identify at least 20k PLN per brand in recoverable or preventable leakage.

Additional kill signals:

- Brands say the pain exists but refuse to share documents even under NDA.
- The work collapses into generic finance cleanup with no retailer-specific recovery advantage.
- Most deductions are valid and uncontestable, and the only value is reporting.
- Enterprise tools or accounting firms already solve this cheaply for the same size of customer.
- The founder cannot access enough category-specific cases to build a compounding deduction taxonomy.

## Budget And Time

- First 30 days: 0-3,000 PLN for domain, simple one-page site, outreach tooling, NDA template review, and basic document-processing tools.
- Optional: up to 5,000 PLN if legal/accounting review is needed for positioning, but do not present legal advice.
- Do not spend the full 30,000 PLN validation capital before paid audits or document-access pilots.
- No custom SaaS build before at least 5 completed audits and repeated evidence of recoverable leakage.
- Weekly time: 6-10 focused hours, mostly outreach, document review, reconciliation, and audit delivery.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

Week 1:

- Define the exact audit scope: one brand, one retailer/channel, 6-12 months of documents.
- Create the target list of 30 brands and identify founder/CFO/head-of-sales contacts.
- Draft the outreach message, NDA, data request list, and audit promise.

Week 2:

- Contact the first 15 brands.
- Run discovery calls focused on deductions, credit notes, promo settlements, retail-media charges, and channel profitability.
- Ask directly for a paid or NDA-backed document-access pilot.

Week 3:

- Contact the next 15 brands.
- Start the first audit if access is granted.
- Build a simple reconciliation model for invoices, remittances, credit notes, promo windows, and price lists.

Week 4:

- Deliver the first leakage register and dispute drafts.
- Ask for success-fee recovery, fixed audit payment, or expanded second-channel audit.
- Decide whether the strict kill condition is trending pass or fail.

## Non-Redundancy Check

Deduplication map of existing confirmed ideas:

| Existing idea | Customer | Wedge | Market/domain | Validation gate | Kill criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distributor-backed Heat-Pump Commissioning Passport | Heat-pump distributors, wholesalers, manufacturer reps, warranty departments | Commissioning passport tied to warranty/preferred-status economics | Polish heat-pump retrofits and renewable-energy installation quality | Written 10-job distributor pilot tied to an economic gate | Kill if no distributor gate, installers ignore it, or scoring does not predict callbacks/warranty issues |
| BatteryFit Retrofit Pack | Small Polish PV installers | Customer-facing retrofit qualification packet | PV batteries, EMS, heat storage, net-billing, subsidies, OSD paperwork | 3 paid installer pilots after 20 outreach attempts | Kill if installers already trust existing tools or fewer than 3 pay/commit |

Why PromoLeak is not a duplicate:

- Different customer: challenger FMCG/beauty/supplement brand founders, CFOs, sales leads, or finance teams, not renewable-energy installers or distributors.
- Different domain: FMCG trade deductions, retail-media charges, marketplaces, drugstores, and commercial finance, not PV, heat pumps, batteries, EMS, subsidies, or OSD workflows.
- Different wedge: recovered/avoided cash from retailer deductions, not a passport, packet, checklist, installation qualification, or external warranty/status gate.
- Different proof artifact: leakage register and dispute/renegotiation evidence from real commercial documents, not commissioning evidence or retrofit recommendation packets.
- Different economic gate: paid audit or success fee based on recovered/prevented leakage, not distributor recognition or installer sales confidence.

Rejected or deferred candidates in the independent generation pass:

- LabelClaim Radar: too close to a compliance-wrapper unless paired with legal authority or launch workflow ownership.
- Retail Media Incrementality Club: interesting later module, but weak as a first business because it needs pooled data before value is obvious.
- Wearable Recovery Cohort, PIT-38 Investor Loss Harvester, and LabTrend Second Opinion: plausible but weaker immediate paid B2B wedge and weaker founder unfair access than FMCG revenue recovery.
