# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack** is a Polish/CEE productized evidence-response service for SaaS and software-product vendors whose regulated-finance renewals or procurements are blocked by live DORA/ICT third-party-risk evidence requests.

The first version is a manual, 7-business-day renewal rescue product. A 20-150 person vendor with 2-10 regulated financial customers submits its live bank, insurer, payment institution, leasing group, or fintech questionnaire plus existing evidence folders. The service returns a buyer-ready Polish/English response pack that organizes what the vendor can safely answer now, what needs technical/security remediation, what needs legal review, what needs management approval, and what should not be answered without advisor input.

This is not DORA certification, legal advice, contract negotiation, ISO/SOC implementation, a formal security audit, or generic DORA readiness consulting.

## One-Sentence Thesis

DORA has pushed ICT supplier evidence pressure down from regulated financial entities to smaller software vendors, and the urgent wedge is not another bank-side GRC platform but a fast, vendor-side renewal evidence pack for live regulated-finance procurement blockers.

## Founder Fit

- The founder can start part-time with written B2B outreach, structured document analysis, regulatory source mapping, and AI-assisted evidence organization.
- The work fits a Warsaw data scientist with finance, analytics, and technical evidence strengths.
- It avoids camera content, influencer dependence, inventory, field operations, and large upfront capital.
- It uses the founder's Polish/EU context without relying on the already-used renewable-energy family edge.
- It can validate before software: either vendors pay for live renewal unblock, or the idea dies quickly.

## Customer And Pain

Primary customer:

- Polish/CEE KYC/AML, fraud/risk/scoring, payments infrastructure, fintech infrastructure, leasing/claims workflow, regulated document workflow, and analytics software vendors.
- Company size: roughly 20-150 employees.
- Existing customer base: 2-10 regulated financial customers.
- Trigger: a live bank, insurer, payment institution, leasing group, or fintech procurement/renewal process is blocked by a DORA/ICT third-party-risk questionnaire, Article 30 contract-gap request, subcontractor request, audit-rights request, exit-plan request, or security/procurement evidence checklist.

Pain:

- The vendor is not necessarily directly supervised like a bank, but its regulated customers must manage ICT third-party risk and ask vendors for evidence.
- The vendor's CTO, COO, security lead, or founder has scattered evidence across contracts, policies, cloud screenshots, Jira/Confluence, subprocessor lists, ISO/SOC artifacts if any, BCP/DR docs, pen-test summaries, SLAs, and incident processes.
- The commercial risk is concrete: a renewal, procurement approval, or strategic financial-sector logo can stall because the vendor cannot answer in buyer-grade language fast enough.
- Generic GRC platforms, Big Four work, cyber audits, and law-firm advice are often too broad, slow, or expensive for the immediate blocker.

## Product Wedge

The first paid product is a fixed-scope **Bank Renewal Evidence Pack for DORA/ICT Supplier Reviews**.

Inputs:

- Live buyer questionnaire, renewal checklist, contract-gap request, security checklist, audit-rights request, subcontractor request, or exit-plan request.
- Existing vendor evidence folders: policies, contracts, DPA/MSA/SLA, BCP/DR docs, architecture notes, cloud/security artifacts, subprocessor records, incident response process, access-control/SDLC/vulnerability materials, and prior buyer responses.

Outputs:

- Buyer-ready evidence index.
- Live questionnaire answer pack.
- DORA Article 28/30 control and clause-topic matrix.
- ICT service description.
- Data-flow and service-country table.
- Subcontractor/subprocessor register.
- SLA and monitoring summary.
- BCP/DR and RTO/RPO summary.
- Incident notification evidence.
- Access-control, SDLC, and vulnerability evidence summary.
- Exit, data-return, and deletion evidence.
- "Changed since last review" memo when prior review exists.
- Executive memo for procurement/security.
- Gap split: answerable now, technical/security remediation needed, legal review needed, management-approved statement needed, unsafe-to-answer without advisor approval.

Review boundary:

- Cyber/security reviewer performs control-evidence issue spotting on flagged sections.
- Legal reviewer performs clause-topic issue spotting on flagged sections when needed.
- Final legal and security decisions remain with the vendor and its advisors.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

Most DORA vendors, consultants, and platforms sell to regulated financial institutions or promise broad compliance readiness. The overlooked buyer is the smaller ICT vendor that experiences DORA as a sales and renewal bottleneck, not as an abstract regulatory program.

The hidden insight is that the bank-side TPRM system creates the burden, but it does not help the vendor answer. The vendor's willingness to pay comes from commercial unblock: preserving a 100k+ PLN renewal, strategic logo, or procurement process without derailing its CTO/security team for weeks.

## Underused Momentum

- DORA has applied from 17 January 2025, creating active ICT third-party-risk pressure across EU financial entities.
- Financial entities remain responsible for ICT third-party risk, contractual arrangements, registers of information, subcontractor oversight, audit/access rights, incident support, and exit planning.
- Polish and EU supervisory materials have turned DORA from future regulation into operational procurement work.
- Bank, insurer, payment, leasing, and fintech procurement teams are still standardizing DORA supplier evidence requests, leaving smaller vendors underprepared.
- The Polish fintech/software-finance ecosystem is large enough for targeted outbound; Cashless reported 383 firms on the Polish Fintech Map 2025.

## Beachhead Market

The first market is not all ICT suppliers and not all software houses. It is Polish/CEE software-product vendors with recurring regulated-finance contracts and a live evidence blocker.

Qualification rules:

- 20-150 employees.
- 2-10 regulated financial customers.
- Active renewal/procurement request already received.
- Renewal/procurement value above 100k PLN or strategically important logo.
- Permission to share documents under NDA.
- CTO, COO, security owner, or founder available for 2-3 working sessions within 7 business days.
- Exclude generic custom software houses unless they have productized recurring financial-sector contracts.

### Cold Start & Resource Bootstrapping 

Initial acquisition:

- Build a 40-company target list from public bank/insurer/fintech case studies, FinTech Poland and CEE fintech maps, Cashless fintech categories, vendor websites, LinkedIn, procurement case studies, and Polish/CEE regtech, payments, KYC/AML, fraud, risk, leasing, claims, and document-workflow vendor pages.
- Send precise written outbound only to companies likely to have regulated financial customers:

```text
Your bank/insurer renewal may now be asking for DORA/ICT supplier evidence.
If you have a live questionnaire, Article 30 request, subcontractor request, audit-rights request, or exit-plan request, I turn your existing evidence folders into a buyer-ready Polish/English response pack in 7 business days.
Fixed 12k-24k PLN. No DORA certification claims, no legal opinion, no generic readiness report.
```

- Use partner-led distribution in parallel: boutique DORA/cyber/legal/ISO/SOC/fractional-CISO firms that keep high-value advisory, remediation, audit, and legal work while referring urgent evidence collation and questionnaire-response overflow.

Foundational resources before first paid delivery:

- Official DORA/KNF/EBA/EUR-Lex source library.
- Article 28/30 and register-template field map.
- Redacted sample pack.
- Secure document-room and evidence-handling process.
- One cyber/security reviewer on paid per-review terms.
- One legal reviewer on paid per-review terms.
- Three interviews with security/compliance people who have seen financial-sector ICT supplier requests.
- First pilots use real vendor questionnaires and evidence folders, not synthetic templates.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture 

Initial pricing:

- First response pack: 12k-24k PLN fixed fee, depending on complexity and urgency.
- Annual renewal refresh: 6k-12k PLN.
- No free pilots.

Cost structure:

- Founder time: evidence intake, mapping, answer drafting, coordination, and final pack assembly.
- Cyber reviewer: paid only for flagged control-evidence sections.
- Legal reviewer: paid only for flagged clause-topic sections.
- Secure document handling: low-cost tooling at validation stage.

Value capture logic:

- A 12k-24k PLN fee is credible only when a 100k+ PLN renewal, procurement, or strategic logo is at risk.
- The offer should be sold as renewal unblock, not compliance improvement.
- The pack also creates a reusable annual evidence snapshot, giving a reason for recurring refresh revenue.

### Distribution Moat 

The first distribution advantage is timing and specificity:

- Outbound is aimed only at vendors likely to have regulated-finance customers.
- Partners refer overflow evidence work they do not want senior staff to handle.
- The service qualifies only live questionnaires and commercial blockers.

The longer-term moat is not DORA knowledge. It is a structured operating dataset:

- Question wording variants by buyer type.
- Evidence object required.
- Answer structure used.
- Reviewer flags.
- Clause topic.
- Buyer pushback.
- Accepted final response.
- Renewal stage and time-to-response.
- Changed-since-last-year delta.

## Monopoly Path

Start as the fastest trusted vendor-side response pack for live DORA bank-renewal evidence blockers in Poland/CEE.

Then compound into:

- Bank-questionnaire pattern library.
- Article 28/30 evidence taxonomy.
- Accepted-answer library.
- Clause-gap map.
- Buyer-pushback record.
- Versioned evidence-room snapshots.
- Annual renewal-change workflow.
- Partner referral network with DORA/cyber/legal/ISO/SOC/fractional-CISO firms.

If the first wedge works, expansion can move into adjacent regulated-buyer evidence packs: NIS2 supplier evidence, AI Act procurement evidence for regulated workflows, and broader financial-sector ICT supplier evidence refresh. Do not expand to bank-side TPRM tooling until vendor-side evidence gravity is proven.

## Local Adaptation Defense

The local defense is real but execution-dependent:

- Polish/CEE vendors need Polish/English evidence wording and local procurement nuance.
- KNF and Polish financial-sector buyer expectations matter in practice, even though DORA is EU-wide.
- Local bank, insurer, payment, leasing, and fintech questionnaires will create patterns that generic global GRC tools do not initially own.
- Local boutique partner relationships can create warm referral channels faster than foreign software platforms can.
- The product is tied to live buyer requests, not a generic foreign-market copy.

Foreign platforms can still enter, so the defense must be built from accepted local answers, buyer pushback history, and recurring evidence refreshes, not from templates.

## Why This Could Compound

- Every paid engagement improves the question-to-evidence map.
- Every renewal creates a "what changed since last review" workflow.
- Every partner referral teaches which evidence issues become advisory, remediation, legal, or security work.
- Every accepted response improves future answer structure.
- Every buyer pushback improves the gap taxonomy.
- Annual refreshes can turn one-off rescue work into recurring evidence infrastructure.

The compounding niche is regulated-finance supplier evidence memory for Polish/CEE vendors.

## Validation Plan

Validation sprint:

1. Build a 40-company target list of Polish/CEE vendors with public bank, insurer, payment, leasing, or fintech customers.
2. Build a 10-part source and artifact library: DORA rules, KNF guidance, register templates, Article 28/30 map, sample subprocessor table, evidence index, issue-split template, executive memo, secure intake process, and NDA scope.
3. Secure paid per-review availability from one cyber/security reviewer and one legal reviewer.
4. Interview 3 security/compliance people who have seen financial-sector supplier evidence requests.
5. Send targeted outbound and partner emails.
6. Accept only live questionnaire/renewal/procurement cases.
7. Close 3 paid pilots at 12k+ PLN.
8. Track whether at least 2 pilots involve urgent deadlines and whether at least one partner source refers a case.
9. Capture anonymized patterns from every engagement.

Pass criteria:

- 3 paid pilots from 40 targeted vendors.
- At least 2 pilots tied to live urgent renewal/procurement deadlines.
- At least one repeatable partner referral source.
- Vendors pay 12k+ PLN without free pilots or months of trust-building.
- At least one buyer-side follow-up shows the pack reduced friction or clarified next steps.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or pivot if any of these happen:

- Fewer than 3 paid pilots after 40 highly targeted vendor contacts and partner outreach.
- Buyers want free template help but will not pay 12k+ PLN for live renewal unblock.
- Most interested companies do not have live questionnaires, only vague DORA curiosity.
- Vendors refuse to share enough evidence under NDA to produce a useful pack.
- Cyber/legal reviewer credibility cannot be secured before outbound.
- Work repeatedly drifts into legal advice, contract negotiation, full DORA readiness, ISO/SOC implementation, or formal audit.
- Delivery cannot be kept to 7 business days for a focused pack.
- No reusable question/evidence patterns emerge from the first 3 engagements.

- It is not BatteryFit, the heat-pump commissioning passport, or HeatQuiet: there is no energy installation, PV/battery, heat-pump quality, acoustic dispute, installer, OSD, or distributor warranty workflow.
- It is not PromoLeak: there is no FMCG trade deduction, retail-media charge, or recovery audit.
- It is not CBAM Import Continuity: there is no customs broker, importer, CN/TARIC classification, supplier emissions evidence, or customs-release continuity memo.
- It is not Data Act Machine Data Liberation: there is no industrial telemetry, compressed-air system, OEM/controller export, or energy-savings memo.
- It is not GreenTender EPREL: there is no public lighting tender, EPREL label matching, or bid evidence pack.
- It is not Supplement Stack Safety: there is no consumer supplement/lab-response safety workflow.
- It is not BioSignal Claim-Risk: there is no investor diligence memo for healthtech claims.
- It is not REDBlocked Amazon Connected-Device Evidence Rescue: there is no marketplace ASIN suppression, baby monitor/camera compliance response, or Amazon account-health agency channel.
- The buyer, trigger, proof artifact, economic gate, resource needs, and compounding data asset are different: DORA-driven financial-sector renewal evidence for ICT vendors.

Candidate lanes considered and rejected/deferred:

- KSeF API failure-drill pack for vertical SaaS/ERP vendors: stronger immediate urgency, but likely shorter window and more QA-like positioning.
- NIS2 supplier cyber evidence pack: large market but crowded, credibility-heavy, and cyber-consultancy shaped.

- Polish deposit-return SKU readiness: FMCG fit but the launch wave has mostly passed.
- WEEE/Battery EPR marketplace proof pack: too fragmented and too compliance-bureau shaped.

This idea is approved only for validation, not for building a platform, hiring a team, or spending serious capital.
