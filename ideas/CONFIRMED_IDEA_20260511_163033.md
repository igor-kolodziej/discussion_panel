# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack** is an investor-paid, fixed-format diligence memo for EU/CEE healthtech investors, accelerators, venture studios, and scout programs evaluating startups that make wearable, EEG, sleep, stress, fatigue, recovery, longevity, remote-monitoring, or digital-biomarker claims.

The first product is manual and document-based. It does not build a wearable app, recruit cohorts, run a clinical trial, certify efficacy, provide legal advice, or collect raw individual health data. It turns available evidence into an IC-ready claim-risk memo that helps a capital allocator decide whether a biosignal claim is plausible, unsupported, overclaimed, confounded, privacy/regulatory risky, or missing specific next evidence.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Early healthtech capital allocators need a cheap, repeatable way to separate plausible biosignal claims from quantified-self theater before clinical proof exists, and the right first product is a paid decision memo used inside IC or accelerator selection workflows.

## Founder Fit

- The founder has neuroscience, data science, and business-analysis training, plus direct interest in EEG, wearables, proactive health, and VC-style evaluation.
- The work can start part-time through written outreach, public teardowns, data-room review, literature review, structured templates, and manual analysis.
- It fits an introverted founder because the sales motion can be targeted private outreach to investors and accelerators, not public influencer content or constant conference networking.
- It uses AI leverage for evidence extraction, literature mapping, cohort/data-quality checks, and memo assembly, but the value is judgment and repeatable methodology, not a generic AI wrapper.
- It keeps capital risk low: no app, API integration, cohort recruitment, clinical study, or platform build before paid demand.

## Customer And Pain

Primary buyer:

- EU/CEE and EU-adjacent healthtech accelerators.
- Venture studios with health/wellness/digital-health dealflow.
- Seed and pre-seed funds evaluating wearable, EEG, recovery, sleep, stress, fatigue, longevity, or digital-biomarker startups.
- Scout programs that need a quick technical-commercial view before escalating a deal.

Pain:

- Early-stage startups often claim their wearable, EEG, HRV, sleep-stage, stress, fatigue, or recovery signal is meaningful before they have clinical-grade proof.
- Investors have to decide anyway, often using informal expert calls, friendly clinicians, advisor opinions, or pattern matching.
- False positives are expensive: a weak biosignal thesis can hide in a polished deck until after a seed check, accelerator slot, or venture-studio build decision.
- Generic market intelligence tools do not answer the narrow question: "Is this biosignal claim credible enough for this investment or selection decision?"
- Full CRO work, regulatory consulting, ORCHA-style assessment, or formal clinical validation is too heavy for many seed-stage decisions.

## Product Wedge

The first deliverable is a **BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack** for one live deal or selection candidate.

Inputs:

- Public product claims, websites, app-store text, whitepapers, and founder interviews.
- Investor-provided pitch deck, data-room documents, cohort descriptions, validation notes, and product screenshots.
- Aggregate or anonymized exports when available.
- Published literature and validation references relevant to the claimed signal.
- Sensor/source details: PPG, accelerometer, EEG, HRV, SpO2, temperature, sleep staging, recovery score, fatigue model, or related digital biomarker.

Outputs:

- IC-ready memo with pass/risk/unknown status by claim.
- Claim-risk map: plausible, unsupported, overclaimed, confounded, privacy/regulatory risky, or evidence-missing.
- DiMe-style evidence framing: sensor verification risk, analytical validation risk, and clinical validation risk.
- Cohort quality and data-completeness review.
- Confounder and baseline-risk review.
- GDPR/HealthKit/Health Connect privacy constraints where relevant.
- MDR, AI Act, and clinical-vs-wellness claim boundary flags.
- Specific evidence request list the investor can send to the startup.
- Recommended decision use: proceed, proceed with conditions, request more evidence, delay, reject, or treat as non-core to the thesis.

Initial pricing:

- 2,500-5,000 PLN per live-deal memo.
- 10,000-20,000 PLN per month for repeated screening after the first paid memo proves value.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

The obvious market story is that healthtech startups need help proving their claims.

The hidden insight is that the better first buyer is not the startup. It is the capital allocator who wants to avoid false positives before clinical proof exists. Founder-paid evidence products risk becoming vanity validation. Investor-paid claim-risk memos borrow authority from the decision workflow itself: the memo matters because it changes an IC, partner meeting, scout decision, or accelerator selection conversation.

The misunderstanding is that early-stage biosignal diligence is either informal expert calls or full clinical/regulatory assessment. There is a missing middle: a fast, fixed, technically literate memo that is explicit about what is known, what is missing, and what claim boundary the investor should not cross.

## Underused Momentum

- Wearables, EEG headsets, recovery scores, HRV features, sleep-stage algorithms, and digital biomarkers are culturally mainstream enough that early-stage startups keep using them as venture narratives.
- The EU Data Act has made connected-product data access more salient, including for smartwatches and other connected devices.
- The EHDS direction makes health-data reuse, interoperability, and governance a more visible investment issue, even if many rules phase in over time.
- HealthKit and Health Connect make user-controlled health/fitness data more accessible but also impose privacy, permission, and disclosure constraints that investors should understand.
- MDR, MDCG software guidance, and AI Act pressure make the boundary between wellness claims, medical-device claims, and high-risk AI more important.
- Digital-medicine frameworks such as DiMe's V3 separate sensor verification, analytical validation, and clinical validation, which is exactly the structure many early-stage biosignal claims lack.
- Seed investors and accelerators often lack a repeatable commercial capture layer for this momentum and still rely on ad hoc expert opinions.

## Beachhead Market

The beachhead is **EU/CEE and EU-adjacent healthtech accelerators, venture studios, seed funds, and scout programs repeatedly evaluating wearable, EEG, sleep, stress, fatigue, recovery, longevity, and digital-biomarker startups**.

Start with accelerators and venture studios before broad VC outreach because they see more companies per cycle and benefit from a standardized evidence screen. Add seed funds where the partner has active digital-health or consumer-health dealflow and a clear IC use case.

### Cold Start & Resource Bootstrapping

Initial user acquisition:

1. Produce two sharp public teardown samples of visible wearable-health or EEG/digital-biomarker startups using only public claims, product pages, research references, and privacy/regulatory caveats.
2. Create a one-page "Biosignal Claim-Risk Evidence Request Checklist" for investors to send to startups.
3. Build a target list of 40 EU/CEE and EU-adjacent healthtech accelerators, venture studios, seed funds, and scout programs.
4. Send targeted private outreach with one relevant sample teardown and a concrete offer: "I will turn one live biosignal deal into an IC-ready claim-risk memo in 72 hours."
5. Continue only if the 40-target campaign produces 8 serious calls, 4 sample-pack review requests, 2 paid pilots or written paid-pilot commitments, and at least 1 memo used in a real IC, partner meeting, selection committee, scout decision, or investment decision.

Foundational resources needed before operation:

- Sample memo template.
- Evidence request checklist.
- Literature and validation-reference library for PPG, HRV, EEG, sleep staging, stress/recovery/fatigue inference, and wearable limitations.
- Reviewer bench: one paid regulatory or clinical reviewer for edge cases, plus optional statistics/methodology reviewer if a case demands it.
- NDA and data-handling language for investor-provided founder materials.
- Private case database: claim type, sensor type, evidence provided, missing evidence, confounders, regulatory/privacy flags, decision impact, and buyer feedback.

Do not build Terra, Thryve, HealthKit, Health Connect, or native wearable integrations until at least 5-10 paid memos show repeated investor demand for data-level review.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture

Initial pricing:

- One-off live-deal memo: 2,500-5,000 PLN.
- Repeated screening pilot: 10,000-20,000 PLN per month.

Expected first-case cost:

- Founder time: 4-10 hours per memo at the beginning.
- Expert review: 500-2,000 PLN only for edge cases.
- Research and tooling: mostly low-cost AI, literature access, templates, and secure storage.

Value capture:

- The fee is small relative to a seed check, accelerator slot, venture-studio build cost, or partner time wasted on weak biosignal claims.
- The memo saves investor time, sharpens founder questioning, prevents overclaiming, and can change valuation, conditions, evidence requests, or rejection decisions.
- Gross margin should stay above 60% after reviewer payments once the memo template stabilizes.

### Distribution Moat

The distribution moat starts through private credibility, not content volume.

Compounding assets:

- Public teardown samples that show unusually sharp claim-risk judgment.
- A reusable investor evidence-request checklist.
- Repeated IC-ready memo language that investors can copy into internal workflows.
- Referrals from one fund, accelerator, or venture studio to another.
- A private benchmark library of recurring claim-risk patterns.
- Named reviewer relationships that add borrowed credibility without turning the product into a full regulatory consultancy.

## Monopoly Path

The monopoly path is to become the default early-stage biosignal claim-risk workflow for a narrow investor segment.

Path:

1. Paid manual live-deal memos.
2. Standardized IC memo format for biosignal claims.
3. Investor evidence-request checklist sent to startups before partner meetings.
4. Benchmark database of recurring claim-risk patterns: PPG validity, HRV overclaiming, EEG signal quality, sleep-stage accuracy, fatigue/stress/recovery inference, cohort weakness, confounding, consumer-wearable limitations, and wellness-vs-medical claim boundaries.
5. Retainer screening for accelerators, venture studios, and seed funds.
6. Founder-facing prep only after investors ask startups to conform to the format.

The defensible asset is not the first memo template. It is the accumulated investor workflow, pattern database, decision language, and trusted claim-risk standard.

## Local Adaptation Defense

This is not a simple foreign-market copy.

The local advantage is not Poland alone. The advantage is EU-specific, early-stage, biosignal-specific diligence at a seed-stage price and speed.

Local/EU defenses:

- EU Data Act, EHDS, GDPR, MDR, MDCG, AI Act, HealthKit, and Health Connect constraints are materially different from a generic US digital-health diligence memo.
- EU/CEE startups and investors often lack access to expensive US-style expert networks or formal clinical-assessment vendors for small early checks.
- A Warsaw-based founder can credibly serve EU/CEE first while expanding to Germany, Nordics, UK, Switzerland, and Netherlands if the buyer signal appears.
- Polish and CEE healthtech ecosystems are small enough for focused outreach, but not enough alone for scale. The first niche must be EU/CEE plus EU-adjacent from day one.

Competitive caveat:

- ORCHA and similar assessment frameworks cover broader digital-health assurance.
- Galen Growth/HealthTech Alpha covers digital-health intelligence for investors and corporates.
- CROs, regulatory consultants, and evidence-generation firms handle heavier proof work.

BioSignal should not compete with them broadly. It should sit earlier in the workflow: fast IC-ready biosignal claim-risk diligence before a full formal assessment is justified.

## Why This Could Compound

Every memo can improve a private claim-risk graph:

- Claimed signal.
- Sensor/source type.
- Validation evidence provided.
- Evidence missing.
- Confounders.
- Cohort weakness.
- Privacy and consent constraints.
- Regulatory/claim-boundary risk.
- Investor decision impact.
- Follow-up evidence requested.
- Whether the startup later satisfied the claim-risk checklist.

That graph compounds because broad competitors may have better general healthtech coverage, but they may not own the narrow, repeated, early-stage biosignal decision language used by investors before IC.

If the niche works, the expansion path is:

1. EU/CEE accelerator and venture-studio screening.
2. EU seed-fund live-deal diligence.
3. Repeated retainer screening.
4. Founder-facing preparation only where investors require the format.
5. Lightweight software for evidence intake and benchmark retrieval only after repeat paid usage.

## Validation Plan

1. Build two public teardown samples from visible wearable-health, EEG, sleep, recovery, fatigue, or stress startups.
2. Create the one-page evidence request checklist.
3. Build a 40-target list of EU/CEE and EU-adjacent healthtech accelerators, venture studios, seed funds, and scout programs.
4. Send targeted private outreach with a specific sample, not a generic "consulting" pitch.
5. Run 8 serious calls if the list produces them.
6. Ask directly for a paid live-deal memo at 2,500-5,000 PLN.
7. Track whether the memo changes a real decision: founder challenge, evidence request, IC discussion, investment delay, rejection, valuation, condition, or confidence increase.
8. Do not build software or integrate data sources until at least 5-10 paid memos show repeated patterns.

Success criteria:

- 8 serious buyer calls from 40 targets.
- 4 requests to review sample packs.
- 2 paid pilots or written paid-pilot commitments.
- 1 memo used in a real decision workflow.
- First 5 paid memos produce recurring claim-risk patterns.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or pause if, after the 40-target campaign, you do not get:

- 8 serious buyer calls.
- 4 requests to review sample packs.
- 2 paid pilots or written paid-pilot commitments.
- 1 memo used in a real IC, partner meeting, selection committee, scout decision, or investment decision.

Additional strict kill criteria:

- Kill if buyers praise the samples but will not pay at least 2,500 PLN for one live-deal memo.
- Kill if all demand comes from startups, not investors, accelerators, venture studios, or scout programs.
- Kill if buyers only want broad healthtech consulting instead of biosignal claim-risk diligence.
- Kill if no buyer will share enough founder material under NDA to make the memo decision-useful.
- Kill if the work becomes custom expert-call coordination rather than a repeatable memo product.
- Kill if paid clinical/regulatory reviewers destroy gross margin before pricing reaches repeatable levels.
- Kill if the memo is read as educational content but not used in an actual decision workflow.
- Kill if the first 5 paid memos do not reveal recurring patterns that can become a proprietary checklist or database.
- Kill if buyer feedback says the risk is real but the decision owner is the startup, not the allocator.
- Kill if serious buyers say they would only trust this from a known regulatory firm, CRO, physician-led team, or formal certifier.
