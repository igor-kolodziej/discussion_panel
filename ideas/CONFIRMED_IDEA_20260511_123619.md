# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk** is a Polish broker-first, manual B2B service for independent compressed-air service companies and energy auditors serving manufacturing SMEs.

The desk helps a service partner use EU Data Act customer authorizations, OEM/controller export playbooks, and temporary logger fallback to turn compressor telemetry into a partner-branded savings evidence memo.

The first niche is Polish compressed-air service firms serving food, plastics, metalworking, and packaging plants with 30-250 kW compressor rooms.

## One-Sentence Thesis

EU Data Act data-access rights create a new opening for independent compressed-air service firms to pull machine data out of OEM silos and convert it into immediate, partner-branded energy-savings evidence for Polish manufacturing SMEs.

## Founder Fit

- The founder can start part-time with structured research, AI-assisted document handling, telemetry analysis, templates, and written B2B outreach.
- The work fits a data scientist: messy industrial data extraction, pattern recognition, savings models, partner evidence packs, and repeatable case tracking.
- It avoids camera-led marketing and does not require the founder to become a charismatic public salesperson.
- Initial capital can stay low: the first proof requires templates, calls, basic analysis, and rented/borrowed logger fallback rather than custom hardware or SaaS.
- The Polish/EU context matters because the wedge depends on EU Data Act rights, Polish energy-efficiency economics, and local service-partner distribution.

## Customer And Pain

Primary customer:

- Independent Polish compressed-air service companies.
- Energy auditors already working with manufacturing plants.
- Smaller industrial maintenance firms that are trusted by plants but do not own OEM data portals or advanced analytics.

End beneficiary:

- Manufacturing SMEs in food, plastics, packaging, and metalworking with 30-250 kW compressor rooms.

Pain:

- Compressor OEMs and large service ecosystems often control the useful telemetry, service history, alerts, or controller data.
- Independent service firms can inspect leaks and maintenance problems, but often lack a clean data-backed memo that helps them sell repairs, control changes, pressure reduction, VSD/resizing, heat recovery, or a formal energy audit.
- Factories know compressed air is expensive, but delay action when the evidence is scattered across controller screens, OEM portals, USB exports, energy bills, and temporary measurements.
- White-certificate and grant opportunities require more formal evidence later; service firms need an upstream proof packet that opens the project without pretending to be a formal audit.

## Product Wedge

The first product is a **partner-branded compressor telemetry savings evidence memo**.

Inputs:

- Customer authorization for data access or export.
- Compressor brand, model, controller, serial number if available, and OEM portal status.
- Operating hours, load/unload profile, pressure profile, alarms, maintenance history, and service files where available.
- Electricity tariff or plant electricity-cost assumption.
- Optional temporary logger data when OEM/controller history is unavailable.

Output:

- One partner-branded memo the service company can show to a named plant.
- Data-access route used: OEM portal, controller export, USB, Modbus/OPC UA, customer file, or logger fallback.
- Savings evidence: idle/load waste, pressure anomalies, leak signals, oversized/undersized compressor clues, run-hour issues, and rough annual PLN/MWh impact.
- Recommended next action: leak repair, pressure reduction, control tuning, VSD/resizing review, heat recovery review, service change, formal energy audit, or no-action finding.
- White-certificate/grant readiness notes that clearly state the memo is not a formal audit.

This is not a generic compressed-air audit. The wedge is the **data-access bottleneck plus partner-ready commercial evidence**.

## Hidden Insight Or Market Misunderstanding

The common reading of the EU Data Act is legal compliance. The hidden commercial reading is different: it can weaken OEM data lock-in and create a new category of independent industrial aftersales services.

Compressed-air savings work already exists, so the opportunity is not "another audit." The misunderstood bottleneck is that independent service partners need a repeatable way to obtain, interpret, and package machine data before the plant will fund the real intervention.

## Underused Momentum

- The EU Data Act has applied since September 12, 2025 and gives users of connected products, including industrial machinery, stronger access and sharing rights for raw/pre-processed data.
- The Commission explicitly frames connected-product data as useful for aftermarket services, repair, maintenance, and operational efficiency.
- Compressed air remains a high-waste industrial utility: official and research sources point to large energy use, leaks, poor controls, over-pressurization, and savings potential.
- Poland already has white-certificate and energy-efficiency support mechanisms, but many SME projects need better evidence before a formal audit or investment case starts.
- Compressor OEMs already run telemetry portals, which proves that useful data exists; the gap is independent access and partner-facing commercialization.

## Beachhead Market

The beachhead is **independent Polish compressed-air service firms and energy auditors serving manufacturing SMEs with 30-250 kW compressor rooms in food, plastics, metalworking, and packaging**.

Why this niche:

- Compressor rooms are large enough for savings to matter but small enough that enterprise energy-management platforms are often overkill.
- Independent service firms already have trust and physical access.
- One partner can bring multiple plant cases.
- Data access paths repeat by OEM, controller, portal, and compressor-room pattern.
- The founder can validate mostly through written outreach, structured calls, and analysis rather than high-energy networking.

### Cold Start & Resource Bootstrapping

Cold start mechanism:

1. Build a target list of 30 Polish compressed-air service firms and energy auditors, plus 30 backup manufacturing plants in food, plastics, metalworking, and packaging.
2. Send a written offer to service partners: "I will attempt 3 customer-authorized data retrievals and produce one partner-branded savings evidence memo. If it is not useful enough to show a plant, do not pay."
3. Ask each partner for one named plant case with permission, or at minimum an anonymized case containing compressor brand/model/controller, portal/export access, operating hours, load/pressure data, and tariff assumptions.
4. Use the first cases to build the OEM/controller access map and prove which data routes work.
5. Convert partners into a paid follow-on price of 1,500-3,000 PLN per successful memo, or a 5-memo pilot.

Foundational resources:

- EU Data Act request and authorization templates in Polish and English.
- OEM/controller data-access map for Atlas Copco, Kaeser, BOGE, Ingersoll Rand, CompAir, Almig, and common third-party controllers.
- Portable logger fallback through rental, a friendly service partner, or a paid specialist visit when OEM history is missing.
- Energy-auditor review partner to keep savings logic credible and avoid pretending to provide formal certification.
- Private case spreadsheet: partner, plant segment, OEM/model/controller, data path, retrieval success, fields obtained, savings flags, recommended intervention, quoted value, paid conversion.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture

Initial pricing:

- Free: up to 3 data-retrieval attempts and one memo for a service partner if they bring a real named case.
- Paid pilot: 5,000-9,000 PLN for 3-5 memos after the free proof.
- Per memo: 1,500-3,000 PLN when the memo is based on usable data and can be shown to the plant.

Why partners pay:

- A memo can help sell leak repair, control changes, pressure optimization, service work, compressor resizing, heat recovery, or formal energy audits.
- The partner captures the downstream implementation margin; the desk captures the upstream data-access and evidence-production fee.
- The founder does not need to finance hardware, installation, or implementation.

Cost structure:

- First month: mainly research, outreach, templates, and analysis time.
- Variable cost: 3-8 hours per case initially, less after repeat controller patterns.
- Logger fallback is only used when a partner or plant agrees the case is worth measuring.

### Distribution Moat

The distribution moat is partner ownership of plant relationships plus a growing playbook that makes each new partner easier to onboard.

Compounding distribution assets:

- Partner-branded sample memos.
- OEM/controller-specific request scripts.
- Reusable plant authorization language.
- Case studies anonymized by sector and compressor room size.
- Benchmarks showing which data paths produce sellable savings evidence fastest.

## Monopoly Path

The monopoly path is a local industrial machine-data access layer, starting with compressed air:

- OEM/controller data-access playbooks by brand, model, portal, export format, and fallback route.
- Savings-pattern library by compressor size, sector, shift pattern, pressure behavior, and load/unload profile.
- Partner trust network among independent service companies and energy auditors.
- Evidence templates that turn raw telemetry into commercial action without becoming generic dashboards.
- Later expansion into other machine-data niches only after compressed air creates repeatable paid cases.

Broad energy-management platforms can analyze data after it is available. The defensible layer here is knowing how to get the data, what it means locally, and how service partners can use it to close projects.

## Local Adaptation Defense

This is not a foreign SaaS copy.

Local defense comes from:

- EU Data Act rights applied in a Polish SME industrial-service context.
- Polish-language authorization, partner memos, and plant communication.
- Local compressed-air service firms that already know the plants.
- Polish white-certificate and energy-efficiency grant context, handled as readiness notes rather than formal audit claims.
- OEM/controller access patterns learned from real Polish plants, including older equipment and mixed compressor rooms.

Foreign software can move into analytics, but it is less likely to own the messy local partner workflow of getting permission, extracting data, interpreting controller quirks, and packaging the memo under a trusted service firm's brand.

## Why This Could Compound

- Every case improves the OEM/controller access map.
- Every successful retrieval creates a reusable data route.
- Every memo teaches which savings signals actually convert into paid interventions.
- Every partner relationship can create multiple plant cases.
- The dataset becomes stronger than a generic audit checklist: it links brand, controller, sector, operating pattern, data availability, intervention type, and conversion.
- The desk can later expand from compressed air into pumps, chillers, boilers, industrial HVAC, or production-line equipment where EU Data Act access and aftermarket service economics repeat.

## Validation Plan

1. Build the target list of 30 compressed-air service firms/energy auditors and 30 backup plants.
2. Create the first authorization template, data-request checklist, and one synthetic sample memo.
3. Contact 15 partners with the offer of 3 free data-retrieval attempts and one memo.
4. In each call or email, ask what they currently use instead: OEM portal, leak audit, service report, energy audit, spreadsheet, no formal evidence, or manufacturer rep.
5. Deliver the first 3-6 real retrieval attempts using customer-authorized data.
6. Track whether the data is usable, whether the partner forwards the memo, whether the plant asks for a quote, and whether the partner commits to paid follow-on work.
7. Expand outreach to the next 15 partners only after the first process is documented.
8. Do not build SaaS before at least 20 real cases and repeated paid demand.

## Kill Criteria

Strict confirmation kill condition:

```text
Kill the idea if, after contacting 30 compressed-air service firms/energy auditors, you do not get at least 5 partners willing to introduce you to a named plant with customer authorization and at least 2 partners willing to pay or commit to a paid follow-on price of 1,500-3,000 PLN per successful savings memo after the free trial.
```

Operational kill conditions:

- Kill or materially pivot if fewer than 3 usable data retrievals happen across the first 6 factory cases.
- Kill if partners like the concept but will not involve named plants or customer-authorized data.
- Kill if the memo is treated as academic analysis and does not help sell repairs, control changes, service work, or formal audits.
- Kill if the work collapses into generic compressed-air auditing without a repeatable Data Act/OEM/controller access advantage.
- Kill if an energy-auditor partner says the savings logic cannot be kept credible without formal audit work from day one.
