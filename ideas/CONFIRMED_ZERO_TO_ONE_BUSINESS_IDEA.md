# Confirmed Zero To One Business Idea

## Idea

**Distributor-backed Heat-Pump Commissioning Passport for Polish single-family heat-pump retrofits**

Build a narrow commissioning passport that a regional heat-pump distributor, wholesaler, manufacturer rep, or warranty department can recognize as a condition for better installer status: extended warranty, faster warranty-claim handling, rebate eligibility, preferred pricing, certified/preferred partner status, or installer ranking.

The key is that this is not a generic AI checklist. The product only becomes interesting if the passport is tied to an external economic gate controlled by a distributor or warranty channel.

## Why This Fits The Founder Profile

- You have unusually good starting access through your parents' renewable-energy installation company near Ostrow Wielkopolski.
- You can start with historical jobs before building software: completed installs, callbacks, complaints, quotes, setup assumptions, customer expectations, and post-install outcomes.
- The work fits your strengths: data science, structured analysis, AI-assisted document processing, energy-domain access, and low-camera B2B execution.
- It avoids influencer marketing and can start part-time with controlled capital.
- The validation question is concrete: will one distributor recognize the passport in a way that changes installer economics?

## Customer And Pain

Primary buyer:

- Regional heat-pump distributors
- Heat-pump wholesalers
- Manufacturer reps
- Warranty departments
- Installer networks that want quality control

End-user pressure:

- Installers want fewer callbacks, warranty fights, customer complaints, and unpaid service visits.
- Distributors and manufacturers want fewer bad installs damaging product reputation.
- Customers want correctly sized, correctly commissioned systems that do not create comfort or bill disappointment.

## Product Wedge

Before an installer gets a warranty fast-track, rebate, preferred procurement status, or certified partner treatment, each heat-pump retrofit job must pass a commissioning passport.

The passport captures:

- Job photos
- Hydraulic layout
- Electrical setup
- Heat-loss assumptions
- Equipment/building match
- ZUM and subsidy-relevant equipment evidence where relevant
- Installer settings
- Customer handover evidence
- Customer expectation notes
- Likely callback triggers
- Installation-risk flags

The output is a pass/risk/rework packet that helps a distributor decide whether the job qualifies for the desired status or needs remediation.

## Zero To One Rationale

Secret:

Polish heat-pump retrofits do not mainly need another calculator or CRM. The hidden pain is trust and quality control at the channel level: bad installs create warranty friction, customer distrust, distributor pain, and brand damage.

Monopoly wedge:

Start with one local distributor or wholesaler and make the passport part of a real economic gate. If the distributor recognizes it for warranty speed, rebate eligibility, preferred installer status, or installer ranking, the workflow becomes harder to ignore.

Defensibility:

The long-term moat is not the form. It is the accumulating dataset of Polish retrofit-risk patterns: house type, install design, equipment match, customer expectation, settings, callback cause, warranty dispute, bill disappointment, and distributor resolution.

Expansion path:

Commissioning passport -> warranty fast-track -> installer quality ranking -> distributor network standard -> manufacturer/warranty analytics -> financing or insurance underwriting for retrofit quality.

## Validation Plan

Do not build SaaS first.

Phase 1: Historical blind test

- Collect 30 to 50 past heat-pump or retrofit jobs from your parents' company.
- For each job, reconstruct pre-install assumptions and post-install outcome.
- Blind-score which jobs should have been accepted, repriced, redesigned, or expectation-managed.
- Compare the scores against real callbacks, complaints, warranty issues, comfort complaints, and bill disappointment.

Phase 2: Passport prototype

- Create a one-page or two-page commissioning passport template.
- Produce 10 sample passports from real or historical jobs.
- Include concrete risk flags, missing evidence, and distributor-facing pass/rework status.

Phase 3: Distributor gate test

- Approach one distributor or wholesaler already supplying your parents' company.
- Ask for a written 10-job pilot.
- The pilot must recognize the passport for at least one real economic gate: warranty fast-track, rebate eligibility, installer ranking, preferred status, or procurement advantage.
- Charge 100 to 300 PLN per job, either to the distributor or participating installers.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or redesign the idea if:

- No distributor agrees to a written 10-job pilot.
- The distributor likes the idea but refuses to tie it to any economic gate.
- Installers treat it as paperwork and will not pay or change behavior.
- The output collapses into a generic checklist that an installer, CRM, or manufacturer can copy without losing anything.
- Historical job scoring does not predict callbacks, complaints, warranty friction, or customer dissatisfaction better than obvious installer intuition.

## Budget And Time

Suggested first budget:

- 0 to 2,000 PLN for no-code tools, data cleanup, document templates, and AI workflow setup.
- 2,000 to 8,000 PLN if a simple prototype interface is needed after the first distributor conversation.
- Do not spend near 30,000 PLN until there is a written distributor pilot.
- Do not consider the 100,000 PLN family capital unless the pilot produces paid repeat usage or a signed expansion path.

Suggested weekly time:

- 5 to 8 hours per week for the first 4 weeks.
- Most work should be data reconstruction, template design, and narrow distributor conversations, not software development.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

Week 1:

- Interview your parents and list the last 30 to 50 relevant heat-pump/retrofit jobs.
- Define outcome labels: callback, complaint, warranty issue, comfort failure, bill disappointment, no issue.
- Draft the commissioning passport sections.

Week 2:

- Build the first spreadsheet/data model.
- Reconstruct 10 to 15 jobs.
- Produce the first 3 sample passports.

Week 3:

- Finish the blind scoring on at least 30 jobs.
- Compare predicted risk to actual outcomes.
- Refine the passport to focus only on fields that predict real problems or matter to distributors.

Week 4:

- Take 5 to 10 polished example passports to one distributor or wholesaler.
- Ask for a written 10-job pilot tied to a real economic gate.
- If the distributor refuses the gate, interview two more distributors before building anything.
