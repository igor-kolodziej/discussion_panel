# Zero-to-One Candidate: VocalReserve

Candidate generation only — not validated. Research snapshot: 2026-07-23 CEST. Employer willingness to pay, employee adoption, causal impact, margins, seat counts, and valuation remain hypotheses.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Create the worker-controlled, on-device vocal-load companion for contact centres: every speaking shift is measured without uploading call content, cues are timed before fatigue becomes disruptive, and employers pay per voluntary active seat for a healthier and more sustainable voice operation rather than another surveillance dashboard.

## Founder Fit

The founder's biosignal, time-series ML, neuroscience, privacy-aware analytics, and behavioural intervention background fits local feature extraction, personal baselines, experimentation, and closed-loop cue design. A desktop-first pilot avoids custom hardware; commodity headsets and an optional smartwatch provide input and haptics.

The founder lacks occupational voice, enterprise contact-centre, employment-law, and audio-device credibility. Those gaps are bounded through a paid speech-language/occupational-voice adviser, fixed-fee labour/privacy counsel, and a limited desktop/audio engineer. The founder does not diagnose dysphonia, claim medical prevention, process call meaning, or sell productivity scoring.

The business can be developed part-time because the first offer is a fixed eight-week cohort, not 24/7 clinical support or a custom headset.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is an operations or employee-wellbeing lead at a Polish outsourced contact centre with 100–1,000 voice agents, high speaking time, existing USB headsets, and visible fatigue, absence, quality, or attrition concerns.

The employee wants to finish a shift without throat discomfort, escalating strain, or fear that asking for a break will be treated as weakness. The employer wants reliable customer conversations and lower avoidable absence/turnover without reducing staffed hours.

Current workarounds include:

- generic break policies that ignore individual load and queue conditions;
- posters, hydration advice, or one-off voice training;
- supervisors noticing problems only after quality drops;
- headset noise/tone analytics oriented toward call quality;
- employees informally lowering effort, using sick leave, or leaving voice work.

The tolerated tradeoff is crude: either fixed breaks that operations sees as costly or continuous speaking until symptoms become visible. The gap is a private, content-blind personal dose model whose intervention occurs at natural workflow moments and whose employer output cannot be repurposed into individual discipline.

## Product Wedge

The founding product is a prepaid 20-seat, eight-week **Vocal Reserve Pilot**:

1. A local desktop client extracts speaking-duration, pause, intensity-proxy, pitch-range, recovery, headset position/quality, and self-check features; raw calls are not uploaded or retained.
2. Each employee sees a personal reserve estimate and receives a small cue—mic-position correction, hydration prompt, brief low-load exercise, or suggested microbreak—at a natural call boundary.
3. An optional commodity watch supplies haptics. No proprietary wearable is required.
4. The employer sees only minimum-cohort aggregated adoption, intervention completion, fatigue trend, and operationally agreed non-content outcomes.
5. Individual data cannot be used for ranking, compensation, discipline, call-content analysis, emotion scoring, or productivity evaluation.
6. The pilot compares baseline and intervention periods while controlling for speaking time and shift pattern. It does not claim clinical treatment or injury prevention.

The first product does not integrate with telephony routing. It detects local call/audio state and asks the employee to act; any automated break scheduling is a later product requiring separate buyer and worker approval.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Contact centres already spend on audio quality, workforce management, QA, training, and turnover. Occupational voice load sits between those budgets: it is economically relevant but not owned by a product category. Headset vendors optimise what the customer hears; wellbeing apps operate outside the speaking workflow; occupational assessments are episodic.

The overlooked insight is that call content is not necessary to estimate much of the mechanical exposure. A product can stay on the worker's side of the privacy boundary, observe the repeated event—speaking time and recovery—and intervene without turning health features into managerial surveillance. If workers trust it, the independent cross-headset position becomes an advantage against vendor-specific tone analytics.

## Underused Momentum

- On-device audio ML can discard raw speech immediately and retain only bounded features.
- USB headsets and watches provide an installed delivery surface, eliminating pre-proof hardware manufacture.
- Occupational-voice literature makes the pain credible enough for targeted paid discovery.
- Poland's large business-services base provides a listable first market and lower-cost product development.
- Enterprise buyers are already investing in AI and employee transformation; that momentum creates access, not proof of this budget.

## Beachhead Market

Polish and CEE outsourced contact centres with:

- at least 100 voice seats;
- a repeatable speaking-heavy queue;
- USB headset/desktop work;
- a named operations and HR/wellbeing owner;
- willingness to run a voluntary 20-person cohort;
- no requirement to stream raw audio or individual health data to management.

Start with customer service rather than sales because the promise is sustainable voice operation, not persuasion performance. Exclude emergency dispatch, clinical call lines, union disputes, and regulated voice diagnostics in the first year.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Founding eight-week, 20-seat pilot: 9,800–12,000 PLN prepaid.
- Production onboarding: 8,000–15,000 PLN per organisation.
- Subscription: 49–69 PLN per voluntary active seat monthly, with a 60-seat or 30,000 PLN annual minimum.
- Optional commodity watches are customer-owned or passed through at cost; hardware margin is not required.

Mature unit assumptions:

- compute, telemetry, and support: 10%–14% of subscription;
- occupational-voice content/adviser royalty and QA: 4%–7%;
- customer success and device/desktop support: 8%–14%;
- gross margin: 72%–80%;
- operating margin at scale: 24%–34%.

At 49 PLN per month, a seat produces 588 PLN annual revenue. The product fails if direct support plus deployment consumes more than 180 PLN per seat-year or if voluntary participation is too low to justify the minimum contract.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First ten organisations**

- Build a named list from ABSL members, contact-centre directories, job listings, and company sites showing Polish/CEE voice operations.
- Send a one-page, non-content risk map to operations/wellbeing leads and offer the exact 20-seat pilot; no free custom assessment.
- Use a paid occupational-voice adviser in the sales call when technical credibility is required, but keep the contract and data architecture with VocalReserve.
- Require two independent prepaid pilots before spending beyond the first tranche.
- Convert only when the employer signs the non-surveillance data covenant and employees can decline without penalty.

**First hundred organisations**

- Turn the first ten deployments into a standard USB/headset compatibility matrix, privacy pack, and workload-specific playbook.
- Publish cohort-level evidence only with contractual permission and minimum-cell safeguards.
- Acquire through direct outbound and high-intent occupational voice/agent wellbeing content first; later benefits brokers or headset resellers may be tested but are not a cold-start dependency.
- A hundred organisations averaging 150 active seats produce the conservative 15,000-seat scale. No account may represent more than 15% of revenue.

## Primary Control Point

The proposed control point is a **contractual Tier 2 active-use route**:

- the local client is present during each covered employee's speaking shift;
- the paid deployment agreement covers selected voice seats for a fixed term;
- the employee retains a personal baseline and intervention history;
- customer and employee terms specify whether bounded, non-content, de-identified derivatives may improve the cross-customer model;
- the non-surveillance covenant and privacy architecture create trust-based switching cost.

It is revocable and therefore not Tier 1. Jabra, Genesys, NICE, Cogito, or another installed platform could copy the features. VocalReserve must prove that independent worker trust, cross-headset operation, and vocal-load outcome data matter before those vendors treat the problem as a feature.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

**Resource needed:** access to paid speaking shifts, an occupationally credible protocol, a content-blind local client, and lawful rights to improve the model.

**How it is secured**

1. Contract one occupational-voice specialist to define non-medical measures and safe cue content.
2. Obtain labour/privacy advice before collecting employee data.
3. Build a local prototype that proves raw speech deletion and auditable feature limits.
4. Sell two independent 20-seat pilots directly to listable centres.
5. Require a signed data covenant, employee-facing notice, voluntary enrollment, minimum cohort reporting, and separate optional model-improvement permission.
6. Within twelve months, secure five annual organisation contracts, at least 500 active seats, and one validated headset/desktop deployment protocol.

The founder can purchase expertise and recruit public businesses. No headset OEM, telephony platform, clinic, insurer, union, or regulator must approve the first paid pilot.

**Proof before serious capital**

- two prepaid pilots;
- at least 70% voluntary enrollment among offered seats;
- at least 70% of enrolled users complete six weeks;
- a useful directional change in pre-specified fatigue/adoption measures without worsening the buyer's agreed service metric;
- at least one annual conversion or second paid cohort;
- support and analysis fit positive pilot contribution.

## Distribution Advantage

The first acquisition mechanism is a narrow named-account offer: a fixed price, fixed cohort, auditable privacy architecture, and a problem specific to voice operations. The company does not need broad brand awareness or employee-by-employee consumer acquisition.

Compounding distribution can come from:

- reusable labour/privacy and IT-security materials;
- compatibility across common USB headsets;
- cohort benchmarks that let an employer estimate expected participation and operational effort;
- worker trust from refusing individual productivity reporting;
- later benefit-broker or BPO-group distribution only after direct renewals prove value.

If procurement requires a telephony-platform contract before any paid pilot, the cold-start thesis fails.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Win voluntary voice-load pilots in Polish/CEE contact centres.
2. Productise cohort setup, headset compatibility, and intervention timing.
3. Expand to interpreters, collections teams, remote sales, teachers, streamers, and other paid voice occupations only where a budgeted organisation and repeated speaking event exist.
4. Add a small neck/skin sensor or dedicated haptic only if software pilots show that microphone features miss a valuable signal and customers fund development.
5. License the on-device reserve model to headset or workforce platforms only after VocalReserve owns a credible dataset and direct customer base.

The expansion advantage is cross-occupation vocal-load knowledge and privacy-preserving intervention, not generic employee wellness.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers a dense business-services base, multilingual operations, lower-cost technical development, and EU privacy expectations that favour content-minimising architecture. European employee-data scrutiny can make a worker-controlled product preferable to individual analytics.

There is no geographic barrier. A headset or contact-centre platform can enter. The defensible local wedge is direct access to CEE operations, multilingual protocols, and a product whose technical and contractual design prevents surveillance rather than promising it in marketing.

## Compounding Advantage

Ordinary paid use can create, within lawful permissions:

- speaking-dose and recovery sequences;
- headset/environment quality features;
- cue timing and acceptance;
- self-reported fatigue and recovery;
- shift context and aggregate operational outcome;
- device/desktop compatibility and false-positive history.

This builds real-time HCI, speech/biosignal ML, longitudinal behavioural/physiological proxies, privacy-preserving workplace interfaces, occupational research relationships, and eventually wearable hardware expertise. The asset is intrinsic to the paid shift intervention.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder remains employed; optional founder distributions remain at or below 5%. The strong case reinvests 100% during scale.

Reinvestment order:

1. privacy/security and auditable edge processing;
2. headset/desktop compatibility and deployment automation;
3. occupational-voice research and safer intervention content;
4. customer acquisition and multilingual support;
5. hardware research only after signal-value evidence.

No separate dataset valuation is added to the enterprise value.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- **Days 1–25:** twenty operations/employee interviews, specialist protocol, counsel-reviewed privacy/labour boundary, and clickable/local audio demonstration.
- **Days 26–60:** targeted outreach to 120 named centres; offer the exact 9,800–12,000 PLN pilot; conduct no more than ten unpaid discovery calls.
- **Days 61–105:** deliver the first 20-seat baseline/intervention pilot using commodity headsets and optional watches.
- **Days 106–140:** deliver or sign a second independent paid pilot; measure adoption, fatigue, support, and agreed non-content operational guardrails.
- **Days 141–180:** obtain one annual conversion, renewal, or prepaid second cohort and document positive contribution.

The founder remains employed. A paid proof is a prepaid pilot from an unrelated employer, not a letter of interest, wellbeing survey, or free employee trial.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- **Tranche 1 — maximum 29,000 PLN before paid proof:** privacy/labour counsel 6k; occupational-voice adviser 5k; local prototype/audio engineering 10k; targeted acquisition 4k; contingency 4k.
- **Tranche 2 — cumulative maximum 55,000 PLN:** after one prepaid pilot and voluntary enrollment, for second-customer compatibility and delivery.
- **Tranche 3 — cumulative maximum 100,000 PLN:** after two independent paying employers, one repeat/annual commitment, positive contribution, and a plausible seat-acquisition channel.
- Family capital remains excluded until multiple independent paying organisations, repeat demand, and a risk-adjusted scaling case exist.
- Maximum founder cash loss before paid proof: **29,000 PLN**.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after:

- at least five independent annual customers or contracted recurring demand covering 1,000+ active seats;
- positive contribution after customer success, security, specialist, and support cost;
- at least two customer-acquisition sources and one renewal cohort;
- a repeatable deployment that does not require founder presence on every shift;
- no unresolved material privacy/employee-relations issue;
- twelve months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If employers do not pay or workers do not trust the product, stop rather than sell individual productivity or emotion analytics. Reusable assets are limited to:

- local content-discarding audio feature extraction;
- consent and minimum-cohort reporting architecture;
- rights-cleared occupational-voice cue content;
- generic headset compatibility work;
- lawful aggregate protocol learnings and relationships.

A consumer singer app, medical diagnostic, call-quality scorer, or proprietary neck wearable is a new candidate and receives no validation credit from these pilots.

## Critical Evidence

- Occupational-voice literature establishes prolonged call-centre voice load as a real problem.
- Closed-loop vocal-fatigue sensing has been technically demonstrated in research.
- Headset incumbents already perform edge audio analysis, proving feasibility and copy risk.
- Poland has a large listable business-services sector, allowing targeted acquisition without privileged introductions.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Employers will pay 9,800–12,000 PLN before an absence/attrition result is known.
- Employees trust and use a voluntary content-blind tool.
- Short pilot measures predict enough economic value to convert to 49–69 PLN per seat monthly.
- Edge features are sufficient for useful cue timing.
- One hundred organisations and 15,000 retained seats are reachable without an OEM/platform partnership.

## Kill Criteria

- No prepaid pilot after 120 targeted accounts and 20 qualified buyer conversations.
- Fewer than 50% of offered employees voluntarily enroll or fewer than 60% complete.
- Raw audio or individual productivity reporting is required by the buyer.
- No credible improvement in pre-specified user outcomes, or agreed service metrics deteriorate.
- No annual conversion, repeat cohort, or budget-backed continuation after the first five paid pilots.
- Mature support/deployment exceeds 30% of seat revenue.
- Privacy counsel identifies a structure that cannot be made proportionate under the proposed design.
- A headset/contact-centre incumbent bundles a trusted equivalent and eliminates independent willingness to pay.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Maximum before paid proof: 29,000 PLN.
- Maximum founder capital in first year: 100,000 PLN, staged.
- Founder: biosignal/audio ML, experiments, product, direct B2B acquisition, about five hours daily.
- Contractors: occupational-voice/speech specialist, fixed-fee labour/privacy counsel, and limited desktop/audio engineer.
