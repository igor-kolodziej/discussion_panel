# Zero-to-One Candidate: ProcedurePosture

Candidate generation only — not validated. Research snapshot: 2026-07-23 CEST. Device performance, professional willingness to pay, retention, margins, customer counts, regulatory position, and valuation remain hypotheses.

## One-Sentence Thesis

Lease dentists a two-module haptic posture system that learns their actual procedure rhythm and cues only at safe, useful moments, turning every paid patient session into better personal calibration and a proprietary procedure-to-load-to-response dataset rather than selling another generic “sit straight” reminder.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build motion-signal pipelines, personal baselines, experiment design, low-latency feedback, and product analytics. The project directly develops wearable hardware and real-time human–computer interaction without requiring clinical diagnosis. Commodity IMUs, vibration modules, and a mobile/desktop app are sufficient for the first paid cohort.

The founder lacks dental ergonomics, medical-device classification, industrial design, and hardware-production experience. These are bounded early inputs: a paid dental ergonomics adviser, a fixed-fee regulatory/claims memo, a contract embedded engineer, and off-the-shelf enclosed modules. Dentists—not universities, clinics, or insurers as institutions—are the first buyers.

The initial cohort can be supported alongside employment because it is limited to twenty users, one hardware configuration, and one metropolitan area.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first customer is a self-employed dentist or dental hygienist performing at least twenty chairside hours weekly, already experiencing end-of-day neck/back discomfort or concern about sustained awkward posture, and willing to pay personally for equipment that does not interrupt patient care.

They really want to preserve a long, high-income career without turning every procedure into a self-monitoring exercise. Their frustration is not ignorance—most know good posture. It is that visibility, patient position, instrument access, and concentration pull them into sustained positions, while generic alerts buzz at the wrong moment.

Current workarounds include:

- one-off ergonomics training;
- changing stool, loupes, patient position, or stretching;
- generic posture wearables that detect upper-body angle;
- phone timers and end-of-day exercises;
- tolerating discomfort because procedure quality has priority.

The tolerated tradeoff is crucial: a dentist accepts imperfect posture to protect precision and speed. A product wins only if it understands when not to intervene.

## Product Wedge

The founding product is a paid six-week lease:

1. Two small washable/enclosed IMU-haptic modules attach outside the sterile field—upper torso and waist/seat reference.
2. The user marks procedure start/end and a broad procedure type with one tap; no patient identity or clinical record enters the system.
3. The first week creates a personal movement and static-load baseline.
4. The second phase introduces low-salience cues only at configured safe moments: between instrument cycles, at procedure transitions, or after a threshold that the user approves.
5. The app shows a private daily “load map,” cue acceptance, and between-patient reset plan.
6. Feedback adapts to whether a cue caused a lower-load position without increasing abrupt movement or being dismissed.

The product makes no claim to diagnose, treat, or prevent musculoskeletal disease. It is an ergonomic feedback and professional-performance aid. The first version does not use cameras, patient data, EMG, dental-unit integration, or proprietary manufacturing.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Generic posture devices assume the desired posture and cue policy are context-independent. Dentistry is a better niche because posture is constrained by a valuable precision task, generic interruptions have unusually high cost, and users are identifiable professionals with high lifetime earnings.

The overlooked requirement is **procedural silence**: the system must infer when a reminder is safe and useful, not merely detect flexion. A direct-pay lease can prove this without convincing an employer, insurer, university, or equipment OEM. If the system creates a useful personal model, the calibration and device relationship renew together.

## Underused Momentum

- Low-cost IMUs and haptics support a sub-40,000 PLN paid cohort.
- Published dental studies indicate that wearable/postural biofeedback is technically and behaviourally plausible.
- EU-OSHA recognises awkward/static posture and repetitive movement as work-related musculoskeletal risk factors, creating broad salience without mandating this product.
- More than 365,000 dentists in the EU provide a countable expansion denominator.
- Direct-to-professional ecommerce and public practice directories make acquisition possible without institutional status.

## Beachhead Market

Begin with private dentists and hygienists in Warsaw and nearby cities who:

- personally control small equipment purchases;
- perform routine outpatient procedures;
- work at least twenty chairside hours weekly;
- have no acute condition requiring medical treatment;
- agree to use a device outside the sterile field and provide procedure labels without patient data.

Exclude surgeons, hospital procurement, diagnostic/therapeutic claims, employee monitoring, and integration into regulated dental equipment. One initial configuration supports right- and left-handed users but no specialty-specific promises.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Founding six-week lease: 599 PLN prepaid plus a 750 PLN refundable security deposit.
- Production subscription: 199–239 PLN per month, including devices, calibration, app, firmware, replacement for ordinary failure, and annual fit review.
- Optional annual prepay: 1,990–2,390 PLN.
- Later practice bundle: same per-seat economics; no employer receives an individual's raw motion history without explicit user control.

Planning assumptions per active seat:

- module pair and charger landed cost: 320–480 PLN, depreciated over 24 months;
- refurbishment/replacement reserve: 10%–14% of revenue;
- compute, app, payment, and shipping: 6%–9%;
- support and fit review: 9%–14%;
- gross margin: 62%–76% depending on scale and device life;
- operating margin: 20%–32% after acquisition, engineering, research, and hardware operations.

At 199 PLN monthly, annual revenue is 2,388 PLN. The model fails if annual hardware/support cost exceeds about 900 PLN or if six-month retention is too low to recover acquisition and device handling.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First ten**

- Build a list of 150 private Warsaw dental professionals from public practice pages and professional directories.
- Run twenty short problem interviews and show a non-working enclosure/cue demo.
- Offer exactly twenty founding leases; require ten 599 PLN payments and deposits before ordering the complete cohort hardware.
- Fit devices outside clinical hours and begin with post-procedure feedback before enabling any in-procedure cue.
- No free custom ergonomics report is offered.

**First hundred**

- Use direct referrals from retained dentists, before/after load-map examples with permission, precise search content, and direct outreach to public practices.
- Add a referral credit only after six-month retention and contribution are known.
- Test one dental educator, equipment retailer, or conference booth as a paid acquisition channel, but no distributor is required for the first hundred.
- Support no more than three common procedure categories until classification and support are stable.

## Primary Control Point

The proposed position is a **conditional Tier 2 installed-use control point**:

- ProcedurePosture owns and leases the calibrated devices rather than selling generic hardware once.
- The personal baseline and cue-response model improve with continued use.
- Contract and consent can permit retention of bounded, non-patient, de-identified procedure/load/cue/outcome derivatives for model improvement.
- Device replacement, firmware, calibration, and accumulated personal history attach to the subscription.

It is not Tier 1. A dentist can cancel and buy a generic posture sensor; an incumbent can purchase IMUs. The strongest copy risk is UPRIGHT GO, Soter, a loupe/stool vendor, or a dental-equipment company adding a procedure mode. Procedure-specific cue timing and retention must become visibly valuable before the installed base compounds.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

**Resource needed:** paid access to real dental working motion, an ergonomically credible cue protocol, leased hardware, and lawful rights to learn from non-patient outcomes.

**How it is secured**

1. Contract a dental ergonomics adviser for the baseline/cue protocol.
2. Commission a regulatory/claims memo confirming the non-medical intended purpose and marketing boundary.
3. Build ten module pairs from certified development hardware and safe enclosures; do not design a production PCB.
4. Sell ten founding leases and deposits directly.
5. Collect only procedure category, motion, cue, device status, and voluntary comfort/usefulness check-ins; no patient identity, audio, video, or clinical record.
6. Within twelve months, secure 100 paying professionals, at least 60 six-month retained users, one reliable manufacturing/refurbishment workflow, and a signed original design/firmware assignment from contractors.

An unknown founder can buy components and expertise, reach public professionals, and offer a reversible lease. No clinic chain, dental OEM, insurer, or research institution must approve the first payment.

**Proof before serious capital**

- ten paid leases;
- at least eight users complete four weeks;
- at least six users request continuation or annual preorder;
- useful cue acceptance without reported procedure disruption;
- device/support cost consistent with positive contribution;
- procedure-aware policy beats a generic fixed-angle cue in a within-user comparison.

## Distribution Advantage

The cold-start mechanism is precise direct-to-professional acquisition. Dentists are publicly listable, control purchases, and can understand a six-week lease without a committee.

Distribution compounds through:

- visible procedure-specific rather than generic-posture positioning;
- retained-user referrals within a concentrated profession;
- a compatibility and fit protocol that lowers return/support rates;
- rights-cleared, aggregate procedure load maps useful for educational content;
- later dental educators and retailers after direct economics are proven.

No association endorsement or key-opinion-leader relationship is assumed. Kill the channel thesis if the first hundred require expensive conference sponsorship or clinical authority.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Win the private-dentist posture niche with between-patient and safe-moment cues.
2. Improve classification and personal cue policies from paid longitudinal use.
3. Add dental hygienists and selected procedure families.
4. Expand to microsurgeons, sonographers, tattoo artists, jewellers, and other precision professions only after a new paid cohort proves their distinct workflow.
5. Add EMG or a custom flexible sensor only if paid data proves meaningful incremental value over IMUs.
6. License the cue policy or hardware module to loupes, stools, or professional-wellness distributors only after direct-user retention creates bargaining power.

The advantage is not “AI posture.” It is knowing when a precision worker can safely change posture and which small cue works for that person.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland supports inexpensive local fitting and hardware iteration, while private dental practices are reachable without hospital procurement. The EU dentist denominator allows expansion from a Polish evidence base.

There is no regulatory or geographic moat. Local defence consists of Polish-language direct distribution, dentist-specific protocol, device service, and a proprietary cue-response corpus. If those do not beat a generic 69 USD sensor, localisation has no value.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid procedure can produce, with explicit permission:

- procedure category and duration;
- personal motion/load sequence;
- cue timing and dismissal;
- immediate motion response;
- between-patient recovery;
- voluntary comfort/usefulness check-in;
- hardware fit, battery, and failure history.

This builds wearable hardware expertise, low-latency haptic HCI, motion ML, professional physiological/behavioural data, hardware reliability knowledge, dental research relationships, and original cue-policy IP. The profitable lease and lab asset are the same activity.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected scenarios reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder keeps corporate employment; optional founder distributions are capped at 5%. Strong success reinvests 100% during scale.

Reinvestment order:

1. device reliability, cleaning, refurbishment, and replacement stock;
2. embedded/ML engineering and lower-support onboarding;
3. ergonomics research and user safety;
4. direct acquisition in the next language/market;
5. custom hardware only after off-the-shelf proof.

The dataset and hardware IP receive no separate valuation on top of enterprise value.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- **Days 1–25:** twenty dentist interviews; adviser protocol; regulatory/claims memo; non-functional enclosure and haptic demonstration.
- **Days 26–55:** sell ten 599 PLN founding leases plus deposits to unrelated professionals; order the cohort hardware only after payment.
- **Days 56–90:** fit devices, collect a no-cue baseline, and deliver private post-procedure maps.
- **Days 91–125:** compare generic fixed-angle reminders with procedure-aware safe-moment cues.
- **Days 126–180:** complete twenty paid users, obtain at least six continued subscriptions/annual preorders, calculate support/device contribution, and document whether procedure-aware timing creates material preference.

The founder remains employed and performs fittings in bounded evening/weekend blocks. Paid proof is the non-refundable lease fee; refundable deposits protect hardware but do not count as revenue proof.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- **Tranche 1 — maximum 34,000 PLN before paid proof:** adviser 5k; regulatory/claims/privacy review 4k; prototype/enclosures and ten module pairs 10k; app/embedded contractor 10k; acquisition 3k; contingency 2k.
- **Tranche 2 — cumulative maximum 60,000 PLN:** after ten paid leases, usable data, and acceptable safety/support, for twenty more devices and automation.
- **Tranche 3 — cumulative maximum 100,000 PLN:** after 50 paying users, at least 60% six-month continuation or annual prepay, positive contribution, and a working acquisition channel.
- Family capital is excluded until multiple independent paying cohorts, repeat demand, and a clear scale case exist.
- Maximum founder cash loss before paid evidence: **34,000 PLN**; devices retain partial resale/reuse value.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after:

- at least 300 active paying professionals across multiple independent cohorts;
- positive contribution after hardware depreciation, replacement, shipping, support, refunds, and CAC;
- six-month retention or annual prepay from multiple cohorts;
- a repeatable direct/referral channel and reliable contract manufacturing/refurbishment;
- no unresolved material regulatory or user-safety issue;
- twelve months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If dentists prefer generic sensors or will not retain, stop before production tooling. Reusable assets include:

- original enclosure/firmware/embedded assignments;
- generic IMU/haptic synchronization and calibration tools;
- lawful, non-patient procedure-motion protocols and aggregate findings;
- supplier, adviser, and professional relationships;
- experience in leased wearable cleaning, fit, failure, and support.

No patient data is retained. Moving into diagnosis, workers' compensation, hospital procurement, another profession, or a custom sensor is a new candidate requiring fresh evaluation.

## Critical Evidence

- Dental wearable and personalised biofeedback studies support technical feasibility.
- EU-OSHA identifies awkward/static posture as an occupational risk factor.
- Eurostat supplies a countable EU dentist denominator.
- UPRIGHT GO proves a cheap generic substitute, making the differentiation test concrete rather than theoretical.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Procedure-aware silence/cue timing is meaningfully better than generic alerts.
- Ten professionals will prepay 599 PLN and at least 60% of an early cohort will continue.
- Two modules provide sufficient context without cameras or clinic integration.
- Device/support economics permit at least 62% gross margin.
- 4,200 EU seats can be acquired without institutional procurement or celebrity endorsement.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than ten paid leases after 150 qualified direct prospects.
- Fewer than 70% of paid users complete four weeks.
- Fewer than 30% of completers continue or annual-prepay; after 50 users, six-month retention below 60%.
- Procedure-aware cueing is not preferred to a generic fixed-angle reminder.
- More than one reported procedure disruption per 100 sessions or any unresolved safety concern.
- Hardware/support cost makes 62% mature gross margin implausible.
- A medical-device or clinical-evidence burden arises before direct paid proof under the intended purpose.
- Customer acquisition exceeds first-year gross profit after three direct/referral tests.
