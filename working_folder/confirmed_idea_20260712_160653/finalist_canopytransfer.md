# Exact Candidate: CanopyTransfer

Evaluate this exact business shape.

Founder constraints: Warsaw data scientist, five hours/day, unknown founder with no privileged introductions, maximum 100,000 PLN founder capital for a 12-month POC. A paid arborist, landscape architect, insured tree-moving contractor, and nursery/aftercare contractor are allowed per job. The founder does not perform tree surgery or promise permit outcomes.

**CanopyTransfer** controls transplantable mature urban trees before construction projects destroy them, then sells the exact living tree, move window, and two-year aftercare contract to premium developments, hotels, campuses, municipal/public-realm contractors, and industrial parks that otherwise buy expensive nursery-grown specimen trees or wait years for canopy. It is not a generic landscaper, tree nursery, carbon-credit project, arborist marketplace, or tree-removal service.

The hidden market mistake is that the seller and buyer see opposite waste streams. A developer with an approved removal plan pays to fell and dispose of a healthy 18–35 cm trunk-diameter tree. A second project pays a large premium for immediate visible canopy and established character. The valuable unit is not “a mature tree” in a catalog; it is a named tree with species, dimensions, health/root-zone survey, legal removal/transplant route, source-site access, dormant-season move window, destination soil/access fit, priced move plan, and reserved buyer.

CanopyTransfer mines public tree-removal permit notices, environmental decisions, redevelopment announcements, and arborist/demolition referrals 6–18 months ahead. A paid arborist screens only transplant-tolerant species and accessible root zones. The company signs an exclusive, site-specific rescue/title agreement over named trees, conditional on permit and buyer reservation, that gives the source owner a fixed deadline and releases it if no buyer closes. The source owner pays nothing and avoids part of removal/disposal work; CanopyTransfer does not pay speculative tree purchase prices.

Buyer acquisition starts from public landscape tenders, premium residential/hotel/office projects under construction, and 100 named landscape architects/general contractors in Mazowieckie and Łódzkie. Each listing is a finite, surveyed tree with delivered price and a defined dormant-season move. The company exercises control and books the mover only after the buyer signs, pays at least 40%, approves destination access/soil preparation, and accepts a written survival protocol. The mover and aftercare contractor are paid from the reservation. CanopyTransfer retains 15% of the price as a two-year aftercare/survival reserve and replaces or refunds only under narrowly defined contractor-controlled failure conditions.

The primary control point is the exclusive rescue/title agreement over a named living asset during its construction deadline, coupled with buyer-funded physical custody and the move/aftercare record. The 12-month proof is 50 screened trees, ten exclusive named-tree agreements, five buyer reservations, three completed moves, two growing-season survivals, and at least 35% contribution margin after arborist, permits, moving, aftercare, failures, acquisition, and founder time. Founder capital is capped at 90,000 PLN: 15,000 surveys, 10,000 legal/insurance, 15,000 buyer/source acquisition, 10,000 monitoring/soil tools, and 40,000 survival/contingency reserve. No move is booked without buyer funds.

Illustrative economics to test, not facts: delivered project price 25,000–80,000 PLN per tree, direct move/site/aftercare cost 12,000–45,000 PLN, and minimum 35% contribution margin. Kill if buyers will not reserve named trees, fewer than 20% of screened trees are technically movable, permit timing misses dormant windows, two-year survival is below 85%, or established nurseries/movers can source equivalent trees without CanopyTransfer.

The EU Nature Restoration Regulation’s urban tree-cover goals are a long-term tailwind, not the immediate sales claim. The domination path is a dense, advance pipeline of source rights and buyer specifications by metro area, followed by portfolio rescue agreements with developers and demonstrated survival/outcome data. Local logistics and site-specific rights prevent a foreign marketplace from supplying the same tree, but regional arborists and nurseries are the strongest copy threat.
