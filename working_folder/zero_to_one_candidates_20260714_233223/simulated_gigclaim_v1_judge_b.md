# Zero-to-One Evaluation: GigClaim Fleet Recovery Rail

## Diagnosis

**Score: 60/100.** The proposal has a sharp aggregation insight, coherent beachhead, excellent kill criteria, and a genuinely reachable paid experiment. However, it proves only that fleet data and public buyer lists exist—not that discrepancies are frequent, fleets economically benefit from recovery, worker authorization is durable, or the proposed human-reviewed service can sustain 78% gross and 32% operating margins. The fleet/worker incentive conflict threatens both the first sale and the supposed control point, while the expected founder-wealth case relies on unsupported scale and margins.

Official sources do support recurring fleet reports containing driver earnings and transaction details, and the directive’s explanation/human-review rights. Those facts establish data availability and timing, not willingness to pay or attractive economics. [Bolt fleet reports](https://bolt.eu/pl-pl/support/articles/360012267794/), [Directive (EU) 2024/2831](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024L2831)

## Strongest Objections and Caveats

1. **Buyer–beneficiary conflict:** the fleet pays the fee, while much recovered cash likely belongs to workers. The proposal does not show that dispatcher savings, retention, or vehicle utilization exceed the fleet’s 204,000 PLN annual fee in its mature-account example.
2. **Wealth case is mislabeled “expected”:** 50 fleets, 30,000 opted-in workers, 15 million PLN annual recovery, 78% gross margin, and 32% operating margin require several unvalidated assumptions to hold simultaneously.
3. **Manual-review economics:** human approval, multilingual evidence collection, support follow-up, privacy operations, and disputed attribution could make the claimed 75 PLN annual variable cost per covered worker materially too low.
4. **Distribution is accessible, not repeatable:** an observable lead list does not prove that unknown-founder outbound can win sensitive payroll/dispute mandates at acceptable CAC.
5. **Rights may not compound cleanly:** revocable worker authorization and privacy limits may restrict retention or cross-client model use; pseudonymization alone does not automatically remove data-protection obligations.
6. **Platform behavior is uncontrolled:** delayed, inconsistent, or low-correction support outcomes can destroy the success-fee pool and dataset quality.

## Control Point

**Tier 2 as designed, but unevidenced.** A signed all-dispute routing mandate plus derivative-data rights could create a repeatably accessible workflow and historical switching costs. It is not Tier 1 because no mandate has been secured, worker authorization is revocable, customers control submissions, platforms control outcomes, and the data are not yet scarce. Until a recurring mandate with reusable rights exists, the practical control resembles Tier 3.

**Strongest copy risk:** an installed fleet/payroll system adds cross-platform reconciliation and appeal tracking using data it already ingests, or a major platform improves native explanations and review. Either can copy the operational wedge through a routine product addition before GigClaim accumulates meaningful closed-outcome history.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The 4× operating-profit valuation is not itself heroic, but the profit underneath it is. The expected case assumes 30,000 covered workers, 500 PLN recovered per worker annually, 78% gross margin, 32% operating margin, 80% founder ownership, and minimal outside capital without evidence for market capacity, authorization, retention, service labor, CAC, or cash requirements. The proposal therefore presents a mathematical path above 5 million PLN, not yet a credible expected-success path.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass, narrowly.** The 5,000 PLN prepaid, 50-worker audit can be sold before a full build; pre-payment exposure is capped at 18,000 PLN, the 180-day plan totals 28,000 PLN, and no platform partnership or employment exit is required. Counsel clearance and one fleet willing to expose fleet-caused errors are serious dependencies, but they are directly testable within the capital and time limits. This passes only for obtaining paid evidence, not for demonstrating recurring demand.

## Fatal Objection

**The proposal does not establish a rational economic buyer.** If recovered cash primarily benefits workers while the fleet funds the service and accepts exposure of its own settlement errors, fleets may rationally refuse both the recurring fee and all-dispute mandate. Without measured fleet-retained value exceeding the fee, the first buyer, recurring revenue, routed workflow, dataset, defensibility, and wealth model all fail together.

## Recommendation

**Research further.** The Reachability Gate permits the capped paid test, but the Founder Wealth Gate and economic-buyer logic prohibit a `pursue` verdict as written.
