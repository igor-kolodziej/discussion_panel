# Zero-to-One Evaluation: IdleFleet Recovery Rail Pivot

Advisory evaluation only — not a simulated or real validation score.

## Diagnosis

IdleFleet correctly removes GigClaim's fatal payer-beneficiary mismatch: the fleet pays only for fleet-owned rental/management revenue and asset utilization. The new wedge remains reachable and the expected wealth arithmetic can clear 5m PLN without venture capital. However, the pivot replaces the known mismatch with an unproved loss pool: there is no evidence that account, onboarding, and reassignment states cause four avoidable idle days per vehicle per year or that an independent workflow can reduce them. The route is worth decision-focused customer research but is not panel-ready because the pain magnitude, buyer budget, and installed-system gap are still assertions.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- **Evidence:** Public Bolt material establishes fleet owners, managed workers/vehicles, operational reports, and a reachable fleet list. Directive (EU) 2024/2831 supports explanation and human review of consequential account decisions.
- **Preserved panel verdict:** Both GigClaim judges found fleet aggregation and a manual paid test useful but rejected worker-recovery economics.
- **Hypothesis:** A 300-vehicle fleet suffers 75 avoidable idle days monthly from platform/assignment states, with at least 150 PLN fleet-owned value per day.
- **Hypothesis:** The workflow—not ordinary replacement, a fleet portal, or staff escalation—can avoid four days per vehicle annually at expected scale.
- **Hypothesis:** Eighty CEE fleets averaging 400 monitored vehicles can be acquired and retained at 10 PLN/vehicle/month plus 10% of verified restored value.
- **Missing facts:** No fleet idle-day distribution, causal breakdown, current resolution time, fleet-owned daily margin, pilot payment, installed-software comparison, or data-right precedent is sourced.

## Overall Score

**71/100.** Weighted points are 142.5 across 20 weight points: `10 × (142.5 / 20) = 71.25`, rounded to 71. Advisory only.

## Control Point

**Tier 2 if contracted; otherwise Tier 3.** An idle-asset routing mandate and closed platform-review→reassignment→rental-billing outcomes are directly contractable and can create switching cost. The strongest copy risk is an installed fleet-management, rental, or payroll platform adding an idle-asset queue and cross-platform case tracking using data it already receives. Without paid repeated outcomes, the proposed graph is not scarce.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, narrowly on the written model.** Conservative, expected, and strong scenarios state customers/volume, pricing, revenue, margins, capital, ownership/dilution, reinvestment, debt, value, and timing. Expected success uses 80 fleets, 32,000 vehicles, 5.76m PLN revenue, 30% operating margin, 80% ownership, 200k debt, and 4× operating profit to produce 5.37m PLN founder equity by year 6. Retained earnings are not double counted. The decisive weakness is that four avoidable idle days per vehicle and 75%/30% margins are unproved; the model has little cushion above the threshold.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** One 5,000 PLN audit covering 300 vehicles and a 60-day baseline can be sold before automation. Pre-proof risk stays below 18,000 PLN, the 180-day cap is 30,000 PLN, and the founder remains employed. No platform integration, legal representation, vehicle ownership, or speculative partnership is required. The audit tests the existential dependencies directly.

## Expansion Check

The Polish multi-platform fleet beachhead is narrow and observable. Reassignment and onboarding evidence are adjacent; delivery and CEE add local resets. Fleet systems and platforms have superior source access, so the business must prove that cross-platform outcome learning and independent routing beat a routine internal queue. Local knowledge helps acquisition and labeling but is not exclusionary.

## Fatal Objections

1. Avoidable platform-caused idle days may be rare; ordinary driver replacement or document correction may solve most loss before it becomes material.
2. A fleet-management system can copy the queue and already owns customer trust and operational data.
3. Personal-data and worker-fairness constraints may limit reusable outcome rights even though fees are fleet-owned.
4. Expected founder wealth has little margin for lower price, fewer fleets, or more human review.
5. No evidence distinguishes platform review latency from fleet operational delay, so attribution and success fees may cause disputes.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

A fleet will rationally pay to route algorithmic-decision cases only when the measured economic outcome is an asset/revenue loss the fleet itself owns.
