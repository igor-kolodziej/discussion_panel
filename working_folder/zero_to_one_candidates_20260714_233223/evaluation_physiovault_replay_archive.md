# Zero-to-One Evaluation: PhysioVault Replay Archive

## Diagnosis

PhysioVault identifies a real technical distinction between retaining files and preserving the executable path from raw physiological data to a reported clinical endpoint. The problem is that the candidate has regulatory support for retention but no market evidence that sponsors will fund a separate replay layer, entrust it to an unknown provider, or pay the proposed recurring custody price when archive, CRO, and internal data teams already exist. The business could produce attractive economics if replay becomes a contracted recurring requirement, but as written its control point is non-exclusive and its delivery risks remaining bespoke clinical-data engineering. Both hard gates are structurally satisfied, but the evidence is not strong enough for a pursue recommendation.

## Evidence and Assumptions

**Sourced evidence**

- EU Clinical Trials Regulation Article 58 establishes a long clinical-trial master-file retention obligation.
- FDA computerized-systems guidance explicitly addresses retrieval from discontinued systems and preservation of relevant computational and extraction methods.
- FDA guidance confirms that DHTs are used for remote clinical-trial data acquisition.
- Arkivum explicitly markets long-term preservation of raw clinical and wearable data; Veeva provides study archival. These prove an existing archival market but also show that trusted incumbents are close to the proposed workflow.

**Candidate hypotheses**

- Sponsors experience an unfunded gap between archival integrity and executable endpoint replay.
- A small sponsor will prepay 30,000–40,000 PLN for a non-production replay mirror within 180 days.
- Sponsors will designate the package as a canonical computational reference and accept external custody.
- Mature migrations support 120,000–180,000 PLN pricing, while annual custody supports 45,000–75,000 PLN per study.
- At least 60% of adapter work becomes reusable and annual replay attaches often enough to produce 68–80% gross margins.
- Forty-five retained studies are attainable without a prohibitive pharmaceutical trust or procurement burden.

**Consequential missing facts**

- No buyer evidence establishes the frequency or financial cost of failed endpoint reconstruction.
- No evidence shows that Arkivum, CROs, DHT vendors, or sponsor validation teams omit executable replay or would not add it routinely.
- Procurement duration, security qualification, professional-liability cost, custody terms, and willingness to accept a new vendor are unknown.
- No observed migration hours, adapter reuse, renewal behavior, CAC, or paid price supports the model.

There are no prior evaluator or chatbot verdicts in the candidate, and this advisory evaluation is not a validation result.

## Overall Score

**55/100.** Weighted points are 110 across a total weight of 20: `110 ÷ 20 = 5.50`; multiplied by 10 gives 55. The score is capped naturally below 80 because a trusted archive or CRO could copy the replay workflow through routine product development and an existing customer relationship.

## Control Point

**Tier 2, contingent on an actual signed first-sale agreement.** The candidate proposes prepaid custody, designation as the canonical computational replay reference, owned generic parsers, and recurring integrity checks. Those elements are directly contractable and can create study-specific switching costs, but they are non-exclusive and do not control the sponsor’s official archive, source vendor, regulatory workflow, or underlying algorithm rights.

The strongest copy risk is Arkivum, a CRO, or another established archive provider adding executable replay and version manifests to its existing migration and retention contract. Accumulated adapters improve delivery speed but do not make incumbent catch-up structurally difficult.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, with material evidence risk.** The candidate supplies conservative, expected, and strong scenarios with studies, pricing, revenue, gross and operating margins, capital, ownership, dilution, reinvestment, liabilities, value, distributions, and timing. It models 95% reinvestment while employed in conservative and expected cases, 100% in strong success, caps employment-period distributions, nets debt from value, and separates post-transition distributions from retained business value.

The conservative case reaches approximately 5.39m PLN only after 45 retained studies, 12 annual migrations, a 4.5× EBIT multiple, and 1.1m PLN of post-transition distributions by year 9. That scale is not inherently heroic across Europe, but it is not yet supported by evidence that sponsors pay separately or that delivery can productize. The decisive weakness is recurring willingness to pay, not arithmetic completeness.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass, but fragile.** The candidate caps pre-proof founder risk at 38,000 PLN, retains corporate employment, builds a bounded demonstrator, and seeks a 50% deposit on a 30,000–40,000 PLN non-production replay package before customer-specific work. It does not require a clinic, proprietary device partnership, official archive replacement, or complete production platform before paid proof.

The critical dependencies are sponsor access rights, availability of transformation logic, security and privacy review, procurement speed, and trust in a new custodian. A paid technical mirror inside 180 days is credible enough for the gate, but failure to obtain a deposit from the first 120 event-qualified accounts would quickly invalidate reachability.

## Expansion Check

The initial niche—closing European small-biotech studies using wearable-derived endpoints—is narrow, visible through registries, and technically aligned with the founder. Expansion within sponsor portfolios is more credible than immediately adding unrelated modalities because the installed customer relationship and custody history provide the main switching cost.

Large archive providers, CROs, and DHT platforms can move into the niche more easily than PhysioVault can move into their broader regulated workflows. Poland improves cost, technical staffing, and EU data-processing credibility, but the business can serve Europe profitably only if adapter reuse and remote delivery materially outweigh regulated sales and assurance costs.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The paid gap is unproved:** retention obligations and archival demand do not establish a separate budget for executable physiological replay.
2. **Incumbents are structurally closer:** Arkivum and CRO/archive vendors already control buyer trust, migration, and long-term custody and can add the feature through routine development.
3. **The first sale is trust-heavy:** a new part-time provider must pass security, privacy, GxP-positioning, liability, procurement, and custody scrutiny before seeing sensitive trial data.
4. **Economics may remain service-like:** vendor-specific exports and missing proprietary transformations can keep migration labor bespoke and prevent the assumed gross margins.
5. **Recurring value is unclear:** once a package is successfully reconstructed and stored, annual 45,000–75,000 PLN replay spend may be hard to justify.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Clinical-trial evidence can remain technically unreadable or irreproducible even when every required file has been retained; the valuable artifact is an independently restorable raw-to-endpoint computation, not storage alone.
