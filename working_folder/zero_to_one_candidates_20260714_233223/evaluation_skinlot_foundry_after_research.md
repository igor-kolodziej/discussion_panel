# Zero-to-One Evaluation: SkinLot Foundry After Public Research

## Diagnosis

The public-only follow-up makes SkinLot structurally weaker, not stronger. Qualified medical converters publicly accept no-MOQ or low-dollar custom work with 10-day to three-week lead times, while Flexcon already combines wearable skin-contact adhesives with customer-specific stock, purchase commitments, call-off sizes, min/max levels, and inventory held for release; Hisco/Precision Converting combines ISO 13485 medical converting with vendor-managed inventory. The claimed continuity control point is therefore not merely easy to copy—it substantially overlaps existing supplier and converter capabilities. Public sources still do not disclose a like-for-like CEE price for exact-lot segregation or buyer willingness to pay, so the remaining commercial question cannot be resolved without non-public transaction evidence; the candidate should remain parked rather than pursued or treated as validated.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Decision-critical public evidence

- [Rapid Die Cut](https://rapiddiecut.com/industries/medical) explicitly covers medical and wearable-sensor parts, states **no minimum order quantity**, and publishes a standard **10-day** lead time for custom parts.
- [Strouse's medical offer](https://www.strouse.com/solutions/medical-device-attachment) covers wearable-device attachment under ISO 13485 and FDA registration. Its [FAQ](https://www.strouse.com/faq) says there is no official MOQ, although complex orders may carry a **USD 500–1,000 press minimum** and hard tooling may cost hundreds to thousands of dollars. This is not a complete comparison-pack quote, but it proves that an established qualified converter can transact at small scale.
- [ADDEV Parafix](https://parafix.com/faqs/) keeps its minimum order value private but publishes **2–3 days** for slit rolls from stocked materials and **2–3 weeks** for rotary-converted parts. It offers bespoke samples, prototypes with or without tooling, ISO 13485, Class 7 cleanroom capability, Certificates of Conformity/PPAP support, and customer-tool storage until 24 months of inactivity.
- German [SMS Converting](https://www.sm-selbstklebetechnik.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SMS-Converting-Focus-and-Products-HD.pdf) advertises one-off units, prototypes, small production runs, **no MOQ**, more than ten material suppliers, and a one-day inquiry response. Polish [CVGS](https://www.glasstec.de/vis-content/event-glasstec2024/exh-glasstec2024.2780576/glasstec-2024-CVGS-Pro-sp.-z-o.o.-sp.-k.-Paper-glasstec2024.2780576-TP92OPkVQomwkpyCnUqcug.pdf) publishes a **1–2 week** prototype lead time and controlled raw-material/finished-goods warehousing. These two are not proven substitutes for ISO 13485 skin-contact production, but they make local geometry, prototyping, and controlled storage ordinary purchasable services.
- [Walker Tape Converting](https://www.walkertapeconverting.com/explaining-small-orders/) discloses a minimum equivalent to one **3-inch × 360-yard roll** for material held in-house and explains that non-stock material may require the customer to absorb the supplier's full minimum. This supports the existence of source-material MOQ friction, while also showing that stockholding converters already reduce that friction with shared inventory.
- [Flexcon's healthcare portfolio](https://www.flexcon.com/markets/healthcare) includes wearable skin-contact adhesives for glucose monitors, insulin pumps, and cardiac monitors. Its [Customer Special Stock Agreement](https://www.flexcon.com/capabilities/customer-special-stock) sets annual volumes, call-off sizes, and min/max levels, then produces and holds inventory for release on demand. Flexcon says custom quoting can take as little as 24 hours, custom orders as little as eight days, and [special stock requires a purchase commitment](https://www.flexcon.com/insights/standard-vs-custom-which-is-appropriate). A representative [DermaFlex wearable adhesive](https://www.flexcon.com/products/43160/dermaflexdfm-100-clear-h-566-80-df-pfw) includes six-month change notification. This covers most of SkinLot's continuity mechanism at the upstream material supplier, although it does not publicly promise one named lot.
- [Precision Converting/Hisco](https://www.precisionconverting.com/single-source-solution) advertises ISO 13485/FDA-registered medical conversion, specialized material repackaging, 37 stocking locations, and vendor-managed inventory. The [Shurmed acquisition release](https://www.hisco.com/userfiles/pdf/pr_shurmed.pdf) ties the same group to medical adhesive applications, 3M-preferred converting, VMI/RFID programs, specialized warehousing, chemical management, and cold storage.
- Public material-change evidence supports pain but not SkinLot's monetization. Flexcon offers change notification; [Screentec](https://screentec.com/articles/2026/06/biocompatibility-in-wearable-medical-devices-complete-guide-for-developers/) states that even minor post-biocompatibility material substitutions can cause additional testing and delay; [Strukmyer's provider case study](https://strukmyer.com/case-studies/custom-adhesive-converter-by-strukmyer/) shows wearable adhesive qualification and lot traceability inside an incumbent converter.

### What public research did not resolve

- No independent buyer case study was found showing that a small wearable OEM paid separately to reserve one exact adhesive lot, could not obtain continuity from an incumbent, or suffered a quantified loss specifically because an exact lot was unavailable.
- No like-for-like CEE quote covers the same manufacturer/product code, exact geometry, 50–5,000 units, single-lot segregation, retained samples, non-substitution, documentation, replenishment reservation, landed price, and lead time.
- No public source validates the proposed 8,000–15,000 PLN comparison-pack price, 18,000–35,000 PLN reservation price, 12,000–30,000 PLN annual continuity fee, 35–60% reservation gross margin, or 60–85% continuity gross margin.
- No public denominator supports 45 recurring programs, five to seven new durable design-ins per year, the stated CAC, repeat-call-off rate, inventory turns, or expiry/write-off assumptions.
- Company pages prove advertised capability, not acceptable CEE commercial terms, delivery performance, buyer satisfaction, or actual willingness to pay. Public research is therefore adverse competitive evidence, not validation.

## Overall Score

**30/100.** The weights sum to **20.0**. Weighted points are **60.5**, so the calculation is `10 × (60.5 / 20.0) = 30.25`, rounded to **30**. The decline from the prior 38 reflects new evidence that special stock, call-offs, change notification, small custom orders, and managed inventory are existing capabilities rather than a merely hypothetical copy response.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** SkinLot can physically own and segregate a named lot after payment, but it does not control a scarce source or exclusive transaction path. The strongest copy risk is no longer hypothetical: Flexcon already produces wearable medical adhesives and offers committed special stock with call-off sizes and min/max inventory, while Hisco/Precision Converting pairs regulated medical conversion with VMI and specialized warehousing. Adding a customer-specific lot identifier, retained sample, and non-substitution clause is a routine quality-agreement extension for organizations that already possess stock, QMS, supplier access, storage, and conversion infrastructure.

The candidate's remaining distinction is the full bundle applied to very small CEE programs. No public source proves that the exact bundle is available at acceptable CEE cost and lead time, but absence of a public price is not control. Customer-specific tooling is also weak: Parafix already stores customer tools for repeat work, and tooling ownership can be assigned by contract.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The conservative, expected, and strong scenarios remain arithmetically coherent and correctly model ownership, debt, distributions, and 95%/100% reinvestment. The conservative case produces about 2.52m PLN of founder net worth and misses the gate. The expected case nominally produces about 7.24m PLN by years 7–8 through 45 programs averaging 200,000 PLN, 9.0m PLN revenue, 42% gross margin, 23% operating margin, 85% founder ownership, and a 4× EBIT valuation.

Public research makes the expected case less credible. Qualified converters already accept no-MOQ or low-dollar custom work; European incumbents publish short lead times; an upstream wearable-adhesive supplier already sells committed call-off stock; and a medical-converting group already offers VMI. The 45-account count, premium prices, 40%+ blended margin, continuity-fee retention, five-to-seven annual design-ins, and 4× value remain unsupported. The scenario therefore assumes that SkinLot captures high-margin revenue for services that stronger suppliers already provide or can contractually add, without evidence of a distribution or cost advantage.

There is no double-counting problem; the failure is the absence of a non-heroic commercial path. Founder Wealth Gate failure prohibits `pursue` independently of reachability.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass, narrowly.** The candidate can still seek a paid 8,000–15,000 PLN comparison pack, deposit, non-cancellable reservation, or cash-backed purchase order within 180 days while risking 32,000 PLN before paid proof and retaining employment. The first exact roll and customer tool are deferred until cash clears, so the proof does not require a factory, full QMS build, leaving employment, or speculative multi-roll inventory.

Public research increases the burden of proof but does not increase the proposed capital beyond 40,000 PLN. The buyer must choose SkinLot after seeing a real comparable incumbent option; a payment obtained only because alternatives were not disclosed would not establish the claimed advantage. The existential dependencies remain buyer access, authentic material terms, a competent R&D converter, legal/insurance boundaries, and a buyer willing to pay an unknown intermediary despite credible direct channels.

This pass is strictly about testability. No public evidence constitutes paid proof, and the public-only route cannot resolve willingness to pay.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead remains narrowly described around a near-term wearable build, 50–5,000 units, and no economical internal conversion route. Its expansion path—repeat batches, full patch stacks, shared inventory, regional stockholding, and controlled conversion—is commercially adjacent.

It is not asymmetrically favorable. Flexcon can move downstream from its wearable adhesive portfolio and special-stock agreements; Hisco/Precision Converting can move across from VMI and medical conversion; BDK, Gergonne, Parafix, Stokvis, JBC, Strouse, and Rapid Die Cut can add or expose inventory terms to existing prototype-to-production offers. Poland lowers shipping and language friction but does not prevent these moves, and Polish/nearby converters already shorten geometry and warehousing lead time. Expansion therefore increases direct competition faster than it creates a moat.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The control point substantially exists.** Wearable-adhesive special stock, call-off sizing, purchase commitments, change notification, medical-converter VMI, specialized warehousing, and repeat-tool storage are all publicly advertised by incumbents.
2. **Small-order access is not structurally absent.** Qualified medical converters publish no MOQ or low press minimums and custom lead times from 10 days to three weeks; European general converters publish no MOQ and short prototype lead times.
3. **No buyer willingness to pay is public.** The search found no independent buyer case showing payment for an exact-lot reservation or quantified loss from unavailable exact-lot stock.
4. **The price and margin wedge is ungrounded.** No like-for-like comparison supports SkinLot's pack, reservation, continuity, or recurring-account pricing after qualified conversion, materials, storage, quality, expiry, freight, insurance, and CAC.
5. **The wealth path remains heroic.** Forty-five recurring programs, 200,000 PLN annual spend, 42% gross margin, 23% operating margin, and five to seven durable wins per year have no market denominator or transaction cohort.
6. **Distribution is not a control point.** Event-driven outbound is a test method, not evidence that an unknown part-time intermediary can displace established supplier and converter relationships at acceptable CAC and cycle time.
7. **The founder lacks the incumbent trust stack.** The business must buy conversion, QMS, regulatory, insurance, and contracting competence while competing with firms that already own those capabilities.

These objections veto `pursue`. They are strongly adverse but do not conclusively satisfy the candidate's exact kill criterion because no public source supplies a complete like-for-like CEE quote with single-lot reservation and acceptable total cost.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The insight worth preserving is that a post-selection material change can create more cost than the material itself because wear, biological-risk, and device evidence may need review or repetition. Public research supports that pain, but it also shows that incumbent suppliers and converters already understand and service continuity; SkinLot has not shown that it can own the transaction.
