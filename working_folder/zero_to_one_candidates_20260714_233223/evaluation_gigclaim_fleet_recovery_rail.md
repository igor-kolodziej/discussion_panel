# Zero-to-One Evaluation: GigClaim Fleet Recovery Rail

Advisory evaluation only — not a simulated or real validation score.

## Diagnosis

GigClaim contains an unusually coherent wedge for an unknown founder: one fleet aggregates many users, a manual paid audit can prove value before software, and the proposed mandate can route a repeated algorithmic-management event without cooperation from the platforms. The strongest element is not regulatory consulting or appeal generation; it is the closed claim→reason→review→settlement loop and the contractual right to learn from it. The largest uncertainty is commercial rather than technical: neither fleet-scale claim losses nor willingness to pay 20 PLN per worker plus recovery fees is yet evidenced, and a fleet may have conflicted incentives when worker discrepancies reveal its own settlement errors. The shape is worth an independent panel because both hard gates pass on a testable plan, but the outcome-data control is Tier 2 and must be earned through live contracts.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- **Sourced evidence:** Directive (EU) 2024/2831 creates portability, transparency, human-oversight, and review rights for algorithmically managed platform work and requires transposition by 2 December 2026.
- **Sourced evidence:** Bolt publicly operates a Polish fleet-owner channel, exposes worker-level earnings/performance information to fleet partners, and maintains a recommended-fleet route. This supports buyer observability and data availability, not willingness to pay.
- **Sourced evidence:** Eurostat shows platform work is a material labor category. FareShare demonstrates technical feasibility of data-assisted missing-wage and deactivation claims and simultaneously proves generic claim assembly is copyable.
- **Founder fact:** The founder's AI/analytics, behavioral-data, and business-analysis background fits anomaly detection, evidence assembly, and human-facing explanations; the first batch can fit 25 hours per week.
- **Hypothesis:** A fleet experiences at least 5,000 PLN monthly of recoverable loss or handling burden, 70% of workers authorize the flow, and expected scale can reach 50 fleets averaging 600 covered workers.
- **Hypothesis:** Pseudonymized claim and outcome derivatives can be retained lawfully and ethically under a counsel-reviewed structure. A worker's consent alone may not be the correct legal basis in every relationship.
- **Missing fact:** No paid buyer, sourced fleet-size denominator, baseline recovery rate, claim-frequency distribution, or tested authorization rate exists yet.

## Overall Score

**81/100.** Weighted component total: 161.5 across 20 total weight points; `10 × (161.5 / 20) = 80.75`, rounded to 81. This is an advisory evaluator score only and does not satisfy any validation gate.

## Control Point

**Tier 2.** A signed all-dispute routing mandate, explicit worker/fleet authorization, and retained pseudonymized outcomes are contractually provable within 12 months and create switching costs. They are non-exclusive and can be withdrawn; the startup does not control the platform's review channel. The strongest copy risk is a fleet-management/payroll vendor or a platform adding cross-statement reconciliation and dispute tracking, then using its installed data access to eliminate the overlay. Catch-up becomes difficult only after GigClaim accumulates cross-platform closed outcomes and a trusted worker explanation history.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, with a major volume/evidence caveat.** The conservative case is economically coherent but below the threshold. The expected case models 30,000 covered workers across 50 fleets, 10.2m PLN revenue, 78% gross margin, 32% operating margin, 80% founder ownership, a 4× EBIT valuation, and 300k PLN liability; founder equity is approximately 10.2m PLN by year 6. The strong case is not needed to pass. The model correctly applies 95% reinvestment while employed, counts no optional distribution, deducts debt, and does not add retained earnings twice. The decisive weakness is that 600 workers per fleet, 500 PLN annual recoverable value per worker, and 50 reachable accounts are not yet sourced; they are validation targets rather than facts.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** A 5,000 PLN prepaid, 50-worker audit can be sold through public fleet contacts before a mobile app, platform API, legal-claims service, or automated system exists. Maximum pre-paid-proof exposure is 18,000 PLN and the first 180-day plan stays at 28,000 PLN, below the 40,000 PLN gate. The founder can batch one pilot alongside employment. The existential dependencies—buyer payment, lawful authorization, measurable recovery, and derivative-data rights—are tested in that transaction rather than deferred to an unspecified partner.

## Expansion Check

The Polish ride-hail fleet beachhead is coherent because the fleet controls settlement support and aggregates acquisition. Delivery fleets and CEE expansion reuse the same algorithmic-decision and portability primitives. The local advantage is temporary, not regulatory exclusivity; a platform or installed fleet/payroll system can enter. Expansion should be allowed only after claim recurrence, worker authorization, and positive contribution are demonstrated, because broad worker-rights software would lose the narrow economic loop.

## Fatal Objections

1. **Unproved buyer alignment:** A fleet may not want an independent system exposing errors attributable to itself, and may prefer fewer worker claims rather than higher worker recoveries.
2. **Unproved unit value:** The 500 PLN recovered per worker and 20 PLN recurring price determine both economics and retention but have no live baseline.
3. **Data-right fragility:** Worker authorization, fleet authority, GDPR legal basis, and reusable derivatives may not align cleanly; consent in a power-imbalanced work setting can be invalid.
4. **Platform-controlled resolution:** GigClaim can improve evidence but cannot compel a correction, explanation, or timely human review, especially before national implementation.
5. **Incumbent access advantage:** Platforms and fleet/payroll systems possess richer source data and existing distribution.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

One fleet buyer aggregates a repeated portfolio of economically small algorithmic-work disputes, allowing an outcome-priced manual service to acquire cross-platform claim→decision→settlement data before software or platform cooperation.
