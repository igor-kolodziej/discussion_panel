# Zero-to-One Evaluation: SkinLot Foundry

## Diagnosis

SkinLot identifies a real operational failure mode—losing access to an already selected skin-contact construction can delay a study or force repeat evaluation—but it has not shown that buyers lack existing prototype-to-production converter routes. The newly supplied incumbent evidence is materially adverse: five established converters publicly offer medical or skin-contact material selection, rapid or tooling-free prototyping, custom conversion, and scale-up, often with ISO 13485 systems, cleanrooms, stocked materials, and established supplier relationships. The proposed control point is therefore Tier 3: prepaid named-lot stock is tangible, but the inventory is not shown to be scarce and an incumbent could add reservation, retention, and call-off terms through a routine contracting and warehouse process. The 180-day test is capital-efficient enough to run while employed, but the expected founder-wealth path depends on unvalidated account count, pricing, margins, retention, and differentiation, so the candidate is not ready to pursue.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Evidence in the candidate

- The customer and event are concrete: a European wearable team needing 50–5,000 units for a wear study, verification build, clinical investigation, field pilot, or early commercial bridge.
- The proposed paid wedge is specific: an 8,000–15,000 PLN comparison pack, an 18,000–35,000 PLN named-lot reservation, controlled geometry, retained samples, non-substitution, and a call-off schedule.
- Capital discipline is unusually explicit. Founder spend is capped at 32,000 PLN before paid proof, 40,000 PLN after a deposit for the first exact-material purchase and tool, and 100,000 PLN in total founder capital.
- The candidate correctly separates component documentation from final-device biological evaluation and regulatory responsibility.
- The founder-wealth scenarios are complete enough to audit and their internal arithmetic is substantially correct:
  - Conservative: 2.5m parts × 1.45 PLN + 375k PLN = 4.0m PLN revenue; 560k EBIT × 3.5 less 200k debt = 1.76m PLN equity; 92% founder ownership plus 900k distributions gives about 2.52m PLN.
  - Expected: 5.0m parts × 1.65 PLN + 750k PLN = 9.0m PLN revenue; 2.07m EBIT × 4.0 less 350k debt = 7.93m PLN equity; 85% founder ownership plus 500k distributions gives about 7.24m PLN.
  - Strong: 12m parts × 1.65 PLN + 1.8m PLN = 21.6m PLN revenue; 6.26m EBIT × 5.0 less 1.2m debt = 30.1m PLN equity; 70% founder ownership gives about 21.1m PLN.
- The conservative and expected scenarios follow the required 95% reinvestment rule while the founder remains employed; the strong scenario uses 100% reinvestment. Retained earnings are not visibly double-counted.

### New external competition evidence

The sources below are official company pages. They establish advertised capabilities, not transacted price, actual lead time, lot-reservation terms, or buyer satisfaction.

- [BDK](https://www.bdk.uk.com/) advertises a full-service medical and adhesive-converting offer with prototyping, pilot production, subcontract manufacturing, cleanroom production with bioburden monitoring, ISO 13485 certification, FDA registration, preferred or authorized relationships with major tape suppliers, and more than 300 materials stocked. This directly weakens the premise that production converters leave prototype and pilot work categorically unserved.
- [Gergonne](https://www.gergonne.com/en/solutions-per-market/medical/die-cut-components/) describes itself as a European leader with ISO 13485 certification, material selection, laboratory, design, prototyping and regulatory support, plus plotter and continuous laser cutting without tooling. Its offer covers the candidate's material-selection-to-custom-geometry bridge and removes tooling as a necessary low-volume barrier.
- [ADDEV Parafix](https://parafix.com/product/laird-8300-0038-44/) advertises rapid prototyping, CAD, plotter and laser cutting, contract converting, medical-grade materials, ISO 13485 and cleanroom capability, and access to multiple major material manufacturers. Its site confirms that it has a minimum order value but does not publicly disclose the amount; it also says prototypes can be produced with or without tooling. The presence of an MOV leaves a possible pricing gap, but the public evidence does not support assuming that the gap is large enough to sustain SkinLot's fees.
- [Stokvis Tapes](https://stokvistapes.co.uk/markets/healthcare/) states that it supports medical customers from concept through full-scale production, offers local support through a global footprint, operates ISO 13485 and ISO 7/8 cleanroom facilities, provides lab testing and several cutting methods, and calls itself the world's largest independent converter of self-adhesive components.
- [JBC Technologies](https://www.jbc-tech.com/markets/consumer-wellness/) explicitly supports wearable OEMs and startups from low-volume prototype through high-volume manufacturing. It advertises rapid prototypes with no tooling investment and, when material is in stock, same-day prototypes, alongside ISO 13485 certification, FDA registration, cleanrooms, supplier relationships, converting, packaging, and scale-up.

Taken together, these competitors do not publicly prove the exact SkinLot bundle of a customer-named production lot, segregated reservation, retained reference samples, non-substitution, and CEE call-off service. They do show that prototype access, material selection, custom geometry, traceability-capable quality systems, stocked materials, and progression to production are already normal incumbent capabilities. The candidate must therefore prove a narrow contracting, price, and response-time gap; it cannot treat low-volume conversion itself as the hidden market gap.

### Consequential hypotheses and missing facts

- No qualified buyer interview, paid comparison pack, deposit, reservation, purchase order, or repeat call-off is reported.
- There is no named-account denominator establishing at least 45 reachable recurring programs, nor evidence that five to seven durable design-ins can be won each year.
- All customer prices, master-roll costs, conversion yields, gross margins, CAC, continuity-fee attachment, inventory turns, expiry losses, and annual account revenue remain hypotheses.
- No competing quote compares the exact same construction, geometry, quantity, lot identity, retained-sample requirement, non-substitution term, lead time, landed cost, and replenishment reservation.
- Supplier purchase, resale, relabeling, traceability, and conversion terms are not confirmed for a first paid project.
- The founder's biosignal knowledge supports customer discovery, but medical-material conversion, QMS, contracting, and regulated supply are delegated dependencies rather than founder advantages.

## Overall Score

**38/100.** The weights sum to **20.0**. Weighted points are **76.0**, so the calculation is `10 × (76.0 / 20.0) = 38.0`, rounded to **38**. The score is not raised by the quality of the plan because a clear test plan is not market evidence.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** A prepaid reservation and physically segregated named lot are directly provable, but the underlying material is commodity-purchasable rather than shown to be scarce, exclusive, or supply-constrained in SkinLot's favor. The strongest copy risk is an established ISO 13485 converter adding a segregated-stock, retained-sample, non-substitution, and scheduled-call-off clause to its existing prototype-to-production service. That requires a routine commercial and warehouse process change, while the incumbent already owns the harder assets: supplier access, stock, conversion equipment, quality systems, qualified staff, and customer trust.

The customer-specific drawing or die provides limited switching friction but not asymmetric control; converters routinely work to controlled customer drawings, and tooling ownership can be assigned contractually. The material library and yield history may compound, but no exclusive data right or performance advantage is demonstrated. Public competitor pages do not prove identical named-lot terms, which is why this is Tier 3 rather than a conclusion that the first-sale mechanism is fictional.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The candidate supplies conservative, expected-success, and strong-success scenarios with pricing, revenue, margins, capital, ownership, reinvestment, valuation, distributions, and timing. The arithmetic and ownership logic are substantially correct, and the expected scenario nominally exceeds 5m PLN of founder net worth by years 7–8.

The decisive weakness is credibility, not spreadsheet completeness. The conservative case ends at about 2.52m PLN, below the gate. The expected case requires 45 recurring programs averaging 200,000 PLN per year, 42% gross margin, 23% operating margin, five to seven durable new design-ins annually across several regions, 85% founder ownership, and a 4× EBIT valuation. None of the account count, annual spend, margin, continuity retention, acquisition rate, or valuation assumptions has customer or transaction evidence. The incumbent evidence makes those assumptions more demanding because established converters already possess prototype, production, supplier, inventory, cleanroom, and quality-system advantages that can compress SkinLot's price and margin while increasing its CAC.

The expected wealth outcome therefore depends on simultaneous success across market size, differentiation, retention, margin, and regional expansion. Until paid buyers and repeat cohorts show that the named-lot premium survives competitive quotes, the path is heroic rather than credible. Failure of this gate prohibits `pursue` regardless of the 38/100 score.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass, narrowly.** The proposed proof is a paid 8,000–15,000 PLN comparison pack, deposit, non-cancellable reservation, or cash-backed purchase order within 180 days. Pre-proof founder exposure is capped at 32,000 PLN, below the 40,000 PLN limit, and the first exact-material purchase is deferred until customer cash clears. The work can be attempted in 15–25 hours per week without leaving employment, and it does not require an owned factory, ISO 13485 certification project, or speculative roll inventory before payment.

The critical pre-payment dependencies are obtaining authentic samples and documents through ordinary channels, qualifying one R&D cutting route, completing bounded legal and liability review, identifying event-matched buyers, and convincing one buyer to pay an unknown supplier for a comparison or reservation service. Those dependencies are meaningful but do not all need full production-scale resolution before a paid comparison pack. Passing this gate means only that the hypothesis can be tested safely; it does not mean the sale is likely.

The first test must compare SkinLot with real incumbent quotes, not merely ask buyers whether procurement is frustrating. Paid proof should count only if the buyer knows the competing quantity, geometry, lot-control, lead-time, and total-cost alternatives. If three qualified buyers report an acceptable equivalent, the candidate's own kill criterion is met and should be enforced.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is well bounded around wearable programs with a near-term build event, 50–5,000 units, no internal conversion, and authority to reserve stock. The proposed adjacencies—repeat batches, multi-layer constructions, shared stock, regional supply, and controlled conversion—follow the same customer workflow.

Expansion does not improve the competitive structure. Each step moves SkinLot toward services already advertised by BDK, Gergonne, ADDEV Parafix, Stokvis, JBC, and other qualified converters. A large converter does not need to build a new factory to enter the claimed niche; it needs only to accept smaller jobs, price a reservation, and expose existing traceability and inventory practices to the customer. Poland can lower response time, language friction, and operating cost, but this is an operational edge rather than a protected regional control point. There is no evidence that the CEE beachhead is large enough to dominate profitably before incumbents respond.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The broad gap is contradicted by incumbent offers.** Multiple established converters already advertise material selection, tooling-free or rapid prototypes, low-volume support, custom conversion, and progression to full production for medical or stick-to-skin products.
2. **The claimed control point is easily copied.** Segregated stock, retained samples, non-substitution, and call-off scheduling are contract and warehouse practices, not exclusive assets. Incumbents already control the scarcer supplier, equipment, QMS, and trust resources.
3. **Willingness to pay is absent.** No buyer has paid 8,000–15,000 PLN for comparison, 18,000–35,000 PLN for reservation, or 12,000–30,000 PLN annually for continuity rather than using an incumbent's engineering and sample service.
4. **The expected wealth case is unsupported.** Forty-five programs at 200,000 PLN each, 42% gross margin, 23% operating margin, and a 4× EBIT exit or equity value cannot be treated as credible without a named market denominator and repeat transaction cohorts.
5. **Distribution is a plan rather than an advantage.** A list of 150 event-matched accounts and technical outbound are useful tests, but there is no response, close, CAC, cycle-time, or renewal evidence.
6. **Qualification asymmetry favors incumbents.** SkinLot would outsource conversion and add counsel, quality, insurance, traceability, and contractor-management costs while competing against firms that already advertise ISO 13485 systems, cleanrooms, regulated production experience, and major supplier relationships.
7. **Retention and inventory compounding may fail together.** If every construction is customer-specific, stock does not turn; if constructions are standard and widely available, an incumbent can supply them and the control point weakens. No repeat call-off evidence resolves this tension.

These objections veto `pursue`. The competition evidence does not by itself prove identical named-lot terms at acceptable CEE price and lead time, so it stops short of conclusively triggering the candidate's three-buyer kill criterion.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight to preserve is that the economically meaningful unit may be **continuity of a selected material construction through a scheduled device-program event**, not tape sold by area: exact identity, controlled geometry, retained evidence, non-substitution, and inventory funded before it becomes obsolete. That insight remains a valid research question even though SkinLot has not shown it can own or defend the transaction.
