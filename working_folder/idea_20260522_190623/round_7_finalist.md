# Round 7 Finalist

## Orphan Casting Pattern Vault

Target buyer: Polish pump-service firms, municipal waterworks/wastewater operators, district-heating maintenance teams, small industrial plants, and foundries that need obsolete cast pump, valve, and fluid-handling parts but lack the original casting pattern.

Hidden insight: the scarce asset in many obsolete industrial repairs is not the pump mechanic, foundry, or CAD scan. It is the old physical casting pattern or core box: the root key that lets a foundry reproduce an impeller, volute, casing, valve body, cover, bracket, or other cast component without starting from expensive reverse engineering. When old foundries, model shops, or plant maintenance departments clear warehouses, these patterns are scrapped, orphaned, or left uncatalogued. Once destroyed, the cost and lead time to make a replacement part jumps.

Market misunderstanding: buyers think they need "a spare part" or "a pump service company." The hidden bottleneck is upstream: nobody has indexed or controlled the old patterns that make one-off or small-batch spare casting economical. Pattern shops and foundries can make new models, but that is slow and expensive compared with finding the existing pattern.

Product wedge: acquire or option orphaned casting patterns and core boxes for selected Polish/EU legacy pump, valve, and fluid-handling equipment; create a searchable pattern passport with photos, dimensions, material notes, compatible part labels, source/provenance, condition, storage location, and likely foundry process; then sell casting access, reservation, or emergency reproduction through qualified foundry/pump-service partners.

The startup does not pretend to be a foundry or repair shop. It controls pattern discovery, ownership/first-refusal, cataloging, buyer matching, and casting access. Qualified foundries produce castings; pump/valve service firms install and accept technical responsibility.

Primary control point:

- physical ownership, consignment, or exclusive first-refusal rights over orphaned casting patterns/core boxes;
- pattern passports and searchable compatibility records;
- option/reservation contracts with pump-service firms or waterworks for specific patterns;
- signed foundry partner terms for using controlled patterns;
- growing map of which obsolete part patterns still exist and who needs them.

Beachhead:

Start with municipal and industrial fluid-handling parts in Poland: sewage pumps, water pumps, district-heating circulation pumps, older valve bodies/covers, pump casings, volutes, impellers, brackets, and covers used by small waterworks, wastewater plants, district heating, food plants, and industrial maintenance teams.

Two-month proof:

- contact 50 foundries, model/pattern shops, pump-service firms, and plant-liquidation/scrap channels;
- sign 2 first-refusal/consignment agreements for pattern libraries or lots;
- physically control or option 50-150 patterns/core boxes, not random metal parts;
- build 30 pattern passports for pump/valve/fluid-handling families;
- get 5 demand confirmations from pump-service firms/waterworks for specific obsolete casting needs;
- secure 2 paid pattern reservations, paid searches, or one paid casting-access order;
- produce one foundry quote using a controlled pattern.

Budget:

20-70k PLN for buying/optioning lots, storage, transport, scanning/photography, part-time pattern/foundry advisor, and one sample foundry quote. Avoid buying large libraries until demand is mapped.

Why an unknown founder might secure it:

Supply-side sellers often see old patterns as storage clutter, scrap, or unmonetized legacy stock. The founder can offer quick cataloging, removal, consignment revenue, or above-scrap purchase without needing prestige. Demand-side buyers only need a credible pattern passport and foundry/service partner, not the founder personally repairing equipment. Parents' installation-company background helps with industrial-local outreach, storage, removal, and contractor discipline; AI/data skills help catalog photos, dimensions, labels, and compatibility records.

Why not generic resale or consulting:

The valuable unit is not a spare-part listing or a repair report. It is physical control of the pattern/core box that reduces reproduction cost and lead time. If the founder cannot control patterns or first-refusal rights, the idea is invalid.

Main risks:

- demand matching may be sparse because many patterns are for unknown or low-demand parts;
- pattern ownership/IP and permission to cast parts may be ambiguous;
- foundries/pump-service firms may already own their best patterns;
- storage and cataloging can become a junkyard if not narrow;
- technical advisor is needed to identify which patterns are usable;
- buyers may prefer full replacement pumps rather than one-off cast repair.

Strongest copy risk:

Foundries, pump-service firms, or industrial salvage companies can catalog their own patterns. Defense is aggregating orphaned patterns across many small sources before they are visible online, building demand-side search memory, and controlling the physical patterns rather than just listing them.

Research anchors checked:

- Polish pump-service firms advertise broad repair/spares for many pump brands, confirming demand for non-OEM service and parts.
- Polish pattern shops/foundry suppliers explicitly offer foundry pattern and core-box production, confirming the technical role and cost center.
- Foundry/pump sources show that one-off pump components, impellers, and obsolete bodies often depend on existing patterns, pattern-making, or reverse engineering.
