Idea: IVF Genetic Add-On Consent Rail for Polish IVF clinics participating in the 2024-2028 government IVF demand wave.

Context: Poland's public IVF program began on 2024-06-01 and runs to 2028, with at least 500 million PLN per year. Official data as of 2026-05-15 reports 50,778 qualified couples, 28,757 clinical pregnancies, and 14,362 births. ESHRE's 2026 expanded-carrier-screening draft for medically assisted reproduction focuses on implementation pathways, genetic counselling requirements, informed consent, privacy/legal frameworks, and reporting thresholds. This creates pressure on IVF clinics: patient flow is rising, but genetic add-ons such as carrier screening, PGT indication checks, donor-related carrier questions, known-family-variant routing, and genetics escalation are ethically sensitive and operationally fragmented.

Hidden insight: IVF clinics do not mainly need another genetic test vendor. They need a neutral, defensible, non-sales workflow that lets coordinators and doctors explain and document genetic add-on choices without wasting doctor time or looking like they are upselling anxious couples.

Buyer and beachhead: mid-sized Polish IVF clinics that participate in the government program but are not large chains with mature in-house genetic counselling operations. First users are IVF coordinators, doctors, and clinic managers handling couples before non-reimbursed genetic add-ons are discussed.

Product wedge: a clinic-branded, founder-operated pre-add-on consent and routing workflow. Every eligible couple in the pilot completes a structured intake covering family history, prior pregnancy losses, known variants, consanguinity, donor/gamete path, prior genetic tests, and clinical triggers chosen by the clinic. The workflow generates: a patient-facing choice packet, doctor-time summary, consent checklist, escalation note when geneticist review is needed, and audit log of what was explained and chosen. It does not diagnose, recommend tests, interpret variants, provide genetic counseling, sell tests, or take lab affiliate commission. Qualified clinical geneticist review is used only for escalations.

Initial acquisition: build the official provider list, target 30-50 clinic managers/coordinators with a written "genetic add-on consent-time and complaint-risk audit", and offer a fixed 20-couple pilot with no software integration and no lab affiliation. The pitch is doctor-time reduction, cleaner consent, lower complaint risk, and transparent add-on governance, not more test sales.

Primary control point and 2-month proof: secure one signed pilot in which the clinic routes all couples who are about to discuss carrier screening or PGT-related genetic add-ons through this workflow before the doctor/add-on conversation. Also pre-reserve one clinical genetics specialist for 10 escalation reviews via paid retainer or per-case agreement. Proof before serious capital: signed clinic pilot and DPA, 20 routed couple packets, at least 5 paid patient or clinic fees, measured doctor/admin time saved, consent completeness rate, escalation rate, opt-in/opt-out distribution, and written clinic confirmation that the workflow reduced ambiguity or missed explanations.

Pricing and economics: 350-650 PLN per routed couple or 4,000-10,000 PLN monthly clinic workflow fee after pilot. Expected escalation rate 15-25%; geneticist reviewer cost 150-350 PLN only on escalation cases. Founder handles intake, packet production, QA, and metrics with AI-assisted templates. Target gross margin 60-75% after reviewer and admin costs. POC budget under 35,000 PLN: legal/DPA templates, reviewer retainer, secure workflow setup, materials, outreach, and pilot delivery.

Domination path: start with one clinic and one workflow moment, then become the neutral add-on governance layer across several Polish IVF clinics. The compounding asset is not a report; it is clinic-routed pre-add-on workflow access, consent template memory, structured family-history and decision-pattern data, reviewer capacity, clinic-specific escalation maps, and benchmarks on where couples get confused or where doctors lose time. Expansion can move from carrier screening/PGT decision routing into donor genetic compatibility documentation, oncology fertility preservation genetic triggers, and cross-clinic quality benchmarking.

Why this is not a generic service/report/dashboard/database wrapper: the POC only counts if the clinic makes the workflow mandatory for a defined patient segment before genetic add-ons are chosen. The control point is routed clinical-administrative workflow plus reserved specialist review capacity, not a static report or dashboard. The business dies if it cannot secure that routed workflow and paid proof quickly.
