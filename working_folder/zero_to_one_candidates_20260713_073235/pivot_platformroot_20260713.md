# PlatformRoot Structural Pivot — 2026-07-13

**Role boundary:** pivoting only. This document does not score, panel, validate, confirm, or contact a chatbot. It accepts the diagnoses in `evaluation_platformroot_v1.md` and `evaluation_platformroot_v2.md` and uses the completed research addendum in `round10_installer_seed_asset_directions.md`. Any surviving revision must return to `$evaluate-zero-to-one` before an independent panel.

## Pivot Boundary

### Strongest insight worth preserving

The useful asset is not a broad parts catalogue or a generic repair assistant. It is a lawful, provenance-linked record joining:

- white/private-label badge and underlying platform ancestry;
- serial/date and hardware, connector, board, firmware, and supersession revision;
- the evidence supporting a compatibility decision;
- the action taken;
- the closed repair outcome, callback, return, and refund reason; and
- an accountable paid event that makes the outcome economically meaningful rather than a volunteered anecdote.

The parents' installer can seed vocabulary, schema, and lawful first-party observations, but it is not counted as payment evidence, market proof, free labour, or distribution.

### Original failure mechanism accepted

The original merchant-of-record shape fails because it requires an unavailable private-label repair-event denominator to support 2,000 accounts buying 20 baskets each, while carrying roughly €38.4 million of annual merchandise COGS, return liquidity, fitment/advice exposure, product-chain obligations, and technical operations. Its proposed data asset is prospective and nonexclusive, and Bluon or a distributor can localise the same three platform families without recreating the whole graph.

### Shapes that must not be repeated

- installer parts commerce, drop-shipping, inventory, or the same merchant flow with a different fee label;
- a generic data licence, repair dashboard, catalogue, consulting service, or API sold before a proprietary corpus exists;
- insurance-regulated risk pooling or open-ended reimbursement of property damage, parts, labour, or consequential loss;
- a business that needs one OEM/importer/distributor acceptance merely to run the first proof;
- a scale plan that accumulates one enterprise anchor at a time without an independently repeatable acquisition mechanism; or
- a self-declared authority mark whose trust depends on industry acceptance rather than a contractually measurable service.

The three routes below are deliberately different from the original on buyer, paid event, value capture, liability, and transaction role. None survives all constraints.

# Zero-to-One Pivot: FitmentBond

**Disposition: closest paid-proof route, but rejected as a standalone founder-wealth business.**

## Preserved Insight

Compatibility is a version-specific decision, not a model-name lookup. A paid decision tied to its evidence, later outcome, and a bounded consequence for an incorrect decision would generate higher-quality ancestry/revision/outcome data than search logs or informal installer reports.

## Structural Change

PlatformRoot stops selling, importing, warehousing, routing, financing, or taking title to parts. The customer buys parts from its chosen existing supplier. PlatformRoot sells a single **Compatibility Decision Certificate** for one identified unit, proposed EU-market part, and stated repair step.

The certificate warrants only that the identified part is compatible with the documented serial/revision and stated connectors/firmware constraints. It does not diagnose the failure, promise the repair will cure it, approve installation quality, or cover bodily injury, property damage, the part price, or consequential loss. If PlatformRoot's documented compatibility decision is wrong, it pays a fixed service-error credit, initially capped at the lower of 3,000 PLN or verified additional technician labour and return freight. Whether this is legally a service warranty rather than regulated insurance is a gating hypothesis for counsel and a bound insurer, not an assumption.

## Target Niche and Buyer

The direct buyer is an unrelated Polish F-gas service company about to order a non-returnable or costly compressor, inverter, control board, or electronics assembly for a post-warranty hydronic private-label unit after it has already made its own diagnosis. Cases below a minimum replacement value and cases requiring OEM-only software, commissioning authority, or a safety-critical modification are excluded.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The event is the decision immediately before an installer commits to an expensive, revision-sensitive part and a return visit. A wrong compatibility decision can strand part cash, add travel and labour, and damage the installer-customer relationship. The willingness-to-pay hypothesis is 1,400–1,800 PLN per high-value certificate when the documented downside is several thousand PLN. No independent payment presently establishes that price or the event frequency.

## Value and Profit Wedge

PlatformRoot charges only for the compatibility decision and keeps no merchandise margin. Initial target economics are 1,600 PLN revenue, no more than 300 PLN expert/verification cost, 120 PLN acquisition/payment/support cost, and a risk reserve of 160 PLN per accepted case, leaving 1,020 PLN contribution before fixed costs. Cases that cannot be resolved from already controlled evidence and a fixed verification protocol are declined, not converted into open-ended consulting.

### Founder-wealth scenarios

These scenarios avoid double-counting retained cash and business value. Conservative and expected assume 95% reinvestment of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder remains employed and no more than 5% optional distribution; the table assumes no employment-period distribution. Strong assumes 100% reinvestment during the high-growth phase.

| Scenario | Paid events and pricing | Revenue and margins | Capital, ownership, and reinvestment | Value and timing | Gate implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 8,000 certificates/year at €350 equivalent | €2.8m revenue; 72% gross margin; 35% operating margin = €0.98m | ≤100k PLN founder capital through proof and corpus build; founder 75%; 95% reinvestment | 5× operating profit = €4.9m company value; founder share ≈€3.7m in years 8–10 | Does not clear €10m. |
| Expected-success | 25,000 certificates/year at €400 | €10.0m revenue; 75% gross margin; 36% operating margin = €3.6m | Retained cash plus a small employee pool; founder 70%; 95% reinvestment | 5× operating profit = €18.0m; founder share ≈€12.6m in years 9–12 | Arithmetic clears, but requires an unproved annual event count and a price far above free incumbent support. |
| Strong-success | 70,000 certificates/year at €450 | €31.5m revenue; 78% gross margin; 40% operating margin = €12.6m | Founder 60% after specialist hiring and EU expansion; 100% reinvestment while employed/growing | 6× operating profit = €75.6m; founder share ≈€45.4m in years 9–12 | Depends on Europe-wide transaction density and retained trust that are not established. |

The route removes the original working-capital problem, but it does not create a credible denominator for 25,000 annual high-value compatibility decisions. Completed research found no matched private-label stock, event frequency, wrong-part rate, or annual eligible cases per firm. Low-repair-cost evidence from unmatched Swiss systems is adverse to assuming a dense premium event stream.

## Domination Wedge

Begin with one repeated private-label platform family and only two or three high-cost revision-sensitive assemblies. Accepting fewer case types is the intended wedge: a certificate is issued only when provenance and revision evidence meet a fixed standard. The hoped-for niche advantage would be a lower accepted-case error rate and faster decision than free distributor phone support.

## Acquisition Channel

After the lawful family-record audit, contact 100 relevant firms filtered from the UDT registry with a concrete offer for a stalled, high-value case. The first proof must come from three unrelated firms. Each must pay, use the certificate in a real part decision, and report the installed outcome. The family installer may test the workflow but cannot count toward proof.

## Primary Control Point

The exact proposed resource is a version-controlled corpus of certificate evidence, accepted and declined decisions, serial/revision mappings, and closed outcomes under explicit contributor commercialisation rights. By month 12 it would need at least 150 unrelated paid certificates, 100 closed outcomes, 30 repeatable mappings, source-level provenance for every mapping, and at least 80% of accepted cases decided without fresh external expert work.

This is not exclusive. The fixed service-error obligation makes the data cleaner, but neither the facts nor contributor rights prevent a rival from recreating them. It is therefore an operating asset, not a protected transaction right.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Maximum pre-proof cash is 30,000 PLN:

- 6,000 PLN for a written opinion on the service-warranty/insurance boundary, liability terms, GDPR, and data provenance;
- 5,000 PLN for a bound professional-liability indication and deductible or an explicit refusal;
- 6,000 PLN for schema, secure intake, and evidence versioning;
- 7,000 PLN for contracted F-gas/electronics verification of a narrow first family;
- 3,000 PLN for direct outreach and payment/administration; and
- 3,000 PLN fixed-credit reserve.

Paid proof by day 180 requires three unrelated certificates at no less than 1,200 PLN each, three documented part decisions, two closed installation outcomes, no unpaid claim, and one repeat certificate from an unrelated payer. No part is purchased by PlatformRoot. Spending stops if counsel or an insurer cannot bound the fixed credit and professional exposure within the remaining proof budget.

## Expansion Path

Expansion would move from a few assemblies in one platform family to additional high-cost assemblies, adjacent private-label families, and later other EU hydronic markets. The route must demonstrate that reuse grows faster than manual review and that the premium paid event is frequent enough before adding geography.

## Minimum People Stack

- founder: evidence graph, intake, measurement, pricing, and direct outbound;
- contracted F-gas/electronics authority for the initial accepted protocols;
- Polish counsel plus insurance broker for the narrow fixed-credit terms; and
- part-time operations support only after repeat paid volume.

## Principal Risk

The paid certificate may be a rare exception rather than a repeatable transaction. Installers can use free supplier/OEM/CNC support, and Bluon or a distributor can add the same guarantee atop superior data, inventory, and trust. If the guarantee is narrowed enough to cap liability, it may cease to be valuable; if broadened enough to change behaviour, professional, product, or insurance-like exposure may become unbounded.

## First Disconfirming Test

Offer the exact 1,200–1,800 PLN certificate to 100 filtered unrelated firms after obtaining the legal/insurance answer. Kill if fewer than three pay by day 180, if fewer than one of the first three requests is solvable under a fixed reusable protocol, if customers demand diagnosis/parts/property coverage to buy, or if two prospects obtain an equally accountable decision free from an incumbent.

Additional month-12 kills: fewer than 150 paid certificates; fewer than 30 mappings reused at least three times; median all-in contribution below 700 PLN; fixed-credit incidence above 3%; more than 20% of accepted cases requiring fresh expert work; or no bottom-up route to at least 25,000 annual premium events across enumerated countries.

## Evidence Preserved

- EPREL and KEYMARK can seed some brand/OEM relations but stop before serial/revision/fitment/outcome detail.
- Public parts examples show that high-value part decisions exist, while proving neither their frequency nor the average basket.
- Daikin's non-returnable special-order terms illustrate the buyer's possible downside.
- CNC and Bluon establish both the incumbent substitute and strongest copy threat.
- No public repair-event, wrong-fit, or willingness-to-pay denominator was found.

## New Hypotheses

- At least three unrelated firms will pay 1,200–1,800 PLN for the narrow certificate.
- A fixed service-error credit can be legally and insurably bounded without turning the product into regulated insurance.
- The eligible event occurs often enough to support 25,000 annual paid certificates at wealth scale.
- The accepted-case corpus produces an accuracy/response-time advantage that Bluon, CNC, or a distributor cannot neutralise before customers switch.

## Strongest Copy Risk

CNC or a regional distributor can attach a documented compatibility guarantee to existing inventory, technical staff, and installer trust. Bluon can localise its much larger data engine and add a bounded certificate through its API or distributor integrations. Either needs to cover only the same high-frequency families.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

FitmentBond has no part sale, inventory, supplier credit, €38.4m COGS, basket refund, or merchant-of-record product role. It sells a bounded decision rather than a basket. It nevertheless retains the original candidate's uncounted repair-event denominator and easy guarantee-copy risk; removing COGS does not create a defendable founder-wealth bridge.

# Zero-to-One Pivot: ContinuityLedger

**Disposition: rejected because control improves only through serial enterprise anchors.**

## Preserved Insight

A white/private-label badge can outlive the commercial usefulness of its importer, documentation, revision history, or original service channel. A serial-level ancestry and outcome record would be more valuable if created when the importer has the underlying files and a direct after-sales obligation, rather than reconstructed after a field failure.

## Structural Change

The buyer changes from installer to the Polish/CEE own-brand importer or supplier placing a private-label heat pump on the market. ContinuityLedger is not a parts merchant and does not promise repair. It becomes the contractually designated independent custodian of a brand's serial/revision/BOM/supersession dossier and after-sales case ledger. The importer pays onboarding plus an annual per-active-serial custody and response fee. The agreement must grant commercial rights to derived, de-identified cross-brand mappings and closed outcomes while preserving confidential source material in tenant-specific access controls.

## Target Niche and Buyer

The initial buyer would be one smaller own-brand hydronic heat-pump importer with thousands of units, fragmented technical records, and a real need to keep post-sale support operational despite supplier or staff turnover. The budget owner is the managing director, product-compliance lead, or after-sales head.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The paid event is a 20,000–40,000 PLN fleet-onboarding and continuity audit followed by an annual 35–50 PLN per active serial. The claimed value is fewer unresolved claims, faster support, preserved revision provenance, and a credible operational handover if an upstream manufacturer or local staff changes. Willingness to grant an unknown founder long-lived access and derivative rights is wholly unproved.

## Value and Profit Wedge

The platform charges for custody, revision-change ingestion, and bounded dossier retrieval, not technical repair decisions or parts. Gross margin could be software-like only after an importer exports clean source files; messy ingestion, confidentiality separation, security, and after-sales requests can make it a bespoke managed service.

### Founder-wealth scenarios

| Scenario | Contracted base and pricing | Revenue and margins | Capital, ownership, and reinvestment | Value and timing | Gate implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 12 importers × 20,000 active serials × €9/year plus onboarding | ≈€2.4m revenue; 65% gross margin; 20% operating margin = €0.48m | ≤100k PLN POC then retained cash; founder 75%; 95% reinvestment, no assumed distributions | 5× operating profit = €2.4m; founder share ≈€1.8m in years 8–10 | Does not clear €10m. |
| Expected-success | 40 importers × 50,000 active serials × €10/year | ≈€20m recurring revenue; 75% gross; 35% operating = €7m | Security and after-sales team funded from contracts; founder 65%; 95% reinvestment | 6× operating profit = €42m; founder share ≈€27.3m in years 9–12 | Clears only by obtaining 40 enterprise anchors and two million active serials. |
| Strong-success | 100 importers × 100,000 active serials × €12/year | €120m revenue; 78% gross; 38% operating = €45.6m | Founder 55% after EU hiring; 100% reinvestment | 7× operating profit = €319m; founder share ≈€176m in years 10–13 | Requires category-scale institutional acceptance and is not founder-reachable from the proposed wedge. |

The only wealth-clearing scenario is explicitly the prohibited serial-enterprise-anchor route. The existing research identifies testimony about a limited number of own-brand importers but no bottom-up EU count, active-serial distribution, custody budget, or willingness to grant derivative data rights.

## Domination Wedge

The proposed wedge would be one importer, one upstream platform family, and one canonical serial/revision escrow. A genuine control point would require ContinuityLedger to be named in after-sales documentation as custodian, receive mandatory revision notices, and retain the case history for the full support period.

## Acquisition Channel

Use public product/importer records and the family installer's field vocabulary to offer a fixed-fee continuity audit to 20 named importers. Family records can demonstrate the information gaps but cannot prove importer demand. The route has no self-serve or bottom-up acquisition path: every new corpus requires management, legal, compliance, security, and technical sign-off at a separate enterprise.

## Primary Control Point

The exact control is a multi-year custody contract, mandatory revision-notice feed, tenant-specific source dossier, and derivative-data/outcome rights. Unlike the original graph, this can include authoritative private source files. The importer and upstream manufacturer, however, control the inputs and can terminate, replace, or duplicate the custodian subject to contract. The control point is acquired one enterprise negotiation at a time rather than generated by an open paid event.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

A maximum 40,000 PLN test could fund security/data architecture, counsel, a tenant-specific prototype from lawfully shareable family material, and outbound. Paid proof would require an unrelated importer to pay at least a 10,000 PLN audit deposit and grant access to a real, bounded serial/revision sample within 180 days. No claim of reachability is justified before such access: an interview, letter of interest, family alpha, or importer meeting is insufficient.

## Expansion Path

Add platform families within the first importer, then acquire separate importers and countries. Cross-tenant derived mappings would help only where contractually permitted and technically comparable. Confidentiality walls limit how directly the source asset can compound across rivals.

## Minimum People Stack

- founder for data architecture and evidence provenance;
- product-compliance and after-sales specialist;
- security/privacy engineer or contractor;
- counsel for confidentiality, database, GDPR, and derivative-right terms; and
- enterprise sales capacity.

## Principal Risk

The importer will not give an unknown founder strategic source files, mandatory feed rights, and cross-brand derivative rights. If ContinuityLedger narrows the rights to win trust, it becomes ordinary document custody/after-sales software. If it preserves broad rights, security and competitive-confidentiality objections lengthen sales and block adoption.

## First Disconfirming Test

Offer the exact paid audit and required rights schedule to 20 named importers. Kill if no unrelated importer pays a deposit and opens a real serial/revision sample by day 180; if buyers require OEM approval before sharing; if derivative cross-brand rights are removed; if annual willingness to pay is below the cost of tenant-specific ingestion/support; or if two buyers prefer an existing PLM, ERP, service portal, distributor, or internal repository.

## Evidence Preserved

KEYMARK rules confirm that private-label/OEM relationships exist and are not always obvious to the market. EPREL and public product records provide starting identifiers. UOKiK import controls show importer/model fragmentation. None proves custody demand, active fleet size, or contractability.

## New Hypotheses

- An own-brand importer has a funded after-sales continuity problem severe enough to grant an unknown custodian strategic access.
- The importer may lawfully grant durable derivative rights across serial/revision/outcome records.
- A recurring per-serial fee survives procurement scrutiny.
- There are enough independently reachable importers and active units for the expected case without heroic share.

## Strongest Copy Risk

An OEM portal, incumbent service-management/PLM provider, large distributor, or the importer itself can add a revision repository and case ledger. The incumbent does not need cross-brand derivative rights to retain its own buyer.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

ContinuityLedger removes installer acquisition, repair-event density, parts commerce, fitment guarantees, inventory, and product resale liability. It seeks authoritative source data at placement rather than reverse-engineering failures. It fails because that stronger control is available only through slow, trust-heavy, serial enterprise contracts—the prohibited dependency—and its first paid proof requires the very access the unknown founder does not possess.

# Zero-to-One Pivot: RepairabilityCovenant

**Disposition: rejected because the transaction right is buyer-created, nonstandard, and must be reacquired contract by contract.**

## Preserved Insight

The ancestry/revision problem can be prevented at procurement: a buyer can require the private-label supplier to deposit the technical identity, revision-change notices, supersession route, and support evidence before units are installed and dispersed.

## Structural Change

The buyer changes to an independent installer, local developer, or procurement group buying a batch of private-label heat pumps. PlatformRoot sells a fixed procurement audit and a **Repairability Covenant** inserted into that buyer's supply order. The supplier must deposit specified serial/revision and after-sales materials with PlatformRoot, notify revisions, and identify the EU economic operator and parts path. PlatformRoot charges the buyer an audit fee and a per-unit custody fee. It neither certifies product safety nor guarantees repair, fitment, supplier performance, or spare availability.

## Target Niche and Buyer

The first niche is a Polish installer or small developer planning a batch purchase large enough that orphaned documentation or silent revision changes could create material future service cost. The buyer must already be choosing between private-label offers and have enough procurement leverage to add the covenant.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The paid event is immediately before a batch order: 8,000–15,000 PLN for the audit and 100–160 PLN per installed unit for document custody and revision notifications. The value proposition is a better-controlled future service obligation, not a promise that a failure will be repairable. No evidence shows small buyers will pay this premium or that suppliers will accept the deposit clause rather than sell through another channel.

## Value and Profit Wedge

PlatformRoot captures a high-margin verification/custody fee before installation, eliminating repair-event uncertainty and parts COGS. The economic problem moves to new-unit procurement volume and contract adoption: each paid event depends on a buyer having a live batch and enough leverage over a supplier.

### Founder-wealth scenarios

| Scenario | Controlled procurement | Revenue and margins | Capital, ownership, and reinvestment | Value and timing | Gate implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 100 buyer accounts × 500 units/year × €35 net recurring equivalent, plus audits | ≈€2.2m revenue; 70% gross; 25% operating = €0.55m | ≤100k PLN founder POC then retained cash; founder 75%; 95% reinvestment | 5× operating profit = €2.75m; founder share ≈€2.1m in years 8–10 | Does not clear €10m. |
| Expected-success | 400 buyer accounts × 1,000 units/year × €35 | ≈€14m revenue; 75% gross; 35% operating = €4.9m | Contract/security team funded from cash; founder 65%; 95% reinvestment | 5.5× operating profit = €27m; founder share ≈€17.5m in years 9–12 | Requires control of 400,000 annual unit purchases through 400 separate buyers. |
| Strong-success | 1,000 buyers × 2,000 units/year × €40 | €80m revenue; 78% gross; 38% operating = €30.4m | Founder 55% after EU expansion; 100% reinvestment | 6× operating profit = €182m; founder share ≈€100m in years 10–13 | Implies category-level procurement adoption and institutional trust. |

The expected case clears arithmetically but requires 400,000 annual unit purchases. The available evidence does not establish an eligible private-label new-unit denominator, typical batch size, procurement leverage, or a repeatable channel to hundreds of buyer frameworks. The original repair denominator has been replaced with an equally unavailable procurement denominator.

## Domination Wedge

One standard covenant for one platform family and one buyer segment, with an explicit completeness checklist and immutable revision-notice record. The hoped-for domination wedge is becoming the default contract language for repairability evidence in a narrow private-label procurement niche.

## Acquisition Channel

Audit the family company's next procurement as alpha without counting it as proof. Then contact 100 UDT-listed firms and selected small developers with a live-batch offer. Paid proof requires three unrelated buyers to pay deposits and at least one supplier to accept the covenant in a real purchase order. That already creates a multi-party dependency, even though PlatformRoot contracts directly with the buyer.

## Primary Control Point

The exact control is being named as dossier custodian and revision-notice recipient in each buyer-supplier purchase contract, with rights to retain the verification history and lawfully derived nonconfidential mappings. This is a direct contractual transaction right for that batch. It is not an independent market control: it must be won in every framework, is limited by supplier terms, and can be replaced by a buyer's lawyer, inspection company, distributor, or internal procurement process.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Maximum 35,000 PLN pre-proof spend:

- 8,000 PLN for product, procurement, confidentiality, database, and GDPR terms;
- 6,000 PLN for the covenant, evidence schema, and secure escrow prototype;
- 6,000 PLN for technical review of one platform family's required dossier;
- 7,000 PLN for 100-buyer outbound and live-batch qualification;
- 5,000 PLN for independent translation/security/procurement support; and
- 3,000 PLN contingency.

By day 180, three unrelated buyers must each pay at least a 3,000 PLN non-refundable audit deposit; one real supplier must accept the complete covenant without removing revision notices or PlatformRoot custody; and the first purchase must preserve positive contribution. A family-company order, unpaid audit, memorandum, or supplier conversation does not count.

## Expansion Path

Expand from one covenant and platform family to more buyer accounts and countries, then seek recognition in procurement templates. The path becomes attractive only if the same template closes with little negotiation and suppliers accept it without bespoke concessions.

## Minimum People Stack

- founder for evidence architecture, measurement, and outbound;
- product-compliance/procurement counsel;
- contracted hydronic technical reviewer; and
- later security and contract operations staff.

## Principal Risk

The economic buyer lacks leverage or urgency before a failure occurs. Suppliers can reject the clause, buyers can choose a familiar brand/distributor, and each purchase becomes a legal/technical negotiation. The control point is contractual but not compounding if every buyer-supplier pair requires bespoke work.

## First Disconfirming Test

Offer the exact paid audit and non-negotiable minimum covenant to 100 filtered buyers with live procurement in the next six months. Kill if fewer than three pay deposits, if no supplier accepts the full minimum terms, if negotiation exceeds ten founder hours per deal, if per-unit willingness to pay is below the ongoing custody cost, or if a standard procurement lawyer/TIC provider can deliver the same clause and custody with existing buyer trust.

Month-12 kill: fewer than ten unrelated completed procurement batches; less than 80% supplier acceptance without bespoke redrafting; no route to a counted 400,000-unit annual flow; or no durable right beyond the individual purchase order.

## Evidence Preserved

Private-label relationships and incomplete public ancestry are evidenced by KEYMARK and EPREL. EU economic-operator, traceability, and product information duties make source provenance commercially relevant. None creates a requirement to use PlatformRoot or proves buyer willingness to add a private covenant.

## New Hypotheses

- Small or mid-sized buyers have enough leverage to demand the full dossier and revision-notice terms.
- Three unrelated buyers will prepay an audit before a failure event.
- Suppliers will accept PlatformRoot as custodian without requiring a recognised inspection authority.
- The same covenant can close repeatedly without consulting-like negotiation.
- A credible, counted procurement denominator supports wealth-scale per-unit volume.

## Strongest Copy Risk

A procurement law firm, technical inspection/certification company, distributor, buying group, or large installer can add the same evidence schedule and escrow arrangement to its standard supply contract. The control can be copied through one process change and does not depend on the accumulated outcome corpus during the initial purchase.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

RepairabilityCovenant moves payment before installation, removes repair frequency, parts commerce, inventory, fitment liability, and refund exposure, and obtains a contract right rather than hoping outcome data becomes defensible. It fails because that right is bespoke, buyer-created, supplier-dependent, and reacquired for every transaction flow. The wealth case requires hundreds of enterprise buyer anchors and a category-scale unit share.

## Comparison and Decision

| Route | What it genuinely fixes | Remaining fatal mechanism | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| FitmentBond | Removes inventory, parts COGS, supplier credit, basket refunds, and merchant product-chain role; keeps direct 180-day paid proof | Retains the unavailable premium repair-event denominator; bounded guarantee may lack value or be uninsurable; corpus remains nonexclusive and easy for Bluon/CNC/distributors to match | Reject as wealth-scale business; cheapest route to falsify only |
| ContinuityLedger | Moves data capture upstream and can obtain authoritative serial/revision files | Requires trust-heavy, serial importer contracts and strategic data rights; confidential source material does not freely compound; conservative scale fails | Reject |
| RepairabilityCovenant | Creates a contractual right at procurement and avoids repair-event/inventory risk | Requires buyer leverage plus supplier acceptance for every flow; creates a new unavailable unit denominator; easily copied by procurement/TIC incumbents | Reject |

## Explicit Conclusion: No Viable PlatformRoot Pivot

No tested structural pivot simultaneously provides:

1. paid proof within 180 days and 40,000 PLN while the founder remains employed;
2. a credible, counted route to €10 million of founder wealth;
3. a control point obtainable by an unknown founder without serial enterprise anchors or speculative partners; and
4. liability and capital exposure that remains bounded.

FitmentBond is the only route with a plausible direct paid test, but its expected-success wealth case still requires 25,000 annual premium compatibility decisions from the same uncounted event pool, and its strongest differentiator can be reproduced by incumbents already holding data, inventory, support staff, and installer trust. ContinuityLedger and RepairabilityCovenant improve data authority only by making importer or buyer contracts the scarce resource; they therefore become exactly the serial-enterprise-anchor pattern the pivot brief prohibits.

Accordingly, **close the PlatformRoot business shape rather than cosmetically revising it**. If the run requires one exact revision to return to `$evaluate-zero-to-one` for formal closure, return **FitmentBond** first because its COGS removal and bounded paid test make it the least assumption-heavy—not because it is recommended for paneling. It must not enter an independent simulated panel unless a new evaluator finds both hard gates pass on evidence not present here.
