# FieldWire Decision-Critical Research

## Questions

1. Is there still a meaningful low-volume/small-shop product gap below Rittal's existing automation?
2. Is the conservative 1,000-machines/year European scale supported by a non-heroic account denominator?

## Findings

### The incumbent moved directly into the proposed beachhead

Rittal announced the Wire Terminal WT L in April 2026 as the compact, lower-investment counterpart to WT C **specifically for small and medium-sized panel builders**. Rittal says it occupies about two square metres, accepts 12 wire feeds, supports 0.5–2.5 mm² or 0.5–6 mm² variants, cuts/strips/crimps/labels automatically, integrates Eplan job data, accelerates preparation up to 11×, and can pay back at 150 control cabinets/year. Rittal does not publish a price, so FieldWire's €12,000 advantage cannot be verified. This removes the candidate's asserted segment white space and leaves an unproved price-only gap against a trusted platform with service and Eplan integration.

Rittal's WT C documentation further confirms fully automatic processing, up to 36 wire types, project-specific labels, job management and approximately 260 wires/hour. Komax/Schleuniger offers an entry cut/strip machine with printer/prefeeder/stacker interfaces plus software for article lists. FieldWire can still integrate commodity modules, but the target outcome is no longer neglected.

### A broad denominator exists, but not an eligible one

An EU-sector page reproducing Eurostat annual detailed enterprise statistics reports about 16,500 EU enterprises in the combined 2020 category of motors, generators, transformers, and electricity-distribution/control apparatus. Older Eurostat material reported about 11,400 enterprises specifically in electricity distribution/control apparatus in 2004. This shows a substantial industrial population, but not the number of 10–50-person shops processing >=1,000 eligible mixed wires/week, not their installed incumbent equipment, and not replacement demand.

At the conservative peak of 1,000 FieldWire units/year and 6,000 installed units, the plan would need a large share of the potentially eligible subset. Because Rittal now targets that same subset, the denominator does not make the scale non-heroic.

## Decision

The research does not support panel readiness. The newest official incumbent release attacks the exact stated beachhead and the wealth case lacks an eligible-account denominator. A price quote could still reveal a narrow low-cost opportunity, but that opportunity would be a price-led machine-integration contest with an incumbent able to move down-market using an existing platform and service channel.
