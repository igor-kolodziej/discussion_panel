# Zero-to-One Pivot: No Viable Peppol Relay Pivot

**Conclusion:** no Peppol Relay structural pivot is viable under the founder's current constraints. The strongest attempted change—buyer-funded activation of a contract-mandated supplier cohort—removes the individual-exporter premium AP and bespoke mapping model, but replaces it with a serial enterprise-anchor acquisition dependency. The other serious routes depend on a revocable AP/ERP partner or on access to payment systems and financial trust. None creates a founder-controlled, repeatable control point before paid proof.

## Preserved Insight

Preserve only **observable-route acquisition**: public Peppol endpoint and profile evidence can identify a named organization with a technically possible document route before outreach. This narrows prospecting and allows a concrete message rather than generic “e-invoicing” marketing.

Do not preserve the original Access Point, SMP, premium exporter subscription, endpoint assignment, custom mapping, 24/7 transport, or claimed route-data moat. Certification is nonexclusive, endpoints are portable, and the evaluator found the recurring and wealth economics unsupported.

## Structural Change

Three materially different structures were considered:

1. **Buyer-funded supplier-cohort activation.** A European industrial buyer, rather than each exporter, would prepay one official activation program for a named supplier cohort. The paid event would be each supplier's first buyer-accepted Peppol order/despatch/invoice test chain. The company would not be an Access Point, would not transport production documents, and would not map supplier ERPs. This is the strongest near-miss because one buyer mandate could supply distribution to many suppliers.
2. **Embedded/assigned AP or ERP route.** An existing certified AP or ERP vendor would embed the activation product and assign a customer cohort. This removes certification and enterprise security burden, but the business would depend on one revocable partner that already owns distribution, customer trust, infrastructure, and the ability to internalize the feature. Rejected.
3. **Payment-linked document event.** Fees would attach to payment release, avoided rejection, or working-capital improvement after accepted documents. This could strengthen willingness to pay, but it requires buyer ERP/AP access, outcome attribution, sensitive financial data, and usually a bank, payment, factoring, or enterprise-system partner. That dependency cannot be obtained safely inside 180 days and 40,000 PLN. Rejected.

The buyer-funded cohort route is examined below only to make the rejection boundary decision-complete. It is not selected as a viable business.

## Target Niche and Buyer

The strongest attempted niche is a 250–2,000 employee European industrial buyer with:

- an existing Peppol endpoint that publicly supports order, despatch, and invoice profiles;
- a defined rollout deadline for at least 25 suppliers;
- one canonical, unmodified Peppol BIS profile pack rather than buyer-specific ERP mappings;
- a procure-to-pay, shared-services, or supplier-enablement owner with change budget; and
- authority to instruct the named supplier cohort to complete a third-party test workflow.

The buyer would be the sole payer. Suppliers could retain any certified AP and any ERP vendor. The company would accept only standard payloads or a finite published input format; unsupported suppliers would obtain mapping help from their own AP/ERP provider. This boundary prevents the company from becoming the original bespoke EDI service.

The niche remains structurally weak for the founder because the first contract requires a mid-market or enterprise buyer to appoint an unknown, part-time company as its official supplier-activation operator. Public endpoint visibility proves technical reachability, not rollout urgency, budget, authority, a named cohort, or willingness to grant that mandate.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The attempted painful event is a buyer's supplier-rollout deadline slipping because long-tail suppliers fail conformance tests, do not know which AP/profile to use, or require repeated chasing. The buyer could value faster adoption because its order-to-pay automation does not deliver full savings while many suppliers remain outside the standard flow.

The fixed paid event would be a **Supplier Cohort Activation Mandate**:

- 10 named suppliers in the proof cohort;
- one buyer-provided canonical profile, sandbox, acceptance rule set, and technical contact;
- official buyer communication appointing the company to run the cohort workflow;
- a 60-day acceptance window;
- a non-refundable €7,500 program deposit;
- €600 for each supplier that completes the buyer-accepted test chain; and
- a conditional 12-month order at €24,000 base plus €300 per active supplier for annual status, version-change reconformance, and cohort records.

These prices and buyer savings are new hypotheses, not evidence. The recurring €300 per supplier is particularly weak: once a supplier is activated, the buyer may see little reason to pay an annual status fee, and its existing AP, ERP vendor, or integration provider may include equivalent onboarding and monitoring.

## Value and Profit Wedge

The attempted value wedge is outcome-priced cohort activation rather than premium transport. The buyer supplies authority and distribution; suppliers self-serve through standard conformance tests; payment follows accepted activation. No live transport, custom mapping, endpoint custody, financial handling, or unlimited support is included.

Target proof economics would require:

- €7,500 prepaid program fee plus €600 per accepted supplier;
- at least 70% of a 10-supplier cohort accepted within 60 days;
- median human support below two hours per supplier;
- no supplier-specific code or field mapping;
- at least 65% contribution margin on the pilot; and
- conversion to €24,000 annual buyer fee plus €300 per active supplier.

Each new buyer requires a fresh mandate, security/procurement process, canonical rule pack, internal sponsor, and supplier communication. The wealth cases therefore expose the serial enterprise-sales problem rather than cure it.

## Domination Wedge

The only contractually defensible wedge would be a prepaid, non-cancellable 12-month appointment as the buyer's sole activation-status registry for a named supplier schedule. During that term, another provider could not replace the company for that cohort without the buyer breaching or changing the contract.

That control is narrow and serial. It does not control the buyer's AP, supplier endpoints, production messages, ERP data, or payment flow. It must be reacquired buyer by buyer, and a buyer can use an incumbent for the next cohort after the term. The proposed self-service tests and status registry use public standards and are cheap for Comarch, an existing AP, ERP vendors, EDI providers, or the buyer's own team to reproduce. The first mandate would be a protected project, not a company-level domination wedge.

## Acquisition Channel

Observable-route targeting would move from exporters to buyers:

1. identify buyers whose public endpoint supports relevant non-invoice profiles;
2. require a second observable rollout signal such as current supplier instructions, a published implementation deadline, a supplier-portal notice, or a direct confirmation from the buyer;
3. contact the buyer's procure-to-pay/shared-services owner with a fixed 10-supplier cohort offer;
4. accept no “innovation partnership,” unpaid proof of concept, reseller dependency, or contingent introduction;
5. require the buyer itself to name and notify suppliers after contract signature.

This channel improves specificity but does not remove enterprise trust or sales cost. The founder has no existing P2P buyer network, Peppol references, security credentials, or supplier-enablement track record. A public endpoint is available to every incumbent and does not lower procurement risk enough to make the mandate repeatable.

## Primary Control Point

**Current control: Tier 3.** The founder holds no buyer mandate, supplier cohort, acceptance workflow, prepayment, AP route, or transaction data.

**Best attainable control:** Tier 1 for one named cohort only after a signed, prepaid mandate containing all of the following:

- a closed schedule of at least 10 suppliers;
- buyer-issued authority and official supplier communication;
- a single canonical profile/test pack and buyer sandbox;
- appointment as the sole activation-status registry for the schedule;
- €7,500 non-refundable prepayment and objective €600 acceptance events;
- 12-month term with no convenience termination, 30-day cure, and all committed base fees due;
- buyer-owned business data but company ownership of generic tests, workflow software, and non-confidential aggregate performance;
- no requirement to own or depend on a supplier endpoint, buyer AP, or financial partner; and
- assignability in a company sale and a defined export of buyer records at termination.

One contract would not create company-level control. A portfolio of independent mandates could become a repeatable contracted position, but acquiring that portfolio is the unproved enterprise-anchor engine. The control is therefore theoretically attainable for a project and practically unproved for a scalable company.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

The strongest attempted proof would avoid certification and production infrastructure entirely.

### Exact 180-day paid-proof plan

**Days 1–30 — observable buyer set; cumulative risk 6,000 PLN.** Build 100 named buyer records with a verified multi-document endpoint and a second rollout signal. Conduct 10 buyer interviews. Build one synthetic, standard-profile conformance demonstration. Do not access live documents or build an AP.

**Days 31–60 — fixed mandate offer; cumulative risk 12,000 PLN.** Complete 20 qualified buyer conversations and issue five identical 10-supplier proposals. Obtain legal review of the mandate, data boundaries, objective acceptance, and refund terms. No custom procurement response or free mapping.

**Days 61–90 — first paid authority; cumulative risk remains 12,000 PLN until payment.** Require one unrelated buyer to prepay €7,500, name at least 10 suppliers, provide the canonical sandbox/rules, and issue official supplier communication. If no buyer does all four, stop.

**Days 91–150 — cohort delivery; cumulative founder risk capped at 25,000 PLN after payment.** Activate at least seven of the ten suppliers with no supplier-specific code, at no more than two human hours per supplier, and receive buyer acceptance plus the €600 success fees. The pilot must be contribution-positive after founder-replacement and contractor cost.

**Days 151–180 — repeat authority; cumulative founder risk capped at 40,000 PLN.** Obtain a second €7,500 deposit from an unrelated buyer and a signed 12-month order from the first buyer near the proposed base/per-supplier price. Do not count an AP referral, unpaid LOI, revocable sandbox invitation, one-off workshop, or supplier-paid mapping job.

**Paid-proof definition:** two unrelated prepaid buyer mandates; at least seven buyer-accepted suppliers in the first cohort; at least 70% self-service completion; no bespoke mappings; positive contribution; one 12-month recurring buyer order; and no more than 40,000 PLN founder capital at risk while employment is retained.

### Capital and 12-month boundary

- Tranche A: up to 12,000 PLN before the first prepayment for legal, account research, synthetic tests, and limited EDI review.
- Tranche B: up to 25,000 PLN cumulative only after the first buyer prepayment, for a bounded workflow prototype and cohort delivery.
- Tranche C: up to 40,000 PLN cumulative only after at least seven accepted suppliers, for the second buyer proof.
- Tranche D: up to 70,000 PLN cumulative after two paid mandates, one annual order, and positive cohort contribution, for productization.
- Tranche E: up to 100,000 PLN cumulative by month 12 only after three independent annual buyer mandates, at least 100 active supplier records, at least 70% self-service completion, and at least 65% gross margin.

No AP certification, live message transport, 24/7 operations, family capital, financial-system integration, or speculative enterprise platform is permitted. By month 12 the resource would need to be three non-cancellable buyer mandates, prepaid cohort schedules, a standard conformance engine, and an official status registry. The founder remains employed; full-time transition would require at least five independent annual buyers, positive recurring contribution, a repeatable acquisition source, and 12 months of personal runway.

Even this tightly bounded plan does not rescue the pivot: its first decisive payment still depends on an enterprise anchor granting official authority to an unknown company.

## Expansion Path

The theoretical sequence would be:

1. one buyer using one public Peppol BIS profile pack;
2. a second unrelated buyer using the same unmodified pack;
3. more suppliers inside those contracted cohorts;
4. additional buyers only where at least 80% of tests and workflows transfer without code;
5. a second profile pack only after the first has repeatable acquisition and renewal.

Expansion is rejected if a buyer adds custom fields, supplier-specific mappings, live exception handling, tax-compliance work, production transport, or a new AP dependency. It is also rejected if every country or buyer requires a fresh security review and bespoke rollout design that makes labor scale with mandates.

The practical copy risk is severe. Existing APs and EDI/ERP vendors can bundle cohort onboarding with services the buyer already buys, use existing references and security approval, and price the status layer at marginal cost. The mandate protects only an already-won cohort; it does not make the next buyer easier to acquire unless implementation evidence materially shortens enterprise sales, which is a new hypothesis.

## Minimum People Stack

The attempted 180-day proof would require:

- founder: 10–12 hours per week for observable buyer research, sales, contract scope, economics, and program ownership;
- part-time Peppol/EDI specialist: standard profile tests and acceptance interpretation, explicitly no mapping work;
- part-time software contractor: self-service workflow and status registry;
- fixed-scope Polish/EU commercial and data lawyer;
- no 24/7 operator, certified AP team, financial partner, integration partner, public tender specialist, or full-time hire.

This stack fits the cash and time limit technically. It does not solve the founder-fit problem: the critical activity is repeated enterprise selling, sponsor building, and procurement trust, which conflicts with the founder's limited social energy and lack of domain references.

## Principal Risk

The principal risk is **serial enterprise-anchor dependence**. The buyer mandate creates genuine authority only after a hard-to-win enterprise contract; each additional revenue block requires another buyer to grant the same authority. Observable endpoints do not prove budget or reduce the security, reputation, and procurement burden enough for an unknown part-time founder.

The second risk is that the product boundary removes the work buyers actually need. If supplier ERP/AP mapping and live exception resolution are excluded, buyers may see little value beyond what their AP already offers. If those services are included, the pivot collapses back into bespoke EDI delivery and loses its margins, founder fit, and compounding path.

## First Disconfirming Test

Spend no more than 12,000 PLN and 60 days to complete 100 evidence-led buyer outreaches, 20 qualified P2P/shared-services conversations, and five identical written 10-supplier proposals.

The structure is disconfirmed immediately if, by day 90, **no unrelated buyer both signs the non-cancellable cohort mandate and prepays €7,500 while naming 10 suppliers, providing one standard sandbox/profile, and issuing official supplier communication**. Do not relax the test to an LOI, free pilot, AP introduction, exporter payment, mapping engagement, or revocable innovation project.

Even if one buyer signs, disconfirm by day 150 if fewer than seven suppliers complete without bespoke mapping, median human time exceeds two hours per supplier, the buyer refuses objective acceptance fees, or it will not place the 12-month base/per-supplier order. These failures would show that the apparent product is either not valuable or is actually bespoke integration.

## Evidence Preserved

- Peppol endpoints and supported profiles can be observed before outreach, making named-route acquisition possible.
- Peppol supports business documents beyond invoices, so a multi-document supplier workflow is technically possible.
- Cheap, nonexclusive AP access, public standards, portable endpoints, and several Polish/global providers are established competitive facts; the pivot must not treat transport or certification as scarce.
- KSeF and broader structured-document adoption create attention, but not exclusion or buyer authority.
- The evaluator's verdict is judgment rather than market evidence; its useful boundary is that the original paid sprint could prove one-off mapping without proving a recurring, defensible platform.

No evidence establishes buyer-funded cohort mandates, the proposed prices, recurring status value, a scalable buyer denominator, self-service completion, or attainable enterprise acquisition.

## New Hypotheses

1. At least 1,400 European industrial buyers have 25 or more suppliers in an active multi-document Peppol rollout using a transferable standard profile pack.
2. A buyer will appoint a small external company as its official activation-status operator without requiring it to be the AP, ERP integrator, or production processor.
3. Two unrelated buyers will prepay the fixed mandate inside 180 days from evidence-led outbound.
4. The buyer can issue supplier authority and one canonical acceptance rule set without bespoke overlays.
5. At least 70% of named suppliers can self-complete using their existing AP/ERP provider, with less than two hours of company support each.
6. Buyers will pay €7,500 deposits, €600 acceptance fees, and approximately €24,000 plus €300 per supplier annually.
7. Annual supplier-status/reconformance has durable value after initial activation.
8. Gross margin can exceed 65% after enterprise sales, supplier support, legal/data work, and software operations.
9. At least 80% of tests/workflow transfer between the first two buyers; otherwise the model is bespoke.
10. Existing APs, ERP vendors, and EDI incumbents do not already include equivalent cohort onboarding at a price the startup cannot match.
11. A direct sales engine can acquire 70 buyer programs for the conservative wealth case without founder labor, CAC, or dilution destroying the economics.

These hypotheses are too numerous and too dependent on the same enterprise mandate to call the route viable before the first payment.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

The attempted cohort structure would materially change every broken dimension:

- **payer:** European buyer, not individual exporter;
- **value event:** accepted supplier cohort activation, not message transport or a mapping sprint;
- **distribution:** buyer-issued supplier mandate, not one-by-one exporter sales;
- **control:** prepaid official cohort appointment, not a portable endpoint or purchasable certificate;
- **delivery:** standard conformance/status workflow, not bespoke ERP mapping or 24/7 AP operations;
- **economics:** buyer program and per-supplier outcome fees, not premium AP ARR benchmarked against €29/month access.

It still fails because the control point appears only after a speculative enterprise-anchor sale and must be reacquired for every expansion block. The embedded and payment-linked alternatives are even more partner-dependent. Thus the business shape changes, but the founder-reachable control problem does not.

## Structural Alternatives Rejected

- **Supplier-paid AP 2.0:** rejected as the original low-scarcity, premium-priced, service-heavy shape.
- **White-label AP/ERP activation product:** rejected because the partner owns customers, route, trust, and replacement power; one routine internal build can eliminate the startup.
- **Buyer-mandated cohort activation:** strongest attempted structure, rejected because every paid control point begins with a fresh enterprise-anchor mandate and the self-service/recurring-value premise is unproved.
- **Payment-release or receivables-linked rail:** rejected because it needs financial-system access, sensitive outcome attribution, and one or more speculative enterprise/financial partners before paid proof.

**Comparison and next step:** none of these pivots should return to `$evaluate-zero-to-one`. Observable-route acquisition remains a useful distribution tactic for a future candidate, but within Peppol it does not create a scarce, founder-reachable resource. The Peppol Relay family should stop here rather than cycle through a renamed AP, onboarding service, enterprise mandate, or payment wrapper.
