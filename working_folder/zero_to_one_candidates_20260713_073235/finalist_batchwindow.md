# Zero-to-One Candidate: BatchWindow

## One-Sentence Thesis

BatchWindow options named, homogeneous production windows from certified shelf-stable food co-packers, fills them only with deposit-backed technically ready brands using one packaging/allergen/process envelope, and becomes the transaction rail for small production runs rather than another manufacturer directory.

## Founder Fit

The founder's data-science and business-analysis skills fit capacity matching, run economics, lead-time prediction, technical-intake automation, demand forecasting and exception analysis. Public brand catalogues and manufacturer directories support precise outbound without status or an audience.

The founder is not a food technologist. Before any plant option, a contracted Polish food technologist/quality manager must define the first product envelope and audit liability boundaries. The POC uses one plant, one format and three brands; it does not require a broad marketplace, warehouse, factory or full-time founder. At 15–20 hours/week the founder handles sourcing, economics and workflow while the technologist handles product/process qualification.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is a small but real condiment or ambient-food brand with repeat retail/e-commerce demand, approved formula/label and cash for production, but too little volume to obtain a reliable date from a certified co-packer.

The brand's real desired outcome is a date it can promise to retail without funding a huge MOQ. It currently accepts long lead times, manual broker searches, poor plant responsiveness, excess inventory or an artisanal producer that cannot scale. The co-packer's inside problem is different: small clients arrive with incomplete specifications, incompatible jars, uncertain forecasts and late money, causing changeover and quality risk. It wants one prepared counterparty, one technical envelope and committed cash.

Existing low-MOQ manufacturers prove demand, while Wonnda and Agilery prove that discovery and sourcing already exist. The unproved gap is a contracted window with nomination rights and deposits—not another list, RFQ form or consultant.

## Product Wedge

Start with one eight-hour ambient-fill window for acidified sauces/condiments under one narrow envelope:

- one jar, closure, label geometry and case format;
- one validated pH/viscosity/fill-temperature band;
- no cold chain, supplements, novel foods, meat, dairy, nuts or high-risk allergens;
- at most three technically approved recipes scheduled in a validated low-changeover sequence;
- plant remains manufacturer of record; each brand approves formula, artwork and label.

BatchWindow signs a nominated/assignable take-or-pay option, publishes the exact slots and deposit deadline, checks each brand's readiness through the contracted technologist, routes brand deposits to the plant obligation, and earns a booking/quality/transaction fee. If brands do not cover the window by the deadline, the option expires before the large plant deposit is due.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Small-batch manufacturing is not mainly a matching problem. It is a coordination-risk problem. A plant can economically run smaller brands when packaging, process, allergens, documentation and cash are homogeneous enough to turn several difficult clients into one prepared production event.

The scarce object is not the manufacturer relationship. It is a named, controlled date plus a readiness standard that makes the date profitable. Brands value delivery certainty; the plant values reduced pre-production work and funded capacity. If a plant will not grant a real nominated window right or brands will not deposit against a named date, the insight is false.

## Underused Momentum

- Viscofill publicly advertises EU co-packing runs from 100 units, and The Good Food Company advertises 500-unit starting MOQs and four-to-eight-week lead times. This proves that small-run production exists; it does not prove option economics.
- Wonnda reports a large brand/retailer/manufacturer marketplace, and Agilery provides co-manufacturer search and implementation. Their presence rejects a directory or generic broker and focuses the wedge on controlled capacity and transaction evidence.
- A growing population of small brands can launch through e-commerce and retailers without owning manufacturing, while certified plants need higher utilisation. Exact eligible brand counts and idle-window supply remain hypotheses.

## Beachhead Market

Polish and nearby CEE condiment brands needing 500–5,000 units per SKU in one standard ambient jar, and one certified Polish plant with documented capability and an otherwise uncertain/cancellable date inside 60–120 days.

The beachhead is one format, one plant, one production day, three unrelated paying brands and one reorder within six months. It excludes bespoke packaging, high-risk allergens, cold chain and formula-development projects.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

**POC hypothesis:** three brands each buy 1,000–3,000 units. Combined production value is 45,000–90,000 PLN. Brands deposit at least 70% before the plant's non-refundable commitment. BatchWindow charges the brand 8–12% or a 4,000–8,000 PLN minimum per slot and may receive a 2–4% utilisation rebate from the plant; all rebates are disclosed.

At scale, the blended net fee should fall to 7–10% of routed production value. Target gross margin is 65–75% after food-technologist review, payment cost, insurance, support and failed-window reserves. Target operating margin is 30%+ after replacing founder labour. BatchWindow never finances ingredients, packaging or plant production from customer receivables; deposits and milestone payments must cover take-or-pay exposure plus a reserve.

Reject any window whose brand deposits do not cover 110% of the plant's non-cancellable obligation, whose fully loaded contribution is below 20%, or whose quality/recall exposure cannot be contractually confined to the responsible plant/brand while BatchWindow remains liable for its own negligence.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

The first ten brands come from public retailer/e-commerce catalogues, trade-fair exhibitor lists, small-brand directories and outbound referencing the exact jar, process envelope, plant region, date and landed unit price. The founder contacts 100 qualified brands, not a general audience. Three deposits fill the first day; ten brands should create three executed days and at least two reorders.

The first 100 brands come from repeated dates in the same format, plant referrals for technically unsuitable micro-clients, packaging-supplier referrals and proven on-time runs. Distribution remains outbound/referral until deposits, reorder rate and acquisition cost justify self-service. Plants are acquired by offering funded, technically screened utilisation rather than software.

## Primary Control Point

The control point is the combined contract and payment position:

- named, dated production-window option with nomination/assignment rights;
- pre-agreed plant price, technical envelope, cancellation and capacity terms;
- brand deposits and take-or-pay orders covering the obligation;
- BatchWindow-controlled technical-readiness gate and run sequence;
- routing of booking, payment, yield, rejection, lead-time and reorder data.

A manufacturer introduction, non-binding availability email or directory listing is not a control point. One executed window is Tier 2 transactional control, not a monopoly. Portfolio control strengthens only when multiple plants reserve repeat capacity because BatchWindow reliably arrives with prepared funded demand.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Contract one independent food technologist for the first product envelope, liability map and plant checklist.
2. Screen 30 Polish/CEE plants for the same jar/process and approach ten with one proposal: a 90-day option on a named day, <=12,000 PLN option fee, plant deposit due only after nominated brands fund it.
3. Verify certification, insurance, recall process, sample approval, minimum batch, changeover, ingredient/packaging custody and manufacturer-of-record status before option.
4. Build 100-brand list and conduct 20 problem/qualification calls around date certainty, current MOQ, stock risk and cash budget.
5. Sign only an option with nomination rights, fixed price and 110% deposit-coverage condition.
6. Collect at least three unrelated brand deposits totalling >=30,000 PLN by day 180. No plant production commitment before that threshold.
7. Complete samples/approvals and execute the first day inside month 12 using brand cash and at most the original 100,000 PLN.

An unknown founder can secure the first right because the plant risks only a date option and receives a single screened coordinator plus committed money. Brands see the named plant, format, price, conditions and refund triggers before paying. Proof before serious capital is the signed window right, technologist approval, three deposits and plant-accepted batch files.

## Distribution Advantage

The offer can name a date, specification and landed price, so outbound is materially more concrete than “we can find a co-packer.” Every rejected brand reveals why batches fail readiness; every run reveals actual yield, changeover and lead time. Plant referrals can route micro-clients that would otherwise consume sales/quality time.

The distribution moat is weak until repeat plants give BatchWindow first look at cancellable or reserved dates and brands reorder through the rail. Paid search, content, influencers and a broad marketplace are excluded from the POC.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Dominate one ambient jar/process envelope in Poland with executed dates and repeat brands.
2. Add a second plant capable of the identical envelope, creating resilience without changing the brand specification.
3. Sell recurring quarterly window subscriptions to brands and take-or-pay calendar blocks to plants.
4. Expand to adjacent jar sizes or shelf-stable liquid categories only after each has a separate validated envelope.
5. Build a European capacity portfolio where plants trust the readiness score because it predicts successful runs and cash collection.
6. Add packaging procurement and quality services only when routed transactions already control demand.

Wonnda, Agilery or a large plant can add reservation features. BatchWindow can defend only through better actual run data, repeat demand, plant reliability and a portfolio of contracted windows. The idea dies if that speed/compounding advantage does not materialise.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers food manufacturing capability, lower operating costs than Western Europe, EU market access and a local-language advantage in plant diligence. CEE brands often need lower runs than large Western co-packers prefer. These are execution advantages, not barriers. EU food-law, label and manufacturer-of-record responsibilities remain with named parties; local knowledge must reduce failed batches and contract time.

## Compounding Advantage

Each window adds non-public actuals: which formulas pass, readiness defects, yield, changeover time, packaging failures, plant reliability, real lead time, landed cost, brand reorder and default behaviour. That data improves which brands can safely share a day and lets BatchWindow price/collateralise options with less waste. Reordering brands make future dates easier to fund; successful funded dates make plants more willing to reserve capacity.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder remains employed and limit optional distributions to 5%. Strong reinvests 100%. Cash funds technologists, plant acquisition, insurance, payment operations and software—not factory assets or brand inventory. Customer deposits must remain segregated/appropriately safeguarded and cannot be treated as profit before the relevant production obligation is satisfied.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–30: food technologist, narrow envelope, plant/brand lists, legal/insurance scoping.
- Days 31–75: ten plant negotiations, one audited named-window option <=12,000 PLN, sample/readiness template.
- Days 76–120: 100 qualified brands contacted, 20 calls, at least six technically/financially qualified.
- Days 121–180: three unrelated deposits totalling >=30,000 PLN and covering 110% of the plant's non-cancellable exposure; plant accepts all three batch files.
- Months 7–12: execute, settle, measure contribution, delivery, rejects and at least one reorder.

Founder pre-proof loss is capped at 35,000 PLN and work remains 15–20 hours/week. The paid proof is the three deposits against a named option, not interviews or an LOI.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Remain employed until at least ten executed windows, ten independent reordering brands, two qualified plants in the same envelope, six months positive fully loaded contribution, repeatable brand acquisition with measured payback, no founder-funded production exposure and 12 months personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If no window or deposits exist, retain only brand/plant maps, the technical envelope, readiness checklist, sample templates, legal/liability map and rejection data. Refund deposits under disclosed conditions. No plant, inventory, packaging stock or specialised equipment is purchased. A failed shared-day thesis may support a narrower paid readiness product, but that service cannot preserve the €10m claim without transaction control.

## Critical Evidence

- Low-MOQ EU co-packing exists.
- Manufacturer-discovery and sourcing platforms already exist, forcing a stronger capacity-control wedge.
- The first control point and demand remain unproved until a signed nominated option and three payments exist.

## Critical Hypotheses

- A certified plant will grant a named nominated option <=12,000 PLN.
- Three brands can share the narrow envelope without uneconomic clean-down or liability.
- Brands pay deposits for date certainty and accept standardized packaging.
- An 8–12% POC fee and 7–10% scale fee survive plant/brand direct dealing.
- Actual run data causes plants to reserve repeat capacity and brands to reorder.

## Kill Criteria

- No qualified plant option after ten plant negotiations.
- Option >12,000 PLN or plant deposit due before brand funding.
- Fewer than six qualified brands after 100 targeted approaches.
- No three unrelated deposits totalling >=30,000 PLN by day 180.
- Deposits cover <110% of non-cancellable obligation.
- Plant will not accept nomination/assignment or all three technical files.
- Platform faces uncapped recall/product liability.
- First run contribution <20%, delivery misses agreed date, or >5% units rejected for platform-preventable readiness errors.
- No brand reorder within six months or no second plant for the same envelope within 12 months.
- Founder must finance ingredients, packaging or production receivables.
- Evidence cannot support the conservative €90m routed-value/25%+ margin/55%+ ownership bridge.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Pre-proof <=35,000 PLN: 8,000 food technologist, 7,000 legal/insurance, 12,000 option, 5,000 samples/travel and 3,000 tooling. Post-proof original-cap ceiling 100,000 PLN including reserves and first execution. Founder time 15–20 hours/week. Minimum stack: founder, independent food technologist/quality manager and food/commercial lawyer; plant performs manufacturing and brand owns formula/label approvals.
