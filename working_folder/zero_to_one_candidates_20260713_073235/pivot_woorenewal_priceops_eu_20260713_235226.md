# Zero-to-One Pivot: WooRenewal PriceOps EU

## Preserved Insight

The scarce initial asset is not plugin source code. It is an official update channel already installed in live WooCommerce stores, combined with merchant configuration, organic discovery, billing, support history, and the right to preserve the workflow.

## Structural Change

Stop building a portfolio of generic annual-licence plugins. Acquire only one WooCommerce bulk price/catalogue editor with an installed organic base, then turn it into **PriceOps EU**: a controlled price-change workflow for EU merchants and WooCommerce agencies that combines bulk editing with preview, maker-checker approval, immutable price history, 30-day reference-price calculation, rollback, and evidence export.

The buyer, painful event, price, and compounding asset change. The buyer becomes an EU merchant or agency responsible for large catalogue price changes. The painful event is not “I need another plugin feature”; it is “a bulk price change can publish the wrong price, erase margin, misstate a promotion, or leave no defensible record.” Value is captured through higher-priced operational and agency subscriptions rather than by acquiring many small renewal books.

## Target Niche and Buyer

The beachhead is Polish and EU WooCommerce merchants with at least 1,000 SKUs or weekly promotional/price-file changes, plus agencies operating price changes for at least five client stores. The budget owner is the ecommerce manager, owner-operator, or agency principal who bears the cost of catalogue mistakes, client remediation, and consumer-law evidence.

The acquisition target is one independent bulk-editor plugin with:

- at least 1,000 organic active installations;
- an official WordPress.org slug and assignable ownership;
- existing paid Freemius or equivalent billing;
- preview or undo capability worth preserving;
- low support and no unresolved critical vulnerability;
- total cash requirement at or below 28,000 PLN before paid proof.

A public 2026 seller post claims exactly this broad asset shape at USD 6,000, 1,000+ installs, USD 317 monthly revenue, zero recent support tickets, organic acquisition, and transferable WordPress/Freemius assets. The seller, product, cohorts, code, and availability remain unverified hypotheses.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The customer changes hundreds or thousands of prices before a promotion, supplier update, margin correction, or seasonal sale. A wrong filter, stale import, conflicting plugin, or failed partial update can publish unintended prices across the catalogue. EU price-reduction rules also require merchants to manage prior-price information; agencies need evidence of what changed, who approved it, and how the displayed reference price was produced.

Existing paid bulk editors and Omnibus plugins prove that merchants already pay separately for editing and reference-price functionality. The pivot hypothesis is that catalogue-heavy merchants and agencies will pay more for a single controlled operational workflow with approval, rollback, and evidence than for another display-only utility.

## Value and Profit Wedge

Preserve the acquired editor for current users, then offer three new annual tiers after legal and technical review:

- Merchant: approximately 990 PLN per store/year for audited bulk changes, rollback, and evidence history;
- Agency: approximately 4,900 PLN/year for up to ten stores, approval roles, and client evidence exports;
- Agency Plus: approximately 9,900 PLN/year for up to thirty stores and centralized change policies.

The Year-5 satisfactory mix is approximately 300 Merchant accounts, 100 Agency accounts, and 22 Agency Plus accounts: about 1,004,800 PLN recognized annual revenue before discounts. At a minimum 25% operating margin, operating profit must exceed 251,000 PLN after support, legal maintenance, security, hosting, contractors, refunds, and founder replacement cost.

This mix is a hypothesis. It must be supported by paid upgrade behavior, not inferred from free installs.

## Domination Wedge

Dominate one narrow job: controlled bulk promotional-price operations for WooCommerce agencies and catalogue-heavy EU merchants. PriceOps does not attempt to become a full ERP, generic compliance suite, store builder, or broad plugin portfolio.

The product becomes the system of record for price-change intent, approval, execution, prior-price calculation, rollback, and evidence. Once a merchant has accumulated price history, approval policies, and agency/client evidence in the workflow, replacement becomes operationally costly.

## Acquisition Channel

The cold-start channel is the acquired WordPress.org installation base and organic listing. Existing users continue to receive the current editor. PriceOps beta is offered only through guideline-compliant, non-spam upgrade surfaces and explicit opt-in communication.

Additional acquisition comes from WordPress directory/search demand for bulk price editing and EU price history, technical documentation, evidence templates, and merchant referrals. No affiliate partnership, influencer, reseller, or platform endorsement is required in the POC.

## Primary Control Point

The initial control point is atomic ownership of the official update channel, installed editor base, billing, licence server, domain, brand, repository/IP, and support history. The compounding control point is each customer's locally retained price-operation ledger: historical prices, change sets, approvals, rollback checkpoints, store-specific rules, and evidence exports.

The merchant owns its data and can export it. PriceOps' advantage is not secret access to merchant data; it is the accumulated workflow state and compatibility knowledge required to execute safe changes. Any telemetry outside the store is explicit opt-in and documented under WordPress rules.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Identify and independently verify the public USD 6,000 seller/product or another named bulk-editor target from the completed 903-plugin screen.
2. Obtain a primary data room proving the WordPress owner, Freemius product, receipts, payer count, refunds, support, code/IP, and transferability.
3. Reject the target unless all assets transfer through escrow for no more than 28,000 PLN cash and total pre-proof exposure remains below 40,000 PLN.
4. Receive an unrelated legacy renewal/new licence after transfer.
5. Conduct independent security review and obtain written Polish/EU counsel scope for the price-history behavior; do not market a blanket legal guarantee.
6. Build only the approval, operation-ledger, rollback, and evidence beta required for the acquired installed base.
7. Obtain at least ten paid PriceOps beta upgrades or agency deposits by Day 180. Legacy renewal alone proves the acquired asset, not the pivot's higher-value demand.

An unknown founder can attempt this because a public target has been offered within the cash cap, formal WordPress/Freemius transfer mechanisms exist, and the founder can present escrow, independent security review, transparent stewardship, and an EU-specific product plan. Seller trust and target legitimacy remain the first disconfirming dependencies.

## Expansion Path

1. Polish catalogue-heavy merchants and WooCommerce agencies.
2. Other EU markets using the same core price-reduction framework, with country-specific legal review and evidence wording.
3. Adjacent controlled catalogue operations—stock, promotions, and supplier price imports—only when they reuse the same approval, operation-ledger, rollback, and evidence engine.
4. A second acquisition only if it brings the same agencies/merchants into the same controlled catalogue workflow.

This expands one operations engine rather than restarting a plugin roll-up.

## Minimum People Stack

- Founder: cohort analysis, product/data workflow, sourcing, AI-assisted engineering and operations.
- Contract senior WordPress/WooCommerce security engineer: acquisition audit and release controls.
- Polish/EU consumer-law counsel: bounded behavior and evidence review, not ongoing legal consulting to customers.
- Part-time support/QA contractor only after paid PriceOps demand.

## Principal Risk

The strongest risk is that existing bulk editors or Omnibus plugins can add approval, history, and rollback quickly, leaving PriceOps as a feature bundle rather than a durable workflow. The second risk is that the public USD 6,000 target is unavailable, unverifiable, or lacks 100 paying sites and renewal history. The third is that agencies will not pay the proposed annual tiers because they prefer manual procedures or existing tools.

## First Disconfirming Test

Before any acquisition commitment, require all three:

1. a named, independently verifiable bulk-editor seller and plugin within the 28,000 PLN cash cap;
2. primary evidence of at least 100 paid sites or an installed base and paid cohort large enough to offer ten credible beta upgrades;
3. five non-binding agency interviews or written responses showing that approval, rollback, price history, and evidence are a budgeted workflow rather than merely desirable features.

No external contact is included in the current research authorization. If these facts cannot be obtained once outreach is authorized, kill PriceOps rather than buying a generic editor.

## Evidence Preserved

- WordPress.org owner transfer is formally documented.
- Freemius product ownership and billing transfer are technically supported.
- The completed public screen contains 903 plugins and 41 pre-data-room WooCommerce workflow leads.
- A public WooCommerce bulk-editor seller claimed a USD 6,000 ask, 1,000+ installs, organic traffic, Freemius billing, and low support.
- A larger WordPress plugin precedent showed that 6,000 free installs can support 500–600 paid users and organic acquisition, although that asset sold for six figures.

## New Hypotheses

- The public target is real, available, secure, and atomically transferable.
- EU catalogue-heavy merchants and agencies perceive bulk price-change evidence as a high-cost operational job.
- Paid annual tiers near 990/4,900/9,900 PLN produce acceptable conversion and retention.
- Per-store history and approval policies create meaningful switching costs.
- Existing competitors lack an integrated price-operations workflow or will not move inward fast enough.
- Legal review can define a useful product without requiring regulated legal advice or a blanket compliance guarantee.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

WooRenewal v2 depended on growing or repeatedly acquiring generic renewal books until 1,100 accounts existed. PriceOps EU uses one installed acquisition as distribution for a higher-value, EU-specific operational workflow and aims for roughly 422 customer accounts across merchant and agency tiers. The scarce resource expands from a transferred update channel to accumulated price-operation history, approval policies, rollback checkpoints, and compatibility knowledge. A second acquisition is optional and subordinate to the same workflow rather than the main growth engine.

## Priority

Return PriceOps EU to research first, then `$evaluate-zero-to-one`, because it directly attacks WooRenewal v2's three fatal weaknesses: low revenue per account, repeated acquisition resets, and absence of a compounding workflow control point. Do not evaluate it as pursued until the named target, competitor gap, EU rule scope, and paid agency pain are researched.
