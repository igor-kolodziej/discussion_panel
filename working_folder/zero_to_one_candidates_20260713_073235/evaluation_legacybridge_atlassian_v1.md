# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Atlassian LegacyBridge v1

## Diagnosis

LegacyBridge improves materially on WooRenewal because Atlassian has formal app transfers, platform billing, higher B2B price tiers and a dated Data Center-to-Cloud discontinuity. The acquired cohort and migration path could solve cold start and produce a real Tier-1 position after closing. As written, however, it is an acquisition specification rather than a reachable transaction: no owner has expressed sale intent, public installs are not paid licences, and the low-install targets that may fit the cap tend to have simple workflows or strong Cloud substitutes. The Year-5 arithmetic is reasonable, but the 10-to-100 customer bridge and an affordable seller are not evidenced.

For this user-directed run, the original Founder Wealth Gate is replaced by the User-Defined Revenue Quality Gate: 1,000,000 PLN recognised annual revenue by Year 5 and at least 25% fully loaded operating margin.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- Sourced: 995 Jira Data Center apps screened through Atlassian's official API; 457 paid apps with 5–500 installs; 226 with no same-key Cloud version detected; 137 in the 5–60-install band.
- Sourced: Atlassian formally transfers sold/acquired apps with proof of purchase and seller approval; Paid-via-Atlassian supplies billing and renewals; affected Data Center apps end in March 2029; Atlassian says a missing reliable migration path can block customer migration.
- Sourced counterevidence: mature Cloud competitors already cover broad email, notification, test-management, traceability and collaboration workflows. A simple legacy feature is not a protected gap.
- Candidate claim: 20,000 PLN cash plus a receipt-capped note can buy a 60,000-PLN-revenue asset. No public target or seller statement supports this price structure.
- Hypothesis: 100 organisations will pay 10,000 PLN recognised annual revenue. No target-specific price, conversion, CAC or Marketplace discovery evidence supports the growth rate.
- Missing: named willing seller, paid licences, renewal, revenue, support load, Cloud competitive gap, code quality, security history, assignment package and Atlassian pre-clearance.

## Overall Score

**65/100 advisory**. Weighted total: `130.5 ÷ 20 × 10 = 65.25`, rounded to 65. This is not a simulated-panel, working-chat or validation score.

## Control Point

**Tier 3 now; Tier 1 only after atomic Atlassian-approved transfer.** The transferred app key/listing, installed paid licences, IP, support history and obligations would be owned. Migration mappings and regression fixtures could become Tier 2. The strongest copy risk is a leading Cloud app adding a configuration importer or Atlassian absorbing the narrow workflow.

## Founder Wealth Gate / User-Defined Revenue Quality Gate

**Fail on evidence, pass on arithmetic only.** Conservative Year 5 is 360,000 PLN revenue and fails the target. Expected is exactly 1,000,000 PLN at 30% margin and strong is 2,160,000 PLN at 36%. Ownership and dilution are stated and margins include platform/support/maintenance. The decisive weakness is not the 100-customer endpoint; it is the unsupported starting asset, 85 gross new-logo requirement, price/CAC, and retention of a small app against mature Cloud substitutes.

## Reachability Gate

**Fail currently.** The proposed post-transfer proof—three independent payments within 180 days including one Cloud payment—is good and the 40,000 PLN tranche is explicit. But the clock cannot start without a willing seller, verified economics, legal/security diligence and Atlassian approval. Those are sequential, uncontrolled dependencies, and no exact target has passed even the public screen. A plausible plan is not current seller access.

## Expansion Check

The inherited cohort is a legitimate beachhead. Marketplace discovery and CMA reduce the need for partnerships. New Cloud customers are a logical adjacency, but a target with a simple feature will be boxed in by incumbents, while a complex target may be unaffordable and too demanding for a part-time founder. There is no material Polish advantage.

## Fatal Objections

1. No willing, affordable seller or qualifying data room; 137 public leads do not equal three transferable targets.
2. Low-install assets likely to fit the cash cap skew toward simple, copyable workflows; valuable high-price apps are likely worth more than the cap.
3. The 10-to-100 paying-organisation bridge has no target-specific conversion, CAC or price evidence.
4. Data Center urgency is also asset decay: the inherited revenue expires if the Cloud successor is late or rejected.
5. The company remains dependent on Atlassian distribution, technical APIs, trust rules and native roadmap.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

An officially transferable, platform-billed B2B app whose customers face a dated forced migration is a stronger distribution asset than a generic plugin update channel.

## Recommendation

research further

The only decision-critical research that can change this verdict is seller-side evidence: three owners must disclose paid licences, renewals, revenue, support load, Cloud plans, complete transfer willingness and a price/seller-note structure inside the capital gates. Public browsing cannot substitute for those disclosures, and seller contact is outside the current authorisation.
