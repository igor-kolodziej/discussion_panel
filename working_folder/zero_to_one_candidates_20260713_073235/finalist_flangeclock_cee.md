# FlangeClock CEE — Conditional Finalist Brief

**Generator status, 13 July 2026:** this is a decision-ready build thesis, not evidence that a protected territory, conforming product, accredited calibration path, or lifecycle gap exists. The route survives only if those conditions are proved in the order below. HYTORC Poland and JB International already bundle much of the apparent offer; Enerpac supplies a broad bolting line with calibration documentation. Those are central objections, not footnotes.

## One-Sentence Thesis

FlangeClock CEE would sell industrial maintenance teams and specialist bolting contractors a named, serialized, shutdown-ready torque/tensioning kit—with documented EU conformity, exact-scope accredited calibration, a reserved loaner, critical-spares priority, and the next calibration slot prepaid—using protected active-selling rights for a selected manufacturer only as the entry wedge, then retaining the installed-base record and lifecycle payment relationship as the durable control path.

The thesis is explicitly conditional: it is false if the territory/customer group is already assigned; if lawful, durable active-selling protection cannot be contracted; if the exact SKUs lack a valid EU conformity route; if independent calibration is not accepted for warranty and procurement; or if HYTORC, Enerpac channels, JB International, another local specialist, or a generic distributor can match the complete fixed offer within five business days and 10% of price.

## Founder Fit

The founder is a Warsaw-based data scientist with 100,000 PLN of available capital, a retained full-time job, no desire to be a public personality, limited social energy, and at most five hours per day for the venture. FlangeClock fits only in a deliberately narrow operating shape:

- The founder owns account selection, structured outreach, quoting logic, supplier economics, deposit collection, installed-base data, and lifecycle renewal automation.
- A contracted technical seller/bolting engineer runs demonstrations and technical qualification one day per week; the founder does not perform on-site bolting, inspection, installation, or shutdown labor.
- Outreach is named-account, asynchronous, and evidence-led: one fixed offer, one conformity pack, one kit comparison, and scheduled demonstrations—not content marketing, events, or founder-led social media.
- Customer deposits finance supplier deposits and freight. Founder capital buys evidence and coordination capacity, not speculative inventory.
- The founder remains employed until the full-time transition gate is met. Family money is excluded.

The founder's data advantage is useful only if it becomes an operational asset: a clean denominator, account scoring, quote conversion history, serial/certificate records, due-date forecasting, loaner utilization, spare demand, and renewal collections. Analytics alone is not the product.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

**Primary buyer:** the maintenance manager, reliability/asset-integrity lead, or owner of an independent industrial bolting contractor in Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Lithuania, Latvia, or Estonia. Procurement and EHS/quality commonly co-sign. The first segment is organizations that own or repeatedly deploy high-torque tools for planned shutdowns, turbines, pressure systems, large flanges, shipyard work, process plants, power assets, or heavy-equipment maintenance.

**Job to be done:** have the correct tool, pump, sockets/accessories, readable conformity and calibration documents, and a replacement path available before a named shutdown window—without assembling the package across vendors or losing the tool for an uncontrolled calibration interval.

**Economic pain:** a missing adapter, uncertain certificate, delayed repair, unavailable loaner, or missed delivery can idle a crew or jeopardize a planned maintenance window. The brief does not assign a euro value to that pain until ten buyers provide documented delay costs or procurement consequences.

**Current workaround and incumbent objection:** buyers can already purchase, rent, calibrate, service, and train through established specialists. HYTORC Poland says it has represented the brand since 1990 and offers sales, demonstrations, technical advice, training, warranty/post-warranty service, rental, and bolting work. JB International says it has operated in Poland since 1990 and offers multi-type torque tools, rental, repair, training, calibration across brands, and certificates through an accredited European laboratory. Enerpac publishes a broad bolting catalogue, calibration certificates/instructions, service, and distributor routing. Buyers may also coordinate a local calibration laboratory or generic industrial distributor themselves.

Therefore the open question is not “do customers need torque tools or calibration?” It is: **will a defined cohort prepay for one fixed delivery-and-lifecycle commitment that incumbents do not already provide on comparable terms?**

## Product Wedge

The first paid product is one configured **FlangeClock Shutdown Kit**, not a catalogue and not a referral:

- one selected hydraulic, pneumatic, electric, or battery torque wrench or tensioning configuration;
- matched pump/power unit where required, hoses, sockets/cassettes, reaction accessories, seals, and a documented critical-spares pack;
- manufacturer EU declaration(s), instructions, serials, labels, and the exact applicable conformity matrix for every component;
- independent initial calibration/certificate from a laboratory whose accredited scope explicitly covers the exact measurand/range/method, plus written manufacturer acceptance that the route preserves warranty;
- Polish and English job cards covering contents, permitted use, pre-use checks, certificate location, due date, and escalation; these do not replace manufacturer instructions or engineering procedures;
- a named delivery window and five-business-day acceptance process;
- a reserved loaner class for the next calibration event, subject to the contracted service level;
- critical-spares priority and prepaid pickup/calibration/return slot for the first 12-month lifecycle period; the actual interval follows the manufacturer, buyer policy, use, and applicable standard rather than a universal annual claim;
- a FlangeClock asset record joining model, serial, accessories, certificate, owner, delivery, warranty, service, replacement, and payment history.

**Launch customer economics, working hypothesis:** €32,000 ex-VAT fixed price per configured kit. Estimated direct cost is €18,200: €14,200 tool/pump/accessories, €1,400 freight/import handling, €800 calibration and documentation, €800 local configuration, and €1,000 warranty/defect reserve. That is €13,800 gross profit and 43.1% gross margin before variable selling cost. Allocating €1,800 for technical selling/demo activity leaves €12,000 contribution, or 37.5%. Quotes must use actual SKU costs and FX buffers; these are test assumptions, not supplier prices.

**Lifecycle renewal hypothesis:** €4,800 ex-VAT per installed kit/year for scheduled collection, exact-scope calibration, certificate/record update, reserved loaner for up to 14 calendar days, and critical-spares priority. Estimated direct cost is €2,100, giving €2,700 gross profit and 56.3% gross margin; after €300 variable administration/selling cost, contribution is €2,400, or 50%.

FlangeClock sells the product and lifecycle commitment directly. The customer owns and controls the equipment after payment. FlangeClock does not perform bolting, certify the customer's joint, inspect a plant, provide engineering sign-off, or earn a lead/referral fee.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The possible gap is narrow: a foreign tool maker may have no visibly listed distributor for this precise CEE territory/customer group, while established Polish offers appear broad but may not commit in one fixed contract to a configured multi-component kit, exact delivery window, independent exact-scope calibration, reserved loaner, spares priority, and a prepaid renewal record.

Tritorc's current distributor page advertises exclusive regional rights, margins, training, and engineering support and visibly lists only the Netherlands under Europe. That proves an available *contracting route*, not that Poland or any CEE territory is free, not that Tritorc is the selected supplier, and not that “exclusive” can lawfully mean an absolute territorial monopoly. The distribution agreement must use counsel-reviewed protected active-selling territory/customer-group terms consistent with EU vertical-restraint law; passive-sales restrictions cannot simply be assumed.

The gap disappears if incumbent mystery-shopping shows that HYTORC Poland, JB International, an Enerpac channel, a local calibration coordinator, or a generic distributor already returns the same complete package, lead time, documents, loaner, and renewal commitment within five business days at no more than 10% price difference. The business cannot rely on “local service” as a novelty: local specialists already advertise it.

## Underused Momentum

FlangeClock does not depend on creating a new maintenance category. It recombines existing, funded behaviors:

- industrial buyers already procure torque/tensioning tools, repairs, rental, calibration, training, and spares;
- shutdowns and certificate due dates create known purchase and renewal windows;
- manufacturers already use distributor agreements and provide technical training/support;
- Poland has a national accreditation system and a searchable laboratory directory; exact scope still must be verified for each model and range;
- product serials, certificates, delivery dates, and loaner reservations are structured data that incumbents may not expose as one buyer-facing lifecycle record;
- a new imported SKU must in any case produce conformity, importer, traceability, warranty, and recall records, so the same compliance work can seed the installed-base system.

The underused combination is a deposit-funded, fixed product-and-lifecycle contract tied to a shutdown date—not growth in industrial tooling, which this brief does not claim.

**Contract territory/customer group:** active selling to independent bolting contractors and industrial maintenance teams in Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia, excluding only named legacy accounts attached to the executed agreement. This is the requested commercial scope, not a claim that rights are available.

**First cohort:** organizations that meet all five filters:

1. own, rent, or deploy powered torque/tensioning equipment at least four times per year;
2. have a named maintenance/shutdown date within nine months;
3. require traceable calibration or procurement evidence;
4. have a maintenance, reliability, or owner-level buyer reachable directly; and
5. can approve a €20,000–€50,000 equipment purchase or deposit without a multi-year public tender.

**Denominator discipline:** Eurostat reports 2.2 million EU manufacturing enterprises and 30.2 million persons employed in manufacturing in 2023. That is only a broad ceiling and must never be presented as the addressable market. By day 45, the founder must produce at least 8,000 named CEE plus adjacent-EU sites/contractors that visibly meet the industrial-use criteria, with country, sector, equipment/use evidence, decision role, and source. Duplicates, generic company records, and sites without likely powered high-torque work do not count. Before the plan exceeds 600 active accounts, expand and document at least 20,000 eligible European accounts; before the strong case relies on 2,000 accounts, document at least 40,000. If 8,000 cannot be built, the wealth case is withdrawn.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Revenue has three direct lines:

1. configured kit sale, initially modeled at €32,000 ex-VAT;
2. prepaid annual lifecycle renewal, initially €4,800 per kit;
3. replacement tools, accessories, compatible spares, and additional kits to the installed account.

There are no referral, consulting, inspection, or on-site bolting revenues in the model. Customer acquisition cost includes all demo and technical seller time, even when a manufacturer subsidizes it.

**Cash terms with the buyer:** 40% order deposit, 50% before dispatch after serial allocation and document-pack approval, and 10% after five-business-day acceptance. The deposit is refundable if conformity evidence fails, the agreed configuration cannot be supplied, or delivery is more than 30 calendar days late; otherwise cancellation costs are deducted only to the extent committed to the supplier. Title transfers after full payment. The first lifecycle slot is itemized in the kit contract; renewal is invoiced 90 days before its due window and must be prepaid to reserve a loaner.

**Target supplier cash terms:** no more than 20% on order and 80% before dispatch for the first ten orders, moving to net-30 after ten paid deliveries. The 40% customer deposit must cover the supplier deposit, freight commitment, and configuration cost. No order may create a negative cash gap above 10,000 PLN without a specific capital-tranche approval.

**Margin gates:** each kit quote must show at least 38% gross margin after landed product, freight, import handling, calibration, configuration, and warranty reserve; lifecycle must show at least 50% gross margin before variable selling/admin. The aggregate model reaches 43%–48% gross margin only through the stated purchase terms, pricing, and lifecycle mix. VAT is excluded; foreign-exchange exposure is quoted with a validity window and buffer.

**Working-capital rule:** inventory is customer-backed except for a capped demo unit, critical spares, and loaner pool. The initial three-unit loaner pool is purchased only after paid deliveries and lifecycle attachment justify it. Supplier credit, customer deposits, and retained gross profit fund scaling; founder/family balance sheets do not silently guarantee it.

Run two regional demo days per month through contracted technical sellers, use manufacturer-routed leads where contractually guaranteed, and ask each installed account for its full fleet schedule. Target 35–45 qualified demonstrations per quarter, a measured quote-to-order rate, and payback on fully loaded acquisition cost within 12 months of gross profit. ## Primary Control Point

The manufacturer right is at most a Tier-2 access advantage after signature; before signature it is no control at all. It must never be represented as a Tier-1 monopoly, and it is not sufficient by itself because another distributor can sign another manufacturer.

The intended control path is the **installed lifecycle position**:

1. At order: FlangeClock creates a kit ID joining selected SKU, serial, accessories, conformity files, certificate, buyer, site, shutdown date, warranty, and payment.
2. At acceptance: the buyer confirms contents and owner; certificate due logic and compatible spare/loaner class enter the record.
3. Ninety days before the applicable calibration window: FlangeClock sends a fixed renewal, loaner class, pickup window, and certificate requirements; prepayment reserves capacity.
4. At exchange: the loaner is dispatched, the owned tool is collected to a qualified lab/service route, QA documents are attached, and the tool is returned.
5. During the year: repairs, spare consumption, configuration changes, downtime requests, and replacement quotes update the account's fleet history.
6. At expansion: the record recommends only compatible additional kits/accessories and reserves inventory against known shutdowns.

By month 12 the position must combine an executed right, complete records for every delivered serial, 60% or greater lifecycle attachment, two independent exact-scope calibration routes accepted by the manufacturer, a customer-funded three-unit loaner pool, renewal prepayments, and observed replacement/spares demand. The customer can leave and owns its equipment; control comes from lower coordination cost, known compatibility, reserved capacity, and accumulated service history—not artificial lock-in.

## Distribution Advantage

The intended advantage is an explicit manufacturer-to-FlangeClock agreement plus a founder-owned named-account process. The agreement sought is:

- **Scope:** listed legal manufacturer, exact SKUs, Poland/Czechia/Slovakia/Lithuania/Latvia/Estonia, and the defined independent-contractor/industrial-maintenance customer group; named legacy accounts are an attached, closed schedule.
- **Protection:** manufacturer will not actively sell or appoint another active seller into the protected group except the legacy schedule; inbound leads from the group route to FlangeClock within one business day. Any permitted manufacturer direct sale pays FlangeClock 15% of net product invoice and transfers the asset/lead record. All terms receive EU VBER counsel review; unlawful passive-sales restrictions are excluded.
- **Term:** three years, no convenience termination during the initial term, automatic two-year renewals if objective order-backed targets are met, and a 90-day cure period for remediable breach. Targets count paid customer orders, not speculative stock purchases.
- **Economics:** EUR transfer-price schedule fixed for 12 months; changes thereafter require 90 days' notice and follow an agreed input-cost formula, while FlangeClock retains independent resale-pricing authority. FCA named manufacturer warehouse, Incoterms 2020, unless a better delivered term is negotiated. First-ten supplier payment target 20/80, then net-30. The schedule must allow at least 40% planned landed gross margin before local lifecycle costs.
- **Lead times and service levels:** stock spares dispatch within five business days, standard tool within eight weeks, response to warranty claim within two business days, replacement/loaner decision within five, and a service credit of 1% of affected transfer price per late week capped at 10% where delay is supplier-caused.
- **Warranty and continuity:** minimum 24-month pass-through warranty, supplier-funded defect parts and reasonable freight, spare/repair availability for seven years after last sale, recall cooperation, product-liability allocation, and inventory buyback at landed cost on supplier-caused termination or lost conformity.
- **Compliance:** complete model-specific EU declarations, instructions, labels, technical-file access protocol for authorities, importer/authorized-representative allocation, change notices, component traceability, incident/recall SLA, and proof of product-liability insurance before a customer deposit becomes non-refundable.
- **Calibration:** written permission for two named independent accredited routes when they follow approved procedures; no warranty invalidation solely because an approved lab performs calibration; provision of fixtures, settings, software, and tolerances needed to calibrate and repair.
- **Capability transfer:** two technical-training days at launch and two per year, demo support one day per month for six months, Polish/Czech/local-language source files, and remote engineering response within two business days.
- **Data and local asset ownership:** FlangeClock owns its customer list, quote data, kit configurations, local job cards, certificate/due-date history, loaner records, and lifecycle payments. The manufacturer receives only the account identity needed for routing/warranty plus agreed aggregate performance data. FlangeClock may continue serving the installed base for at least 24 months after termination and retains all legally permitted customer records.
- **Non-circumvention and exit:** no bypass of introduced accounts during the term and 12 months after it, subject to counsel; assignment on sale/change of control with consent not unreasonably withheld; arbitration/forum and governing law stated; all unresolved legacy accounts attached at signing.

These are target terms, not current rights. A distributor recruitment page or verbal promise does not satisfy them.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

The first advantage is faster coordination around a configured, conforming, calibrated kit. It compounds only if the business learns which configurations, certificates, spares, loaners, and delivery windows each cohort actually buys.

Expansion proceeds in this order:

1. more tools/accessories and lifecycle renewals inside the same installed accounts;
2. fleet-wide records and replacement schedules for multi-kit contractor and plant accounts;
3. the same fixed lifecycle format in the six-country customer group, with local-language documents and contracted demonstrators;
4. a second manufacturer only where compatibility and customer data remain FlangeClock-controlled and the first agreement allows it;
5. adjacent EU customer groups only after the denominator and local conformity/service routes are rebuilt.

Incumbent counter-moves are expected: HYTORC can discount, bundle rental/service/training, offer on-site work, or use its installed reputation; Enerpac channels can bundle a broad catalogue and calibration support; JB International can service multiple brands and reserve rental stock; local labs and generic distributors can copy document coordination. FlangeClock's answer must be measured, not asserted: higher on-time completeness, lower certificate/loaner coordination time, predictable renewal, and lower acquisition cost from installed-base expansion. If those differences are absent, the expansion path stops.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Local adaptation consists of SKU-level compliance and operational detail:

- Polish and applicable local-language instructions/job cards paired with, not substituted for, manufacturer documentation;
- an applicable-law matrix for every exact SKU and component. Equipment placed on the EU market before the Machinery Regulation applies must follow the then-current regime; Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027. Machinery, EMC, low-voltage, pressure-equipment, RoHS, ATEX, battery, packaging/EPR, and other obligations are included only where counsel's SKU analysis says they apply;
- valid EU declaration(s), CE marking where required, serial traceability, importer details, safety instructions, authority technical-file process, complaint/incident/recall log, and product-liability cover before sale;
- a PCA or equivalently accredited laboratory whose public scope explicitly covers the exact torque/force/pressure measurand, range, uncertainty, and method needed. A laboratory's general ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation or force/pressure scope does not by itself prove torque-tool coverage;
- written acceptance by the manufacturer and target buyer that the independent calibration route preserves warranty and procurement eligibility;
- shutdown-date pickup/return, a compatible loaner class, and local critical spares;
- one Polish/English record joining conformity, serial, certificate, accessories, repair, loaner, and payment.

This defense is useful only if it is faster or more reliable than established local offers. HYTORC Poland already advertises local warranty/post-warranty service, calibration, training, rental, and bolting work. JB International already advertises cross-brand calibration/repair/rental and accredited-laboratory certificates. Their presence makes “Polish service” or “local calibration” alone non-defensible.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid kit can improve five assets:

1. **configuration graph:** which tool, pump, socket, reaction accessory, certificate, and spare combinations work for each use class;
2. **lifecycle calendar:** actual due dates, pickup duration, loaner demand, repair probability, and renewal payment behavior;
3. **procurement evidence:** accepted declarations, certificate formats, buyer objections, price/lead-time benchmarks, and approval routes;
4. **inventory forecast:** cohort-level spare and loaner demand that reduces idle stock while increasing availability;
5. **distribution efficiency:** demonstrated conversion and replacement economics by account type, country, and trigger.

The loop is: more paid kits → denser serial/certificate history → better-timed renewal and inventory → higher on-time availability and lower coordination cost → more fleet expansion and renewals → more paid kits. Data that is not tied to a paid asset, renewal, or operating decision is not counted as compounding advantage.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Retained cash is deployed in this order:

1. warranty/recall and tax reserves;
2. customer-backed working capital and supplier-term improvement;
3. exact-demand loaners and critical spares, using due-date and utilization evidence;
4. technical sellers and service coordination in the densest paid cohorts;
5. compliance/localization and laboratory qualification for the next country/SKU;
6. installed-base record automation and buyer portal;
7. only then, a second supplier or geography.

Capital is not reinvested into broad catalogue inventory, a prestige office, unmanaged trade shows, founder salary before the transition gate, or unrelated consulting. Each reinvestment must show a paid cohort, capacity bottleneck, expected gross-profit effect, and a reversible first tranche.

| Incumbent differentiation | same-spec mystery shops and ten paid/lost quote comparisons | offer becomes a generic distributorship | stop if five-day/10%-price parity is observed |
| Denominator and repeatability | 8,000 audited eligible accounts, then 20,000/40,000 gates | wealth cases require implausible penetration | withdraw affected wealth case and halt geographic spend |
| Product liability/recall insurance | binding cover and supplier allocation | one incident can exceed capital cap | no commercial delivery |
| Working capital | customer deposit covers committed cash; supplier terms improve | founder maximum is breached | cap orders, sequence delivery, or stop; no family bridge |

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder leaves employment only when every condition has held for at least three consecutive months:

- at least 15 unrelated paid accounts and 30 delivered kits;
- rolling-12-month recognized gross profit of at least 300,000 PLN and positive operating contribution after contractor, travel, warranty, insurance, and founder-replacement cost;
- at least 60% lifecycle attachment and either five prepaid renewals or five repeat hardware orders;
- fully loaded customer acquisition payback within 12 months of gross profit;
- executed supplier protection with at least 24 months of expected remaining term and no uncured material dispute;
- two manufacturer-accepted exact-scope calibration routes and a customer-funded loaner pool meeting promised availability;
- a technical seller/service coordinator can run demos and exchanges without founder field labor;
- 12 months of personal runway held outside the 100,000 PLN business-risk budget, plus six months of business fixed costs in the company;
- no unresolved conformity, safety, product-liability, or recall issue.

Founder excitement, a large pipeline, one strategic account, or a signed distributor agreement is not a transition trigger.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If the thesis stops, recover or retain:

- the audited named-account denominator, buyer interviews, incumbent quotes, and coded loss data;
- counsel-reviewed distribution, customer-deposit, conformity, warranty, data, and exit clauses;
- SKU conformity matrix, document checklist, translated job-card template, and recall/serial schema;
- qualified calibration scope records and logistics quotes;
- configuration BOMs, landed-cost calculator, pricing logic, and demo script;
- the installed-base data model and renewal workflow;
- returnable demo equipment, resalable unopened spares, and refundable deposits under the negotiated clauses.

These assets may support another conforming product-led industrial route, but this brief does not authorize a consulting service, referral brokerage, inspection business, generic catalogue distributor, or on-site bolting company as a fallback. Recovery is designed to keep the maximum loss bounded, not to rationalize continuing a failed shape.

## Critical Hypotheses

1. A suitable manufacturer has the defined six-country/customer-group active-selling scope unassigned and will sign the exact protection, economics, data, compliance, calibration, continuity, and exit terms.
2. At least one launch SKU can be lawfully imported/placed on the EU market with complete model-specific documents and tolerable product-liability risk.
3. Two accredited laboratories have the exact scope/range/method, can meet turnaround, and are accepted by manufacturer warranty and buyer procurement.
4. Ten or more launch buyers prefer the fixed shutdown-kit/lifecycle promise to the existing HYTORC, Enerpac, JB International, local-lab, rental, and generic-distributor options.
5. Buyers will place a 40% deposit before FlangeClock holds the product.
6. The €32,000 kit supports at least 38% landed gross margin and positive contribution; the €4,800 lifecycle plan supports at least 50% gross margin.
7. At least 60% of installed kits attach to prepaid lifecycle service and renew often enough to fund loaner capacity.
8. A contracted technical seller can run demos and qualification safely without founder field labor and without consuming the gross margin.
9. The founder can source at least 8,000 audited eligible accounts in the initial/adjacent geography and later 20,000/40,000 for the larger cases.
10. Installed serial, certificate, loaner, compatible-spare, and payment history materially lowers coordination cost or raises availability versus incumbent offers.
11. Supplier lead times, warranty remedies, spares, and transfer pricing remain stable enough for fixed customer commitments.
12. Customer deposits, retained gross profit, and supplier terms finance working capital without breaching 100,000 PLN of founder capital.

## Kill Criteria

Stop this specific business shape if any of the following occurs:

- the six-country/customer-group territory is assigned, materially encumbered, or the legacy-account schedule removes the beachhead;
- the supplier offers only revocable/non-protected resale, insists on convenience termination, direct account ownership, speculative stock targets, or terms weaker than a three-year order-backed agreement with cure and exit rights;
- EU competition counsel cannot support the proposed active-selling protection or the economics depend on unlawful passive-sales restrictions;
- any launch SKU lacks a valid model-specific EU conformity/importer/technical-file/recall path before sale;
- exact independent calibration cannot be shown in two laboratory scopes, is rejected by the manufacturer or target buyers, or leaves the lifecycle economics/control entirely with the OEM;
- HYTORC Poland, an Enerpac channel, JB International, another local specialist, or a generic distributor can provide the same configured/calibrated/documented kit, comparable loaner/spares commitment, and renewal record within five business days and within 10% of price in three of five same-spec mystery shops;
- unbacked inventory or demo commitments exceed 25,000 PLN, founder cash at risk exceeds 40,000 PLN by day 180, or any plan requires more than 100,000 PLN of founder capital;
- actual kit gross margin is below 38% after landed cost, configuration, calibration, and warranty reserve, or lifecycle gross margin is below 50%;
- fewer than two unrelated 40% deposits exist by day 120 or fewer than three by day 180;
- the first delivery is not contribution-positive after technical selling, logistics, calibration, and warranty reserve;
- month-12 lifecycle attachment is below 60%, or no repeat order/renewal prepayment exists;
- the founder must perform on-site bolting, inspection, engineering sign-off, routine field service, or more than five hours/day to fulfill the promise;
- a qualified 8,000-account bottom-up denominator cannot be produced by day 45, 20,000 before relying on more than 600 accounts, or 40,000 before the 2,000-account strong case;
- product-liability insurance, supplier recall allocation, or warranty continuity cannot be secured within the capital cap.

Passing a kill criterion requires contemporaneous documents, quotes, paid orders, and delivery records; optimism, manufacturer assurances, or pipeline value do not override it.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

**180-day founder cash budget: 40,000 PLN maximum**

- 8,000 PLN — distribution/VBER, customer-contract, importer/conformity, liability, and data/exit legal review;
- 5,000 PLN — exact-SKU document review, translations, labels/job cards, and conformity checklist;
- 12,000 PLN — refundable demo-unit deposit or rental, sample freight, and inspection;
- 4,000 PLN — two calibration-route scope checks, sample calibration/turnaround, and logistics qualification;
- 5,000 PLN — contracted technical seller/demonstrator;
- 3,000 PLN — named-account data, outreach, travel, and demo consumables;
- 1,500 PLN — product-liability/transport insurance setup and bookkeeping;
- 1,500 PLN — contingency, released only after a deposit or compliance need.

Supplier product deposits for customer orders must be funded by customer deposits and are not hidden inside the founder-risk budget.

**Founder time:** 18–22 hours per week, never more than five hours in a day. Suggested allocation: eight hours account research/outreach, five hours supplier/contract/compliance, four hours quotes/follow-up, three hours data/operations, and two hours contractor coordination. Field demos are batched and led by the contractor.

**Minimum people stack:**

- founder: account system, commercial ownership, deposits, economics, data, and supplier negotiation;
- contract EU distribution/product lawyer: approximately 15–25 hours through the first executable agreement;
- contract bolting engineer/technical seller: one day per week during proof, paid per day plus a capped order commission included in acquisition cost;
- two independent accredited calibration/service routes with exact written scopes;
- freight/customs broker and product-liability insurer;
- part-time bookkeeping/VAT support.

No full-time employee, cofounder, warehouse staff, or family labor is required before the month-12 gate. If fulfillment needs them earlier, the cold-start design has failed.
