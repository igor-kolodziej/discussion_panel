# Zero-to-One Evaluation: MicroRouteFort Bronze Conversion v1

**Candidate integrity:** SHA-256 confirmed as `4a9d50855db8f53a4f54ffbe33e212416fc2ff786485b0c847c1de79c4832a0e`.

## Diagnosis

This is a disciplined, capital-gated way to test whether a serviced-yard lease can be turned into saleable secure-parking inventory, and it fits the founder substantially better than a greenfield truck-stop project. The narrow site can plausibly become a profitable local asset if the host bundle, lease, conversion quote and fleet deposits all exist on the stated terms. However, the advantage is mainly site-level contractual control rather than a durable corridor-wide monopoly, and every expansion site resets the hardest host, legal, service and fleet dependencies. The paid-proof plan is credible enough to pass the Reachability Gate, but the stated path to at least €10m of founder net worth depends on too many favorable scale assumptions with almost no cushion, so the Founder Wealth Gate fails.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- **Founder facts:** Warsaw-based data scientist, employed full-time, maximum five hours per day, 100,000 PLN original capital, no privileged introductions, low preference for continuous high-energy selling, and a requirement to remain employed until the transition gate. The analytical mapping, pricing and automation work fits; repeated cold landlord/fleet selling and physical-asset operations fit less well.
- **Candidate-reported external evidence, not independently revalidated in this judgment:** an EU shortage of 390,057 safe and secure spaces, explicit Bronze standards, policy support for rapid upgrades and prior CEF funding. These facts support pain and timing, not the availability or economics of a particular Polish host.
- **Specific proof design:** a named host option, service documentation, legal review, independent pre-audit, a fixed conversion quote and deposits from two unrelated fleets. These are proposed gates, not yet executed evidence.
- **Consequential hypotheses:** suitable fully serviced and separable host inventory exists; a host will grant a 10–15-year assignable lease and service SLA; conversion fits 60,000 PLN; fleets will pay €16–20 per night and deposit before certification; fully loaded site EBITDA remains at least 25%; and the model can repeat across 35 sites averaging 80 bays.
- **Missing evidence:** executed host rights, a pre-audit, integrator and insurer terms, actual fleet deposits or take-or-pay contracts, net-price/VAT treatment, fully loaded operating costs, occupancy/renewal data, a scalable host count, and a year-by-year financing/dilution bridge.
- **External verdicts:** none inspected or used. This judgment is independent.

## Overall Score

**64/100.** Weighted points are **128.5** across a total weight of **20**: `10 × (128.5 / 20) = 64.25`, rounded to **64**.

## Control Point

**Tier 1, conditionally, at each executed site.** An executed 10–15-year assignable lease with explicit HGV use, service SLA, fit-out, booking, data and sub-use rights directly controls scarce named inventory; fleet blocks and payment flow strengthen that control. It is only a proposed Tier-1 control point until those documents and payments exist, and it remains local rather than portfolio-wide.

The strongest copy risk is an incumbent truck-service-yard operator, certified-parking chain or fleet-booking platform applying the same conversion playbook to other serviced yards. Such an incumbent may already possess landlord trust, fleet demand, insurance credibility, operations staff and booking distribution, while an owner-operator can avoid MicroRouteFort's lease/service margin layer. The long lease protects one chosen parcel, but not the method or surrounding corridor inventory. Because the playbook can be copied through routine contracting and equipment, the control point does not justify an 80+ score without stronger demonstrated speed, switching costs or network compounding.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.**

The candidate correctly excludes distributions from the scenarios, avoids double-counting retained earnings and deducts stated obligations. Nevertheless, the only supposedly conservative outcome clears the €10m target by about 5%, while requiring 35 controlled sites averaging 80 bays, 30% EBITDA, 65% founder ownership, a 7× multiple and only €9m of net obligations after roughly €30m of cumulative site capital. A modest miss in occupancy, margin, multiple, liabilities or ownership removes the target.

The shortage-share comparison does not cure this weakness. The relevant denominator is not all missing European bays but the much smaller set of legally usable, fully serviced, 24/7, separable, underused yards whose owners will grant long assignable control at acceptable economics. The proposal does not evidence enough such inventory or provide a year-by-year cash, debt-service, tax, dilution and lease-renewal bridge showing that its favorable assumptions can coexist. The conservative case is a plausible upside case, not a sufficiently conservative founder-wealth path.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS, narrowly.**

The proposed paid proof is a genuine payment rather than an interview or non-binding expression of interest. It can be attempted within 180 days with pre-proof founder exposure capped at 30,000 PLN, no hardware purchase, continued corporate employment and 15–20 hours per week. A named option, service documentation, legal review, pre-audit and fixed quote make the fleet offer concrete.

The pass is narrow because an unknown founder must align several independent counterparties before payment. The minimum 15,000 PLN combined deposit also covers only part of the roughly 65,000–81,000 PLN value of 12 bays across 90 nights at the stated price and is refundable on disclosed failure conditions. It is still budget-backed evidence, but it does not establish full-price utilisation, retention or recurring take-or-pay demand.

## Expansion Check

The 20-bay beachhead is well chosen: it is narrow, route-specific, sellable to a small number of recurring fleets and large enough to produce useful operating data. Expansion along the same fleets' adjacent Polish/CEE nodes is commercially coherent, and Polish-language landlord sourcing plus local diligence can help a Warsaw-based team.

The asymmetry is weaker than claimed. A successful first 20-bay site does not establish the availability of 35 sites averaging 80 suitable bays, and each new location requires fresh legal access, a host SLA, conversion economics, certification and fleet demand. Broad developers may be slower, but integrated yard owners and parking networks can move inward cheaply and may have structurally better economics. Local execution helps acquisition; it does not create exclusive regional supply.

## Fatal Objections

1. **Founder-wealth fragility — fatal to the Founder Wealth Gate and therefore fatal to a pursue recommendation.** The conservative result clears the target with negligible resilience and no integrated financing/ownership bridge.
2. **Unproved scalable host supply — fatal to the current €10m thesis, not to the first paid-proof attempt.** The plan assumes a portfolio of large, fully serviced, separable and leasable sections without evidence that this asset pool exists at the needed economics.
3. **Unproved fully loaded site economics — not fatal before proof; fatal to conversion or expansion if the stated thresholds fail.** The 125,000 PLN EBITDA excludes central founder replacement cost, while service, power, insurance, VAT/payment treatment and operational overhead remain assumptions.
4. **Opening-budget compression — not fatal to Reachability; potentially fatal to delivery.** Spending 30,000 PLN before proof and up to 60,000 PLN on the quoted layer leaves only 10,000 PLN of founder capital for deposits, timing gaps, working capital and overruns; conditional customer deposits are liabilities, not loss-absorbing capital.
5. **Weak company-wide durability — not fatal to one profitable site, but fatal to a domination claim as currently supported.** Each lease is defensible locally, while the broader method is standardisable by better-connected incumbents.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is that the expensive asset is the legally usable, already serviced corridor location, not the cameras: control the precise host rights and paid fleet inventory before committing conversion capital. That is commercially meaningful and should be preserved by any later structural revision; no revision is proposed here.

## Recommendation

**research further**

The idea merits credit for a real pain point, a specific asset-control wedge, disciplined kill criteria, founder-compatible downside limits and an unusually concrete paid-proof design. It does not qualify for pursue under the governing standard because the Founder Wealth Gate fails and the durable advantage remains local and replicable. Passing the 180-day deposit gate would establish permission to test one conversion; it would not by itself cure the wealth or domination weaknesses.
