# Zero-to-One Evaluation: RatioSnap

## Diagnosis

**Score: 60/100.** RatioSnap addresses a real, manufacturer-documented quality workflow and has an unusually disciplined paid-proof plan, but the case has not yet established that automating the post-shot weighing and record step creates enough recurring value at the proposed price. The product does not remove nozzle installation, purging, pressure stabilization, or production-flow/shot setup, and the standard method already captures A and B simultaneously; its economic gain is therefore narrower than the “90-second ritual” thesis suggests. The most important weakness is not technical feasibility but an unproved combination of check frequency, consumable willingness to pay, material savings, and addressable installed base large enough to support the founder-wealth outcome.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- **Sourced evidence:** Graco explicitly recommends last-point ratio checks at the dispense valve, separate container tare, separate A/B capture, weighing, density conversion where needed, repetition, and investigation of air, blockage, wear, and pressure issues. This validates the workflow and failure mechanism, not the market size or willingness to pay. [Graco ratio-check guidelines](https://www.graco.com/us/en/in-plant-manufacturing/solutions/articles/ratio-check-guidelines-for-dispensing-2k-materials.html)
- **Sourced evidence:** An official Graco PR6 manual recommends a by-weight ratio check at least daily and once per shift in multi-shift production, showing that frequent manual checks exist for at least some equipment; it does not establish frequency across the proposed cross-brand market. [Graco PR6 manual](https://www.graco.com/content/dam/graco/tech_documents/manuals/3A0/3A0234/3A0234EN-A.pdf)
- **Sourced evidence:** Nordson already sells ratio-check caps that keep components separately ported, and Techcon lists ratio-check blocks. These are not equivalent signed dual-mass products, but they show that incumbents already control the critical fluid-interface accessory and can extend it. [Nordson 2K mixer accessories](https://www.nordson.com/en/Products/EFD-Products/2K-Mixer-Accessories), [Techcon TSM50FR](https://store.techcon.com/en-us/products/two-component-dispensing/meter-mix/TSM50FR)
- **Founder/candidate claim:** Glenmarc says it has more than 10,000 machines installed worldwide. The claim is genuine manufacturer marketing, but it spans decades and many configurations and says nothing about current operation, daily manual last-point checking, cassette compatibility, or buyer willingness. It therefore does not validate the candidate's 40,000 relevant-cell denominator. [Glenmarc meter/mix systems](https://www.glenmarc.com/meter.htm)
- **Candidate arithmetic:** At 350 checks/year, cassettes cost €1,925/year. Four minutes saved per check equals only 23.3 labor hours/year, so cassettes alone cost €82.50 per labor hour saved; with optional €900 care, the recurring cost is about €121 per hour saved, before the €3,500 dock. Consequently, the labor-saving threshold cannot by itself justify the offer for many CEE plants. Repeated material savings or avoided record/failure cost must carry the economics.
- **Consequential hypotheses:** €4–€5.50 cassette willingness to pay; at least 200–350 checks per dock per year; at least 30% sample/handling-waste reduction despite unchanged production shot and purge requirements; compatibility without bespoke engineering; and at least 25,000 relevant active cells. None is yet paid or independently measured evidence.
- No equivalent keyed cassette/reader was found in the limited decision-critical search, but absence from search is not FTO, patent clearance, or proof of an empty competitive category.

## Overall Score

**60/100.** Weighted points are **120 / 20**, producing a weighted average of **6.0/10** and an overall score of **60**. The score is below 80 because the proposed control point is copyable through ordinary equipment/process extension and no specific compounding asset yet makes catch-up difficult.

## Control Point

**Current: Tier 3.** Today the cassette geometry, tooling, adapters, firmware, signing keys, recipes, reliability history, installed work instructions, and customer corpus are planned rather than owned or deployed. The underlying load cells, electronics, ratio caps, containers, and record software are commodity-purchasable or routinely buildable.

**Potential: Tier 2, not Tier 1.** After assigned IP, proven multi-brand packs, accepted installations, cassette-lot reliability, and embedded signed histories exist, the company could have repeatably accessible switching costs and compounding qualification evidence. It still would not own an exclusive scarce right, required channel, unique data source, or transaction flow.

**Strongest copy/substitution risk:** an OEM, integrator, or plant combines its existing ratio-check cap/block with a dual-scale fixture, reusable or standard cups, and automatic QMS logging. That routine extension can capture most of the value without a €5.50 proprietary disposable, while leveraging an already trusted equipment relationship.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.** The tables are arithmetically coherent and properly state pricing, margins, capital, ownership, reinvestment, obligations, valuation, and timing. The problem is scenario credibility: the conservative €15.2 million founder-value case needs 6,000 active docks, 350 cassettes per dock each year, 24% operating margin, 68% founder ownership, and a 7× operating-profit value by year 12. Six thousand docks are 15% of a 40,000-cell denominator that the candidate itself calls unproved; Glenmarc's 10,000 cumulative installation claim cannot establish the number of active, compatible cells still doing valuable manual last-point checks. The model also has no evidenced distribution/support system for thousands of global hardware installations and chemistry/adapter qualifications. Because all three cases scale the same unproved denominator, recurring-use, and value assumptions, they are sensitivities rather than conservative/expected/strong evidence-backed wealth paths.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS, with material execution risk.** The candidate defines a legitimate paid-proof event—two unrelated 6,000–8,000 PLN prepaid pilots by day 180—after benign-fluid agreement testing and before production tooling. Pre-proof founder risk is capped at 28,000 PLN, the founder keeps employment, customer chemistry is deferred until host safety procedures apply, and the dependencies can be tested sequentially without an OEM licence, distributor, donated dataset, or full product. The caveat is that 50 qualified industrial approaches, six nozzle-specific demonstrations, safety/FTO input, and two prepayments are ambitious for a founder without current plant access; nevertheless, this is a credible bounded test rather than an existential build-first plan.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is directionally good only when narrowed by actual daily check frequency, costly material or rework, separable streams, and missing independent records. Cross-brand expansion is plausible but not asymmetric yet: an OEM can move inward through its own ratio-check hardware and installed service channel faster than RatioSnap can qualify every chemistry and valve family. CEE proximity helps early service cost and access, not durable defense. Higher-regulation sectors add qualification burden and should not be counted as automatic adjacency in the wealth model.

## Fatal Objections

1. **Fatal to a pursue verdict now: Founder Wealth Gate failure.** The required €10 million path depends on a 6,000-dock “conservative” outcome drawn from an unverified relevant-cell denominator, without paid evidence of recurring use or a global repeatable channel. Under the governing criteria, this alone prohibits `pursue`.
2. **Potentially fatal commercial objection:** the candidate's own four-minute speed threshold does not justify the proposed recurring price. Unless pilots prove repeatable material savings or avoided record/failure cost, the product is a costly convenience layered onto a cheap manual check.
3. **Potentially fatal control-point objection:** ordinary cups/reusable wells on a dual-scale dock with automatic logging, or an OEM/integrator accessory, may capture most of the benefit and remove the proprietary cassette annuity.

No independently established fatal technical objection was found. Agreement, compatibility, false-pass behavior, and waste handling remain explicit kill tests rather than proven blockers.

## Strongest Objections and Caveats

- The “90-second” framing excludes common setup and purge time, so it risks overstating whole-workflow savings.
- Manual A/B capture is already simultaneous; the novelty is mainly identity/tare/weighing/calculation/record integration.
- A 30% reduction in sample/handling waste is not mechanically explained when the prescribed production shot and stabilization purges remain unchanged.
- The pilot gate accepts four minutes saved **or** material/error value, but time saving alone may not produce positive ROI at €4–€5.50 per check.
- Hardware gross margins omit evidence of warranty reserve, certification/compliance, calibration traceability, field failures, low-volume molding losses, distributor margin, and chemical/product-liability cost.
- Direct selling to plant quality/process owners is evidence-rich but socially and technically demanding for this founder; the proposed contractor stack does not include an experienced application salesperson until after traction.
- The installed-base claim counts machines, not active relevant cells, and gives no denominator for manual checks, shot frequency, supported chemistries, or plants without equivalent records.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Independent, last-point physical ratio evidence for legacy/semi-automatic 2K cells is a sharper and more defensible problem definition than generic dispensing monitoring, especially when the output is tied to material, valve, recipe, operator, and lot without claiming cure or bond assurance.
