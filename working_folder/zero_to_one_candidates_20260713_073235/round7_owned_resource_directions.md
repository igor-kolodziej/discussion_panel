## 1. TachoTrace Inspection Kit

### Insider insight and exact buyer/event

The approved tachograph workshop does not primarily want a “better security seal.” It wants every mandatory inspection to close without an invalid seal, missing serial record, wrong workshop mark, or a second order for the installation plaque. The exact paid event is the workshop replenishing a ready-to-use closeout kit for one statutory tachograph inspection: certified seals plus a workshop-personalised installation plaque and machine-readable serial record.

The workshop is the buyer and controls which compliant seal stock it installs. No fleet, carrier, vehicle OEM, or regulator must agree to the workshop's purchase.

### Wedge and founder-owned resource

- A two-seal inspection pack with EN 16882-certified mechanical seals, a pre-personalised installation plaque, human-readable and Data Matrix serials, and an offline batch register/export.
- The founder owns the seal geometry, production tool, certification file, JRC-assigned manufacturer code, serial namespace, plaque stock specification, and pack-assembly QA. Contract manufacturing is execution capacity, not the control point.
- **Control path:** Tier 3 while the design is uncertified; Tier 1 for the exact seal after AIMPLAS certification and publication of the founder's manufacturer code by the JRC. The digital register is supporting switching cost, not the claimed moat.

### Counted denominator and momentum

- ACEA counted about **6.0 million trucks and 679,802 buses** on EU roads in 2023. EU law requires periodic tachograph inspection at least every two years, implying a floor of roughly **3.34 million inspection events per year** before vans are counted.
- From **1 July 2026**, tachographs also apply to 2.5–3.5 tonne light commercial vehicles used in international carriage for hire or reward. This is upside only; do not count all 30.1 million EU vans.
- The JRC list updated 25 June 2026 shows only **eight assigned manufacturer codes**, seven with a displayed EN 16882 certification date. Only certified seals may bear the T logo. This is a real, bounded approval position, although the incumbents are serious security-seal companies.

### Economics and founder-wealth bridge

All numbers below are hypotheses until workshop interviews, a production quote, and an AIMPLAS quote exist. A public retail reference is €5.40 for one T-logo seal, so a €14 closeout-kit hypothesis is not obviously impossible but must be tested.

- Unit hypothesis: €14 per two-seal/plaque/serial kit; €3.80 landed variable cost; 73% gross margin before distribution; 27–32% operating margin at scale.
- Conservative: 7% of the 3.34 million event floor = 234,000 kits, €3.28 million revenue, 27% operating margin. At 6x operating profit and 80% founder ownership, equity is about €4.25 million; even several years of non-double-counted distributions is unlikely to clear €10 million quickly. This case does **not** carry the wealth gate.
- Expected-success hypothesis: 12% = 401,000 kits, €5.61 million revenue, 30% operating margin. At 7x operating profit, enterprise value is €11.8 million; 78% founder ownership is €9.2 million. More than €0.8 million of cumulative after-tax founder distributions after the full-time transition would clear €10 million without counting retained cash twice. Estimated year 8–11.
- Strong-success hypothesis: 20% = 668,000 kits, €9.35 million revenue, 32% operating margin. At 7x operating profit and 75% ownership, founder equity is about €15.7 million before any distributions. Estimated year 7–9.
- Capital: ≤40,000 PLN before paid proof for the standard, CAD, prototype/soft tool, pre-tests, legal review, and workshop samples; ≤100,000 PLN total only if the certification and production-tool quotes fit and deposits exist. Expected and strong scenarios assume later working capital is financed from retained earnings or purchase-order finance, not the original founder capital.
- Reinvestment: 95% of after-tax distributable cash is reinvested while the founder remains employed in conservative and expected cases; the strong case uses 100%. Optional employment-period distributions stay ≤5%.

### Cold start and first proof

1. Obtain written AIMPLAS scope, cost, sample count, failure/retest terms, and lead time before commissioning a production tool.
2. Build the official Poland workshop census from the GUM/TRANS-TACHO register; interview 25 workshops selected across independent, chain, urban, and border-route operators.
3. Ask for two unrelated workshops to pay a conditional deposit for 500 kits each, refundable only if certification or a stated delivery date is missed. The product and price must be fixed in the deposit document.
4. Spend beyond 40,000 PLN only if deposits exist and the all-in certification/tooling path remains within 100,000 PLN.

**First disconfirming test:** if fewer than two of 25 workshops pay a deposit at a gross-margin-supporting price, or AIMPLAS plus tooling exceeds the 100,000 PLN resource cap, kill before certification.

### Compounding moat, copy risk, and fatal risks

- Compounding asset: an approved EU seal, installed workshop base, replenishment cadence, serial history, distributor reorder data, and manufacturing yield knowledge. One certification can serve the EU rather than resetting at every country border.
- Strongest copy risk: an existing certified seal manufacturer can bundle a plaque and serial export quickly. The founder must prove that pre-personalised inspection packs materially reduce technician time/errors and achieve recurring direct replenishment before incumbents react.
- Fatal risks: two seals may not be consumed at every inspection; workshops may buy on price through existing distributors; certification cost/time may exceed the cap; the €14 kit may be rejected; and 12% EU share may be too ambitious for an unknown entrant. Do not treat the 3.34 million inspections as automatic kit demand.

## 2. FrySafe Closed-Loop Dock

### Insider insight and exact buyer/event

Independent fry-heavy kitchens do not merely want longer oil life. The owner wants the closing shift to filter oil every time without asking a tired employee to move, expose, scrape, and clean a hot-oil machine. Existing filtration savings disappear when the device sits unused or the dirtiest step—cleaning sediment from the filter—still remains.

The exact buyer is the owner of a one-to-four-fryer independent fried-chicken, fish, doughnut, kebab, or high-volume snack kitchen using a fryer without dependable built-in filtration. The recurring event is a sealed crumb/filter cassette replacement after a fixed number of oil cycles. The kitchen controls adoption without a landlord, platform, oil collector, or fryer OEM.

### Wedge and founder-owned resource

- A wheeled, closed-loop dock that connects to a small set of common fryer drains, receives hot oil into an enclosed vessel, filters it through a sealable crumb cassette, and pumps it back without staff pouring exposed oil or cleaning a reusable filter screen.
- The founder owns the dock CAD, safety architecture, connector kit, cassette geometry, molds/tooling, endurance-test protocol, firmware, and consumable supply. The first alpha can be fabricated before any kitchen cooperates and bench-tested with heated oil and standardized crumb loads.
- **Control path:** current Tier 3; potential Tier 2 after a safe endurance-tested dock, owned cassette tool, recurring paid cassette orders, and design protection. It is not Tier 1 because appliance manufacturers can engineer alternatives.

### Counted denominator and momentum

- Statistics Poland counted 22,666 catering establishments in enterprises with more than nine workers in 2024 and states these represented 22.3% of all catering establishments, implying approximately **101,600 Polish establishments** in the statistical frame.
- That is not the eligible denominator. A bottom-up census must identify fry-heavy independent sites with compatible non-filtering fryers. No wealth case may use 101,600 as if every establishment qualified.
- HSE guidance treats emptying/cleaning fryers as a burn risk and explicitly discusses manual, semi-automatic, and automatic filtering. VITO markets in-tank systems that claim up to 50% oil-cost savings and no contact with hot oil; this confirms the job and also sets a high incumbent bar.

### Economics and founder-wealth bridge

Hypotheses: €95/site/month dock-and-cassette plan; 65% gross margin after depreciation, cartridges, warranty, and parcel fulfilment; activation deposit covers the first dock's variable build cost.

- Conservative: 6,000 EU sites, €6.84 million recurring revenue, 20% operating margin, 5x value, 75% ownership = about €5.1 million founder equity. Does not clear the wealth gate on equity alone.
- Expected-success: 12,000 sites, €13.68 million revenue, 25% operating margin, 5.5x value, 72% ownership = about €13.5 million founder equity. Estimated year 8–11.
- Strong-success: 25,000 sites, €28.5 million revenue, 28% operating margin, 6x value, 65% ownership = about €31.1 million founder equity. Estimated year 7–10.
- The expected case needs a census-supported claim that 12,000 compatible fry-heavy sites are a modest share of the reachable Poland/Germany/CEE/EU population. Until then, it is arithmetic, not evidence.
- 95% of after-tax distributable cash is reinvested while employed in conservative and expected cases; strong uses 100%; employment-period distributions stay ≤5%. Later tooling and inventory require customer deposits, retained cash, or asset finance.

### Cold start and first proof

1. Spend ≤8,000 PLN to create a named census of 1,000 Polish fry-heavy independent sites from official registers plus menu/listing verification; record fryer type where observable.
2. Spend ≤22,000 PLN on one bench-safe alpha and standardized 100-cycle oil/crumb/temperature tests. Do not install it in a live kitchen before an independent machinery/electrical safety review.
3. Show the fixed demo to 30 owners from the census. Require five paid, conditional 2,000 PLN deposits for a 30-day unit; deposits define the €95-equivalent ongoing fee and cassette cadence.
4. Total pre-proof exposure ≤38,000 PLN. Release the rest of the original 100,000 PLN only after two unrelated kitchens complete 30 days, use the dock on at least 80% of scheduled cycles, reorder cassettes, and show positive contribution margin.

**First disconfirming test:** kill if seven of ten observed kitchens cannot use the alpha correctly without founder attendance, if measured oil-plus-labour savings are below twice the monthly fee, or if fewer than five of 30 owners pay deposits.

### Compounding moat, copy risk, and fatal risks

- Compounding asset: installed docks, proprietary cassette demand, compatible-drain library, reliability data, kitchen savings benchmarks, and a distributor/replenishment route.
- Strongest copy risk: VITO, fryer OEMs, or mobile-filter manufacturers can add a disposable sealed filter. Only rapid installed-base growth, reliable connectors, and replenishment economics make catch-up costly.
- Fatal risks: drain variation creates custom field service; hot-oil liability makes warranty/insurance uneconomic; staff still refuse the workflow; cartridge waste offsets savings; built-in filtration spreads; and the counted eligible population is far smaller than required.

## 3. CopperGate Intake Classifier

### Insider insight and exact buyer/event

At a small non-ferrous scrap yard, the painful job is not sorting a conveyor stream; it is setting the purchase price for a seller standing at the scale. Experienced graders recognize common copper cable, motor winding, mixed brass, and contaminated categories, but the skill is difficult to transfer and a wrong grade immediately destroys purchase margin. A €19,900 handheld XRF is too expensive for many small yards and still tests only a point.

The buyer is the owner of a small licensed non-ferrous intake yard. The event is pricing a lot before paying the seller. The yard controls adoption.

### Wedge and founder-owned resource

- A fixed lightbox/scale with RGB imaging, low-cost conductivity/eddy-current response, weight and operator prompts, limited initially to five economically important copper/brass intake classes. It recommends a class and confidence; low-confidence lots remain manual or XRF-tested.
- Before customer cooperation, the founder can purchase, lab-verify, destructively sample, photograph, and rescan a stratified reference collection. The founder owns the physical sample bank, labeled multimodal dataset, fixture, model, and error-cost matrix.
- **Control path:** Tier 3 until blind lot-level accuracy exists; potential Tier 2 if the proprietary sample/dataset produces stable margin improvement and paid yards contribute outcome labels under contract. It never becomes Tier 1 merely because the dataset is private.

### Denominator, economics, and cold start

- Eurostat counted **21.0 million tonnes of EU metal recyclable-material exports in 2023**, proving transaction value but not the number of eligible yards.
- A decision-grade bottom-up account census is missing. Build it from Poland's BDO public register and analogous national licensed-facility registers, excluding brokers, municipal sites, and steel-only yards. Do not use tonnes as a customer denominator.
- Economics hypothesis: €2,500 hardware/activation plus €90/month model/price support; 8,000 active EU sites would produce €8.64 million recurring revenue plus hardware, with a plausible 25% operating margin. At 6x operating profit and 70% founder ownership, equity is about €9.1 million; modest non-double-counted cumulative distributions would be needed to clear €10 million. The 8,000-site population is presently unproved.
- Pre-proof budget ≤35,000 PLN: 15,000 PLN materials/sample bank, 10,000 PLN fixture/sensors, 5,000 PLN reference lab/XRF checks, 5,000 PLN insurance/legal/census. Seek paid trials only after a blind test.

**First disconfirming test:** on 200 blind, whole-lot samples representing the five classes, the alpha must reduce value-weighted pricing error versus trained humans without sending more than 20% of lots to “unknown.” If it cannot, reject the technology rather than changing the claimed grade set after seeing results.

### Compounding moat, copy risk, and fatal risks

- Potential compounder: independently owned reference samples and multimodal observations tied to realized downstream settlements, not generic photographs.
- Strongest copy risk: falling XRF/LIBS prices or an experienced operator makes the low-cost classifier unnecessary; industrial vision vendors can reproduce the fixture once demand is visible.
- Fatal risks: surface appearance and conductivity cannot resolve valuable alloy/contamination distinctions; lab labels do not match whole-lot economics; radiation-free accuracy is too low; the BDO census is small; and liability for a wrong purchase price destroys willingness to pay.
- **Generation decision:** hold, not a finalist. Only a preregistered blind result and counted facility census can reopen it.

## 4. HerdTag Direct — Rejected

### Insight and resource thesis

The appealing thesis is a farmer-direct, fast-turn supplier of official ear tags: own the molds/laser process, pass the technical specification, join the ARiMR supplier list, connect to the IRZplus API, and earn on every birth/replacement. The farmer directly chooses a listed supplier and sends the order; ARiMR does not intermediate the sale. This is unusually clean buyer authority.

The resource can in principle be secured before customers: compliant means of identification, ARiMR listing/API access, and ICAR certification for electronic identifiers. Poland's live registry as of 30 June 2026 counts 242,004 cattle premises and 6,221,090 cattle; the EU counted 72 million bovines in 2024. The event base is real.

### Why it is rejected

- ARiMR's current page lists **19 suppliers**, with many already covering conventional cattle, sheep/goat, and pig tags and several offering electronic tags.
- The technical specification/listing is an entry ticket shared by many competitors, not a scarce right. Faster web ordering and API automation can be copied without new equipment or approval.
- Expansion resets national approval, database integration, numbering, language, fulfilment, and farmer distribution. An EU herd count is therefore not one directly reachable market.
- A wealth bridge needs millions of low-price tags and significant multi-country share against Allflex/MSD and entrenched local suppliers. No bottom-up price/margin evidence supports that path under 100,000 PLN.
- Paid proof before listing would be a conditional commodity preorder, not evidence of durable preference.

**First disconfirming test that already fails the generation screen:** a current field with 19 listed suppliers and no demonstrated defect-driven switching reason does not justify spending on certification or tooling.

## 5. ReturnCell Pack Pool — Rejected

### Insight and resource thesis

An independent e-bike dealer can sell or service a battery but may be unable to send a damaged/defective pack through an ordinary parcel flow. The apparent wedge is a founder-owned pool of reusable, serialized P908/P911-oriented battery return packs, rented per shipment with packing instructions and deposit-funded reverse logistics. The dealer controls the decision to rent a pack.

The founder could own the first tested pack designs and a small physical pool before customer cooperation. CONEBI counted 5.1 million European e-bike sales in 2023, so the installed base is large, and ADR imposes real packing rules for damaged/defective lithium batteries.

### Why it is rejected

- Annual e-bike sales are not a count of damaged batteries needing third-party shipment. The return/defect/shipping rate is the decisive denominator and is not evidenced.
- The dealer cannot unilaterally make the transaction happen. A carrier must accept the dangerous-goods shipment and a repairer, OEM, recycler, or waste operator must receive it. Reactive batteries may require P911/LP906 or competent-authority conditions.
- Fortum already rents battery boxes as part of a compliant recycling service, and Reneos coordinates end-of-life EV battery packaging and cross-border collection. Their network position is more important than box ownership.
- Reverse logistics, cleaning, inspection, loss, dangerous-goods documentation, and country/carrier rules make cost scale with each shipment. A founder-owned box does not remove those dependencies.
- A €10 million founder-wealth case would require an invented rental count or a capital-heavy fleet before a repeatable route exists. This repeats the rejected multi-party lane-pool failure, despite changing the cargo.

**First disconfirming fact:** without a signed carrier acceptance matrix and an actual dealer/OEM return-event census, the box is inventory awaiting permission, not a routed transaction.

- **TachoTrace** is the only direction here with an already counted statutory repeat event and a genuinely scarce EU-wide approval asset. - **HerdTag Direct** and **ReturnCell** should be discarded, not cosmetically rewritten.
