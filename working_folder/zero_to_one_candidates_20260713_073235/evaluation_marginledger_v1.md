# Zero-to-One Evaluation: MarginLedger EU

## Diagnosis

MarginLedger addresses a genuine, cash-measurable supplier pain, and the 4,000 PLN audit is a sensible first paid artifact. As written, however, it is a trust-heavy managed recovery service entering a market where an established UK operator already offers the same supplier-side, contingency-priced, relationship-preserving recovery service and SPS markets Revenue Recovery in Europe. The proposed mandate and anonymised outcome library are not scarce at company level: each customer can grant the same revocable rights to an incumbent, while retailer portal and confidentiality terms can override what the supplier is able to delegate or reuse. The algebraic wealth scenarios clear EUR 10 million, but their denominator, annual value, margin, acquisition, retention and labour assumptions are not evidenced; both hard gates therefore fail for the exact candidate.

### Founder claims and consequential hypotheses

- **Founder claims:** 4,000 PLN prepaid audit; 15%-22% of actual recovery; 36,000 PLN maximum pre-proof risk; no retailer partnership required; three to ten mandates within 12 months; one part-time specialist plus fixed-scope counsel.
- **Unproved demand hypotheses:** five of fifteen interviews will show at least EUR 100,000 disputed annually; finance leaders will prepay an unknown operator despite a no-upfront incumbent; customers will grant evidence, portal/agent and outcome-data rights; fear of retailer retaliation will not block purchase.
- **Unproved operating hypotheses:** one lane can be learned without bespoke legal work per claim; 50 claims can produce one bank-verified recovery inside 180 days; cash recovery can be attributed unambiguously to MarginLedger; human minutes per line decline sufficiently for 72%-78% gross margins.
- **Unproved distribution hypotheses:** public exporter/exhibitor lists yield a repeatable channel to finance decision-makers; the first 500 reachable accounts exist; high-trust sales and security review fit a part-time founder; retention supports 800-3,000 active customers.
- **Not market evidence:** the candidate's scenario table, self-classified control tier, named 12,000 requirement, and competitor-coverage assumption are generation hypotheses, not validation results.

### Outcome-fee and collection audit

At a 15%-22% contingency rate, EUR 22,000 of annual value requires roughly EUR 100,000-EUR 146,700 of attributable recovered or prevented deductions per customer each year unless the monthly floor supplies the difference. Eligibility at EUR 100,000 of *disputed* value does not establish that amount is invalid, recoverable, collected, or attributable. Retailers may net credits against later remittances, settle multiple issues together, reverse entries, or trade a payment against another commercial concession; the proposal neither routes the recovered cash nor independently controls the bank/remittance ledger. The supplier therefore controls the measurement data and payment of MarginLedger's fee, creating attribution, audit and collection disputes that are not addressed by the mandate alone.

## Overall Score

**45/100.** Weighted points are `89.5` over a total weight of `20`; `10 × (89.5 / 20) = 44.75`, rounded to `45`.

This is an advisory evaluator score only. It is not a simulated-panel score, chatbot score, confirmation or validation gate.

## Control Point

**Current tier: Tier 3 (prospective); possible Tier 2 after proof, but not Tier 1 at company level.** A signed mandate would contractually control one client's authorised claim workflow for its term, but it is non-exclusive in economic substance, customer-dependent, portal-dependent and likely revocable. MarginLedger does not route the recovered cash, control retailer access independently, or own the underlying remittance/contracts. Reusable metadata could become Tier 2 only after executed rights survive retailer confidentiality terms and the library demonstrably improves win rate, cycle time or labour across customers.

**Strongest copy risk:** Salitix or SPS can target CEE exporters using existing retailer workflows, security credibility and outcome/recovery expertise. That requires a regional sales hire or local contractor, not a new technical control point. A European ERP/EDI provider, HighRadius-style receivables platform, accounting BPO or retailer consultant can also bundle the workflow once demand is visible.

## Reachability Gate

**FAIL (the capital envelope is plausible; executable paid proof is not yet credible for this founder).** A manual 100-line audit can be produced below 36,000 PLN and beside employment. The problem is not software build cost; it is that the founder must first obtain employer clearance, win a sensitive-data sale without relevant reputation or security credentials, prove a lawful access/submission route for one selected retailer, secure data-processing and reusable-outcome rights, and wait for an actual retailer repayment. Those are several uncontrolled dependencies before the candidate's own 180-day proof standard of three prepaid audits, two mandates, 50 filed claims and one recovery.

Written employer clearance is an existential first gate because the founder works for a large FMCG supplier operating in the same retailer ecosystem. Reckitt's public policy requires disclosure before the conduct, and excluding Reckitt plus direct competitors does not remove overlap with its customers, suppliers, trade-spend workflow or sector knowledge. Until approval exists, even customer discovery and prospecting may be restricted.

Portal feasibility is mixed rather than assumed impossible: Sainsbury publicly supports registered third-party agents, while other portal terms can require retailer approval and restrict data reuse. The candidate has not selected and cleared one route. The 4,000 PLN prepayment is modest, but Salitix advertises no-upfront contingency recovery and established retailer relationships, so an unknown part-time entrant must overcome a meaningful trust and data-security disadvantage. Paid proof within 180 days is possible only after multiple facts turn favourable; the exact candidate does not presently pass the gate.

## Expansion Check

- **Beachhead:** Specific in theory—CEE food/drink suppliers with high deduction volume in one UK grocer lane—but neither the grocer nor a named account set is fixed.
- **Adjacent markets:** Additional UK grocers, then Poland/Germany, trade-spend reconciliation and prevention are logical adjacencies. Each country-retailer pair may require new law, contracts, portal permissions, evidence mappings and security approval; this is serial re-entry, not yet asymmetric expansion.
- **Competitor entry:** Salitix already operates the core UK managed-service shape. SPS already has a European network and markets Revenue Recovery. Localising sales to CEE is a normal commercial expansion for either.
- **Local advantage:** Polish language, lower labour cost and exporter-list access may reduce CAC and service cost, but do not create exclusion, outcome-data rights, retailer acceptance or enterprise trust.
- **Incumbent catch-up:** The claimed control can be copied through a client mandate plus a regional claims team; existing EDI/ERP/AP data access makes catch-up easier for incumbents than expansion is for MarginLedger.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The purported UK market gap is contradicted by a direct incumbent.** Salitix already offers supplier-side managed profit recovery, aged-debt/deduction review, contingency pricing, separation from sensitive commercial channels and established retailer relationships. MarginLedger's initial wedge is not materially different in outcome or model.
2. **The control point is customer-by-customer permission, not a scarce company asset.** Supplier authorisation cannot override retailer portal, confidentiality, security or reuse restrictions; the company does not control the retailer or recovered transaction flow.
3. **The Founder Wealth Gate is built on an uncounted market and untested service economics.** No named 12,000 denominator, EUR 22,000 realised annual value, 72% GM, 28% OM, retention or acquisition system supports 800 customers. The labelled conservative case is therefore not conservative evidence.
4. **Employer clearance is an uncontrolled existential dependency.** The business overlaps the founder's current FMCG employer's retailers, category ecosystem and financial workflow. Public policy requires prior disclosure; no written approval exists.
5. **The Reachability Gate requires simultaneous trust, security, access, data-rights and timing wins.** An unknown part-time founder must receive sensitive contracts/remittances, obtain portal or agent authority, avoid legal overreach and produce a bank-verified retailer recovery within 180 days while a no-upfront specialist already exists.
6. **The data moat may be contractually unusable and operationally weak.** GDPR-compliant anonymisation does not remove retailer confidentiality or database restrictions, and outcome labels from heterogeneous retailer-country lanes may not transfer enough to reduce human effort.
7. **Outcome-fee attribution and collection are not controlled.** The supplier and retailer control remittance, offsets and broader commercial settlements. MarginLedger has a contractual claim to a fee, not direct transaction-flow control.
8. **Scale remains service-like.** Each new account brings sensitive-data onboarding, claim review, portal rules, commercial-relationship judgment and exceptions. No evidence shows labour stops scaling approximately with clients and lanes.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is to sell a finance buyer a cash-measured recovery outcome from evidence the buyer already owns, using a small prepaid diagnostic before larger spend. That insight is commercially useful, but the retail-deduction recovery shape, customer trust mechanism and mandate-based control should not be preserved automatically.

## Recommendation

**kill**

Kill the exact MarginLedger EU business shape rather than send it to an independent simulated panel. Both hard gates fail, and the combination of a direct UK incumbent, prospective/revocable control, employer conflict, high-trust data access and unsupported scale economics is structural rather than a missing feature or modest research gap.
