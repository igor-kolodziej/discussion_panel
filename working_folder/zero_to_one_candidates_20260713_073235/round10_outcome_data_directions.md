# Round 10 — Founder-Created Outcome Assets

**Research date:** 2026-07-13  
**Role:** candidate generation and research only. No scoring, advisory evaluation, pivot, panel, chatbot, or validation was performed.  
**Scope:** software/data/transaction businesses outside Reckitt/CPG in which the founder can create the first analytical asset, the first customer contract grants usable outcome rights, and marginal delivery labour should decline as contracted outcomes accumulate.

## Non-Negotiable Screen Used

Every retained direction had to begin with one payer-controlled cash or loss event, support three unrelated prepaid customers within 180 days and 40,000 PLN, and create a contractually provable data/model control point inside 12 months and 100,000 PLN. Raw customer data remains the customer's; the proposed pilot terms grant the company a perpetual right to use **irreversibly de-identified derived features, labels, aggregates and model parameters** across customers. Personal data, customer names, test specimens, drawings and trade-secret source documents are excluded from the reusable corpus.

The search avoided generic consulting, generic dashboards, compliance wrappers, recovery/chargeback work, public-data routing, two-sided marketplaces, speculative partnerships and employer-adjacent CPG work. Six directions were researched; four were stopped after exact competitor checks. No padding was added.

## 1. LabMargin — Pre-Acceptance Margin Underwriting for Commercial Test Labs

### Inside Customer Truth and Loss Event

An independent commercial test laboratory does not merely sell a method; it promises a scope and turnaround before it knows whether the sample will be unsuitable, prep will expand, a run will need repeating, an instrument queue will slip, work will be subcontracted or a rush commitment will consume scarce capacity. The manager's real question is not “what does this standard method cost on average?” but “will this exact quote still earn its intended contribution margin after the sample and schedule reveal themselves?”

The loss event is measurable and controlled by the payer: the lab issues, reprices, conditions or declines the quote. The result is the realised labour, consumables, instrument time, repeats, subcontract cost, turnaround performance and contribution margin for that order.

### Product Wedge and First Analytical Asset

The founder can build `Quote Replay` without a full LIMS:

1. a method/sample/turnaround risk ontology;
2. a deterministic cost-and-capacity simulator;
3. import adapters for a quote export, work-order export and invoice/cost export;
4. a counterfactual replay showing which historical quotes needed a surcharge, sample-acceptance condition, extra lead time, subcontract decision or no-bid;
5. an uncertainty-aware quote recommendation, never a scientific-result recommendation.

The fixed-scope first product is a prepaid **20-quote or 100-order replay**, not general process consulting. It produces a reusable risk model, clause library and import mapping. Later delivery changes from analyst-led replay to a quote-time API or batch file: expected variable cost, overrun band, minimum price, acceptance conditions and reason codes.

### Primary Control Point

The first contract must grant reusable rights to de-identified features and outcome labels such as method family, sample-condition code, promised turnaround band, preparation complexity, queue/load band, repeat/rework code, subcontract flag, realised hours/cost and margin band. It must not grant access to customer identity, analytical result, specimen or confidential formulation.

**12-month proof target:** ten independent contracts, at least 10,000 closed-order rows, three different lab systems and a held-out demonstration that the pooled model improves realised-margin or turnaround calibration over each lab's price-list baseline. The company owns the ontology, mappings, derived corpus and model parameters. Each additional lab adds reusable mappings and outcome coverage; routine delivery should fall from roughly 20 founder-hours per pilot to under two support-hours per customer-month.

### Cold Start and 180-Day Paid Proof

- Build a named list of 150 privately operated Polish materials, environmental and electrical test laboratories from the live PCA directory, then verify commercial ordering on each firm's own site. Do not treat all 1,293 accredited testing laboratories as eligible buyers.
- Send a concrete one-page offer to laboratory directors/CFOs: **5,000 PLN prepaid for a 20-quote replay**, a minimum-price/acceptance-rule file and a quantified retrospective. No free custom analysis.
- Close three unrelated labs at 5,000 PLN each. Each supplies 12 months of de-identified quote/work-order/cost outcomes under the derived-rights clause.
- Pre-proof spend ceiling: 18,000 PLN—legal/DPA and contract clause 4,000; security and isolated storage 3,000; founder-built ingestion/model 3,000; targeted list/outreach and travel 5,000; contingency 3,000. Family capital: zero.
- Kill before additional spend if fewer than three of 100 qualified approaches buy, if no lab can export both quoted and realised cost/time, or if two of the first three refuse reusable derived-outcome rights.

### Counted Beachhead and Expansion Denominator

- The official Polish accreditation portal currently lists **1,293 testing laboratories** under its testing-laboratory filter. This is a raw upper bound, not an eligible commercial-lab count.
- A current directory built from six national accreditation-body records lists **10,051 ISO 17025 laboratories** across Germany, the UK, France, the Netherlands, Italy and Spain. Its component counts are visible by country. Again, internal and public labs must be removed before a wealth claim.
- European Accreditation reported more than **35,080 accreditations under the EA MLA during 2024** across all conformity-assessment categories; it corroborates a large adjacent system but is not a test-lab customer denominator.

The narrow Polish beachhead is commercially ordering materials/environmental/electrical labs with at least 1,000 priced orders per year and exportable quote plus job-cost data. Expansion is method-adjacent labs in Germany, Czechia, the UK, Spain, Italy and France, then calibration/inspection only after the same quote-outcome schema is proven.

### Exact Competitor Check

- **Confience LIMS** explicitly runs commercial testing from quotation through analytical report and invoice, with test/group price lists, customer-specific pricing and priority-based turnaround charges.
- **WiseLIMS** spans quotation, work order and report and advertises anomaly detection and forecasting.
- **eValuate** integrates finance, HR and LIMS data to calculate cost, volume, profit and repeatable cost per test.
- These products strongly disprove any claim that laboratories lack quotation or cost software. The narrower unchecked gap is **pre-acceptance expected-loss pricing from realised order outcomes across independent labs**, delivered as an overlay rather than a new LIMS.

The strongest copy risk is Confience or another LIMS adding realised-cost calibration. The defence must therefore be contracted cross-lab outcome rights, fast multi-LIMS mappings and a validated pooled benchmark—not interface features. If ten contracts cannot create a materially better pooled model, the control point remains a copyable feature and the idea must die.

### Unit Economics and Value Capture — Hypotheses to Test

- Pilot: 5,000 PLN prepaid; target direct delivery cost below 1,500 PLN after the third mapping.
- Production: €9,000 annual base plus €2–€8 per priced order, hypothesised €24,000 blended annual value for a lab with meaningful commercial volume; target 82% gross margin and sub-nine-month CAC payback.
- Value test: the price must be no more than 20% of independently documented annual margin/turnaround improvement. Do not infer willingness to pay from LIMS prices.
- No outcome guarantee, scientific decision or accreditation claim. The system prices operating risk; the laboratory retains the quote and test decision.

### Founder Wealth Screen

All figures below are hypotheses and value retained earnings only through business value; they do not add those earnings again as personal distributions.

| Scenario | Customers / blended annual price | Revenue | GM / operating margin | Capital and ownership | Reinvestment | Business value and founder wealth | Timing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 750 labs × €24k | €18.0m | 82% / 30% | ≤€0.8m cumulative, funded mainly after revenue; 75% founder ownership | 95% of after-tax distributable cash while employed; ≤5% optional distributions | 5× operating profit = €27.0m company; 75% stake = **€20.25m** | first 3 paid pilots by month 6; 30 labs year 2; 180 year 4; 750 year 8–9 |
| Expected-success | 1,800 × €30k | €54.0m | 85% / 36% | ≤€3m growth capital from retained cash/optional minority round; 70% ownership | 95% while employed, then continued majority reinvestment | 6× operating profit = €116.6m; 70% stake = **€81.6m** | 100 labs year 3; 600 year 5; 1,800 year 8 |
| Strong-success | 4,000 × €38k | €152m | 87% / 40% | ≤€10m including expansion financing; 62% ownership | 100% during high-growth phase | 7× operating profit = €425.6m; 62% stake = **€263.9m** | 250 labs year 3; 1,500 year 5; 4,000 year 7–8 |

The conservative case uses 7.5% of the currently visible six-country raw laboratory count and clears the €10m founder target by about 2×. That is apparent headroom, but it is **not yet a hard-gate pass** because the commercially eligible denominator and €24k ACV are unproved. A counted list of at least 3,000 eligible commercial labs and evidence that one pilot prevents at least €25k of annual loss are decision-critical.

### Founder Fit, Dependencies, and Recovery

- Strong data-science and uncertainty-modelling fit; no camera or public brand required.
- The founder does not interpret laboratory results, so scientific/accreditation liability is narrower than a diagnostic product.
- Critical dependencies: exportable job-cost outcomes, legal sufficiency of derived rights, adequate event density, and LIMS integration. None requires a speculative distribution partner.
- If cross-lab learning fails, reusable assets remain: method/sample risk ontology, three integration adapters, quote-replay engine, contract language and paid discovery evidence. They could support one lab's private deployment, but that fallback is a small software/service business and does not rescue the wealth gate.

### Generation Decision

**Finalist-worthy, conditional.** It is the only Round 10 direction with a direct payer, countable current population, founder-buildable seed asset, explicit first-contract data rights, a credible labour-decline mechanism and a conservative wealth model with more than marginal arithmetic headroom. The evaluator must treat eligible-lab count, ACV, loss baseline, data export and derived-right acceptance as possible vetoes—not details to assume.

## 2. RepairScope — Quote-Variance Underwriting for Electromechanical Repair Shops

### Inside Customer Truth and Loss Event

Motor, pump and gearbox repair shops frequently promise price and turnaround when intake notes, photographs and a preliminary test reveal less than teardown will. The manager is not trying to diagnose the plant's machine remotely; the commercially painful decision is how much uncertainty to reserve, what strip fee or scope condition to require, and when a fixed quote is unsafe.

The payer directly issues the quote. The closed outcome is quote value, intake symptoms/tests, teardown finding, approved scope, parts, labour, delay and realised contribution margin.

### Product and Control Point

The founder builds a symptom/inspection/asset taxonomy, strip-fee and contingency calculator, historical quote replay, and CSV/PDF intake parser. A first customer pays **6,000 PLN** to replay 30–50 jobs and grants perpetual rights to derived, non-customer-identifying intake features, teardown codes, cost variance and outcome bands. The production product recommends fixed quote, paid strip, range quote or exclusion before acceptance.

The 12-month control proof is ten signed shops, 5,000 closed repair jobs, three accounting/work-order mappings, and a held-out demonstration that pooled outcomes improve quote-variance calibration. Delivery should move from 20 hours per first shop to a standard import plus exception review.

### Cold Start, Denominator, and Economics

- EASA states that it has **more than 1,700 electromechanical sales and service firms in nearly 70 countries** covering motors, pumps, drives, gearboxes and other rotating machinery. This is a named global population, not a Poland count.
- Acquire the first three from independently researched Polish/German/Czech shop sites and industrial-fair exhibitor lists; do not use EASA profiles for marketing because its directory explicitly prohibits that use.
- Three prepaid 6,000 PLN replays fit a 20,000 PLN pre-proof budget. No OEM, insurer, plant or association partnership is needed.
- Hypothesised production price: €12k base plus per-quote fees, €36k blended ACV for larger multi-discipline shops.

**Wealth sensitivity:** 400 shops × €36k = €14.4m revenue; at 82% GM, 30% operating margin, 5× operating profit and 75% founder ownership, stake value is €16.2m. Expected 900 × €42k = €37.8m revenue; at 35% operating margin, 6× and 70% ownership, stake value is €55.6m. Strong 1,700 × €50k = €85m revenue; at 38%, 6× and 65%, stake value is €126m. Conservative/expected reinvest 95% while employed and cap optional distributions at 5%; strong reinvests 100% during growth. Capital hypotheses are €0.6m/€2m/€6m respectively, with 75%/70%/65% founder ownership and no double-counted distributions. Timings are years 9/8/7.

### Exact Competitor Check and Fatal Unknown

- **IQMotorBase** already connects intake, formal RFQs, work orders, motor testing and service history and says repeat-motor history helps shops quote and plan.
- **EMiR** has specialised in electromechanical repair, assembly, sales and service software for more than 30 years and includes job costing.
- **RepairStack** is an operating system for motor, pump and gearbox repair shops with variable scope and pricing.
- **ServiceCPQ** shows AI-generated hydraulic-pump repair quotes and predictive part-failure analysis.

None of the checked product pages clearly claims cross-shop intake-to-teardown quote-variance underwriting, but these incumbents own the workflow and can add it. The fatal unknown is whether enough shops have structured closed-job cost data and will pay roughly €36k annually. The conservative case needs 23.5% of EASA's entire member count at that ACV; nonmember shops are likely but uncounted. This is not substantial enough denominator headroom for finalist status yet.

### Generation Decision

**Reserve, not yet a finalist.** Promote only after 15 owner interviews yield at least three written 6,000 PLN pilot intents, at least five exportable datasets of 500+ closed jobs, and a counted eligible population at least four times the conservative customer target. Otherwise kill as a feature for EMiR/IQMotorBase.

## 3. WindowMargin — Configured-Order to Remake/Claim Learning

### Proposed Shape

Polish window/door exporters accept thousands of configured products whose apparent quoted margin can disappear through nonstandard geometry, dealer input error, production rework, installation exceptions or warranty claims. A first contract could grant rights to derived configuration, exception, remake/claim and actual-margin features; a 5,000 PLN historical replay could be sold to three unrelated manufacturers. The founder could build the first outcome joiner and risk taxonomy himself.

### Exact Competitor Check and Rejection

- **Logikal** already offers live manufacturer data, plausibility checks, ERP sync, production data and more than 18,000 users.
- **Klaes** calculates material and labour costs, manages orders, production and inventory in one vertical suite.
- **Cyncly Soft Tech V6 Advantage** provides CPQ, digital twins, BOMs, production, ERP, service and finance; it explicitly markets reliable estimates, true production costs and reduced rework.
- **InsightPro** is purpose-built warranty software for window/door manufacturers that captures installation data, service notes and failure patterns and distinguishes product, installation and environmental causes.

The proposed outcome graph is a valuable feature, but vertical incumbents already touch both sides of the loop and can copy it through one analytics module or customer-data agreement. Poland's export strength does not create founder control. Reject rather than claim a cross-customer corpus that does not exist.

## 4. GuaranteeMargin — Placement Guarantee-Loss Pricing for Recruitment Agencies

### Proposed Shape

Contingent recruiters price a placement fee while accepting a refund/free-replacement window whose expected labour and fee loss varies by client, role, process and guarantee terms. A founder-built replay could join placement terms to early-exit/replacement outcomes, suggest fee/guarantee combinations and retain only nonpersonal derived features under contract. WEC reports 2.5 million direct-recruitment placements in 2024, making the event real and large.

### Exact Competitor Check and Rejection

- **PRODICTA** directly sells recruiters a `Placement Survival Score`, flags live placements and positions the product as protection against fee/rebate risk.
- **Recruitly** tracks the guarantee period, rebate opportunities, billing and placement lifecycle.
- **Happlicant** uses historical data and predictive analytics to forecast candidate success and retention for small agencies.
- RecruitiFi's current guarantee help confirms that an employer can request a fee refund when the placement terminates within the guarantee period.

The contract-pricing angle is narrower than candidate survival, but it is one feature away for products already holding the outcome. It also introduces GDPR/fairness risk if candidate features leak into decisions. Reject; do not disguise a crowded hiring-prediction product as an actuarial control point.

## 5. RentalReserve — Per-Hire Damage and Maintenance Reserve Pricing

### Proposed Shape

Equipment rental operators accept a hire rate and waiver before knowing the customer's usage will create damage, unbilled repair or accelerated maintenance. A contract could grant rights to derived machine/hire/inspection/repair outcomes; the founder could initially price expected loss per contract rather than recover claims. ERA reports more than 18,000 European rental companies and about €31bn annual turnover, so the population and loss event are meaningful.

### Exact Competitor Check and Rejection

- **Texada** launched purpose-built AI damage detection for rental in March 2026, says it processes thousands of reports daily and has already identified millions of dollars in damage; it is integrated at checkout/check-in.
- **FieldFix** combines repair, service history and real cost per machine with failure-pattern analysis and cost prediction.
- **HireCheck** provides timestamped/GPS condition evidence and signatures at low monthly prices.
- Existing rental tools calculate damage waivers, repair costs, utilisation and maintenance inside the transaction workflow.

Texada already owns the high-quality before/after image and damage-outcome loop at enterprise customers. Pricing a waiver or reserve is an adjacent module, not founder-controlled infrastructure. Reject rather than enter a capital-rich vertical-software contest.

## 6. YieldMargin EMS — Quote-to-Realised Margin for Electronics Manufacturing Services

### Proposed Shape

High-mix EMS businesses quote a BOM, labour, yield and overhead before sourcing and production expose substitutions, engineering time, scrap and line yield. The payer controls the RFQ response. A first contract could grant rights to derived BOM/process/risk and realised-margin features, while raw BOMs and customer identities remain excluded.

### Exact Competitor Check and Rejection

- **CalcuQuote** says its Quote product has processed 986,000 BOMs and 641,000 RFQs. It already covers BOM cleanup, supplier pricing, labour, overhead, markup, margin, risk visibility, approvals and quote outcomes.
- CalcuQuote has operated since 2014, serves hundreds of electronics manufacturers and is rolling out industrial AI backed by Elisa Industriq.
- **aPriori** already models manufacturing costs and compares quoted and actual costs; general job-shop tools such as TrueQuote also advertise job-actual tracking.

The startup would begin with less data, less workflow access and no exclusive supply information against a vendor that already owns nearly one million BOM events. Cross-customer derived rights do not solve cold start because EMS customers are especially sensitive about BOM/process confidentiality. Reject.

## Decision-Critical Research Addendum — Exact LabMargin v1

**Research date:** 2026-07-13  
**Route:** follow-up research only after the advisory `research further` recommendation. No candidate wording, score, evaluation, pivot, panel, chatbot validation or customer contact was performed.  
**Interpretation:** this addendum supersedes the earlier LabMargin factual assumptions wherever they conflict. It tests the exact candidate: independent commercial ISO/IEC 17025 laboratories in Germany, the United Kingdom, France, the Netherlands, Italy and Spain; 750 accounts at €24,000 blended ACV; price no more than 20% of independently documented annual value.

### Result at a Glance

| Decision-critical fact | Public evidence result | Consequence for the exact candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Independent commercial buying-entity denominator | **Unavailable** | 750-account penetration cannot be calculated; 7.45% is only a theoretical floor against inconsistent raw directory entries. |
| ≥€120,000 recoverable annual value per lab from LabMargin's exact mechanism | **Unavailable** | €24,000 ACV does not satisfy the candidate's own 20%-of-value rule. |
| Quote-to-actual fields and exportability | **Partial, configuration-dependent** | Some LIMS/ERP products expose component fields, but no common order-level schema or prevalence evidence exists. |
| Perpetual de-identified derived-label/model rights | **Legally possible in principle; target authority unproved** | A clause is not a control point until each lab's upstream rights, confidentiality and anonymisation position are verified and signed. |
| Pooled cross-lab lift over within-lab calibration | **Unavailable** | The claimed data moat is a future experiment, not an evidenced advantage; adjacent literature highlights domain-shift risk. |
| Competitor capability and pricing | **Material overlap; only two relevant vendors publish prices** | Incumbents already hold quotes, costs, retests, utilisation and accounting connections; the uncopied layer is narrower than the original brief implied. |

### 1. Bottom-Up Count of Independent Commercial Buying Entities

No current public source supplies the requested denominator. The official national sources expose laboratories, accreditations, accredited bodies, locations or mixed departmental totals—not the intersection of:

1. a distinct legal entity rather than a site, scope or accreditation reference;
2. privately controlled and commercially selling testing to third parties rather than an internal industrial, public, university, forensic or research laboratory;
3. an independent purchasing decision rather than a branch of a group with central procurement; and
4. enough priced external orders and local data authority to be an eligible LabMargin buyer.

The best current official checks are therefore boundary evidence, not a bottom-up buying-account count:

- **United Kingdom:** the [UKAS 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ukas.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/741381_UKAS_AnnualReport-website.pdf) reports 1,170 ISO/IEC 17025 testing laboratories in 2025. It does not classify commercial status, ownership, order volume or purchasing authority. UKAS schedules also show that one accredited organisation can operate multiple locations; the [UKAS sanctions list](https://www.ukas.com/customer-area/sanctions/organisations-under-sanction/) has separately referenced locations under the same legal entity, including Eurofins Electrical & Electronic UK Limited.
- **France:** [Cofrac's Laboratories division](https://www.cofrac.fr/en/a-propos-du-cofrac/our-organisation/laboratories-division) reports 1,820 accredited entities, but that division includes testing and calibration laboratories, proficiency-testing organisers and reference-material producers. It is not an independent-commercial testing buyer count.
- **Spain:** [ENAC](https://www.enac.es/web/english/what-we-do/accredited-services/testing-laboratories) says “over 900” accredited testing laboratories but provides no current commercial/private or buying-authority classification. ENAC's [2019 figures](https://www.enac.es/documents/7020/3555061/cifras-memoria-2019/1c7a2c77-ca5f-4a43-93c3-c217b199c434) separately reported 900 testing entities and 1,103 testing accreditation certificates, demonstrating why certificates or records cannot be assumed to equal buyers.
- **Italy:** [Accredia's current headline](https://accredia.it/en/) combines 1,397 testing laboratories, medical laboratories and proficiency-testing providers. The public aggregate does not isolate independent commercial ISO 17025 test-lab legal entities or centrally buying groups.
- **Germany and the Netherlands:** [DAkkS](https://www.dakks.de/en/testing-and-calibration-laboratories.html) and [RvA](https://www.rva.nl/en/test-laboratories/) provide accredited-body search routes, but their public pages do not publish the requested independent-commercial, legal-entity, purchase-authority count.

The non-official ScopeMatch interface is not a substitute. Its [current homepage](https://scopematch.eu/) simultaneously states 10,051 verified laboratories while its lower country figures sum to 10,067 (Germany 3,188; UK 2,381; France 1,301; Netherlands 584; Italy 1,424; Spain 1,189). A second navigation section on the same page displays still higher country counts. Even a perfectly reconciled record total would still contain sites, group subsidiaries and ineligible internal/public bodies.

**Evidence conclusion:** a bottom-up six-country count of independent commercial buying entities is **unavailable** from current public data. Producing it would require legal-entity normalisation, group-parent matching, public/private and captive/commercial classification, verification of external ordering, and identification of central versus site-level buying authority. The exact candidate cannot use 10,051, 10,067, 1,170, 1,820, 1,397 or “over 900” as its eligible-account denominator.

### 2. Proof of ≥€120,000 Annual Recoverable Value per Lab

The exact rule is arithmetic, not optional positioning:

`€24,000 annual price ÷ 20% maximum value share = €120,000 minimum independently documented annual improvement per lab.`

Public case evidence confirms that laboratory digitisation can produce substantial operational benefits, but none isolates at least €120,000 per year from **pre-acceptance quote underpricing, sample-preparation variance, rework/retest risk and turnaround-risk pricing** in an independent commercial test lab:

- Thermo Fisher's [Examen case](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/DSD/Reference-Materials/samplemanager-lims-examen-case-study.pdf) reports administrative effort falling from 82.5 to 13.75 hours/month and reporting from 41.25 to 4.5 hours/month—about 1,266 hours/year combined. It supplies no cash value and attributes the gain to broad LIMS automation, not quote-time margin underwriting.
- Thermo Fisher's [Patheon Cork case](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/DSD/brochures/Patheon%20PS_Cork_SampleManager%20Case%20Study_EN.pdf) reports 832 analyst hours/year saved on balance calibrations. Patheon is a large pharmaceutical manufacturing site, and the mechanism is laboratory execution automation, not commercial-order quote selection.
- Confience's [Colorado Analytical Laboratories case](https://www.confience.io/customer-story/colorado-analytical-laboratories) says auto-reporting, invoicing and emailing freed two administrative employees for a second location and that instrument integration saved several hours per week per instrument. It gives no salary, contribution-margin or quote-underpricing amount and does not isolate the LabMargin mechanism.
- UST's [global industrial testing case](https://www.ust.com/en/insights/ust-helped-global-industrial-testing-firm-modernize-legacy-lab-systems-increased-productivity-50-percentand-nps-30-percent) reports a 50% throughput increase and projected 20% OPEX reduction, but the client operated 248 labs, had at least 48 LIMS applications and undertook an enterprise-wide consolidation. No per-independent-lab cash value or quote-time attribution is disclosed.
- A current [MLO laboratory-analytics article](https://www.mlo-online.com/information-technology/analytics/article/53062533/lab-analytics-five-ways-to-grow-your-lab-using-real-time-data) claims up to 15% bottom-line impact from test/client profitability visibility. It is an illustrative/vendor-linked clinical-lab article, not an audited independent commercial-testing baseline and not a quote-to-realised-outcome study.

These cases cannot be added together or monetised with an assumed hourly rate. They prove broad workflow value and incumbent capability; they do not prove the exact product's incremental value after existing LIMS, finance and pricing tools.

**Evidence conclusion:** a quantified public baseline of at least €120,000 annual recoverable value per target lab attributable to LabMargin is **unavailable**. The earlier “at least €25,000 of annual loss” research threshold was arithmetically wrong and is superseded by €120,000. On current evidence the €24,000 ACV fails its own 20%-share proof requirement.

### 4. Contractual Precedent and Legal Limits for Derived Rights

There is general SaaS precedent for persistent rights in de-identified/aggregated data:

- [Validity's current terms](https://www.validity.com/legal/terms-of-service/) use a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive right to generate and use de-identified and aggregated customer data for products and services, subject to not identifying the source or disclosing it beyond subcontractors.
- [Midmark's current software terms](https://www.midmark.com/docs/default-source/legal-documents/smart-view-terms-of-use.pdf) preserve customer ownership, permit de-identified/aggregated analytics and new offerings, require safeguards against re-identification, and separately address identifiable-data deletion after termination.
- [Backlight's current terms](https://www.backlightapp.ai/terms) permit analytics, benchmarking, research and product improvement from aggregated/de-identified data only if it is not reasonably linkable to a customer, user, repository, organisation or person, with no re-identification and equivalent recipient restrictions.

That establishes **contract form**, not LabMargin's authority chain. Public LIMS terms do not supply the missing proof. [QBench's standard terms](https://qbench.com/terms), for example, state that customers retain private content and prohibit uploading information a customer has no right to provide under law, contract, fiduciary duty, confidentiality or trade-secret rights; a separate negotiated MSA may govern.

The exact limits are material:

- Under [GDPR Recital 26](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679), only information that no longer relates to an identifiable person, considering means reasonably likely to be used, falls outside the GDPR. The [EDPB's 2025 pseudonymisation guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-adopts-pseudonymisation-guidelines-and-paves-the-way-to-improve-cooperation-with_en) confirms pseudonymised data remains personal data.
- The [EDPB Opinion 28/2024 summary](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-opinion-on-ai-models-gdpr-principles-support-responsible-ai_ga) says model anonymity is case-specific: it must be very unlikely both that people can be identified and that training personal data can be extracted through queries. “Model parameters” are therefore not automatically anonymous.
- ISO/IEC 17025 confidentiality is directly relevant. [ISO's explanatory material](https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/news/magazine/ISOfocus%20%282013-NOW%29/en/2016/ISOfocus117/isofocus_117.pdf) says information obtained or created during laboratory performance is generally confidential except public information or as agreed by the laboratory and customer; [UKAS guidance](https://www.ukas.com/wp-content/uploads/schedule_uploads/759162/LAB-13-Guidance-on-the-Application-of-ISO-IEC-17025-Opinions-and-Interpretations.pdf) notes legally enforceable confidentiality commitments also need to cover subcontractors.
- The [EU Trade Secrets Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/943/oj/eng/) treats use or disclosure in breach of a confidentiality agreement or contractual duty to limit use as unlawful. A lab cannot grant LabMargin rights that its own customer contract withholds.
- The [EU Database Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=celex%3A31996L0009) permits a database right to be licensed but protects substantial extraction/re-utilisation and existing rights in the contents. The correct grantor and scope must therefore be established.

“Perpetual” and “irreversibly de-identified” do not override source-customer confidentiality, trade-secret, database, personal-data, retention or deletion obligations. A defensible proof would require a signed negotiated rider, a sample audit of the lab's upstream customer terms, field-level exclusions, an anonymisation and model-extraction assessment, survival/deletion language, and proof the signatory controls the relevant database rights. No public evidence establishes that independent commercial labs in the six countries routinely possess and will grant this exact authority.

**Evidence conclusion:** the clause is **possible in principle but unproved for the target data and grantors**. No public laboratory-specific precedent establishes perpetual cross-lab reuse of quote-to-actual commercial outcome labels or model parameters. The proposed control point cannot be treated as secured until signed and rights-audited.

### 5. Does Pooled Cross-Lab Data Beat Within-Lab Calibration?

No direct study or product case was found comparing:

- a lab's own quote/history baseline;
- a within-lab model trained on that lab's realised orders; and
- a pooled, de-identified cross-lab model for quote underpricing, preparation variance, rework, turnaround and realised margin.

The adjacent analytical literature is not evidence of business-model lift and, if anything, warns against assuming frictionless transfer:

- A 2025 [cross-instrument and cross-coal-type study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40903127/) says instrument and material differences can make established models fail. Its domain-adversarial method improved transfer, but still required target calibration samples.
- A 2017 [review of calibration transfer](https://opg.optica.org/as/abstract.cfm?uri=as-72-3-340) says statistically identical results across instruments remain elusive because of instrument, reference-value, mathematics and sample-presentation differences.
- A 2024 [review of instrument cloning and model maintenance](https://www.cambridge.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/6639d77291aefa6ce15c281a) frames cross-instrument use as a dataset-shift/domain-adaptation problem requiring explicit model maintenance.

These papers concern measurements, not quote economics, so they cannot be used to reject pooling scientifically. They do show why different method families, costing policies, labour rates, instruments, currencies, customer mixes, sample coding and turnaround definitions can destroy naive comparability. The candidate's proposed held-out pooled-versus-local test is the **required experiment**, not evidence already in hand.

**Evidence conclusion:** evidence that pooled cross-lab commercial-outcome data outperforms within-lab calibration is **unavailable**. Until demonstrated on multiple independent labs with leave-one-lab-out testing and local recalibration controls, the cross-lab corpus is not a proved control point or switching cost.

### 7. Correct Scenario Arithmetic and Eligible Penetration

The fixed conservative case calculates as follows:

- 750 accounts × €24,000 = **€18.0m revenue**.
- 82% gross margin = **€14.76m gross profit**.
- 30% operating margin = **€5.40m operating profit**.
- 5× operating profit = **€27.0m business value**.
- 75% founder ownership = **€20.25m founder stake value**.
- Under the 20% value-share rule, each account must have at least **€120,000 annual independently documented improvement**; 750 accounts therefore require at least **€90.0m annual customer value** in aggregate.

The other published scenario arithmetic is:

- Expected: €54.0m revenue × 36% operating margin × 6× value multiple × 70% founder ownership = **€81.648m** founder stake value.
- Strong: €152.0m revenue × 40% operating margin × 7× value multiple × 62% founder ownership = **€263.872m** founder stake value.

Those calculations do not repair the denominator. Let `E` be the unknown count of eligible independent commercial buying entities. The exact conservative penetration is `750 / E`:

| Eligible buying entities `E` | Required penetration for 750 accounts |
|---:|---:|
| 10,067 raw directory entries | 7.45% theoretical floor only |
| 7,500 | 10.0% |
| 5,000 | 15.0% |
| 3,000 | 25.0% |
| 2,000 | 37.5% |
| 1,500 | 50.0% |
| 1,000 | 75.0% |

At least 7,500 genuinely eligible buying entities are required merely to keep the conservative case at 10% penetration. A 5% penetration case would require 15,000 eligible entities—already above the lower six-country raw record total. The earlier proposed threshold of 3,000 eligible labs would make 750 customers a **25%** share, not substantial denominator headroom. Against the same raw 10,067 entries, the expected 1,800-customer case is 17.88% and the strong 4,000-customer case is 39.73%; their true eligible shares would be higher.

**Evidence conclusion:** the valuation multiplication is internally correct after rounding, but eligible penetration is **not calculable**. The 7.45% statement is a best-case lower bound against inconsistent, non-deduplicated, non-commercial records, not a market-share estimate.

### Research Disposition for the Exact Candidate

The exact LabMargin v1 evidence request cannot be closed positively:

- the independent commercial buying-entity denominator is unavailable;
- the required €120,000-per-lab annual value baseline for the exact mechanism is unavailable;
- a complete, standard quote-to-actual export is not established across common systems;
- general derived-data clauses exist, but target labs' authority to grant the exact pooled rights is unproved and legally constrained;
- pooled cross-lab lift over within-lab calibration is unavailable; and
- incumbents already control much of the source data, quote action and adjacent analytics at public prices below or near the proposed ACV.

Accordingly, this research record supports **closing the exact LabMargin v1 shape from the current funnel before any panel or real validation**. That is a research disposition, not an advisory score or simulated/real verdict. Reopening the same shape would require new non-public documentary evidence—not inference—covering a reconciled eligible-entity list, audited or customer-documented ≥€120,000 annual quote-related value, three complete quote-to-actual exports, signed and rights-audited derived-data riders, and a multi-lab pooled-versus-local lift result.
