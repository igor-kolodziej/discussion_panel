# WooRenewal v2 — Merchant-Workflow Acquisition

## Idea

WooRenewal buys one neglected but cash-flowing WooCommerce plugin only when the official WordPress owner/update channel, installed merchant base, paid licences and renewals, licence server, billing history, domain, brand, complete source and contractor IP, customer-transfer rights, and support history transfer together through escrow.

The first target must sit inside a repeated merchant workflow—order handling, fulfilment, returns, catalogue, inventory, or checkout operations—where removal forces configuration migration or interrupts live work. WooRenewal preserves compatibility and security first; it does not require a new feature, advertising channel, speculative partnership, or price increase to produce the first post-transfer receipt.

For this revision, success means a credible route to **1,000,000 PLN of recognized annual plugin revenue by Year 5 with at least 25% operating margin**. Recognized revenue excludes merchant GMV, pass-through payments, VAT, and revenue belonging to third parties. This Revenue Quality Gate replaces the prior founder-wealth target for this run.

## One-Sentence Thesis

In GPL merchant software, code is replicable but the official update path into configured stores and the attached renewal relationship are scarce distribution assets that an unknown technical founder may be able to buy before building a new product.

## Founder Fit

The founder is a Warsaw data scientist who can audit cohorts, automate sourcing and support triage, model acquisitions, and use AI with a contract WordPress security engineer. The business is global, asynchronous, low-camera, and constrained to 15–25 founder hours per week while corporate employment continues.

The minimum POC stack is the founder, a contract WordPress security engineer, and a transaction/privacy lawyer. A support contractor is added only after transferred receipts exist. The founder is not presented as an established WordPress acquirer; seller trust is an explicit dependency.

## Customer and Pain

The paying customer is a WooCommerce merchant with a plugin embedded in a repeated operational workflow. The merchant renews because supported updates, security, and backward compatibility cost less than reconfiguration, migration, operational interruption, and retraining.

The seller is an independent developer or small team with a paid cohort but insufficient time or strategic interest. WooRenewal offers immediate cash, a receipt-capped seller note, escrow, a public stewardship plan, and continuity for users. WordPress.org's current warning about unknown acquisition approaches means cold seller outreach is a hypothesis, not established access.

## Acquisition Wedge

Acquire one plugin meeting every condition:

- 100–250 independent paying sites and at least 12 months of billing/cohort evidence;
- at least 75% annual logo renewal or an equivalent evidenced retention measure;
- preferably 4,000–10,000 active free installations, so the installed free channel can support measured paid conversion without requiring a transfer above the 10,000-user manual-review threshold;
- current recognized annual revenue of roughly 15,000–60,000 PLN, with low monetization caused by founder neglect rather than weak merchant need;
- total price no higher than 2.0× verified trailing owner earnings or 2.5× recurring gross profit;
- no more than 28,000 PLN cash before post-transfer paid proof; remaining consideration is a 24-month note serviced only from transferred receipts and capped at the agreed price;
- no more than ten support hours monthly and no unresolved critical vulnerability;
- primarily organic WordPress directory, search, direct, update, or merchant-referral acquisition;
- full assignability of official owner/update access, repository and contractor IP, domain/brand, licence server, billing/payout product, customer-transfer rights, analytics, and support history.

Any missing update, billing, customer-transfer, or IP asset kills the deal. Source code alone has zero acquisition value.

## Commercial Insight and Market Gap

Small software acquisitions often value code and reported profit while underweighting the official installed update path, merchant configurations, renewal cohorts, support history, search position, and billing relationship. GPL competitors can copy features, but they do not inherit those customer-specific assets.

The market gap is below institutional software acquisition: assets too small for private equity but large enough to contain paying users. The public evidence proves that small plugin sales and formal transfer mechanisms exist. It does not prove that a target satisfying WooRenewal's retention, price, trust, and atomic-transfer constraints is currently available.

## Underused Momentum

WordPress.org formally supports owner transfer and abandoned-plugin adoption, and Freemius supports product-ownership transfer. Public listings show small plugin assets offered between approximately USD 830 and USD 6,000, including a WooCommerce plugin claiming organic installs and transferable Freemius billing.

The countervailing momentum is more important than v1 acknowledged: WordPress.org now explicitly warns developers that unsolicited purchase offers are usually untrustworthy and that acquired plugins have harmed users. WooRenewal must therefore win seller and user trust through transparent ownership, independent code review, two-person release approval, public change logs, no undisclosed telemetry, and no aggressive post-acquisition monetization.

## Beachhead

One operational plugin family only: catalogue/inventory operations, order handling, fulfilment, returns, or checkout controls for WooCommerce stores with 1–20 staff. The first asset must store meaningful configuration, require recurring compatibility/security work, and solve a repeated operational job.

No unrelated second acquisition is permitted. A second asset must share merchants, keywords, support/security infrastructure, or the same job and must show at least 10% measured cross-sell or material shared operating cost before a third product is considered.

## Primary Control Point

After closing, the control point is Tier 1: simultaneous ownership of the official WordPress.org owner/commit/update channel, installed configurations, paid licence and renewal records, lawful billing/payout rights, licence server, domain and search surface, brand, repository and complete IP, and compatibility/support history.

Before closing, the control point is Tier 3 because no target is reserved or contracted. The strongest copy risk is a larger plugin suite adding equivalent features and bundling them into an existing customer base. It cannot instantly copy the acquired configurations, update credential, renewal cohorts, support history, or merchant trust, but it can erode new sales and pricing.

### Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Use the completed 903-plugin public screen to prioritize 41 pre-data-room leads.
2. Only after separate outreach authorization, approach 100 owners through public marketplace or developer channels using a named founder identity, stewardship policy, escrow terms, and proof of security/legal advisers.
3. Require five read-only data rooms covering owner/update rights, billing, cohorts, refunds, acquisition source, support, repository/IP, licence server, privacy, and transfer mechanics.
4. Spend at most 5,000 PLN on sourcing/accounts and at most 7,000 PLN on legal, security, code, and privacy diligence after a credible data room exists.
5. Release no more than 28,000 PLN cash through escrow only after every listed asset transfers. Any note is paid only from transferred receipts.
6. Receive the first unrelated automatic renewal or new licence in the founder entity by Day 180.
7. Freeze pricing and features for the first 60 days except security and compatibility fixes. Publish the ownership change, audit summary, privacy position, and release process.

The resource-control proof is the executed asset schedule, official owner transfer, test payout, complete IP chain, and first unrelated post-transfer receipt—not the marketplace listing or seller's spreadsheet.

### Unit Economics and Value Capture

The initial asset must have no-growth cash payback below 30 months. Existing customers are grandfathered for at least one renewal cycle. New annual tiers may range from 600 to 1,500 PLN per site or agency account only after willingness to pay and churn are measured.

The Year-5 target requires 1,000,000 PLN recognized revenue and at least 250,000 PLN operating profit after payment fees, refunds, support, hosting, security, privacy/legal work, contractor development, founder replacement cost, and normal reserves. Acquisition principal and interest remain financing cash flows and liabilities; they are not hidden from founder-equity calculations.

| Scenario | Customers, pricing and milestones | Year-5 economics | Capital, ownership and reinvestment | Indicative founder stake at Year 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | One asset; 600 paid accounts at 900 PLN blended recognized revenue. Year 1 transfer, Year 2 retention proof, Years 3–5 organic conversion and measured price packaging. | 540,000 PLN revenue; 88% gross margin; 25% operating margin = 135,000 PLN. This does **not** reach the satisfactory revenue target. | Up to 80,000 PLN cumulative cash and notes; founder 100%; 95% of after-tax distributable cash reinvested while employed; distributions no more than 5%. | At 3× operating profit less 50,000 PLN obligations/reserves: about 355,000 PLN founder equity. |
| Expected-success | Two related assets; 1,100 paid accounts at 910 PLN blended recognized revenue. 150 accounts after first transfer, 350 by Year 2, 600 by Year 3, 850 by Year 4, 1,100 by Year 5. | 1,001,000 PLN revenue; 90% gross margin; 30% operating margin = 300,300 PLN. | Up to 250,000 PLN cumulative consideration funded by original capital, retained cash, and receipt-capped notes; founder 95% after a 5% employee/contractor pool; 95% reinvestment while employed. | At 3× operating profit less 100,000 PLN obligations/reserves: about 761,000 PLN founder equity. |
| Strong-success | Three tightly related assets; 2,000 paid accounts at 1,100 PLN blended recognized revenue, with agency tiers and demonstrated cross-sell. | 2,200,000 PLN revenue; 92% gross margin; 38% operating margin = 836,000 PLN. | Up to 600,000 PLN cumulative consideration after repeatable economics; founder 85% after 10% employee pool and 5% outside financing; 100% reinvestment until scale. | At 4× operating profit less 200,000 PLN obligations/reserves: about 2.67m PLN founder equity. |

The expected scenario is the satisfactory case. It is a hypothesis, not a conservative result: growing from roughly 100–150 paid accounts to 1,100 in five years requires strong organic conversion, retained renewal, and one related acquisition. Kill the Year-5 thesis if 12-month evidence does not support at least 35% annual recognized-revenue growth without churn or support deterioration.

### Distribution Advantage

The first customers and first receipt come from the transferred cohort. Later acquisition uses organic WordPress directory/search visibility, permitted documentation and upgrade surfaces, referrals, renewal communication, and cross-sell to the same merchant job. Tracking or external communication requires explicit consent under WordPress rules.

Distribution compounds only if organic installs continue, free-to-paid conversion is measurable, renewal remains at least 75%, and the second asset reaches the same merchants. If every product needs a fresh seller funnel and a separate customer-acquisition engine, the roll-up thesis fails.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion

1. Preserve and audit one asset for six months.
2. Measure renewal, refunds, support, security, organic installs, free-to-paid conversion, and new-price acceptance.
3. Build release automation, compatibility tests, and a public stewardship record around the same operational job.
4. Add one adjacent asset only after the first shows positive contribution and a no-growth payback below 30 months.
5. Require merchant overlap, at least 10% cross-sell, or material shared support/security savings.

The acquired update channel creates a head start, not permanent monopoly. Durability comes from installed configuration, accumulated compatibility/support knowledge, merchant trust, and shared workflow distribution. These advantages are narrower than a network effect and remain exposed to WooCommerce platform policy and AI-driven code commoditization.

## Local Adaptation Defense

Poland provides lower-cost technical/security talent, EU privacy discipline, and founder fit, but no regulatory moat is claimed. The market and acquired distribution are global. Seller trust cannot be replaced with a Polish cost advantage.

## Why This Could Compound

Renewal cash can fund security, compatibility, support automation, and one adjacent acquisition. Each genuinely related asset can add installed merchants, update permission, support cases, compatibility knowledge, documentation/search traffic, and cross-sell. Compounding is conditional: two assets must prove shared distribution or operating leverage before the portfolio claim is accepted.

## Revenue Quality Gate

**Not yet passed by evidence.** The expected scenario reaches 1m PLN recognized revenue at 30% operating margin, but the public record does not prove a qualifying first target or the required 1,100-account growth bridge. The conservative scenario reaches only 540,000 PLN.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–30: prioritize the 41 public leads, prepare the founder identity, stewardship policy, data-room request, escrow schedule, seller-note term sheet, and diligence checklist.
- Days 31–75: only after outreach authorization, contact 100 owners and require five credible data rooms. Public listings alone do not pass.
- Days 76–120: verify 12-month receipts/cohorts, payer count, refunds, support, acquisition source, ownership, code/security, privacy, and every transfer.
- Days 121–160: close one acquisition with no more than 28,000 PLN cash and no more than 40,000 PLN total pre-proof exposure.
- By Day 180: receive an unrelated renewal or new licence in the founder entity and calculate post-transfer contribution and support/security time.

**Gate result: plausible but unproved.** It is compatible with employment and the cash cap, and a public USD 6,000 WooCommerce listing shows that an asset can fit the envelope. The decisive uncontrolled dependency is whether a legitimate seller will trust this unknown founder and provide a qualifying, assignable data room.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

1. Up to 5,000 PLN for sourcing, entity, accounts, and templates.
2. Up to 12,000 PLN total only after a credible read-only data room, for legal/security/code/privacy diligence.
3. Up to 40,000 PLN total only after verified cohorts and escrow-ready transfers; cash consideration remains at or below 28,000 PLN.
4. Remaining original founder capital up to 100,000 PLN only after official transfer, first receipt, 90 days of stable contribution/security/support, and no-growth payback below 30 months.
5. Family capital remains excluded until two related assets, six months of post-transfer cohort evidence, and repeatable positive contribution exist.

## Critical Dependencies

| Dependency | Controlled test | Kill response |
|---|---|---|
| A legitimate qualifying seller trusts the founder | 100 authorized contacts and five primary data rooms | Kill acquisition sourcing if the funnel fails; do not buy code-only substitutes |
| Official update ownership transfers | Confirmed owner transfer before escrow release | No release; deal dies |
| Billing, licences and customers transfer lawfully | Processor/product approval, contracts/consents and test payout | No release; deal dies |
| IP and code are complete and secure | Assignments, repository history, dependency/security review | Reject unresolved ownership or critical vulnerability |
| Renewal survives transfer | First receipt and six-month cohort tracking | Stop new acquisitions below 75% annualized renewal |
| One asset can grow organically | Paid conversion, recognized-revenue growth and support load | Kill the 1m Year-5 claim below 35% evidence-backed annual growth |
| A second asset compounds distribution | Merchant overlap, 10% cross-sell or shared operating savings | Kill portfolio expansion if distribution resets |

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder remains employed until there are at least two related assets or equivalent recurring products, 500 independent paying accounts, six months of post-transfer cohorts, positive contribution after support/security/refunds/notes, evidence of repeatable organic conversion or acquisition sourcing, and 12 months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

A rejected target leaves the 903-row sourcing database, cohort/valuation model, security scripts, asset schedule, privacy checklist, and explicit rejection evidence. Escrow limits purchase-price loss. No migration or rebrand is forced on acquired merchants.

## Validation Plan

The first 180 days test seller access, data truth, transfer completeness, first cash, and employment-compatible operation. Months 7–12 test renewal, pricing, organic installs, support, security, and recognized-revenue growth. Only executed transfers, processor payouts, cohorts, and operating logs count as proof.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than five credible data rooms after 100 authorized owner contacts, or no qualifying LOI by Day 90.
- Cash requirement above 28,000 PLN before the first unrelated post-transfer receipt.
- Missing or conditional owner/update, billing/payout, licence-server, IP, domain, customer-transfer, or support-history asset.
- Seller identity or financial claims cannot be independently verified.
- Customer acquisition depends on seller personality, non-transferable affiliates, prohibited tracking, or uneconomic paid ads.
- No unrelated post-transfer receipt by Day 180.
- Annualized renewal below 75%, unresolved critical vulnerability beyond 14 days, fully loaded support/security above 15% of revenue, or no-growth cash payback above 30 months.
- Evidence after 12 months does not support at least 35% annual recognized-revenue growth toward 1m PLN or shows that pricing growth requires destructive churn.
- A second asset has no merchant overlap, 10% cross-sell, or material shared operating saving.

## Budget and Time

Pre-proof exposure is capped at 40,000 PLN: 5,000 PLN sourcing/entity/accounts, up to 7,000 PLN additional legal/security/code/privacy diligence, and at most 28,000 PLN acquisition cash. First-year founder capital remains capped at 100,000 PLN and is released only by the stated gates. Founder time is 15–25 hours weekly.

## 30-Day Plan

1. Select one merchant workflow family from the 41 public leads.
2. Prepare the public stewardship policy, founder identity package, data-room request, asset schedule, seller-note terms, and security/privacy checklist.
3. Verify three public sale precedents and record why each does or does not meet the target specification.
4. Do not contact a seller until outreach is separately authorized.

## Yearly Plan

- Year 1: acquire one qualifying asset or kill sourcing; preserve users; collect six months of cohort, support, and security evidence.
- Year 2: reach approximately 350 paid accounts and prove measured organic conversion; evaluate one related asset.
- Year 3: reach approximately 600 paid accounts across one or two related assets; centralize release/security/support.
- Year 4: reach approximately 850 paid accounts with at least 25% operating margin and evidence-backed growth to the target.
- Year 5: reach at least 1m PLN recognized annual revenue and 250,000 PLN operating profit, or state that the satisfactory target was not achieved.
