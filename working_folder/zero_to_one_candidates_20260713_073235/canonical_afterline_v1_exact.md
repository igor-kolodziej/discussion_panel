# Confirmed Business Idea

## Idea

**Afterline Installed-Base Succession Buyout** acquires the complete aftermarket position of one retiring or non-core Polish/CEE niche machine builder at a time: technical files, tooling, remaining spares, service history, product/service mark, transferable service contracts, and the seller-authorised route to customers who already operate the machines.

The first transaction is a product-line carveout, not a whole-company acquisition and not a spare-parts brokerage. Afterline signs a 90–120 day exclusive option for no more than 8,000 PLN upfront. During the option, the seller co-signs a continuity offer to the installed base. Afterline closes only after at least three unrelated customers prepay at least 25,000 PLN in total and the first fulfilled order demonstrates at least 45% contribution margin. Seller consideration is predominantly a capped earn-out from realised gross profit, so demand—not founder optimism—finances the transfer.

## One-Sentence Thesis

The hidden asset inside a retiring niche machine OEM is its captive installed base plus technical truth, and an unknown founder can acquire that distribution-and-rights bundle on seller-financed terms only after its existing customers have prepaid for continuity.

## Founder Fit

The founder is a Warsaw data scientist with neuroscience, business-analysis, finance, FMCG, and AI experience. He can build the target database, automate document ingestion and parts matching, model installed-base economics, and standardise diligence and quoting. His parents' seven-person renewable-installation company gives him practical familiarity with field service, electrical work, quoting, and owner-led operations, but it is not a required supplier or sales channel.

The minimum stack during proof is the founder at 15–25 hours per week, a contract machinery engineer, a transaction/IP lawyer, and order-paid field technicians or fabricators. The founder is not the permanent chief technician. This fits employment because sourcing, data-room analysis, and written outreach are asynchronous; calls and site work are batched or contracted.

## Customer And Pain

The first paying customer is a Polish/CEE food, packaging, wood, building-products, or light-manufacturing factory operating a maintenance-critical niche machine from a small owner-led OEM. When the OEM founder retires or abandons a product line, the factory faces long downtime, undocumented substitutions, technician loss, and pressure to replace an otherwise serviceable machine. Current workarounds are calling the founder personally, hoarding parts, commissioning one-off reverse engineering, or replacing the machine.

The asset seller is an owner, estate, or small manufacturer that wants retirement income and customer continuity without running an aftermarket organisation. The offer preserves the seller's name and customer relationships while converting a neglected service obligation into an auditable earn-out.

## Product/Project/Idea Wedge

Start with one non-regulated, maintenance-critical machine family with 100–1,500 traceable active machines, repeat spare or retrofit demand, no dominant global OEM support, and no safety case requiring an unowned certification or manufacturer authorisation.

The 90–120 day option covers a named schedule of drawings, bills of material, manuals, firmware/source rights where applicable, service history, supplier files, jigs, moulds, fixtures, remaining spares, product/service mark, transferable service agreements, liabilities, and seller-approved installed-base communication. During the option, Afterline sells three fixed products: an 8,000–25,000 PLN annual continuity plan, essential-spares orders with 45–70% target gross margin, and planned retrofits with 25–40% target gross margin. Emergency field work is priced to cover contractors and collect failure knowledge; it is not the core profit pool.

## Commercial Insight Or Market Gap

Business brokers sell whole companies, service firms sell labour, and parts dealers sell catalogued inventory. The overlooked unit is the complete aftermarket position of a small installed base: customer access, technical documentation, legal permission, tooling, supplier recipes, and service history together. Separately these assets look too small for private equity and too specialised for a general broker. Assembled under exclusive rights, they create the only authorised continuity route for customers who cannot switch without machine replacement, requalification, or reverse engineering.

## Underused Momentum

On 23 June 2026 the European Commission described SME ownership transfer as a pressing demographic challenge, with growing numbers of owners approaching retirement without successors and potentially millions of firms affected during the next decade. The Commission's 22 June 2026 recommendation calls for better transfer preparation, financing, and matchmaking. More than 60% of European companies are family businesses. EU repair and ecodesign policy is also increasing the strategic value of repair information and spare-part continuity. These are timing signals, not proof that a particular seller will transact or that an industrial product is directly covered by consumer repair rules.

## Beachhead Market

The beachhead is one owner-led Polish OEM or product-line carveout in non-regulated food, packaging, woodworking, building-products, or light-industrial machinery within practical reach of Wielkopolska/Warsaw. It must have 100–1,500 verifiably active machines, historical parts/service invoices, identifiable customers, clean assignable rights, and an owner approaching exit. One machine family is small enough to dominate and valuable enough to produce 2–4 million PLN of annual aftermarket revenue if the demand hypothesis is true.

## Primary Control Point

The control point is an executed exclusive option and, at closing, assignment or exclusive licence to the complete aftermarket position for the named product line: technical IP/files, tooling, spares, service mark, transferable contracts, service history, and seller-authorised installed-base communication. It is Tier 1 only when every essential right is enforceable and non-revocable. If the seller merely introduces customers, retains an essential right, or can license the same package again, the control point collapses and the deal is killed.

### Cold Start & Resource Bootstrapping

1. Build a 100-target list from used-machine CE plates, trade-fair catalogues, dealer listings, KRS/CEIDG activity and owner-age signals, public restructuring data, and direct written outreach.
2. Offer the owner a product-line transfer rather than a whole-company sale: 3,000–8,000 PLN option fee, 10–25% cash at a demand-gated closing, and 20–35% of realised gross profit for three to five years, capped at the agreed price.
3. Complete at least 40 targeted approaches, 10 owner conversations, four NDAs/data rooms, two technical screens, and one signed option.
4. Spend up to 12,000 PLN on legal/IP/contract review and up to 8,000 PLN on engineer-led technical and safety review only after a credible data room exists.
5. Use seller-authorised installed-base outreach. Require three unrelated customers, at least 25,000 PLN prepaid, and one delivered order with at least 45% contribution before closing.

An unknown founder can plausibly win because the target asset is too small for conventional buyout funds, the seller receives ongoing income and reputation continuity, and Afterline does not ask the owner to finance a speculative startup. This is a hypothesis until a signed option exists. Proof before serious capital is the option, rights schedule, installed-base sample, seller co-signed outreach, prepayments, first delivery, contribution calculation, and signed earn-out formula.

### Unit Economics & Value Capture

A mature line with 300 active machines could produce 2.2–4.0 million PLN annual revenue through 40–70 continuity plans, parts/orders from 20–35% of the base, and 10–20 retrofits. Target blended gross margin is 58–65%; target operating margin after technicians, warranty reserve, and central overhead is 18–27%. These figures are hypotheses.

The line is rejected if delivered contribution is below 35%, plan renewal is below 75%, acquisition-cash payback exceeds 30 months, or founder/technician hours still scale approximately one-for-one with revenue after month 12. Seller earn-outs and customer prepayment protect cash conversion; no inventory is bought merely because it may someday be needed.

### Distribution Moat

The first customer channel is transferred rather than invented: the seller is contractually authorised to notify existing machine owners, and Afterline owns the subsequent continuity relationship. The first ten customers must come from that installed base. The first hundred come from three to five acquired lines, exact machine-model/part-number search pages, used-machinery dealers and refurbishers, and cross-sell to plants owning several acquired lines.

The strongest copy risk is a better-capitalised industrial-service consolidator outbidding Afterline for future sellers. It cannot copy an already exclusive line in one vendor contract. Afterline must win later lines through small-deal speed, standard rights diligence, seller references, revenue-share alignment, and shared technicians/suppliers that raise what it can pay without destroying returns.

## Competitive Advantage And Expansion Path

1. Become the only authorised custodian for one machine family.
2. Acquire adjacent product lines sharing technicians, suppliers, parts processes, or customer factories.
3. Build a cross-line compatibility graph, failure history, demand forecast, documentation standard, and contractor dispatch system.
4. Expand from Poland into Czechia, Slovakia, and the Baltics through the same owner-succession pattern.
5. Offer portfolio-level uptime plans and selective remanufacturing only after repeated demand justifies inventory and tooling.

Broad service companies can hire technicians, but they cannot lawfully recreate an assigned archive, mark, tooling set, installed-base history, and exclusive communication rights for an already acquired line. If Afterline cannot reuse technicians, customers, suppliers, or systems across the first two lines, the portfolio thesis is false.

## Local Adaptation Defense

Poland combines a dense SME manufacturing base, many founder-led firms created after 1989, local-language owner/customer trust, and lower field-service costs than Western Europe. The June 2026 EU transfer initiative may improve financing and matchmaking. The defence is not regulation. It is being early in small CEE product-line carveouts that global consolidators ignore, then using proximity, acquired rights, documented delivery, and seller references to expand.

## Why This Could Compound

Each acquired line adds installed customers, technical files, supplier history, recurring cash flow, exact-match search demand, and a seller reference. Centralised forecasting lowers inventory; common contractors improve utilisation; exact model pages lower inbound CAC; standard contracts shorten diligence; retained earnings finance the next option. Documentation and product managers replace founder memory, so owner labour need not scale with revenue.

## Reinvestment And Compounding Model

During corporate employment, conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution into options, closing payments, working capital, documentation, warranty reserves, and the next line; optional founder distributions are capped at 5%. The strong case reinvests 100%. After the full-time gate, distributions can increase only after maintaining warranty reserves, debt covenants, and liquidity for two acquisition tranches. Seller earn-outs align purchase price with realised gross profit and avoid using the founder's 100,000 PLN to buy unproved goodwill.

## Reachability And 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- **Days 1–30:** define the target scorecard, identify 100 product lines, send 40 owner approaches, and interview five maintenance buyers without selling.
- **Days 31–60:** obtain 10 owner conversations, four NDAs/data rooms, and two redacted technical/economic screens.
- **Days 61–90:** sign one exclusive option for no more than 8,000 PLN; complete the rights, liability, active-installed-base, and safety schedules.
- **Days 91–135:** the seller sends the continuity notice; Afterline offers fixed prepaid plans, essential-spares orders, and one planned retrofit.
- **Days 136–180:** require three independent customers, at least 25,000 PLN prepaid, one successful delivery at 45%+ contribution, and a seller-financed closing formula.

Maximum planned pre-proof spend is 34,000 PLN. The founder does not leave employment and does not build a general platform or buy a company before this evidence exists.

## Capital Tranches And Maximum Loss

1. **0–8,000 PLN:** target list, basic site, travel, and owner interviews. Stop if 40 qualified approaches produce no two credible data-room invitations.
2. **Up to 34,000 PLN total before paid proof:** option fee, legal/IP review, engineer, insurance consultation, and first-fulfilment preparation. No acquisition closing.
3. **Up to 65,000 PLN after paid proof:** only after three prepayments, successful initial delivery, and at least 45% contribution; fund limited closing cash and ordered parts.
4. **Up to 100,000 PLN founder capital:** only after five independent customers, repeat orders or renewal signal, and positive portfolio contribution.
5. **Possible family 100,000 PLN remains excluded:** release only after two acquired lines or one line with six months of repeat demand, positive contribution, clean rights, and a documented risk-adjusted scaling case.

Maximum pre-proof loss is 34,000 PLN plus founder time. The option and closing documents must exclude uncapped inherited liabilities.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder remains employed until at least two acquired lines or equivalent contracted recurring demand produce six independent paying customers, positive contribution after contractor and warranty costs, six months of repeat or renewal evidence, a repeatable owner-sourcing signal, and at least 12 months of personal runway. One exciting acquisition never satisfies the gate.

## Failure Recovery And Reusable Assets

If the first option expires, the founder retains the target database, diligence checklist, rights schedule, installed-base interview scripts, supplier quotes, pricing tests, and a documented rejection reason. Those are reusable execution assets, not a claimed moat. Conditional customer deposits are refunded if legal rights or the delivery condition fail. No support promise is made without assigned rights and an engineer-approved fulfilment path.

## Validation Plan

The 180-day commercial test validates seller access, rights transfer, installed-base activity, prepayment, and first-order margin. Months 7–12 validate delivery, plan renewal intent, parts reorder, founder-time decoupling, and whether a second owner will sign the same structure. Before line two, obtain at least five independent customers, two repeat events, 35%+ delivered contribution, an 18% mature operating-margin path, and one second qualified seller data room. Before scaling inventory, show two reorder cycles for the same part family.

Evidence is separated from hypothesis: EU/PARP/GUS sources establish succession and industrial context; only executed options, payments, delivery records, renewals, and contribution statements establish this business.

## Kill Criteria

- No two qualified owners open a credible data room after 40 targeted approaches, or no acceptable option after 100.
- Any option requires more than 8,000 PLN before installed-base verification or more than 40,000 PLN total before customer prepayment.
- Essential IP, tooling title, service mark, contracts, or customer communication cannot be assigned or exclusively licensed cleanly.
- Fewer than three unrelated customers or less than 25,000 PLN prepaid by day 180.
- First fulfilment contribution below 45%, mature-line contribution below 35%, plan-renewal intent below 75%, or acquisition-cash payback above 30 months.
- More than 25% of expected revenue depends on safety-critical work requiring certification or authority Afterline does not own or contractually control.
- Founder or technician labour continues scaling approximately one-for-one with revenue after month 12.
- The first two lines do not share technicians, suppliers, customers, or systems, or there is no credible path to at least 12 lines, 50m PLN revenue, and retained ownership sufficient for €10m founder net worth.

## Budget And Time

Pre-proof budget: up to 34,000 PLN—8,000 PLN sourcing/travel/site, 8,000 PLN option, 12,000 PLN legal/IP/contract review, and 6,000 PLN engineer/insurance/first-fulfilment preparation. Months 7–12 may use up to the remaining 66,000 PLN of founder capital only after paid proof and positive contribution. Founder time is capped at five hours per day and normally 15–25 hours per week. The minimum people stack is the founder, one contract lawyer, one contract machinery engineer, bookkeeping/tax support, and order-paid technicians/fabricators.

## 30-Day Execution Plan

1. Write the acquisition scorecard and exclusions: non-regulated, active installed base, repeat parts/retrofits, assignable files/mark/tooling, and seller-finance willingness.
2. Build 100 targets and a machine-family/owner evidence table from public sources.
3. Interview five factory maintenance buyers about an actual orphaned machine, downtime event, spend, and acceptable prepaid continuity offer.
4. Send 40 personalised owner letters offering a product-line continuity transfer, not consultancy or a whole-company purchase.
5. Prepare a two-page option term sheet, rights schedule, installed-base data template, safety exclusion checklist, and three fixed customer offers.
6. Stop or change the target niche if the first 40 approaches yield neither two credible data-room invitations nor evidence of owner willingness.

## Yearly Execution Plan

- **Year 1:** sign one option, secure three prepayments, close one rights-clean line, deliver safely, obtain five paying customers and two repeat events, document line-level contribution, and open a second qualified seller data room.
- **Year 2:** acquire two to four additional adjacent lines only when shared technician/supplier/customer economics are visible; hire the first product-line operator; centralise documentation and parts forecasting.
- **Year 3:** reach four to six lines, launch model/part-number inbound pages, prove 75%+ plan renewal, negotiate working-capital debt against orders rather than founder guarantees, and decide whether the founder transition gate is met.
- **Years 4–6:** reach eight to twelve Polish/CEE lines, add regional technician density and portfolio uptime plans, and use retained earnings for acquisitions.
- **Years 7–11:** expand to 14+ lines and 70m PLN expected-scenario revenue while protecting 23% operating margin and at least 82% founder ownership.

## Applicable Non-Refundable Funding (optional)

No grant is required or assumed in the POC. Enterprise Europe Network and future Polish/EU business-transfer programmes may help with matchmaking or financing after paid proof, but debt guarantees and InvestEU-style finance are not non-refundable funding and are excluded from the base economics. Any later PARP/FENG grant is treated as upside only after eligibility is confirmed in writing; it never justifies closing an otherwise unproved acquisition.
