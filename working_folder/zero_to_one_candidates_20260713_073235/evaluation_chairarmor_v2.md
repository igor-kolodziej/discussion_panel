# Zero-to-One Evaluation: ChairArmor Flight Kit — Post-Research Re-evaluation

## Diagnosis

The research addendum confirms the customer pain and shows that a used example from each proposed chair family can be bought within the 12-month/100,000 PLN envelope, but it does not establish the commercial facts required by this exact candidate. There is still no count of unique eligible buyers, no evidence of concentration in the proposed chair families, no willingness-to-pay evidence near €1,100, no repeatable access to duplicate exact configurations, and no binding insurance or freedom-to-operate result. Material within-family variation and public true-protection anchors of $249 and $495 make the price and SKU-scale assumptions weaker than they appeared in v1. The repaired evidence order is disciplined, but it exposes a Reachability Gate violation: ChairArmor must build, document, classify, and make insurable most of the complete minimum product before it can responsibly accept deposits. With the authorized public-research route exhausted, the exact premium DTC, configuration-by-configuration hardware shape should be killed rather than researched indefinitely.

## Overall Score

**39/100.** Weighted points are 77.5 across 20.0 total weight; `10 × (77.5 / 20.0) = 38.75`, rounded to 39. This is an advisory evaluation score only, not a simulated-panel, working-chat, fresh-chat, confirmation, or validation score.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** Model-family chairs are publicly purchasable, and the remaining assets—measurement, CAD, test files, BOMs, owner photos, instructions, and non-exclusive supplier capacity—are obtainable through ordinary contracts and process work. Exact duplicate access is not yet secured. Three configurations do not create meaningful switching cost, network effect, exclusive rights, or transaction control; each buyer only needs one configuration, and a one-off purchase produces limited repeat data.

**Strongest copy risk:** an OEM uses its exact CAD, order-option data, installed base, dealer fitment, warranty relationship, and customer checkout to release a branded travel kit for its own chairs. This is stronger than the AEGIS/Wheel Handi threat because the OEM need not reproduce a cross-brand corpus; it only needs to cover its own customers, one routine accessory-development program at a time. AEGIS's announced power phase and Wheel Handi's existing power-chair carrier are the strongest specialist copy risks. The new FTO uncertainty adds downside without improving control.

## Reachability Gate

**FAIL.** The revised sequencing improves maximum-loss control but does not satisfy the user's rule against building the complete product before paid proof.

Before the first responsible €200 deposit, the exact sequence requires:

1. completed purchase or signed access to two examples of one frozen geometry, or one whole chair plus duplicate protrusion assemblies;
2. configuration documentation and clearance mapping;
3. CAD, BOM, reversible attachment design, a functional prototype, and repeat-fit checks;
4. documented compression, impact, snag, retention, installation, removal, clearance, and no-damage testing;
5. a GPSR/MDR classification memo and technical-file skeleton;
6. an insurance submission and written coverage conditions that do not exclude intended aircraft transport; and
7. publication-quality tested evidence, exclusions, installation material, and claims boundaries.

That is most of a complete minimum one-configuration product, not a lightweight prototype or paid discovery engagement. It costs up to 28,000 PLN before acquisition starts and up to 32,000 PLN before the deposit target is known. The process remains contingent on exact access, a founder-created test basis, safe non-interference, classification, specialist aviation/product cover, and direct owner reach. Exact duplicate access under the 9,000 PLN first-stage ceiling is unavailable; the cheapest observed whole-chair examples do not establish it. Twenty deposits must then come from unrelated owners of the same frozen geometry, despite no model-concentration, audience, WTP, or CAC evidence.

The founder can retain employment and stop at 9,000, 19,000, or 28,000 PLN, so downside governance is good. A stop condition, however, proves recoverability rather than a credible paid-proof path. Deposits remain fully refundable and segregated until conversion; five paid deliveries would be strong proof, but the candidate has not shown that they can be reached inside 180 days without first resolving several uncontrolled dependencies and substantially completing the product. Further founder capital and all family capital remain correctly unavailable.

## Expansion Check

- **Beachhead:** exact frozen configuration plus near-term flight intent is precise but likely tiny; public research cannot identify a concentrated first configuration.
- **Configuration economics:** material variation means a three-family catalogue is not three SKUs. Each option envelope can require separate access, fit checks, exclusions, tests, support, and inventory.
- **Adjacency:** rail, coach, vehicle loading, and replacement modules share some design knowledge but have no established price, acquisition, or repeat economics.
- **Incumbent entry:** OEMs can cover their own model families with better geometry, distribution, trust, and warranty context; AEGIS and Wheel Handi already possess category positioning.
- **Local defense:** Polish prototyping cost is useful but easily replicated and does not offset global logistics, insurance, customer trust, or regulatory duties.
- **Compounding test:** the data asset compounds only after many paid, field-used configurations. The candidate has no advantaged mechanism for reaching that state before competitors, and one-off purchases provide weak switching cost.

## Fatal Objections

1. **Founder Wealth Gate veto:** after the bounded research, no unique-buyer count or lower bound supports the roughly 8,400 annual kits needed at the candidate's own threshold economics. Journey counts cannot substitute for buyers.
2. **Reachability Gate veto:** safe deposit-taking requires access, engineering, a functional product, repeat-fit verification, testing, classification, and insurability first—most of the minimum product and multiple sequential existential dependencies before paid proof.
3. **Willingness-to-pay veto:** no evidence supports €1,100, while the closest true-protection public anchors are $249 and $495. Loss severity is not the same as an owner-controlled self-pay budget.
4. **Beachhead veto:** model concentration is unavailable and material option-level variation is established, so the first exact configuration has no demonstrated customer density.
5. **Control-point veto:** the initial corpus remains Tier 3. OEMs hold superior source data and distribution; specialist competitors already operate in flight protection.
6. **Insurance and liability veto:** standard public policies may exclude aviation/airport or medical-device exposures, while binding specialist cover, classification, test acceptance, and recall terms are unavailable.
7. **Distribution veto:** no owned audience, permissioned community channel, dealer access, search volume, conversion, CAC, or first-100-customer path was established. The founder lacks the lived-experience trust used by current category entrants.
8. **FTO veto:** two adjacent companies claim patent-pending products, and the bounded search cannot establish claim scope or freedom to operate.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The commercially valuable insight remains that complex power-chair owners are protecting continuity of autonomy, not merely repair cost, and that the most usable protection may cover only fragile protrusions while preserving chair visibility and rollability. The evidence does not support the exact €1,100 DTC, configuration-by-configuration hardware business shape built around that insight.

## Recommendation

**kill**

Kill the exact unchanged ChairArmor business shape for this run. The prior `research further` route was followed, the bounded authorized research is exhausted, and the decisive gaps remain coupled to structural disadvantages: fragmented option-level demand, complete-product work before deposits, weak distribution, nonexclusive fit/test assets, specialist insurance uncertainty, and OEM copy advantage. Do not panel this wording and do not preserve its scores for a later cosmetic revision.
