# Zero-to-One Candidate: WooRenewal Merchant-Workflow Acquisitions

## One-Sentence Thesis

Acquire neglected but cash-flowing WooCommerce plugins only when the official WordPress update channel, installed merchant base, paid renewals, licence server, billing history, domain, brand, and IP transfer together, then consolidate operationally critical merchant workflows whose removal is costlier than renewal.

## Founder Fit

The founder can audit cohort and billing data, review Python/PHP/JavaScript with AI and contractors, automate support triage, run security QA, and allocate acquisition capital analytically. The business is global, written, low-camera, and compatible with employment. A WordPress security contractor and transaction lawyer cover narrow gaps; no sales-heavy public persona is needed.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The end customer is a small/mid-sized WooCommerce merchant using a plugin inside checkout, shipping, order, catalogue, subscription, or fulfilment operations. Removing it risks broken workflows, migration time, lost configuration, and revenue interruption. The asset seller is a plugin owner with an installed product and renewals but declining interest or support capacity. Current outcomes are neglect, retirement, abandonment, or sale of code without preserving the update/payment rail.

## Product Wedge

Acquire one plugin for no more than 30,000 PLN cash plus a seller note payable only from transferred receipts. The target must have at least 100 independent paying merchants, 12 months of billing and cohort data, at least 75% logo renewal or an evidenced equivalent retention metric, less than ten support hours per month, organic WordPress/search/direct acquisition, no personal-audience dependency, and no regulated/AI/API/platform dependency outside WordPress/WooCommerce.

Escrow releases only after official ownership/update transfer, repository and contractor-IP delivery, domain/brand, licence server, customer contracts/consents, support history, and billing/payout control. The proof event is the first unrelated new licence or automatic renewal settled to the founder entity.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

GPL source is not the scarce asset. The scarce asset is the trusted update path to already installed merchants plus the renewal rail and configuration switching cost. Small developers and buyers often price code while underpricing distribution, support history, and installed workflow. A disciplined buyer can buy only the distribution/payment bundle, centralise security/support, and cross-sell adjacent workflow modules.

## Underused Momentum

WordPress formally supports plugin ownership transfer; Freemius explicitly recommends sale/adoption when developers retire products. Public listings show vetted plugins with 10,000+ installs and organic recurring income, and a sold verified plugin business with renewal-led revenue and limited weekly support. These are ecosystem/transaction precedents, not proof of a specific target under the price cap.

## Beachhead Market

One operational WooCommerce workflow family for stores with 1–20 staff—post-purchase order routing, fulfilment handoff, returns authorisation, checkout controls, or catalogue operations—where the plugin stores configuration and receives recurring updates. Exclude tax/compliance, payments custody, health, crypto, generative-AI wrappers, one-off themes, and products whose main value is the seller's support labour.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Acquire at no more than 2.0× verified trailing owner earnings or 2.5× verified annual recurring gross profit, with at least 50% of consideration paid from future receipts when the target is below 25,000 PLN ARR. Maintain annual licences of 300–1,500 PLN per merchant and add multi-plugin bundles only after renewal is preserved. Target 88–94% gross margin and 30–45% operating margin after support, security, platform, legal, and acquisition amortisation. Kill if fully loaded support exceeds 15% of revenue, annual logo renewal falls below 75%, or cash payback exceeds 30 months.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

The first ten are transferred renewals or existing merchants buying an added seat/site; they are not founder-acquired leads. The first hundred are the existing installed base. Growth then comes from the official directory/update surface, documentation SEO, merchant referrals, and cross-sell between acquired plugins serving the same workflow. No acquisition is approved when customers came mainly from the seller's personal audience or paid ads.

## Primary Control Point

Tier-1 target asset: official WordPress.org ownership and commit/update authority; installed base; paid licences/subscriptions; licence/update server; billing and payout rights; domain/brand; repository and complete contractor IP; support history; and lawful customer data/consents. Transfer must be simultaneous through escrow. Source code alone is Tier 3 and worthless.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Create acquisition accounts and screen 150 marketplace/off-market targets from WordPress retirement/adoption signals, developer communities, Flippa/Acquire, and direct outreach.
2. Require read-only WordPress, billing, analytics, helpdesk, repository, and licence-server evidence before an LOI.
3. Spend <=5,000 PLN on sourcing/accounts, <=7,000 PLN on security/code/privacy/legal diligence, and <=28,000 PLN cash at close.
4. Make any remaining price a 24-month note paid only from transferred renewals; escrow releases after every asset and official owner/update transfer completes.
5. Receive one unrelated renewal/new licence within 180 days. Preserve the product before adding features.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: marketplaces and retirement channels explicitly exist; the founder brings immediate cash, a narrow transition checklist, and ongoing customer support. The first acquisition filter targets assets too small for software private equity. Proof before more capital is ownership transfer plus cash receipt, not seller claims.

## Distribution Advantage

The acquired installed base and official update surface solve cold start. Centralised security, support, billing, documentation, and cross-sell lower costs across later assets. The strongest copy risk is a GPL fork or a larger plugin vendor replicating features; it cannot copy installed configurations, reviews, owner/update authority, renewal history, or existing merchant trust in one contract.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. Preserve one acquired plugin and verify renewal/support economics for six months.
2. Acquire a second plugin serving the same merchant workflow and prove at least 10% cross-sell or shared-support savings.
3. Consolidate into a trusted merchant-operations suite without forcing migration.
4. Expand from WooCommerce into adjacent WordPress workflows only after one family reaches 5,000 paying merchants.
5. Use retained earnings and receipts-capped seller notes rather than venture capital for later acquisitions.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

The company is global from Poland. The local advantage is cost-efficient technical/security operations, EU privacy discipline, and founder analytics, not customer geography. Poland is not claimed as a moat; the moat is acquired update/payment distribution and merchant workflow switching cost.

## Compounding Advantage

Every acquisition adds installed merchants, recurring cash, update permission, support cases, compatibility knowledge, keywords, and cross-sell. Shared QA/security/support increases value and lowers failure risk. The portfolio compounds only when merchants or support infrastructure overlap; unrelated plugins are prohibited.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder remains employed; optional distributions are <=5%. Strong reinvests 100%. Cash funds code/security diligence, seller notes, support automation, and related assets. No unrelated acquisitions and no feature-growth spend before retention is stable.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: create acquisition scorecard, inspect 50 listings/retirement signals, and contact 30 owners. Days 31–75: obtain five read-only data rooms and sign one LOI. Days 76–120: verify 12-month receipts/cohorts, code/IP/security, support burden, and organic acquisition. Days 121–160: close with <=28,000 PLN cash and official transfers. By day 180: receive one independent renewal/new licence to the founder entity. Total pre-proof founder risk <=40,000 PLN; work remains part-time and no product build is required.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- 5,000 PLN sourcing and accounts.
- Up to 12,000 PLN total only after a read-only data room, for diligence and legal work.
- Up to 40,000 PLN total only after verified cohorts and escrow-ready transfers; cash at close <=28,000 PLN.
- Remaining 60,000 PLN founder capital only after ownership transfer, first receipt, 90-day stable support/security, and positive contribution.
- Family capital excluded until two assets, six months of renewal data, shared distribution/support evidence, and positive portfolio contribution.

## Critical Dependency Map

Official ownership transfer, update authority, clean contractor IP, billing/account transfer, subscriber consent/contracts, code security, organic acquisition, renewal, and low support burden must all be verified before escrow release. Missing one essential transfer kills the deal; no informal seller assistance substitutes.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

At least two acquired assets, 500 independent paying merchants, six months of post-transfer renewals, positive contribution after support/security, one repeatable acquisition source, and 12 months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

A failed target leaves the diligence checklist, target database, security scripts, contract schedules, valuation model, and rejection evidence. Escrow prevents purchase-price loss when transfers fail. No user is forced to migrate because acquisitions preserve the existing update path.

## Critical Evidence

Official owner-transfer documentation; Freemius retirement/adoption guidance; vetted and sold plugin-business listings showing installed bases, organic renewals, limited weekly support, and high margins. Seller-reported listings are precedent, not target verification.

## Critical Hypotheses

A qualifying asset can be bought with <=28,000 PLN cash plus receipts-capped note; official transfer and billing will complete; renewal survives the owner change; GPL forks do not overwhelm the acquired distribution; related assets cross-sell and share support.

## Kill Criteria

No five read-only data rooms after 100 qualified owner contacts; no qualifying signed LOI by day 90; target cash requirement >28,000 PLN; any missing owner/update/billing/IP transfer; first post-transfer receipt absent by day 180; renewal <75%; support >15% revenue; critical vulnerability not patched within 14 days; customer acquisition mainly seller audience/paid ads; second asset has no shared merchant/support advantage.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Founder 15–25 hours/week; contract WordPress security engineer 4,000–7,000 PLN; transaction/privacy lawyer 3,000–5,000 PLN; support contractor only after cash flow. Pre-proof <=40,000 PLN; first-year founder capital <=100,000 PLN.
