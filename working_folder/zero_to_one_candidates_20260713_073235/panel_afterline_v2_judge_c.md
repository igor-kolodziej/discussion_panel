## Diagnosis

Afterline contains a strong structural insight: a prepaid transition mandate could turn a retiring seller from an unpaid diligence dependency into the first customer while creating contracted access to technical truth and the installed base. Once the specified mandate is executed, the rights bundle is a genuine deal-level control point, and the candidate has unusually disciplined capital gates, liability exclusions, and kill criteria. The central problem is that the commercial inversion is still only a hypothesis: an owner with a valuable line may expect to be paid, while an owner willing to pay an unknown prospective acquirer may disproportionately own a weak, incomplete, or liability-heavy line. The plan is therefore testable and worth disciplined research, but it is not yet strong enough to establish repeatable seller acquisition, attractive first-mandate economics, or a credible conservative path to more than EUR 10 million of founder wealth.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Candidate-supported facts or commitments

- The proposed mandate is priced at 12,000–20,000 PLN and requires prepayment, archive custody, temporary commercial-use rights, seller-authorised installed-base communication, exclusivity, and a call option.
- Founder cash is capped at 18,000 PLN before a paid seller mandate and 40,000 PLN before installed-base proof.
- Acquisition closing is gated on three unrelated machine-owner commitments totalling at least 25,000 PLN and one delivery at at least 45% contribution margin.
- The candidate provides explicit line-selection rules, liability exclusions, capital tranches, return provisions, and kill criteria.
- Founder time is limited to 15–25 hours per week, supported by a transaction/IP lawyer, a machinery engineer, and order-paid technicians.

### Hypotheses, not evidence

- Retiring or discontinuing OEM owners will pay 12,000–20,000 PLN to a previously unknown prospective acquirer and grant the full exclusivity-and-rights package.
- Eighty qualified approaches can produce one prepaid mandate within 120 days.
- A first mandate can deliver at 55%+ contribution after legal, engineering, founder, travel, insurance, and archive-verification costs.
- Thirty active machines and three customer commitments can be verified and monetised within the remaining 60 days.
- A transferred line can sustain 58–65% gross margin, 20–28% operating margin after earn-outs, at least 75% plan renewal, and less than 30-month acquisition-cash payback.
- Multiple acquired lines will share enough technicians, suppliers, customers, and systems to compound rather than create a portfolio of bespoke operational burdens.

### Unverified external claims

The candidate's claims about 2026 European Commission business-transfer guidance, European family-business prevalence, and EU repair/ecodesign momentum were not independently verified for this evaluation. Even if accurate, they support timing more than seller willingness to prepay or line-level profitability.

### Consequential missing evidence

- No seller has demonstrated budget, signed a conditional data-room invitation, or accepted the mandate terms.
- No line-level historical service ledger, installed-base activity sample, acquisition consideration schedule, warranty reserve, or delivered contribution statement is shown.
- No evidence establishes the density of 100 genuinely qualified dated-exit targets within one chosen vertical and the stated regions.
- The wealth model does not show a yearly acquisition cash-flow bridge explaining how 8–30m PLN of cumulative capital and repeated purchase obligations are financed while preserving 70–88% founder ownership.

## Control Point

**Tier 1, conditional on execution of the mandate exactly as specified.**

An executed contract that provides prepaid exclusivity, archive custody, temporary commercial-use rights, installed-base communication authority, and an option over scheduled assets meets the Tier-1 definition because it directly controls scarce rights, data, workflow, and distribution access. The control point is deal-specific rather than market-wide: it protects a signed line, not the supply of future mandates or all aftermarket spending. If any essential right is revocable, non-exclusive, unowned by the seller, non-transferable, or withheld, the candidate correctly collapses to Tier 3.

### Strongest copy risk

A better-capitalised machinery consolidator, service group, or vertical OEM can offer the same contractual package, waive the seller-paid fee, pay more upfront, and use existing engineering credibility to win future sellers. It cannot copy an already executed exclusive mandate, but it can copy the acquisition process with one tailored contract and superior balance-sheet reassurance. The proposed vertical data and operating synergies become a harder-to-copy advantage only after several rights-clean lines actually share assets and customers.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.**

The candidate includes the required scenario categories, makes liabilities explicit, and avoids obvious double-counting in the stated formulas. However, the supposed conservative case reaches only 47.24m PLN against a 43m PLN threshold, so a modest reduction in the 4.5x multiple, 20% operating margin, 88% ownership, or 5m PLN of non-retained distributions removes the result. More importantly, it assumes 15 acquired lines, 60m PLN revenue, 12m PLN operating profit, and only 6m PLN of terminal obligations without a yearly cash-flow bridge from 100,000 PLN of founder capital to the stated 8m PLN of cumulative equity/working capital and all acquisition consideration.

The model also assumes portfolio-level margins and synergies before one line has demonstrated service revenue, warranty cost, technician intensity, renewal, or acquisition payback. Fifteen small machinery lines can just as easily create fifteen sets of bespoke parts, safety knowledge, urgent customer obligations, and key-person dependencies. The expected and strong cases increase line count, margins, pace, and valuation multiples rather than repairing the missing capital and operating bridge. A path to EUR 10m is conceivable, but the candidate as written does not yet make it conservative or credible enough for the hard gate.

## Reachability Gate

**PASS, narrowly.**

The requested paid proof is a 12,000–20,000 PLN prepaid exclusive mandate by day 120. The plan limits founder spending to 18,000 PLN before that proof, requires no acquisition close or full platform, retains employment, and uses a bounded lawyer/engineer-supported deliverable. This satisfies the formal gate better than an unpaid option or speculative acquisition would.

The pass is narrow because the proof requires one owner to do three difficult things simultaneously: trust an unknown founder, pay the prospective buyer, and grant broad exclusivity/data/use rights. Target identification, owner meetings, archive review, site verification, and machinery-customer calls may also require daytime availability inconsistent with a full-time job unless specialists genuinely absorb the work. The 55% mandate-contribution requirement conflicts with the stated allowance of up to 12,000 PLN of lawyer/engineer review: at a 12,000 PLN fee, external cost must be no more than 5,400 PLN before any other allocated delivery cost; even at 20,000 PLN it must be no more than 9,000 PLN. The gate passes because this conflict can be falsified before further capital is released, not because the economics have been proved.

## Expansion Check

- **Beachhead:** The selection logic is good, but the candidate has not yet selected and evidenced one machinery vertical with enough qualified sellers, sufficient installed-base value, and manageable liability patterns.
- **Adjacency:** Expansion through lines sharing technicians, suppliers, customers, and documentation is coherent. Requiring two shared operating assets is a useful discipline, though it may be too weak to guarantee genuine economies of scope.
- **Competitor entry:** Existing consolidators and OEM/service firms can copy future mandate terms and may have more trust, engineers, insurance history, and acquisition capital. Afterline's defence becomes meaningful only after exclusive contracts and accumulated vertical assets exist.
- **Local advantage:** Poland/CEE can offer overlooked subscale transitions, lower service costs, and language proximity. These improve execution but do not compensate for weak seller economics or unsafe inherited obligations.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is the **prepaid, exclusive transition mandate as a capital-light control instrument**: no substantive diligence or customer fulfilment begins until the seller pays and transfers custody, use rights, installed-base access, exclusivity, and a defined option. This is the part worth preserving through validation because it simultaneously tests budget, trust, rights quality, and control without risking acquisition capital.

## Recommendation

**research further**

Run only the capped seller-budget and full-rights mandate test already specified. Do not treat owner interest, free data-room access, advisory revenue without exclusivity, or a nonbinding promise as proof. The current candidate should not be classified as `pursue` unless the Founder Wealth Gate is later supported by real line-level acquisition, margin, liability, renewal, and financing evidence.
