# Round 9 — Transferable Renewal, Commission, and Administrator Books

**Research timestamp:** 2026-07-13 18:57 CEST  
**Role boundary:** candidate generation and research only. No scoring, advisory evaluation, pivoting, panel simulation, chatbot contact, or validation was performed.

## Search thesis and screens

This pass looked for a single legal entity, appointment book, group certificate, accreditation, or commission book that an unknown founder could control first and then grow organically. It excludes app/plugin/SaaS roll-ups, serial micro-acquisitions, ordinary client-list purchases, service routes, generic consulting, and every family blacklisted in `01_overlap_and_rejections.md`.

A direction was retained only while it could plausibly satisfy all of these at once:

- a direct contractual payer that controls the paid event;
- an owned or contracted resource under 100,000 PLN within 12 months;
- paid evidence within 180 days and no more than 40,000 PLN at risk;
- a bottom-up reachable population rather than a top-down market percentage;
- a conservative founder-wealth path with meaningful headroom over €10 million;
- no requirement for several speculative partners, a new technical breakthrough, or serial site approvals.

The six directions below are the non-padded set that survived long enough to justify source work. Five are closed by official facts. One is a conditional research reserve, not yet a finalist.

## Direction 1 — `RenewalCo PL`: share-acquire one registered commercial-insurance agency

### Commercial insight

Small and mid-sized contractors do not primarily want another annual price comparison. They want one party to preserve continuity as their equipment, subcontracting, cyber exposure, warranty obligations, and contract requirements change. A share acquisition of an existing legal-person agent could begin with an already paid renewal calendar and carrier appointments, while the founder adds a narrow risk-data and renewal workflow for renewable-energy and safety-critical contractors.

This is materially different from a generic greenfield brokerage only if the acquired asset is the **operating legal entity plus carrier agreements, powers, lawful servicing rights, renewal records, qualified people, and current commission receipts**. A customer spreadsheet alone is worthless.

### Resource and legal path

- KNF states that an insurance agent is an entrepreneur operating under an agency agreement with an insurer and entered in the register. For a legal-person agent, at least half of the management board must meet the statutory conditions; individuals performing agency activities must also be registered. Insurers conduct the required exams and grant the written powers used by the agent.
- The public register records shareholders holding at least 10%, board members, insurers represented, and powers. This supports a share-deal path in which the legal entity remains the registered agent while ownership and board data are updated.
- **Critical legal inference, not yet evidence:** preserving the legal entity may preserve agreements, but carrier change-of-control, termination, data-use, and commission clauses can override that. Written insurer confirmation is therefore a condition precedent, not an assumption.

The only acceptable first transaction is an option or majority-share purchase with at most 40,000 PLN paid at closing, the balance as a seller note contingent on retained commissions, the seller or another qualified operator retained during transition, and insurer confirmation obtained before closing. Total founder cash committed to secure and prove control must remain below 100,000 PLN.

### Direct payer, denominator, and paid proof

- Direct contractual payers are the insurers that owe commission under the acquired agency agreements; insured SMEs control renewal and retention behavior.
- KNF counted **28,365 registered agents** at year-end 2024, evidence of a fragmented acquisition-sourcing population rather than proof of an available target.
- Statistics Poland counted **2.3745 million non-financial enterprises** in 2024: 2.0% small, 0.6% medium, and 0.2% large. That implies about **66,500 enterprises with at least 10 employed people**, a transparent first denominator for commercial lines. It is not yet a bottom-up list of attractive contractor accounts.
- Paid proof is the first post-close commission remittance plus retention of at least 85% of the acquired commission run-rate over one renewal window. Before that, spend is limited to legal/financial diligence, carrier calls, and a refundable or creditable share option.

### Conservative founder-wealth path test

The following is a hypothesis boundary, not a forecast. At 1,800 client firms (2.7% of the 66,500-firm denominator), 220,000 PLN average annual premium, 12% commission, 32% operating margin, 75% founder ownership, and a 6x operating-profit value, founder equity would be about 68.4 million PLN (roughly €15.9 million at 4.3 PLN/€). Reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable business cash while employed; take no more than 5% distributions. A 400-account milestone by year 5 and 1,000 by year 8 would have to precede the 1,800-account year-11/12 case.

The arithmetic has headroom, and 1,800 accounts is bottom-up legible. The economic assumptions do **not** yet have support: average premium, commission rate, retention, staff load, acquisition cost, and 32% operating margin must be verified from one actual target. Broad market size cannot substitute for those records.

### Why it is not finalist-ready

The control point is currently hypothetical. There is no named seller, carrier consent, commission statement, persistency cohort, or proof that a sub-100,000 PLN founder-cash structure is available. Once the book is secured, broad agents can copy a contractor specialization; the proposed claims/renewal dataset does not become defensible until it contains enough proprietary loss and placement history. The direction should advance only after the disconfirming acquisition search below.

### First disconfirming test

Within 30 days, contact 100 legal-person agents from the public register and 10 specialist insurance accountants/M&A advisers. Seek one signed, non-binding term sheet containing: current recurring commission by client and carrier; 24-month persistency; loss of revenue on producer departure; carrier agreement and change-of-control clauses; lawful customer-data basis; qualified transition operator; liabilities/complaints; and no more than 40,000 PLN upfront with the remaining price contingent on retained commission. Obtain written continuity responses from the two largest relevant carriers. No compliant term sheet means close the direction.

### Research decision

**Conditional research reserve; the only direction in this pass credible enough for a gated finalist draft if the named-asset test succeeds.** Do not draft it as a finalist from market arithmetic alone.

## Direction 2 — FSC group Chain-of-Custody certificate and member-renewal book

### Commercial insight and operative control

Small wood, furniture, packaging, printing, and trading firms can lose orders when buyers demand FSC claims, but an individual certificate is disproportionately costly and administratively difficult. Under FSC rules, two or more qualifying independent legal entities in the same country can operate under one group CoC certificate. The central office manages the certificate and internal monitoring; group members do not hold individual certificates. The group entity therefore holds a real operative resource rather than merely advising customers.

### Denominator, cold start, and economics

- Poland had 2,509 CoC certificates in September 2022; the EU furniture sector alone had 192,392 companies in 2023. These figures show a real population but do not identify the still-uncertified, eligible, order-blocked Polish firms.
- A group can begin with two qualifying firms. Ten member deposits could fund procedures and the certification-body audit within the pre-proof cap, subject to a real quote.
- Official FSC regional guidance caps a group at 500 companies. A UK group offer shows a mature benchmark of £1,295-£1,395 in year one and £395-£445 thereafter, including audit and licence fees.

At an unsupported €700 annual revenue per member, 35% operating margin, 75% founder ownership and 6x value, more than 9,000 active members are needed for €10 million founder equity. That implies at least 19 full group certificates even before churn and country restrictions. It lacks real conservative headroom.

### Closure facts

This is not an overlooked Polish gap. CertWood already advertises Poland's largest FSC producer group, and Cert Group System also markets rapid admission, annual training, documentation and internal audits. The certificate is vulnerable to suspension from member breaches, while each country and group still requires administration. The operational control is real, but the revenue density and competitive gap are not.

### Research decision

Rejected: existing Polish group managers already offer the exact bundle, the per-member price ceiling is visible, and wealth requires many group certificates rather than compounding from one book.

## Direction 3 — GLOBALG.A.P. Option 2 producer-group administrator

### Commercial insight and operative control

Small growers may need GLOBALG.A.P. certification to reach retail/export channels but cannot justify individual QMS overhead. Under current rules, an Option 2 producer group with at least three producers operates one QMS; the group legal entity is the only certificate holder and contracts with members. That appears at first to create a valuable membership and renewal book.

### Official boundary that changes the business shape

The group legal entity must have the legal right to carry out agricultural production and/or trading, represent members, and accept legal responsibility for the registered production. Members are not legal certificate holders and cannot market product under their own names with reference to the group certificate. The asset is therefore not a passive administrator book: it is bound to product control, member audits, and the group's market-facing role.

GLOBALG.A.P.'s 2024 integrity report counted **4,191 producers and 2,313 certificates in Poland**. Poland also has 1.335 million agricultural holdings, but that total is not a valid addressable denominator; most do not need this certificate or supply the relevant retail/export channels.

### Cold start and wealth boundary

Three growers, a QMS, and a buyer-backed order could theoretically fit under 40,000 PLN if fees are prepaid. In practice the first paid proof requires simultaneous grower contracts, compliant production, certification-body acceptance, and a route to market. The founder does not control agriculture, trading demand, or the internal-audit capability. A hypothetical 15,000 members at €700 annual revenue and 30% operating margin could create wealth-scale equity, but that is 3.6 times Poland's entire current certified-producer population and would require multi-country operational groups. It is not a conservative path.

### Research decision

Rejected: the legal certificate is a genuine control point, but it brings product-flow and producer responsibility that an unknown part-time founder cannot prove with one direct payer under the cap. The counted Polish certified base does not support the wealth case.

## Direction 4 — accredited proficiency-testing program and laboratory-renewal book

### Commercial insight and operative control

Accredited laboratories need recurring proficiency-testing participation to demonstrate competence. An ISO/IEC 17043-accredited provider can own the program design, historical peer-performance data, participant renewal book, and accredited scope. A narrow mail-in round could, in principle, expand across Europe without local service sites.

### Evidence and proof boundary

- The European Commission's JRC says accredited laboratories are required to participate in proficiency tests to demonstrate competence.
- PCA maintains a current list of Polish providers accredited to ISO/IEC 17043. JRC itself operates multiple programs and the European market also includes national reference laboratories and commercial providers.
- A share purchase of an existing accredited legal entity could preserve the program and recurring participants only if PCA accepts the ownership/personnel change and the seller transfers the technical team, methods, reference materials, and liability history. No such target or price has been found.

Launching an unaccredited round does not prove demand for a round that counts toward an accredited laboratory's obligations. Acquiring or reproducing homogeneity/stability studies, reference values, sample production, expert staff, insurance and accreditation under 100,000 PLN is unsupported. The research did not produce a counted, reachable lab population for one underserved analyte, so even a hypothetical 7,500-lab recurring book at €1,000 annually would be a top-down assertion and only narrowly clears the wealth value at ordinary margins.

### Research decision

Rejected for this run: recurring need and data compounding are attractive, but the accredited resource is neither named nor shown transferable under the cash cap, and paid evidence before accreditation would not validate the real product.

## Direction 5 — REACH Only Representative appointment book

### Commercial insight and operative control

Non-EEA chemical manufacturers can appoint one EEA legal entity to assume importer obligations under REACH, allowing EU importers to act as downstream users and preserving the manufacturer's confidential substance information. The appointment is a real recurring legal role, and the non-EEA manufacturer is the direct payer.

### Official constraints

ECHA requires the Only Representative to be physically established in the EEA, possess sufficient practical chemical knowledge, register substances, maintain importer/tonnage inventories, update safety information, and keep each non-EU manufacturer in a separate registration/account structure. ECHA explicitly states that manufacturers can change their Only Representative; legal-entity and OR changes are supported in REACH-IT.

### Reach and wealth boundary

A paid scoping retainer could fit under 40,000 PLN, but it would validate consulting, not the founder's capacity to accept the legal registration obligations. A qualified chemical-regulatory lead, professional insurance, dossier access and registration/data-sharing costs remain uncontrolled. At a hypothetical €4,000 recurring revenue per manufacturer, 30% operating margin, 75% ownership and 6x value, roughly 1,850 manufacturer mandates are needed just to reach €10 million founder equity. Each mandate still has separate records, technical exposure and switching rights; no official bottom-up reachable manufacturer count was found.

### Research decision

Rejected: this is a high-liability compliance appointment book with customer-controlled switching, not a durable proprietary control point. The first payment does not prove safe scalable delivery or the wealth denominator.

## Direction 6 — regulated-product Responsible/Authorised Representative book

Two adjacent forms were checked because both create recurring mandate books. Neither survives as a separate finalist.

### A. MDR/IVDR medical-device Authorised Representative

From 28 May 2026, the EUDAMED actor module is mandatory. A non-EU manufacturer must have an active EU Authorised Representative and submit a mandate summary. This creates sharp timing and a direct manufacturer payer.

The same official rules make the book unsuitable for this founder: the representative must continuously have a qualified person responsible for regulatory compliance, verify documentation, cooperate with authorities and vigilance actions, and may be jointly and severally liable for defective devices when the non-EU manufacturer fails its obligations. Manufacturers can change representative through an agreement between outgoing and incoming parties. A first retainer would not establish that technical-document risk, insurance and potential product liability fit the 100,000 PLN control budget. A hypothetical 1,500 manufacturers at €5,000 annually, 30% operating margin, 75% ownership and 6x value only just reaches the founder-wealth threshold and carries no liability-adjusted headroom.

**Decision:** rejected; mandatory demand is real, but qualification, liability, changeability and trust dominate the mandate.

### B. cosmetics Responsible Person

EU cosmetics rules require each product placed on the EU market to have an EU-established Responsible Person. The Responsible Person ensures compliance, maintains the Product Information File for ten years after the last batch, submits CPNP notifications, handles serious undesirable effects and cooperates with authorities. A non-EU manufacturer or importer may designate the role by written mandate.

Unlike MDR, no comparable PRRC qualification was found, so a first paid mandate could fit the reachability cap by subcontracting expert safety assessment. But the responsible person is legally attached to product compliance, safety files, labelling and corrective action, while the designation remains contractual. CPNP is free and EU-wide; that lowers customer friction but also removes a proprietary filing rail. No public bottom-up count of reachable non-EU brand owners, mandate pricing, retention or claim-adjusted margin was found. A hypothetical 3,700 brands at €2,000 annual revenue, 30% margin, 75% ownership and 6x value reaches only about €10 million, without headroom for liability or staff.

**Decision:** rejected; this is a generic, high-trust compliance mandate with weak switching protection and an uncounted wealth population.

### 3. Bottom-up reachable contractor population

The earlier 66,500-enterprise figure is a broad Statistics Poland size denominator. It is not a reachable list of safety-critical or renewable contractors and should not be used in the wealth model.

Two live UDT registers provide a real bottom-up starting list:

- The F-gas enterprise register returned **19,612 certificate records**, including **17,963 with status “Wydany”** and **17,881 unique NIPs** in that status. Only **716 unique issued records included a KRS number**; absence of KRS does not prove a microbusiness, but it shows that the list is dominated by sole traders or other non-KRS entities rather than an evidenced 10+ employee cohort.
- The OZE installer register returned **10,006 individual certificates**, of which **3,900 were valid**. Among valid records, 2,673 named a workplace and represented **2,005 unique workplace strings**. The register identifies certified people, not company size, insurance buyer, budget, written premium, renewal date or decision-maker.

Even before de-duplicating firms that appear in both registers, the observable ceiling is **19,886 unique F-gas NIPs plus OZE workplace strings**, not 66,500 qualified accounts. The 1,800-client wealth case therefore requires at least **9.1%** of this raw, partially overlapping and mostly unqualified list. The true required share is higher after removing overlap, inactive/non-commercial records, micro-firms, employees listed at the same firm, firms without the target insurance spend, and unreachable decision-makers.

The registers make list building possible; they do not validate customer economics. A proper reachable cohort would require enriching every entity with employment/turnover, fleet/equipment, public-contract and project exposure, insurer/renewal date, buyer identity, and an evidenced annual premium band. That enrichment has not produced even a 100-company qualified sample, so the 1,800-account case is not bottom-up supported.
