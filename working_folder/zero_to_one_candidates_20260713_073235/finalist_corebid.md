# Zero-to-One Candidate: CoreBid PL

Candidate generation only — not validated. All prices, margins, conversion rates, acceptance rates, capital requirements, valuation multiples, and timelines below are hypotheses unless tied to a cited source.

## One-Sentence Thesis

CoreBid PL buys high-value automotive OE cores from Polish repair workshops only after two remanufacturers or core brokers have supplied written SKU-specific bids and acceptance rules, then compounds owned inventory turns, workshop pickup rights, and realised buyer-acceptance data into a demand-backed CEE core-supply position.

## Founder Fit

- The founder's comparative advantage is not automotive repair. It is building a disciplined pricing, classification, loss-control, and route-allocation system from messy part numbers, photos, condition signals, buyer rules, realised prices, and inventory turns.
- The first 180 days can be run alongside corporate employment in 15–20 focused hours per week. Pickup is consolidated weekly; physical inspection is paid per batch; the founder handles buyer contracting, supplier acquisition, pricing, and analysis.
- AI can assist casting-number OCR, photo triage, duplicate detection, price-book matching, rejection prediction, and route planning, but the business is not an AI wrapper. It buys title to physical inventory and earns an inventory spread.
- The founder does not need a public persona, privileged introduction, or large engineering build. Buyers already publicly solicit cores; workshops can be reached through precise written and telephone outreach.
- The founder must not pretend to be a mechanic. A contracted remanufacturing-parts specialist provides the acceptance playbook and checks the first batches.
- Reckitt time, data, vendors, and systems remain outside the venture. Any employment conflict must be cleared before trading.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

### Supply-side customer

The first supplier is an independent workshop or small repair chain in the Warsaw–Łódź–Poznań corridor that replaces eligible alternators, starters, turbochargers, steering racks, injectors, air-conditioning compressors, or brake calipers.

Its desired outcome is simple: turn an oily removed part into certain cash and free floor space without identifying every buyer program, preserving boxes for months, reconciling deposit accounts, or shipping one unit at a time.

Current workarounds include:

- returning the core through the same distributor's deposit program, when a matching remanufactured unit was bought and all timing, box, condition, and account rules are met;
- holding the part for a local collector or remanufacturer;
- selling it for scrap value despite higher remanufacturing value;
- allowing cores to accumulate until someone makes a mixed-lot offer; or
- losing part of the refund because the unit is late, mismatched, incomplete, incorrectly identified, or outside the relevant purchase account.

CoremanNet confirms that full credit commonly depends on the material number, technical criteria, and a matching remanufactured-part purchase within the program period. Its core-bank option also confirms that unmatched or over-returned cores require a separate balance mechanism. This does not prove widespread leakage in Poland; it identifies the behaviour to test. [CoremanNet FAQ](https://www.coremannet.com/frequently-asked-questions)

### Economic payer

The payer is a remanufacturer or established core broker that already needs specific OE cores and can issue a standing buy list, objective acceptance rules, and payment terms. It wants reliable, correctly identified, consolidated input rather than mixed scrap.

BORG reports more than two million remanufactured units per year, more than 13,700 part numbers, and warehouse capacity above one million cores. APRA Europe calls cores the essential input to remanufacturing and describes core dealers as an established link between supply and demand. These facts prove industrial demand and also prove that CoreBid faces sophisticated incumbents. [BORG Automotive Reman](https://borgautomotive-reman.com/what-we-do), [APRA Europe](https://www.apraeurope.org/)

## Product Wedge

The initial offer is **Guaranteed Core Pickup for a Closed Wanted List**:

1. CoreBid publishes only 30–50 eligible core families for which two downstream buyers have supplied current written bids and acceptance rules.
2. A workshop submits four required photos, the casting/OE number, vehicle identifier or fitment, and a declaration of lawful origin.
3. CoreBid returns a conditional fixed purchase price valid for seven days.
4. CoreBid buys title at consolidated weekly pickup, verifies the unit, and pays the workshop.
5. The company routes the owned core to the buyer with the highest expected realised contribution after rejection risk and freight.

The first version is a phone, web form, spreadsheet/database, buyer-rule engine, and physical batch process. There is no marketplace, workshop subscription, speculative catalog, custom warehouse, remanufacturing line, or promise to buy every removed part.

The first beachhead excludes catalytic converters, batteries, airbags, whole vehicles, and unidentified parts because theft, hazardous-goods, waste, and provenance risks would overwhelm the initial proof.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The market is not missing core-return systems. It may be missing a narrow **multi-buyer liquidity layer for cores that are valuable but inconvenient outside a workshop's matching distributor account**.

The contrarian commercial belief is:

> Workshops will accept a slightly lower theoretical price in return for a fast, certain, local purchase, while different downstream buyers will value the same eligible core differently enough that multi-buyer routing can fund that convenience and still leave at least 100 PLN of realised contribution per acquired core.

This belief is useful only if three behaviours appear together in paid evidence:

- workshops repeatedly sell eligible cores rather than wait for maximum price;
- two or more buyers provide enforceable, current price books and honour them; and
- the price dispersion exceeds identification, rejection, freight, fraud, and working-capital costs.

If those behaviours do not coexist, CoreBid is merely a low-margin collector competing with integrated remanufacturers and scrap traders.

## Beachhead Market

The first market is deliberately narrower than "automotive cores":

- geography: a weekly pickup corridor covering Warsaw, Łódź, Poznań, and towns along the connecting routes;
- suppliers: independent workshops and small chains with at least two eligible high-value removals per month;
- product families: 30–50 buyer-preapproved OE core families, initially in no more than three categories;
- buyers: at least two unrelated remanufacturers or core brokers with written bid books and payment within 30 days;
- transaction: title purchase at a conditional fixed price, not consignment and not deposit recovery on behalf of the workshop.

The niche is economically meaningful because the input-value denominator is published and individual items can carry hundreds of PLN of remanufacturing value. It is small enough to test because the first wanted list, route, and inspection playbook can fit on a few pages.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

### Revenue model

CoreBid buys title and resells owned goods. The intended accounting is gross merchandise revenue because the company acts as principal and bears price, condition, and inventory risk; a Polish accountant must confirm revenue recognition and VAT treatment before the first invoice.

### Initial weighted-core hypothesis

| Item | PLN per acquired core | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Average workshop purchase price | 350 | Hypothesis |
| Packaging, pickup, and outbound freight | 80 | Hypothesis |
| Inspection and handling | 25 | Hypothesis |
| Rejection, fraud, and price-change reserve | 45 | Hypothesis |
| Average buyer payment | 650 | Hypothesis |
| Realised contribution | 150 | Hypothesis |

The 150 PLN contribution is measured across **every acquired core**, including rejected and downgraded units. A high contribution on accepted units cannot hide losses on rejects.

### Operating thresholds

- at least 100 PLN realised contribution per acquired core after all direct costs;
- no more than 15% complete rejection by count and no more than 10% unexpected value loss by purchase cost;
- average inventory holding period below 30 days;
- buyer cash received within 30 days of accepted delivery;
- workshop acquisition cost below 900 PLN and recovered within three months;
- each active workshop supplies at least three eligible cores per month by month six of its cohort;
- no buyer exceeds 50% of gross profit after the first year and 35% after year three.

### Cash conversion

The workshop is paid at or shortly after pickup, while the downstream buyer pays after inspection. CoreBid therefore finances inventory, rejection, and receivables. This is the central capital risk. Scale requires buyer prepayment, inventory-backed credit, receivables finance, or short-duration working-capital debt after repeatable turns; margin must not be manufactured by delaying workshop payment.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

Here "customer" includes the supply-side workshop whose repeated behaviour creates the asset; the downstream buyer is the economic payer.

### First 10 workshops

1. Secure two buyer bid books before outreach.
2. Build a list of 80 independent workshops along one weekly route using public maps, directories, and repair specialisations.
3. Send each a one-page closed wanted list with exact photo requirements and fixed example prices; follow up by phone without requiring an app demo.
4. Offer the first ten workshops payment at pickup after a photo pre-check, a seven-day price validity, and no subscription or exclusivity.
5. After one paid, accepted lot, offer a 12-month first-call agreement: the workshop sends CoreBid the eligible core before selling it elsewhere, and CoreBid must quote within one business day. The workshop remains free to reject the quote.

The first ten are not counted as acquired until they complete a paid sale, and they are not counted as repeat suppliers until a second sale occurs.

### First 100 workshops

- Expand route by route, not nationally.
- Use the realised wanted list to target specialists by core category rather than buying generic garage lead lists.
- Pay a 150 PLN referral only after the referred workshop completes its second profitable sale.
- Give active suppliers transparent acceptance photos and monthly statements; do not offer software as the reason to stay.
- Add one pickup day only when the existing day's contribution covers courier/driver cost at least twice.
- Maintain at least three active downstream buyers before passing 100 suppliers.

No distributor partnership is required for the first 100. Standard storage, courier, and pallet freight are purchased as ordinary vendor services.

## Primary Control Point

The control point is not the website, classifier, or generic relationship claim. It is the combined ownership of:

1. **Contracted demand:** two or more signed, SKU-specific buyer bid books containing acceptance criteria, floor prices, validity periods, and payment terms.
2. **Owned supply:** paid-for eligible cores with documented lawful origin and chain of custody.
3. **Earned first-call rights:** 6–12 month workshop agreements obtained only after successful paid transactions.
4. **Realised acceptance graph:** item-level mapping of OE/casting number, photos, declared defect, condition, buyer rule, quoted price, actual acceptance, downgrade reason, freight, days held, and cash received.
5. **Route density:** a pickup schedule whose cost per eligible core falls as repeat suppliers cluster geographically.

At the start, none of this is controlled. By month 12, it is directly creatable within 100,000 PLN if the candidate's assumptions are true. The first buyer contracts turn speculative inventory into demand-backed inventory; the first paid workshop contracts and item histories begin the compounding layer.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

### Resource needed

The foundational resource is buyer-committed demand for a closed SKU list. Workshop supply, inventory, and data are worthless if no buyer commits to objective acceptance and price terms.

### How it is secured

1. Approach remanufacturers and core brokers already advertising purchases or core-return programs.
2. Request a normal procurement artifact, not a strategic partnership: a dated wanted list, minimum batch, condition rules, floor prices, delivery destination, inspection turnaround, and payment term.
3. Require two unrelated buyers for each initial category or exclude the category.
4. Obtain Polish legal/accounting advice on title, lawful-origin declarations, waste/product classification, VAT, cross-border shipment, and rejected-unit disposal.
5. Buy the first inventory only after items 2–4 are documented.

Premier Components publicly describes itself as a European core broker that buys and sells passenger, light-commercial, and heavy-commercial cores. This supports reachability of a buyer conversation but does not guarantee a contract. [Premier Components](https://www.premiercore.com/)

### Why an unknown founder can secure it

- The request is economically ordinary: deliver specified raw material under a standing price list.
- The founder does not ask the buyer to endorse a new compliance claim, adopt software, or recruit its customers.
- The first batch is small and buyer-defined.
- The founder's credibility comes from traceable inventory, conservative inspection, and correct batches rather than status.
- Workshops are paid sellers, not institutions being asked to approve a new standard.

### Proof before serious capital

Before more than 10,000 PLN is committed, the file must contain two bid books, buyer company verification, a legal/accounting memo, and a batch contribution model. Before more than 40,000 PLN is at risk, two independent buyer invoices must be paid and reconciled item by item.

## Distribution Advantage

CoreBid's first acquisition mechanism is a **buyer-funded wanted list delivered to the exact workshops likely to remove those parts**. It avoids broad brand marketing.

The potential distribution advantage compounds when:

- realised buyer demand narrows outreach to workshops specialising in the profitable makes and systems;
- repeat pickup density reduces logistics cost;
- first-call agreements increase the probability that eligible supply is seen before a general collector buys it;
- fast, explainable acceptance feedback creates workshop trust; and
- the best buyer for a given core is selected from actual realised outcomes rather than public list price.

This advantage is unproved. If workshops sell only on the highest spot price, or incumbent collectors already pick up just as quickly, there is no distribution moat.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

### Current competitors and likely response

- **BORG/CoremanNet:** vertically integrated remanufacturing, selection, credit, pickup, and core-bank infrastructure. It can improve local buying or prices.
- **Premier and other core brokers:** existing multi-category procurement and international buyer access.
- **Local remanufacturers and collectors:** direct relationships, technical judgment, cash buying, and low-cost routes.
- **Parts distributors:** deposit accounts tied to remanufactured-unit sales and routine return logistics.
- **Scrap and catalyst buyers:** immediate liquidity and established workshop visits, even if they sometimes underprice remanufacturing value.

CoreBid is not protected from price competition. An incumbent can copy its form, pickup offer, or list with one process change. The only credible catch-up cost is the accumulated cross-buyer acceptance graph plus dense first-call supply. If that asset does not improve realised price or reduce loss faster than incumbents react, the candidate should be killed.

### Expansion sequence

1. Dominate three high-value core categories on one Polish corridor.
2. Add adjacent Polish routes only after each route achieves positive standalone contribution.
3. Add buyer programs in Czechia, Germany, Denmark, and Spain where a Polish collection base can feed existing remanufacturing plants.
4. Add light-commercial and truck cores using the same buyer-first rule; do not assume passenger-car rules transfer.
5. Offer buyer-funded exclusive sourcing campaigns for scarce OE numbers only after the company already controls supply density.
6. Consider EV thermal-management, drive-unit, and power-electronics cores only when downstream buyers publish actual acceptance and pricing; do not use electrification as speculative TAM.

CoreBid never expands into remanufacturing without separate proof, capital, liability analysis, and a different decision.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

- Poland combines a large repairable vehicle base, 832,294 medium/heavy commercial vehicles, and established remanufacturing production. BORG operates production in Poland; Valeo describes a European remanufacturing hub in Czechowice, Poland. [ACEA](https://www.acea.auto/files/ACEA_Report_-_Vehicles_on_European_roads_2025.pdf), [Valeo](https://www.valeoservice.com/en-com/newsroom/valeo-reman-good-new-good-your-budget-good-planet)
- The April 2025 Polish study supplies a local category-value map rather than a generic global remanufacturing forecast.
- Labour and storage can begin below Western European cost while the output is transportable to nearby EU buyers.
- Polish-language workshop acquisition and local weekly pickup create an execution edge that a foreign buyer may not prioritise.

The defense is operational, not regulatory. A European incumbent can enter Poland, and a local incumbent may already have better routes. The candidate survives only if paid cohorts show supply that incumbents are not already purchasing efficiently.

## Compounding Advantage

Each successful transaction can improve four future variables:

- **pricing:** actual buyer acceptance replaces list-price guesses;
- **selection:** rejected patterns are removed before purchase;
- **distribution:** the workshop becomes a repeat first-call supplier;
- **logistics:** route density lowers cost per acquired core.

The compound is multiplicative only if data is portable across suppliers and buyer programs. If each core remains a bespoke expert judgment and each workshop needs constant manual chasing, the business will scale labour rather than advantage.

### Ownership and dilution

- The founder begins with 90% after reserving up to 10% for a genuine operations/automotive co-founder or long-term incentive pool; contractors receive cash, not default equity.
- Conservative ownership of 78% allows 12 percentage points of later dilution for key management or a small equity round.
- Expected ownership of 70% allows 20 points of later dilution.
- Strong ownership of 65% allows 25 points of later dilution.
- Inventory and receivables should primarily use secured, short-duration debt after evidence. Founder guarantees, covenants, and interest are liabilities and must be included in the net-debt deduction.

### Intermediate milestones

- **Month 6:** two buyers have paid for separate batches; at least 80 acquired cores have completed the loop; contribution is at least 100 PLN per acquired core.
- **Year 1:** 1,500 cores, about 0.9m PLN revenue, three buyers, 100 transacting workshops, and no buyer above 50% of gross profit.
- **Year 3:** 25,000 cores, about 15.5m PLN revenue at conservative pricing, 300 repeat workshops, five buyers, positive operating cash generation, and inventory days below 30.
- **Year 5:** 75,000 cores, about 46.5m PLN revenue, 700 repeat workshops, at least 5% operating margin, and two profitable pickup hubs.
- **Year 8:** 160,000 cores, about 99.2m PLN revenue, 1,500 repeat workshops, CEE buyer exposure, and no country or buyer creating an existential concentration.
- **Years 10–12:** conservative 275,000-core outcome and founder-equity threshold.

### Denominator discipline

At 620 PLN average resale, the Polish study's 1.8 billion PLN potential core value implies about 2.9 million core-value units. The conservative 275,000 units equal roughly 9.5% of that implied count. This is demanding national-leader scale, not an automatic result. The first 12 months must determine whether the report represents accessible annual flow, a theoretical installed opportunity, or a mixture. If accessible flow is less than 1.2 billion PLN or the addressable closed-list share cannot support 275,000 profitable annual cores, the conservative wealth bridge fails.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

### While the founder keeps the corporate job

- Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax business cash available for distribution; optional founder distributions are capped at 5% and are not included in the wealth calculation.
- Cash funds wanted-list inventory, rejection reserves, supplier payment speed, route expansion, data quality, and key hires.
- The strong case reinvests 100% during the expansion period.

### Capital productivity rules

- New capital is released by profitable inventory turns, not warehouse ambition.
- No owned warehouse before three months of throughput makes rented space more expensive than ownership on a risk-adjusted basis.
- No remanufacturing equipment is bought under this candidate.
- A route is added only when contracted buyer demand and supplier density support at least 2× direct route cost.
- A new category is added only with two current buyer bids and a paid blind batch.
- Debt maturity must exceed the cash-conversion cycle by at least 60 days; long-lived debt must not finance obsolete cores.

### No double counting

Retained earnings and working inventory support the operating-company value and are not also counted as personal wealth. Any future founder distributions would reduce company cash or enterprise-equity value and must be accounted for separately at that time.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

### Days 1–30: prove demand before inventory

- Contact 20 verified remanufacturers/core brokers.
- Obtain at least two dated bid books covering overlapping core families.
- Require minimum batch, acceptance photos, technical rejection criteria, price validity, delivery location, inspection time, and payment term.
- Commission a Polish legal/accounting memo on title, provenance, VAT, product-versus-waste classification, transport, rejected inventory, and cross-border sale.
- Spend ceiling: 10,000 PLN. No speculative core purchases.

### Days 31–60: build the closed loop

- Select no more than 50 core families with two-buyer coverage.
- Have a contracted automotive-parts specialist produce the photo and physical acceptance checklist.
- Create the lawful-origin declaration, purchase contract, item ID, chain-of-custody record, and buyer batch manifest.
- Recruit 20 corridor workshops and collect non-binding examples of actual removed inventory.

### Days 61–100: first paid batch

- Buy 20–35 prechecked cores with a total purchase exposure below 12,000 PLN.
- Consolidate, inspect, and sell the batch to buyer A.
- Record every downgrade, rejection, direct cost, day held, and cash receipt.
- Paid proof 1 is buyer A's cleared payment, not a workshop interview or letter of intent.

### Days 101–140: independent paid replication

- Correct the wanted list using actual rejection evidence.
- Buy a second batch from at least five workshops, including at least three not used in batch one.
- Sell to unrelated buyer B.
- Paid proof 2 is buyer B's cleared payment with at least 100 PLN realised contribution per acquired core across the batch.

### Days 141–180: repeat and control-point proof

- Obtain second transactions from at least eight workshops.
- Sign first-call agreements with at least five repeat suppliers.
- Complete a third batch without increasing original capital at risk above 40,000 PLN by recycling paid cash.
- Produce a 90-day inventory-turn, rejection, contribution, cohort, and concentration record.

### Reachability conditions

The proof passes only if:

- two unrelated buyers pay;
- total original pre-proof capital at risk never exceeds 40,000 PLN;
- the founder keeps employment and average founder time remains at or below 20 hours per week;
- realised contribution is at least 100 PLN per acquired core;
- full rejection is at most 15%;
- cash conversion is at most 30 days after buyer delivery;
- at least eight workshops transact twice; and
- no buyer contract depends on the buyer recruiting or approving the workshop.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

### Tranche 0 — 0 to 10,000 PLN

- buyer verification and travel: 2,000 PLN;
- legal/accounting memo: 5,000 PLN;
- specialist acceptance playbook: 2,000 PLN;
- simple form, domain, phone, and documents: 1,000 PLN.

Release condition for inventory: two bid books and no legal veto.

### Tranche 1 — up to 39,000 PLN total original capital at risk

- first and second batch inventory: 17,000 PLN;
- contracted inspection/handling: 3,000 PLN;
- packaging, rented storage, and labels: 2,500 PLN;
- pickup and outbound freight: 4,000 PLN;
- outreach and workshop onboarding: 1,500 PLN;
- contingency/rejection reserve: 1,000 PLN;
- Tranche 0: 10,000 PLN.

Release condition for further spend: two paid batches, positive contribution, and no legal/provenance failure.

### Tranche 2 — up to 65,000 PLN of the founder's original capital

Released only after two unrelated buyer payments, successful initial delivery, contribution above 100 PLN per acquired core, and at least five repeat workshops. It funds only wanted-list inventory, standard crates, and working-capital reserve.

### Tranche 3 — up to the full 100,000 PLN original founder capital

Released only after three profitable batches, at least two buyers, eight repeat workshops, an inventory cycle below 30 days, and a documented workshop acquisition channel. No fixed facility or custom software build is authorised.

### Family capital

The possible additional 100,000 PLN from family remains excluded until at least three buyers have paid, 20 workshops supply repeatedly, 90-day contribution is positive after founder-paid labour, and a risk-adjusted inventory expansion case is documented. If released, it finances only contracted, fast-turn inventory and rejection reserves.

### Maximum loss

Before paid proof, maximum original cash loss is 39,000 PLN. Inventory is not assumed fully recoverable; the loss model treats rejected units as potentially worth only scrap. Founder guarantees and debt are prohibited before the initial proof.

## Critical Dependency Map

| Dependency | Control status at start | 12-month control/proof | Failure response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two buyer price books | Uncontrolled | Signed bids before inventory | Kill if absent by day 30 |
| Buyer acceptance and payment | Uncontrolled counterparty | Two paid blind batches; diversify | Kill if rules are subjective or payment >45 days |
| Lawful product status, VAT, provenance | Unknown | Written Polish advice, declarations, item records | Kill affected flows; do not relabel waste as product |
| Repeat workshop supply | Uncontrolled | Paid repeat cohorts and first-call rights | Kill if fewer than 8/20 repeat by day 180 |

| Positive price dispersion | Market-dependent | Realised contribution after every cost | Kill if <100 PLN per acquired core |

| Buyer concentration | Initially high | Three buyers by year one; caps thereafter | Do not scale if one buyer controls >50% GP |

| Founder time and employment | Founder-controlled | 20-hour weekly cap and conflict clearance | Hire ops only after paid proof or stop |

The business has three existential dependencies during proof: enforceable buyer demand, repeat workshop supply, and positive realised spread. It is not permitted to replace a failure in one with optimistic scale in another.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder remains employed until all conditions hold simultaneously:

- at least five independent downstream buyers have paid during the prior six months;
- at least 50 workshops have supplied twice and no workshop supplies more than 5% of gross profit;
- trailing-six-month contribution is positive after market-rate founder labour and contracted inspection;
- inventory turns in less than 30 days and buyer receivables in less than 35 days;
- no buyer exceeds 35% of gross profit;
- workshop acquisition has a repeatable cohort payback below three months;
- there is at least 12 months of personal runway outside business inventory;
- legal, tax, provenance, storage, insurance, and rejected-unit processes have survived review; and
- the business can pay a market salary without reducing required working capital.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If the expansion thesis fails after paid proof:

- buyer-contracted eligible inventory can be sold through the standing bids; rejected inventory is conservatively liquidated as lawful scrap through an authorised route;
- the price/rejection graph remains useful for a narrower procurement operation or can be sold/licensed only if contracts and data rights permit;
- buyer bid books and workshop transaction histories may support a profitable single-category broker, but only if that narrower business independently meets the wealth gate;
- standard crates, scanners, shelving, and rented storage have low sunk cost;
- the founder retains expertise in physical-inventory pricing, fraud control, route economics, and working-capital analytics.

The company must not rescue a failed model by accumulating speculative parts, opening a remanufacturing plant, or becoming a generic workshop consultancy.

- ACEA documents a large, old EU vehicle fleet and 832,294 medium/heavy commercial vehicles in Poland. [ACEA](https://www.acea.auto/files/ACEA_Report_-_Vehicles_on_European_roads_2025.pdf)
- Premier publicly solicits passenger, light-commercial, and heavy-commercial cores, proving that independent buyer conversations are possible. [Premier Components](https://www.premiercore.com/)

## Critical Hypotheses

1. At least two unrelated buyers will give an unknown Polish founder contract-grade bid books rather than indicative quotes.
2. At least 40 of the initial 80 target workshops have cores outside an economically superior matching deposit return.
3. Eight of 20 activated workshops will transact twice within 180 days.
4. The weighted realised buyer payment is at least 650 PLN for the selected closed list.
5. Rejection plus downgrade loss stays below 15% by count and 10% by purchase value.
6. Full contribution remains above 100 PLN per acquired core after pickup, inspection, freight, storage, payment cost, fraud, and founder-paid labour.
7. Inventory cash conversion stays below 30 days without delaying workshop payment.
8. The 1.8 billion PLN figure represents enough current annual accessible transaction flow to support 275,000 profitable cores at maturity.
9. Workshops accept first-call rights because certainty and pickup outweigh the option value of spot selling.
10. Cross-buyer outcome data improves pricing enough to survive incumbent price response.
11. Polish and cross-border rules allow documented eligible cores to move as remanufacturing inputs; rejected items follow lawful waste channels.
12. Later working-capital lenders finance short, documented inventory cycles without requiring destructive founder dilution or guarantees.

## Kill Criteria

Kill the candidate or the affected category immediately if any condition occurs:

1. Two unrelated buyers do not provide dated SKU prices, objective acceptance rules, and payment terms within 30 days.
2. Counsel cannot establish a lawful, documented product/provenance/VAT path for the initial flow within the 10,000 PLN research tranche.
3. A buyer can change the price after delivery without a narrow, objective, auditable downgrade rule.
4. Either of the first two batches is unpaid 45 days after accepted delivery.
5. Across 80 acquired cores, complete rejection exceeds 15%, unexpected value loss exceeds 10% of purchase cost, or realised contribution is below 100 PLN per acquired core.
6. Fewer than eight of 20 activated workshops sell a second eligible core by day 180.
7. Average inventory holding exceeds 30 days or the business needs more than 40,000 PLN original risk before two buyer payments.
8. More than 20% of acquired units lack complete seller identity, origin declaration, serial/casting evidence, and chain-of-custody record.
9. The best workshop offer must equal the highest downstream list price, leaving no margin for rejection and logistics.
10. One incumbent can eliminate the observed spread simply by offering the same weekly pickup and price to the initial cohort, and first-call retention remains below 40%.
11. Buyer concentration cannot fall below 50% of gross profit by year one or 35% by year three.
12. Bottom-up paid data indicates fewer than 1.2 billion PLN of accessible annual Polish core flow or no credible CEE extension, making the conservative founder-wealth bridge dependent on more than roughly 10% of the accessible market.
13. The founder must work more than 20 hours per week for two consecutive months before the full-time transition gate.
14. Scaling requires speculative obsolete inventory, hazardous categories, or remanufacturing equipment rather than contracted demand.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

### First 180 days

- Original capital at risk: maximum 39,000 PLN.
- Founder time: 15–20 hours per week, with buyer work scheduled during permitted hours and no employer resources.
- Storage: rented by the shelf, cage, or small room; no lease requiring build-out.
- Transport: standard courier/pallet freight plus one consolidated local pickup day; no owned van before paid route evidence.

### Minimum people stack

- founder: buyer contracts, pricing, database, supplier acquisition, analytics, and capital control;
- contracted automotive remanufacturing-parts specialist: 6–10 hours per batch during proof;
- bookkeeper/accountant with Polish VAT and cross-border goods experience;
- lawyer for title, provenance, waste/product, terms, and liability;
- standard courier/driver and storage vendors;
- after proof only: one part-time operations/inspection coordinator.

The first 100,000 PLN buys proof and fast-turn inventory, not a team, warehouse, fleet, marketplace, or classifier project.
