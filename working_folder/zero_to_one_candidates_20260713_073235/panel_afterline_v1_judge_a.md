## 1. Independent score

**76/100**

This is a materially stronger concept than a generic industrial-service roll-up because it identifies a contractible control point, makes the first acquisition conditional on customer cash, and defines unusually disciplined kill criteria. It remains below qualification because most of the attractive economics are still hypotheses, the acquisition engine must repeat across many heterogeneous sellers, and the founder-wealth calculation does not convert enterprise value into founder net equity after debt and earn-out liabilities.

## 2. Direct diagnosis

Afterline has a real Zero-to-One-shaped wedge: buy the technical truth, seller-authorised installed-base route, mark, tooling, and history together, instead of competing as another labour seller or parts broker. A clean exclusive option followed by enforceable assignment can create a dominant position around one orphaned machine family, and the prepayment-before-closing structure is excellent downside control. The weakness is that a protected first line is not yet a protected portfolio engine: every expansion line requires another rare seller, clean rights, trustworthy records, acceptable earn-out terms, safe fulfilment, and customer demand, while cross-line synergies are asserted rather than demonstrated. The first 180-day test can validate one transaction, but it cannot by itself validate the 12–16-line scale, margins, or founder-wealth outcome.

The recent EU succession timing signal is genuine, but it only supports the existence of a broad ownership-transfer problem; it does not establish that owners of attractive machine lines will accept this exact low-cash carveout structure. The Commission's June 2026 material itself emphasizes the general SME-transfer challenge and matchmaking environment, not Afterline's deal supply or economics ([European Commission recommendation](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/publications/commission-recommendation-facilitate-transfer-small-and-medium-sized-enterprises_en)).

## 3. Strongest objections and caveats

1. **The founder-wealth arithmetic is not equity-value arithmetic.** Multiplying operating profit by 4.5–6.0 produces a business/enterprise value unless net debt and debt-like obligations are then deducted. The scenarios explicitly depend on seller finance, working-capital debt, and continuing capped earn-outs, but provide no outstanding-liability schedule and do not state clearly whether earn-out payments are already deducted in reported operating margin. In the conservative case, the stated 44.1m PLN founder value clears the 43.0m PLN threshold by only 1.1m PLN before any liability deduction. **Fatal to the Founder Wealth Gate as written.**

2. **The supposed repeatable distribution engine is also a repeatable acquisition engine, and neither side is evidenced.** The projected 40 approaches producing ten owner conversations, four data rooms, and a signed option within 90 days is an aggressive funnel for an unknown, part-time founder asking an owner to transfer reputation-sensitive assets and customer access. Reaching 14–16 clean lines requires this to work repeatedly without acquisition prices rising or seller terms consuming the synergy. This is not fatal to the first experiment, but it is potentially fatal to the portfolio thesis.

3. **The control point is narrower than the language sometimes implies.** Assigned files, tooling, marks, contracts, and seller communication rights are valuable, but they do not grant exclusivity over customers' maintenance budgets. Customers can still use independent technicians, substitute generic components, self-maintain, or commission reverse engineering. The asset bundle gives Afterline a cost, speed, trust, and information advantage; it does not automatically create a legal monopoly over aftermarket demand.

4. **The unit economics are coherent targets, not evidence.** A 25,000 PLN aggregate prepayment and one 45% contribution order prove that one small transaction can happen. They do not establish 15–25% continuity-plan penetration, 58–65% gross margin, 18–27% operating margin, 75% renewal, 20–35% installed-base ordering, or 30-month acquisition payback. Seller earn-outs equal to 20–35% of realised gross profit for three to five years could materially reduce cash generation, and their treatment in the stated margins is ambiguous.

5. **“Non-regulated” does not remove machinery and product-liability exposure.** Under Article 18 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, an actor making a substantial machinery modification can assume manufacturer obligations, including conformity responsibility ([EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2023/1230](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/en)). Retrofits, reproduced parts, firmware, incomplete technical files, and historic machine variants can therefore create engineering, insurance, warranty, and documentation costs that a generic contractor quote will not necessarily capture. The candidate recognizes this risk, but the exclusion rule must work in practice or the margin thesis fails.

6. **Founder fit is good for sourcing analytics and systemisation but weak for seller trust and industrial accountability.** Contract engineers, lawyers, and technicians can supply expertise, yet the founder still must judge incomplete archives, seller representations, safety boundaries, service quality, and transaction trade-offs. At 15–25 hours per week beside full-time employment, a simultaneous seller process, legal diligence, customer sale, and first fulfilment is feasible only with unusually reliable contractors and strict scope control.

**Fatality judgment:** Objection 1 is fatal to the mandatory founder-wealth gate for the exact candidate as written. Objections 2–6 are not logically fatal before testing, but any failure to obtain a rights-clean option, deliver safely at the stated contribution, show repeat demand, or reproduce seller sourcing would kill either the first line or the portfolio thesis under the candidate's own criteria.

## 4. Control-point tier and reason

**Tier 1, conditional on execution.**

An enforceable exclusive option controls the acquisition opportunity during diligence; after closing, an assignment or irrevocable exclusive licence to the named technical archive, tooling, mark, service history, transferable contracts, and installed-base communication route is an owned or contracted scarce bundle. That satisfies Tier 1 only for the specified machine line and only if title, software rights, tooling ownership, data-use rights, liabilities, seller non-compete/non-licensing obligations, and revocability are clean. If the arrangement is merely an introduction, non-exclusive licence, informal cooperation, or incomplete archive, it drops directly to Tier 3. The customer relationship itself is not Tier 1 unless customers are bound by transferable contracts.

## 5. Strongest copy risk

The strongest copy risk is **bypass rather than literal copying**: ex-technicians, incumbent service firms, suppliers, or customers can inspect the machines, reverse-engineer the most profitable parts, source commodity components, and serve the same installed base without owning Afterline's archive or mark. They can cherry-pick attractive orders while Afterline bears continuity obligations, slow-moving documentation, warranty reserves, and low-frequency SKUs. For future lines, a better-capitalised consolidator can also outbid Afterline, but bypass of non-exclusive customer spend is the more immediate threat to margin retention on an already acquired line.

## 6. Founder Wealth Gate

**FAIL.**

The candidate correctly separates distributions from retained company value and provides conservative, expected, and strong scenarios; that is better than most wealth models. However, it values operating profit and applies founder ownership without subtracting outstanding secured debt, working-capital debt, seller notes, accrued/capped earn-outs, or other debt-like acquisition obligations. It also does not make clear whether 20–35% gross-profit earn-outs are included in operating expenses before the stated 18–27% operating margins.

At the candidate's 4.3 PLN/€ conversion, €10m equals 43.0m PLN. The conservative 44.1m PLN total has only a 1.1m PLN cushion before liabilities, so it does not survive the required liability deduction. The expected 66.0m PLN figure has more headroom, but it rests on 14 acquisitions, 70m PLN revenue, 23% operating margin, 82% founder ownership, and an unspecified liability balance; it is an upside case, not enough to cure the failed conservative accounting. The business may have a path to more than €10m, but the exact model does not yet demonstrate it without omitted liabilities or heroic portfolio execution.

## 7. Reachability Gate

**PASS, narrowly.**

The proof plan keeps stated pre-proof cash exposure at 34,000 PLN, below the 40,000 PLN ceiling; retains employment; avoids building a general platform; does not close an acquisition before demand evidence; and asks for valid commercial proof: three unrelated customers, at least 25,000 PLN prepaid, and one fulfilled order at 45%+ contribution. Work can be divided among the founder, a machinery engineer, a lawyer, and order-paid technicians, so the scope is possible within five hours per day.

The pass is narrow because the 180-day sequence depends on one unknown founder obtaining a reputation-sensitive exclusive option rapidly and then coordinating rights diligence, customer outreach, and safe fulfilment. There is also a pre-close circularity: the first order must be sold and delivered before Afterline owns the assets, so the option-period agreement must already grant enforceable temporary use/access rights and allocate insurance, warranty, conformity, and product liability. If it does not, the proposed paid-proof event is not legally or operationally reachable on the stated terms. Even allowing for that caveat, the bounded spend, sequential dependencies, contractors, and explicit stop rules make the experiment reachable enough to pass this gate.

## Bottom line

Afterline has a strong, potentially Tier 1 first-line control point and a credible low-capital paid-proof test. It does **not** clear the governing evaluation as written because the Founder Wealth Gate fails, and the single-line proof does not validate the repeated-acquisition economics needed to repair that failure.
