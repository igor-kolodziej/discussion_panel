# Round 12 — Acquired-Distribution Adjacent Directions

Date: 2026-07-14  
Generator role only: this file does not score, pivot, or validate candidates.

## Trigger And Preserved Insight

WooRenewal v2 and WooRenewal PriceOps EU were two materially different structures that failed for overlapping structural reasons. The preserved insight is narrower than either proposal: **buy a platform-recognised route to existing users and payments, not merely source code**. This round changes the platform, customer, wedge, or value-capture mechanism while preserving that insight.

The user-defined success gate for this round is 1,000,000 PLN of recognised annual revenue by Year 5 with at least a 25% fully loaded operating margin. It replaces the original founder-wealth gate. The Reachability and capital gates remain unchanged.

## Evidence Added In This Round

- The official Atlassian Marketplace API returned 995 Jira Data Center apps. Of 457 paid-via-Atlassian apps with 5–500 installs, 226 had no Cloud version detected under the same app key; 137 were in the 5–60-install band. Installs are not paid licences, and no vendor's willingness to sell is public.
- Atlassian formally supports app transfers after a sale or acquisition, subject to proof of purchase and seller approval: <https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/marketplace/knowledge-base/transfering-apps-between-vendors/>.
- Atlassian will end sales to existing Data Center customers in March 2028 and end affected Data Center products and Marketplace apps in March 2029. A missing app migration path can block a customer's cloud migration: <https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life> and <https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/marketplace/moving-apps-and-customers-to-cloud/>.
- Paid-via-Atlassian supplies platform licensing, billing, renewal notices and revenue share. The published October 2026 partner share is 83% for Forge, 75% for Connect and 75% for Data Center: <https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/marketplace/pricing-payment-and-billing/>.
- Google Play formally transfers users, statistics, comments, ratings and subscriptions with an app, while pre-transfer orders and historical earnings reports stay with the seller: <https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247>.
- Apple app transfers preserve the bundle ID, reviews, ratings, updates and continuing availability, subject to transfer criteria and subscription-secret handover: <https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/overview-of-app-transfer>.
- GitHub formally permits transfer of a GitHub App registration, and GitHub Marketplace can bill paid app subscriptions: <https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/apps/maintaining-github-apps/transferring-ownership-of-a-github-app> and <https://docs.github.com/en/apps/oauth-apps/using-oauth-apps/installing-an-oauth-app-in-your-organization>.
- Google Workspace listing ownership can change through Cloud-project ownership, but moving a project between organisations can require replicating the listing. This is weaker continuity than Atlassian, Apple or Google Play: <https://developers.google.com/workspace/marketplace/manage-app-listing>.
- Current public mobile-app sale examples below the 40,000 PLN first-tranche ceiling exist, but the inspected examples either have no revenue, weak paid cohorts, content/platform risk, or require implausible growth to reach the revenue gate. Examples include Jawsi, ELO AI, Lyrix and Pharmacy Connect on Flippa. A password-manager listing has a meaningful store asset but asks EUR 30,000, above the total founder-capital limit before fees.

## Finalist A — Atlassian LegacyBridge

Acquire one low-liability, paid Jira Data Center app with 10–60 verified paid customer organisations, no Cloud version, configuration data that customers must preserve, and a seller willing to transfer the listing, code/IP, documentation, support history and customer obligations through Atlassian's formal process. Build the Forge Cloud successor and a documented/automated migration path. The first beachhead is the acquired customer cohort approaching Data Center end of life; the expansion wedge is the same narrow workflow for new Cloud customers.

The attractive arithmetic is about 100 organisations at 10,000 PLN recognised annual revenue each, not thousands of consumer accounts. That is only credible if the acquired workflow is valuable enough to support the price, the exact seller transfers for no more than 28,000 PLN cash plus receipt-capped consideration, and at least three customers pay for Cloud renewal or migration inside 180 days. Public research proves the pool and platform mechanics, but not an affordable transaction.

## Finalist B — ProUtility AppID

Acquire a professionally useful Android app whose App ID, ratings, users and subscriptions transfer formally. Require at least 200 verified paying subscribers, at least 10,000 monthly organic store visits or downloads, 12 months of cohort retention, clean policy history, and a price of no more than 28,000 PLN cash plus a receipt-capped seller note. Improve one high-intent workflow and use the inherited listing to cross-sell an annual Pro tier.

The revenue bridge would require roughly 2,100 subscribers at 40 PLN monthly recognised revenue or 1,000 subscribers at 1,000 PLN annual value. The current public sub-cap listings inspected in this round do not meet the entry specification. The direction is retained as a finalist because transfer continuity and public sell-side inventory are real, but it is not acquisition-ready.

## Finalist C — GitHub Workflow Sentinel

Acquire a paid GitHub App used by software organisations for one narrow, recurring repository-control workflow. Transfer the GitHub App registration, source/IP, domain, billing records and support history; retain existing installations and sell an evidence-producing policy workflow at about 5,000–15,000 PLN per organisation per year. Two hundred customers at 5,000 PLN or 100 at 10,000 PLN can reach the revenue gate.

The transfer mechanism is official, but this round found no public set of three sellers, no verified price inside the capital cap, and no narrow workflow whose control survives GitHub-native feature expansion and open-source copying. It remains a finalist only so the evaluator can test whether those are fatal rather than letting the generator score its own output.

## Generator Handoff

- Finalist A has the strongest mechanics and underused momentum, but affordable seller access is unproved.
- Finalist B has the strongest explicit sell-side inventory, but no inspected target has the required revenue bridge.
- Finalist C has formal technical transfer, but neither sell-side supply nor defensibility is proved.
- None is panel-ready from generator work alone. Each must receive a separate advisory evaluation under the exact user-defined revenue gate before any independent panel.
