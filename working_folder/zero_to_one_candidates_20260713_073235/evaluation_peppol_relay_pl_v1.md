# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Peppol Relay PL

## Diagnosis

The candidate identifies a plausible operational pain between cheap Peppol transport and buyer-specific ERP/document execution, and its evidence-led route sprint is a disciplined way to ask customers to pay before certification. However, the proposed Access Point is entering an intentionally interoperable market with five Poland-based certified providers, global managed access from €29/month, wholesale infrastructure at €50/month plus €0.05/message, and a €4,500/year mid-market offer already advertising ERP integration. Certification is inexpensive and nonexclusive, customer endpoints are portable, and the proposed differentiation—mapping, activation, monitoring, and exception support—risks scaling as an EDI service rather than a compounding product. The paid-proof path is narrowly reachable, but the conservative founder-wealth case is not credible as written.

## Evidence and Assumptions

### Sourced evidence

- OpenPeppol publishes a concrete certification sequence and small-company AP+SMP fees totalling €7,050 before national extras, implementation, security, and operations. This supports attainability but also shows that the certificate is not financially scarce.
- Poland's official Peppol page lists five Poland-based certified providers: All For One Poland, Anachron Technology Poland, Comarch, EDITEL Polska, and Infinite.
- Peppox advertises managed AP access at €29/month per company.
- Qvalia describes partner infrastructure at €50/month plus €0.05 per message, including AP/SMP use, routing, validation, operational monitoring, maintenance, onboarding, and support.
- B2Brouter advertises €110/year and €300/year plans with Peppol features.
- PeppolSoft advertises €4,500/year for a 51–200 employee Growth tier including ERP integration.
- Comarch already advertises Peppol, KSeF, multiple document formats, orders and ASNs, dedicated project management, more than 130,000 connected entities, and 60-country coverage.
- Peppol's architecture permits customer choice of Access Point. The candidate itself acknowledges that endpoints are transferable, outbound sending may be less exclusive, every AP can reach the network, and there is no proprietary Peppol network effect.
- Eurostat supplies data from which a denominator could be constructed, but the candidate has not yet produced the required count of eligible mid-market exporters with multi-document Peppol counterparties.

### Candidate hypotheses, not evidence

- Customers suffer at least 40,000 PLN of annual pain on eligible routes and will pay 4,000 PLN for discovery, 8,000–25,000 PLN for activation, and approximately €4,800 ARR for managed routing.
- A meaningful segment needs order, response, dispatch, invoice, and exception handling rather than invoice-only transport.
- One route can be mapped and tested in no more than 20 hours, later implementations will reuse components, and mature gross margin can reach 72% with 31% operating margin.
- Founder-led evidence-based outbound can produce 25 qualified conversations and three prepayments from 150 accounts, then scale to 100 customers without a required channel partner.
- At least 30,000 eligible accounts exist across a few transferable European corridors, making 1,500 accounts a non-heroic share.
- Endpoint assignments, mappings, exception history, and local operating procedures will produce gross retention above 90% despite easy provider switching.
- A data scientist without Peppol operating history can secure mid-market finance trust and cost-effective 24/7 coverage while remaining employed.

### Prior verdict context

No independent reviewer, simulated-panel, or real-chat verdict exists for this exact candidate. The rejection ledger repeatedly identifies service-heavy wedges, prospective data assets, easy incumbent copy, and founder-wealth bridges that clear €10 million only narrowly as failure patterns. That history is comparison evidence, not a score or validation result; Peppol Relay PL still needs judgment on its own facts.

## Overall Score

**42/100.** Weighted points are 84.0 across 20.0 total weight, for a weighted average of 4.20/10 and an overall score of 42 after multiplying by 10. The strong risk discipline and reachable paid test do not offset the low scores for defensibility, durable profit, repeatable distribution, and founder wealth.

## Control Point

**Current: Tier 3. Attainable: weak Tier 2.** The founder currently has no Service Provider Agreement, production PKI certificate, AP/SMP status, customer endpoint authorisation, or routed flow. Certification is nonexclusive and routinely purchasable; the public standards and endpoint information are not controlled resources.

A signed customer authorisation would route that customer's inbound documents through the provider during the contract term, so it is a real transaction position rather than an unspecified partnership. At company level, however, it remains weak Tier 2 rather than durable Tier 1: the endpoint is revocable and portable, outbound traffic may be multi-homed, customers are free to switch APs, and no scarce right prevents another certified provider from offering the same service. Switching costs and compounding mappings are hypotheses.

**Strongest copy risk:** an existing Polish AP, global low-cost AP, enterprise EDI suite, or ERP vendor can package the same fixed-scope route activation and exception support on top of infrastructure it already operates, then use installed distribution, certifications, references, and lower marginal acquisition cost to match price or speed.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The candidate supplies all three required scenarios, separates equity value from distributions, uses 95% reinvestment in the conservative and expected cases, allows 100% in the strong case, and states ownership, capital, margins, valuation, and timing. The problem is economic credibility rather than missing table fields.

The conservative case produces about €10.5 million of founder equity—only €0.5 million above the gate—by combining 1,500 accounts at €4,800 ARR, 72% gross margin, 31% operating margin, a 6.5× operating-profit multiple, 75% founder ownership, and only €0.5 million of net obligations. None of the critical inputs has market proof. The price sits near PeppolSoft's advertised €4,500 mid-market ERP-integrated tier and far above commodity access; support and custom mapping threaten both margins and operating leverage; the 30,000-account eligible denominator is explicitly absent; and reaching 1,500 mid-market customers through founder-led route sales is not supported by a channel or CAC history.

The bridge is too sensitive to ordinary downside. A modest reduction in price, margin, ownership, or multiple—or higher security, sales, support, and international expansion costs—takes founder equity below €10 million. The expected and strong scenarios scale the same unproved assumptions rather than supplying independent evidence. Until a counted denominator, non-bespoke delivery, durable renewal, and willingness to pay at the proposed recurring price exist, the wealth model is aspirational.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass narrowly.** The proposed 4,000 PLN non-refundable route-readiness sprint is a legitimate paid discovery deliverable if it includes a customer document sample, verified endpoint/profile, usable field map, test output, and a signed conditional production order. The plan targets three unrelated prepayments by day 75, limits pre-proof founder risk to 8,000–12,000 PLN, and defers major certification spending until paid delivery and positive initial contribution. Ten to twelve hours per week plus bounded contractors is compatible with continued corporate employment and below the founder's maximum available time.

This pass covers paid evidence only; it does not prove the business. The founder is asking a mid-market exporter to share sensitive documents and prepay an unknown, uncertified vendor. A counterparty must already support useful profiles, and the sprint may prove one-off mapping demand while the customer later selects a cheaper certified provider. Certification, production reliability, 24/7 support, recurring €4,800 willingness to pay, and endpoint assignment remain unresolved after the first payment. The plan correctly stops before serious capital if those facts fail.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is appropriately narrow in customer description but not yet demonstrated as economically meaningful. Multi-document industrial routes may be rare relative to invoice-only Peppol use, and the candidate has not counted how many Polish/CEE 50–500 employee exporters have a compatible counterparty, material exception cost, budget, and endpoint authority.

Expansion from two corridors to CEE and wider Europe is technically plausible because the underlying network is interoperable. It is not asymmetric: the same interoperability lets Comarch, ecosio, the five Polish providers, global low-cost APs, and ERP vendors enter the beachhead. Public endpoint targeting is available to competitors, while country rules and ERP variants can add service work to the startup. Poland helps with language and KSeF timing but supplies no exclusion right or durable cost advantage.

## Fatal Objections

1. **The conservative founder-wealth bridge is heroic.** It clears the target by only about 5% while relying simultaneously on 1,500 customers, premium recurring price, software-like margins, high ownership, a favourable multiple, low obligations, and an uncounted eligible market.
2. **The control point is not scarce or durable.** Certification is inexpensive and nonexclusive; customer endpoints are portable; any AP can reach the network; and no contractually protected distribution or exclusive transaction class exists.
3. **The value layer risks remaining bespoke EDI service.** Buyer-specific ERP mappings, tests, exceptions, security, and 24/7 support can scale with customers and routes. Reuse at customer 100 is a target, not evidence.
4. **Competitive pricing attacks both willingness to pay and margins.** €29/month managed access and €50/month plus €0.05/message infrastructure commoditise transport, while a €4,500/year mid-market competitor already advertises ERP integration near the candidate's €4,800 ARR assumption.
5. **The beachhead denominator is missing.** Broad exporter and enterprise statistics do not count firms with supported multi-document counterparties, sufficient pain, endpoint authority, budget, and willingness to switch.
6. **Incumbents possess stronger trust and distribution.** Five Polish certified providers, global APs, ERP vendors, and enterprise EDI suites can make the same process change while offering installed references, security credentials, and operating coverage an employed unknown founder lacks.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The strongest insight is **observable-route acquisition**: use public endpoint/profile evidence to approach only firms with a named, technically reachable counterparty, then ask for a prepaid fixed-output readiness engagement before committing certification capital. This is a useful proof and distribution mechanism even though the proposed AP-and-managed-mapping business does not yet support durable wealth economics.

## Recommendation

**pivot**

The structural revision must come from the pivoter role. This advisory evaluation does not propose or validate a revised concept.
