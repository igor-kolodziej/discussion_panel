## Search brief and hard filters

This pass looked only for B2B or professional buyers who can be enumerated from an official/public registry or a mandatory filing, who directly control the paid action, and whose first contract can give the founder a present right, data asset, or workflow position. The first proof had to mean three unrelated payments within 180 days and 40,000 PLN, not one anchor programme. The foundational resource had to be ownable or provable within 12 months and 100,000 PLN. A conservative founder-wealth path had to clear €10 million with headroom, not merely touch it through aggressive share, margin, or valuation assumptions.

The pass excluded filing assistance that becomes generic compliance software, non-exclusive certification, enterprise-anchor supplier programmes, two-sided marketplaces, recovery/contingency services, local physical sites, distributor exclusives, and all shapes already blacklisted in `01_overlap_and_rejections.md`.

Six directions received decision-critical research. None survives all the hard filters. The set is deliberately not padded.

## Direction 1 — FQuota Block: principal HFC-authorisation inventory for equipment importers

### Customer point of view

An EU importer of pre-charged refrigeration, air-conditioning, heat-pump equipment, or metered-dose inhalers wants the customs release to proceed without guessing whether a promised authorisation is genuine. The importer needs sufficient authorisation at import, wants it recorded in the F-gas Portal, and would pay for a small, immediately usable block rather than negotiate with several gas groups.

### Registry-reachable payer and paid action

- The European Commission publishes the 2026 quota-holder list and the much larger list of undertakings invited to report 2025 F-gas activity. The latter is a named acquisition universe, but it does **not** expose a clean role field that separates eligible pre-charged-equipment importers from bulk-gas firms, exporters, service companies, and other reporters.
- The importer is the one direct payer and controls accepting the delegated authorisation in the F-gas Portal.
- The Commission's older impact analysis reported roughly 1,000 equipment-importing companies reporting for 2020. That is historical evidence, not a current named denominator for the precise beachhead.

### Wedge and first-contract control

The founder would register as an authorisation manager, contract with a reference-value quota holder, and require a block to be entered and accepted in the F-gas Portal before approaching buyers. Article 21 permits a quota holder to authorise a manager; the manager may delegate it once to an equipment importer. Authorisations are not time-bound until used. A Portal-recorded block is therefore a real current right rather than an unsigned partnership promise.

### Three-payment and resource proof

- Resource proof: a signed quota-holder purchase agreement and an accepted 400 tCO2e authorisation in the manager's Portal account.
- Illustrative proof: buy 400 tCO2e at the Q1 2026 reported average purchase price of €17.19/tCO2e (about €6,876, roughly 29,500 PLN), then obtain three prepaid orders of 100 tCO2e each at a **hypothetical** €18.50/tCO2e.
- This fits the cash cap, but the illustrative spread produces only €393 gross profit on the three orders. It proves execution, not attractive contribution economics.

### Bottom-up denominator and wealth bridge

- Official maximum HFC quantity: 42,874,410 tCO2e in 2025–2026; 21,665,691 in 2027–2029; 9,132,097 in 2030–2032; zero from 2050.
- At the Q1 2026 authorisation purchase price of €17.19/tCO2e, a company with a 20% operating margin, 75% founder ownership, and a 6× operating-profit value would need about €11.1 million revenue, or about 647,000 tCO2e routed annually, merely to create €10 million of founder equity value. That is about 1.5% of the 2026 statutory cap but 7.1% of the 2030 cap. A more conservative 8% operating margin raises required annual volume to roughly 1.62 million tCO2e, or 17.7% of the 2030 cap.
- Those are not conservative-with-headroom shares for an unknown entrant, especially in a legally shrinking market. Retained cash is not added to the business value in this bridge, avoiding double counting.

### Direct competitor and copy risk

- `fgas-service.de` already advertises quotas from its own stock, Portal transfers, and authorisation in one to three working days.
- The Commission itself offers an in-Portal matchmaking list between quota holders, authorisation seekers, and managers.
- Any established gas distributor or quota manager can recreate the wedge with one supply contract. The supposed control is inventory availability, not an exclusive right or compounding data asset.

### Funnel decision — reject

Reachable transaction proof exists, but it is low-spread commodity inventory with an official matching channel, a live stock competitor, no clean current importer denominator, and a statutory volume cliff. The resource does not compound: every block must be reacquired, and the underlying market intentionally disappears. Do not recycle as an F-gas broker, only-representative bundle, audit bundle, or alternative territory.

## Direction 2 — PoolSure Principal: optioned FuelEU Maritime compliance surplus

### Customer point of view

A ship's Document of Compliance holder with a verified FuelEU deficit wants a fixed-price alternative to the statutory penalty and does not want a late-April scramble involving uncertain counterparties, KYC, and inconsistent vessel data.

### Registry-reachable payer and paid action

- THETIS-MRV makes ship emissions data publicly available. Historical Commission/EMSA reporting covered around 10,800 ships over 5,000 GT; this is a named vessel universe, although the exact verified 2026 surplus/deficit set appears only through the compliance workflow.
- The deficit company's DOC holder is the direct payer and controls putting its vessel into a pool.

### Wedge and first-contract control

Act as principal, not as a marketplace: take an exclusive option over a verified surplus block from one vessel, then sell fixed-price allocations to three deficit DOC holders. The option is a contractual right, but the regulatory transaction is not controlled by that bilateral contract alone. Every company in the pool must approve, and the pool starter's verifier must confirm it in THETIS-MRV.

### Three-payment and resource proof

- Three deficit holders could theoretically pay non-refundable reservation fees for 2026-vintage surplus before the April 2027 deadline.
- At OceanScore's March 2026 index of €197/tCO2e, outright ownership of only 500 tCO2e costs about €98,500, already above the entire 100,000 PLN founder-resource cap. A low-cost option would leave the seller's performance and final verified balance uncontrolled.
- Therefore the first-cycle asset is either unaffordable inventory or a speculative counterparty promise. It does not meet the present-control requirement.

### Bottom-up denominator and wealth bridge

- Named universe: approximately 10,800 monitored ships in the first MRV period. BetterSea now claims 5,000+ vessels connected to its marketplace, demonstrating that a major competitor already touches close to half that historical universe.
- At €197/tCO2e, 15% operating margin, 75% founder ownership, and 6× operating profit, the business would need about €14.8 million annual principal revenue, or approximately 75,000 tCO2e sold. This calculation is not sufficient because there is no official bottom-up count of addressable deficit tonnes available before verification and no demonstrated gross spread.
- If revenue is only a 5% transaction fee rather than principal revenue, the required GMV rises to roughly €296 million. Neither case forms a conservative wealth path with headroom.

### Direct competitor and copy risk

- OceanScore operates a live pooling marketplace and publishes a price index.
- BetterSea claims 5,000+ connected vessels with no subscription or upfront fee.
- STX offers fixed-price access to surplus and pool registration.
- The first pool still requires multi-party approval, KYC, aligned data, and verifier confirmation. One failed participant can invalidate the structure.

### Funnel decision — reject

This is the excluded marketplace/coordination shape even when relabelled principal inventory. It has multiple existential approvals, inventory above the cap, strong incumbents, and no reusable asset that avoids reacquiring annual surplus. Do not panel or revive as a token, exchange, pool insurer, or CEE broker.

## Direction 3 — PMEF Reserve: owned Polish white-certificate blocks for obligated energy companies

### Customer point of view

An obligated energy seller wants to meet its energy-efficiency obligation without paying the substitute fee or discovering near the deadline that a project-origin certificate was delayed.

### Registry-reachable payer and paid action

- URE publishes obligation guidance and, for 2026, a named list of 36 obligated entities offering non-refundable energy-efficiency programmes.
- TGE publishes PMEF_F prices and volumes. The obligated entity is the direct payer and controls purchase and surrender.

### Wedge and first-contract control

Buy an issued PMEF_F property-right block through the exchange or a broker and resell small guaranteed-delivery lots. Once purchased, the certificate is a current asset. The flaw is that TGE itself is the transparent transaction rail: buyers do not need the founder, and a generic project-origination service moves back into ESCO consulting.

### Three-payment and resource proof

- March 2026 PMEF_F averaged 2,328.48 PLN/toe with 13.39 ktoe monthly volume.
- Ten toe costs about 23,285 PLN and could be owned inside the first cap. Three tiny resale invoices are mechanically possible.
- This does not demonstrate positive contribution: after brokerage and spread, a ten-toe block cannot support meaningful gross profit, while a larger proprietary position consumes the founder's capital.

### Bottom-up denominator and wealth bridge

- March 2026 market value was roughly 31.2 million PLN (13.39 ktoe × 2,328.48 PLN/toe). Annualising that single month only as a sensitivity gives about 374 million PLN, not a forecast.
- Even €15 million of annual principal revenue would represent roughly 17% of that annualised market sensitivity before recognizing that most revenue is pass-through certificate cost. A commission model requires a still larger market share.
- The named list of 36 programme operators is far too concentrated for three unrelated early payments and a conservative wealth path without enterprise-anchor dependence.

### Direct competitor and copy risk

- Veolia ESCO says it has served more than 3,000 white-certificate clients and describes itself as the market leader.
- KAPE, Enea, Fortum, DB Energy, and many energy consultancies already originate, monetise, or assist with certificates.
- Any TGE member/broker can source the same fungible right. There is no exclusive supply or proprietary workflow at the first contract.

### Funnel decision — reject

The rights are real, liquid, and controllable, but their liquidity destroys the intermediary moat. Origination is a mature audit/ESCO service; inventory is capital-heavy pass-through. Do not revive through a different certificate colour, energy-saving calculator, or family-installation lead channel.

## Direction 4 — TrialClose CTIS: fixed-scope overdue-results closeout

### Customer point of view

A small biotech, university sponsor, or investigator-led trial team does not want a broad transparency transformation programme. It wants one ended trial converted into the CTIS Annex IV result summary and Annex V layperson summary, checked for personal data and confidential information, and submitted before enforcement escalates.

### Registry-reachable payer and paid action

- CTIS publicly identifies trial, status, end date, and primary sponsor. Regulation 536/2014 Article 37(4) requires results within 12 months of trial end, or six months for paediatric trials.
- A public registry-derived scoreboard dated 2026-07-10 reports 3,479 ended CTIS trials, 1,029 with results, 859 overdue, and 1,591 still in the grace period. These counts are independently derived from CTIS, not an EMA-certified scoreboard.
- The sponsor is the one direct payer and controls granting delegated CTIS access and submitting the result.

### Wedge and first-contract control

A fixed-fee work order grants access to the protocol, statistical report, result tables, and CTIS role. The founder can own transformation templates, validation checks, and a non-confidential field mapping. The sponsor's underlying data and trial-specific learning remain confidential and cannot become a broadly licensable proprietary dataset.

### Three-payment and resource proof

- Contact 60 overdue sponsors selected for simple, non-paediatric, non-oncology interventional trials; sell three fixed-scope paid closeouts at a **hypothetical** €3,500–€7,500 each.
- Before sale, retain a senior clinical-regulatory writer for capped review and build only a secure template/checking workflow. A 35,000 PLN proof budget is plausible alongside employment.
- The proof would establish willingness to pay, but delivery remains expert-led and trust-heavy.

### Bottom-up denominator and wealth bridge

- The observed current backlog is 859 overdue trials; 1,792 trials ended in 2025 and 876 in 2026 in the independent snapshot, many still in grace.
- At €7,500 per trial, even 1,800 annual closeouts represent only €13.5 million total annual revenue. With 25% operating margin, 75% founder ownership, and a 6× operating-profit value, the founder needs about €8.9 million revenue, or roughly 1,185 closeouts per year—about two-thirds of that illustrative annual event flow.
- Global registry expansion adds more rules, languages, and expert review rather than reusing one hard asset. The conservative wealth path therefore depends on heroic share and an indefinitely large service team.

### Direct competitor and copy risk

- Instem/d-wise sells clinical-trial transparency strategy, anonymisation, and outsourced services backed by proprietary software.
- CROs and regulatory-writing firms can add the same fixed-scope package; sponsors can train staff using EMA's detailed handbook and modules.
- A mapping/template can be copied. The trusted output is the writer's judgement, not a control point owned at first contract.

### Funnel decision — reject

This has unusually clean event-based acquisition and can obtain three paid proofs, but it is a bounded regulatory-writing service whose entire EU event denominator barely supports the needed revenue. It fails the founder-wealth gate and compounding-control requirement. Do not revive as a CTIS dashboard, overdue lead feed, lay-summary AI wrapper, or sponsor cohort programme.

- European Commission clinical-trial regulation overview: https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/clinical-trials/clinical-trials-regulation-eu-no-5362014_en

## Direction 5 — VetPV Desk: outsourced veterinary QPPV and product-lifecycle mandate

### Customer point of view

A micro veterinary marketing-authorisation holder with a handful of low-event products wants one accountable EU QPPV desk that keeps its pharmacovigilance system master file, adverse-event reporting, signal review, training, and Union Product Database updates audit-ready without hiring a full internal team.

### Registry-reachable payer and paid action

- EMA's public Union Product Database exposes authorised veterinary medicines, availability, and marketing-authorisation holders. Each MAH can be enumerated by product.
- Regulation (EU) 2019/6 permits outsourcing QPPV functions under detailed contracts, while ultimate responsibility remains with the MAH.
- The MAH is the one direct payer and controls signing the QPPV mandate and providing its safety/product records.

### Wedge and first-contract control

The first contract is an exclusive outsourced QPPV mandate for a defined product portfolio plus access to the pharmacovigilance system. This is workflow access, not durable ownership: the MAH can revoke the mandate, and confidential safety data cannot be pooled into a saleable cross-client asset.

### Three-payment and resource proof

- Before outreach, sign a capped subcontract with an experienced veterinarian/QPPV and deputy, obtain professional liability cover, SOPs, secure case intake, and an inspection-ready continuity plan.
- Three micro-MAHs could pay onboarding fees and quarterly retainers inside 180 days, but the founder cannot personally deliver or credibly sell the service without the specialist. The specialist is therefore a critical, non-owned resource at proof stage.
- A 40,000 PLN pre-proof cap is possible only with unusually cheap part-time QPPV coverage; the 24/7 responsibility and insurance quote are unverified.

### Bottom-up denominator and wealth bridge

- UPD provides a target list but the research did not recover an authoritative count of independent micro-MAHs with one to five products, the actual beachhead denominator. Product counts would materially overstate buyers because one MAH can hold many national authorisations.
- At a **hypothetical** €15,000 annual revenue per micro-MAH, the €8.9 million revenue needed for €10 million founder equity under 25% operating margin, 75% ownership, and 6× operating profit requires about 593 active MAHs. Even €30,000 per MAH still requires nearly 300 high-liability accounts.
- That is an expert-services organisation, not a conservative, founder-leveraged compounding model.

### Direct competitor and copy risk

- Tekrolab offers complete outsourced veterinary PV including EU QPPV.
- Medfiles offers veterinary QPPV, signal detection, case reporting, and quality systems.
- Pharmafile markets EU/UK QPPV coverage for veterinary MAHs.
- Each competitor already has specialist credibility and deputies; workflow templates are purchasable and the mandate is revocable.

### Funnel decision — reject

The buyers are perfectly registry-reachable and three retainers are conceivable, but the control point is rented specialist trust plus revocable client access. The founder lacks veterinary PV authority, no precise micro-MAH denominator was established, and wealth requires hundreds of high-liability service accounts. Do not revive as a generic UPD tool, QPPV marketplace, deputy network, or AI signal service.

## Direction 6 — MarkDirect Renewal: prepaid EUIPO renewal mandates

### Customer point of view

An owner of one to ten EU trade marks wants the renewal paid correctly, on time, at the official fee plus a transparent fixed handling charge. It dislikes opaque law-firm invoices and fraudulent-looking renewal solicitations.

### Registry-reachable payer and paid action

- The EUIPO register exposes owner, representative, status, classes, and renewal date. The exact approaching-renewal cohort can be generated without a partner.
- EUIPO requires the renewal request and payment in the six months before expiry. The rights owner is the sole direct payer and controls the instruction.
- EUIPO received 196,957 EUTM applications in 2025, an official event-flow reference, but it is not the current renewal denominator because marks renew on a ten-year cycle and attrition matters.

### Wedge and first-contract control

The owner prepays the official fee and a fixed service fee under a limited renewal mandate. That mandate and client money control the transaction for one renewal. It is not a durable payment rail: the client can renew directly through EUIPO, use its lawyer, or change provider at the next ten-year event.

### Three-payment and resource proof

- Select 100 unrepresented owner-SMEs with renewals due in 90–180 days and offer official fee at cost plus a **hypothetical** €250 handling fee and insured payment guarantee.
- Three prepaid renewals are plausible well inside 40,000 PLN because official fees are customer-funded.
- The proof validates convenience but yields only €750 gross service revenue before payment risk, support, acquisition, and insurance.

### Bottom-up denominator and wealth bridge

- At €250 service revenue, 35% operating margin, 75% founder ownership, and a 6× operating-profit value, creating €10 million of founder equity requires about €6.35 million annual service revenue, or roughly 25,400 renewals per year.
- Against 196,957 annual applications as a rough mature-cohort ceiling, that is about 13% of a Europe-wide event flow before attrition. It is not a conservative share for an unknown entrant facing direct self-service and dominant annuity providers.
- Ten-year event frequency makes retention and reinvestment weak; client funds/official fees must not be counted as economic revenue.

### Direct competitor and copy risk

- Dennemeyer manages global trademark renewals and markets itself as a leading full-service provider.
- Clarivate/CPA Global, Questel, Anaqua, and established IP firms already combine portfolio records, reminders, payments, and legal support.
- EUIPO itself warns owners about unsolicited renewal solicitations. Cold outreach from an unknown Polish founder begins with a trust deficit and can resemble the very scam pattern the customer fears.

### Funnel decision — reject

This is the cleanest three-payment path of the round, but it has negligible revenue density, a ten-year repeat interval, direct official self-service, dominant trusted incumbents, and no compounding control. Do not revive as patent annuities, design renewals, deadline insurance, or an AI docketing wrapper.

## Cross-direction conclusion

### What the registry constraint did solve

- It eliminated vague customer acquisition: every direction has a named or mechanically enumerable acquisition universe.
- It produced direct payer/action alignment in CTIS, veterinary PV, EUIPO renewal, and F-gas authorisation.
- It showed that three small payments are not equivalent to a scalable business. Several directions can invoice three customers but still fail contribution economics or founder wealth.

### Repeated fatal pattern

The official registry usually makes the same event visible to everyone. Unless the founder owns the scarce input to the transaction, registry-based acquisition is a lead list rather than a moat. In this pass, the scarce inputs were either:

- fungible and available through an official exchange or matching rail (F-gas, PMEF_F);
- reacquired every compliance year and subject to several approvals (FuelEU);
- expert judgement and revocable client data (CTIS, veterinary PV); or
- no scarcer than a prepaid administrative mandate (EUIPO).

None becomes harder to copy as more customers are served. Several are exactly the generic compliance, marketplace, or service shapes the run is required to reject.

## Finalist-worthy directions

**None.**

FQuota Block was the strongest control candidate because a Portal-accepted authorisation is a real transferable-use right at the first contract. It is still not finalist-worthy: an exact stock competitor exists, the Commission provides matching, the current spread does not fund attractive proof, and the statutory market cap falls from 42.9 million tCO2e in 2026 to 9.1 million in 2030. TrialClose CTIS was the strongest reachability candidate, but its entire observed annual event flow cannot support a conservative €10 million founder-wealth path without dominating a trust-heavy service market.
