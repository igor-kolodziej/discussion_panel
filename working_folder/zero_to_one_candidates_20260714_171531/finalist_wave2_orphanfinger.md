# Zero-to-One Candidate: OrphanFinger

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build an in-stock European long-tail catalogue of clean-room reverse-engineered, batch-documented replacement elastomer fingers for discontinued soft-gripper SKUs used in food-packaging operations.

## Founder Fit

The founder fits data-driven SKU discovery, search acquisition, pricing, QA analytics and inventory systems. Elastomer, mold, food-contact documentation and mechanical testing use fixed-scope contractors.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

Food-packaging operators and integrators face downtime or premature gripper replacement when elastomer fingers wear and an OEM discontinues the exact SKU. Generic molding shops can reproduce geometry but often lack rapid stock, batch documentation and application-specific compound history.

## Product Wedge

One customer-funded orphan SKU at a time: a 20–30% deposit funds clean-room CAD, mold and first articles; finished 4–6-finger sets cost 1,200–2,400 PLN and ship from owned stock. Claims are limited to tested compatibility; no OEM affiliation is implied.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The original OEM has chosen not to serve the long tail, while operators value immediate compatible stock more than novel robotics. Each prepaid mold creates a reusable inventory asset and exact-intent search page.

## Underused Momentum

Soft grippers are operational wear products; public discontinuation and preventive-replacement notices expose exact SKU events. European reshoring and food automation expand the installed maintenance base.

## Beachhead Market

Discontinued traditional soft-gripper fingers used in non-sterile food primary/secondary packaging where drawings/parts can be lawfully clean-room recreated and material compliance is documentable.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

1,200–2,400 PLN per set, expected 1,800 PLN; deposit covers first mold. Expected direct set cost 500–650 PLN, 60–70% gross margin and 20% operating margin after tooling amortization, QA, stock, insurance and search sales. Stressed contribution remains positive.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First ten from discontinued-SKU search pages, OEM notices, used-equipment listings, integrator service pages and exact part-number inquiries. First 100 through a growing exact-SKU catalogue, European automation-parts distributors and paid search; no distributor is required for first order.

## Primary Control Point

Owned clean-room CAD, molds, compound specifications, batch/compatibility records and finished long-tail inventory funded by deposits. Customer buys an already controlled article, not sourcing labor.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

M3: two compounds and one geometry/test fixture under 12k PLN. M6: one paid deposit and first lot before 35k cumulative spend. M12: three orphan SKUs, two repeat buyers, stocked inventory and <100k PLN founder capital.

## Distribution Advantage

Each obscure part number has high search intent and low content competition. Stock availability, delivery history and a paid tooling bank compound across distributors and installed machines.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with one discontinued soft-gripper family; expand to 30–60 orphan fingers, bellows, suction interfaces and compliant robot wear parts. The moat is catalogue/tooling/stock/documentation speed, not one elastomer shape.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland provides low-cost molding/test access and fast EU fulfilment. EU food-contact and batch documentation can differentiate from anonymous imports, subject to counsel/testing.

## Compounding Advantage

Each paid mold, installed-base clue, repeat interval, batch result and in-stock SKU lowers later CAC and working-capital risk, building a physical-AI MRO distribution asset.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

95% reinvestment and ≤5% optional distributions conservative/expected while employed; strong 100%. Paid mold M6, three SKUs M12, ten SKUs year 2–3, 30 by year 8.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Create one tested first article and exact-SKU page, then approach 100 public integrators/operators/used-equipment sellers for a 20–30% deposit. Maximum pre-proof spend 12–25k PLN; production mold follows deposit.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

25k pre-proof maximum; ≤35k against first deposit; ≤100k only after delivery and repeat/second buyer; family capital excluded.

## Critical Dependency Map

Lawful clean-room/FTO, food-contact compliance, installed-base volume, liability, exact compatibility, stock obsolescence and distributor response.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

At least ten active SKUs, multiple repeat buyers, positive contribution after liability/QA/stock, repeatable exact-intent acquisition and 12 months runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Retain molds, elastomer test data, fatigue rig, exact-SKU traffic, customers and inventory for other compliant robot wear parts. Avoid generic molding service.

## Critical Evidence

Soft Robotics published a traditional-finger discontinuation notice and preventive replacement material; active alternatives exist.

## Critical Hypotheses

Installed orphan fleets produce 500 sets/SKU/year; reverse engineering is lawful/insurable; documentation beats local molding; deposits fund tooling.

## Kill Criteria

No deposit in 180 days/100 accounts; FTO/compliance blocks first SKU; fewer than 200 annual sets per mature SKU; stressed contribution non-positive; generic shops or OEM successor stock satisfy demand; liability insurance destroys margin.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Founder 10–15 hours/week; elastomer/mold contractor, test lab, IP/food-contact counsel, contract manufacturer. Pre-proof 25k PLN.
