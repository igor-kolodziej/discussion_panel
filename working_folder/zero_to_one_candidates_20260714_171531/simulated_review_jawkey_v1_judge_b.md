## Verdict

**Score: 57/100**

## Strongest objections and caveats

- The existential assumption remains unproven: a behind-ear dry-EMG/IMU device must distinguish four to six gestures across diverse users while suppressing speech, chewing, clenching, movement, sweat, placement drift, and fatigue. Published dry-EMG assistive evidence supports feasibility mainly as a single switch and in small samples, not JawKey's multi-command daily reliability claim.
- The 38,000 PLN paid-proof budget omits a serious dependency. Bluetooth SIG requires qualification before selling or distributing a branded Bluetooth product; the current adopter qualification fee is $12,000, before RED/CE, cybersecurity, safety, battery, and test costs. This contradicts the claim that standard HID makes the product sellable without approval and may break the reachability gate unless the preorder is legally structured outside “selling”—which the proposal does not establish.
- Twenty-five potentially refundable preorders show interest, but not delivered reliability, return rates, fitting burden, support cost, or positive contribution. The 55% margin omits credible allowances for certification, small-batch yield, warranty, returns, electrode/pad failures, customer support, and accessibility onboarding.
- Forums, directories, search, and device loans are discoverability tactics, not yet a repeatable distribution system. Built-in sound, camera, head-gesture, and AirPods inputs reduce urgency for some users.
- The founder-wealth gate is not credibly passed. The expected case depends on roughly 25,000 devices supporting 5.17m PLN normalized EBIT and 70% ownership, but no segment sizing, acquisition throughput, retention/replacement behavior, or channel evidence makes that a non-heroic expected outcome. The conservative case explicitly falls far short.
- A solo, employed founder coordinating biosignal R&D, embedded hardware, manufacturing, compliance, accessibility support, and international DTC operations within 25 hours/week and 95,000 PLN is materially execution-constrained even with bounded contractors.
- Freedom-to-operate is unresolved: an active US patent family already claims earpiece facial EMG, inertial sensing, wireless transmission, gesture interpretation, and commands—close enough to require professional analysis before relying on global expansion.

## Control point

**Tier 3.** The owned prototype, electrode geometry, firmware, pads, and serialized inventory are ordinary product assets, not scarce control. No exclusive rights, contracted distribution, protected supply, prepaid channel, installed base, or proven switching costs exist. The proposed dataset is prospective, opt-in, initially tiny, and may compound too slowly to prevent imitation. A competent incumbent could reproduce the functional wedge through routine hardware, firmware, and classification work.

## Strongest copy risk

**Emego/GSPK**, not merely GlassOuse. Emego already sells a wireless, body-worn dry-EMG assistive switch usable around the jaw, with CE Class I positioning and real-user development; adding an IMU, multi-gesture classifier, and direct HID output is an incremental extension rather than a new capability stack. Its existing technical and compliance base makes it the fastest credible copier.
