# ActionKey — Exact Candidate v1

ActionKey sells enterprises a separately trusted e-ink approval terminal that displays the exact arguments of a consequential AI-agent action, records an explicit human decision, signs the receipt, and can physically block or kill routed actions across agent platforms. The buyer is a CISO or agent-platform owner before allowing an agent to pay, delete, send, change permissions, or release sensitive data. Current software approvals, logs, PAM controls, and ordinary security keys often authenticate a user without proving that the human-readable recipient, amount, resource, or permission shown to the approver is exactly what the agent executes. The wedge is one irreversible action whose host/agent compromise threat is explicit, not a broad governance suite.

A 20,000–40,000 PLN design pilot supplies five terminals and one tool connector. Each terminal receives a canonical signed payload over an isolated channel, displays the exact action arguments, requires local input, signs a receipt with a secure element, and exposes a hardware kill switch. A small gateway refuses the covered call without the receipt. Production terminals cost 2,500–4,000 PLN with an expected 800–1,200 PLN BOM; the policy/receipt gateway costs 40,000–100,000 PLN annually per account. Expected blended gross margin is about 72%. Under a 20% pilot-price reduction, 30% higher prototype/delivery cost, and doubled CAC, the first pilot retains positive contribution.

The first-sale control point is the owned trusted-display firmware, secure-element issuer keys, canonical action schema, terminal inventory, signed receipt registry, and a customer contract routing every covered approval through the gateway. The strongest copy risk is Microsoft, an agent platform, FIDO/Yubico, or a PAM vendor adding exact-action trusted confirmation and bundling it through existing distribution. ActionKey survives only if the separately trusted, cross-vendor display and receipt block altered-argument/compromised-host attacks that platform-local approval cannot, and if deployed keys, connectors, audited schemas, and neutral verification create switching cost.

Cold acquisition uses 100 named enterprise-agent builders, NIST agent-security respondents, regulated SaaS firms, security vendors, and enterprise AI engineering teams. At 5% positive response, one prepaid pilot is plausible when demonstrated with a working altered-argument attack against one tool flow. Pre-proof spend is capped at 35,000 PLN for five secure-element/e-ink prototypes, issuer service, one connector, threat model, counsel, and an attack demo. No production run or broad platform is built before payment. By month 12 and below 100,000 PLN cumulative founder spend, proof requires two unrelated paying customers, two different agent stacks, a routed signed-receipt registry, and an independent penetration report.

The founder remains employed and works no more than 25 hours weekly, owning threat modeling, action schemas, AI/tool integration, analytics, documentation, and sales. A secure-hardware engineer, penetration tester, and cryptography/legal reviewer work fixed scopes. Additional original capital follows only paid delivery, positive stressed contribution, and a second customer/contracted recurring demand. Family capital is excluded. Full-time transition requires three independent paying accounts or contracted ARR, repeatable acquisition, positive contribution including security/audit cost, and 12 months personal runway.

Founder-wealth scenarios deduct debt and do not double-count retained earnings. Conservative year 7: 60 accounts at 80,000 PLN gives 4.8 million PLN revenue, 28% EBIT, 82% ownership, and about 4.4 million PLN founder equity at 4×. Expected year 8: 160 accounts at 100,000 PLN gives 16 million PLN revenue, 35% EBIT, 75% ownership, and 16.8–25.2 million PLN founder equity at 4–6×. Strong year 9: 350 accounts at 140,000 PLN gives 49 million PLN revenue, 38% EBIT, 62% ownership, and about 69.3 million PLN founder equity at 6×. While employed, 95% of distributable after-tax cash is reinvested in conservative and expected cases with optional distributions capped at 5%; strong success reinvests 100%.

The expansion path is from one exact-action approval to a library of canonical schemas, dual control, policy-bound hardware receipts, physical emergency stop, and licensed integration into terminals or wearables. Future-lab assets are a secure real-time human–AI authorization protocol, hardware/cryptographic expertise, connector IP, signed behavioral decision data, and enterprise distribution.

Kill the idea if no prepaid pilot appears after 100 qualified accounts and 180 days; buyers accept platform-local software/PAM approval as equivalent; two agent stacks cannot be routed under 100,000 PLN; an independent red team shows that displayed and executed arguments are not bound; enterprise procurement makes paid proof impossible while employed; or a platform/FIDO/PAM feature removes willingness to pay before customer three.
