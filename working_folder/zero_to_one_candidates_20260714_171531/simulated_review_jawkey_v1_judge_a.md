## Strongest objections and caveats

- The founder-wealth case is arithmetic, not yet credible: 25,000 annual devices, 18% operating margin, 70% ownership, and a 4–6× EBIT valuation lack a bottom-up reachable-market, CAC, return-rate, support-cost, working-capital, and channel model. The assistive beachhead may be far smaller than the expected case requires; industrial and AR/VR adjacencies involve different buyers and cannot rescue it.
- Technical feasibility is plausible, but daily utility is existentially unproved. Ear-worn jaw sensing has worked in controlled research, yet that does not establish reliable separation of four to six gestures from eating, speech, clenching, movement, contact drift, hair/skin variation, fatigue, and disability-specific motor patterns during independent all-day use.
- Twenty-five refundable preorders can demonstrate interest, but not delivered reliability, fitting labor, retention, returns, pad consumption, or repeatable CAC. The requirement for positive delivered contribution before releasing post-proof spending is circular unless the first deliverable batch can be manufactured and supported inside the 38,000 PLN pre-proof cap.
- Distribution remains a list of public channels, not a repeatable mechanism. Forums, accessibility directories, search, and device loans offer access but no demonstrated path from 25 early adopters to 25,000 annual purchases without expensive education, trust-building, demonstrations, and support.
- “Non-medical” avoids relying on medical approval but does not make a Bluetooth wearable compliance-light. EU radio-equipment conformity, product-safety documentation, traceability, warnings, complaint handling, warranty/returns, and production QA remain material; the 95,000 PLN first-year allowance may be tight once these and inventory are included.

## Control point

**Tier 3.** The current control point is an owned but non-scarce product configuration assembled from purchasable electrodes, analog electronics, IMUs, Bluetooth, firmware, pads, and public distribution. The neuromotor dataset, calibration advantage, installed base, and switching costs are prospective rather than controlled assets, while the pad interface is readily replicable. It could compound later, but nothing presently prevents a routine incumbent response.

## Strongest copy risk

An established assistive-input vendor extends an existing muscle switch into a jaw-positioned, multi-gesture Bluetooth product using a wearable/EMG contractor, then wins through its installed devices, accessory catalogue, support reputation, and existing accessibility distribution. GlassOuse already sells a $129 muscle-activated switch alongside cheek, blink, bite, proximity, and head-control products, making this an immediate adjacency rather than a hypothetical future entry.
