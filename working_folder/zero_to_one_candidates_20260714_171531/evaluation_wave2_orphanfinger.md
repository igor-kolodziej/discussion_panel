# Zero-to-One Evaluation: OrphanFinger

## Diagnosis

OrphanFinger has unusually good paid-proof reachability because an exact discontinued part number creates urgent search intent and a customer deposit can fund the mold. It also owns real inventory/tooling at first sale rather than future data. However, the expected wealth case requires an unsupported 500 sets per year across 30 orphan SKUs, while food-contact/FTO/liability and generic molding constrain margins. The narrow wedge is worth retaining as an MRO tactic but fails the founder-wealth gate as a standalone company.

## Evidence and Assumptions

OEM discontinuation and preventive replacement notices establish the event. Installed-base counts, set frequency, food-contact path, price and search conversion are hypotheses. Stress economics are positive; the 200-set sensitivity fails wealth.

## Overall Score

**75/100.** Weighted points 149/200; rounded score 75.

## Control Point

**Tier 1 per SKU, Tier 2 portfolio.** Owned molds/specs/stock are direct; the catalogue is non-exclusive. Strongest copy risk: a generic compliant molder duplicates the most profitable SKU or an OEM successor restores supply.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** A defensible 30-SKU ×200-set case yields about 6.2m PLN founder equity at 4×. The stated 500 sets per SKU is not evidenced and strong expansion is doing the work.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** Exact-intent outreach and a deposit can fund the first mold within 180 days and 25k PLN.

## Expansion Check

Adjacent wear parts exist, but expanding broadly becomes an MRO catalogue against established distributors. Local EU stock helps but does not rescue wealth.

## Fatal Objections

1. Installed orphan volume too small.
2. Food-contact/FTO/liability burden.
3. Generic molders copy high-volume SKUs.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Use customer deposits to turn abandoned exact-part demand into owned long-tail tooling and inventory.

kill
