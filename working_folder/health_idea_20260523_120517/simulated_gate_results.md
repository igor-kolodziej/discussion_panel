## Passing Candidate

**IVF Carrier-Screening Consent & Routing Layer** scored **91/100**.

Subagent caveats:

- Clinic sales may be slower than 2 months for an unknown founder.
- Medical/genetic trust is the main adoption barrier.
- Clinics may resist a neutral layer if labs already subsidize education.
- Per-couple pricing must avoid looking like an upsell tax.
- Defensibility depends on owning cross-clinic workflow standards, not documents.

Why it passed the simulated gate:

- Concrete control point: one clinic's routed IVF genetic-add-on intake workflow.
- 2-month proof path: 20 routed couples, one reserved geneticist reviewer, measured time saved, consent completion, escalation count, and at least 5 paid fees.
- Better economics than the EEG/elderly finalists: recurring patient flow, low marginal COGS after template setup, potential per-cycle or retainer revenue, and no field-operations burden.
- Underused momentum: Polish national IVF funding increases IVF flow, while genetic carrier screening / PGT decisions remain sensitive and operationally fragmented.

## Killed Or Deferred Shapes

- Pediatric Sleep-EEG Fill-Rate Rail was strong but too copyable unless transformed into a broader recovered-slot guarantee with proprietary standby lists and cross-lab benchmarks.
- CareProof was strong but too much like insurance until a complaint/inspection crisis makes urgency obvious.
- Trial-Ready Epilepsy Packets had stronger upside but weak distribution, slow buyer sales cycle, and GDPR/trust friction.
- BRCA routing had policy momentum but could be absorbed by clinics/labs and has uncertain payer urgency.
- Senior discharge bridge has real pain but is service-heavy, margin-thin, and vulnerable to home-care agencies.
