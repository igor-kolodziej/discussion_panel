## Finalist: DoseRoute Dishwasher Dosing Pump Base Roll-Up

### Exact Scored Idea

**DoseRoute Dishwasher Dosing Pump Base Roll-Up** acquires or options one tiny local HoReCa/professional-cleaning chemical distributor's installed base of dishwasher chemical dosing pumps in Polish restaurants, hotels, bakeries, canteens, cafes, and catering kitchens. The target asset is not "chemical sales" in general. It is the phone/domain/Google profile, customer list, installed pump register, pump ownership records, dishwasher model notes, dosing settings, water-hardness notes, review dates, recurring invoices, chemical SKU history, spare pump/tube stock, and 30-day owner handoff.

DoseRoute then sells continuity bundles: pump service, emergency pump swap, detergent/rinse-aid monthly replenishment, dosage recalibration, and simple HACCP-friendly cleaning-log reminders. The wedge is the moment when an old rep/operator retires, stops servicing pumps, or cannot reliably supply chemicals; the kitchen already has a pump installed and needs compatible chemical supply without staff guessing dosage.

Buyer: independent restaurants, bakeries, hotels, small catering kitchens, cafes, canteens, and small food processors with professional dishwashers and installed detergent/rinse-aid pumps.

Acute trigger: dishes come out dirty, the pump tube fails, detergent runs out before weekend service, kitchen changes dishwasher/chemistry/water softener, hygiene audit pressure, or the current supplier/installer disappears.

Primary control point: installed dosing pumps and customer-site chemical setup, plus signed route-book option and customer continuity authorizations. The POC only counts pumps where DoseRoute can prove ownership/custody, settings, SKU history, and customer acceptance of monthly supply or prepaid refill credits.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN:

- sign one 60-day option/earnout over an operator with 40-120 active installed pumps;
- secure phone/domain/Google profile/inbox forwarding and owner transition notice;
- verify 50 installed pumps with photos, customer contacts, pump model, chemical SKU, and last invoice;
- obtain 25 customer continuity authorizations;
- collect 15 prepaid refill/service credits of 300-1,200 PLN;
- complete 30 chemical deliveries/refills and 5 paid pump tube/service/swap events;
- prove >40% gross margin after chemicals, delivery, tubes, subcontract technician time, and refunds;
- convert 10 sites to monthly minimum supply agreements or standing refill orders.

Budget: 10k-25k PLN seller option, 15k-30k PLN chemical and spare-parts stock, 10k-20k PLN pump/tube reserve, 5k-10k PLN insurance/legal/SDS/safety review, 10k-20k PLN delivery/contractor buffer.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: small local cleaning-chemical reps and micro-distributors often own customer relationships and pump placements but lack succession, software, route discipline, or capital. A low-upfront option plus earnout is attractive to a retiring seller because chemical margins continue without them doing deliveries. Customers are reached through the seller's existing phone and owner notice, not cold ads.

Why it is not a generic service/marketplace/dashboard: the control point is physical installed pumps plus recurring chemical consumption and pump settings at customer sites. DoseRoute owns or controls the installed-base ledger, refill schedule, pump-service route, and prepaid refill credits.

## Simulated Zero To One Review

Score: **89/100**

Control-point tier: **Tier A-: installed device base plus recurring consumable transaction flow.**

Why it clears `>87`:

- The physical asset sits at the customer site and creates repeated consumption, not one-off reorders.
- The POC can prove control with photos, pump ownership/custody, customer continuity authorizations, prepaid refill credits, and delivered chemistry inside 60 days.
- Gross margin and payback are testable quickly because chemical refills recur weekly/monthly.
- CAC starts from the acquired route book and owner handoff, not paid ads.
- Incumbents can copy the category, but they cannot copy the exact installed base, phone/domain, pump settings, and customer purchase habit without replacing pumps or winning each site.
- The founder's data/ops edge matters: route density, reorder reminders, pump failure prediction, SKU standardization, and seller-book scoring.

Strongest objections:

- Large incumbents and local janitorial suppliers can compete; the first beachhead must be under-served independent HoReCa, not hotel chains already served by Ecolab-like vendors.
- Pump ownership and customer contracts must be clean; if the pump belongs to the restaurant or another supplier, the control point weakens.
- Chemicals require SDS, safe handling, liability insurance, and staff instructions, though this is much less complex than medical or pressure equipment.
- Restaurants churn; the route must prove standing refill orders, not only emergency deliveries.
- Field operations may be annoying for a data-science founder; contractor/delivery support is needed from day one.

Decision: **advance to real Zero To One validation.**
