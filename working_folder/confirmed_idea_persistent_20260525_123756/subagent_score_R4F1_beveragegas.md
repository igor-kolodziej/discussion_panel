**Score: 83/100**

**Control-Point Tier: Tier 2**
Acquired local distribution asset: deployed certified cylinders, deposit ledger, customer phone habit, invoices, refill terms, and seller handoff. This is meaningfully stronger than starting a cylinder-delivery service from scratch, but it is not a monopoly. The control is route-specific, not category-wide.

**Diagnosis**
This is a good boring-business wedge with real acute pain: venues lose sales immediately when beverage gas runs out. The idea is strongest when treated as an acquisition-first route roll-up, not a greenfield “buy cylinders and deliver gas” startup. The 2-month proof path is concrete and realistically securable if a tiny retiring/operator-owned route can be found, so I would not cap it for lack of proof path. I do cap it below 85 because existing gas/fire-safety/gastro suppliers can copy the “scheduled never-run-out + emergency” service layer for their own customers.

**Strongest Copy Risk**
A local incumbent already delivering gas cylinders around Warsaw adds scheduled swaps, weekend SLA, leak checks, and two-cylinder changeover packages as an internal process change. They cannot instantly copy your acquired phone number, deposit ledger, and deployed float, but they can copy the offer and defend their customer base.

**Strongest Objections / Caveats**
- The hardest proof item is not customer demand; it is finding a legitimate micro-route under `70,000 PLN` upfront with clean invoices, real active customers, and reconciled cylinder deposits.
- Cylinder ledgers can be messy: missing bottles, expired certification, disputed deposits, customers who think cylinders are theirs, or accounts that only bought once.
- `20,000+ PLN cash from 30 deliveries` implies roughly `667 PLN` per delivery unless upgrades/deposits are included. That must be separated into revenue, deposits, VAT, and one-time accessory sales.
- Weekend emergency delivery conflicts with part-time solo founder constraints unless there is driver/operator coverage from day one.
- Safety/compliance is not cosmetic: food-grade CO2/E290, pressure cylinder inspection, transport, leak checks, and liability need a compliant supplier/insurer setup. EU food-additive rules cover E290, and UDT/inspection obligations around pressure equipment are a real diligence item.
- Supplier dependence is material. If refill terms worsen or cylinders are unavailable before peak weekends, the “never-run-out” promise breaks.

**Economics View**
At 30 active customers and 60 cylinders, this is probably a proof route, not yet a strong owner-earnings business. The economics become attractive only if swaps are dense, scheduled, and priced as continuity rather than commodity gas. A credible acquisition should be mostly earnout/seller-note: pay for retained active customers, reconciled certified cylinders, working phone/domain transfer, and 60-90 day revenue persistence.

The 35% gross margin target is plausible after refill, delivery labor, and depreciation if routes are clustered and emergency jobs carry premium fees. It is not credible if the route is scattered, labor is ad hoc, or deposits/accessory sales are counted as operating margin.

**Pursue / Pivot / Kill**
**Pursue, narrowly.** Only pursue as an optioned/acquired micro-route with verified customers and cylinder ledger. Kill the greenfield version. The first 30 days should be route sourcing and diligence; if no credible seller appears under the capital constraint, pivot away.
