Evaluate this business idea. Rate it from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Please cap the score below 82 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under the constraints below. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, gross margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and owner earnings more heavily than originality.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist/software-capable operator; can start part-time with contractors; max 100,000 PLN before proof; no celebrity/influencer access; no privileged marketplace/platform introductions; strong analytical/data/software diligence skills; can cold outbound to plugin authors, small agencies, Polish/CEE e-commerce software houses, e-commerce integrator freelancers, and merchants.

Idea: Prepaid ERP Connector Succession Escrow.

This is a seller-mediated continuity acquisition for neglected Polish/CEE e-commerce operations connectors. It fixes the common dead-plugin failure by requiring customer re-consent and prepaid maintenance cash before closing the acquisition.

Buyer: Polish/CEE merchants already using a small connector between their e-commerce platform and operational systems such as warehouse/order sync, COD reconciliation, invoice export, order-status synchronization, accounting exports, Subiekt/Comarch ERP Optima/enova365-style ERP workflows, Fakturownia-style invoicing workflows, or custom local operations modules. The first target must be a legacy, messy, high-friction operations connector where replacement is painful and not already well solved by Base/BaseLinker, official free integrations, or a cheap standard module.

Acute trigger: the connector's creator is tired, inactive, retiring, overloaded, or planning to stop support. Merchants risk losing order, invoice, warehouse, accounting, COD reconciliation, or operational sync workflow after platform/API/PHP/ERP changes. Rebuilding or migrating under pressure is more expensive than prepaying continuity while the original seller still has trust and customer access.

Primary control point: the founder does not buy a connector based on free installs, historical downloads, or assumed renewals. The founder signs a conditional acquisition option with the seller, then runs a seller-endorsed customer continuity campaign before close.

Control exists only if all of these are secured:
- signed option over source code, IP, repo, release/update credentials, domain/docs, license server where applicable, support inbox, issue history, implementation notes, and narrow non-compete;
- seller-mediated notice to named active customers;
- explicit customer re-consent to data/support transfer;
- prepaid 12-month maintenance or transition-support contracts from named merchants;
- closing condition that customer cash/re-consent must clear before the acquisition completes;
- support terms limiting included work to connector compatibility/security/API maintenance, with bespoke store debugging, old PHP migrations, server/theme/plugin conflicts, custom checkout work, and accounting cleanup separately paid.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace wrapper: the startup controls source/update/support assets plus signed prepaid merchant contracts. It is not selling advice, a dashboard, a list, or a marketplace. It closes only when payment flow and asset custody are locked.

60-day proof under 100,000 PLN:
- Build a screened list of 40-60 Polish/CEE ERP/accounting/order-operation connectors and the agencies/freelancers behind them.
- Reject generic payment gateway plugins, broad shipping plugins, free-only plugins, generic WooCommerce-to-Fakturownia invoicing, and any workflow already well covered by Base/BaseLinker, official/free provider integrations, or a cheap Sfera/API integrator.
- Sign one conditional option with a seller for under 20,000 PLN upfront option fee plus seller earnout paid only from re-consented customer cash.
- Seller sends continuity notice to at least 30 named active customers.
- Secure at least 20 customer re-consents and at least 25,000 PLN prepaid maintenance/transition-support cash before closing.
- Transfer repo/update/support assets only after cash/re-consent threshold is met.
- Ship one scoped compatibility/security/API patch within 14 days of close.
- Document support-ticket distribution showing that at least 70% of issues are connector-level maintenance, not bespoke store debugging.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to small agencies, freelancers, and module authors using a seller-friendly offer:
"You have an annoying support tail. I will not ask you to sell blind. Sign a conditional option, introduce me to active customers under your endorsement, and you get a fixed option fee plus earnout only if customers prepay continuity."

Screening sources:
- Polish PrestaShop/WooCommerce module shops and small e-commerce agencies;
- GitHub repos and support pages for local ERP/accounting/order-operation connectors;
- Polish e-commerce groups/forums with "not updated", "sync broken", "developer disappeared", or "ERP export stopped working" complaints;
- module shops with stale changelogs but existing paid support history.

Economics:
- Option fee: 5,000-20,000 PLN.
- Seller earnout: 20-35% of collected re-consented maintenance/support revenue for 12-24 months.
- Customer offer: 1,000-2,500 PLN per merchant for 12 months of compatibility/security/API maintenance and transition support, excluding bespoke store fixes.
- Proof target: 20 merchants x average 1,250 PLN = 25,000 PLN before close, covering option/legal/diligence and reducing acquisition risk.
- Developer patch cost: 5,000-15,000 PLN for the first scoped fix if codebase is patchable.
- Payback is immediate if prepaid customer cash exceeds the option fee and first patch cost.
- Gross margins should be software-like only if support scope is enforced and the target is one reusable connector, not custom per-merchant work.

Defensibility:
A freelancer can offer replacement support, but cannot instantly copy the seller-endorsed handoff, prepaid re-consented customer contracts, update/release channel, issue history, version-specific implementation notes, support inbox, and the fact that customers already paid to keep the current connector alive.

Repeatability:
After the first acquisition, repeat the same conditional-option and prepaid-continuity process across adjacent neglected local operations connectors. Do not roll up commodity plugins; only acquire books where customers prepay before close and ticket history proves reusable connector-level maintenance.

Kill criteria:
- No seller with named active paying customers signs a conditional option within 45 days.
- Seller cannot legally introduce customers or transfer source/update/support assets.
- Fewer than 20 named customers re-consent and prepay at least 25,000 PLN before close.
- Customers say they can cheaply migrate to Base/BaseLinker, official integrations, an active standard plugin, or one Sfera/API integrator.
- The support history shows mostly bespoke store debugging or accounting cleanup rather than connector-level maintenance.
- The first patch requires a rewrite rather than a scoped compatibility/security/API update.
- Annual maintenance willingness is below 1,000 PLN/customer.
- The acquisition requires meaningful upfront cash before customer prepayment clears.
