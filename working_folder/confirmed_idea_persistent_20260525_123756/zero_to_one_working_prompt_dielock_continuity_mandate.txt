Idea: DieLock Continuity Mandate.

DieLock is a Poland-first micro-roll-up of orphan industrial production memory. It targets retiring or succession-blocked gasket/rubber-cutting workshops, but it does not buy the whole archive first. It signs a 60-day exclusive option with the owner covering phone/domain use, owner introductions, non-compete, selective custody of dies/templates/CAD/material notes, and earnout terms. During the option period, the old owner jointly contacts active/recent customers whose machines still use those parts.

DieLock asks each customer to sign a continuity mandate authorizing transfer/custody of that customer's historical production files, dies/templates, material notes, and reorder history, and to pay a non-refundable 300-1,500 PLN reorder credit that is applied to their next order or urgent slot. Only customer-mandated archive assets are acquired.

Buyer: plant maintenance/procurement managers, machine shops, food/packaging manufacturers, farms, local MRO suppliers, and small industrial operators whose machines still depend on exact recurring cut gaskets, pads, washers, spacers, felt/PTFE/rubber profiles, or old MRO parts made by a retiring local shop.

Acute trigger: the trusted old workshop is retiring/closing, the customer may lose the only fast reorder source for an odd machine part, or a maintenance window is approaching and the original die/spec/material note must not disappear.

Hard control point: signed customer continuity mandates plus prepaid reorder credits, combined with a seller option over the relevant phone/domain, physical dies/templates, CAD/scan files, material notes, and owner handoff. The customer mandate legally and commercially routes future reorders to DieLock before the founder spends serious capital.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one 60-day seller option for 5,000-15,000 PLN plus earnout; jointly contact 40 active/recent customers; secure 12 customer-signed continuity mandates; collect 8 paid reorder credits/deposits of 300-1,500 PLN; acquire/custody only the mandated files/dies/templates; complete 5 paid reorders at 500-5,000 PLN with >45% gross margin through subcontract cutters or a leased/simple press; get 2 standing reorder agreements. Exclude certified, high-pressure, medical, aerospace, or safety-critical parts unless specs, insurance, and professional-use responsibility are explicit.

Distribution: first seller acquisition is succession outreach to micro gasket/rubber/cutting shops; first customer acquisition is the old owner introduction plus a concrete "do not lose your die/spec/source" continuity offer. No vague partnerships are required. Subcontract cutting is commodity and is tested after paid reorder credits.

Why this is not a generic service, marketplace, report, dashboard, or database: DieLock does not merely list suppliers or offer generic cutting. It controls customer-authorized production memory, physical dies/templates, files, material notes, and prepaid reorder flow for named parts before production work begins.
