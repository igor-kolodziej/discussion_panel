Founder constraints: unknown solo founder in Poland, max 100,000 PLN starting capital, no privileged introductions, part-time at first, must prove a hard control point in <=2 months, must solve cold start with concrete acquisition, and must have plausible gross margins, willingness to pay, CAC/payback, retention/repeatability, and owner earnings.

Important cap: cap the score below 82 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under those constraints. Also be skeptical of generic services, reports, dashboards, databases, marketplaces, or partner-dependent coordination.

Scored idea wording:

DomainDeadman DNS Renewal Book Roll-Up.

Buyer: Polish SMBs whose website, email, DNS, SSL, ecommerce, or business-critical domain is informally managed by a retiring webmaster, tiny hosting reseller, old WordPress agency, freelancer, or local IT person.

Acute trigger: the domain, email, DNS, SSL, hosting, or website renewal can fail because the original webmaster is retiring, unreachable, ill, closing the business, or holds the only registrar/hosting/admin accounts. The buyer often does not know what registrar, DNS zone, mailbox, SSL certificate, backups, CMS admin, or renewal calendar keeps the business online until something expires or breaks.

Product wedge: option or acquire one retiring webmaster or micro-hosting reseller's active client book, then convert it into a continuity workflow: domain/hosting account inventory, renewal calendar, DNS/SSL/email map, admin recovery, registrant confirmation, renewal invoicing, and emergency access handoff. The founder does not cold-sell generic IT support. He acquires a live book where renewal-critical assets, client contacts, invoices, panels, phone/email/domain routing, and seller handoff already exist.

Primary control point: a signed option/acquisition agreement covering the seller's active client book, reseller/registrar/hosting panel access where transferable, DNS/SSL/email/hosting records, renewal calendar, active invoices, phone/email/domain routing, non-compete, and 30-day owner handoff. A client counts as controlled only after written consent, registrar/hosting access migration, delegated admin access, or first renewal/continuity invoice paid through the founder-controlled workflow.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one option/earnout with a retiring webmaster, tiny hosting reseller, or micro-agency covering 30-150 clients; move or delegate 20 domains/hosting/DNS/SSL assets into a founder-controlled renewal workflow with client consent; collect 10 continuity or renewal invoices; fix 3 access/renewal/SSL/email rescue issues; document that seller phone/email/domain routing now sends urgent renewals to the founder-controlled desk.

Cold start acquisition: supply-side outreach to retiring webmasters, small hosting resellers, tiny WordPress agencies, local IT freelancers, business-sale listings, and accountants who know retiring one-person operators. Demand-side CAC is inherited through the acquired book, phone/email/domain routing, active renewal invoices, and seller handoff. No vague partnership or influencer distribution is needed.

Economics: annual domain/hosting/SSL/email renewal margins, 500-2,500 PLN continuity setup and admin-recovery fees, 99-499 PLN/month care plans for critical businesses, and upsells for backup, DNSSEC, email deliverability, security cleanup, and hosting migration. Capex is low; acquisition can be earnout-based; cash conversion is fast because renewal and rescue invoices are near-term. Payback target: recover option fee and first contractor/admin cost from the first 10-20 renewal/continuity invoices.

Why it is not a generic service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace: the company must control actual admin access, registrar/hosting/DNS/SSL records, renewal calendars, phone/email/domain routing, active invoices, and client payment flow from an existing operator book. The scarce resource is continuity custody over renewal-critical internet assets that SMB owners do not understand and cannot easily reconstruct under deadline pressure, not software or advice.

Main risks: client consent and GDPR/data processing must be handled cleanly; registrar or hosting reseller access may not be transferable; some clients may churn when the personal webmaster relationship ends; large registrars/MSPs/agencies can offer similar managed services; the founder must avoid becoming a low-margin helpdesk by only acquiring books with renewal invoices, admin custody, and paid continuity fees.
