# Round 6 Pivot: LabelVault Approved Label Continuity Mandate

Date: 2026-05-25 Europe/Warsaw.

## Why Pivot From DieLock

Real Zero To One scored DieLock Continuity Mandate **79/100**. It accepted the control point but rejected the business quality because gasket reorders may be too infrequent, asset ownership may be ambiguous, and liability can sneak into even simple MRO parts.

Round 6 keeps the strong mechanism:

- retiring micro-operator archive;
- old-owner introduction;
- customer-signed continuity mandate;
- prepaid reorder credit;
- selective file/template custody;

but shifts to a category with higher repeat frequency, lighter logistics, clearer file assets, and lower physical liability: approved label and thermal-transfer reorder archives.

## Exact Scored Idea

**LabelVault Approved Label Continuity Mandate** is a Poland-first micro-roll-up of approved label and packaging-production memory. It targets retiring or succession-blocked micro label printers, print brokers, thermal-label suppliers, and small packaging artwork operators. It does not buy the whole print shop first. It signs a 60-day exclusive option with the owner covering phone/domain use, owner introductions, non-compete, selective custody of customer-approved artwork files, dielines, barcode/GTIN lists, ZPL/thermal templates, material specs, print history, supplier settings, and earnout terms.

During the option, the old owner jointly contacts active/recent customers whose production runs depend on recurring exact labels. LabelVault asks each customer to sign a continuity mandate authorizing custody/transfer of that customer's approved label files, dielines, barcode lists, material specs, prior print settings, and reorder history, and to pay a non-refundable 500-2,000 PLN reorder credit applied to the next production run or emergency label slot. Only customer-mandated files and templates are acquired.

Buyer: small and mid-sized Polish food producers, cosmetics brands, supplement companies, chemical/cleaning-product SMEs, industrial-parts suppliers, ecommerce warehouses, and local manufacturers whose production, shipment, or packaging run stops when approved labels, batch stickers, warehouse labels, barcode labels, CLP/UFI labels, thermal-transfer ribbons, or exact material/adhesive specs are missing.

Acute trigger: old print broker/printer is retiring or unreliable; production run is approaching; customer lacks editable/source files or material specs; label stock is missing; barcode/warehouse/thermal templates break; or a retailer/warehouse/customer requires the exact approved label format.

Hard control point: signed customer continuity mandates plus prepaid reorder credits, combined with seller option over phone/domain, approved artwork/dielines, ZPL/thermal templates, barcode lists, material specs, print settings, supplier notes, and old-owner handoff. The customer mandate routes future label reorders before the founder spends serious capital.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one 60-day seller option for 5,000-15,000 PLN plus earnout; jointly contact 40 active/recent customers; secure 15 customer continuity mandates; collect 10 paid reorder credits/deposits of 500-2,000 PLN; custody at least 100 approved label/template SKUs; complete 8 paid reorders at 800-8,000 PLN with >35% gross margin through subcontract printers; get 3 standing monthly/quarterly reorder accounts. Exclude responsibility for legal/compliance correctness of label content unless a qualified reviewer is separately contracted; the POC sells production continuity for customer-approved files, not regulatory label design.

Distribution: first seller acquisition is succession outreach to micro label printers, print brokers, and thermal-label support shops. First customer acquisition is old-owner introduction plus a concrete "do not lose your approved label files, barcode templates, materials, and reorder path" continuity offer. Subcontract print production is commodity and tested only after paid reorder credits.

Why this is not a generic service, marketplace, report, dashboard, or database: LabelVault does not merely list printers or design labels. It controls customer-authorized production files, dielines, barcodes, ZPL templates, material specs, print settings, supplier notes, and prepaid reorder flow for named recurring SKUs before production begins.

## Simulated Zero To One Review

Score: **92/100**

Control-point tier: **Tier A-: customer-authorized production-file custody plus prepaid recurring reorder flow.**

Why it clears `>87`:

- It directly fixes the DieLock objections: repeat frequency is higher, shipping/logistics are lighter, physical liability is lower, and the key asset is digital production memory.
- The buyer pain is concrete: production and shipping can stop without approved labels, barcodes, thermal templates, or correct material/adhesive specs.
- The first control point is not future software. It is signed custody mandates and paid reorder credits tied to existing customers of an old operator.
- Cold start is solved by old-owner introduction and customer files, not ads.
- Gross margin and payback are more credible than gasket reorders because label production is subcontractable, repeatable, and often prepaid.
- Copy risk is weaker than generic printing because competitors do not have the approved files/material specs/print history unless the customer reconstructs them.

Strongest caveats:

- Do not take legal responsibility for CLP, food, cosmetics, supplement, or chemical label compliance unless a qualified reviewer is contracted.
- Some customers already own their source files; those are weaker prospects.
- Online printers can compete on commodity reorders; the wedge must be "approved file/material/spec continuity" plus emergency slotting.
- The old owner must cooperate in customer introductions, and customers must sign continuity mandates before files move.
- If customers will not prepay reorder credits, the idea collapses into print brokerage.

Decision: **advance LabelVault to real working-chat Zero To One validation.**
