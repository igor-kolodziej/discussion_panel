## Repeated Fatal Objections

1. Backup/escrow mechanics can be copied by existing service firms unless the startup controls a signed source-release route before incumbents notice.
2. Factory trust is a serious obstacle because PLC/HMI source, passwords, restore files, and engineering images are sensitive operational and cyber assets.
3. The POC must prove customer-authorized custody and legal release, not just possession of an old laptop or integrator archive.
4. Recurring custody revenue is not guaranteed; some factories may prefer a one-time handoff to their existing maintenance firm.

## Pivot Lesson

The strongest shape is still PLC/automation continuity, but it must become a transaction-control and legal-release product:

- the retiring integrator gets paid only when a factory signs a source-release/continuity agreement;
- the factory receives a cyber-safe third-party custody pack, not merely file backup;
- the startup controls the first-call emergency restore workflow by contract for a defined period;
- POC proof must include signed customer authorization, paid release deposits, verified project open, checksum/custody logs, and at least one annual or quarterly restore SLA.

Next round should test whether adding paid release rights, chain-of-custody, and first-call restore SLA raises the score above 87, or whether the whole PLC continuity shape remains capped.
