# Round 35 Candidate Set: Mobile App Store Asset Acquisitions

Created: 2026-05-25

## Why This Pivot

Real validation punished the last two territories for labor-heavy delivery and target scarcity:

- connector assets had good control design but scarce qualified targets;
- accounting books had real client-book control but too much labor/liability.

Round 35 tests a cleaner digital asset:

- App Store / Google Play app ownership transfer;
- existing users, ratings, reviews, store rank, subscriptions, and IAP/ad revenue;
- source code and analytics/revenue stack;
- low support burden if the app is a narrow utility/content app;
- acquisition proof through actual transferred platform revenue.

This is not a greenfield app build or generic app idea. The only acceptable control point is transferred app-store ownership plus live revenue/users.

## Candidate 1: Polish Exam/Certification App Store Asset Buyout

### Buyer

Polish candidates preparing for practical paid/licensed exams and vocational certifications, such as SEP G1/G2/G3, UDT forklift/crane/loader, ADR, transport qualification, real-estate/admin exams, medical/nursing continuing education niches, drone licenses, or other repeatable question-bank certifications.

### Acute Trigger

Candidates pay because they need to pass a scheduled exam or maintain a qualification for work. Existing apps with rankings, reviews, question banks, and subscribers can keep organic app-store demand even if the indie developer is tired or under-maintaining updates.

### Primary Control Point

Acquire a live exam-prep app asset, not build from zero:

- iOS/Android app transfer where platform criteria allow;
- source code and backend;
- app-store listing, ratings, reviews, screenshots, store rank, keywords;
- IAP/subscription/ad revenue;
- question-bank/content license or content ownership;
- analytics/revenue access such as App Store Connect, Google Play Console, RevenueCat, AdMob, Firebase;
- support inbox and push/email channels;
- seller non-compete in the narrow exam category.

Apple and Google both document app-transfer processes where store listing/reviews/users/subscriptions can continue or transfer under platform rules.

### Two-Month Proof

Within 60 days and under 100,000 PLN:

- screen 100 Polish/CEE exam/certification apps using App Store, Google Play, Sensor Tower/Appfigures-style estimates, reviews, update recency, ad/subscription clues, and direct owner outreach;
- reject pure free/no-revenue apps and categories with official dominant free apps;
- sign one LOI for a small but live app with at least 300-1,500 PLN/month verified net revenue or 10,000+ organic monthly downloads plus a realistic monetization fix;
- acquisition structure: no more than 40,000 PLN upfront, with seller note/earnout tied to retained platform revenue;
- platform transfer of app listing, users/ratings/reviews where allowed, source code, content rights, RevenueCat/AdMob/Firebase/analytics, and support channels;
- ship one maintenance/content/update release within 14 days of transfer;
- collect the first transferred payout or verify subscription/IAP/ad revenue under buyer control;
- document payback model and support hours.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Outbound to Polish/CEE indie developers with:

- old changelogs;
- app reviews asking for updates;
- niche exam apps with rankings but low polish;
- one-person app portfolios;
- no recent marketing;
- apps using outdated UI/SDKs but still getting organic demand.

Seller pitch:

"Your app still has users and reviews but needs maintenance. I can buy the app listing/source/content and pay a small upfront plus earnout from retained revenue."

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

- App-store revenue has high gross margin after Apple/Google fees, content updates, and occasional developer maintenance.
- A 300-1,500 PLN/month app is not a company alone, but a 5-10 app portfolio in adjacent certification categories can become meaningful if acquisition multiples are low and support is shared.
- Proof must show the first app can be transferred and monetized without bespoke customer support.

### Copy Risk

Competitors can build another app, but cannot instantly copy:

- existing app-store ranking;
- reviews/ratings;
- installed base;
- active subscribers/IAP buyers;
- app listing history;
- push/update channel;
- content pack if owned/licensed.

### Why It Is Not A Service/Report/Dashboard/Marketplace Wrapper

The startup controls app-store assets and platform revenue directly. It is not selling consulting or building a marketplace.

### Kill Criteria

- No transferable app with live revenue signs under 40,000 PLN upfront plus earnout within 45 days.
- App transfer fails under Apple/Google rules.
- Content/question-bank rights cannot transfer.
- Revenue is under 300 PLN/month and downloads cannot be monetized.
- Support/content-update burden exceeds app revenue.
- Official/free apps dominate the exam category.
- Retention or conversion is too weak to justify acquisition.

## Candidate 2: Local Utility Subscription App Roll-Up

Acquire narrow Polish/CEE utility apps with live subscriptions/IAP, such as parking zone helpers, public-service reminders, tax deadline reminders, PDF/form tools, or local compliance utilities. Reject categories with platform-native replacement or low willingness to pay.

## Candidate 3: Ad-Supported Niche Calculator App Portfolio

Acquire small Android utility/calculator apps with existing organic traffic and AdMob revenue. Control is Google Play listing, users, reviews, and ad account transfer/reintegration. Risk: low ARPU and ad-policy/platform risk.

## Candidate 4: Abandoned Language/Flashcard App With Paid Users

Acquire a small paid language/flashcard app with subscriptions and content rights. Risk: crowded market and high churn.

## Candidate 5: Professional Reference App Continuity Buyout

Acquire a narrow professional reference app used by nurses, construction workers, electricians, or transport workers. Control is app listing, content rights, subscriptions, and reviews. Risk: regulated content accuracy/liability.

## Candidate 6: Kill-The-Territory Control Variant

If scoring says mobile apps are merely generic micro-acquisitions with poor target economics under 100,000 PLN, kill the territory. Do not lower the bar because the app-transfer control point is real.

## Source Anchors

- Apple App Store Connect documents app transfer and says transferred apps can retain reviews/ratings and users continue receiving updates.
- Google Play documents app transfer and says users, statistics, data, comments, ratings, subscriptions, and related app assets transfer to the new account.
- Mobile-app acquisition marketplaces show apps are traded as digital assets, but valuation multiples can exceed the 100,000 PLN proof budget.

## Gate Rule For Scorers

Apply the existing persistent-loop caps:

- cap below `82` if no concrete 2-month control proof;
- cap below `85` if copyable by one vendor contract, equipment purchase, or internal process;
- cap below `87` if CAC, payback, margins, owner earnings, target sourcing, transferability, or platform risk are not credible;
- require simulated score strictly `>87` to reach the real Zero To One browser gate.
