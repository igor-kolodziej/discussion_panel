## Key Objections From Zero To One

- The hard asset is real: old reorder paths, customer memory, dies/files/material notes, phone/domain, and owner handoff.
- The idea is still capped because the central uncertainty is whether the seller's customer book, records, phone/domain, and owner trust can be legally transferred and commercially reactivated within 2 months by an unknown part-time founder.
- Custom gasket cutting is already fairly commoditized in Poland/EU through CNC, die-cutting, waterjet, scanning, and one-off providers; the production method is not defensible.
- Customer goodwill may not transfer from the retiring operator to the new founder.
- Customer and invoice records need GDPR/legal-basis handling, data minimization, customer notice, and a clean B2B continuity rationale.
- Historical invoices may not show repeatable current demand, real margin, technical specs, or whether customers still run the machines.
- Small urgent orders can destroy owner time if quoting, material confirmation, subcontractor coordination, shipping, and rework are too manual.
- Liability can appear even in apparently simple non-certified MRO gaskets.

## Feedback To Carry Forward

Do not polish DieLock further unless the business changes materially. The real validator said it could move to `86-88` only after actual paid reactivation, clean legal transfer, repeat orders, and >45% real gross margin are proven. That makes it a promising POC but not a confirmed pre-POC idea under the current gate.

Next candidate should have a stronger pre-POC control point where the routed transaction flow is less dependent on personal goodwill transfer, or where the buyer's urgent regulatory/operational requirement forces earlier prepaid conversion.
