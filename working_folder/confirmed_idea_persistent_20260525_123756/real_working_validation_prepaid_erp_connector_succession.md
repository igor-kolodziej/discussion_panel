## Key Objections

- Target supply may be thin. The seller must have a painful connector, active named customers, enough trust to get prepayment, transferable source/update/support assets, patchable code, no strong official/BaseLinker-style replacement, and willingness to take a small option fee plus earnout.
- The 60-day proof is demanding: 20 re-consents plus 25,000 PLN prepaid before close while coordinating seller, customers, legal transfer, diligence, and first patch planning.
- The best niches are narrower than broad e-commerce connectors. Avoid payment gateways, shipping labels, parcel lockers, and obvious WooCommerce modules unless there is a specific neglected paid book.
- Support creep is the biggest margin killer. If merchants need PHP upgrades, theme fixes, custom checkout repair, accounting cleanup, server debugging, or one-off ERP mapping, the business becomes a small software agency.
- Merchant prepayment may reveal weak perceived value.
- Seller incentives need careful structuring because the seller must actively help with notices, re-consents, issue history, handoff calls, and implementation notes.
- Legal/data transfer is not a detail. Customer lists, support inboxes, license servers, issue histories, and implementation notes may include personal data, credentials, confidential information, or contractual restrictions.
