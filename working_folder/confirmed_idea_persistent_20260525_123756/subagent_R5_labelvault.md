**Control Tier:** B-/Tier 2. You can get temporary control through an optioned archive, approved files, seller handoff, non-compete, and customer custody agreements. But it is not deep control unless customers sign fresh custody/reorder contracts and subcontract printers are blocked from bypassing you.

**Diagnosis:** Stronger than a normal print broker because the asset is the approved reorder memory: files, materials, dielines, roll direction, UFI/CLP versions, barcode versions, order history. The pain is real: EU food and chemical labels carry mandatory information, and hazardous chemical products require CLP/UFI labelling discipline, so exact reorders matter commercially. ([europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-requirements/food-labelling/general-rules/indexamp_en.htm)) ([europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-requirements/chemicals/classification-labelling-packaging/indexamp_en.htm)) But the business is still copyable, operational, and probably too small unless each acquired archive has much more recurring spend than the POC target shows.

**Strongest Copy Risk:** one existing printer or broker can copy the whole offer: “we keep your approved files, guarantee emergency reorders, hold backup stock.” The seller, subcontract printer, or a packaging supplier can also bypass you unless you have enforceable non-solicit terms, exclusive customer custody, and fresh customer consent.

**Main Objections:**
- The POC proves demand, not attractive owner earnings. `40k PLN` revenue at `35%` gross margin is only `14k PLN` gross profit before owner time, errors, courier, prepress checks, accounting, and the option cost.
- If the `40k PLN` option fee is non-refundable, 60-day payback fails.
- `25 customers / 30 prepaid reorders` in 60 days is aggressive unless the archive is genuinely active and reorder cycles are short.
- Customer data and consents are a real diligence issue. EU GDPR processing needs a valid basis, and reuse for a changed purpose may require compatibility analysis or new consent. ([commission.europa.eu](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/rules-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data/grounds-processing/when-can-personal-data-be-processed_en)) ([commission.europa.eu](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/rules-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr/purpose-data-processing/can-we-use-data-another-purpose_en))
- File ownership may be messy: artwork, dielines, photos, fonts, regulatory label versions, and customer approvals may not all be transferable.
- Part-time founder fit is only moderate. The data/CRM side fits; urgent print execution, complaint handling, and supplier control are operations-heavy.

**Economics:** The model becomes interesting only if one archive has at least `150k-300k PLN` annual reorder revenue with `30-45%` gross margin, low refunds/reprints, and acquisition payback under `6-9 months`. The proposed POC should require `40k PLN revenue`, `14k+ gross profit`, `10k+ contribution profit after fulfillment/admin`, and at least `5-10 paid custody retainers`, not just verbal agreements.

**Verdict:** **Pursue as a constrained POC, not as a purchase.** Kill or pivot if you cannot get exclusive customer custody, clean file/data rights, and contribution-profit payback evidence within 60 days. Best pivot inside the same idea: narrower “regulated emergency reorder custody for chemicals/cosmetics labels” with paid retainers plus emergency premium, not generic label brokerage.
