Score: **84/100**

Tier: **Strong watchlist, not finalist-advance**

Copy risk: **Medium-high.** The beachhead is real, timely, and locally dense, but the control asset is fragile unless the founder gets exclusive or semi-exclusive pickup/settlement rights. Operators already sign with small shops, and by April 2026 Poland had 52k return points, including about 24k small-shop points, with over 40k manual-collection shops, so this is an active operational layer, not a theoretical market. Sources: Gov.pl launch note and April 2026 update: https://www.gov.pl/web/climate/the-deposit-refund-system-has-been-launched, https://www.gov.pl/web/klimat/cenne-suowce-wracaja-do-obiegu-za-nami-trzy-miesiace-pelnej-mocy-systemu-kaucyjnego

Objections:
- The buyer may be misidentified. Small shops want the pain removed, but the durable payer may be the system operator, not the shop.
- “Route rights” must be concrete: written, exclusive enough, tied to settlement, and hard for an operator to bypass.
- Manual counting plus pickup can become low-margin labor unless density is very high.
- RVM vendors, waste/logistics firms, or operators can copy the process once the route proves volume.
- The first 10 shops prove demand, but not domination unless the founder controls a whole dense district.

Economics:
- CAC is credible: door-to-door local selling to small stores is cheap and feasible for a solo Polish founder.
- Capex under 100k PLN is plausible for cages, bags, labels, handheld process, and basic transport if starting small.
- Payback is not yet proven. At 10k-30k units in 60 days, per-unit handling alone may be too thin unless paired with pickup subscription, operator-paid service fee, or settlement spread.
- Owner earnings become credible only around dense multi-shop routes with predictable pickup frequency, low failed trips, fast operator settlement, and 50-150+ stores per compact geography.

Verdict: **Do not advance.** It has a concrete 60-day control proof and should not be penalized merely for being unexecuted, but it is capped below 85 because one operator, vendor, or logistics contractor can absorb the route unless exclusivity and settlement control are real. Advance threshold would require signed exclusive district/store rights plus confirmed operator economics showing attractive owner earnings.
