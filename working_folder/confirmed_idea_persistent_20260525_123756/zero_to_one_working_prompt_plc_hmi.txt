Founder constraints: unknown solo founder in Poland, max 100,000 PLN starting capital, no privileged introductions, part-time at first, can use paid contractors, must prove a hard control point in <=2 months, must solve cold start with concrete acquisition, and must have plausible gross margins, willingness to pay, CAC/payback, retention/repeatability, and owner earnings.

Important cap: cap the score below 82 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under those constraints. Also cap below 85 if the core control point can be copied by one vendor contract, one equipment purchase, or one internal process change. Cap below 87 if CAC/payback, margins, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Be skeptical of generic services, reports, dashboards, databases, marketplaces, and commodity resale.

Scored idea wording:

PLC/HMI Line-Down Installed-Base Exchange Book.

Buyer: Polish factory maintenance heads, plant managers, machine builders, independent automation integrators, and maintenance teams in food, packaging, furniture, plastics, metalworking, logistics, and chemicals whose production lines still run on discontinued or aging PLC/HMI/I/O/drive modules.

Acute trigger: a line or machine is down, unstable, or at obvious obsolescence risk because an exact Siemens S7-300/ET200M module, legacy HMI, Omron/Lenze/Allen-Bradley PLC, I/O card, power supply, communication module, drive control board, memory card, or panel failed and a full migration is too slow, too risky, or not budget-approved. Siemens S7-300/ET200M type discontinuation became effective on 1 October 2025, while many plants still need bridge spares before migration.

Product wedge: do not build a generic spare-parts marketplace. Acquire/option one retiring or under-digitized Polish automation integrator's emergency stock and installed-base records, then combine it with founder-owned, bench-tested exact-SKU inventory. The inherited book should include service phone/domain routing, plant/customer contacts where lawful, machine/module lists, prior service tickets, programming-cable/software compatibility notes, firmware/version notes, decommissioned-stock sources, and 30-day owner support. The product sold to buyers is a fast emergency exchange/rental/reservation desk for exact modules already known to be installed in their machines.

Primary control point: a signed 60-90 day option/earnout covering:
1. 30-50 named PLC/HMI/I/O/drive/power-supply SKUs physically owned by the seller or acquired by the founder;
2. clean-title transfer, consignment, or first-refusal terms for those modules and future decommissioned stock;
3. bench-test videos, serial photos, firmware/revision logs, and condition tags for at least 20 modules;
4. the retiring integrator's service phone/domain/email routing, installed-base archive, customer/machine map, prior tickets, and 30-day owner handoff where lawful;
5. non-compete or first-refusal language for local obsolete-module emergency sourcing.

A module counts as controlled only when it is physically held or contractually reserved, clean-title/consigned, photographed by serial, bench-tested or failure-labeled, and listed with emergency sale/rental terms. A customer/account counts as controlled only after a paid emergency order, paid rental deposit, paid critical-spare reservation, or written buyer authorization to audit/reserve exact installed modules.

2-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- sign one stock/archive option with a retiring solo automation integrator, small machine-rebuild shop, plant maintenance clear-out seller, or industrial-surplus operator covering 30-50 exact SKUs plus installed-base/service records and owner handoff;
- acquire or reserve 30-50 modules and bench-test at least 20 with a paid automation contractor using a small test bench, programming cable/software access, power supply, and video proof;
- build a compatibility and risk map for the first 50-100 installed machines/modules from the inherited records and public outreach;
- collect at least 3 paid emergency sales, weekly rentals, repair-exchange deposits, or critical-spare reservation deposits from plants/integrators, each tied to a named SKU and plant/machine;
- keep total cash spend below 100,000 PLN by buying narrow SKUs first, using consignment/earnout where possible, and not buying broad unlabeled pallets.

Cold start acquisition:
- supply-side: cold-call retiring small automation integrators, independent Siemens/Omron/Lenze technicians, machine rebuild shops, plant closure/modernization sellers, Allegro/OLX industrial surplus sellers, and local factories with obsolete spare shelves; offer immediate cash for tested high-confidence SKUs plus earnout/consignment for the long tail and a reputation-preserving handoff for their old clients;
- demand-side: use the inherited installed-base records plus exact-SKU outreach, not generic ads: "You have 6ES7/legacy module X in line Y; we have a tested local emergency spare/rental and can reserve it for 60 days." Also list exact SKUs on Allegro/SEO and call automation integrators who need same-day Polish stock.

Economics:
- acquire modules from clear-outs/surplus/retiring integrators at roughly 200-2,500 PLN for common modules and selectively more for rare CPUs/HMIs/drives;
- sell urgent tested modules at 1,200-8,000+ PLN depending on SKU and scarcity, or charge 10-20% of replacement value per week as rental/bridge exchange with deposit;
- charge 500-2,000 PLN for installed-base audit/reservation setup and 300-1,500 PLN/month for a critical-spare reservation shelf for plants with named modules;
- target 40-70% gross margin on resale/rental after testing, shipping, returns reserve, and seller earnout. One 4,000-15,000 PLN urgent sale/rental can pay back several small inventory buys.

Why this can compound:
- each installed-base archive teaches which modules are actually still deployed, which SKUs fail, which plants postpone migration, and which integrators need local emergency stock;
- the control point is not a database or marketplace: it is physical custody of tested scarce modules plus inherited installed-base/service records and routed emergency demand;
- competitors such as Radwell, EU Automation, Gal-Industry, Automa.net, Allegro, and eBay prove demand but are not fatal if the wedge is local same-day Polish custody, bench-test proof, and plant-specific installed-base knowledge;
- the beachhead can expand from S7-300/ET200M and legacy HMI modules into drives, motion cards, power supplies, safety relays, industrial PCs, machine-specific memory cards, and migration-readiness audits.

Main risks to evaluate hard:
- commodity resale competition from global parts brokers and local surplus sellers;
- fake, damaged, stolen, locked, or wrong-firmware modules;
- warranty/returns and field-fit risk;
- cybersecurity and safety boundaries if touching source code or networked equipment;
- seller archive may not include lawful customer contact permissions;
- emergency frequency may be lower than expected unless reservation deposits convert.
