## Control-Point Tier

**Tier A-: owned/optioned exact-match inventory plus dentist-paid transaction gate.**

The control point is not "knowledge of implants" alone. It is the combination of:

- owned or optioned reusable legacy drivers, torque tips, scan bodies, impression copings, lab analogs, healing-cap ID samples, catalogs, and CAD libraries for 3-5 named systems;
- item-level photos, reference numbers, provenance notes, sterilization/no-use status, and compatibility mapping;
- professional-only rental/sale/search deposits, so urgent cases route through controlled inventory rather than a public directory;
- a contracted implant prosthetics technician who screens compatibility and no-go categories.

The tier is not full Tier A because a respected dental distributor or lab network could copy the model after proof. It is still strong enough for the gate because exact lots and options cannot be copied retroactively, and broad incumbents usually ignore low-volume orphan systems until a focused operator proves the demand.

## Strongest Copy Risk

The strongest copy risk is a dental lab network, implant distributor, or senior prosthodontist building an obsolete-system desk after seeing paid demand. They could buy some old kits, publish a compatibility table, and use existing clinician trust. The startup's defense is to secure fragmented asset options first, focus on 3-5 named systems rather than all implants, collect paid case deposits, and become the first-call Polish/CEE rescue bank for orphan implant tooling before incumbents decide the long tail is worth organizing.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **Dentist trust is the gating buyer risk.** Patients may be desperate, but dentists decide whether to use or rent a third-party-sourced tool or scan-body workflow. The first validation must be dentist-paid deposits, not patient interest.
- **MDR boundary must be disciplined.** The POC should exclude unsealed, expired, repackaged, unknown-provenance, or patient-sourced implantable components. A short regulatory memo and distributor/no-go checklist are mandatory before any transaction.
- **Asset quality may be weak.** Retiring dentists and labs may have incomplete kits, damaged items, or systems with no active demand. The KPI is usable exact matches for named systems, not total item count.
- **Demand is episodic.** This compounds only if dentists/labs submit repeated orphan cases and the bank expands by system/geography.
- **Clinical liability cannot be implied away.** The startup must provide inventory, references, provenance, and compatibility support; licensed dentists/labs make clinical decisions.
- **Founder needs a dental technical contractor.** A data scientist can build the catalog and acquisition engine, but not credibly triage implant-platform compatibility alone.
