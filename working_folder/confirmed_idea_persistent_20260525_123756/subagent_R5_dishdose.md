**Score: 80/100**

**Control Tier: B+**
Concrete 2-month control proof exists: option a route, inherit 25 active pump sites, run two order cycles, collect revenue, prove margin, convert prepaid accounts. That is strong enough to test cash flow. It is not strong enough to prove scalable acquisition, defensibility, or owner earnings beyond a micro-route.

**Diagnosis**
This is a solid boring-cashflow idea, not a true Zero-to-One monopoly idea. The pain is real: HoReCa operators need clean dishes, hygiene continuity, dosing reliability, and fast service when pumps fail. Recurring consumables plus installed pumps create mild switching friction. The ceiling is capped because any chemical supplier, gastro-equipment service company, or competent internal ops person can copy the bundle.

**Cap Applied**
Capped below 85 because the model is copyable by one vendor/equipment/service operator.

**Strongest Copy Risk**
A local chemical distributor or dishwasher service company can offer: free/cheap pump install, bundled detergent/rinse aid, periodic calibration, and emergency swap. They already have supplier terms, route density, repair capability, and customer trust. Your main defense is acquiring neglected micro-routes and operating them better, not product uniqueness.

**Economics**
The POC math is plausible but not yet exciting.

At 25 sites and 25k PLN over two cycles, you are implying about 500 PLN/site/month if cycles are monthly. If 25k is VAT-inclusive, net sales are closer to 20.3k PLN. At >40% gross margin after chemicals, pump amortization, and route labor, contribution is roughly 8k-10k PLN over two months, or 4k-5k PLN/month.

Against a 60k PLN route option price, payback is about 12-15 months before admin, tax, churn, bad debt, emergency replacements, and owner time. That is acceptable for a small acquisition, but not a slam dunk.

Public checks support plausibility: professional dishwasher liquids retail around 159-187 PLN net per 10L, and dosing pumps are publicly available around a few hundred PLN, so supply is easy and the moat is weak. Labor also matters: Poland’s 2026 minimum hourly service rate is 31.40 PLN, before real fully loaded route cost.

**Main Objections**
Route acquisition under 60k PLN may be the hardest assumption if the accounts are truly active and transferable.

Customer transfer risk is high: micro-HoReCa relationships may belong to the owner, not the route assets.

Part-time founder fit is weak unless service response is outsourced or tightly scheduled. Emergency pump swaps do not respect part-time availability.

Margin can disappear through low-density routes, unpaid invoices, excessive emergency calls, pump failures, and small-ticket deliveries.

Scaling customer acquisition after the first inherited 25 sites is unproven. Buying routes is CAC; door-to-door selling may be slow.

**Best Move: Pursue, But Only As A Controlled Acquisition POC**
Do the 2-month POC exactly as written, but add three kill criteria:

1. At least 20 of 25 accounts reorder without owner intervention.
2. Contribution margin stays >40% on net revenue, not VAT-inclusive receipts.
3. 10 prepaid accounts convert without discounting more than one month of margin.

If those fail, kill the roll-up. If they pass, narrow into dense Warsaw districts and buy/option only neglected routes where the seller’s weak operations, not weak demand, is the problem.
