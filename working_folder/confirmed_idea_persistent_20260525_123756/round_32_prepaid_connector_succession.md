# Round 32 Candidate Set: Prepaid Connector Succession

Created: 2026-05-25

## Round 31 Real-Gate Failure To Fix

The real Zero To One working chat scored Orphaned Polish E-Commerce Connector Payment-Flow Acquisition at `79/100`.

The strongest objections were:

- many stale connectors have free installs but no paid dependency;
- marketplace/payment/customer transfer is not automatic;
- official free connectors or BaseLinker/Base can compress broad shipping/payment niches;
- support can become bespoke store debugging;
- the founder may buy dead code before proving customer payment and re-consent.

Round 32 changes the shape from "acquire first, then convert renewals" to **seller-mediated prepaid continuity before acquisition close**.

## Candidate 1: Prepaid ERP Connector Succession Escrow

### Buyer

Polish/CEE merchants already using a small connector between their e-commerce platform and operational systems such as Subiekt, Comarch ERP Optima, enova365, Fakturownia, warehouse/order-sync tools, COD reconciliation, invoice export, order-status synchronization, or accounting exports.

### Acute Trigger

The connector's creator is tired, inactive, retiring, overloaded, or planning to stop support. Merchants risk losing order, invoice, warehouse, accounting, or COD reconciliation workflow after platform/API/PHP/ERP changes. Rebuilding or migrating under pressure is more expensive than prepaying continuity.

### Primary Control Point

The founder does not buy a connector based on free installs or assumed renewals. The founder signs a conditional acquisition option with the seller, then runs a seller-endorsed customer continuity campaign before close.

Control exists only if all are secured:

- signed option over source code, IP, repo, release/update credentials, domain/docs, license server, support inbox, issue history, and narrow non-compete;
- seller-mediated notice to named active customers;
- explicit customer re-consent to data/support transfer;
- prepaid 12-month maintenance or transition-support contracts from named merchants;
- closing condition that customer cash/re-consent must clear before the acquisition completes;
- support terms limiting included work to connector compatibility/security/API maintenance, with bespoke store debugging separately paid.

The founder controls signed customer contracts and prepaid cash before paying the seller. This directly fixes the Round 31 transferability and "dead free plugin" risk.

### Two-Month Proof

Within 60 days and under 100,000 PLN:

- build a screened list of 40-60 Polish/CEE ERP/accounting/order-operation connectors and agencies/freelancers behind them;
- reject generic payment gateway, broad shipping, and free-only plugin targets;
- sign one conditional option with a seller for under 20,000 PLN upfront option fee plus seller earnout paid only from re-consented customer cash;
- seller sends continuity notice to at least 30 named active customers;
- secure at least 20 customer re-consents and at least 25,000 PLN prepaid maintenance/transition-support cash before closing;
- transfer repo/update/support assets only after cash/re-consent threshold is met;
- ship one scoped compatibility/security/API patch within 14 days of close;
- document support-ticket distribution showing that at least 70% of issues are connector-level, not bespoke store debugging.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Outbound to small agencies, freelancers, and module authors using a seller-friendly offer:

"You have an annoying support tail. I will not ask you to sell blind. Sign a conditional option, introduce me to active customers under your endorsement, and you get a fixed option fee plus earnout only if customers prepay continuity."

Screening sources:

- Polish PrestaShop/WooCommerce module shops;
- GitHub repos for Subiekt, Optima, enova365, Fakturownia, invoice export, warehouse sync, order status, and COD reconciliation;
- WordPress.org and PrestaShop module listings where paid support/store exists;
- Polish e-commerce groups/forums with "not updated", "sync broken", or "developer disappeared" complaints;
- small agencies with old modules and support pages.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

- Option fee: 5,000-20,000 PLN.
- Seller earnout: 20-35% of collected re-consented maintenance/support revenue for 12-24 months.
- Customer offer: 1,000-2,500 PLN per merchant for 12 months of compatibility/security/API maintenance and transition support, excluding bespoke store fixes.
- Proof target: 20 merchants x average 1,250 PLN = 25,000 PLN before close, covering option/legal/diligence and reducing acquisition risk.
- Developer patch cost: 5,000-15,000 PLN for the first scoped fix if codebase is patchable.
- Payback is immediate if prepaid customer cash exceeds the option fee and first patch cost.

### Copy Risk

A freelancer can offer replacement support, but cannot instantly copy:

- seller-endorsed handoff;
- prepaid re-consented customer contracts;
- update/release channel;
- issue history and version-specific implementation notes;
- support inbox;
- the fact that customers already paid to keep the current connector alive.

### Why It Is Not A Service/Report/Dashboard/Marketplace Wrapper

The startup controls source/update/support assets plus signed prepaid merchant contracts. It is not selling advice or building a marketplace. It closes only when payment flow and asset custody are locked.

## Candidate 2: COD Reconciliation Connector Continuity Buyout

### Buyer

Polish merchants using fragile COD reconciliation or settlement export connectors between stores, courier reports, accounting software, and bank/import workflows.

### Acute Trigger

COD mismatches, payout reconciliation, and accounting exports break when courier formats, store exports, or accounting imports change.

### Control Point

Conditional option over a COD reconciliation connector plus seller-mediated prepaid continuity contracts from active merchants before close.

### Two-Month Proof

Option one COD connector, get 15 merchants to prepay 1,000-2,000 PLN, transfer code/support assets, and ship one format/API patch.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Target small agencies that built reconciliation scripts for multiple merchants.

### Economics

Lower official-free-connector risk than shipping labels; high accounting pain; moderate ticket size.

### Copy Risk

Moderate. Replacement scripts are possible, but historical mapping and customer contracts help.

## Candidate 3: Fakturownia/Subiekt Export Maintainer Takeover

### Buyer

Merchants using small invoice/export connectors from WooCommerce/PrestaShop to Fakturownia, Subiekt, or accounting imports.

### Acute Trigger

Invoice export errors create accounting backlog, VAT correction work, and manual data entry.

### Control Point

Source/update/support option plus prepaid merchant continuity contracts before close.

### Two-Month Proof

Secure 20 prepaid customers and one patchable connector with no strong official/free replacement.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Author/agency outbound around old paid modules and support tickets.

### Economics

1,000-2,500 PLN per merchant annual maintenance; high gross margin if support is connector-level.

### Copy Risk

Moderate-low if seller handoff and update channel are transferred. Higher if Fakturownia/Subiekt ecosystems have strong official integrations.

## Candidate 4: PrestaShop Local Operations Module Continuity Pool

### Buyer

PrestaShop merchants using older Polish modules for operational exports, invoices, order status, warehouse sync, or accounting, not generic checkout cosmetics.

### Acute Trigger

PrestaShop/PHP upgrades and API changes break operations modules.

### Control Point

Conditional acquisition option plus customer prepayment and re-consent before closing.

### Two-Month Proof

Option one seller's module, collect 25,000 PLN prepaid maintenance from 20 merchants, and ship one update.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

PrestaShop module shops with stale changelogs and paid users.

### Economics

Similar to Candidate 1, but narrower to PrestaShop.

### Copy Risk

Higher if marketplace/listing transfer is blocked, lower if seller-mediated re-consent creates the payment flow.

## Candidate 5: WooCommerce ERP Sync Customer Contract Assignment

### Buyer

WooCommerce stores using paid ERP/order sync connectors for Polish accounting/warehouse tools.

### Acute Trigger

WordPress/PHP/WooCommerce updates break sync, stock, invoices, or order transfer.

### Control Point

WordPress.org/commercial listing or release channel plus prepaid customer support contracts assigned through seller handoff.

### Two-Month Proof

Find one plugin with commercial users, get 20 customers to prepay, transfer ownership where allowed, and ship one scoped update.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

WordPress plugin authors and agencies with abandoned commercial add-ons.

### Economics

Good if paid support ARPU is above 1,000 PLN/year; poor for $49 commodity plugins.

### Copy Risk

Moderate. WordPress plugin transfer is possible, but WordPress support market is crowded.

## Candidate 6: Agency Support-Tail Conversion With Revenue Floor

### Buyer

Clients of a small e-commerce agency that rely on one repeated operational connector or internal module.

### Acute Trigger

Agency wants to exit a low-status support tail; clients need continuity.

### Control Point

The founder acquires code/support only after clients prepay 12-month maintenance under a standardized scope.

### Two-Month Proof

One agency introduces 10-20 clients, at least 8 prepay 2,000-4,000 PLN, and one patch/update is shipped.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Small agencies with old modules, repeated client support, and low appetite for maintenance.

### Economics

Higher ticket but higher service-risk. Must be a repeated connector, not bespoke project work.

### Copy Risk

Higher than Candidate 1 unless the client contracts and deployment/update access are assigned.

## Gate Rule For Scorers

Apply the existing persistent-loop caps:

- cap below `82` if no concrete 2-month control proof;
- cap below `85` if copyable by one vendor contract, equipment purchase, or internal process;
- cap below `87` if CAC, payback, margins, or owner earnings are not credible;
- require simulated score strictly `>87` to reach the real Zero To One browser gate.
