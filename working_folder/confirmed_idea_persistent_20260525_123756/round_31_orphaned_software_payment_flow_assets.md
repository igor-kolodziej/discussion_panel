# Round 31 Candidate Set: Orphaned Software Payment-Flow Assets

Created: 2026-05-25

## Round 30 Failure To Fix

Round 30 still failed because field-service/operator ideas were capped by:

- incumbent service trust;
- copyability by local integrators, OEMs, or internal processes;
- uncertain owner earnings after travel, liability, and labor;
- fragile one-operator acquisition sourcing.

Round 31 pivots to micro-software assets where the founder can acquire hard digital control:

- source code and IP;
- marketplace listing or plugin directory account;
- update channel;
- license server;
- subscription/payment flow;
- support inbox and customer list;
- existing renewals or support contracts.

This fits a Warsaw data/software founder better than field acquisition and can create proof through actual transferred revenue, not only buyer intent.

## Candidate 1: Orphaned Polish E-Commerce Connector Payment-Flow Acquisition

### Buyer

Existing Polish WooCommerce, PrestaShop, Shoper, Magento, IdoSell, or Baselinker-connected merchants using a small plugin/module for a local carrier, payment provider, ERP, or marketplace workflow.

### Acute Trigger

Checkout, shipping labels, tracking, COD, parcel-locker delivery, payment status updates, or order export can break when carrier/payment/marketplace APIs, PHP, WordPress, PrestaShop, or platform versions change. A shop already using the module does not want a migration project during live sales.

### Control Point

Acquire or option the abandoned/small plugin business itself:

- source code and IP assignment;
- marketplace/plugin listing or seller account where transferable;
- update channel and release credentials;
- license server;
- domain/docs;
- support inbox;
- customer list and renewal schedule;
- Stripe/PayPal/Przelewy24/Fakturownia/Shoplo-style subscription or annual-license payment flow where transferable;
- seller handoff and non-compete for the niche.

The control point is the installed update/payment channel, not a generic software development service.

### Two-Month Proof

Within 60 days and under 100,000 PLN:

- identify 30-50 Polish/localized e-commerce connectors with abandonment signals, stale releases, support complaints, or owner fatigue;
- sign one option/acquisition LOI with less than 40,000 PLN upfront plus seller earnout;
- transfer source repo, domain/docs, support inbox, license server, and renewal/payment flow or collect explicit customer re-consent;
- verify at least 50 active paid installs or 20 recurring support customers;
- ship one compatibility/security/API update within 14 days of control;
- collect at least 15,000-30,000 PLN in renewals, support packages, acquisition-transition payments, or prepaid maintenance commitments.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Cold outbound to plugin/module authors in Poland and nearby CEE markets:

- WordPress.org / WooCommerce plugin authors with Polish support threads;
- PrestaShop Addons sellers;
- GitHub repos for InPost, DPD, DHL, Allegro, Przelewy24, PayU, Blik, Fakturownia, Subiekt, Baselinker, and ERP/order-export connectors;
- small agencies that built one client connector and no longer want maintenance;
- forum/plugin reviews showing "not updated" or "API broken" complaints.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

- Acquisition structure: 15,000-40,000 PLN upfront plus 20-35% earnout on retained renewals.
- Existing annual-license/support flow can repay upfront in 3-6 months if at least 50 customers pay 500-1,500 PLN/year or if 20-30 merchants buy 1,000-2,000 PLN transition support.
- Gross margins are software-like after support, hosting, marketplace fees, and contractor fixes.
- Founder can operate part-time with one freelance PHP/PrestaShop/WooCommerce developer and legal template support.

### Copy Risk

Competitors can write another connector, but they cannot copy:

- the exact installed customer base;
- plugin reviews/listing/history;
- update channel;
- support inbox;
- license server renewals;
- customer bug history;
- merchant trust in the already-installed module.

This is materially stronger than building a new plugin from zero because distribution and payment flow are acquired.

### Why It Is Not A Service/Report/Dashboard/Marketplace Wrapper

The startup owns or controls source code, update credentials, renewal/payment flow, and customer support handoff. The proof is transferred revenue plus shipped updates, not a dashboard or advisory deliverable.

## Candidate 2: Abandoned WordPress Plugin Update-Channel Rescue

### Buyer

Small businesses and agencies using a niche WordPress plugin with paid add-ons or annual licenses.

### Acute Trigger

Security vulnerability, WordPress/PHP incompatibility, or payment/form/booking workflow breakage.

### Control Point

Acquire the WordPress.org listing, commercial add-on store, license server, source repo, support inbox, mailing list, and payment flow.

### Two-Month Proof

Acquire one plugin with 1,000+ active free installs and 50+ paid annual-license users; ship a security/compatibility release; collect renewal/support cash.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Outbound to plugin authors with abandoned support threads, old changelogs, and low commercial energy.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Low hosting/support cost; contractor patching; annual licenses and support packages provide near-term cash.

### Copy Risk

Lower if update channel/listing transfers. Higher if only source code transfers and users must migrate.

## Candidate 3: PrestaShop Module Renewal Book Acquisition

### Buyer

Polish/CEE PrestaShop merchants using local payment, carrier, invoice, ERP, warehouse, or tax/shipping modules.

### Acute Trigger

Module incompatibility blocks checkout, invoices, labels, or ERP synchronization after PrestaShop/PHP/API updates.

### Control Point

Acquire PrestaShop Addons seller account where allowed, module IP, support contacts, buyer history, and renewal/maintenance offers.

### Two-Month Proof

Option one module with 30-100 historical buyers, contact the installed base, collect 10 paid maintenance renewals, and ship one update.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Author/agency outreach to modules with old release dates and recent bad reviews.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Annual support and compatibility updates; one developer can maintain several modules.

### Copy Risk

Moderate. Existing installed base and buyer history help, but transferability of the marketplace account may be limited.

## Candidate 4: Polish ERP Connector Source And Support Book

### Buyer

Merchants or wholesalers using small connectors between Subiekt, enova365, Comarch ERP Optima, Baselinker, WooCommerce, PrestaShop, or warehouse tools.

### Acute Trigger

Orders, invoices, stock, or shipping labels stop syncing after an API, schema, or version change.

### Control Point

Acquire source code, installer/update mechanism, support inbox, customer contracts, and version-specific implementation notes.

### Two-Month Proof

Acquire one connector book with 10-30 paying businesses, collect maintenance renewals, and fix one live sync break.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Small software houses and freelancers who no longer want low-ticket support obligations.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Higher ticket than commodity plugins; annual maintenance 1,000-5,000 PLN per customer.

### Copy Risk

Lower where the connector has site-specific mappings and historical bug knowledge. Higher if a large ERP partner already serves the same niche.

## Candidate 5: Abandoned SaaS Micro-Tool With Recurring Billing

### Buyer

Existing users of a narrow Polish/CEE SaaS micro-tool, such as appointment reminders, invoice attachments, order alerts, warehouse exports, delivery notifications, or lead routing.

### Acute Trigger

Tool abandonment, integration breakage, or customer support silence threatens daily workflow.

### Control Point

Acquire domain, app source, database, customer contracts, payment account migration, support inbox, and seller handoff.

### Two-Month Proof

Acquire one tool at 1-3x monthly recurring revenue with under 40,000 PLN upfront, retain 80% of MRR after handoff, and ship one reliability fix.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

MicroAcquire-style outreach, Indie Hackers/forum outreach, Polish software-house cleanup, and direct emails to neglected tools.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Immediate MRR and low infrastructure cost. Main risk is code quality/support burden.

### Copy Risk

Good if payment flow and customer workflow are already embedded. Weak if users can switch in one afternoon.

## Candidate 6: Agency-Built Internal Tool License Book

### Buyer

Agencies or small SaaS shops that built reusable tools for multiple clients but have no desire to maintain them.

### Acute Trigger

Clients still rely on the tool but the agency wants to stop support, creating continuity risk and unhappy clients.

### Control Point

Acquire the codebase, client license/support contracts, deployment access, support history, and agency referral/handoff.

### Two-Month Proof

Option one agency tool with 5-15 paying clients, collect transition support fees, and sign 12-month maintenance contracts.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Outbound to small agencies offering to buy their "annoying support tail" for cash plus earnout.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Higher-touch than plugins, but immediate support fees can cover acquisition. Good if tool is narrow and repeated.

### Copy Risk

Moderate. The contracts and deployment knowledge are the asset; a competitor cannot easily recreate the client-specific integrations but can compete for future work.

## Source Anchors To Check

- WordPress.org plugin directory shows active-install counts, changelogs, support threads, author profiles, and abandonment signals.
- PrestaShop Addons shows paid module categories and seller/module histories.
- WooCommerce, WordPress, PrestaShop, carrier, and payment-provider ecosystems have recurring API/version compatibility risk.
- Acquisition marketplaces and indie founder forums show that small software assets with existing revenue can trade below institutional acquisition thresholds.

## Gate Rule For Scorers

Apply the existing persistent-loop caps:

- cap below `82` if no concrete 2-month control proof;
- cap below `85` if copyable by one vendor contract, equipment purchase, or internal process;
- cap below `87` if CAC, payback, margins, or owner earnings are not credible;
- require simulated score strictly `>87` to reach the real Zero To One browser gate.
