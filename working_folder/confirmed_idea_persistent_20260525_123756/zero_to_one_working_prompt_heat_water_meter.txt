Founder constraints: unknown solo founder in Poland, max 100,000 PLN starting capital, no privileged introductions, part-time at first, must prove a hard control point in <=2 months, must solve cold start with concrete acquisition, and must have plausible gross margins, willingness to pay, CAC/payback, retention/repeatability, and owner earnings.

Important cap: cap the score below 82 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under those constraints. Also be skeptical of generic services, reports, dashboards, databases, marketplaces, or partner-dependent coordination.

Scored idea wording:

Heat/Water Meter Installed-Base Acquisition Rail.

Buyer: housing communities, small cooperatives, property managers, and building administrators in Poland that already depend on a tiny local metering/utility-allocation operator for annual water-meter, heat-meter, or heat-cost-allocator readings and resident settlement files.

Acute trigger: a micro-operator retires, becomes unreliable, loses staff, or cannot handle radio-read upgrade/legalization cycles; the administrator cannot let annual readings, heat/water cost allocation, resident bills, device replacement dates, or complaint records fail.

Product wedge: acquire or option tiny metering and settlement operator books before they collapse, then preserve continuity for administrators: installed-device database, reading schedules, meter IDs, seal/legalization dates, historic settlement files, resident complaint notes, billing templates, device inventory, service phone/domain, and owner handoff.

Primary control point: a signed option or acquisition agreement covering assigned service contracts where assignable, administrator transfer acknowledgments, installed-device database, reading and settlement schedules, billing files/templates, device inventory, service phone/domain routing, and 30-60 days of retiring-owner transition support. The control point is not a lead list; it is the existing installed-base workflow and recurring settlement obligation.

2-month proof under 100,000 PLN: sign one retiring micro-operator option covering 300-1,000 flats; obtain written transfer acknowledgment or continuation consent from at least 2 administrators/housing communities; collect one paid reading/settlement cycle, renewal deposit, or transition retainer; complete 20-50 meter/allocator readings or one settlement handoff using the acquired records; document that the operator's phone/domain and database now route through the founder-controlled workflow.

Cold start acquisition: outbound to local one-person and family metering/settlement firms, CEIDG/KRS businesses offering wodomierze/ciepłomierze/podzielniki rozliczenia, retiring installers, property-manager referrals, and local business-sale listings. Demand-side cold start is solved by the seller's administrator book and active schedules, not by selling generic metering software to strangers.

Economics: charge recurring annual reading/settlement fees per flat/building, transition retainers from administrators, and margin on meter replacement/legalization/radio-read upgrades. A 300-1,000 flat book can produce immediate cashflow if annual readings or settlement deadlines are near; gross margin is attractive because the founder buys continuity rights and records cheaply from a retiring operator, then uses contractors for field reads and billing while retaining the administrator relationship. Payback target: option fee plus contractor cost recovered from first renewal/settlement cycle or replacement work.

Why it is not a generic service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace: the startup must control or option the actual administrator contracts/consents, installed-device records, reading schedules, settlement files, phone/domain routing, and owner handoff. The scarce resource is continuity of an existing legally and operationally sensitive installed-base workflow, not software.

Main risks: contract assignment may fail; GDPR/data transfer must be lawful; administrators may resist an unknown successor; large incumbents such as ista/Techem/local billing firms can compete; field-read labor and settlement errors can create complaints; the business may be a profitable regional roll-up rather than a venture-scale company unless many micro-books can be acquired.
