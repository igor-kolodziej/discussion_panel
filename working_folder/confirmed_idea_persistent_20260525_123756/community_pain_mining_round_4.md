# Community And Search Pain Mining - Round 4

Date: 2026-05-25 Europe/Warsaw.

Purpose: find operator/customer pains that can support hard transferable control points under 100,000 PLN. This round looked for pains where the first resource can be owned, optioned, routed, prepaid, or assigned inside two months.

## Pain Patterns

### 1. Fiscal cash-register service continuity

- Community/source clue: Polish search results show repeated "przeglad kasy fiskalnej po terminie", "zmiana serwisanta kasy fiskalnej", and "kasa lub drukarka fiskalna nie dziala i nie moge sprzedawac". Dotykacka support states that when the fiscal cash register/printer fails, sales may need to stop until fiscal recording is possible. Several fiscal-service sites sell urgent overdue-review and service-change help.
- Buyer: small retailers, restaurants, beauty salons, taxis, kiosks, local services; also the retiring fiscal-service owner as the supply-side seller.
- Acute trigger: failed fiscal printer/cash register, overdue two-year technical review, prior service provider disappears, KSeF setup confusion at the same counter/POS layer.
- Possible control point: option/acquire one tiny service operator's active service book: customer list, device serials, review dates, phone/domain/Google profile, support inbox, spare parts, service history, manufacturer authorization roster, and owner handoff.
- First acquisition: outbound to one-person fiscal service shops aged/succession-stressed by KSeF; offer low upfront option plus earnout and 30-day phone/customer transition.

### 2. Legal-for-trade scale review and replacement

- Source clue: search results around "legalizacja wagi" show inspection/mandate fear, trade-use obligation, and business disruption when scales are not legalised or accurate.
- Buyer: small food retail, markets, pharmacies, warehouses using scales for invoicing or trade.
- Acute trigger: inspection, expired sticker, broken scale, inability to invoice by weight.
- Control point: option service-route book plus certified mass standards/loaner scales; paid review calendar.
- First acquisition: acquire a retiring scale/fiscal-device service provider, not a generic lead list.

### 3. Grease separator / BDO waste pickup routing

- Source clue: Polish forum/search terms around BDO/KPO, separator grease, and "nie odebral odpadow" show that paperwork and pickup failures can stop compliant restaurant operation.
- Buyer: restaurants, hotels, bakeries, food production sites.
- Acute trigger: separator full, BDO/KPO waste-card error, landlord/sanepid/municipal pressure.
- Control point: signed route book from a permitted micro waste operator plus disposal intake agreement and barrels.
- First acquisition: option a small route operator with BDO processes already working.

### 4. Returnable packaging deposit recovery

- Source clue: closure/liquidation discussions and local classified listings show piles of pallets, crates, kegs, cylinders, and roll-cages that operators want gone.
- Buyer: closing HoReCa/retail operators and distributors with deposit/reuse systems.
- Acute trigger: premises handover, closure, clutter, missing deposit documents.
- Control point: physical custody plus signed title/assignment only where legal transfer is clean; authorized return routes.
- First acquisition: closure accountants, landlords, liquidation groups, OLX/Facebook local groups.

### 5. Cash register memory/end-of-life paperwork

- Source clue: fiscal-service sites describe procedures for ending use of a fiscal register, memory readout, reports, and tax-office filing.
- Buyer: closing small businesses and retiring shop owners.
- Acute trigger: liquidation, device end of life, memory readout cannot be delayed.
- Control point: acquired fiscal-service book plus service technician capacity and reusable closure packet templates.
- First acquisition: same service-book roll-up as pain pattern 1.

### 6. Intercom/access module emergency spares

- Source clue: Polish forums show installer-code and legacy intercom replacement problems in apartment buildings.
- Buyer: property managers and low-voltage installers.
- Acute trigger: dead access panel or central module; building residents cannot enter normally.
- Control point: first-look rights from modernization contractors plus tested inventory.
- First acquisition: modernization installers and decommissioning jobs.

### 7. Boiler PCB emergency spares

- Source clue: Polish used boiler-board listings and HVAC search results show urgent old-board replacement demand.
- Buyer: heating technicians.
- Acute trigger: no heat/hot water because exact board is obsolete or slow.
- Control point: tested donor board inventory plus technician deposits.
- First acquisition: boiler replacement crews and used-board sellers.

### 8. Forklift controller/charger spares

- Source clue: repair firms describe long lead times and expensive controller repairs; warehouses face downtime.
- Buyer: forklift service techs and small warehouses.
- Acute trigger: forklift/charger failure blocking loading.
- Control point: tested controller/charger donor bank plus technician request deposits.
- First acquisition: closing warehouses and forklift service shops.

### 9. ISPM-15 export pallet rescue

- Source clue: export wood packaging compliance creates last-minute shipment holds, but prior scoring found commodity copy risk.
- Buyer: exporters and freight forwarders.
- Acute trigger: shipment ready but pallets/crates are not heat-treated/stamped/documented.
- Control point: reserved compliant stock in right locations.
- First acquisition: small certified heat-treatment shops.

### 10. KSeF local integrator overload

- Source clue: Reddit/search discussions show small-business confusion around KSeF, free tools, banks, tokens, and software choices. Official KSeF 2.0 became mandatory in 2026.
- Buyer: micro-retail and service businesses already using a local fiscal/POS/ERP technician.
- Acute trigger: cannot issue/receive invoices correctly, accountant asks for KSeF setup, POS/fiscal workflow does not match software.
- Control point: not a generic KSeF tool; use acquired fiscal/POS service book and remote/admin custody from an existing operator.
- First acquisition: transition one retiring local fiscal/POS service book, then upsell KSeF readiness to its customers.

## Round 4 Takeaway

The strongest new pattern is fiscal-device service-book succession. It combines:

- mandatory recurring transaction flow;
- public legal pressure;
- urgent break/fix downtime;
- KSeF timing;
- a transferable local operator asset;
- a buyer relationship that already exists before the unknown founder appears.

It is stronger than a generic KSeF patchbench because the first acquisition mechanism is not cold trust-building. The founder buys or options the trust and transaction calendar from an existing micro-operator.
