## Key Objections From Zero To One

- The idea is materially stronger than a custom-gasket shop because the asset is production memory plus routed demand.
- The hard uncertainty is not production; it is whether the seller's customer book, records, phone/domain, and owner trust can be legally transferred and commercially reactivated by an unknown part-time founder within 2 months.
- Customer goodwill may not transfer. A maintenance manager may trust the old local operator, not the new founder.
- Customer database transfer and invoice history need GDPR/data-transfer discipline: lawful basis, data minimization, customer notice, and potentially customer consent/contract.
- Last-24-month invoices may be too weak unless they show reorder frequency, gross margin, payment behavior, and current machine continuity.
- Archive quality can be poor: invoices may not contain material grade, thickness, tolerance, use case, fluid/temperature exposure, food-contact requirements, or failure notes.
- Subcontract cutting is acceptable for POC but not defensible; the moat must be the archive and customer route.
- Small urgent orders can destroy solo-founder time unless minimum order, rush pricing, and reorder-only workflows are enforced.
- Liability can sneak in even for "simple" parts; confirmations, traceability, and clear professional-use responsibility language are needed.
- Seller acquisition may be harder than customer acquisition because retiring owners may overvalue the archive, distrust the buyer, or have disorganized records.

## Feedback To Carry Forward

The business can be improved only if the core resource changes from "seller archive plus hoped-for reactivation" to a stronger dual-sided control point:

- seller option is conditional and low-cost;
- customer contact is done jointly with the seller before acquisition;
- customers sign explicit continuity mandates authorizing transfer/custody of their production files/templates/dies;
- customers pay non-refundable reorder credits or deposits before the archive is acquired;
- legal/data-transfer risk is converted from vague legitimate interest into customer-signed operational continuity contracts;
- seller earns out only on paid reactivated customers.

The revised idea must prove customer route control before spending serious capital.
