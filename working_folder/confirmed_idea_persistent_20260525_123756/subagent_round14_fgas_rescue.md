Score: **84/100**

Tier: **Strong niche, not automatic advance**

Copy risk: **Medium-high.** The wedge is real, but an existing HVAC firm, refrigerant distributor, or maintenance network can copy the basic model by stocking compliant reclaimed gas and offering emergency routing. The defensibility depends on locking scarce legal inventory, provenance trust, and technician availability faster than incumbents care to.

Objections:
- Control proof is concrete enough: compliant cylinders, provenance records, certified tech slots, and paid emergency jobs.
- But the control is not yet exclusive. Inventory legality is a barrier, not a moat.
- 100,000 PLN may be tight once cylinder deposits, working capital, compliance, testing, insurance, and emergency logistics are included.
- The buyer may default to their existing maintenance contractor unless this is positioned as a supplier/backup bank for contractors, not a competing service.
- Demand is urgent but episodic; recurring economics need reserve contracts, priority access fees, or contractor inventory subscriptions.

Economics: **Plausible but not yet above the 87 bar.** Emergency willingness to pay is credible because downtime in grocery, pharmacy, and cold rooms can be expensive. CAC can be low through HVAC technicians and small refrigeration firms if referral economics work. Gross margin could be attractive on scarce refrigerant plus dispatch, but owner earnings are not proven without SKU-level supply costs, average kg/job, emergency ticket size, spoilage-avoidance pricing, and technician payout math.

Verdict: **Do not advance** under the `>87` rule. It is securable and worth a 60-day test, but capped at **84** because copyability and economics are not yet strong enough.
