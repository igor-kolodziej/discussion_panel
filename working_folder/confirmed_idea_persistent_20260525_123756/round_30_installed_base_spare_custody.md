# Round 30 Candidate Set: Installed-Base Spare Custody

Created: 2026-05-25

## Round 29 Failure To Fix

Round 29's strongest industrial-controls candidate scored `84`, but failed the simulated gate because it was still a hidden-inventory bet:

- plant audit CAC was too high;
- demand was stochastic because exact failed SKU matching was unknown;
- public obsolete-parts competitors could buy the same visible inventory;
- buyer trust and testing liability were not solved by stock ownership alone;
- owner earnings were not proven after bench testing, returns, misdiagnosis, and dead inventory.

Round 30 changes the shape from speculative inventory to customer-specific continuity custody. The founder must control the buyer's installed-base register and payment flow before building the spare pool.

## Candidate 1: Line-Down Controls Continuity Retainer

### Buyer

Polish SME factories with legacy PLC/HMI/servo/drive installed bases in packaging, food processing, plastics, wood, automotive suppliers, and local manufacturing plants.

### Acute Trigger

One obsolete automation component failure can stop a production line. Many plants cannot modernize every legacy machine immediately because shutdown windows, integrator availability, and capex timing are constrained.

### Control Point

Signed customer installed-base register plus a spare-custody retainer:

- the plant gives an audited list of exact PLC, HMI, drive, servo, power-supply, safety relay, and I/O module SKUs;
- the founder classifies top failure-critical SKUs;
- the founder physically owns or contractually reserves exact matching tested spares for that customer cluster;
- customers pay a monthly continuity retainer and agree to call the founder first for listed SKUs;
- emergency sales/rentals run through the founder's payment terms.

The control point is not broad inventory. It is the combination of buyer-side installed-base custody, paid call-first access, exact SKU matching, and physically controlled spares.

### Two-Month Proof

Within 60 days and under 100,000 PLN:

- sign 5 factories on 1,500-3,000 PLN/month continuity retainers or 5,000-10,000 PLN setup deposits;
- audit 50-100 exact installed-base components;
- buy, test, or reserve the top 20 exact high-risk SKUs for less than 50,000 PLN;
- run one paid emergency replacement, paid live drill, or written SLA test event;
- document serial photos, bench-test videos, and custody location for each SKU.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Use founder-local channels, not broad content marketing:

- parents' renewable-energy and industrial electrical contacts near Ostrów Wielkopolski;
- automation electricians and small integrators that already service local plants;
- industrial parks in Wielkopolska, Łódź, Śląsk, and Lower Silesia;
- a one-page "obsolete controls continuity audit" offer for plant managers and maintenance heads.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

- Retainer revenue: 5 customers x 2,000 PLN/month = 10,000 PLN MRR before meaningful inventory sale.
- Setup/audit deposits can fund the first exact-SKU stock purchases.
- Emergency sale/rental markup on tested obsolete controls can be high because the buyer is paying to avoid line downtime, not to buy the cheapest used module.
- Inventory risk is reduced because stock is bought against named installed bases, not public guesses.
- Founder labor is front-loaded into audits, testing, and technician network setup.

### Copy Risk

Generic obsolete-parts resellers can copy the category, but not the exact customer register, call-first relationship, custody agreement, and cluster-specific spare map once signed. Incumbent automation integrators can compete, but many prefer project work and do not want to finance customer-specific obsolete inventory.

### Why It Is Not The Failed Donor Bank

The failed donor bank bought speculative scarce stock and waited for line-down demand. This version sells continuity custody first, uses customer payment and installed-base data to choose inventory, and turns the exact register into the scarce asset.

## Candidate 2: HMI Image And Replacement Custody

### Buyer

Factories running older HMI panels whose recipes, screens, passwords, or runtime configurations are not cleanly backed up.

### Acute Trigger

An HMI failure can stop production even when the PLC remains healthy because operators lose recipe entry, alarms, or line controls.

### Control Point

Admin custody over HMI backups plus one physically tested replacement panel for the customer's exact platform. The founder holds restore media, firmware/version notes, serial photos, and a tested spare.

### Two-Month Proof

Secure 3 paid customers, back up 10 HMI panels, buy 3 exact replacement panels, and run one restore drill.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Technician referrals and maintenance-manager outreach around "can you restore this HMI tomorrow if it dies?"

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Setup fee plus monthly backup/spare custody fee. Emergency restore and panel rental/sale are high-margin.

### Copy Risk

Copyable by an integrator if the plant already trusts one. Strong only where the founder gets admin custody and a tested spare before the incumbent acts.

## Candidate 3: Servo/Drive Parameter And Donor Drive Retainer

### Buyer

Plants with older Lenze, SEW, Omron, Siemens, ABB, Danfoss, Schneider, Yaskawa, or Indramat drives on packaging, conveyor, cutting, press, and process equipment.

### Acute Trigger

A failed drive is not solved by buying a used unit if parameters, firmware, encoder feedback, or motor matching are missing.

### Control Point

Customer drive parameter backups plus tested donor drives for exact models. The founder controls restore files and the donor pool.

### Two-Month Proof

Sign 4 plants, collect 30 drive parameter sets, buy/test 10 top matching drives, and complete one paid swap drill.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Partner with one drive repair shop or electrician who gets a referral fee per audited cabinet.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Setup fees fund testing; retainers create base revenue; emergency donor-drive rental/sale captures urgency premium.

### Copy Risk

Moderate. Repair shops can copy if they hold the relationship, but many do not maintain a retainer-backed exact donor pool.

## Candidate 4: Industrial Estate Critical-SKU Cabinet

### Buyer

Clusters of small manufacturers in one industrial estate or town using overlapping automation brands.

### Acute Trigger

Any factory in the cluster can lose a day waiting for a module couriered from another country.

### Control Point

One local physical cabinet containing exact critical SKUs selected from signed installed-base audits across multiple nearby plants. Access is sold as a shared reserve.

### Two-Month Proof

Audit 8 nearby plants, identify 15 overlapping SKUs, collect 1,000 PLN/month each from 5 plants, and stock a tested local cabinet.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Door-to-door maintenance-manager visits in one industrial estate, supported by one local electrician.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Shared inventory improves utilization. Retainers fund the cabinet even before emergency sales.

### Copy Risk

Local defensibility if the founder signs the first cluster and controls the cabinet. Weak if a large distributor targets the same estate.

## Candidate 5: Retiring Integrator Spare Book With Customer Retainers

### Buyer

Factories previously serviced by a retiring automation electrician/integrator who has customer history, old parts, and emergency phone traffic.

### Acute Trigger

The trusted local automation person retires, leaving plants without continuity support for legacy machines.

### Control Point

Option or acquisition of the retiring operator's phone/domain, customer list, service-ticket archive, spare stock, and 30-day handoff, then convert plants to paid spare-custody retainers.

### Two-Month Proof

Sign one option letter with a retiring operator, acquire or consign 30 tested SKUs, transfer emergency call routing, and convert 3 customers to paid retainers.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Direct outreach to solo integrators/electricians aged 55+ through local business networks and supplier referrals.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Customer-book acquisition lowers CAC; existing emergency traffic creates faster cash; stock can be monetized through retainers and repairs.

### Copy Risk

Lower than public parts arbitrage if phone/domain/customer archive are transferred. Execution depends on finding the right retiring operator quickly.

## Candidate 6: Customer-Owned Escrow Spares Administration

### Buyer

Factories that already own random spare parts but do not know what is tested, matched, or usable.

### Acute Trigger

During a line-down event, the plant discovers that its "spare" is wrong firmware, dead, missing a connector, or unlocatable.

### Control Point

Founder takes admin custody over customer-owned spares: testing, labeling, serial photos, location, cross-match to installed-base list, call-first emergency procedure, and replenishment rights.

### Two-Month Proof

Sign 5 plants, test 100 existing spares, identify 20 missing critical SKUs, and sell/lease 5 missing spares from the founder's pool.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Offer a paid "spare cabinet forensic audit" to maintenance heads.

### Gross Margin And Payback Logic

Lower inventory spend because customer already owns some stock. Revenue is audit fee, retainer, and missing-spare sale/rental margin.

### Copy Risk

Higher because this can become a service unless the founder secures replenishment rights, call-first terms, and missing-spare ownership.

## Current Source Checks

- Siemens states SIMATIC S7-300 / ET 200M systems remain in spare-part availability through 2033, and 2025 Siemens migration material describes product cancellation and spare-part-only phases.
- Public competitors already sell or repair obsolete automation parts in Poland/EU, including Gal-Industry, ADEGIS, EU Automation, PLC West, and similar suppliers.
- Therefore the only defensible Round 30 shape is not a parts shop. It must be a signed installed-base custody and retainer model.
