# Zero-to-One Evaluation: PulsePhantom Bench v1 After Panel

Applied with `$evaluate-zero-to-one` to the exact canonical text and complete panel objections. Advisory only.

## Diagnosis

The paid proof remains capital-disciplined, but the panel exposes a technical and economic coupling the original evaluation underweighted: a repeatable synthetic bench must also correlate with human-device failures, yet proving that correlation pushes toward human studies and metrology credibility before buyers trust it. The fixture is owned IP but not a scarce economic gate, and established simulator vendors can make the same product extension. The 500-customer conservative case and annual subscription do not survive this copy/denominator uncertainty. The product-sale shape should be abandoned.

## Evidence and Assumptions

- Evidence supports PPG test protocols, phantom feasibility, variable-dependent errors, and existing simulators.
- Hypotheses remain: predictive correlation, paid reservations, self-service geometry support, subscription renewal, 500 qualified buyers, 25% operating margin.
- External judges scored 65 and 59; both independently identify predictive validity, reproducibility by incumbents, subscription weakness, and wealth denominator.

## Overall Score

Weighted points 105.5 / 20; overall **53/100**.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** Owned product IP is not the same as controlling a scarce workflow. Strongest copy risk is a trusted simulator company or internal lab reproducing the bench in one development cycle.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The conservative customer count, subscription, margin, and 3× value all depend on unproved productisation and a narrow unsourced denominator; modest downside drops founder equity below 5m.

## Reachability Gate

**Marginal pass.** A paid sprint/reservation is formally reachable under 180 days/35k/employment, but an unknown founder may need more technical proof before payment, and sprint demand may not validate hardware.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is coherent; incumbent entry is easy and adjacent biosignals do not inherit the same device physics.

## Fatal Objections

1. Synthetic repeatability is not yet correlated with real human-device failures.
2. Established test-equipment vendors can reproduce the feature set with greater trust.
3. No mandatory recurring event supports the subscription.
4. The wealth denominator is not conservative.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Wearable failures become economically valuable when the test/fixture is embedded in a recurring operational decision such as a return, replacement, repair, or release—not when the fixture itself must create a new budget.

## Recommendation

**pivot**

The revision must change buyer, budget, recurring event, value capture, and control. It must not remain a standalone phantom/test-equipment sale.
