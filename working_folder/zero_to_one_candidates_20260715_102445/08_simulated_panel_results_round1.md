- Judge B: **68/100**

- Real-chat eligibility: **No**.

### Judge A Complete Objections and Caveats

- Human-centred RGB-D, hand-kinematic, and optional biosignal data may not materially improve policies for differing grippers, geometries, force profiles, and workcells; buyers may require on-robot trajectories.
- Buyers with the highest willingness to pay may insist on exclusive data, proprietary setups, or collection on their own hardware, destroying retained rights.
- Re-licensing, blended margin, and delivery leverage are assumed; conversions, support, recapture, and customer-specific validation may keep it service-heavy.
- Twenty-four global accounts at 350,000 PLN average annual revenue is a major success, not an evidence-backed conservative case.
- Cold outreach lacks robotics credibility, warm access, or an accepted benchmark; six-month technical procurement is aggressive.
- Founder fit is mixed because manipulation learning, robotics buyer relationships, and data-licensing experience are missing under the five-hour daily limit.
- Much of the pack is built before budget-backed interest, while decisive paid usefulness arrives late in the 180-day window.

Strongest copy risk: a robotics company or broad data vendor hires comparable technicians, uses commodity capture equipment, and collects the same task on the buyer's preferred embodiment with exclusive rights.

### Judge B Complete Objections and Caveats

- The existential hypothesis is whether data captured without the buyer's robot materially improves a target policy or reduces on-robot collection; EMG/gaze may be commercially irrelevant.
- Catalogue economics conflict with buyer specificity and exclusive/on-robot procurement.
- The conservative case depends on unsupported 350,000 PLN annual account value, 24 accounts, 28% operating margin, and catalogue reuse.
- The 100-account outbound test is credible but the assumed 15 technical evaluations and paid licence may exceed robotics sales cycles.
- Founder fit is strong in data/experiments but weak in robot-learning credibility, capture engineering, and specialist enterprise sales.

Strongest copy risk: a funded robotics company or broad collector can sign equivalent performers, buy the same rig, and generate task-specific data on the relevant embodiment through routine contracts and equipment.

### Common Fatal Objection

Both judges independently found that legal ownership of readily reproducible recordings is not a scarce control point. The potentially paying buyer may prefer exclusive, on-robot data, which simultaneously defeats technical utility, retained rights, re-licensing margin, and the wealth model. Under the workflow, this exact business shape is abandoned rather than cosmetically revised.

## TiltLock v1

- Canonical SHA-256: `7d0ce653b592e90b8ba025f222b7987ff04c922c077072c6e17aef979407fba4`
- Judge A: **65/100**
- Judge B: **57/100**
- Official lower score: **57/100**
- Control point: both classified it **Tier 3 today**, with only a conditional path to Tier 2 after difficult outcome and retention proof.
- Real-chat eligibility: **No**.

### Judge A Complete Objections and Caveats

- The user most in need of enforcement is also most likely to disable it, while disciplined users may not need a paid tool.
- Physiology may add little beyond loss, time, sleep, tempo, and explicit behaviour; noisy HR/HRV and sparse labels weaken the dataset.
- Local enforcement is bypassable, while stronger enforcement increases permissions, support, security, and trust burden.
- Six thousand subscribers is not a conservative case without a proven global channel, retention, and CAC.
- Five deposits and ten cohort users do not validate physiology, causal benefit, or a compounding model; “exposure avoided” is partly counterfactual.
- A legal opinion cannot guarantee platform permission; policy change, gambling-harm positioning, and privacy remain material.
- Hands-on labelling, client maintenance, sensors, support, and acquisition may contradict the proposed margins.

Strongest copy risk: an existing tracker, bankroll tool, mental-game product, or operator adds user-authored limits and wearable input using an installed base and stronger telemetry.

### Judge B Complete Objections and Caveats

- The self-binding paradox is existential: disciplined users may use static tools, while high-need users can uninstall or circumvent controls during tilt.
- Physiology may not improve prediction or false positives over behavioural signals, collapsing the differentiated dataset thesis.
- Local blocking faces client updates, multiple sites, mobile play, virtual machines, permissions, and policy uncertainty.
- Poker-community access is ordinary, promotion-restricted, pseudonymous, privacy-sensitive, and not proprietary distribution.
- Gambling outcome plus physiology data creates privacy, consent, reputation, and regulatory burden.
- The 85% gross and 25%–35% operating margins underestimate support and client-maintenance work.
- Six thousand retained subscribers is a substantial consumer-subscription success, not a conservative case.
- Poker expansion into trading/esports is thematic rather than privileged.
- The decisive early proof should be continued enforcement under adverse sessions and incremental physiology lift, not only deposits or three renewals.

Strongest copy risk: a tracker or operator adds rules and account-level lockouts; operators have non-bypassable controls and direct telemetry.

### Common Fatal Objection

Both judges independently found that the buyer and enforcement mechanism are structurally misaligned: the high-need user can bypass the tool, while the parties that own non-bypassable controls and installed distribution are incumbents. They also agreed that commodity physiology may not add enough lift to become a moat. The exact candidate must return to advisory diagnosis and structural pivoting.
