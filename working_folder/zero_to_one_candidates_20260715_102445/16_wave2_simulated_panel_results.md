## GymSignal Common Objections

Both judges found the same fatal structure: advertiser renewal/fill, not venue signing or sensing, is the economic constraint; six- to twelve-month venue rights can be outbid by established DOOH networks; and 300 sites at 3,000 PLN monthly yield is not conservative. Attention may be a vanity metric if offer conversion is sparse, while a two-sided sales/field operation conflicts with founder fit and projected margins. Strongest copy risk: an existing DOOH operator offers a higher guarantee and bundles gym inventory into its sales network.

## ScopeBand Common Objections

Both judges praised the fast paid test but agreed that current control is a behavioural promise around commodity components. The qualified denominator is much smaller than all construction microenterprises; acquiring/supporting 1,200 fragmented contractors may destroy margin; crew/client friction can break the capture→approval→deposit chain; and an incumbent Polish CRM/accounting/payment provider can bundle the feature. Strongest copy risk: installed software adds press-to-speak change orders and deposits.

## CharacterContinuity Common Objections

Both judges agreed that the proposed indie buyer may pay less than the annual licence because periodic actor sessions/direct contracts are cheaper. Temporary performer options do not solve multi-year continuity, studios may demand direct/perpetual ownership, and model portability plus human QA are expensive. Two-sided rights are not scarce before casting and can be bypassed by direct studio–performer contracting. Strongest copy risk: a studio, localisation agency, or ElevenLabs packages the same workflow using existing distribution.
