# Zero-to-One Candidate: WorkScore Passport

Wave-2 candidate generation only — not validated. All commercial numbers are hypotheses.

## One-Sentence Thesis

WorkScore Passport is a worker-paid, cross-platform record of ratings, allocation, earnings, automated decisions, and human-review outcomes that uses the EU platform-work portability right to make algorithmic work history durable when a courier or driver changes platform or challenges a harmful decision.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build data import, decision timelines, anomaly detection, evidence extraction, and human-review interfaces. The first product accepts user exports and screenshots; it does not wait for APIs. A fixed-fee labour/privacy lawyer sets boundaries, while the service never promises legal representation or case success.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first member is a Polish courier or ride-hailing driver using two or more platforms. Ratings, allocation, earnings adjustments, and deactivation evidence remain fragmented inside each platform. When access or pay changes, the worker scrambles through screenshots and support chats; switching platforms destroys context and unions/lawyers often see only the final incident.

The desired outcome is a continuously updated, platform-independent work record and a fast evidence-backed request for human review—not another generic expense tracker.

## Product Wedge

- 299 PLN founding annual membership stores user-authorised exports, screenshots, weekly earnings, ratings/reviews, allocation changes, support messages, and automated-decision notices.
- The member receives a monthly work-history summary and one standard evidence pack.
- A 199–499 PLN incident fee creates a timestamped chronology, identifies missing platform data, and prepares a human-review/data-access request; it is an administrative evidence service, not legal advice.
- As national transposition takes effect, request direct platform transmission to WorkScore where available under the Directive's portability rule.
- Cross-platform comparisons remain private to the member; only separately lawful, de-identified aggregates become research/benchmark derivatives.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Platform companies own the worker's reputation inside closed systems. The Directive creates a right for ratings/reviews to be transmitted to a requested third party, but rights become economically useful only when someone maintains continuity before a crisis and turns fragmented records into a review-ready chronology. Workers may pay a modest amount to preserve earning access if the product proves concrete incident value.

## Underused Momentum

The EU cites 28.3m platform workers and requires Directive transposition by 2 December 2026. Regulation supplies access momentum, not a budget. Multi-platform work, automated allocation, and direct worker communities create a reachable starting population.

## Beachhead Market

Polish ride-hailing and delivery workers active on at least two platforms, earning at least 4,000 PLN monthly from platform work, and already concerned about ratings, pay adjustments, or access. Exclude credit scoring, guaranteed appeals, litigation funding, and employment-status legal advice.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Founding annual plan: 299 PLN; standard later 39 PLN/month or 399 PLN/year.
- Incident evidence pack: 199–499 PLN depending on platforms/documents.
- Optional fleet/association bulk plan only after direct worker proof; the member retains data authority.
- Scaled average annual revenue hypothesis: 468 PLN per member; gross margin 80%; operating margin 24%–32% after support, privacy, acquisition, and data requests.
- Value proof: time to assemble a complete human-review request, recovered/clarified pay, restored access, corrected record, or portable evidence used on another platform. No outcome guarantee.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

- Map 30 public Polish driver/courier groups, forums, tax/accounting channels, and physical rest points; follow group promotion rules.
- Offer 100 multi-platform workers a free 15-minute export completeness check, then a fixed 299 PLN founding annual plan; no free custom incident work.
- Conversion event: paid membership, first two-platform upload, monthly update permission, and incident/transfer request where needed.
- First ten: direct community and rest-point outreach using a privacy-first Polish demo.
- First 100: member referrals credited only after a paid annual renewal, search content on export/review procedures, and transparent aggregate incident reports. Do not depend on a union/platform partnership.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed compounding Tier 2:** member-authorised direct transfer requests, longitudinal cross-platform history, incident chronology, and appeal/review outcomes. The legal right is non-exclusive and any competitor can request transfer; switching cost arises only from accumulated continuity, trusted custody, multi-platform schema, and prior outcomes.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Needed resource: paid member permission and continuing platform records.

1. Counsel maps Polish privacy/labour/legal-service boundaries and exact Directive/national implementation status.
2. Build an encrypted manual vault and two-platform schema using member exports.
3. Convert ten of 100 checked workers into paid annual members before automation.
4. Deliver five incident packs and document platform response/outcome.
5. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, prove 500 paid members, 100 monthly active records, 50 incident packs, at least two usable direct-transfer procedures, and acceptable support/CAC.

An unknown founder can recruit workers directly and deliver value with their own records; platform cooperation is not required before paid proof.

## Distribution Advantage

Each member's ongoing record and referral can lower acquisition. Public, procedure-specific content captures high-intent workers before severe incidents. Cross-platform support is the wedge against platform-native exports; privacy and member control distinguish the service from employer/fleet surveillance.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with Polish courier/ride-hailing. Expand to other EU languages and online freelance platforms only after one country's retention and incident economics work. Build structured human-review benchmarks and worker-authorised agent interfaces. Unions/lawyers can use member-exported packs, but WorkScore keeps the continuous record and direct relationship.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

The Directive and Polish transposition provide timely access momentum. Polish language, platform procedures, tax context, and local communities support a beachhead. Competitors can use the same law; durability must come from longitudinal records, trust, schema, and distribution.

## Compounding Advantage

Each member month links ratings, earnings, allocation, automated notices, worker actions, review requests, and outcomes. This improves anomaly detection, missing-evidence prompts, platform procedure knowledge, and review preparation. Ordinary delivery builds behavioural/algorithmic-management data, human-AI review interfaces, proprietary taxonomies, ML capability, and worker distribution.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–30: legal/privacy memo, 30 worker interviews, two-platform schema.
- Days 31–60: secure vault, export check, and 100-worker outreach.
- Days 61–120: ten paid annual memberships and five complete incident packs.
- Days 121–180: 30 paid members, at least ten monthly updates, five platform responses, one renewal/prepaid second year or member referral cohort, and positive support contribution.

Paid proof is a worker payment. Founder remains employed/five hours daily.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Pre-proof maximum 22,000 PLN: legal/privacy 6k; security/prototype 7k; outreach/translation 4k; support/contingency 5k.
- Up to 55,000 PLN after 30 payers and successful delivery.
- Up to 100,000 PLN after 500 paid members or repeatable acquisition/retention.
- Family capital excluded until recurring demand and contribution economics.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after 1,000+ paying members from multiple independent channels, annual renewal/contracted recurring demand, positive contribution after support, repeatable CAC below first-year gross profit, operations not founder-bound, and 12 months personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Members can export/delete their records. Reusable assets are privacy/security architecture, platform schemas, procedure research, and de-identified derivatives lawfully retained. A legal-claims or union business is a different candidate.

## Critical Evidence

The Directive provides automated-decision and third-party portability momentum; the EU worker denominator is large. No source proves paid membership demand or platform implementation quality.

## Critical Hypotheses

Workers pay 299 PLN before severe harm; communities provide economical access; direct transfer works; ongoing records retain users; incident packs create clear value; 20,000 members can be acquired with 24% operating margin.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than ten paid members from 100 qualified workers.
- No useful platform response/outcome across ten incident packs.
- National implementation/direct transfer is materially weaker than assumed and manual exports do not retain users.
- Six-month active/paid retention below 50% after 100 members.
- Support/legal complexity consumes more than 35% of revenue or CAC exceeds first-year gross profit.
- Conservative founder wealth falls below 5m after real retention/CAC/support inputs.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

22,000 PLN pre-proof; 100,000 PLN first year. Founder product/data/sales; fixed-fee labour/privacy lawyer; part-time support after payment; no platform or union partnership required.
