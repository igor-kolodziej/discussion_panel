## Distilled Failure Lessons From 76 Tried Shapes

1. Owning a sensor, parser, analysis, benchmark, or report does not mean owning the buyer's decision or workflow.
2. Physiological and behavioral data matter only when the paid contract grants lawful, durable reuse rights with workable withdrawal and deletion economics.
3. A participant panel is recruitable supply, not a moat, unless transaction routing, longitudinal continuity, or hard-to-recreate outcomes compound.
4. Purchasable inventory, ordinary components, common test rigs, and filed-but-unoptioned patents are Tier 3 rather than controlled resources.
5. Regulation creates urgency but not a budget or vendor mandate.
6. Clinical, safety, certification, QMS, and OEM trust cannot be assumed inside a six-month unknown-founder sale.
7. Intelligent-wearable businesses are channel businesses before they are sensor businesses; incumbents often own installed buyers, support, conformity, and distribution.
8. Reachable pilots do not rescue a small denominator, implausible annual account value, or expected cases requiring near-total niche penetration.
9. Hardware whose main feature can be added by an incumbent through routine development fails unless the startup controls data, workflow, distribution, or switching cost that the feature cannot recreate.
10. The strongest desired shape routes a recurring economically mandatory event and accumulates a future-lab asset during normal paid delivery.

## Rejected Shape Families

- Reference phantoms, calibration coupons, artificial materials, and internal QA rigs.
- Generic wearable components, accessories, chargers, connectors, batteries, or repair parts without exclusive rights.
- Clinical-study QA, archival, vigilance, or acceptance software requiring sponsor/OEM/QMS authority.
- Accessibility or biometric participant panels that depend on revocable, non-exclusive data supply.
- Smart-PPE and safety devices requiring certification or incumbent channel acceptance before paid proof.
- Human–AI approval hardware or privacy gates that cannot enforce behavior inside the underlying platform.
- Generic AI wrappers, dashboards, CRMs, consulting, evidence memos, and custom research projects.

## Material-Improvement Standard

A prior shape may re-enter only if at least one structural element changes: paying buyer, painful event, budget owner, acquisition channel, value capture, trust mechanism, data rights, scarce resource, transaction control, or founder-wealth denominator. A renamed product or added feature does not qualify.
