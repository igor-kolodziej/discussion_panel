# Zero-to-One Candidate: CatchPool AI

Candidate generation only — not validated. All commercial numbers are hypotheses to test.

## One-Sentence Thesis

CatchPool AI turns hidden AI-agent mistakes into a paid internal correction market: employers pre-fund bounties for verified catches, while the payout workflow builds a proprietary prompt-action-correction-outcome corpus and prevention models.

## Founder Fit

The founder understands AI/ML, enterprise analytics, behaviour, experimentation, and Polish corporate work. The first version is a secure manual workflow with structured evidence and simple integrations. It can be sold and delivered alongside employment with an external privacy lawyer and no hardware or clinical dependency.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is an operations, transformation, quality, or risk leader at a 300–3,000-person Polish/CEE business-services centre using generative AI or agents in document-heavy work such as invoices, claims, orders, research, support, or master data.

Employees catch hallucinations, wrong actions, fabricated citations, unsafe data handling, or silent rework. Reporting creates extra work, can feel career-negative, and rarely produces immediate benefit for the reporter. Current channels—service desk tickets, QA samples, risk registers, and manager escalation—underreport near misses and separate the error from the actual correction and avoided loss.

## Product Wedge

A six-week “AI Catch Pool” in one invited workflow:

- employer prepays a 10,000 PLN bounty pool and pays a 15,000 PLN pilot fee;
- employees submit a minimal evidence packet: AI input/context fingerprint, action/output, proposed correction, and affected business event;
- duplicate reports merge; a pre-agreed rubric verifies severity, avoided rework/loss, and whether the catch produced a correction;
- bounties of 100–1,500 PLN are paid from the employer's pool after verification;
- the employer receives a prevention sprint and a repeat-error ledger; the startup retains only contracted, de-identified taxonomies and derived features suitable for cross-client learning.

No employee monitoring score or individual productivity ranking is produced.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

AI governance usually asks employees to report problems for the organisation's benefit. Bug bounties work because they create an immediate market price for valuable disclosure. In internal AI operations, the same mechanism can buy examples that ordinary sampling misses—provided the programme rewards verified correction value, not report volume. The economically important event is the payout tied to an accepted fix, not a dashboard entry.

## Underused Momentum

- ABSL reports a concentrated Polish business-services market with high AI adoption and increased training.
- AI-incident taxonomies are still fragmented, making labelled operational examples strategically valuable.
- AI literacy obligations and executive pressure put attention on reliable deployment, though neither guarantees budget.
- Employers already understand innovation awards, quality incentives, and security bug bounties; CatchPool applies the mechanism to a narrow AI workflow.

## Beachhead Market

Polish/CEE business-services centres with 300–3,000 staff, one high-volume generative-AI workflow, an English-speaking operations team, and a local transformation or quality budget. Exclude clinical, credit, employee-selection, and safety-critical decisions in the POC.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Pilot: 15,000 PLN service/platform fee plus 10,000 PLN client-owned bounty pool.
- Annual: 60,000–160,000 PLN platform and prevention fee per workflow, plus 15%–20% administration fee on bounty pools and optional 20,000–50,000 PLN model-improvement sprints.
- Pilot gross-margin hypothesis: 60% after adjudication, privacy, and workshop time.
- Scaled gross-margin hypothesis: 75%; operating-margin hypothesis: 20%–30%.
- Employer value proof: accepted catches must document at least 3× the pilot fee in avoided rework, corrected transaction value, prevented recurrence, or auditable control improvement. Bounties are client money and not counted as revenue except the administration fee.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

- Assemble a named list from the 2,081 centres in ABSL's published denominator, public employer directories, job postings mentioning copilots/automation, and local shared-services associations.
- First outreach: 100 operations/transformation/quality leaders by email and LinkedIn, each with a two-page “hidden AI error market” design for one public workflow and a fixed 25,000 PLN total pilot commitment.
- Conversion event: signed workflow scope, paid 15,000 PLN fee, transferred or employer-administered 10,000 PLN bounty pool, employee communication approval, evidence-access method, and derived-data clause.
- First ten: founder-led Poland/CEE outbound and small invitation-only roundtables using anonymised pilot examples, not influencer distribution.
- First 100: partner only after proof with RPA/AI implementation boutiques whose contracts already include adoption and QA; pay referral fees from first-year gross profit without granting data rights or exclusivity.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed compounding Tier 2, not yet proven:** a signed employer mandate routing bounties and accepted corrections for the invited AI workflow, plus rights to retain a de-identified error/correction/outcome taxonomy. It becomes stronger as CatchPool integrates with the workflow and owns cross-client prevention benchmarks; it is not Tier 1 because the employer can stop or recreate the programme.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Needed resource: one live workflow, a funded payout pool, employee access, correction outcomes, and derived-data rights.

Bootstrap:

1. Interview 20 operations and quality leaders using a concrete sample rubric.
2. Build a secure form, duplicate matcher, human review queue, and auditable payout register using existing tools.
3. Contract a privacy lawyer for the participation notice, client DPA, de-identification boundary, and works-council/employee-monitoring check.
4. Sell one fixed six-week pilot before integration. Employees can paste redacted evidence or upload client-approved hashes/screenshots; no direct production-system access is required for proof.
5. Prove control with a funded pool and accepted claims; prove compounding with repeat error categories and a signed derived-taxonomy clause.

An unknown founder can sell a bounded operational experiment because it does not claim authority over the client's entire AI programme and does not require a long platform replacement.

## Distribution Advantage

The first distribution wedge is Poland's concentrated business-services buyer map. Each pilot creates an executive-ready loss/correction case study, a worker-facing participation template, and an implementation pattern for the next similar workflow. Later, implementation boutiques distribute CatchPool because it makes the reliability of their AI deployments visible; they receive referral economics but not ownership of the workflow or dataset.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with a single document-heavy workflow. Expand to:

1. recurring bounty seasons and always-on high-severity reporting;
2. prevention rules and evaluation sets built from accepted corrections;
3. agent rollback and human-intervention triggers;
4. cross-company benchmarks by workflow and error class;
5. outcome-priced reliability contracts once sufficient loss data exists.

Generic incident tools can add rewards, but CatchPool's advantage must become the accepted-correction corpus, payout calibration, employee trust, and workflow integrations that reduce repeat errors.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers a dense shared-services beachhead, multilingual workflows, and founder-reachable buyers. EU privacy and worker-governance requirements reward a design that explicitly avoids productivity scoring and minimises raw employee data. This is a go-to-market and trust advantage, not a legal barrier to competitors.

## Compounding Advantage

Every accepted payout yields a high-information label: what the AI attempted, what a human noticed, how it was corrected, estimated impact, recurrence, and preventive change. This corpus improves severity triage, duplicate detection, evaluation suites, and intervention triggers. Ordinary paid delivery accumulates human-AI behavioural data, proprietary datasets/IP, advanced ML, real-time human-computer review interfaces, and distribution into enterprise AI workflows.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash while the founder remains employed; optional distributions are capped at 5%. The strong case reinvests 100%. Reinvestment funds security, adjudication automation, integrations, worker trust research, and evaluator datasets that increase gross margin and switching cost.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–30: 20 buyer interviews, one privacy/workplace-law memo, and a severity/value rubric.
- Days 31–60: secure workflow, payout, participation, and derived-data templates; build manual portal.
- Days 61–120: 100-account outreach, 15 qualified calls, five fixed-price proposals.
- Days 121–180: one paid 15,000 PLN pilot plus a 10,000 PLN bounty pool; recruit at least 20 participating employees; adjudicate at least ten submissions; show at least three accepted corrections and one prevented recurrence.

No complete platform or direct client-system integration is needed. The founder stays employed and caps work at five hours daily.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Tranche 1: maximum 32,000 PLN before paid proof — legal/privacy 7k, secure prototype 8k, contractor adjudication 4k, outreach/roundtable 5k, security/contingency 8k.
- Tranche 2: cumulative 65,000 PLN only after paid pilot, successful delivery, and positive contribution.
- Tranche 3: up to 100,000 PLN only after three independent payers or one repeatable acquisition channel.
- Family capital remains excluded until repeat demand and economics are documented.
- Maximum pre-proof loss: 32,000 PLN plus founder time.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after at least five independent paying centres or ten contracted workflows, recurring gross profit covers 1.5× planned company burn, contribution margin is positive, one acquisition channel has repeatable conversion, and the founder has 12 months personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If bounties fail, reusable assets include a privacy-reviewed AI-error schema, workflow-specific evaluation sets, buyer interviews, and a prevention toolkit. A consulting fallback is not counted as success and would require a new business evaluation. Client raw evidence remains segregated and is deleted according to contract.

## Critical Evidence

- ABSL documents a concentrated installed base and high AI adoption.
- OECD documents the need for comparable AI incident information.
- Manual payout and adjudication can be tested before integration.

## Critical Hypotheses

- A 25,000 PLN total commitment is available from operations, quality, or transformation budgets.
- Verified catches yield measurable economic value rather than generic complaints.
- Employee rewards increase valuable reporting without inducing fraud or overreporting.
- 90 recurring workflows at 120,000 PLN are reachable without enterprise sales costs overwhelming margin.

## Kill Criteria

- No paid pilot within 180 days or fewer than five qualified calls from 100 named accounts.
- Client will fund training or a dashboard but not a real bounty pool.
- Fewer than three economically meaningful accepted corrections from 20 participants in six weeks.
- Adjudication and programme management exceed 50% of fee revenue with no standardisation path.
- Legal/privacy review requires individual monitoring or labour mechanisms clients will not accept.
- All serious clients refuse useful derived-taxonomy rights or keep CatchPool outside the correction workflow.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Maximum 32,000 PLN before proof; 100,000 PLN in the 12-month founder-funded cycle.
- Founder: sales, product, ML, facilitation, five hours daily maximum.
- Fixed-fee privacy/employment lawyer.
- Part-time adjudicator or domain QA contractor after payment.
- Security contractor after the first signed DPA.
