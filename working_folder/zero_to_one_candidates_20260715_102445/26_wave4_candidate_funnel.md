## Material Improvement Records

### PPG validation research → PulsePhantom Bench

- Converts one-off human validation into a repeatable non-clinical engineering regression tool; it does not replace clinical validation or claim certification.
- Combines a modular optical/mechanical wrist phantom, contact-pressure/motion fixture, controlled waveforms, version comparison, and annual cartridges/software.
- Requires paid design-partner reservations before a full multi-condition device and makes the first product a narrow green/red/IR PPG bench.
- Treats recent inclusive-phantom research and IEC test methods as timing, while explicitly testing whether buyers will purchase this particular fixture.

### Dexterity Rights Foundry → HarnessCell

- Stops asking robotics companies to buy generic demonstrations or accept retained dataset rights as the primary product.
- Customers buy urgently needed finished harnesses; the company owns only generalised process telemetry, fixtures, and manufacturing methods while customer design IP stays isolated.
- Every paid assembly produces step-level manipulation/error/test labels needed for traceability and AI-guided work, so data acquisition is a by-product rather than the sale.
- The earlier buyer-on-robot/exclusivity objection no longer applies, but intense rapid-harness competition creates a new veto to test.

### EOL-device continuity → NeuroPort Uptime Library

- Uses one public connector transition only as initial urgency, not as the long-term revenue base.
- Sells a multi-vendor research-only adapter/trigger/loaner library and annual signal-chain uptime contract from the beginning.
- Owns tested physical inventory, validation fixtures, compatibility protocols, and customer configuration history rather than coordinating manufacturer service.
- The prior shrinking-inventory failure still applies if labs will buy only one-off EOL parts rather than recurring multi-vendor uptime.

## Finalists
