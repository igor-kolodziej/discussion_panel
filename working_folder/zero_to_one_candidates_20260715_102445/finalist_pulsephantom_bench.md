# Zero-to-One Candidate: PulsePhantom Bench

Wave-4 candidate generation only — not validated. All commercial numbers are hypotheses unless cited.

## One-Sentence Thesis

PulsePhantom Bench sells wearable R&D teams a compact, non-clinical regression fixture that makes wrist-PPG sensor performance repeatable across optical skin properties, vessel depth, contact pressure, motion, and waveforms—then turns every firmware and hardware revision into an automated comparable test.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build the test software, AI analysis, automated reports, experiment database, and customer discovery while employed. A contract optical/mechanical engineer designs the first fixture after deposits; a metrology adviser reviews calibration. The company does not claim medical-device certification, replace human validation, or certify clinical accuracy.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is an EU sports/wellness wearable startup, corporate wearable R&D group, or university lab developing a reflective wrist PPG device. A change to LED drive, detector geometry, strap pressure, enclosure, filtering, or firmware can alter performance, yet live-subject tests are variable, slow, and expensive. Teams use their own wrists, ad hoc silicone blocks, electronic signal generators, human studies, and specialist simulators; version comparisons often mix hardware, physiology, and motion effects.

The buyer wants to catch a regression before another human study or pilot run, document the exact condition that failed, and reproduce it after remediation.

## Product Wedge

- A 10,000 PLN paid Regression Design Sprint: test-plan mapping, a simple optical/contact-pressure fixture, baseline-versus-new-device comparison, and a design-partner specification. The team ships two non-clinical devices or runs the session remotely.
- An 8,000 PLN non-refundable beta reservation, credited to hardware, only after the sprint or a technical demo.
- First product hypothesis: 28,000–40,000 PLN bench plus 8,000 PLN/year software/calibration-cartridge subscription. It varies green/red/IR optical response, programmable pulse waveform, replaceable optical/tissue-depth layers, controlled contact pressure, and repeatable motion; it logs raw output through the customer's research interface.
- Results are engineering regression evidence, not a clinical, regulatory, IEC-conformity, skin-tone-equivalence, or safety certificate.
- Customer device data remain isolated. PulsePhantom owns its fixture designs, calibration methods, synthetic conditions, reference traces, and only contractually permitted de-identified performance derivatives.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Wearable teams spend scarce human-study capacity answering questions that should first be deterministic engineering tests. Electrical patient simulators generate signals, while recent research phantoms can reproduce optical/mechanical conditions; the gap is a compact product that combines those conditions with version control and an automated pass/fail history for a wearable team. The economic value is fewer wasted pilot builds and human sessions, not “more data.”

## Underused Momentum

IEC now defines a PPG wearable heart-rate measurement protocol, recent research demonstrates dynamic wrist phantoms across skin tone and vessel depth, and commercial PPG simulators prove a test-equipment budget. None establishes demand for this exact integrated fixture. Faster wearable iteration and scrutiny of skin-tone/motion performance create timing while also attracting established test-equipment competitors.

## Beachhead Market

European non-clinical wrist-wearable R&D teams using green/red/IR reflective PPG, with fewer than 50 engineers and at least two hardware/firmware revisions per year. Start with sports, wellness, academic, and research products; exclude clinical release decisions, pulse-oximetry claims, implantables, and teams without accessible raw/research output.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Sprint: 10,000 PLN; target 65% gross margin after contractor time.
- Beta reservation: 8,000 PLN credited to a 28,000–40,000 PLN bench.
- Standard annual software/calibration subscription: 8,000 PLN; replacement optical layers/cartridges and calibration: 2,000–8,000 PLN/year.
- At production, hardware COGS hypothesis 10,000–13,000 PLN at 32,000 PLN average price; blended gross margin 62%–70%; operating margin 25%–32% after R&D, sales, calibration support, returns, and quality.
- The product must be self-serve after onboarding. If every customer needs bespoke optics or interpretation, margins fail.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

- Create 100 named prospects from wearable-company product pages, EU-funded project databases, recent PPG-wearable publications/patents, conference exhibitor lists, and engineering job posts.
- Send an engineering artifact: the same device/firmware tested under two contact pressures and motion conditions, with reproducible raw traces and no public naming of the device.
- Fixed conversion event: 10,000 PLN sprint or 8,000 PLN beta reservation, signed non-clinical scope, raw-data access, and one agreed regression condition.
- First ten: direct founder outreach to technical leads; no distributor, university endorsement, or standards-body relationship required.
- First 100: publish anonymised repeatability studies, ship evaluation units against deposits, attend technical conferences from revenue, and allow paid referrals by design houses while keeping the hardware/subscription contract direct.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed Tier 1 IP/physical control with a compounding Tier 2 data layer:** company-owned phantom mechanics/material recipes, calibration fixtures, test waveform/condition library, firmware, automated report logic, and serialised reference benches. Version histories and permitted de-identified failure patterns compound after delivery. Present control is unproved until the prototype is owned, repeatable, and customers pay to route revisions through it.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Interview 20 target engineers about current regression rigs, human-study cost, and acceptable evidence.
2. Build a one-wavelength/simple-pressure demonstration from off-the-shelf optics, actuator, silicone layers, and a customer-accessible research wearable.
3. Sell three 10,000 PLN sprints or 8,000 PLN reservations before building the multi-wavelength automated beta.
4. Contract the optical/mechanical engineer only against paid scope; protect new fixture/software inventions and document freedom-to-operate questions before patent expense.
5. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, own three beta benches, calibration/reference fixtures, at least 50 reusable test conditions, five paying customers, ten version comparisons, and one documented caught regression later fixed by a customer.

The founder can buy all first-stage components and access customers directly. Serious capital waits for paid repeatability proof.

## Distribution Advantage

The target is enumerable from public technical artifacts rather than broad consumer advertising. A good regression report is shareable inside engineering and QA teams and can travel with an employee or design house. Each supported device geometry and condition shortens the next demonstration, but repeatable outbound and referrals remain hypotheses.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with reflective wrist PPG regression. Expand to pulse-oximetry engineering tests only after expertise, then contact-pressure/motion fixtures for EDA, bioimpedance, ECG electrodes, EMG, and other intelligent wearables. The durable advantage must be a faster, trusted multi-condition regression workflow and reference corpus, not a claim that phantoms or signal generators are novel.

WhaleTeq, medical-test-equipment makers, wearable companies, and university labs can reproduce parts. They can catch up if the fixture is merely a signal source; modular physical conditions, calibration, version history, and developer workflow must produce demonstrably lower test time and higher regression detection.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers lower optical/mechanical prototyping and software cost, EU shipping, and proximity to the IEC-driven European wearable ecosystem. EU research grants and standards create informed buyers. Geography and standards do not prevent entry; narrow price, rapid customisation, and accumulated device-condition evidence are the local defence.

## Compounding Advantage

Ordinary hardware delivery builds wearable hardware/optics expertise, advanced biosignal AI/ML, proprietary phantom/calibration IP, reference test data, research and wearable-company relationships, skilled engineer access, and specialised distribution. Each supported sensor geometry and observed regression expands the condition library and improves automated diagnosis.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after ten independent hardware customers, five annual subscriptions, positive fully loaded contribution on three consecutive builds, a repeatable acquisition channel, documented repeatability and regression value, recurring gross profit covering 1.5× business burn, and 12 months personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Reusable assets include optical/mechanical fixtures, control software, synthetic PPG waveforms, calibration procedures, signal-analysis code, customer interviews, and wearable-engineer distribution. Equipment can be resold at uncertain value. A generic wearable consultancy is not the fallback.

## Critical Evidence

Standards and research support repeatable PPG testing and inclusive phantom conditions; commercial simulators prove existing spend. No evidence yet proves an integrated small bench's price, the 500-customer denominator, or superiority over WhaleTeq/internal rigs.

## Critical Hypotheses

Three teams prepay; the beta is buildable under 100k; contact/motion/optical layers reproduce conditions sufficiently for engineering regression; self-serve support works; 500 teams buy at 32k plus recurring software.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than three paid sprints/reservations after 30 qualified proposals by day 120.
- Fixture repeatability is insufficient to distinguish customer versions.
- Buyers require certification/clinical validation rather than engineering regression.
- No quantified time, study, or respin saving over existing tools.
- Gross margin remains below 55% or support remains bespoke after five customers.
- Freedom-to-operate requires unaffordable licensing.
- Bottom-up evidence makes 500 conservative customers implausible or founder wealth falls below 5m.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

35,000 PLN before paid proof; 100,000 PLN first year. Founder software/AI/sales; contract optical/mechanical engineer; fixed-fee metrology and IP/legal advisers; contract electronics/manufacturing support after payment.
