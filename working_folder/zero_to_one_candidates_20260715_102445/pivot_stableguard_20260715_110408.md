# Zero-to-One Pivot: StableGuard Bankroll Control Rail

Pivot generated from TiltLock v1 plus the advisory diagnosis and two independent panel objections. No score or validation is assigned.

## Preserved Insight

High-stakes digital performers often know the correct rule but violate it when fatigue, arousal, and losses impair judgment. A precommitted intervention is valuable only when the paying party is economically aligned with compliance and controls a consequence the performer cannot casually bypass.

## Structural Change

Change the buyer from an individual poker player buying a local blocker to a professional poker backing stable or organised staking group that supplies bankroll under a player contract. Change the control mechanism from bypassable OS blocking to the stable's next bankroll release, continued backing, and contract-compliance workflow. Physiology becomes an optional experimental input; hand-history and bankroll-rule compliance must create value without it.

## Target Niche and Buyer

Small and mid-sized online-poker backing stables managing 20–200 active players, paying or allocating player bankroll, and already requiring hand-history or result reporting. The economic buyer is the stable owner, risk manager, or head coach. The user is a contracted player who opts into sensitive-data processing as part of a transparent risk programme.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The recurring costly event is preventable stake-capital loss or player churn after a backed player breaks pre-agreed loss, duration, game-selection, or cooldown rules. The stable currently discovers violations after bankroll damage through spreadsheets, chat, coach review, or delayed cashier reports.

Hypothesis: a stable will pay 15,000–25,000 PLN for an eight-week, 10–20-player pilot and 500–1,500 PLN per active player annually if the programme reduces rule-breaking loss and coach-review time. The bounty is economically aligned: the stable protects deployed capital and player lifetime value.

## Value and Profit Wedge

- Import player-authorised hand histories, session summaries, and the stable's bankroll ledger.
- Before play, record the stable/player risk agreement: permitted games, stake band, duration, drawdown, and mandatory review/cooldown rules.
- Flag objective breaches and require a documented review before the stable issues the next bankroll tranche or continues the backing contract.
- Provide no poker action, bet, or table-selection advice.
- Run a behaviour-only baseline first; add wearable state only if it produces measurable lift in predicting objective breaches.

Revenue is a paid pilot followed by annual per-active-player software/workflow fees. Stable-held bankroll is never held or transferred by the startup.

## Domination Wedge

Own the contracted compliance and bankroll-release evidence workflow for a narrow segment of serious stables rather than competing for individual player attention. Standardise rule templates, breach evidence, player appeals, coach interventions, and stable-level loss curves across many small operators that cannot build an internal system.

## Acquisition Channel

Build a named list of 100 public backing stables, staking communities, and coaching organisations from public websites, tournament sponsorships, player application pages, Discords, poker forums, and staking marketplaces. Send each owner a fixed 15,000 PLN pilot offer built from its public player rules. Conversion requires a paid invoice, ten contracted players, authorised hand-history access, stable bankroll-ledger export, and a clause that the next stake/review event is routed through the programme.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed contracted Tier 1 workflow/transaction control:** the stable MSA makes StableGuard's compliance record a required input to the next bankroll release or continued-backing decision for enrolled players. The startup also receives durable rights to de-identified breach/intervention/outcome taxonomies. It does not control poker accounts or client money; the stable retains final employment/contract authority.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Identify 100 stables with public application or backing activity; interview 15 owners/coaches.
2. Secure a fixed-fee gaming/privacy/contract review and a player notice.
3. Sell one 15,000 PLN eight-week pilot before building integrations.
4. Deliver with CSV hand histories, a stable ledger export, signed risk rules, and a manual review queue for 10–20 players.
5. Proof within 180 days and 35,000 PLN: one independent paid stable, at least ten enrolled players, at least three objective breaches, one documented bankroll-release/review decision routed through the system, and positive pilot contribution.
6. Proof within 12 months and 100,000 PLN: three independent paying stables, at least 100 active player-months, a repeat renewal, and retained de-identified taxonomy rights.

No operator partnership, poker-platform API, clinical claim, custom sensor, or startup custody of bankroll is required.

## Expansion Path

First expand to adjacent poker stables and tournament-backing groups. Only after repeatability, test contract-based risk workflows for proprietary trading academies or esports teams; these are separate markets and do not rescue failure in poker.

## Minimum People Stack

- Founder: product, data, ML, sales, and pilot analysis, maximum five hours daily.
- Fixed-fee gaming/privacy/commercial lawyer.
- Part-time poker-stable operations adviser paid for bounded discovery, not an exclusive channel.
- Contract engineer only after the first paid pilot.

## Principal Risk

The buyer universe may be too small, opaque, low-budget, or reputationally difficult to support a 5m PLN founder-wealth path. Stables may prefer human coach discretion, refuse reusable rights, or lack clean bankroll-ledger data. A contract may “route” review without making StableGuard hard to replace.

## First Disconfirming Test

Contact 100 qualified stables with the fixed pilot. Kill the shape if fewer than five owner calls occur, no one pays within 180 days, no payer makes the compliance record a required input to a real bankroll/review decision, or the account/value denominator cannot support a non-heroic conservative wealth case.

## Evidence Preserved

- Players can export their own hand histories.
- Platform policy makes non-strategic risk compliance safer than real-time poker advice.
- The original proof path showed that paid manual delivery and commodity wearables are capital-efficient.
- Panel feedback establishes that the economically aligned enforcer, not the impaired individual, must own the consequence.

## New Hypotheses

- At least 100 reachable stables exist with 20+ active players and formal bankroll rules.
- One stable will pay 15,000–25,000 PLN and route a real bankroll/review decision through a new unknown vendor.
- A 500–1,500 PLN annual player fee can support software margins without founder-heavy coaching.
- Stables can lawfully grant useful de-identified taxonomy rights alongside player transparency and withdrawal/deletion rules.
- Behaviour-only compliance produces enough value; physiology adds optional lift rather than being required.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

TiltLock sold a bypassable restriction to the person whose impaired state undermined demand, and incumbents owned stronger controls. StableGuard sells capital-protection workflow to the party that funds play and can make the next bankroll/review event contingent on compliance. It changes buyer, budget, authority, channel, value capture, control point, and minimum viable product; it is not a renamed consumer blocker.

## Pivot Priority

StableGuard is the only recommended pivot to research and return to `$evaluate-zero-to-one` because it directly repairs buyer/enforcer misalignment while preserving the state-dependent intervention insight. It should be abandoned immediately if the public buyer denominator or bankroll-routing willingness is too weak; no second cosmetic variant is warranted.
