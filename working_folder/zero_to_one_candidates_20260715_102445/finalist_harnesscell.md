# Zero-to-One Candidate: HarnessCell

Wave-4 candidate generation only — not validated. All commercial numbers are hypotheses unless cited.

## One-Sentence Thesis

HarnessCell is a ten-day, low-volume cable-harness microfactory for CEE robotics and intelligent-wearable teams whose AI-guided digital travellers, instrumented assembly steps, fixtures, and electrical tests turn every paid order into a proprietary manipulation-and-quality dataset.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build quoting, work instructions, computer vision, traceability, and customer acquisition, with practical electrical-installation access already identified in the earlier run. An IPC-trained contract assembler and quality adviser deliver first builds after payment. The first scope is non-safety-critical low-voltage prototypes—not automotive, aerospace, medical, mains, or certified production.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The buyer is a Polish/CEE mobile-robot, drone, research-wearable, or industrial-device team needing 5–200 low-voltage harnesses through frequent revisions. Engineers currently hand-build internally, wait for a job shop, or use a global supplier. A wrong pin, late connector, or four-week quote can block a prototype build; low volumes receive poor attention.

## Product Wedge

- 5,000 PLN paid design-for-assembly/setup package: BOM/pinout review, approved drawing, digital traveller, first-article fixture/test profile, and one sample.
- Batch pricing at components plus a quoted assembly/test charge; 50% deposit before parts, balance after first-article approval.
- Ten-business-day target after all components/design answers; every unit receives continuity/pinout test, serial record, and customer-visible traceability.
- Customer owns its drawings/product IP. HarnessCell owns generic work-instruction software, fixtures not uniquely paid/assigned to the customer, de-identified process telemetry, error taxonomy, and derived manufacturing models only under explicit contract.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Fast low-volume harness supply is already competitive, so speed alone is not the insight. The overlooked unit of value is fast revision with auditable first-article learning: hardware teams repeatedly change the same family of harnesses, while generic job shops quote each revision and retrain workers. A retained fixture/test/traveller history can shorten the next revision and make high-mix work profitable.

## Underused Momentum

Robotics and wearables require high-mix interconnects; IPC provides a trainable quality standard; inexpensive cameras, tool sensors, and AI make step-level digital guidance affordable. The same tools are available to incumbents, so only accumulated revision economics and trace data can create advantage.

## Beachhead Market

CEE teams with a live robotics or non-clinical intelligent-wearable prototype, 5–200 units per revision, low-voltage connectors, and at least two expected revisions/year. Exclude regulated/high-voltage/safety-critical work until certifications and capital follow paid demand.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- 5,000–15,000 PLN NRE/setup per harness family; typical first batch 10,000–40,000 PLN; annual recurring family value hypothesis 100,000–300,000 PLN.
- Target 45%–50% gross margin on blended NRE, component handling, assembly, testing, and expedited work; 20%–25% operating margin after labour, scrap, quality, sales, rent, and software.
- Deposits fund customer-specific parts. Standard crimpers/test fixtures are reinvestment assets.
- Kill if the dataset adds no measurable quoting/training/defect advantage; the manufacturing business must stand on cash economics.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

- Build 100 named accounts from PARP/fair catalogues, robotics/wearable product pages, EU project databases, incubator portfolios, job posts, and public prototype announcements.
- Offer a 48-hour DFM response and fixed 5,000 PLN setup; conversion is payment, released BOM/drawing, parts deposit, and first-article signoff.
- First ten through direct technical outreach and sample teardown content; no integrator partnership.
- First 100 through repeat families, public lead-time/defect benchmarks, engineering referrals, and a customer portal. Volume transfer is optional; recurring high-mix work remains the core.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed Tier 1 owned process-data/IP layer over a Tier 2 recurring workflow:** signed data/IP terms; company-owned digital-traveller software, fixtures/test profiles, step-level process/error/test corpus, and revision history. It is unproved and is not exclusive customer design data. Incumbents can install cameras and software; catch-up is hard only if accumulated trace data measurably reduces quote time, training, rework, and lead time.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Buy only universal bench tools, continuity tester, camera, and ESD equipment after 20 interviews.
2. Use a standard sample harness to create the digital traveller and traceability report.
3. Sell a 5,000 PLN setup plus parts deposit before buying customer inventory or specialised tooling.
4. Deliver from a small compliant bench with a contract IPC-experienced assembler; pursue certification after repeat orders.
5. Within 12 months/100,000 PLN, own three workstations, fixtures/test profiles for 20 recurring families, 5,000+ labelled assembly steps, five paying customers, and three paid revisions that demonstrate faster setup/reduced defects.

## Distribution Advantage

The public hardware-team list makes proof reachable. Each successful revision and retained fixture creates a reason to return. The company must demonstrate paid referrals and repeat families; local proximity alone is not a channel moat.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with low-voltage harnesses for robots/wearables, expand to sensor subassemblies, intelligent-textile interconnects, pilot box-build, and AI-guided high-mix workcells. Long-term IP can power internal automation or licensed work instructions. Compete on revision economics/traceability in a narrow vertical, not commodity unit cost.

Loombotic, OurPCB, MiniProto, Polish shops, and larger EMS providers can copy the visible offer. If the digital traveller does not create a compounding advantage before they respond, the shape is ordinary contract manufacturing.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers skilled electrical labour, EU shipping, and proximity to CEE hardware teams. Polish/English documentation and ten-day local iteration can beat distant supply for urgent revisions. Existing Polish suppliers sharply limit this defence.

## Compounding Advantage

Ordinary delivery accumulates multimodal expert-manipulation video, tool/settings/connector data, defects, rework, and electrical outcomes; owned fixtures and revision histories improve quoting, guidance, QA, and automation. It builds advanced AI/ML, wearable/robotics hardware expertise, proprietary IP/data, skilled technician access, capital, and hardware-team distribution.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative/expected reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash during employment and cap distributions at 5%; strong reinvests 100%. Reinvestment funds certification, workcells, testing, automation, standard connector stock, and sales—not speculative volume inventory.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–30: 20 interviews, bench scope, 100 accounts, standard sample traveller.
- Days 31–75: 30 DFM offers and ten technical reviews.
- Days 76–120: paid 5,000 PLN setup plus parts deposit.
- Days 121–180: approved first article, delivered batch, fully loaded margin, and proposal for a paid revision/second customer.

Founder stays employed/five hours daily; contract labour delivers paid production.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Pre-proof maximum 30,000 PLN: bench/test/ESD 12k; standard tools 6k; contractor/training 5k; legal/insurance 3k; outreach 2k; contingency 2k.
- Up to 60,000 PLN after paid setup/deposit and positive first batch.
- Up to 100,000 PLN after three independent customers and one repeat revision.
- Family capital excluded until repeatable demand, contribution, and acquisition.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after five independent recurring customers, positive fully loaded contribution for six months, three paid revisions, one repeatable acquisition source, two trained assemblers independent of founder, recurring gross profit above 1.5× burn, and 12 months personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Tools/fixtures may be resold; reusable assets include work-instruction software, traceability methods, connector/test library, process data, training, and hardware-team distribution. Do not fall back to generic electrical contracting.

## Critical Evidence

Standards and many competitors establish a real manufacturing category and accessible training. They also show the rapid low-MOQ gap is already served. No evidence proves the narrower revision/data advantage or economics.

## Critical Hypotheses

One team pays in 180 days; 60 suitable recurring buyers exist; 20% operating margin survives; process telemetry creates measurable lift; customer contracts allow reusable generalised data.

## Kill Criteria

- No paid setup/deposit after 30 qualified offers.
- Existing suppliers match lead time/traceability at lower total cost.
- No paid revision among five customers.
- Gross margin below 35% or founder labour scales one-for-one.
- No measurable data-enabled improvement by 20 harness families.
- Conservative wealth falls below 5m.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

30,000 PLN pre-proof; 100,000 PLN year one. Founder software/sales; IPC-experienced contract assembler; quality adviser; part-time second assembler after repeat orders.
