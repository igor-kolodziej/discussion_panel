# Zero-to-One Candidate: CharacterContinuity Rail

**Canonical business specification: v1, frozen 2026-07-15 Europe/Warsaw for independent simulation.**

Wave-2 candidate generation only — not validated. All commercial numbers are hypotheses.

## One-Sentence Thesis

CharacterContinuity Rail signs narrow, consent-cleared synthetic-voice rights with CEE performers and gives update-heavy game studios one contract, model, direction memory, usage report, and royalty rail for every future line of an already cast character.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build speech-data pipelines, model evaluation, direction/pronunciation memory, consent logs, and studio-facing workflow. Existing synthesis vendors can be replaced; no foundational model needs to be built before revenue. Fixed-fee performer/IP counsel and freelance audio direction cover bounded gaps. Warsaw has a documented game-studio base and accessible performer supply.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The buyer is a 5–100-person live-service, episodic, mobile, simulation, or narrative game studio that expects repeated updates but cannot repeatedly book, direct, and contract actors in many languages. A character's voice continuity matters to players, while unlicensed AI use creates platform, performer, and reputation risk.

Current workarounds are ordinary recording sessions, generic voice libraries, direct voice-clone contracts, or silent/unvoiced updates. Generic libraries solve generation but not character-specific continuity, studio usage reports, actor consent scope, royalties, direction memory, or replacement of the underlying model vendor.

## Product Wedge

- Sign ten Polish/CEE performers to 24-month, narrowly defined synthetic-voice options covering specified game-character categories, languages, compensation, prohibited uses, security, renewal, and deletion/new-generation terms.
- Sell one studio a 15,000–25,000 PLN character setup and first-line pack.
- Studio casts a voice and signs a character-continuity licence: every synthetic line for that character during the term routes through CharacterContinuity.
- Store source recordings, model provenance, pronunciation/direction memory, line approvals, disclosure metadata, usage reports, and royalty calculations.
- Pay the performer a contracted revenue share or minimum plus usage compensation. Human performer approval is available for new emotional/semantic categories.
- Underlying TTS/model providers are processors/subcontractors and can be replaced without changing studio/performer contracts.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Voice marketplaces maximise interchangeable voice supply. Games create the opposite dynamic: once a voice becomes a named character, continuity and rights history make it less interchangeable. The control point is therefore created at casting—two-sided exclusive use/representation terms plus every later line and royalty—not by owning a generic clone.

## Underused Momentum

Poland has 824 game producers/publishers and strong localisation/asset-production capability; the EU has about 5,000 studios. Steam now requires AI-content disclosure, the EU AI Act adds synthetic-content transparency, and performer agreements increasingly define consent, usage reporting, and compensation. ElevenLabs proves both demand and formidable marketplace competition.

## Beachhead Market

Polish/CEE indie studios with an unreleased or newly launched update-heavy title, 5–100 staff, and one recurring NPC/narrator whose annual conventional recording coordination is costly relative to the game's budget. Start with Polish and English; exclude celebrity imitation, minors, political ads, adult content, and real-time unconstrained player speech.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Character setup and source session: 15,000–25,000 PLN.
- Annual continuity licence: 24,000–60,000 PLN per active character, including a line allowance, rights administration, model/vendor portability, direction memory, reports, and disclosure metadata.
- Overage or new emotional category: per-line/per-minute fee; performer receives 25%–40% of relevant revenue or negotiated minimum.
- Scaled gross-margin hypothesis: 65% after performer royalties, synthesis, audio QA, storage, and direction; operating margin 25%–32%.
- Studio value proof: lower time/cost to ship approved voiced updates with consistent character output and auditable rights versus repeated studio sessions.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

- Performer list: 50 non-celebrity Polish/CEE voice actors from public casting communities and agencies; sign no more than ten limited options before studio demand.
- Studio list: PARP catalogue, Steam upcoming/update-heavy titles, accelerator portfolios, and public studio directories; first outreach to 100 named producers/audio leads.
- Offer a genre-specific demo using company-owned sample text and a fixed 15,000 PLN paid character pilot; never clone a public actor/character without rights.
- Conversion event: paid invoice, performer option exercised, studio character-continuity licence, approved first line pack, and recurring-line/usage reporting route.
- First ten studios: founder-led Polish/CEE outbound and game-industry events using paid case studies, not status introductions.
- First 100: a searchable rights-cleared casting catalogue, engine/API plugins, and localisation partners paid referral fees without owning performer or studio contracts.

## Primary Control Point

**Proposed Tier 1 per cast character:** time/category/language-defined performer-model representation and sublicensing rights, studio character-continuity exclusivity, source/model provenance, and all future synthetic line/royalty flow during the term. Generic actor options alone are not scarce; the control becomes valuable only after a studio casts and integrates the character.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Needed resources are ten lawful performer options and one paid character contract.

1. Counsel drafts performer option, studio licence, prohibited-use schedule, compensation, security, and termination terms.
2. Sign ten 24-month options using revenue share and at most 1,000 PLN guarantee each; no claim of perpetual consent.
3. Record only two demo voices at professional quality before studio payment.
4. Sell 100 studios a paid character setup; exercise/record the selected performer only after payment.
5. Twelve-month proof: three independent paying studios, five active characters, at least two paid update cycles on the same character, performer payments, and one model-vendor portability test within 100,000 PLN.

An unknown founder can recruit working non-celebrity performers with transparent minimums and sell a bounded character pack to indie studios without union celebrity access or AAA procurement.

## Distribution Advantage

Each active character becomes recurring studio demand and a performer case study. A studio can add characters under one legal/technical workflow; performers bring legitimate samples and referrals. Engine/localisation integrations reduce friction while the direct rights and billing relationship remains with CharacterContinuity.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Dominate consent-cleared Polish/CEE update-heavy character voices, then add languages, localisation, and real-time constrained NPC dialogue only after guardrail proof. The company can later licence its consent/usage/royalty infrastructure to studios using their own actors. ElevenLabs or studios can sign actors directly, but cannot use a specifically contracted performer-character combination or recreate its direction/approval history during the term without renegotiation and switching.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland provides a large documented studio base, voice/localisation talent, and lower production cost. EU/Steam transparency and consent expectations make auditable rights useful. The defence is not regulation or Polish language alone; it is cast-character exclusivity, performer trust, and embedded update workflow.

## Compounding Advantage

Every approved line adds pronunciation, emotional direction, rejected alternatives, model/version performance, disclosure, usage, and royalty data tied to a commercial character. This improves QA, model routing, direction memory, and contract pricing. Ordinary delivery builds licensed speech/persona IP, advanced voice AI/ML, performer and game-studio distribution, human–AI direction interfaces, and skilled audio talent access.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative/expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash during employment and cap optional distributions at 5%; strong reinvests 100%. Reinvestment funds lawful performer options only against demand, secure voice storage, direction tooling, QA automation, engine integrations, and studio acquisition.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- Days 1–30: 20 performer and 20 studio interviews; counsel terms; two sample voices.
- Days 31–75: sign ten bounded performer options; 100-studio list and personalised outreach.
- Days 76–135: five studio evaluations and three fixed proposals.
- Days 136–180: one paid 15,000–25,000 PLN setup, exercised performer right, approved line pack, and signed recurring continuity/royalty route.

Founder stays employed/five hours daily. Paid proof is a studio invoice, not actor sign-ups.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Pre-proof maximum 32,000 PLN: legal/IP/privacy 8k; two demos 5k; option guarantees up to 10k; sales/events 4k; tools/security/contingency 5k.
- Up to 65,000 PLN after paid setup and performer payment with positive contribution.
- Up to 100,000 PLN after three independent studios or two paid update cycles.
- Family capital excluded until repeat demand and clear margin.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after five independent paying studios or ten contracted active characters, two repeat update orders, positive contribution after royalties/QA, repeatable studio acquisition, contracted recurring revenue, and 12 months personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Unused options expire; performers retain promised payments. Reusable assets are consent/usage/royalty software, lawful company-owned demos, model-evaluation tools, and buyer research. No performer model is repurposed outside scope. A generic voice marketplace is not the fallback.

## Critical Evidence

Game/studio denominators are documented; Steam/AI Act/performer agreements make disclosure and consent workflows salient; ElevenLabs proves the generic marketplace is crowded.

## Critical Hypotheses

Non-celebrity performers accept narrow options; indie studios pay 15,000–25,000 PLN; continuing characters generate 45,000 PLN annual revenue; contracts create sufficient continuity; model vendor portability works; 200 active characters are reachable.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than ten acceptable performer options from 50 offers.
- No paid studio after 100 qualified outreaches within 180 days.
- Studios demand perpetual rights or direct model ownership incompatible with recurring routing.
- No paid second update for any of the first three characters within 12 months.
- Gross margin below 50% after performer, synthesis, QA, sales, and legal cost.
- A studio can switch to a generic provider without recasting/renegotiation or losing useful direction history.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

32,000 PLN pre-proof; 100,000 PLN first year. Founder product/ML/sales; fixed-fee IP/privacy lawyer; freelance audio director/engineer per paid project; no full-time hire before repeat orders.
