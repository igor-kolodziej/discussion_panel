# Pivot Round 32: Assigned Late-Payment Ancillary Claims

## Why Pivot Here

CollectorVAT failed fresh validation at `77/100` because it had too many veto points: collector willingness, accountant acceptance, regulated tax-advice boundaries, and copyability by collectors/accountants.

The next shape preserves the strongest lesson: routed access to already-identified money rights can be valuable. It changes the weak part by targeting rights the startup can actually own or exclusively control through assignment/mandate.

## Candidate

**LatePayClaim Pool.** A claim-acquisition and recovery desk that buys or takes exclusive mandates over statutory late-payment interest and fixed recovery-cost compensation claims from Polish SMEs after the principal invoice has already been paid late, starting with completed public-sector or large-buyer B2B supply contracts where the seller has low relationship risk.

## Exact Candidate Wording

LatePayClaim Pool. A claim-acquisition and recovery desk for Polish SMEs that have already been paid late by public-sector buyers, hospitals, universities, utilities, municipalities, construction general contractors, or large corporate buyers, but never claimed the residual statutory late-payment interest and fixed recovery-cost compensation. It does not chase unpaid principal. It targets suppliers after principal has been paid and the commercial relationship is ended, low-dependence, or public-procurement-based. The seller uploads invoice list, due dates, payment dates, contract/payment terms, buyer identity, prior demands, and proof of performance/payment. The system computes invoice-level statutory interest and 40/70/100 EUR compensation under Polish commercial-transaction late-payment rules, legal reviewer filters prohibited assignments, disputed contracts, relationship-sensitive accounts, and weak evidence, then the seller signs either a cession/assignment of specific ancillary claims or an exclusive collection mandate. The desk sends batch demand letters and routes EPU/lawyer escalation only for clean claims. Control point: owned or exclusively mandated ancillary receivable rights over specific already-paid-late invoices, plus evidence folders and debtor response data. Two-month proof: sign 5 sellers, review 1,000 invoices or 2M+ PLN paid-late principal, acquire or mandate 100k PLN face value of ancillary claims, send 50 demand packets, get 20k PLN collected, settled, acknowledged, or court-filed, with legal-opinion approval of the assignment/mandate template. Pricing: claim purchase at 15-30% of verified face value, or no-upfront mandate with 30-45% recovery fee. Hidden insight: SMEs often will not confront buyers over post-payment late-interest rights because the main invoice is already paid and the residual claim feels socially awkward; an assignee can turn this ignored right into cash and build a repeatable debtor-response dataset.

## Simulated Zero To One Score

- Score: `84/100`

## Strongest Objections

- Claim values may be too small per seller unless invoice volume and lateness are high.
- Assignment can be contractually restricted or relationship-sensitive; public-sector and completed-contract focus reduces but does not eliminate this.
- Courts may scrutinize mass recovery-cost-compensation claims, especially if they look abusive or disconnected from real recovery costs.
- Debt collectors and law firms can copy the legal workflow if demand is obvious.
- Recovery timing may exceed 2 months unless early proof accepts signed assignments, demand packets, acknowledgments, and first settlements rather than full collection.

## Control-Point Tier

Tier 2.5, potentially Tier 1 for assigned claims. The strongest control point is not a dashboard, report, or channel relationship; it is ownership or exclusive control over specific ancillary receivable rights.

## Copy Risk

Debt collectors, commercial lawyers, online payment-order platforms, and accounting software vendors can copy parts of the workflow. Defensibility must come from fast acquisition of assigned claim inventory, debtor-response data, evidence templates, and narrow sector focus.

## Fatal Objection

No immediate fatal objection. Fatal risk appears if the first 5 seller audits show little eligible face value, broad assignment prohibitions, or sellers refusing to assign because relationship risk remains too high.

## Market Context Checked

- UOKiK notes that late commercial-payment consequences include statutory interest and fixed compensation from 40 to 100 EUR depending on the monetary benefit value.
- Gov.pl/MRIT materials describe anti-payment-gridlock measures, including shortened public-sector payment terms and differentiated 40/70/100 EUR compensation.
- ISAP statutory text confirms the 40/70/100 EUR compensation tiers in the Act on counteracting excessive delays in commercial transactions.
