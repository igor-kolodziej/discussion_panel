# Pivot Round 16 - OrphanPump Service-Book Rescue

## Why This Pivot

HeatPump Orphan Parts Bank scored highest in the last batch (`76/100`) because the pain is real and the founder has local/family-domain access. It failed because random orphan parts create SKU dispersion, inventory risk, liability, and weak control.

This pivot changes the control point from parts inventory to a signed installer service-book mandate: customer contacts, installation records, model data, photos, settings, warranty status, and routed post-install service demand from one overloaded/exiting installer.

## Candidate

**OrphanPump Service-Book Rescue** is a B2B2C after-sales continuity rail for small Polish heat-pump/PV installers, importers, and subsidy-era operators that have 50-400 installed heat-pump customers but are exiting heat pumps, overloaded with callbacks, losing manufacturer support, or unable to keep servicing private-label/importer brands.

The startup does not cold-sell homeowners and does not start as a broad repair marketplace. It signs a narrow service-book transfer/mandate with one installer/importer/service company at a time. The installer gets a fixed payout or revenue share. The founder receives access, with proper consent/process review, to:

- customer contact list;
- installed model families;
- installation dates, photos, settings, invoices, warranty status, and service history where available;
- known recurring faults and supplier/parts notes;
- right to send a co-branded "service continuity transfer" notice.

The customer offer is a paid continuity product:

- annual service/triage plan: 399-899 PLN;
- paid emergency remote diagnosis: 199-399 PLN;
- paid maintenance or repair routing through certified local installers;
- parts sourcing only after a paid case or deposit, with inventory exposure capped.

Field work stays with certified HVAC/F-gas installers. The founder controls the service book, intake, diagnosis packet, model/error corpus, customer continuity archive, and paid plan/diagnostic flow.

## Primary Control Point

- Signed mandate or acquisition/right-of-first-refusal over one installer's installed-base service book.
- Co-branded customer transfer notice instead of cold homeowner outreach.
- Installation documentation and model/error history that outside repairers do not have.
- Paid annual plans and diagnostics that route demand before emergency failures.
- Private model/error/part/service-outcome corpus for repeat model families.

## Two-Month Proof

- Sign one service-book mandate covering at least 80 installed units.
- Secure legal/GDPR/process review for customer communication and liability boundaries.
- Contact the first 80 customers with a co-branded service-continuity notice.
- Convert at least 20 customers to paid plans or diagnostics.
- Complete 10 paid remote/field cases through certified installers.
- Generate at least 20,000 PLN gross revenue and 8,000 PLN gross margin after contractor costs.
- Build an 80-unit model/error/parts map.
- Keep unrecovered parts/inventory exposure below 15,000 PLN.

## Why It Is Not Existing Heat-Pump Ideas

- Not BatteryFit: no PV battery/EMS retrofit qualification.
- Not the heat-pump commissioning passport: no distributor/warranty gate for new installs.
- Not HeatQuiet: no acoustic/noise-dispute siting packet.
- Not HeatPump Orphan Parts Bank: this does not start from speculative parts; it starts from controlled installed-base records and routed customer demand.

## Kill Criteria

- No overloaded/exiting installer will sign a service-book mandate with communication rights.
- Legal/GDPR review makes co-branded customer transfer notices impractical.
- Customers treat the transfer as spam or distrust it; fewer than 15% convert to any paid plan/diagnostic.
- Service cases do not cluster around repeat model families.
- Certified installers refuse the routed work or demand most of the margin.
- Liability cannot be cleanly separated from the original installer's warranty obligations.
