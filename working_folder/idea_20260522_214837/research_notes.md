# Pet-Safety Research Notes

## Market/Competitor Context

- Tractive already offers virtual fences for dogs/cats with app alerts when a pet enters or leaves a safe/no-go zone. Default tracking checks about every two minutes; LIVE tracking updates every two or three seconds. Source: https://help.tractive.com/hc/en-us/articles/205664361-How-Virtual-Fences-help-you-keep-your-pet-safe-with-Tractive
- Halo Collar is a full GPS fence/containment product with dual-frequency GPS, on-collar fence storage, patented direction-aware feedback, optional static correction, and subscription from $9.99/month. It claims 500,000+ dog parents and supports wireless fences at home/travel/rural properties. Source: https://www.halocollar.com/dog-fence-features/virtual-gps/
- SpotOn sells GPS dog fence collars. Its tracking plan is optional; fences can work without a cellular subscription, while tracking/alerts require paid plans from about $7.49-$9.95/month. Source: https://spotonfence.com/pages/subscription
- Invoxia Biotracker 2026 adds real-time second-by-second dog GPS plus heart/respiratory/sleep/health monitoring, with free Basic and paid Plus/Max GPS tiers. Source: https://www.invoxia.com/blog/petcare/new-biotracker-2026-edition/
- FEDIAF 2025 statistics page says Europe has 299 million pets and 139 million pet-owning households, with 2023 data published in June 2025. Source: https://europeanpetfood.org/about/statistics/

## Regulatory/Timing Context

- Poland's KROPiK law: Sejm adopted mandatory identification and registration for all dogs and owned/shelter/foster cats; target registry includes about 8 million dogs and 6 million cats. Vets mark/register animals; owners can access data through mObywatel and get rabies reminders. Source: https://www.gov.pl/web/rolnictwo/czipowanie-psow-obowiazkowe-w-calej-polsce-sejm-przyjal-ustawe-o-kropik
- European Parliament approved first EU standards for cats/dogs on 28 April 2026. It includes mandatory microchipping/registration in interoperable national databases, with four-year preparation for sellers/breeders/shelters and longer phase-ins for private owners. It also bans or restricts several abusive practices. Source: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20260423IPR41833/first-eu-rules-to-protect-cats-and-dogs-from-abuses

## Strategic Implications

- A plain GPS collar subscription is likely a weak idea: active incumbents already own hardware, app, subscriptions, accuracy claims, and consumer brand.
- Static/electric containment should not be the wedge in Poland/EU without a much deeper legal/welfare review. It creates avoidable trust and animal-welfare risk.
- Stronger pet-safety angles should use existing trackers at first and control a workflow where someone has temporary duty of care: adoption handoff, foster transition, boarding, walkers, pet transport, shelter/foundation compliance, or high-risk travel.
- Stronger control points are not "we sell collars"; they are routed handoff rights, signed SOPs, owned tracker fleets, deposits, adoption/boarding contract addenda, incident-response records, and repeated safety data for a narrow channel.
