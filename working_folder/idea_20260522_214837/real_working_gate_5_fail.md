# Real Working-Chat Gate 5 Fail

Candidate: `OZE SiteGuard Jobsite Custody Kit`

Adjusted final threshold: `>=82`

Real working-chat score: `78/100`

## Exact Scored Wording

OZE SiteGuard Jobsite Custody Kit is a paid, concierge GPS/geofence and custody-evidence workflow for small Polish PV, heat-pump, HVAC, and electrical installation companies that leave expensive tools, heat-pump units, inverters, batteries, copper, or PV materials on customer sites, vans, or temporary storage. It does not sell generic fleet tracking or promise 24/7 security response. It sells a 499 PLN protected job-week or 999-1,499 PLN/month per crew package with refundable tracker deposit. Each package uses off-the-shelf rugged GPS/LTE trackers and Bluetooth tags hidden in high-value pallets, toolboxes, and materials; geofence/tamper alerts; QR/photo handoff checklists; overnight custody logs; supplier/customer delivery evidence; and a police/insurer-ready incident packet if loss occurs. The first pilot and reference case is the founder's family renewable-energy installation company near Ostrow Wielkopolski. Hardware is bought only after paid preorders or deposits. The primary control point is installer-specific custody workflow, family-company reference access, crew onboarding, paid tracker deposits, per-job evidence logs, and potential wholesaler/insurance-broker referral routes. Two-month proof: one family-company pilot, five non-family installers paying upfront for at least 20 protected job-weeks, 10 tracker kits deployed, two wholesaler/supplier or insurance-broker referral letters, one incident/near-miss evidence packet if it occurs, and positive contribution margin after hardware depreciation, SIM fees, delivery, support, and deposits.

## Gate Feedback

- Strong pain and good niche specificity.
- Family installer access is meaningful for a pilot and credibility.
- Positive contribution margin is plausible in 2 months if hardware is prepaid/deposit-backed and the service is narrow.
- The score was capped because the control point is not yet proven, the moat is operational rather than hard, and the proof depends heavily on warm-network conversion.
- Main objection: customers may see it as commodity GPS tracking plus extra admin.
- Main copy risk: GPS/fleet/security vendors can copy geofences, QR handoffs, and photo logs.
- Retention risk: theft frequency may be episodic unless the custody workflow becomes habitual.
- Operational risk: incident support could break the founder's 5h/day constraint.

## Pivot Lesson

The next candidate needs a harder reserved/routed/prepaid control point than "installer workflow around commodity GPS." Prefer a business where the first wedge is tied to money already stuck, a deadline, a formal gate, or a buyer/channel that can route demand before the founder builds inventory or handles ongoing support.
