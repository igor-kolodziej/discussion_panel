# Simulated Gate: KSC/NIS2 Supplier-Risk Renewal Evidence Pack

## Exact Candidate Wording
KSC/NIS2 Supplier-Risk Renewal Evidence Pack. A Polish/CEE productized evidence-response service for 15-150 person ICT, OT, SaaS, managed-service, building-automation, medical-software, water/utility, logistics, and industrial-maintenance vendors whose non-financial customers are now asking KSC/NIS2 supply-chain cybersecurity questions during a live procurement, tender, renewal, framework agreement, or vendor re-approval. It does not sell broad NIS2 compliance, certification, penetration tests, or legal advice. It activates only when a hospital, municipality, water/sewage utility, energy/heat operator, transport/logistics operator, public entity, food/chemical/manufacturing important entity, or their integrator asks for supplier-security evidence under the amended Polish KSC/NIS2 regime: supply-chain risk questionnaire, high-risk supplier declaration, MFA/remote-admin evidence, incident-reporting support, backup/BCP/DR evidence, access-control screenshots, subcontractor/hosting-country list, vulnerability/patching evidence, OT remote-access controls, or contract-clause gaps. The vendor submits the live buyer request plus existing policies, contracts, architecture notes, screenshots, asset/service descriptions, cloud/subprocessor data, prior security answers, and customer-specific service scope. The desk returns a buyer-ready Polish evidence pack: answer matrix, evidence index, supplier-risk declaration, service-scope statement, remote-access/MFA/backup/incident summary, subcontractor and hosting-country table, high-risk supplier exposure note, "answerable now vs remediate vs legal/cyber review" split, and executive memo for the buyer's procurement/security owner. Review is by a KSC/NIS2 cyber practitioner; legal review is optional for contract clauses. Control point: live revenue-blocking buyer evidence requests routed through the vendor, reusable KSC/NIS2 supplier-question corpus outside regulated finance, accepted/rejected answer memory by buyer sector, and partner overflow from cyber/legal firms that want remediation work but not urgent evidence assembly. Two-month proof: 3 paid urgent packs from live non-finance KSC/NIS2 buyer requests; at least one buyer accepts/forwards the pack enough to progress a procurement or renewal; one cyber/KSC reviewer on paid terms; 30 reusable question/evidence mappings; 30k PLN collected; at least one referral from a cyber/legal/ISO partner. Pricing: 10k-20k PLN per urgent pack or 5k-8k PLN renewal refresh. Hidden insight: Poland's amended KSC/NIS2 regime creates a wave of supplier-risk questions outside finance, but small vendors experience it as a blocked renewal or tender answer, not as a desire to buy a generic compliance project; the fastest wedge is buyer-ready evidence from their existing artifacts.

## Simulated Score
86/100.

## Why It Clears 82
This borrows the strongest pattern from prior confirmed evidence-pack ideas but changes the control surface: the target is non-financial KSC/NIS2 supply-chain evidence for vendors to hospitals, municipalities, water utilities, energy/heat, logistics, and industrial operators. The control point is a live buyer request tied to procurement/renewal progress, not a speculative compliance audit. The Polish KSC amendment is current, broad, and locally specific.

## Strongest Caveats
- It risks being seen as a near-duplicate of DORA evidence packs unless kept strictly outside regulated finance and tied to Polish KSC/NIS2 sectors.
- Cyber consultancies, ISO firms, lawyers, and fractional CISOs can copy the service if they focus on urgent evidence assembly.
- The buyer-side questionnaire wave may still be uneven in the first two months.
- Legal/cyber boundaries must be explicit; the desk organizes evidence and flags gaps, not certifies compliance.
- Public-sector procurement cycles can be slow, so the POC must require live blockers and collected cash.

## Decision
Pass simulated gate. Submit to real working-chat gate.
