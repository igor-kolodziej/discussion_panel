Founder context:
Unknown Warsaw-based solo founder, contractors allowed, part-time execution, maximum starting capital about 100,000 PLN, no celebrity/influencer distribution, no privileged industrial introductions, no ability to brute-force trust with status. The founder is analytical and can build data/workflow systems, but must prove the business through concrete resource control and paid demand before serious capital is spent.

Idea name:
Factory Control-Spares Donor Bank

One-sentence thesis:
A Polish/CEE emergency inventory business that buys or controls titled, tested lots of obsolete factory automation control parts, then sells or rents exact serial-verified replacements during line-down events where a retrofit would take weeks and a compatible module can restart production.

Exact buyer:
Maintenance managers, plant managers, automation integrators, machine rebuilders, and industrial service technicians serving Polish and CEE factories that run legacy Siemens, Omron, Lenze, ABB, Schneider, Mitsubishi, B&R, Festo, Beckhoff, Danfoss, SEW, or similar control systems. The first buyer has an active installed base with discontinued or long-lead PLC CPUs, I/O modules, HMI panels, drives, encoder cards, communication cards, power supplies, operator panels, or safety-adjacent control boards.

Acute trigger:
A production line or machine is down because an old control module failed. The OEM part is discontinued, official lead time is too slow, an integrator retrofit needs weeks, and the factory is losing production every shift. The buyer does not want advice or a marketplace search; they want a known-good exact module, fast courier, test evidence, clear return terms, and a realistic path to restart production.

Product wedge:
The startup starts with a narrow emergency desk for exact-SKU control spares. It buys only after combining supply control with buyer evidence:

1. first-look, option, or consignment rights from retiring automation technicians, plant decommissioners, bankrupt machine shops, machine rebuilders, industrial electricians, and integrators with old stock;
2. serial-level photos, firmware/revision notes, bench-test records, origin/title paperwork, and limited warranty/return terms;
3. paid reservations, emergency stock agreements, rentals, or preorders from factories and integrators for named SKUs or named installed systems.

Primary control point:
Specific titled or optioned physical inventory plus test records, serial/revision metadata, seller-origin history, first-look supply rights, compatibility notes, and paid emergency buyer reservations. The control point is not a listing site. It is a small, verified inventory bank of exact parts that a factory can use now.

Initial acquisition mechanism:
Start from the supply side and integrator side, not broad marketing. Build a Polish "line-down old controls" call list by scraping and manually checking public companies and technician signals: plant liquidation notices, industrial auction lots, automation electricians, machine rebuilders, control-panel shops, used machinery dealers, Allegro/OLX industrial sellers, LinkedIn posts from retiring technicians, and integrators advertising PLC retrofits. Offer decommissioners and technicians either immediate cash for titled lots or a consignment/first-look agreement. In parallel, call 40-60 automation integrators and maintenance managers with a concrete ask: name the exact discontinued SKUs that stop your customers' machines, and reserve or prepay for top-priority emergency availability only if the lot matches those SKUs.

60-day proof:

- Sign two first-look, option, or consignment supply agreements with clear ownership/origin terms.
- Secure one inspected lot under 65,000 PLN only after buyer interviews identify demand for the included families/SKUs.
- Create serial-photo/test/revision logs for at least 80% of the lot using a qualified automation technician contractor.
- Complete at least 20 buyer interviews naming exact SKUs, machine families, or failure-prone installed systems.
- Obtain five paid reservations, deposits, emergency stock agreements, rentals, or sales from factories/integrators.
- Collect at least 30,000 PLN in paid deposits, preorders, rentals, or sales.
- Prove gross margin above 45% after acquisition cost allocation, testing, dead-stock reserve, warranty/returns, courier, and seller earnout.

12-month POC:
Control 3-6 supply relationships, buy or consign 250-600 verified parts across a small number of high-demand families, build a private compatibility/demand map from 100+ buyer calls and real transactions, complete 30-80 paid sales/rentals/reservations, and show that the top 20% of SKUs turn quickly enough to fund dead stock. Continue only if the business produces at least 250,000 PLN revenue, 45%+ gross margin after realistic returns/dead-stock reserve, and repeat purchase/reservation behavior from integrators or factories.

Economics:
Revenue comes from emergency sale margin, short-term rental/loaner fees with deposits, reserved-stock retainers for integrators/factories, consignment margins, and paid sourcing fees tied to a specific buyer request. A factory facing line downtime can rationally pay a premium for a tested 2,000-20,000 PLN module if the alternative is days of lost production or a weeks-long retrofit. The founder avoids random inventory by buying only after a supply right plus buyer demand evidence exists.

Why incumbents may miss the wedge:
Large used-automation dealers exist, but many factories and local integrators still resolve line-down problems through informal technician networks, panicked calls, eBay/Radwell/PLC Center searches, and uncertain used listings. A local bank with verified serials, Polish/CEE pickup/courier speed, origin records, and exact installed-base notes can win urgent cases before global catalogs or generic marketplaces can respond.

Domination path:
Start with a small number of old but common control families in Poland and CEE. Become the trusted emergency source for those families by accumulating serial-level demand data, failure notes, revision compatibility, tested inventory, supply-source relationships, and integrator referrals. Expand from emergency sales into reserved-stock retainers, buyback routes for decommissioned lines, and exact-family inventory plans for factories that cannot yet retrofit.

Local adaptation defense:
Local title diligence, Polish-language technician relationships, fast domestic/CEE logistics, decommissioning/source relationships, and knowledge of the actual installed base create a local execution barrier. A global dealer can sell parts, but it cannot instantly control local decommissioning flow, local first-call urgency, local trust with integrators, and the private map of which Polish plants still run which control families.

Why this is not merely a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The company owns, consigns, or options specific physical parts, tests them, serializes them, and sells or rents them into urgent downtime. It does not sell advice, a dashboard, lead generation, generic brokerage, or a database. The proof is paid deposits, stocked tested modules, and emergency transactions.

Key risks:
Wrong inventory can strand capital. Firmware, revision, licensing, safety configuration, and hidden damage can make a part unusable. Used-part incumbents and integrators already exist. Some parts may be stolen or have unclear title, requiring strict diligence. Testing quality and warranty/return policy are crucial. Line-down demand is lumpy. The founder needs qualified automation-technician contractors and must avoid making safety-engineering claims; qualified factory staff or integrators handle installation, machine validation, and responsibility for production restart.
