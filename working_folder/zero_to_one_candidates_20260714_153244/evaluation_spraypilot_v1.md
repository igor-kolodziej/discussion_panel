# Zero-to-One Evaluation: SprayPilot

## Diagnosis

SprayPilot is a genuine human-augmentation product with an acute shop-floor loss, cheap shadow-mode proof, and an outcome-labeled motor-skill asset that generic training simulators do not own. The key uncertainty is technical: gun motion, setup, coating, booth, and part geometry may be too confounded for a reusable model, and hazardous-area hardware can destroy reachability. If shadow instrumentation is admissible and the first paid dataset predicts a measurable outcome, the model can become a recurring booth product rather than consulting. The expected and conservative wealth cases pass, although both depend on deployment becoming template-driven.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: multiple commercial VR spray trainers show training spend exists; affordable inertial/haptic hardware exists; workplace-wearable guidance supports transparent, privacy-minimized deployment. Hypotheses: shops incur sufficient measurable rework/material loss; they pay 10,000–18,000 PLN for shadow proof and 4,500–7,000 PLN/month; a sealed sensor is admissible; motion features predict defects; derivative features are reusable across shops. No reviewer verdict exists. Missing evidence: hazardous-zone design cost, shop count by manual process, loss size, and actual predictive performance.

## Overall Score

77/100. Weighted total 154 across total weight 20: `10 × (154 / 20) = 77`.

## Control Point

Tier 2, with a path toward Tier 1 data. The firm directly owns software/calibration and can contract rights to non-identifying technique–outcome derivatives; installed recipes/history create switching cost. Strongest copy risk: a coating-equipment or VR-training vendor adds an IMU and outcome loop using its existing distribution. The idea remains below 80 because the hardware is commodity and data access begins non-exclusive.

## Founder Wealth Gate

Pass. The conservative year-8 case reaches about 10.3m PLN founder net worth from an 85% stake in debt-adjusted equity plus actual post-transition distributions; the expected year-7 case reaches about 37.1m at 75% ownership. Both respect 95% reinvestment and at most 5% optional distribution while employed; retained earnings are not separately added. The decisive risk is reaching 120–300 recurring booths with template-driven deployment rather than founder-heavy customization.

## Reachability Gate

Pass conditionally on a low-cost hazardous-area/shadow method. The 10,000–15,000 PLN paid pilot uses 20–30 normal pieces and existing quality labels; pre-proof risk is capped at 15,000 PLN and fits employment with two fixed-scope specialists. No equipment partner, clinic, participant cohort, or complete coaching product is needed. If safety admission costs above 40,000 PLN before a deposit, the gate fails and the candidate must be killed.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is repeated manual coating in small high-mix shops. Part/coating recipes, onboarding, and adjacent skilled tool paths are logical. VR trainers and equipment makers can enter; cross-shop outcome data and faster calibration must make catch-up hard. The Polish wedge is access/cost, not durable exclusion.

## Fatal Objections

1. Motion may not explain defects well enough after booth, coating, gun, and surface confounders.
2. Hazardous-area requirements may make even shadow hardware too expensive or slow.
3. Small shops may resist instrumentation or lack reliable outcome labels/budget.
4. Deployment may remain bespoke, blocking the margin and wealth model.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Skilled-worker augmentation becomes defensible when real production motion is joined to objective outcome data and used in a closed feedback loop.
