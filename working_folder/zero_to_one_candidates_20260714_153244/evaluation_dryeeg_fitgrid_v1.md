# Zero-to-One Evaluation: DryEEG FitGrid

## Diagnosis

DryEEG FitGrid has a crisp engineering job and directly buildable neurotechnology asset, but its stand-alone dry-EEG customer universe may be too small for the modeled kit volumes. Fraunhofer and academic phantom work validate the problem while raising FTO, credibility, and copy concerns; a competent team can also build a one-off phantom cheaply. The paid comparison is reachable, but the wealth path depends on high subscription attachment and expansion before the beachhead is proven. The candidate should not enter a panel in this form.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: peer-reviewed low-cost conductive phantoms; dry-electrode literature connecting hair/pressure/geometry/motion to signal quality; Fraunhofer PhantomHead; Brain Products LabSim. Hypotheses: product teams pay 15,000–28,000 PLN before human studies; 180 kits and 220 subscriptions are achievable by year 7; three headforms predict decisions; FTO permits commercialization; annual scripts/calibration retain customers. No external reviewer verdict exists. Critical missing facts are the count of funded product teams, their internal phantom capability, and patent/FTO boundaries.

## Overall Score

73/100. Weighted total 145 across total weight 20: `10 × (145 / 20) = 72.5`, rounded to 73.

## Control Point

Tier 2. Calibrated designs, recipes, scripts, and comparative data are directly owned and repeatably accessible. Strongest copy risk: a product team or test-equipment vendor reproduces a headform with published methods and commodity printing/load/impedance equipment. FitGrid remains below 80 because neither calibration nor data is exclusive and Fraunhofer already occupies adjacent ground.

## Founder Wealth Gate

Pass on the expected case, but with weak confidence. Expected year-7 founder net worth is modeled around 22.4m PLN from 80% of equity value plus actual post-transition distributions, with 95% employment-period reinvestment and no retained-cash double count. The conservative case reaches only about 9.2m. The decisive weakness is that 180 kits and 220 subscriptions may be a heroic share of a poorly sized niche; strong success cannot cure that uncertainty.

## Reachability Gate

Pass. A three-headform prototype and repeatability benchmark cost up to 30,000 PLN; a 15,000–22,000 PLN paid comparison with customer-shipped parts can be sold within 180 days while employed. No clinic, participants, partnership, accreditation, or finished product is required. IP/FTO review is a controllable early dependency.

## Expansion Check

Dry-EEG is a coherent beachhead; ear-EEG and other head-worn interfaces may expand it. Fraunhofer, academic labs, and existing simulator vendors can move inward. Poland/EU cost and proximity are useful but not durable. Expansion must be proven before the wealth model is trusted.

## Fatal Objections

1. The dry-EEG buyer pool and recurring test frequency may be too small for 10m PLN founder wealth.
2. Published low-cost phantom methods and Fraunhofer activity make DIY/copy risk high.
3. FTO and research credibility may require status, validation, or collaboration beyond the founder's reach.
4. One-off design comparisons may not attach a recurring subscription.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Head-worn biosensor teams need repeatable contact-mechanics ground truth before noisy, costly human iteration.

## Recommendation

kill
