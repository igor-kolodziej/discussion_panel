# Zero-to-One Candidate: DryEEG FitGrid

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build the calibrated mechanical-electrical headform system that lets dry-EEG and ear-EEG teams compare electrode and mount revisions against hair, pressure, fit, motion, and repeated-donning failures before spending human-study budget.

## Founder Fit

This is a small, research-use hardware/benchmark business that can begin with 3D printing, impedance measurement, OpenBCI-class electronics, and contracted mechanical/electronics talent. It builds neurotechnology hardware expertise, objective test IP, benchmark data, research/company relationships, and signal-processing capability without a clinic or patient data.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is a dry-EEG/ear-EEG hardware lead about to freeze an electrode, spring, mount, or industrial-design revision. Hair, anthropometry, pressure, curvature, movement, and repeated donning create unstable contact and comfort failures. Teams currently iterate on employees or small convenience samples, use a simple conductive phantom, or discover fit problems late in a human study. Human results mix mechanical failure with physiological variability and lack a known ground truth.

## Product Wedge

Three modular headforms with different geometry and hair analogues, instrumented with load/pressure and impedance measurement, execute repeatable head motion and donning cycles. A customer ships two electrode/mount variants; FitGrid produces a comparative mechanical-electrical failure map and a replayable test script. V1 is research-use/pre-compliance engineering and makes no comfort, clinical, or certification claim.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

EEG signal simulators test electronics and academic phantoms test isolated questions, but dry-contact product teams make mechanical decisions across head geometry, hair, pressure, movement, and repeated fit. The commercially valuable gap is a reproducible product-design bench between CAD/bench electronics and expensive, noisy human tests.

## Underused Momentum

Mobile neurotechnology, dry electrodes, BCI procurement, and wearables push EEG outside controlled labs. Primary research and Fraunhofer's PhantomHead explicitly identify missing ground truth and motion/muscle artifacts. Low-cost conductive 3D printing makes a credible private POC possible now. This is technical momentum, not proof of budget.

## Beachhead Market

European dry-EEG headband, cap, earable, BCI, and neurofeedback hardware teams with a physical design revision scheduled within six months. The initial niche excludes wet-electrode clinical systems, pure software firms, and buyers requiring accredited biocompatibility or clinical validation.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Hypothesized two-variant comparison: 15,000–28,000 PLN; direct labor/material/shipping 5,000–9,000 PLN; 60–72% contribution margin.
- Calibrated three-headform kit after proof: 38,000–58,000 PLN; build cost 16,000–25,000 PLN; 45–60% hardware gross margin.
- Annual scripts/calibration/benchmark access: 15,000–28,000 PLN per site; target 75–85% gross margin.
- Later custom anthropometry/hair module: 8,000–18,000 PLN. Do not build before a deposit.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10: list dry-electrode/ear-EEG teams from CORDIS, EUDAMED where applicable, recent publications, European BCI programs, and public product pages. Publish one comparison of two mounts on three headforms. Offer a fixed two-variant sprint with 40% deposit and customer-shipped parts. Target product teams with a named redesign event, not general labs.

First 100: sell standardized calibrated kits and subscriptions globally through published benchmarks, distributor-light direct sales, and later licensed calibration centers. Use customer-funded modules to expand anthropometry/hair/electrode coverage. Academic labs are secondary demonstration customers; product companies remain the economic core.

## Primary Control Point

Directly owned modular headform designs, conductive/hair recipes, mechanical calibration, pressure/contact maps, motion and donning scripts, electrode geometry database, and longitudinal variant outcomes. Twelve-month proof is two calibrated headform generations, repeatability data, three paid comparisons, and contracts preserving ownership of generic fixtures and anonymized design/failure features. Conduct a patent/FTO search before locking geometry.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Resource needed: a repeatable mechanical-electrical phantom family. Secure it directly with 3D printing, conductive materials, load cells, impedance measurement, OpenBCI, and a simple motion fixture for 18,000–30,000 PLN. Benchmark a legitimately purchased cap/electrode combination. Customer-specific interfaces are built only after a deposit and receipt of the customer's parts. Proof before serious capital: repeated-donning measurements have low enough variance to distinguish two known mechanical variants.

## Distribution Advantage

The addressable teams are technically visible through papers, grants, EUDAMED, product launches, and job postings, and a design-revision event can be inferred from announcements or qualified in one technical call. A useful open benchmark gives the unknown founder credibility. Each supported electrode/mount reduces onboarding time for adjacent teams.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with dry scalp EEG fit mechanics, then expand to ear-EEG, neurostimulation contact, AR/VR head-worn biosensors, and other skin-contact neural wearables. Brain Products LabSim owns signal-chain simulation; Fraunhofer validates but may also occupy parts of the phantom space. FitGrid must win on productized repeated-donning mechanics, modular hair/geometry, fast commercial delivery, and a growing cross-device benchmark. Later recurring calibration/scripts make it more than a one-time fixture.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland offers cost-efficient printing, mechatronics, and neuroengineering talent while European product teams are geographically accessible. Research-use/pre-compliance positioning avoids claiming a medical purpose. There is no regulatory moat; the defense is faster iteration, specialized EU support, accumulated calibration/data, and installed scripts.

## Compounding Advantage

Every customer-funded interface adds geometry, contact, and failure cases. Calibrated installed kits create recurring script/calibration revenue and a de-identified normative benchmark. The data improves design recommendations and future real-time neural-wearable research. Manufacturing jigs and QA reduce cost with volume.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of distributable after-tax cash while the founder remains employed and cap optional distributions at 5%. Strong success reinvests 100%. Reinvestment funds calibration equipment, manufacturing QA, inventory only against orders, and sales engineering. Retained cash is not counted twice; post-transition distributions are separately stated.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–45: build three headforms and establish repeated-donning variance with one purchased EEG system. Days 46–75: publish mechanical-electrical comparison and complete FTO/regulatory-positioning review. Days 76–135: event-driven technical outbound to 60–90 named product teams; offer a 15,000–22,000 PLN two-variant comparison with 40% deposit. Days 136–180: deliver the comparison using customer-shipped parts and seek a second paid module or kit preorder. Pre-proof founder risk is capped at 30,000 PLN. No clinic, participants, medical claims, or research partnership is required.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

1. 0–8,000 PLN: material/electrical feasibility and FTO scan.
2. Up to 30,000 PLN total: three-headform demo and repeatability benchmark; hard pre-proof cap.
3. Up to 65,000 PLN only after a deposit, successful delivery, and positive contribution margin; fund customer-specific tooling.
4. Up to 100,000 PLN only after three independent payers and a kit preorder or repeat comparison.
5. No family capital before repeatable demand and inventory economics.

## Critical Dependency Map

- Technical repeatability: the phantom must distinguish known variants; directly testable.
- Buyer budget: product teams must pay before human testing; proven only by deposit.
- Market size: dry-EEG alone may be too small; subscription attachment and adjacent head-worn markets are required.
- IP/FTO: Fraunhofer, academic, and patent claims may constrain geometry; use a professional search before product freeze.
- Credibility: publish calibration/repeatability, not authority claims.
- Data rights: preserve generic geometry/failure derivatives without exposing customer designs.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Remain employed until at least five independent paying customers or contracted recurring sites, positive contribution margin on three jobs, an acquisition path producing qualified technical calls, and 12 months of personal runway exist. Inventory must be financed by deposits/orders, not founder speculation.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If dry-EEG demand is too small, the headform, motion rigs, calibration code, and contact-mechanics knowledge can serve earables, neurostimulation, AR/VR fit testing, or be licensed as research apparatus. The founder keeps hardware and neuro-interface capability; no patient dataset is stranded.

## Critical Evidence

Peer-reviewed low-cost conductive phantoms support buildability; dry-electrode work links geometry/pressure/hair to signal quality; Fraunhofer validates the ground-truth problem; LabSim establishes the neighboring incumbent boundary.

## Critical Hypotheses

Product teams have a paid pre-human-test budget; three headforms predict enough mechanical failure to change design decisions; kits can be manufactured at 45–60% gross margin; annual calibration/scripts attach to at least half the installed base; FTO allows a differentiated commercial design; adjacent head-worn segments are reachable.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or structurally pivot if repeated-donning variance cannot distinguish intentionally different mounts; no paid deposit by day 180; fewer than 3 technical calls from 60 qualified teams; three teams say employee/academic phantoms are sufficient and free; FTO reveals blocking claims without a design-around; kit gross margin projects below 45%; or buyers want only one-off sub-10,000 PLN comparisons with no kit/subscription path.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Pre-proof maximum 30,000 PLN and 20–25 founder hours/week. Minimum stack: founder for product/data/sales, contract mechatronics/industrial design, part-time neuroengineering calibration review, and a short IP/regulatory positioning review. No full-time hire before paid evidence.
