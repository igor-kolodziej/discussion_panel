# Zero-to-One Evaluation: SignalChain Sentinel

## Diagnosis

SignalChain Sentinel identifies a real release-event failure mode and turns inexpensive hardware into a recurring product-line regression workflow rather than a generic report. Its best feature is the full physical-device-to-cloud baseline and accumulated cross-version history; its weakness is that sophisticated OEMs can build an internal harness and existing simulator vendors can move upward. The expected wealth case and 180-day deposit plan are coherent, but all willingness-to-pay, renewal, and cross-vendor reuse claims remain hypotheses. The idea merits a panel only as a tightly bounded ECG/IMU release-regression product, not as external regulatory validation.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: published wearable-update research found changes that can affect prior validation; data-quality literature documents missingness, opacity, and packet loss; EUDAMED/POLMED/PARP provide observable accounts; COTS hardware is affordable; WhaleTeq proves adjacent simulation supply. Hypotheses: a release owner pays 18,000–30,000 PLN before accredited testing; annual monitoring reaches 48,000–96,000 PLN; adapters reuse across devices; external independence matters; generic failure derivatives are contractually retainable. External reviewer verdicts: none. Missing market evidence: paid conversion, renewal rate, internal QA budgets, and adapter hours.

## Overall Score

76/100. Weighted total 152 across total weight 20: `10 × (152 / 20) = 76`.

## Control Point

Tier 2. The company directly owns replay software, adapters, fingerprints, and longitudinal failure history; switching costs grow with recurring releases. Strongest copy risk: an OEM combines an existing WhaleTeq-class simulator with one internal test engineer and keeps the entire history in-house. This remains under 80 because the control point is not exclusive and can initially be reproduced with equipment plus process.

## Founder Wealth Gate

Pass, based on expected success rather than strong success. The expected case reaches 13.63m PLN revenue, 5.04m EBIT, 27.2m equity value net of debt, 80% founder ownership, and about 23.8m PLN founder net worth in year 7 including only actual post-transition distributions. Ninety-five percent reinvestment and the 5% employment-period distribution cap are respected, and retained cash is not double-counted. The conservative case reaches about 9.2m and therefore does not independently pass; the decisive risk is achieving 150 recurring product lines without OEM internalization.

## Reachability Gate

Pass. A public ECG/IMU cross-version demonstration supports technical outbound to 100 named release-event accounts; one 18,000–24,000 PLN integration with a 40% deposit can occur within 180 days. Pre-proof founder capital is capped at 35,000 PLN. A COTS device, motion jig, and contractor suffice alongside employment; no accreditation, participant cohort, clinic, partner, or finished multi-sensor product is required.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is European ECG/IMU product lines with raw access and planned releases. Adjacent modalities and portfolio/CRO use are credible after paid adapters. A simulator vendor or strong OEM can enter; longitudinal independent history and multi-vendor comparison must outrun them. EU engineering/data handling provides a modest local wedge, not a moat.

## Fatal Objections

1. External willingness to pay may be near zero because capable OEMs view regression as internal QA and small teams accept risk.
2. Cross-vendor adapters may remain device-specific labor, preventing recurring margins and founder-wealth scale.
3. Existing simulator vendors can extend into end-to-end software, compressing differentiation.
4. Buyers may require accreditation or clinical/regulatory authority the company cannot claim.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Wearable teams need a persistent known-input baseline spanning physical sensor, wireless link, SDK, app, transformations, and cloud export across releases.
