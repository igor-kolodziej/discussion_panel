# SignalChain Sentinel

SignalChain Sentinel is an independent hardware-in-the-loop regression system for European wearable-device and digital-biomarker teams. It answers one release-critical question: did a firmware, SDK, phone/app, wireless, or cloud change silently alter the physiological signal or timing the product delivers?

The first buyer is the product, firmware, data-quality, or verification lead at a 10–100-person ECG/IMU wearable company with raw or near-raw data access and a release planned within six months. The urgent event is a pre-release verification, a mandatory SDK/OS update, or an unexplained data-quality regression. Today many teams rely on component unit tests, spot human recordings, vendor notes, and ad hoc scripts. Those methods often do not replay a known physical input through the actual sensor, wireless link, phone, SDK, transformations, and cloud export. Published research has found wearable updates capable of affecting earlier validation and documents packet loss, missingness, inconsistent definitions, and undisclosed algorithm changes.

The wedge is deliberately narrow. V1 electrically replays known ECG waveforms, mechanically replays known IMU trajectories, and measures packet/timestamp behavior through the customer's real device-to-cloud chain. It produces a machine-readable release fingerprint and a diff against the last accepted version. PPG, EDA, temperature, and multi-device synchronization are added only when paying customers fund those adapters. The company sells an operating regression station and repeated release runs; it does not sell a generic report, certification, accredited testing, clinical validation, or regulatory approval.

The commercial hypothesis is an 18,000–30,000 PLN paid first integration with 40% deposit, followed by 48,000–96,000 PLN per product line per year for scheduled and emergency release checks. Early integration contribution margin is expected to be 55–70%; mature monitoring gross margin is expected to be 70–80% as adapters and automation are reused. An optional on-prem station can sell for 45,000–75,000 PLN, always tied to software, calibration, and the longitudinal release baseline. The offer is killed if buyers will not pay before accreditation, adapter work stays bespoke, or annual monitoring does not create measurable release confidence or investigation savings.

Cold start does not require a clinic, manufacturer partnership, research partner, human cohort, or complete multi-sensor product. The founder buys public raw-data ECG/IMU devices, two test phones, an electrical replay interface, and a programmable motion jig; public SDKs and open waveforms provide the first known inputs. The founder's maximum pre-proof loss is 35,000 PLN. A published before/after cross-version demonstration is used for technical outbound. Customer-specific devices or fixtures are bought only after a deposit.

The first 100 target accounts are visible through mandatory/public EUDAMED modules, POLMED and PARP directories, CORDIS wearable projects, public SDK repositories, firmware release notes, conference programs, and hiring. The founder approaches only teams with a named release or regression event, not generic medtech prospects. The first ten customers come from founder-led event-based outbound using the technical demonstration. Customers 11–100 come from anonymized cross-version benchmark content, installed-customer referrals, release-note monitoring, and later licensed stations operated by independent test labs or CROs; no channel partnership is assumed during proof.

The directly controlled asset is the cross-vendor adapter library, known-input waveform and motion library, device/firmware/SDK release fingerprints, failure taxonomy, automated comparison engine, and each customer's longitudinal accepted baseline. Customer confidential data stays customer-owned. Contracts preserve company ownership of pre-existing and generic test methods and permit only non-identifying aggregate failure derivatives. Within twelve months, the control-point proof is three independent paid product lines, two repeated release checks, at least two reused adapters or test modules, and signed rights to retain generic methods and anonymized failure patterns. If those rights or reuse fail, the business is treated as a testing service and stopped or changed.

The competitive boundary is explicit. WhaleTeq-class products already simulate ECG/PPG signals, and capable OEMs can build internal fixtures. SignalChain Sentinel must therefore win on full physical-device-to-cloud coverage, cross-vendor release intelligence, fast support for small European teams, and longitudinal independent history—not on owning a signal generator. Every adapter and observed failure should shorten the next integration; every accepted release should make replacement more costly. The company is non-viable if an OEM can reproduce the same decision value through one equipment purchase and routine internal QA work.

The beachhead is European ECG/IMU product lines with raw access and at least two meaningful releases per year. Expansion proceeds customer-funded into PPG/EDA and then into portfolio-wide release gates, CRO device fleets, firmware-specific equivalence checks, and on-prem licensed stations. Poland supplies cost-efficient embedded, test, and signal-processing talent and direct access to a visible EU medtech base. EU data handling and local support help entry but are not claimed as a legal moat.

Founder fit is strong in signal processing, noisy physiological data, ML, analytics, and AI-assisted product development. The missing embedded/mechatronics work is purchased as a bounded 80–120-hour contractor engagement. The founder remains employed and works no more than about five hours daily. The founder does not leave employment until there are at least five independent paying customers or contracted recurring product lines, positive contribution margin on two delivered integrations, a repeatable acquisition signal, no single customer above 40% of contracted next-12-month gross profit, and twelve months of personal runway.

Founder wealth scenarios are hypotheses and do not count retained earnings twice. Business values are equity values after debt; separately listed founder cash is only cash actually distributed. While the founder remains employed, the conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash otherwise available for distribution and cap optional distributions at 5%; the strong case reinvests 100% until scale.

- Conservative, year 8: 75 monitored product lines at 68,000 PLN annual revenue, 20 integrations at 24,000 PLN, and eight stations at 55,000 PLN. Revenue is 6.02m PLN, gross margin 72%, operating margin 28%, and EBIT 1.69m PLN. With 86% founder ownership, 200,000 PLN debt, a 5.5× EBIT value, and 1.4m PLN of actual post-transition founder distributions, estimated founder net worth is about 9.2m PLN. This downside case does not reach the target.
- Expected success, year 7: 150 monitored lines at 80,000 PLN, 35 integrations at 26,000 PLN, and twelve stations at 60,000 PLN. Revenue is 13.63m PLN, gross margin 76%, operating margin 37%, and EBIT 5.04m PLN. After up to 20% dilution and less than 500,000 PLN debt, a 5.5× EBIT equity value gives the founder about 21.8m PLN of equity plus 2.0m PLN of actual post-transition distributions, or about 23.8m PLN net worth. Milestones are three paid lines in year one, eighteen in year two, and a repeatable channel in year three.
- Strong success, year 7: 400 monitored lines at 100,000 PLN plus 5m PLN of integrations/stations. Revenue is 45m PLN, gross margin 80%, operating margin 42%, and EBIT 18.9m PLN. After 40% dilution and 2m PLN net debt, a 6× EBIT equity value leaves the founder about 66.8m PLN. No distributions are added.

The expected case, not the strong case, supports the 10m PLN founder-wealth path. If eighteen recurring product lines are not reached by the end of year two, the model is not assumed to compound as projected.

The 180-day plan is staged. Days 1–30 build the ECG/IMU known-input demo. Days 31–60 publish the cross-version result and assemble 100 named release-event accounts. Days 61–120 run technical demonstrations and offer one fixed 18,000–24,000 PLN integration with a 40% deposit. Days 121–180 deliver the first device-to-cloud baseline and sell the first repeated release check. Spending stops at 12,000 PLN if replay is unstable and at 35,000 PLN without paid proof. Further spending up to 70,000 PLN requires a paid deposit, successful delivery, and positive contribution margin. Spending up to the founder's 100,000 PLN ceiling requires three independent paying customers plus a repeated check or a documented viable acquisition channel. Possible family capital remains excluded until repeatable paid demand.

Kill criteria are: fewer than four technical calls from 80 qualified release-event accounts; no deposit by day 180; three qualified teams report that internal tests already cover the physical-to-cloud chain; the first integration consumes more than 80 founder/contractor hours without reusable code; accreditation or clinical authority is required before payment; two otherwise willing buyers reject generic method/failure-derivative rights; or mature adapter economics cannot support at least 70% monitoring gross margin.

If the company fails, the owned station, adapter code, known-input library, release-diff software, and signal-chain expertise remain useful for internal engineering-kit sales, CRO/test-lab licensing, and the future human-interface lab. Paid validation would mean only that this is strong enough to pursue through staged experiments; it would not authorize serious capital spending.
