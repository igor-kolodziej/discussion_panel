# Zero-to-One Candidate: Haptic Commissioning Ledger

## One-Sentence Thesis

Turn every PV/battery installation into a connection-level digital passport by using hands-free scan and haptic confirmation to prove serials, polarity, torque, settings, and later service outcomes—reducing callbacks while building the fault graph installers and asset owners lack.

## Founder Fit

The founder has a lawful non-paying testbed in the family renewable-installation company and can combine AI workflow parsing, sensor integration, field HCI, and data products while retaining employment. The project creates wearable expertise, real-time interfaces, technician behavior data, physical-asset graphs, installer distribution, and future robotic-service knowledge. Independent paid proof must come from another installer.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is the owner/service manager of a 5–30-technician Polish PV-plus-battery installer. The acute event is commissioning a new battery/PV job whose hidden connection/configuration errors may become a callback, poor output, warranty dispute, or undocumented service problem. Today technicians use paper/app checklists, phone photos, separate inverter apps, torque tools, and memory. Evidence is asset-level, not connection-level, and is hard to search when a failure appears months later.

## Product Wedge

For one battery/inverter family, a phone scanner plus optional wrist/ring button/haptic actuator compiles the bill of materials and wiring plan into a verified execution graph. The technician scans components/terminals, imports Bluetooth torque data where available, records settings/photos, and receives silent confirmation or exception cues. The owner receives a searchable connection passport and service handover. V1 does not control electrical equipment or claim to guarantee safety.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Installers do not want generic digital instructions; they want less unpaid return work and stronger proof when manufacturers/customers question workmanship. Manufacturer apps see their device, while the installer's liability spans modules, strings, inverter, battery, protection, settings, and people. The underused control point is the longitudinal connection graph joining physical actions to later failure/service outcomes across brands.

## Underused Momentum

Poland has more than 1.6 million RES micro-installations and a public UDT installer register. Battery retrofits and multi-vendor systems increase commissioning complexity. UDT explicitly links correct installation to performance, safety, and warranty. Affordable scanners, connected torque tools, vision models, and haptics make hands-free evidence capture feasible. These facts do not prove price.

## Beachhead Market

Polish installer firms completing at least 15 battery/PV jobs monthly, carrying callback/warranty risk, and using one of two initially supported inverter/battery families. Exclude one-person firms, pure subcontract crews without service liability, and jobs whose OEM workflow already gives complete cross-system connection evidence.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Hypothesized independent 10-job pilot: 8,000–15,000 PLN including setup/hardware; direct cost 3,000–6,000 PLN.
- Production pricing: 180–280 PLN per completed passport plus 600–1,500 PLN/month company subscription; target gross margin 72–82% after templates stabilize.
- Optional connected-tool/wearable kit: 3,500–7,000 PLN at 30–45% hardware gross margin; device is not the profit center.
- Target customer value: avoided callback/evidence/admin cost at least 2× annual price.
- Mature CAC 5,000–12,000 PLN with payback below nine months. All numbers are hypotheses.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10: use the family company solely to create a demo and measure capture time. Build a list from the public UDT register and PORT PC/industry directories, filtered for battery/PV activity and staff size. Conduct direct owner outreach with a 10-job fixed pilot; require 40% deposit. Win ten independent firms through founder-led sales and customer referrals, not an OEM/manufacturer partnership.

First 100: publish anonymized callback/failure patterns, create install templates for common device combinations, use installer referrals and technical content, and later allow electrical wholesalers/training firms to resell without exclusivity. The public registry supports systematic regional expansion; each template lowers onboarding cost.

## Primary Control Point

Directly owned plan-to-execution compiler, device/connection templates, event schema, technician HCI, installed connection graph, and contractually retained non-identifying link between commissioning actions and later service/failure outcomes. Twelve-month proof is three independent paid installers, at least 100 completed passports, one documented callback/service resolution, two device-family templates, and contracts preserving generic templates/fault derivatives. The installer/customer owns identifiable asset records.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Resource needed: a credible workflow and connection dataset. Use the family business only to map one device family and test scan/haptic ergonomics for 5,000–10,000 PLN; no claim of market proof. Then sell an independent 10-job pilot before purchasing connected tools beyond one demo unit. The paying customer provides job access and normal devices as part of delivery. A contract grants rights to generic templates and anonymized fault patterns while keeping asset data private. Proof before serious capital is a deposit, completed passports under acceptable technician time, and one owner-verified reduction in evidence/rework cost.

## Distribution Advantage

The UDT registry and public installer databases expose thousands of target firms without privileged access. Device-family specialization makes outbound specific. Once a company stores completed asset histories, service lookup and future jobs create switching costs. Templates, not founder presence, must drive onboarding.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with PV/battery commissioning, then add heat pumps, EV chargers, electrical panels, and service/inspection histories. OEM apps remain single-brand; generic field-service/checklist tools lack connection semantics and hands-free verification. The competitive advantage exists only if the cross-brand graph predicts/shortens fault resolution and template onboarding becomes fast. Later the connection passport may be licensed to asset managers or service networks, but no such partnership is required for the first year.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Local electrical practices, Polish documentation, public installer certification, and fragmented multi-brand systems create a credible local wedge. Expansion requires country-specific templates and contractor channels. No formal barrier prevents foreign entry; accumulated local graph data, installed histories, and device templates provide the defense.

## Compounding Advantage

Every job expands supported connection/device combinations and later failure evidence. Templates reduce setup cost; service history raises customer switching costs; anonymized fault patterns improve exception detection. The field HCI and physical workflow data become future-lab assets for technician augmentation and service robotics.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Conservative/expected scenarios reinvest 95% of distributable after-tax cash while employed, with optional founder distributions capped at 5%; strong success reinvests 100%. Funds build templates, secure data architecture, technical onboarding, and productized hardware. Founder cash is counted only after transition. Retained cash is not added separately to the valuation.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: map one battery/inverter workflow in the family testbed and build the phone/button/haptic prototype. Days 31–60: measure added technician time and assemble 100 independent target installers. Days 61–120: owner outreach and demonstration; sell an 8,000–12,000 PLN 10-job pilot with 40% deposit. Days 121–180: complete passports, track capture time and any evidence/rework benefit, and seek paid continuation. Maximum pre-proof spend 12,000 PLN; five founder-hours/day; no OEM approval, partnership, complete product, or family-company payment required.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

1. Up to 5,000 PLN: testbed workflow and software feasibility.
2. Up to 12,000 PLN total: polished independent demo; hard pre-proof cap.
3. Up to 40,000 PLN only against a paid pilot and successful field-safety review.
4. Up to 70,000 PLN after delivery, positive contribution, and second independent payer.
5. Up to 100,000 PLN after three independent payers and repeat/recurring usage. Family capital excluded until repeatable demand.

## Critical Dependency Map

- Technician burden: capture must add little time; test directly in testbed and paid jobs.
- Buyer value: avoided callback/evidence cost must exceed price; prove with paid continuation.
- Tool/API access: V1 must work with manual scan/photo evidence if Bluetooth torque is unavailable.
- Template reuse: essential to margin; measure onboarding hours by third installer.
- Data rights/privacy: asset records stay customer-owned; only generic templates/non-identifying patterns compound.
- Substitution: OEM or generic field apps may be sufficient; qualify before sale.
- Founder conflict: family company is a testbed only; maintain corporate IP/time separation.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Remain employed until five independent paying installers or recurring contracts, positive contribution on three deliveries, at least two paid continuations, repeatable non-family acquisition, productized onboarding, and 12 months personal runway. A large public registry or family-company use is not proof.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If installers will not pay, the connection schema, hands-free HCI, plan parser, device templates, and field usability learning can transfer to electrical-panel assembly, EV chargers, heat pumps, or service robotics. Identifiable customer asset records are deleted/returned as contracted.

## Critical Evidence

URE documents the installed-base scale; UDT links installation quality with performance/safety/warranty and supplies a public acquisition list. Connected tools and scanners make the workflow feasible. Evidence does not establish per-job willingness to pay or a fault-prediction moat.

## Critical Hypotheses

Installers suffer callback/evidence costs high enough to pay 180–280 PLN/job; connection-level capture adds less than eight minutes; two device families cover a useful share; generic templates/fault derivatives are contractually acceptable; later failures can be linked to commissioning actions; 350 firms are reachable without OEM acceptance.

## Kill Criteria

Kill if no independent deposit by day 180; fewer than 4 serious calls from 80 qualified installers; capture adds more than eight minutes after iteration; three firms say existing OEM/checklist evidence is sufficient; no measurable avoided cost or paid continuation after 30 jobs; generic rights are rejected twice; onboarding remains above one day per common device family by customer 10; or contribution margin projects below 65%.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Pre-proof maximum 12,000 PLN and 20–25 founder hours/week. Minimum stack: founder; short mobile/embedded contractor engagement; electrical commissioning reviewer; privacy/contract review. No full-time hire or device inventory before proof.
