# Zero-to-One Candidate: SprayPilot

## One-Sentence Thesis

Build a clip-on real-booth motion sensor and private haptic coach that learns which spray technique produces defects on each high-mix part family, giving manual coaters robot-like process feedback without the economics of a robot.

## Founder Fit

The founder can combine biosignal-style time-series processing, sensor fusion, ML, and human feedback design with a contracted coating expert and hardware engineer. The business creates skilled-motor behavior data, real-time HCI, edge ML, wearable/hardware capability, and industrial distribution. A safe shadow-mode proof can run beside corporate employment without making safety or medical claims.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The beachhead buyer is the owner or production/quality manager of a 10–60-person Polish wet-paint or powder-coating shop handling changing geometries and meaningful manual work. The acute event is a new or recurring part family with rework, film-thickness variation, excess material, or dependence on one expert painter. Shops currently rely on apprenticeship, visual inspection, dry-film measurements after coating, offline training simulators, or expensive automation that struggles with high mix.

## Product Wedge

A safe, non-intrusive sensor mounted on the spray gun estimates speed, angle, distance proxy, orientation, and overlap; a wrist actuator can later give simple private cues. The first paid pilot is shadow mode: record 20–30 pieces in one repeated part family, pair motion with ordinary film-thickness/defect outcomes, and identify whether technique variables predict rework. Only after predictive evidence does the system deliver haptic corrections. V1 never controls the gun or safety system.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Offline simulators teach generic motion and robots repeat known geometry, but the under-served customer has changing real parts, booth airflow, coating, gun setup, and experienced manual labor. The commercially valuable asset is the empirical link between technique and actual production outcome in that shop—not generic motion tracking. A small shop will buy only if avoided rework/material and faster onboarding exceed the subscription by a clear multiple.

## Underused Momentum

Affordable IMUs/UWB/depth sensing, edge inference, haptic actuators, and labor shortages make live augmentation technically accessible. Existing VR spray simulators validate training spend while leaving the real-production loop open. The product deliberately avoids cameras in the booth and individual ranking to reduce privacy and operational resistance.

## Beachhead Market

Polish high-mix manual industrial coating shops with at least one repeated metal part family, existing film-thickness/defect records, and monthly rework/material loss plausibly above 12,000 PLN. Exclude automotive-scale robot lines, artistic shops, facilities unwilling to share outcome labels, and hazardous-area configurations that cannot accept a certified/isolated shadow sensor.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- Hypothesized paid shadow pilot: 10,000–18,000 PLN; direct sensor, contractor, and analysis cost 4,000–7,000 PLN; 50–65% contribution margin.
- Deployment: 12,000–22,000 PLN hardware/setup per booth at 40–55% gross margin.
- Subscription: 4,500–7,000 PLN per booth/month for recipes, training, analytics, and model updates; target 72–82% software/service gross margin once remote.
- Customer value gate: documented monthly scrap/rework/material/onboarding benefit at least 1.5× recurring fee in pilot and 2× by month six.
- Target mature blended gross margin: 66–73%; CAC 10,000–22,000 PLN; payback below 12 months. All are hypotheses.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10: compile 120 Polish coating shops from public business directories, trade-fair/exhibitor lists, and supplier customer references; qualify repeated geometry and defect measurement by phone/email. Build a safe bench demo and offer one part-family shadow pilot. Founder leads six local sales; documented outcomes/referrals acquire four adjacent shops. No coating-equipment partnership is required.

First 100: publish anonymized technique-to-outcome benchmarks by part/coating class, use targeted technical search and customer referrals, hire one coating applications engineer, and enter Czech/German high-mix clusters. Later sell through distributors only after direct unit economics are proven; do not grant exclusivity.

## Primary Control Point

Directly owned sensor/edge software, calibration, part/coating technique features, haptic vocabulary, and the longitudinal mapping from booth/part/gun motion to measured thickness and defect outcomes. Twelve-month proof is three independent paid shops, at least two part families, contractual rights to retain non-identifying feature/outcome derivatives, and a model that improves prediction or outcomes across repeat runs. Raw shop video/process secrets remain customer-owned.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Resource needed: safe motion-to-outcome data in a real booth. Build a sealed shadow-mode IMU prototype and use a legitimately purchased thickness gauge for about 8,000–15,000 PLN. An independent paying shop supplies its gun, booth, parts, standard PPE, and normal quality outcomes under a fixed pilot; customer-specific hardware is funded from a 40% deposit. Proof before serious capital is a paid pilot showing technique features explain a useful share of defect/thickness variance. Explosion/hazard review is completed before any booth use; if shadow hardware cannot be safely admitted, kill the shape.

## Distribution Advantage

The owner-led beachhead is locally enumerable and the pain can be qualified through repeated-part and rework questions. A short live demonstration is more credible than founder status. Once outcome improvement is measured, coating-shop owners and suppliers produce dense referrals. Installed recipes and historical outcome data create switching costs.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Start with one manual spray process and repeated metal geometry. Expand to powder/wet variants, onboarding mode, recipe transfer across sites, and other skilled tool paths such as sanding, sealant, and finishing. VR trainers remain offline; robot vendors are uneconomic for the beachhead; generic IMUs do not own the outcome-labeled corpus. The company becomes harder to copy if its cross-shop model reduces calibration time and if haptic intervention improves yield—not merely because it mounts a sensor.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Poland has many owner-led industrial subcontractors and lower-cost access for on-site learning, with adjacent Czech/German markets. Local language, safe on-prem processing, privacy-minimized operator IDs, and shop-specific integration help entry. There is no regulatory moat; hazardous-area compliance and industrial trust are execution requirements.

## Compounding Advantage

Each part family adds motion/outcome examples, accelerates calibration, improves failure taxonomy, and grows haptic recipes. Recurring software revenue funds better hardware and model automation. The long-term asset is a skilled-motor outcome corpus usable for human augmentation and future robot learning, while deployment eventually becomes installer-led rather than founder-led.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

While employed, conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of distributable after-tax cash in certified hardware design, calibration automation, technical sales, and model/data infrastructure; optional distributions remain at or below 5%. Strong success reinvests 100%. Inventory is bought against deposits. Equity value excludes retained excess cash; separately counted founder cash consists only of post-transition distributions.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: hazard/sensor feasibility, thickness-gauge bench demonstration, and 40-shop list. Days 31–75: qualify 15 shops and obtain sample motion outside a hazardous booth or in an approved setup; offer a 10,000–15,000 PLN shadow pilot with 40% deposit. Days 76–135: instrument 20–30 normal pieces and join motion to existing quality outcomes. Days 136–180: report whether the signal predicts defects; offer paid haptic/deployment phase only if evidence supports it. Maximum pre-proof risk: 15,000 PLN. Delivery fits five founder-hours/day with a coating applications contractor and no complete product.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

1. Up to 5,000 PLN: bench feasibility and safety consultation.
2. Up to 15,000 PLN total: sealed shadow prototype and demo; hard pre-proof cap.
3. Up to 40,000 PLN only against a paid pilot and approved booth method.
4. Up to 75,000 PLN after successful initial delivery, positive contribution, and a second independent payer.
5. Up to 100,000 PLN after three payers plus recurring demand/acquisition evidence. Family capital excluded until repeatability.

## Critical Dependency Map

- Safety/hazard admission: controllable review; fatal if economical shadow hardware is impossible.
- Technical signal: technique features must predict outcome; directly testable in first pilot.
- Outcome labels: shop must already measure thickness/defects; contractual customer obligation.
- Worker adoption: private cues and no ranking; observe, do not assume.
- Cross-shop transfer: required for margin/wealth; test by third shop.
- Rights: retain anonymized feature/failure derivatives; reject engagements that erase the corpus.
- Distribution: direct shop owner channel must produce paid pilots at workable CAC.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Leave employment only after five independent paying shops or contracted recurring booths, positive contribution on three pilots, at least two conversions to subscription, a repeatable acquisition signal, safe repeatable installation, and 12 months personal runway. One reference plant or grant does not qualify.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

If coating demand fails, the sealed sensor stack, edge motion models, haptic patterns, industrial safety learning, and outcome-labeled skilled-movement corpus can transfer to sanding, sealant, assembly, or robot demonstration capture. No regulated health data is stranded.

## Critical Evidence

Existing VR spray systems validate training as a category but not this real-production wedge. Affordable sensors and outcome gauges make shadow-mode proof feasible. Broader workplace-wearable evidence requires participation and transparent data use.

## Critical Hypotheses

Manual technique explains enough measured outcome variance; high-mix shops have recurring losses above the fee; a safe sensor can be admitted economically; owners will allow derivative features; workers accept private haptics; calibration generalizes enough for 67%+ gross margin; 120 global/European booths are reachable without heroic share.

## Kill Criteria

Kill if safe booth operation cannot be approved under 40,000 PLN; no deposit by day 180; fewer than 3 serious trials from 60 qualified shops; motion features fail to predict a decision-relevant outcome on two part families; measured value is below 1.5× recurring fee; workers reject the device; derivative rights fail twice; or the tenth deployment still requires more than 30 custom engineering hours.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Pre-proof maximum 15,000 PLN and 20–25 founder hours/week. Minimum stack: founder; fixed-scope coating applications specialist; electronics/enclosure contractor; one hazardous-area/safety review. No hire or inventory before paid proof.
