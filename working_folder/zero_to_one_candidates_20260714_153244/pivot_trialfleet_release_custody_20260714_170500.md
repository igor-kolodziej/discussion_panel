# Zero-to-One Pivot: TrialFleet Release Custody

## Preserved Insight

Known-input physical-to-cloud replay can reveal firmware, SDK, timing, and transformation changes that component tests miss.

## Structural Change

Change the buyer from the wearable OEM that can internalize QA to a digital-trial operator or CRO that manages multi-vendor device fleets and bears the cost when a vendor update corrupts an active study. Change value capture from optional product-line testing to a contracted release-custody workflow: the buyer routes every fleet-affecting update through a physically held golden-device library and a pre-agreed accept/hold/escalate rule before field rollout.

## Target Niche and Buyer

Small European digital-trial CROs and decentralized-study technology providers operating ECG/IMU wearable fleets from at least two vendors, with active longitudinal studies and no in-house hardware test lab. Buyer: head of data operations, device operations, or quality.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

A device vendor announces or silently pushes a firmware/SDK change during an active study, creating missingness, discontinuity, or reprocessing risk across hundreds of participants. Hypothesis: the operator pays 20,000–35,000 PLN for initial fleet custody and 80,000–180,000 PLN annually because one corrupted study can cost far more. Payment is for release control and evidence, not a generic test report.

## Value and Profit Wedge

The CRO deposits two golden devices per supported model plus the approved phone/app configuration. TrialFleet runs known-input ECG/IMU and packet/timing regression before the operator authorizes a release; emergency vendor updates trigger a rapid run. The subscription includes physical custody, configuration fingerprints, release manifests, and an incident playbook. Additional vendors are prepaid modules.

## Domination Wedge

Own the release-custody workflow for small multi-vendor trial fleets that cannot justify a lab. Every supported device and study configuration adds adapter reuse and an independent continuity history across vendors.

## Acquisition Channel

Build named accounts from public trial-technology directories, CORDIS projects, conference programs, device/API documentation, and job posts mentioning wearable fleet/data operations. Offer a paid 8,000–12,000 PLN release-risk discovery using the operator's current device inventory; require an annual contract or deposit before building fixtures.

## Primary Control Point

Contracted custody of the buyer's golden devices, the signed release-routing procedure, accepted configuration fingerprints, and longitudinal multi-vendor continuity history. This controls workflow and physical reference inventory rather than merely owning equipment.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Use the existing ECG/IMU replay prototype. The first buyer ships golden devices and pays discovery before customer-specific work. Founder spend remains below 35,000 PLN. Proof within 12 months is two independent operators contractually routing release decisions through the service, at least one repeated release run, and retained generic adapter/failure rights.

## Expansion Path

Expand from small CRO fleets to sponsor portfolios, device substitution pre-scoping, multi-site configuration custody, and on-prem licensed stations. Do not claim clinical endpoint equivalence or regulatory approval.

## Minimum People Stack

Founder, bounded embedded/test contractor, and fixed-scope quality/legal adviser. Add device-operations support only after recurring contracts.

## Principal Risk

CROs may have slow trust/procurement cycles, require a mature QMS, or internalize the lab; release authority may remain with the sponsor or device vendor, leaving the buyer unable to route the decision.

## First Disconfirming Test

From 50 qualified multi-vendor operators, obtain one paid release-risk discovery and written confirmation that the operator can contractually route a real release decision. Kill if the buyer can only request an advisory report or procurement requires accreditation/QMS investment above the 40,000 PLN pre-proof limit.

## Evidence Preserved

Published wearable update and data-quality evidence; affordable replay hardware; public technical account discovery; the exact panel consensus that optional OEM QA is copyable.

## New Hypotheses

Small CROs lack internal benches; they control the release-routing decision; golden-device custody is acceptable; an unknown founder can qualify within 180 days; 80,000+ PLN annual WTP exists; adapter reuse supports recurring margin; sponsor/vendor contracts permit independent testing.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

The buyer is no longer the device maker that can cheaply substitute its internal QA. The paid event is an active-study fleet update, the value is continuity protection, and the control point is contracted custody plus release-routing workflow. It still fails if the CRO lacks decision authority or demands an uncontracted advisory report.

## Priority

This is the only pivot worth re-evaluating because it preserves the technical insight while materially changing buyer, urgent event, value capture, and control point. Its trust/procurement risk may still require killing the family.
