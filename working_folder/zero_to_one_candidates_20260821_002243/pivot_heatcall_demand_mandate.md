# Zero-to-One Pivot: HeatCall Demand Mandate

## Preserved Insight

A waste-heat feasibility exercise becomes commercially valuable when it ends in a controlled, transferable project right rather than a report that either party can freely shop.

Preserve that rights-conversion insight, but accept the evaluator’s diagnosis: the original source-paid option is structurally unattractive. The repair must establish a committed demand-side payer before requesting source exclusivity and must convert “first negotiation” into a counsel-approved, objectively exercisable heat-supply call.

## Structural Change

The venture becomes the disclosed acquisition agent for a named heat buyer, not a seller of studies to heat sources.

Material changes:

1. **Payer:** a privately controlled heat buyer prepays the first engagement.
2. **Source economics:** the source receives, rather than pays, an option premium and prospective indexed heat revenue.
3. **Order of commitment:** the buyer’s paid mandate and demand envelope exist before source outreach.
4. **Owned control:** a tripartite conditional heat-supply call contains an attached heat-agreement form and objective exercise conditions, not merely a promise to negotiate.
5. **Assignment perimeter:** transfer is limited to the named buyer or a defined financing SPV, not an unknown future assignee.
6. **Risk allocation:** the buyer funds source premiums, engineering and later development; the source retains the premium if conditions fail; the venture never funds pipes, equipment or construction guarantees.
7. **First paid event:** a non-refundable buyer origination retainer, not a source-paid feasibility report.
8. **Value capture:** portfolio retainers, rights milestones, closing fees and later rights administration replace dependence on reselling six speculative options annually.

## Circumvention Design

The transaction sequence is:

1. A private heat buyer signs a 90–120-day exclusive procurement mandate for one defined demand envelope and prepays a non-refundable retainer.
2. The buyer supplies twelve months of demand data, temperature/availability requirements, maximum delivered-price formula, permitted radius, technical constraints, budget authority and permission to identify it to screened sources.
3. The venture searches only inside that funded envelope.
4. A qualifying source signs a tripartite conditional heat-supply call with the venture and named buyer. The buyer-funded premium is paid to the source at signing.
5. The call includes an attached long-form heat-agreement form or objectively complete commercial schedule: meter/delivery point, volume band, availability tolerance, price/index formula, permitted operational curtailment, data/site access, duration, liability perimeter, conditions precedent, exercise deadline and assignment only to the named buyer’s approved SPV.
6. A contracted thermal engineer tests the objective conditions. The buyer funds this work.
7. If the conditions pass, the buyer exercises the call or pays for a defined extension. If they fail, the source keeps the premium, exclusivity expires and no party has construction liability.
8. The venture earns a rights milestone when the call is signed and a closing fee only when the definitive heat agreement or project financing closes.

No source is asked to surrender rights before a named buyer and cash exist. No buyer is asked to buy a vague “first negotiation” right.

## Lawful-Structure Check

The structure contains no deception, coercion or prohibited reliance.

Polish energy and contract counsel must determine whether the appropriate instrument is a conditional offer, preliminary agreement, option, agency arrangement or another enforceable form. It must be sufficiently definite to create the intended exercise right. If objective price, volume, availability and transfer mechanics cannot be made enforceable, the pivot ends.

The venture is explicitly the buyer’s acquisition agent; it does not claim neutral engineering advice or secretly represent both parties. The source can obtain independent advice. The engineer signs technical outputs. Construction, heat supply, land access, permitting, regulated activity and financing remain with the buyer or project SPV after separate approval. The initial beachhead excludes municipal procurement and other buyers that cannot lawfully sign the mandate directly.

Confidential source data remains source-controlled. Only explicitly licensed aggregate features, negotiation outcomes and non-confidential metadata may enter the reusable dataset.

## Target Niche and Buyer

Initial niche: privately controlled industrial-park or closed-heat-network operators in the Wielkopolska–Łódź–Mazovia corridor that:

- have an existing or board-authorised 1–5 MW low- or medium-temperature replacement load;
- can provide a measured demand curve;
- are considering boiler replacement, capacity expansion or a defined decarbonisation project;
- control their procurement budget without a municipal tender;
- can finance feasibility and later sponsor an SPV; and
- lack a dedicated external heat-source origination team.

The first source search is performed only after one such buyer pays. Public utilities, speculative developers without capital authority and buyers seeking unpaid success-only scouting are excluded from the proof phase.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The painful event is an approaching boiler-replacement, expansion or decarbonisation investment decision where the buyer must determine whether nearby external heat can replace part of the planned fuel and equipment spend.

The retainer is justified only if the buyer believes a bounded, independent search can prevent a costly wrong-site or wrong-technology decision and secure a source before another buyer does. This willingness to prepay remains a hypothesis requiring direct proof.

For the source, the exchange is concrete: cash now, a named credible buyer, a short exclusivity period and prospective heat revenue. It no longer pays for the study or grants an open-ended right to an uncommitted intermediary.

## Value and Profit Wedge

All prices and conversions below are hypotheses requiring research.

Initial transaction:

- Buyer mandate retainer: PLN 25k–40k prepaid.
- Pre-sale founder expenditure: no more than PLN 15k for counsel, engineer availability, public-data mapping and outreach.
- Source-call milestone: PLN 60k–100k, funding source premium, engineering and legal work.
- Definitive-agreement or financing close: PLN 180k–300k.
- Later annual portfolio mandate: PLN 100k–200k per buyer.
- Rights-calendar, measurement-baseline and amendment administration: PLN 20k–50k per commissioned project annually.

Founder-wealth shape, without adding retained cash to EBIT-based value:

- **Adverse, year 1:** no paid mandate after 40 qualified buyers; stop with no more than PLN 15k lost.
- **Conservative, year 7:** seven annual mandates at PLN 120k, four closings at PLN 180k and eight administered projects at PLN 30k produce about PLN 1.8m revenue. At 22% operating margin, 90% founder ownership and 3× EBIT, founder equity is about PLN 1.1m and does not reach the target.
- **Expected-success, year 8:** sixteen mandates at PLN 150k, twelve closings at PLN 250k and thirty administered projects at PLN 40k produce PLN 6.6m revenue. At 68% gross margin, 32% operating margin, 80% founder ownership and 4× EBIT, founder equity is about PLN 6.8m.
- **Strong success, year 9:** thirty mandates at PLN 180k, twenty-four closings at PLN 300k and sixty administered projects at PLN 50k produce PLN 15.6m revenue. At 35% operating margin, 75% ownership and 5× EBIT, founder equity is about PLN 20.5m.

While the founder remains employed, conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash and cap optional distributions at 5%; strong success reinvests 100%. The expected case must be rejected if prospect mapping cannot support its buyer denominator, twelve annual closings require founder-led bespoke work, or actual pricing/margins fall materially below these hypotheses.

## Domination Wedge

The initial niche is independent, buyer-side acquisition of executable heat rights for private closed networks—not general energy consulting, engineering or an open marketplace.

The compounding assets are:

- paid, buyer-specific demand envelopes with real price ceilings;
- source calls containing comparable objective exercise terms;
- measured pass/fail thermodynamic and route outcomes;
- contract-clause and negotiation-outcome data that may be reused;
- repeat portfolio mandates with buyers; and
- a live graph of which source conditions match which funded demand profiles.

A public source map alone is not a moat. The wedge becomes difficult to route around only if buyers renew exclusive portfolio mandates and the venture controls several live source calls. If incumbents can obtain equivalent mandates with one contract change before that portfolio forms, there is no domination case.

## Acquisition Channel

Build a named list of forty privately controlled heat buyers from industrial-estate records, environmental permits, company expansion notices, heat-network maps and energy-transition disclosures.

Outbound uses one concrete artifact: a redacted sample demand-ring screen showing how a measured buyer load becomes a source shortlist and an objectively exercisable call. The offer is a fixed, paid procurement sprint—not a free audit or broad consulting proposal.

The family renewables company may provide a non-paying workflow test or introduction, but neither family revenue nor a family signature counts as independent paid proof. No influencer presence, public personal brand or unspecified partnership is required.

## Primary Control Point

The control point is the combination of:

1. a **prepaid exclusive buyer procurement mandate** for a defined load, geography and term; and
2. a **tripartite, buyer-backed conditional heat-supply call** with definite commercial terms, an attached agreement form, source compensation, objective exercise conditions and transfer limited to the named buyer’s approved SPV.

The mandate protects the venture’s transaction and fee. The call controls the source opportunity. Neither element alone is sufficient.

Within twelve months and PLN 100k, control proof requires two unrelated paid buyer mandates, one executed tripartite call, one paid rights milestone and either exercise into a definitive heat agreement or a buyer-funded extension.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Before selling:

- spend no more than PLN 7k on the mandate/call perimeter with Polish counsel;
- reserve named thermal-engineer availability under a fixed-scope contractor agreement for no more than PLN 3k;
- spend no more than PLN 2k on a public-data demonstration map; and
- reserve PLN 3k for outreach and travel.

No source premium, meter deployment or bespoke engineering is funded before the buyer retainer. The retainer funds the first source premium and engineering screen. Further founder capital is released only after successful paid delivery and a second independent buyer.

## Expansion Path

After one exercised call:

1. sell annual multi-source mandates to the same buyer;
2. cover adjacent private networks and industrial campuses in Poland;
3. add contracted regional originators using the same demand-envelope and call process;
4. expand to neighbouring CEE markets only after local counsel adapts the contract perimeter; and
5. add rights administration and portfolio measurement after commissioned projects create recurring work.

Do not launch a marketplace before at least five unrelated paid buyers and several live controlled calls. An unfunded source listing platform would recreate the original weak asset.

## Minimum People Stack

Proof phase:

- founder: demand/source data model, mapping, buyer acquisition and process control;
- one named thermal engineer: fixed-scope technical acceptance work;
- one Polish energy/contract lawyer: mandate and call instrument;
- route surveyor only after buyer funding and a viable source call.

After repeat proof, add a commissioned regional originator and project coordinator. Construction, plant operation, equipment procurement and project finance never sit in the founder’s proof-stage company.

## Principal Risk

The decisive risk is that qualified buyers will not prepay an origination mandate and will pay only after a fully de-risked project exists. If so, the venture cannot finance source compensation from customer cash, an unknown founder remains exposed to long unpaid development cycles, and the pivot fails Reachability.

## Residual Rule, Reputation, Technical, and Economic Risks

- **Law:** exact call definiteness, assignment, agency, energy-supply, permitting and future procurement treatment require counsel.
- **Reputation:** private buyers and sources may still demand references, insurance and engineering credentials beyond a named contractor.
- **Technical:** no source in the funded radius may satisfy temperature, continuity, simultaneity or route economics.
- **Economic:** retainers, milestones and recurring administration may be lower than assumed; sales and project cycles may exceed employment-compatible capacity.
- **Competition:** HeatRec, Veolia or another ESCO can add buyer-side origination and bring stronger engineering and finance credibility.
- **Confidentiality:** data permissions may prevent cross-project learning.
- **Durability:** buyers may internalise sourcing after the first transaction or refuse portfolio exclusivity.
- **Capital:** the buyer may fund diligence but fail to finance construction; no founder guarantee is permitted.
- **Standardisation:** multi-party negotiation may remain too bespoke to support twelve annual closings.

## First Disconfirming Test

Days 1–30:

- execute the bounded engineer arrangement and obtain counsel’s go/no-go view on the mandate and tripartite call;
- build one public-data demonstration ring and forty-buyer list;
- founder spend remains at or below PLN 12k.

Days 31–100:

- approach forty qualified private buyers;
- require one buyer to provide its measured demand envelope, sign the exclusive mandate and prepay at least PLN 20k;
- kill the pivot if every buyer demands unpaid success-only work or no buyer prepays.

Days 101–165:

- screen at least thirty sources inside the paid buyer’s envelope;
- use buyer funds for source premium and engineering;
- require one source to sign the exact tripartite conditional call with definite price, volume, availability and exercise mechanics.

By day 180:

- require the buyer to pay a second, non-refundable rights milestone of at least PLN 25k after the source call;
- total founder cash at risk remains at or below PLN 15k;
- founder remains within five hours per day.

Kill if the call can only promise future negotiation, the source refuses short compensated exclusivity, the buyer will not fund the source premium, the engineer cannot create objective conditions, or the second milestone is not paid.

## Evidence Preserved

- [Directive (EU) 2023/1791](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/1791/oj/) supports narrow policy momentum and defined cost-benefit analysis, not compulsory private contracting.
- The [Commission’s April 2026 recommendation](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/document/download/dfa0c3df-0384-4383-ac9e-cae4ab2114c7_en?filename=C_2026_2676_1_EN_ACT_part1_v4.pdf) supports the importance of long-term heating/cooling agreements and derisking; this motivates securing definite rights.
- [HeatRec](https://heatrec.eu/en/) and [Veolia ESCO Polska](https://escopolska.pl/) prove that ordinary feasibility and implementation already have capable incumbents.
- [ORLEN’s accelerator](https://www.orlen.pl/en/about-the-company/innovations/accelerator) proves industrial attention to waste-heat integration, not buyer willingness to prepay this mandate.
- The [Polish Civil Code](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2026/795/ogl) provides general contractual tools; the exact tripartite instrument remains counsel-required.

## New Hypotheses

The following require research and paid-market proof:

- at least forty privately controlled Polish buyers fit the initial authority, load and budget screen;
- one such buyer will prepay PLN 20k–40k within 180 days;
- buyers will provide real demand curves and price ceilings before a source exists;
- a source will accept a short, compensated, named-buyer call;
- objective price, volume, availability and assignment terms can be made enforceable;
- buyer-funded premiums of PLN 5k–15k are sufficient for source commitment;
- the buyer will pay a second milestone on controlled rights before permitting and construction;
- portfolio mandates of PLN 100k–200k annually are commercially supportable;
- completed projects support recurring rights administration;
- sixteen recurring buyers and twelve annual closings are reachable by year eight with a small team;
- permitted aggregate data reduce sourcing and negotiation cost;
- independent buyer representation matters enough to overcome incumbent credibility;
- route, financing and operational conditions can remain with the buyer/SPV without founder guarantees.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

The original source paid cash and surrendered 24 months of exclusivity before a buyer or definite heat right existed. This pivot reverses every decisive element:

- the buyer pays first;
- the source receives compensation;
- the buyer is named and supplies the capital;
- exclusivity is shorter and buyer-specific;
- the controlled instrument leads to an attached heat agreement on objective terms;
- assignment is limited to the named buyer’s financing SPV;
- the venture does not speculate on unfunded source options;
- revenue begins with a demand-side retainer and rights milestone;
- construction risk remains outside the venture; and
- regulation is not used to imply customer compulsion.

This is the only pivot worth returning to a fresh `$evaluate-zero-to-one` agent first. A source-funded revision repeats the fatal exchange, a founder-funded free-study option retains buyer absence and capital risk, and a generic heat marketplace lacks controlled rights.
