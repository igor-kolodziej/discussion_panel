# Zero-to-One Candidate: GridClock SPV Call Book

## One-Sentence Thesis

Acquire enforceable, assignable call options over 100% of Polish project SPVs holding post-30-April-2026 electricity connection conditions that are approaching their one-year expiry, then sell a diligence reservation or option assignment to storage/renewable developers before the venture ever exercises or funds construction.

## Founder Fit

The founder can mine public planning/environmental/KRS records, quantify project clocks, manage diligence data, and analyse option economics. Finance knowledge fits contingent claims; the family renewable installer can sanity-check small-project documents but is neither the seller nor the buyer. Work is asynchronous enough for five hours/day until live diligence calls intensify.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

Supply-side customer: a small project owner who spent on land, planning, studies, and a grid application but cannot fund or complete the connection agreement before conditions lapse. It currently lists the whole project, hires a broker, rushes a sale, or loses sunk value. Demand-side customer: a developer that wants a screened grid path but will not buy an opaque SPV before technical, land, tax, and change-of-control diligence.

## Product Wedge

The venture pays a small option fee only after document review. The owner grants a time-limited call or irrevocable offer over all SPV shares in the legally required form, plus non-disposal, penalty, data-room access, and assignment/co-exercise rights. A downstream buyer pays a non-refundable diligence reservation credited to a fixed assignment/exercise fee. The venture never describes the grid condition itself as freely transferable.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

The 2026 rule makes ordinary electricity conditions expire after one year and creates security/deposit pressure. Listings expose projects after owners decide to sell; the venture proposes a clock-indexed salvage book that secures the corporate control right first and sells staged diligence rather than the project. The gap is unproved until one owner grants the option cheaply and one buyer prepays before exercise.

## Underused Momentum

Poland's crowded connection queue, new one-year validity, transition security, and storage demand create more expiring corporate shells with partial work. The statute supplies urgency, not transferability, seller consent, or buyer budget.

## Beachhead Market

Polish solar, onshore-wind, or battery SPVs with conditions delivered after 30 April 2026; no signed connection agreement; one clearly identified shareholder group; documented land access; power small enough that seller expectations and diligence fit an option under PLN 15k; at least 90 days remain when optioned. Exclude offshore, nuclear, rail exceptions, disputed shares, and projects requiring technology-change assumptions.

## Incentive and Power Map

- **Loss bearer:** current SPV owner loses sunk development value if the clock expires.
- **Payer:** downstream developer pays reservation/assignment; owner receives option and strike/closing value.
- **Beneficiaries:** owner salvages value; buyer obtains a screened contingent path.
- **Authority holders:** DSO controls conditions/connection agreement; shareholders control shares; landowners and public bodies control site/permits.
- **Information holders:** SPV/DSO hold decisive documents; venture organises the clock/data room.
- **Risk bearer / transaction owner:** venture risks only option/diligence spend and owns the corporate call; buyer bears project capital after exercise.
- **First paid event:** buyer prepays one non-refundable diligence reservation.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Illustrative hypotheses: PLN 5k–12k option fee; PLN 8k–15k legal/technical diligence per selected SPV; buyer reservation PLN 20k–40k; closing assignment/exercise fee PLN 150k–350k, with reservation credited; owner strike separately agreed and never financed by the venture unless buyer funds simultaneous closing. Target net contribution after owner option, counsel, engineer, and failed-option allocation is PLN 100k–250k per closed assignment.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

First 10 controlled options: mine public environmental/planning decisions and company records, contact the exact owners, request conditions/land/corporate documents, and pay nothing before red-flag review. Buyers: directly approach named Polish/CEE IPPs, storage developers, infrastructure funds, and EPC-development arms with anonymised clock cards, then data-room access under NDA after reservation terms. One hundred options require a repeatable analyst/counsel network and referrals from owners whose otherwise stranded value was recovered; no marketplace is built first.

## Rule and Constraint Classification

- `LAW / BINDING RULE`: amended Energy Law Article 7(8i) makes ordinary electricity conditions valid one year, with explicit exceptions.
- `CONTRACTUAL RULE`: share form, option assignment, non-disposal, land, DSO, and change-of-control clauses determine whether the option has value.
- `ECONOMIC LIMITATION`: buyer value must exceed owner strike, remaining security/connection cost, diligence, and time-to-expiry risk.

## Lawful-Structure Check

Polish energy/M&A counsel must review every option and underlying SPV. A transfer of sp. z o.o. shares or an irrevocable offer must use the required form. Competition, AML, beneficial-owner, tax, brokerage/licensing, public-aid, land, and DSO issues are checked before payment. The venture makes no representation that a share transfer changes the DSO's rights or extends any clock.

## Constraint-Circumvention Attempts

An owner can list or sell directly; a buyer can mine the same records; a broker can ask for exclusivity; a DSO condition may be valueless or lapse. The venture has control only during an enforceable option term with a penalty and document access. It must win by identifying time-sensitive sellers earlier, standardising red flags, and accumulating closed-buyer trust—not by claiming exclusive public data.

## Primary Control Point

Tier-1/2 legal control conditional on enforceability: a call or irrevocable offer over 100% of a named SPV, in required form, with non-disposal covenant, liquidated damages, data/site access, assignment/co-exercise, and a strike/long-stop. The DSO condition itself remains controlled by law/DSO and is not the venture's asset.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Commission one fixed-fee Polish energy/M&A template and one technical red-flag checklist. Build 100 public project-owner records. Review ten document sets, option only one, and market it to 20 named buyers. Do not build software, exercise shares, post DSO security, or order equipment during proof.

## Distribution Advantage

Public planning/environmental records reveal projects before sale listings; KRS reveals owners. A clock-specific offer—"recover optionality before your conditions expire without a forced immediate sale"—is concrete. Buyer outreach uses a comparable one-page risk card rather than status-based networking.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Each failed and closed option improves the red-flag model, owner pricing, buyer-fit graph, and standard terms. Expansion is from one DSO/technology/size band to adjacent Polish regions, then only later to jurisdictions with separately verified corporate/connection rules.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

The wedge depends on Poland's 2026 Energy Law, Polish SPV/share formalities, DSO documents, land instruments, planning records, and buyer practice. Foreign project brokers cannot reuse a generic contract without local diligence; Polish incumbents can still copy after observing demand.

## Compounding Advantage

A controlled book gives buyers repeated access to comparable options and gives owners evidence the venture can close. Diligence outcomes reveal which DSO/land/share clauses predict closing. Compounding fails if sellers demand full project value up front or buyers refuse reservations before complete RTB status.

## Founder Wealth Model

All figures are hypotheses, before personal tax, and exclude any speculative retained project equity.

- **Adverse, year 3:** two closings/year x PLN 100k net contribution = PLN 0.2m against PLN 0.5m fixed cost; close the book, founder wealth near zero.
- **Conservative, year 7:** 24 controlled options/year, 10 closings x PLN 250k net contribution = PLN 2.5m; PLN 1.5m EBITDA after PLN 1.0m team/data/legal overhead; 5x = PLN 7.5m enterprise value; 75% founder ownership after a 10% option pool and 15% financing dilution = **PLN 5.63m founder equity**.
- **Expected, year 7:** 40 controlled options/year, 18 closings x PLN 350k net contribution = PLN 6.3m; PLN 4.1m EBITDA after PLN 2.2m overhead; 6x = PLN 24.6m; 70% founder ownership = **PLN 17.22m founder equity**.

The model fails if the venture must buy shares/DSO security before buyer funding, or if ten annual closings is not supported by a much larger qualified owner denominator.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Reinvest 95% of distributable profit through year 5 into owner sourcing, fixed-scope counsel, engineers, data security, buyer coverage, and small option deposits. Retain 5% distributions. Model 15% outside dilution to fund a broader option book only after two paid reservations and one closing; construction capital and buyer strike never sit on the founder balance sheet.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: counsel freezes form/checklist; create 100-owner list. Days 31–75: obtain ten document rooms and written seller price expectations. Days 76–110: sign one option for <=PLN 10k after technical/legal review. Days 111–180: approach 20 named buyers, collect a PLN 20k–40k non-refundable diligence reservation, and provide controlled data-room access. Maximum spend: PLN 40k (PLN 12k legal, PLN 8k technical, PLN 10k option, PLN 5k data/security, PLN 5k contingency).

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- PLN 12k legal form/perimeter; stop if no enforceable assignable instrument.
- PLN 8k sourcing/data; stop if fewer than five owners share documents.
- PLN 15k option plus technical review only after one project passes red flags.
- PLN 5k contingency after buyer outreach begins.
- Twelve-month cap PLN 100k for three options; never exercise or post project security without simultaneous buyer funding.

## Critical Dependency Map

1. Correctly dated in-scope connection conditions.
2. Clean SPV/share/beneficial-owner authority.
3. Land and DSO terms that survive contemplated control change.
4. Enforceable, assignable option in required form.
5. Named buyer willingness to reserve before RTB.
6. Simultaneous closing structure that avoids project capital on venture balance sheet.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

Remain employed until at least three option assignments close, the trailing six-month gross contribution exceeds PLN 300k, eight additional enforceable options have >120 days remaining, two unrelated buyers have paid, and no founder guarantee/project construction capital is required.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

Unexercised options expire at capped loss. Reusable assets are counsel-approved forms, public project-owner map, buyer graph, diligence taxonomy, and non-confidential outcome statistics. No project or land is stranded on the venture balance sheet.

## Critical Evidence

- Exact 2026 statute and effective/transitional treatment.
- One post-30-April condition plus draft connection agreement.
- Share/land/change-of-control counsel memo.
- One executed option and one buyer reservation.
- One simultaneous assignment/closing before expiry for commercial confirmation.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Near-expiry owners accept small option fees rather than full upfront sales.
- Public records identify enough owners early.
- Buyers pay for pre-RTB optionality under controlled diligence.
- Option form deters circumvention and is assignable.
- Ten annual closings and PLN 250k net contribution are conservative by year 7.

## Kill Criteria

Kill if counsel cannot make the option enforceable/assignable; seller demands >PLN 15k before buyer proof; fewer than five of 100 owners share conditions; no option is signed by day 110; no buyer pays a reservation by day 180; DSO/land terms make share control worthless; the venture must fund strike/security/construction; or the first closing produces <PLN 100k net contribution after failed-option allocation.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Paid proof: <=PLN 40k and <=180 days. Control proof: three enforceable options plus one paid buyer/closing within <=PLN 100k and <=12 months. People: founder, Polish energy/M&A counsel, fixed-fee grid engineer, accountant/tax review; no employees or unspecified development partner before proof.
