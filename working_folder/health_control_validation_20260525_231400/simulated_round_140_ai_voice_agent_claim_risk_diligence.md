## Why It Clears Internal Simulation

- The buyer is not the AI vendor. It is a fund, searcher, PE buyer, corporate development team, or strategic acquirer with a live check, LOI, procurement award, or acquisition thesis at risk.
- The claim is falsifiable inside a short sprint: containment, transfer quality, hallucinations, disclosure behavior, call recovery, language/accent performance, latency, integration/tool-use failures, and cost per resolved call versus human baseline.
- The artifact can change a real decision: proceed, discount valuation, add earnout/holdback, demand more evidence, limit scope, or kill the deal.
- The 60-day proof is paid and decision-linked: three live diligence mandates, not free opinions.
- Founder capital requirement is low; expert review can be contracted only for call-center ops, privacy, and AI eval edge cases.

## Internal Caps Applied

- Not capped below 82: 60-day control proof requires paid live mandates and target artifacts, not interviews.
- Not capped below 85: a generic AI consultant can copy a checklist, but cannot copy the live paid decision file, test-call corpus, buyer-specific adjustment memo, or repeated accepted decision language once a mandate is signed.
- Not capped below 87: economics are credible only if the buyer has a live financing, LOI, acquisition, or enterprise procurement decision worth far more than the 8,000-35,000 PLN fee.

## Why Not Higher

- Technical diligence firms, call-center consultants, and AI advisers can copy parts.
- Access to target recordings, logs, and economics may be restricted.
- Some investors and buyers can run their own tests.
- If the output is not tied to a live deal/procurement decision, it becomes a generic AI report and should be killed.
