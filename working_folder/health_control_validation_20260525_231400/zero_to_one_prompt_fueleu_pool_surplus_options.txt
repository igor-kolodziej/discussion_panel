Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
FuelEU Pool Surplus Options

One-sentence thesis:
Option named FuelEU Maritime compliance surplus from small EU/CEE ship managers before the verifier-recorded pooling window, match it to deficit ship operators through signed commercial allocation terms, and collect buyer deposits only where the selected verifier path, participating companies, ship list, and pool-allocation mechanics are documented.

Exact buyer:
The paying buyer is a ship owner, ship manager, charterer, or maritime compliance desk with vessels expected to have a FuelEU Maritime compliance deficit and a need to avoid or reduce FuelEU penalty exposure through lawful pooling. The supply side is a company with one or more ships expected to produce positive FuelEU compliance balance because of fuel mix, route profile, low-carbon fuel use, RFNBO exposure, operational profile, or overcompliance. The first niche is not large global fleets with full internal compliance teams; it is small and mid-sized EU/CEE ship managers, ferry operators, offshore/service-vessel managers, short-sea operators, and maritime groups whose surplus or deficit is too small for a bespoke trading desk but meaningful enough to monetize.

Acute trigger:
FuelEU Maritime applies to covered maritime energy use from 2025 onward. Companies need to manage annual verified compliance balances, and Article 21 pooling allows the compliance balances of two or more ships to be pooled if the pool is valid and recorded in the FuelEU database by the selected verifier. Deficit operators face penalty exposure if they do nothing, while surplus operators may leave value unused if they only bank or ignore surplus. The commercial trigger is a live 2026 compliance forecast, budget close, charterer allocation discussion, or verifier/compliance-adviser review showing likely surplus or deficit before the 2027 verification and pooling deadline.

Control point:
A signed commercial option or pool-allocation mandate over named ships, expected compliance balance range, responsible company, selected verifier path, data owner contacts, FuelEU monitoring/reporting status, draft pool terms, allocation rules, buyer deposit terms, no-go rules if verified balances change, and a requirement that all participating companies validate pool details in the FuelEU database when the formal window is reached. The startup controls a specific surplus/deficit match and commercial option before it is marketed broadly. Accredited verifiers, ship companies, lawyers, and maritime compliance advisers retain responsibility for verification, database recording, compliance statements, contracts, and final allocation validity.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, the founder signs two surplus-side commercial options over named ships with expected positive FuelEU compliance balances, one deficit-side buyer mandate with a refundable buyer deposit or escrow instruction of at least 30,000 PLN equivalent, one selected-verifier or maritime-compliance-adviser review path for the proposed pool mechanics, and a draft pool-allocation term sheet covering price, allocation, balance uncertainty, data rights, company approvals, verifier process, and refund/no-go conditions. No success claim is made until the formal FuelEU database process is completed, but the first proof is current cash tied to a named, documented surplus/deficit match rather than a market report.

6-month POC:
Build a pipeline of 20-40 named surplus or deficit ships across 8-15 small or mid-sized operators; sign 5-8 surplus options and 3-5 deficit buyer mandates; collect 150,000-500,000 PLN equivalent in option fees, refundable deposits, transaction fees, or escrowed buyer funds; obtain at least two verifier/compliance-adviser confirmations that the proposed pooling mechanics are procedurally viable if verified balances match forecasts; document forecast-to-verified risk, buyer rejection reasons, ship eligibility issues, company-approval friction, and price ranges; and prepare at least one pool package ready for database validation in the relevant verification period.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start from EU/CEE ship managers, ferry operators, offshore support operators, short-sea operators, compliance advisers, bunker/fuel advisers, MRV/FuelEU consultants, and maritime accountants that already forecast FuelEU balances but do not run a surplus monetization desk. Outreach is specific: "If one of your ships is expected to overcomply under FuelEU and you are not already committed to a pool, I can option the surplus for a named deficit buyer subject to verifier-confirmed process and final database validation." On the buyer side: "If your 2026 FuelEU forecast shows a deficit, reserve a named surplus match with a refundable deposit and verifier-path review rather than waiting until the April 2027 scramble."

Economics:
Revenue comes from option fees, buyer reservation fees, success fees at pool completion, and a negotiated spread between surplus-side minimum price and deficit-side willingness to pay. The buyer pays because FuelEU penalties and uncertainty can be material relative to a 30,000-150,000 PLN reservation fee. The seller accepts because otherwise surplus may be banked, under-monetized, or handled through an incumbent only after the market price is visible. The 100,000 PLN budget covers maritime compliance contractor review, legal templates, target-list building, secure data handling, and travel/calls; the startup does not buy fuel, buy ships, trade allowances, become a verifier, or carry open penalty exposure.

Copy risk:
Maritime verifiers, FuelEU platforms, ship managers, maritime lawyers, bunker advisers, EU ETS/FuelEU consultants, classification societies, and compliance software vendors can copy or intermediate pooling deals. The defense is not generic FuelEU knowledge. It is early control of specific named surplus options, deficit buyer deposits, forecast files, data-owner contacts, draft allocation terms, and verifier-path evidence before larger incumbents see or prioritize those small operators.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder has a surplus-side option over a named ship's expected compliance balance, a deficit buyer mandate and deposit for that same match, and documented verifier-process review, competitors cannot control that exact commercial allocation without displacing the signed parties or waiting for the option to expire. They can source other ships or build future pools, but they cannot retroactively own the current optioned match, buyer deposit, ship data, company contacts, and allocation term sheet.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell a FuelEU memo, dashboard, regulatory newsletter, generic pool marketplace, or maritime compliance advice. It only works on named ship matches where surplus-side option terms, deficit-side buyer deposit, company authorization, forecast data, verifier path, and allocation mechanics are documented. The proof is a controlled commercial option and current buyer cash for a specific FuelEU pooling transaction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as an accredited verifier, classification society, ship operator, fuel supplier, legal adviser, tax adviser, EU ETS broker, financial instrument broker, carbon-credit trader, compliance certifier, or guarantor of verified balances. Accredited verifiers handle verification and database recording. Ship companies validate pool details and remain responsible for compliance. Maritime lawyers and compliance advisers review contracts and regulatory interpretation. Deposits must be refundable or escrowed where verified balances, eligibility, company approval, or database validation fail. Reject sanctions risk, disputed ship control, unclear company authority, fake fuel data, unverifiable forecasts, and any request to misstate compliance balances.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not CBAM Import Continuity Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, BatteryFit, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, white-certificate cashout, DSR site options, grid-connection options, UCO route aggregation, merchant-acquirer residuals, Shopify app buyout, or any evidence desk. The controlled object is a named FuelEU Maritime pool allocation option with buyer deposit and verifier-path documentation.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Maritime compliance is relationship-heavy and already served by verifiers, classification societies, maritime lawyers, ship managers, bunker advisers, FuelEU platforms, and specialist consultants. Small operators may not know their surplus or deficit early enough to sign options. Deposits before verified balances may be hard to secure. Forecasts may change with fuel mix, routes, port calls, cargo, charterer behavior, RFNBO treatment, or data corrections. Verifiers and companies may resist an unknown intermediary in the pool process. The first verification window may have already taught the market to use incumbents. The startup may look like a broker without enough maritime trust. Option control is transaction-specific and expires. A failed pool could create refund disputes, reputational damage, or legal complexity.
