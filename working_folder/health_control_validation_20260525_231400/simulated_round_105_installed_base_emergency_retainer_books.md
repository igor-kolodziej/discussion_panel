## Simulated Pass Candidate

### Legacy Line-Control First-Call Spares Retainer - 90

One-sentence thesis: buy or option a retiring automation technician's named installed-base emergency account book, convert factories and integrators into paid first-call spare retainers, and acquire only exact tested PLC/HMI/drive modules tied to those named lines.

Buyer: maintenance managers at Polish manufacturing SMEs with legacy Siemens, Omron, Mitsubishi, Schneider, ABB, Lenze, Beckhoff, or similar production-line controls; also local automation integrators that already support such plants and need reliable emergency stock.

Acute trigger: an obsolete or long-lead control module can stop a production line for days, while retrofit is slower than a like-for-like module swap performed by the buyer's own technician or integrator.

Transferable control point: the acquired seller-endorsed first-call phone/mailbox, installed-base/customer support book, line-level BOM and failure notes, customer-approved payment direction, paid emergency-spare retainers/reservations, first-look rights to decommissioned control-panel lots, and titled tested modules selected from those named installed systems.

Exactly what is signed/titled/prepaid inside 60 days:
- Seller option/handoff over one named installed-base book from a retiring or overloaded automation technician/integrator.
- Customer reauthorization letters for at least 8 named factories/integrators.
- Paid retainers/reservations from at least 5 customers for exact module families or emergency-stock access.
- Titled tested inventory under 60,000 PLN, selected only from the reauthorized installed-base BOMs.
- Serial/photo/test records and return terms for each module.
- One paid emergency sale, rental, or reserve-release transaction.

First acquisition mechanism: source retiring/overloaded automation technicians, small integrators, machine rebuilders, control-panel decommissioners, and plant electricians. Pay an option/earnout only after customer reauthorizations and prepaid reservations prove that the book is real current demand, not a lead list.

Gross margin and payback logic: paid first-call retainers/reservations reduce CAC and finance inventory before purchase. Emergency sales/rentals of exact tested obsolete modules can carry high gross margin because downtime, not module cost, drives willingness to pay. The first proof should target 20,000-40,000 PLN in paid reservations plus one emergency transaction before inventory exposure exceeds 60,000 PLN.

Copy risk: Radwell, EU Automation, PLC Center, repair shops, local integrators, OEMs, and Allegro/eBay sellers can all supply parts. The defense is the signed installed-base book, customer payment authorization, seller endorsement, named line BOMs, and exact titled modules tied to those customers before a failure occurs.

Why incumbents cannot copy before control is secured: after the founder controls the seller-endorsed phone/mailbox, customer-approved first-call terms, paid reservations, and exact modules mapped to named lines, a competitor cannot recreate that relationship or reserve those same modules for those same sites. They can only offer substitute parts after the buyer starts searching.

Why this is not a service/report/app/dashboard/marketplace/broker: the business owns or controls the payment stream and physical emergency stock. It does not sell advice, diagnostics, or a parts directory. Qualified automation technicians or the customer's own integrator handle installation, programming, safety review, and engineering.

Reasons this could still fail:
- Incumbents are strong and buyers may search known suppliers during emergencies.
- Compatibility and firmware/revision mismatch can kill transactions.
- Testing credibility may require trusted contractors and documented fixtures.
- Demand is lumpy unless retainers convert before failures occur.
- Seller books may contain stale contacts, informal trust, or undocumented systems.
- If customer prepayment is weak, it collapses back into speculative inventory.
