2. Program ownership and confidentiality may be unclear across supplier customers, end customers/OEMs, the metrology shop, the programmer, prior employers, and software/vendor ecosystems.
3. Hidden support may be bespoke metrology consulting rather than continuity: drawing interpretation, fixture debugging, datum strategy, quality disputes, audit support, and operator training.
4. Incorrect program changes can cause false accepts, false rejects, scrap, shipment delays, customer complaints, PPAP/FAI failure, payment holds, and warranty disputes.
5. Customer acceptance remains outside founder control because calibration, fixtures, probe qualification, operators, drawing revisions, datum disputes, and customer templates can break the workflow.
6. A part-time founder is weak for shipment, audit, PPAP, or drawing-revision tickets that need fast response.
7. Customers may switch to established metrology labs, CMM resellers, OEM service teams, in-house quality engineers, or larger programming firms during the handoff.
8. Programs may depend on specific software, machine controller, probe rack, stylus configuration, alignment routine, fixture, report export macro, and customer template.
9. Six prepaid customers prove transferability but not durable owner earnings; a three-customer book is only freelance job continuity.
10. Scope can expand into inspection execution, calibration, gauge R&R, MSA, PPAP consulting, audit defense, and method disputes.
