## Finalist Review

### FlexMW Site Option Rail

Why it advances:

- The founder controls a named site option and meter-data authorization, not a lead list.
- The payor is an aggregator that wants accepted kW/MW; the startup earns only after the site is signed and accepted.
- The 60-day proof is binary: site exclusivity, meter data, aggregator acceptance, and bounty/deposit cash. Interest does not count.
- It avoids becoming an energy trader or DSR operator; licensed/registered aggregators keep market operations, dispatch, settlement, balancing, performance, and compliance.
- It is different from grid-connection and white-certificate failures: the object is an operating facility's controllable flexibility account, not a slow permit/certificate or speculative grid site.

Strict objections:

- Existing aggregators have sales teams and may not pay meaningful bounties to an unknown founder.
- Site qualification is technical and trust-heavy: load profiles, operations, metering, baselines, interruption tolerance, and penalties matter.
- Big obvious sites are already enrolled; the wedge must be neglected mid-size sites.
- It can collapse into brokered introductions if site exclusivity and meter-data rights are weak.
- Part-time founder fit is weak unless scope is limited to sourcing and prequalification.

## Internal Simulated Score

**88.0 / 100**

This clears the simulation gate only because the proof is a signed, site-specific MW option plus aggregator-accepted cash movement. It should be killed if the real validator treats it as ordinary energy lead generation, if aggregators refuse deposits/bounties, or if site acquisition requires the founder to perform regulated aggregation/energy-advisory work.
