## Search Frame

Recent recovery and service-ticket rounds failed because the control point was either too copyable, too narrow, or too perishable. This round tests a harder asset class: source archives where the founder can control custody, indexing rights, release permissions, and retrieval workflow before valuable documents are discarded, sold, or split.

The top candidate is a retiring architect/engineer as-built archive. The valuable unit is not a report or a database; it is legal custody and documented retrieval rights over old project drawings and technical folders tied to named addresses. Buyers pay when renovations, tenant fit-outs, insurance/fire checks, refinancing, sales, legalization, or engineering work are blocked because the owner lacks original plans or as-built documents.

## Simulated Pass Candidate

### AsBuilt Archive Retrieval Rights - 91

One-sentence thesis: acquire or option retiring architect, engineer, MEP, and small design-office project archives, index them by address and project, and sell owner-authorized retrieval packages of original drawings, as-built plans, calculations, permits, and technical folders when buildings need missing documentation for renovations, tenant fit-outs, insurance, sale, refinancing, legalization, or engineering work.

Buyer: property owners, facility managers, boutique developers, building administrators, architects, MEP engineers, fire-safety consultants, insurers' survey follow-up teams, and renovation firms responsible for commercial, mixed-use, multifamily, and premium residential buildings where original project documents are missing or incomplete. Best first buyers have a named address and a current documentation blocker.

Acute trigger: a renovation, tenant fit-out, change of use, building sale, refinancing, fire/insurance review, energy retrofit, legalization, warranty claim, or engineering investigation is delayed because the owner or project team lacks original floor plans, structural/MEP drawings, as-built updates, calculations, permit folders, material specs, or commissioning records.

Transferable control point: written archive acquisition, custody, consignment, or exclusive retrieval agreement from a retiring architect/engineer/design office; box/file inventory; address index; copyright/release/scan terms where available; data-handling rules; owner-authorization workflow; and paid retrieval deposits for named addresses. The startup controls the source archive before it is discarded, fragmented, or forgotten.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:

- 2 written archive options or custody agreements with retiring design offices, engineers, MEP designers, or estate/clear-out sellers.
- At least 200 project folders or drawing sets indexed by address, year, designer, discipline, and document type.
- Release rules documented for every archive: who may receive copies, what owner authorization is needed, what copyright limits apply, and what cannot be released.
- 5 paid retrieval deposits from owners, architects, facility managers, or renovation firms for named addresses.
- At least 2 delivered retrieval packages with source-document scans and provenance notes.
- First 15,000-50,000 PLN collected in retrieval, scan, rush, or archive-search fees.

First acquisition mechanism: call retiring architects, structural engineers, MEP designers, small design offices, building administrators, insolvency/estate clear-out firms, document storage vendors, and specialist renovators. The supply offer is: "do not throw away or split old project archives; I pay, remove, index, and only release documents under written rules to documented owners or their authorized project teams." Demand outreach goes to architects, facility managers, boutique developers, insurers' survey follow-up contacts, and property administrators with a named-address search offer.

Gross margin and payback logic: charge 500-1,500 PLN for address search/no-hit or light retrieval, 2,000-6,000 PLN for a standard drawing/folder retrieval, 6,000-15,000 PLN for rush multi-discipline retrieval, scanning, and provenance pack, and custom pricing for large commercial folders. Archive acquisition and scanning costs are low relative to the delay cost of re-surveying, opening walls, commissioning fresh measurements, or delaying a tenant fit-out or sale. The same archive can produce many paid retrievals over years.

Copy risk: municipal archives, building owners, original architects, scanning firms, facility managers, and design offices can solve some cases. The defense is source custody over specific private archives, address indexing, release discipline, and paid retrieval memory. Competitors can build their own archives, but they cannot retrieve documents from an archive the startup already controls.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: once the startup has physical/digital custody, address index, release terms, owner-authorization workflow, and paid retrieval request for a named building, competitors cannot access those specific source documents unless the archive owner or requester switches. The compounding asset is controlled archive depth by city, designer, building type, discipline, address, and retrieval outcome.

Why this is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: the product is owner-authorized retrieval of controlled source documents from specific archives. The index is internal; the output is scans/copies/provenance notes from original project folders, not a research report, marketplace listing, dashboard, or generic building-doc database. No archive custody or release right means no sale.

Reasons this could still fail:

- Archive ownership, copyright, confidentiality, and release rights can be messy.
- Municipal or owner-held records may already solve many cases.
- Retiring architects may not want to transfer archives or may have contractual duties to clients.
- Indexing old boxes can be labor-intensive.
- Demand is hard to trigger because owners may not know which designer held the archive.
- Some documents may be outdated, incomplete, or not valid as as-built evidence.
- Professional liability boundaries must be strict; the startup cannot certify engineering correctness.
- Paid retrieval frequency may be too low unless enough archives are controlled.

Decision: advance this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
