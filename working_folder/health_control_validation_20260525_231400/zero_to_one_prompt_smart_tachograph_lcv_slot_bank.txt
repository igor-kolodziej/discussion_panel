Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: Smart Tachograph G2V2 LCV Slot Bank

One-sentence thesis:
A buyer-deposit-backed slot bank that secures authorized smart tachograph version 2 installation capacity and device availability for Polish and CEE light commercial fleets with 2.5-3.5 tonne vans that must be equipped before the 1 July 2026 cross-border transport deadline.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE courier subcontractors, express freight operators, small international transport companies, trades/logistics firms, marketplace-delivery operators, and van fleets running light commercial vehicles above 2.5 tonnes and up to 3.5 tonnes in international transport or cabotage for hire or reward. Best first buyers have 5-80 vans, cross-border revenue in July/August 2026, and no internal transport-compliance team.

Acute trigger:
From 1 July 2026, in-scope light commercial vehicles used in international goods transport or cabotage must be equipped with a smart tachograph version 2 and drivers must use driver cards/records. A missed installation can risk cancelled cross-border jobs, roadside fines, vehicle downtime, subcontractor delisting, or route handoff to compliant competitors. The date is close enough that authorized workshop slots and device availability are the scarce current asset.

Control point:
Dated authorized workshop installation slots, confirmed device/fitment availability, VIN/vehicle register, fleet deposit, installation quotation, workshop acceptance, customer payment direction, and post-install certificate/calibration handoff for named vehicles. The startup controls a slot calendar tied to VINs and deposits, not a generic lead list.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three to five authorized tachograph workshop slot-block agreements covering at least 60 named van installation slots before and immediately after 1 July 2026.
2. Written confirmation of G2V2 device and fitment availability for the accepted vehicle models.
3. Thirty to fifty fleet-paid deposits tied to named VINs, dates, and workshops.
4. At least ten completed installs or workshop-issued installation/calibration certificates.
5. At least 40,000-100,000 PLN collected in slot/admin margin, deposits, or fleet coordination fees.
6. Signed customer terms stating that authorized workshops remain installer, calibrator, and provider of record, while fleets remain responsible for legal scope, driver cards, driving-time rules, and operational compliance.

6-month POC:
Month 1: sign workshop slot-block agreements, confirm device/fitment availability, build a VIN intake form, and contact 150-250 fleet operators, courier subcontractor groups, van leasing brokers, transport accountants, and local transport associations.
Month 2: close 30-50 paid VIN-tied deposits, fill workshop calendars, and complete the first 10 installs or certificates.
Month 3: complete 60+ installs or documented workshop handoffs, collect fleet coordination fees, record every failed case by reason: no in-scope vehicle, already booked, no driver cards, workshop cannot fit model, no budget, or legal/admin uncertainty.
Months 4-6: convert the same VIN/customer base into a calibration/download/calendar book only if customers prepay. Prove at least 100 total VINs scheduled or installed, 80,000-180,000 PLN gross margin, fewer than 15% date failures after deposit, one workshop repeats the block model, and founder handling falls below 20 minutes per vehicle after intake.

First acquisition mechanism:
Call authorized tachograph workshops and ask for dated LCV G2V2 capacity they can sell in blocks if the founder brings named VINs and prepaid fleets. In parallel, contact courier subcontractor groups, fleet accountants, transport associations, van leasing brokers, cross-border marketplace-delivery operators, and small international freight firms with a concrete offer: "send your VIN list and deposit by date X; receive a named workshop slot and device-fitment confirmation before the July deadline."

Economics:
The workshop charges the fleet for installation, calibration, and device work or sells a wholesale slot/device bundle. The startup earns a 350-900 PLN per-vehicle coordination margin, 5-10% device/slot spread, or 2,500-8,000 PLN fleet scheduling fee for multi-vehicle batches. A 50-van first block can produce 25,000-60,000 PLN gross margin quickly with low inventory risk if deposits precede workshop commitment. The buyer pays because one delayed van or lost cross-border route can cost more than the slot margin.

Copy risk:
Authorized workshops, telematics providers, fleet compliance firms, transport accountants, and tachograph equipment distributors can copy the general offer. The defense is deadline speed, named slot/device/VIN control, payment-before-slot discipline, workshop capacity already blocked, and direct access to fragmented courier/LCV fleets that are not being proactively scheduled.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup controls a dated workshop slot block, matching G2V2 device availability, named VIN list, fleet deposits, and payment direction, competitors cannot use that specific capacity unless the workshop or fleet breaks the booking. The asset is perishable and date-specific, but that is the point: before 1 July 2026, the scarce unit is not advice, it is a named install slot.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell compliance advice, a checklist, telematics software, a marketplace of workshops, or a dashboard. It sells controlled installation capacity for named vehicles: dated slot, device-fitment confirmation, deposit, workshop handoff, and installation/certificate status. The proof is completed installs and certificates for VINs before the deadline.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not install, calibrate, seal, certify, inspect, or provide legal transport-compliance advice. Authorized tachograph workshops remain installer, calibrator, and provider of record. Fleets remain responsible for determining whether vehicles and operations are in scope, arranging driver cards, obeying driving-time and rest rules, retaining records, and passing roadside or authority checks. The startup only coordinates prepaid installation capacity for named VINs and records handoff/completion status.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet. It is also not a generic route book or evidence desk. The material distinction is a short-deadline transport hardware retrofit slot bank with named VINs, authorized workshop capacity, device availability, fleet deposits, and installation certificates.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Authorized workshops may not reserve capacity for an unknown founder.
Workshops and telematics distributors can sell directly to fleets.
Many serious fleets may already have slots booked before the founder starts.
The deadline spike is short-lived; recurring revenue after July must come from calibration/download/calendar books or future transport-compliance slot events.
Fitment can be messy by van model, device availability, wiring, workshop authorization, and calibration requirements.
Driver-card and driving-time compliance can create legal/admin scope creep.
Part-time founder must avoid day-of operational chaos and only sell prepaid named slots.
Margin per van may be too thin if the founder cannot secure block pricing or fleet scheduling fees.
