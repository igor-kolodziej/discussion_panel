Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
FactorableInvoice Acceptance Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A fixed-fee payment-release desk for CEE suppliers whose invoices are approved commercially but cannot be funded by a factor, invoice-finance provider, or supply-chain-finance platform until debtor acceptance, portal status, goods receipt, service entry, EDI acceptance, assignment notice, or no-dispute verification is documented.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE manufacturers, industrial distributors, equipment sellers, component suppliers, SaaS vendors, staffing firms, logistics providers, professional-services firms, and exporters with named invoices or invoice schedules rejected, delayed, discounted, or excluded by a factor, invoice-discounting provider, ABL lender, or supply-chain-finance platform because debtor acceptance evidence is missing. The economic buyer is the owner, CFO, controller, AR lead, working-capital lead, or factoring broker responsible for turning approved invoices into funded cash.

Acute trigger:
The supplier has a written factor, lender, broker, or platform deficiency: invoice not eligible until the debtor confirms no dispute; debtor portal shows accepted invoice; PO-flip clears; goods receipt or service entry exists; EDI invoice is accepted; assignment notice is acknowledged; buyer AP confirms payment schedule; debtor name/legal entity matches; or a large customer portal export proves the invoice is payable. Without that evidence, the invoice cannot be funded, is advanced at a lower rate, is excluded from the borrowing base, or remains trapped until normal customer payment.

Control point:
Signed limited mandate over a named debtor and invoice schedule; factor/lender/platform deficiency text; invoice list; approved PO or contract reference; debtor portal/AP status where authorized; goods receipt or service-entry evidence where relevant; EDI acceptance logs where relevant; assignment-notice wording supplied by the factor where relevant; debtor no-dispute or payable acknowledgement; customer contact map; factor submission trail; accepted invoice schedule or funding decision; and no-go rules for real debtor disputes, credit underwriting, concentration limits, legal assignment disputes, tax advice, insolvency, fraud review, sanctions, customer refusal to acknowledge a payable, or cases requiring collection pressure.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign five prepaid cases referred by suppliers, factoring brokers, factors, or invoice-finance platforms; put at least 1,500,000 PLN of named invoice value under mandate; obtain three factor-accepted invoice schedules, debtor acknowledgements, funded invoices, remittance confirmations, eligibility releases, or clean no-go decisions; move at least 1,000,000 PLN of invoice value to funded, eligible, scheduled, or clean no-go status; collect 60,000-120,000 PLN in fixed fees and success-tied fees; and keep a rejection log above 50% for debtor disputes, concentration caps, credit risk, underwriting decisions, legal assignment issues, tax problems, debtor insolvency, no acknowledged payable, and generic AR chasing.

6-month POC:
Handle 30-70 invoice-funding release cases across 15-40 suppliers and 3-8 factor/broker channels; put 10,000,000-40,000,000 PLN of invoice value under mandate; achieve 20+ funded invoice, accepted-schedule, debtor-acknowledged, eligibility-release, remittance-confirmed, or clean no-go outcomes; collect 350,000-1,200,000 PLN in fees; build a private taxonomy by factor, broker, debtor, portal, blocker type, goods-receipt path, service-entry path, EDI failure, assignment notice, no-dispute wording, debtor response time, and funding outcome; and maintain gross margin above 60% after AR-ops contractor, portal/EDI specialist, document QA, and secure workflow costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to factoring brokers, invoice-finance originators, ABL/factoring salespeople, fractional CFOs, AR outsourcing firms, and suppliers that use invoice finance. The message to channels: "If a supplier is approved but named invoices are excluded because debtor acceptance, portal proof, goods receipt, service entry, EDI acceptance, assignment notice, or no-dispute confirmation is missing, I clear the evidence path and return a funded, eligible, scheduled, or clean no-go outcome." The message to suppliers: "Send the factor deficiency and invoice schedule. I do not chase disputed debt; I clear the debtor acceptance evidence that lets the factor fund."

Economics:
Charge 6,000-15,000 PLN fixed fee for a simple debtor acceptance or portal-proof release, 15,000-35,000 PLN for multi-invoice or multi-portal schedules, and 1-4% success-tied fee on invoice value newly funded or made eligible where permitted and clearly outside collections or finance-broker activity. Fees are justified only when the invoice schedule is materially larger than the fee and the factor deficiency is specific. Channels may receive a referral fee only where legal and disclosed.

Copy risk:
Factors, invoice-finance platforms, brokers, supplier finance teams, AR outsourcers, EDI consultants, ERP consultants, and customer AP teams can solve parts of this workflow. The defense is narrow factor-deficiency intake, broker/channel routing, CEE supplier focus, repeated debtor/portal/factor evidence memory, fast evidence-room assembly, and active control over the named invoice schedule, factor submission trail, debtor response, and funding proof once the mandate is signed.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the supplier signs the mandate, prepays, grants authorized portal/AP visibility, provides the factor deficiency, invoice schedule, PO or contract reference, debtor evidence path, EDI or goods-receipt logs where relevant, and factor submission trail, the startup controls the active invoice-eligibility file for that named debtor and factor. A broker, factor, or consultant can compete for future cases, but cannot control the same debtor acceptance chronology, evidence room, and funding proof without the supplier switching mid-release and restarting the factor review.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell generic AR outsourcing, collections, invoice financing, factoring brokerage, a dashboard, an EDI product, a lender marketplace, or debtor introductions. It accepts only named invoice schedules with a written funding deficiency and returns operational artifacts: debtor acknowledgement, accepted portal export, goods-receipt/service-entry proof, EDI acceptance, acknowledged assignment notice, accepted factor schedule, funded invoice, remittance confirmation, eligibility release, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not act as a factor, lender, broker of credit, collection agency, lawyer, tax adviser, accountant of record, payment institution, debtor representative, or custodian of funds. Do not pressure disputed debtors, negotiate payment terms, provide legal assignment advice, certify delivery, change bank details, provide tax opinions, chase insolvent debtors, or handle cases involving fraud, sanctions, fake invoices, fake delivery evidence, customer refusal to acknowledge a payable, buyer insolvency, or underwriting decisions. Suppliers remain responsible for invoice truth, delivery facts, customer relationship, assignment authorization, and finance-provider relationship. Factors/lenders/platforms remain responsible for credit, eligibility, funding, and underwriting decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not BorrowingBase Certificate Book Buyout, BorrowingBase Draw-Release Desk, factor reserve release, unapplied cash, SupplierPortal Invoice Release, credit memo cleanup, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, retailer OTIF chargebacks, freight audit, SAM/NCAGE, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, or a generic evidence desk. The controlled object is a named invoice schedule with a factor/invoice-finance funding deficiency, and the proof is factor eligibility, funded invoice, debtor acknowledgement, remittance confirmation, or clean no-go.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Factors and factoring brokers may already verify debtor acceptance themselves. Some deficiencies are actually credit underwriting, concentration limits, debtor disputes, legal assignment issues, tax questions, fraud reviews, or buyer insolvency outside founder control. Debtor AP teams may ignore third parties. Success-tied fees can create collection-agency or credit-broker boundary risk if not scoped tightly. Many suppliers may be too small or too disorganized to pay. A factor may prefer to reject the invoice rather than let a third party repair evidence. The first cases may look like AR outsourcing unless the desk accepts only written funding deficiencies tied to material named invoice schedules.
