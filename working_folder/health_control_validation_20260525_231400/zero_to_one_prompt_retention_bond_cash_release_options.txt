Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RetentionBond Cash Release Options

One-sentence thesis:
A CEE construction cash-release desk for specialist subcontractors whose completed-work cash retention can contractually be replaced by an accepted bank or insurance retention guarantee, with the startup controlling signed clean release mandates and guarantee-option packets until retained cash is actually paid out.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE specialist subcontractors and mid-sized contractors with 10-80 million PLN annual revenue in fit-out, MEP, facade, roofing, roadworks, industrial installation, and specialist construction trades that have clean accepted works, cash retentions or warranty retentions above 100,000 PLN, and contracts that allow retained cash to be replaced by a bank guarantee, insurance guarantee, retention bond, maintenance bond, or other beneficiary-accepted security.

Acute trigger:
The contractor has completed and accepted work, has invoices partly paid with 5-10% retained cash, needs working capital for payroll, materials, bond capacity, or the next project, and the contract contains a clause or beneficiary practice allowing release of the retained amount if an acceptable bank or insurance guarantee is provided. The target case is not a disputed debt; it is clean cash currently withheld only as security.

Control point:
Signed contractor mandate over a specific retention release file; contract clauses; retention ledger; invoices and payment history; acceptance/handover protocol; defect-free or no-current-claim confirmation where available; beneficiary guarantee wording or template; guarantee-provider quote/term sheet from an authorized bank, insurer, surety broker, or guarantee broker; beneficiary acceptance request; release instruction; and release-status log. The startup controls the release workflow and option packet, not the legal opinion or guarantee issuance.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign three paid mandates over clean retention files with at least 600,000 PLN aggregate retained cash, obtain two written guarantee quotes or term sheets from authorized bank/insurance/broker channels, submit two beneficiary acceptance requests, obtain one accepted replacement guarantee or conditional acceptance, and complete one released-cash event where the contractor receives retained money and pays the startup from a fixed release fee or success fee. Reject cases with defects, penalties, setoff, insolvency, anti-assignment or anti-substitution uncertainty, beneficiary refusal, or unclear guarantee wording.

6-month POC:
Handle 25-50 clean retention substitution files across 10-25 contractors, control 8-20 million PLN of retained cash under mandate, complete 8-15 released-cash events, generate 500,000-1,500,000 PLN in fixed fees, urgency fees, and success fees, secure 4+ repeat routes from construction accountants, CFO advisers, guarantee brokers, surety brokers, lawyers, quantity surveyors, or subcontractor associations, and build a private library of accepted beneficiary wording, guarantee templates, contract clauses, no-go patterns, release timing, and contractor eligibility requirements.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target specialist subcontractors and contractor CFOs with language that avoids debt collection: "If your completed-work retention is clean and your contract allows a bank or insurance guarantee instead of cash retention, I package the release file, obtain guarantee options through authorized providers, and push for beneficiary acceptance. No disputes, no claims, no legal advice, no founder escrow." Initial channels are construction accountants, CFO advisers, guarantee brokers, surety brokers, quantity surveyors, construction lawyers, subcontractor associations, and direct outreach to fit-out/MEP/facade/roofing contractors.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-12,000 PLN fixed screening and guarantee-option fee per clean file, plus 3-8% success fee on cash actually released, with minimum total fee of 15,000 PLN for releases above 150,000 PLN. For portfolio cases, charge 20,000-50,000 PLN upfront plus lower success fee. Buyer payback is immediate because a 200,000-1,000,000 PLN retained-cash release can fund payroll, materials, bonding capacity, and the next project. Costs are broker/provider coordination, legal issue spotting on red flags, document review, and founder operations; no founder capital is used to buy claims or provide guarantees.

Copy risk:
Guarantee brokers, insurance brokers, banks, surety providers, construction lawyers, accountants, CFO advisers, and main contractors can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is narrow clean-case qualification, signed contractor mandate, beneficiary-specific guarantee wording memory, fast guarantee-option routing, release timing data, and repeat contractor/CFO/accountant channels. Incumbents often sell guarantees when asked; they do not always proactively mine subcontractors' existing retention ledgers for cash that can be released by substitution.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the contractor signs a mandate over a specific retention file, pays the screening fee, sends the contract, ledger, invoices, protocols, and beneficiary details, and the startup obtains dated guarantee quotes or terms for that file, the immediate cash-release workflow is controlled. A broker or lawyer can compete later, but cannot instantly take the already-packaged file, provider quotes, beneficiary wording history, and release request without the contractor switching mid-case.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It accepts only clean retained-cash files where the contract or beneficiary practice allows substitution. The output is an operational cash-release package: contract clause extraction, retention ledger, no-dispute/no-current-claim screen, guarantee-provider quote options, beneficiary wording request, accepted guarantee path, release instruction tracker, and clean no-go where substitution is not available. The buyer pays for released retained cash, not a dashboard, legal memo, marketplace, debt-collection service, or broker introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No legal advice, no insurance brokerage without licensed partner, no bank or insurance guarantee issuance, no debt collection, no claim purchase, no escrow, no payment services, no litigation, no construction defect assessment, no public-procurement legal opinion, no promise that a beneficiary must accept a guarantee, and no work on disputed, insolvent, defect, setoff, penalty, or unclear-title cases. Authorized banks, insurers, surety brokers, guarantee brokers, contractor counsel, beneficiary counsel, and accountants remain responsible for guarantee issuance, legal interpretation, tax/accounting treatment, and final acceptance.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not TraceFaktura because it is not municipal animal-care event invoicing. It is not PromoLeak because it is not retailer deduction recovery. It is not CBAM, Data Act, DORA, REDBlocked, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeritageDoor, BioSignal, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, RAGProbe, CyberBind, or a generic recovery mandate. It differs from the failed Warranty Retention Release Lockbox because it does not chase disputed or matured retentions and does not use founder escrow; it only converts clean cash security into an accepted third-party guarantee where the contract allows substitution.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Guarantee brokers and construction lawyers may already handle this. Many contracts may not allow substitution or may require beneficiary discretion. Beneficiaries may refuse replacement guarantees, demand their own wording, or delay acceptance. Guarantee providers may reject weaker subcontractors or require collateral, reducing cash-release value. Clean files may be too easy for contractors to handle themselves, while valuable files may contain defects, setoff, penalties, disputed handover, insolvency risk, or legal ambiguity. A Warsaw solo founder must avoid unlicensed insurance brokerage and legal advice. Success fees may be negotiated down if the guarantee broker already receives commission. Case volume may be lumpy and working-capital stress may attract bad-fit contractors.
