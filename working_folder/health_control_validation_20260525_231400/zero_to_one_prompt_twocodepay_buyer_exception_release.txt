Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
TwoCodePay Buyer-Issued Exception Release Queue

One-sentence thesis:
Help SME suppliers release already-accepted payable invoices that are stuck behind buyer-issued AP or portal exception codes by taking only two narrow exception families, controlling the signed supplier mandate and portal/ticket trail, and getting paid from cleared status, scheduled remittance, or recovered cash.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE SME suppliers selling services, maintenance, installation, logistics, packaging, ingredients, or components to large enterprise buyers where 150,000+ PLN of accepted-payable invoices are delayed by explicit buyer AP or supplier-portal exception messages.

Acute trigger:
The supplier has delivered the work or goods and the buyer has not rejected the commercial obligation, but payment is blocked by a named exception in the buyer's AP workflow. Accepted cases are limited to:
1. missing service entry, goods receipt, work confirmation, delivery acceptance, or PO-line acceptance after accepted work or delivery; or
2. invoice, EDI, or portal-state correction where the buyer has already indicated the invoice is processable/payable after the specific correction.

Control point:
A signed supplier mandate over a specific invoice queue, the buyer-issued exception text/code, before-state screenshots or exports, supplier-approved portal/ticket access through a named contact, authority to submit the correction packet or coordinate the named buyer action, and a fee right tied to cleared exception status, scheduled remittance, or paid cash.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Four to five paid supplier mandates covering 1m-2m PLN of accepted-payable invoice value, each with buyer-issued exception evidence before intake. Proof should include at least three buyer acknowledgements, cleared exception statuses, scheduled remittance notices, or explicit no-go reasons, plus one to two paid releases/remittances and 40k-100k PLN of setup fees, success fees, or fee invoices tied to released cash.

6-month POC:
Build a repeatable two-code playbook around 20-30 accepted-payable invoice queues, maintain before/after evidence for every case, show median time-to-clear for each exception family, collect 250k-500k PLN in fees or fee invoices, and prove that rejects are disciplined rather than becoming generic AR chasing.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to supplier controllers, owner-managed B2B suppliers, accountants, and fractional CFOs using the specific wedge: "send the buyer's exception text for invoices already accepted but unpaid." Initial intake asks for the exception message, invoice value, buyer name, accepted-work or delivery proof, and whether the issue falls into the two accepted families.

Economics:
Minimum invoice queue of 150k-250k PLN. Charge a 3k-8k PLN triage/setup fee per queue plus 3-7% of released invoice value, with caps for large queues. Gross margin should be high because the work is structured evidence capture, portal/ticket submission, and buyer-process follow-up inside two known workflows rather than open-ended consulting. Payback is fast when one released 300k-500k PLN invoice queue generates a 15k-35k PLN fee.

Copy risk:
AR consultants, accountants, AP outsourcing firms, and supplier-portal consultants can copy the general activity. The defense is not software; it is early control of specific signed mandates, exception queues, before-state evidence, and a narrow reputation for resolving exactly these two buyer-issued payment-block states.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Before intake, incumbents do not know which suppliers have these exact buyer-issued exception queues. Once the founder has the signed mandate, exception evidence, portal/ticket trail, named buyer contacts, and fee right for the live invoices, a later entrant cannot take that specific payment stream without replacing the supplier's active mandate and work history. The control is case-level, not market-level.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell analysis, dashboards, introductions, or generic AR support. It accepts only live buyer-issued exception queues with accepted-payable invoices, signs a mandate over those invoices, submits or coordinates the correction path, and gets paid when the exception clears, payment is scheduled, cash arrives, or the buyer gives a documented no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No debt collection, litigation threats, legal advice, tax advice, sanctions or UBO remediation, bank-change/vendor-master changes, fraud/compliance cases, price disputes, quality disputes, PO exhaustion, or cases where the buyer has not issued a specific exception. The supplier remains seller of record and account owner; the founder acts only under supplier authorization for process correction and release tracking.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Different from PromoLeak because it is not retailer promotion leakage or broad deduction recovery; it is buyer-issued AP exception release for accepted-payable invoices. Different from TraceFaktura because it is not municipal invoice routing. Different from DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, and GreenTender because it is not an evidence compliance pack; the proof artifact is a signed payment-release mandate and live buyer exception queue.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The buyer may still be slow even after the exception is fixed. Suppliers may view this as ordinary finance follow-up they should do internally. The two-code scope may be too narrow for repeatable acquisition. Some cases may quietly involve disputes, cash delay, or vendor-master problems despite supplier claims. Accountants, AR consultants, or AP outsourcing firms could offer the same work once the pattern is visible.
