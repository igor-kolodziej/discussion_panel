## Strongest Objections

1. Candidate discrepancies may mostly collapse into explainable timing, returns, failed delivery, split transfers, netted COD fees, wrong COD fields, broker settlement rules, or seller bookkeeping errors.
2. COD is structurally declining in Poland, making the market a shrinking messy niche rather than the center of ecommerce growth.
3. Courier and broker cooperation is the bottleneck; third-party workflows may be rejected or slow.
4. Success-fee economics can become unpaid support-ticket labor if cases are small and slow.
5. Defensibility is weak after proof because accountants, 3PLs, brokers, Baselinker operators, and ecommerce agencies can copy the workflow.
6. The signed mandate creates temporary control over one seller's queue, not a durable moat.
7. The business may be mostly one-time cleanup rather than repeat monthly recovery.
8. Part-time execution is constrained because data cleanup, evidence packets, client chasing, courier tickets, and follow-up loops are labor-heavy.

## Evaluator's Killer Test

Run a manual mandate-based sprint with three sellers and three months of COD data. Measure:

- candidate discrepancy;
- valid claim;
- courier acknowledged;
- cash or credit recovered;
- fee collected;
- repeat month.

Continue only if at least 20-30% of candidate PLN converts into recovered or credited cash and the average case resolves with low labor.

## Lesson

Current cash recovery still fails if the valid discrepancy pool is uncertain and the debtor channel is slow. Future recovery mandates need faster acknowledgement, larger individual claim values, and less dependence on repetitive support tickets.
