## Strongest Objections

1. The product itself is highly commoditized; the only defensible asset is the specific customer/order archive.
2. The right seller may be hard to find: repeat B2B customers, current POs, organized artwork, clear specs, reusable serial rules, decent margins, and seller transition.
3. Small orders can consume margin through quoting, artwork lookup, serial confirmation, production coordination, packing, courier, and invoice handling.
4. Wrong serial numbers, CE data, machine ratings, valve tags, materials, hole positions, adhesive, color, logo, or finish can cause rework and trust damage.
5. Regulatory-label boundaries matter; the customer/manufacturer remains responsible for CE/product compliance.
6. Outsourced production may reduce margin and control through lead-time issues, batch charges, quality variation, rush fees, confidentiality concerns, and rework delays.
7. The seller handoff can trigger customer switching to another engraver, OEM supplier, online plate shop, or internal marking process.
8. Reorder archive value may be overstated; last-12-month and current-PO proof matter more than lifetime customer count.
9. The business can become a generic job shop if it accepts one-off signage, trophy work, decorative engraving, or open-ended design.
10. Scaling by acquisitions is messy because every seller book has different files, formats, naming conventions, pricing, materials, customers, and production methods.
