Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RAGModule AppSec Overflow Bench

One-sentence thesis:
A white-label RAG/agent AI-security module for appsec boutiques, SOC2 consultants, and fractional-CISO firms that need buyer-safe test traces and report annexes for client RAG or agent features, without staffing a full AI security bench.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized application-security boutiques, penetration-testing firms, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, virtual security teams, and security-questionnaire firms serving 20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors with non-regulated RAG, AI assistant, knowledge-base, document-processing, support automation, workflow automation, or tool-using agent features. The partner already owns the client relationship and broader security engagement, but needs a bounded AI module for prompt injection, cross-source leakage, tool permissions, logging, human review, and buyer evidence.

Acute trigger:
The partner's SaaS client has an enterprise buyer, renewal, procurement, or security-review blocker asking for evidence around RAG/agent AI security, or the partner's existing security review now needs an AI annex. The partner can do normal appsec, SOC2, privacy, or questionnaire work, but does not have repeatable RAG/agent test recipes, buyer-safe trace formatting, no-go wording, or AI-specific evidence annexes.

Control point:
Signed 90-day white-label module agreement with the appsec/SOC2/fractional-CISO partner covering current AI-module tickets, report-annex integration, non-circumvention, evidence-handling rules, partner workspace delivery, payment/revenue-share terms, scope limits, and escalation/no-go boundaries. For each ticket, the startup controls the scoped RAG/agent module: test plan, synthetic prompts, source-isolation checks, tool-permission tests, logging/human-review evidence, traces, rework/no-go findings, and buyer-safe annex that the partner inserts into its client report or procurement response.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three appsec/SOC2/fractional-CISO partners sign written module agreements; six live client AI-module tickets are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 12,000-35,000 PLN each; every ticket has a named client, buyer or security-review blocker, non-high-risk RAG/agent scope, authorized test workspace, and partner report path; at least four modules are accepted into partner-branded reports or buyer responses; two partners route a second module; gross margin stays above 60% after AI security reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Secure 8-12 module partners; deliver 30-60 paid RAG/agent modules; collect 500,000-1,500,000 PLN in wholesale, direct-bill, and urgent module fees; document 25+ partner-accepted report annexes, 20+ buyer-progress outcomes, and 10 remediation/no-go decisions; get at least five partners to route 5+ modules; keep turnaround under 7 business days for bounded evidence-ready modules; maintain a private taxonomy of prompt-injection tests, source-isolation checks, tool-permission failures, logging gaps, human-review defects, buyer-safe wording, report-annex formats, and escalation triggers.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of 150 Polish/CEE/EU appsec boutiques, small penetration-testing firms, SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, and security-questionnaire firms. Pitch only overflow module economics: "Keep the client and the report. When your SaaS client has a RAG or agent feature and the buyer asks for AI-security evidence, route that module to me. I work inside your workspace, produce bounded test traces and a buyer-safe annex, and send pass/rework/no-go in seven business days. You stay the trusted security provider."

Economics:
Charge partners 10,000-16,000 PLN wholesale for narrow prompt/source-isolation modules, 18,000-30,000 PLN for multi-feature RAG or tool-using agent modules, and 30,000-50,000 PLN for urgent high-value renewal or procurement annexes. Direct-bill clients 20,000-60,000 PLN where the partner wants referral economics. AI security reviewer costs are 5,000-15,000 PLN per module depending on scope. The partner pays or routes payment because the module protects the partner's client relationship, expands the partner's report, and avoids losing urgent AI evidence work to a larger AI security firm.

Copy risk:
Appsec firms, AI security vendors, penetration testers, red-team providers, GRC platforms, and internal security teams can copy pieces. The defense is signed partner module lanes, fast white-label delivery inside partner workspaces, small-SaaS RAG/agent test recipes, buyer-safe trace formatting, accepted/rejected annex memory, no-go discipline, and being the repeat AI module bench before partners justify internal hires.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the appsec/SOC2 partner signs the module agreement, opens a current client ticket, authorizes the test workspace, and guarantees payment, the startup controls the active RAG/agent module traces and report annex for that client deadline. A larger AI security vendor would need to displace the partner-approved module bench from inside the partner's existing client engagement while the report or buyer response is already underway.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only partner-routed RAG/agent AI-security modules tied to a current appsec, SOC2, CISO, security-questionnaire, buyer-response, renewal, or procurement deadline. The output is a white-label report annex and evidence artifact with scoped test plan, prompt-injection traces, source-isolation checks, tool-permission tests, log/human-review evidence, rework/no-go table, and buyer-safe wording. It is not generic AI security consulting, full red-team service, product remediation, legal compliance, SaaS, dashboard, database, marketplace, template pack, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification opinions, DPIAs, high-risk AI conformity assessment, SOC2 certification, ISO 42001 implementation, formal penetration-test certification, model validation, safety certification, product remediation, production incident response, or guarantees of buyer approval. The partner remains the client-facing security provider and owns final report framing, legal escalation, certification claims, and remediation scope. Cases involving regulated high-risk AI, autonomous adverse decisions, clinical data, credit/insurance/HR decisioning, biometrics, law enforcement, live production sensitive data without proper authorization, destructive agent actions, or buyer demands for formal certification are declined or routed to qualified firms.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle ICT third-party-risk, Article 28/30 DORA evidence, exit plans, subcontracting, audit rights, or regulated-finance operational resilience. It is not RAGProbe Buyer Security Evidence Sprint because the buyer and control point are incumbent appsec/SOC2 partners and their report-annex module lanes, not a standalone partner-routed procurement sprint. It is not AIQ Partner Ticket Book because the artifact is bounded test traces and report annexes, not questionnaire answer packaging. It is not AI Model-Risk Renewal Evidence Pack, VoiceAI Article 50, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BESSGuard, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Appsec partners may internalize after a few modules if the recipes are learnable. Larger AI security firms have stronger credibility for formal red-team work. Some buyers will demand formal penetration-test reports, certification, model validation, or legal AI classification outside scope. Partner wholesale margins can compress after reviewer costs. Sensitive source, prompt, log, and tool-permission access must stay inside partner-controlled workspaces. The startup could become hidden low-margin subcontract labor unless it controls a repeatable RAG/agent annex module with accepted buyer wording and strict no-go boundaries.
