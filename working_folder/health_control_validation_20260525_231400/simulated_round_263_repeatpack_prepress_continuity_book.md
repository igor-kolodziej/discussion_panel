## Selected Candidate

### Idea Name

RepeatPack Prepress Continuity Book Buyout.

### Thesis

Buy or option the current repeat-job book of a retiring or overloaded packaging prepress micro-studio, flexo plate coordinator, color-profile freelancer, or label-artwork operator, then convert exact customer-approved files, proofs, color profiles, plate/die IDs, job histories, and current repeat packaging jobs into prepaid continuity revenue before paying the seller.

### Buyer / Seller Pair

Seller: a one-to-five-person packaging prepress, label-artwork, flexo plate coordination, proofing/color, or print-file micro-operator with active repeat customers but no succession plan.

Economic payor: label printers, flexible-packaging converters, carton printers, private-label brands, cosmetics/household/non-regulated food-adjacent packaging teams, and industrial label buyers with current repeat SKUs whose next print run depends on exact files, approval history, plates/dies, profile settings, proof records, and job notes.

### Acute Trigger

The old file owner is retiring, shutting down, stopping support, being acquired, or no longer wants small repeat-job work. A customer has active packaging reorders, retailer/private-label launches, seasonal restocks, or printer handoffs that depend on exact artwork, dielines, barcode positions, color targets, plate/die references, proof approvals, variable-data settings, and prior job tickets.

### Transferable Control Point

- Seller option or management handoff over named customers and repeat SKU files.
- Customer-approved transfer of production files, proof history, color targets, profiles, plate/die references, job tickets, print notes, customer approval emails, and active order calendar.
- Phone, domain, mailbox, folder, and invoicing continuity where useful.
- Customer payment-direction approval or prepaid continuity order before seller payout.
- Contractor review of file custody and basic maintainability.
- Strict exclusion of legal label content review, regulated certification, food-contact compliance, pharma artwork approval, safety warnings, and customer-specific product claims.

### 60-Day Proof

- One seller LOI or option over a named prepress/repeat packaging book.
- Eight customer transfer approvals or seller-mediated continuity orders.
- At least 50 repeat SKUs under controlled file custody with customer authorization.
- At least 40,000 PLN collected or escrowed in prepaid continuity, current repeat-job release, or file-custody fees before seller payout.
- One current repeat job released under the new operator with printer/customer acceptance or paid clean no-go.
- One packaging prepress/color contractor confirms that files, profiles, proofs, and job notes are usable enough for bounded continuity.

### Internal Cap Application

The candidate clears simulation only as a current paid production-book acquisition with customer-approved transfer and first job cash. It would fall below the simulation gate if it becomes cold prepress consulting, a design agency, a label-compliance service, a print broker, a file archive database, or a support book with no current jobs and no payment direction.

### 6-Month POC

- Option or acquire two repeat-packaging prepress books.
- Transfer 25-60 customers and 200-600 repeat SKUs.
- Complete 30-80 current repeat-job releases, file handoffs, or paid no-go outcomes.
- Collect 250,000-800,000 PLN in continuity fees, repeat-job release fees, file custody, urgent transfer fees, and seller-routed renewals.
- Maintain gross margin above 60% after contractor review, file cleanup, secure storage, seller earnout, and admin.
- Keep bespoke design/new-artwork/change work below 25% of delivery time.
- Continue only if at least five customers reorder or approve a second SKU/job under the new operator.

### First Acquisition Mechanism

Build a target list from Polish/CEE packaging prepress shops, flexo plate coordinators, color consultants, label-artwork freelancers, proofing operators, small print brokers with file custody, and retiring graphic production operators.

Seller pitch:

"If you are tired of repeat label and packaging files, I will option the active book and pay you from retained customer cash. You introduce customers, I custody the exact files/proofs/job notes, contractors handle bounded prepress continuity, and seller payout only starts after customers approve transfer and pay."

Customer pitch through seller:

"Your existing repeat packaging files and approval history are being handed to a continuity operator. Pay only for continuity on the files and jobs you still use. No redesign, no legal label review, no print brokering unless you request a separate printer."

### Economics

- 2,000-8,000 PLN per urgent repeat-job release or printer handoff.
- 3,000-12,000 PLN annual continuity/file-custody fee for a small customer with active repeat SKUs.
- 500-2,000 PLN per SKU for structured file inventory, proof history, and transfer-ready package.
- 10,000-30,000 PLN for multi-SKU handoff from one brand or printer.
- Seller receives a small option fee plus 20-40% earnout on retained cash for 12-18 months.

The buyer pays because recreating missing or poorly documented packaging files can delay a print run, require new proofs, trigger retailer/private-label reapproval, create color/barcode mistakes, waste substrate, or force a rushed design/prepress rebuild.

### Copy Risk

Packaging printers, prepress shops, flexo plate houses, color consultants, Esko/GMG integrators, and design agencies can copy the category. The defense is transaction-specific: exact active customer book, seller endorsement, customer-approved file transfer, proof history, plate/die references, payment direction, and first repeat-job cash.

### Why Incumbents Cannot Copy Before Control

Once the seller signs the option, customers approve transfer, files/proofs/job notes move into custody, and current job or continuity cash is paid, a competitor cannot instantly control the same production file set and seller/customer handoff without replacing the active transfer owner. Printers and agencies can compete for future work, but not for the already-transferred file book and current job calendar.

### Why It Is Not A Service, Report, App, Dashboard, Database, Marketplace, Or Generic Broker

The product is an acquired repeat-production book with exact customer-approved files, production history, proof artifacts, plate/die references, current jobs, and transferred payment direction. The startup does not sell design, generic prepress, label compliance, software, a file database, a dashboard, a print marketplace, or introductions.

### Boundaries

No legal label review, food-law advice, cosmetics claims review, pharma artwork approval, safety-warning judgment, GS1 issuance, barcode-number ownership, product compliance certification, print-quality warranty, substrate selection, color certification, regulated packaging release, or customer claim approval. Printers, brand owners, regulatory reviewers, legal advisers, GS1 owners, color specialists, and packaging engineers remain responsible for final production, label content, compliance, and print acceptance.

Reject pharma, medical, high-risk food-safety, child-safety, hazardous chemical, regulated warning, or disputed-IP cases unless qualified providers own those decisions and the startup only transfers existing production files.

### Duplicate-Risk Distinction

This is not OneStack, Atlassian, MaintainerExit, Lab Interface Continuity, CMM Program Continuity, OrphanMold, MachinePlate, CodeRibbon, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

The controlled object is a repeat packaging production-file book and current repeat-job cash, not a software support book, custom industrial connector, physical mold/tool, consumables route, public tender evidence pack, regulated compliance response, or generic design agency.

### Strongest Anticipated Objections

1. The right seller may be rare: enough active customers, clean files, transferable rights, and willingness to accept earnout.
2. Customers may use the handoff moment to move work to their printer, agency, or internal designer.
3. Missing fonts, plugins, source images, die files, proof targets, profiles, or customer approvals can make the book less usable than it appears.
4. Label content and barcode mistakes can create liability if boundaries are weak.
5. Printers and prepress houses are natural incumbents and can bundle continuity with printing.
6. Some files may be owned by brands, printers, designers, or agencies rather than the seller.
7. Jobs can become custom redesign, regulatory wording, packaging engineering, or color troubleshooting.
8. ARPU may be modest unless the first book has active repeat SKUs and urgent customer jobs.
9. A file book can decay if customers change packaging, product claims, SKUs, or printers.
10. One acquired book proves a cash-flow asset, not a company, unless seller sourcing repeats.
