Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
SupplementTIC Amazon 90-Day Case Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A white-label live-case desk for Amazon account-health, reinstatement, and marketplace-compliance agencies serving supplement brands hit by Amazon's 2026 dietary-supplement TIC/cGMP notices, turning the live Account Health request, ASIN list, labels, manufacturer documents, COAs, claims, and TIC-provider route into a fast case map that gets the seller to the right approved-TIC initiation, clean evidence submission path, or no-go before ASIN revenue is lost.

Exact buyer:
Primary channel buyer: Amazon account-health agencies, reinstatement consultants, marketplace-compliance agencies, supplement Amazon operators, and e-commerce agencies that already support supplement sellers.

Economic buyer: Amazon dietary-supplement sellers, including EU/CEE supplement brands selling on Amazon.com, that have received a live dietary-supplement TIC/cGMP notice, ASIN compliance warning, Account Health request, document-rejection message, or deactivation risk.

Best first cases are sellers with meaningful current ASIN revenue, an actual Amazon notice, a 90-day or similarly short process clock, and manufacturer/lab/certification documents that may or may not fit Amazon's approved TIC route.

Acute trigger:
Amazon's 2026 dietary-supplement enforcement requires affected sellers to work through approved testing, inspection, and certification providers. Sellers with live notices must initiate or validate the required documentation quickly. High-revenue ASINs can be deactivated or delayed if the seller misunderstands the request, chooses the wrong TIC path, has manufacturer documents that do not match the ASIN, has Supplement Facts / detail-page claim mismatches, or assumes old COAs can be uploaded directly by the seller.

Control point:
Agency-routed live case with:
- seller authorization;
- Amazon notice / Account Health request text;
- ASIN and variation list;
- Supplement Facts panel and product images;
- manufacturer/facility GMP certificate status;
- COAs, lab reports, lot/batch details, product specs, and existing certification evidence where available;
- detail-page title, bullet, image, ingredient, potency, and claim inventory;
- selected Amazon-approved TIC provider or accepted certification-program route;
- TIC request form, intake confirmation, quote, document-review start, or lab acknowledgement where obtained;
- direct TIC/lab payment by seller, not by the startup;
- fixed output: case map, missing-evidence list, claim-panel mismatch list, TIC initiation checklist, no-go/escalation memo, and agency/Seller Central response language.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid live Amazon supplement TIC/compliance cases at 6,000-20,000 PLN each.
2. At least two cases routed through Amazon account-health, reinstatement, marketplace, or supplement-compliance agencies.
3. At least two TIC provider initiations, intake confirmations, quote acknowledgements, or accepted-document-review starts.
4. At least one case produces a concrete platform/commercial outcome: ASIN remains live during the 90-day process, deactivated ASIN moves to document-review/reinstatement path, TIC confirms existing documentation can be reviewed, seller receives a clean no-go before wasting lab spend, or claim-panel mismatch is corrected before submission.
5. One named supplement regulatory/TIC-process reviewer on paid per-case terms.
6. Private taxonomy across 20+ rejected/missing evidence patterns: certificate issuer, facility scope, COA age, product name mismatch, batch/lot mismatch, label claim mismatch, Supplement Facts mismatch, certification-program eligibility, high-risk category testing, and seller/TIC submission routing.

6-month POC:
Month 1: recruit one supplement regulatory/TIC-process reviewer; build a sample case map using public Amazon dietary-supplement policy summaries, approved-TIC provider materials, and anonymized marketplace notices where available; build a target list of 50 Amazon account-health, reinstatement, marketplace-compliance, and supplement Amazon agencies; create a strict intake form that requires a live Amazon notice before any paid case.

Month 2: close three prepaid live cases through agencies or supplement Amazon operators. For each case, map the Amazon notice, ASINs, labels, Supplement Facts, detail-page claims, COAs, GMP certificates, certification-program evidence, and TIC route. Deliver missing-evidence list, TIC initiation checklist, mismatch corrections, no-go/escalation notes, and agency/Seller Central response language.

Month 3: document whether at least one case moves commercially: TIC intake started, approved provider acknowledges review, ASIN remains live during the process, deactivated ASIN moves into review/reinstatement, or seller avoids bad lab spend because the case is a clean no-go.

Months 4-6: complete 12-25 paid cases, collect 120,000-350,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, sign 2-3 recurring agency routing relationships, and build a private taxonomy of Amazon supplement TIC rejection/missing-document/claim-mismatch patterns.

First acquisition mechanism:
Agency pitch:
"Your supplement clients are getting Amazon TIC/cGMP notices and document rejections. I do not test, certify, sell supplements, or give medical claims advice. Send me one live Account Health request and the ASIN/documents. I return a case map, TIC initiation checklist, mismatch list, no-go/escalation notes, and response language so your agency can keep the seller moving while the approved TIC provider does the actual verification."

Seller pitch through agency:
"If Amazon has given you a supplement TIC/cGMP notice, we organize the live case before you waste weeks: what Amazon is asking, which ASINs and labels are involved, what existing documents can be routed to an approved TIC, which claims or Supplement Facts mismatches need correction, and what must be escalated to the TIC, manufacturer, regulatory reviewer, or lab."

Economics:
Charge 6,000-10,000 PLN for a single-ASIN or simple variation-family case map, 12,000-20,000 PLN for a multi-ASIN batch with reviewer pass and TIC initiation support, and 25,000-50,000 PLN/month for an agency supplement-compliance queue. Agencies keep 25-35% margin or pay wholesale. TIC, lab, certification, manufacturer, and legal/regulatory costs are paid directly by the seller. The seller pays because one blocked high-revenue ASIN or wasted TIC/lab cycle can cost far more than the case fee.

Copy risk:
Amazon agencies, supplement regulatory consultants, TIC providers, labs, manufacturers, law firms, and experienced supplement operators can copy pieces. The defense is agency routing at the live Account Health moment, fast Amazon-specific case memory, direct familiarity with TIC initiation patterns, label/claim mismatch taxonomy, and private outcome memory across repeated notices and provider routes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once an agency routes a live Amazon notice, the seller authorizes the case, the startup maps the ASINs/documents/claims, the seller starts the selected TIC provider path, and the case has a deadline, another provider must displace an active enforcement workflow. The controlled unit is the live Account Health case, seller authorization, document set, agency channel, TIC initiation trail, and outcome memory.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live Amazon supplement TIC/cGMP cases with an actual notice, warning, document rejection, or deactivation risk. The output is an Account Health case artifact: TIC initiation checklist, missing-evidence map, mismatch list, no-go/escalation memo, and response language tied to a current ASIN deadline. It is not a supplement brand, supplement seller, lab, TIC provider, certification body, medical adviser, claims consultant, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or expert-call broker.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not sell, resell, advertise, formulate, recommend, test, certify, clear, prescribe, or ship supplements. It does not provide medical, clinical, legal, FDA, FTC, Amazon policy, certification, GMP, lab, toxicology, or product-safety opinions. It does not state that a product is safe, effective, compliant, or eligible for sale. Approved TIC providers, labs, certification bodies, manufacturers, counsel, and qualified regulatory advisers remain responsible for their roles. The seller remains responsible for product, label, claims, Amazon account actions, and all commercial decisions. Cases involving drugs, controlled substances, disease-treatment claims, adverse-event handling, or unresolved safety concerns are declined or escalated.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not Supplement Stack Safety because it is not a consumer supplement/lab-response safety report, does not collect personal health data, does not review a person's supplement regimen, and does not make practitioner-facing safety suggestions. The buyer is an Amazon agency or supplement seller with a live marketplace enforcement case.

It is not REDBlocked because it is not EU connected-device RED/CE/GPSR evidence for baby monitors or cameras; it is Amazon dietary-supplement TIC/cGMP case routing through approved testing/certification providers. It is not DORA, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, or any Rx/clinical/patient workflow.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Approved TIC providers and supplement regulatory consultants may own the natural workflow.
- Amazon agencies may prefer to refer sellers directly to TIC providers instead of paying an intermediate desk.
- A solo Warsaw founder has weak credibility unless a named supplement regulatory/TIC-process reviewer is attached.
- Many cases cannot be saved because the manufacturer lacks a valid GMP certificate, COAs are stale or mismatched, labels are inaccurate, or the product needs new testing.
- Amazon outcomes are opaque and platform policy can change quickly.
- The work may drift into supplement claims, FDA/FTC legal advice, lab interpretation, formulation, safety, or medical-adjacent advice unless boundaries are strict.
- Seller willingness to pay is strongest only for high-revenue ASINs; low-revenue sellers may churn or wait.
- The US Amazon policy focus weakens the Warsaw/local advantage unless EU/CEE supplement exporters and agencies are reachable.
- Case memory may be useful but not deep if approved TIC providers standardize the workflow.
