Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CSIRE Reserve-Sales Exit Rail

One-sentence thesis:
Polish multi-site SMEs and C&I customers pushed onto reserve/default electricity sales, or stuck in failed supplier switches during the CSIRE rollout, need a broker-routed data-and-switch evidence rail that controls signed customer authorization, PPE/site data, reserve-sale notices, switch-status defects, supplier offer packets, and completed switch/no-go proof before another expensive billing cycle hits.

Exact buyer:
Primary buyer: independent Polish energy brokers, outsourced energy-procurement advisers, ESCOs, and energy-accounting firms that serve multi-site SMEs, retail chains, franchises, light industrial groups, warehouses, offices, hospitality groups, and property portfolios.

Economic beneficiary: the C&I customer currently on reserve/default electricity sale, facing a failed or delayed supplier switch, or approaching contract expiry with messy PPE/site data.

Best first buyer has at least 10-100 PPE points, at least one live reserve-sale notice, failed switch notice, switch rejection, or urgent contract-expiry case, and an existing broker/adviser who can choose the new electricity seller and own commercial advice.

Acute trigger:
A customer is paying or about to pay reserve/default electricity-sale prices, has received a reserve-sale notice, has a supplier switch rejected or delayed, has PPE/site data mismatches after CSIRE rollout, or must complete a new electricity sales/complex contract before the next billing cycle. The broker has commercial commission or client retention at stake but does not want to manually chase PPE lists, historical invoices, authorization wording, switch-status evidence, DSO/OIRE messages, old seller data, site contacts, and correction/no-go trails across many sites.

Control point:
The startup controls a signed customer authorization packet and broker routing agreement for named PPE/site clusters. The controlled file includes customer entity authority, PPE list, reserve-sale notice or switch rejection, current invoice and contract-expiry data, broker-of-record instruction, DSO/OIRE/CSIRE status trail where available, site-level mismatch log, new supplier offer packet assembled by the broker, switch-submission checklist, and proof of completed switch, accepted correction, broker-recognized no-go, or continued reserve-sale reason.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Two written broker/adviser routing agreements covering reserve-sale exit or failed-switch cases.
2. Five customer-signed authorization packets covering at least 50 PPE points in total.
3. At least three live reserve-sale notices, failed-switch notices, or contract-expiry cases accepted under strict intake rules.
4. At least two completed switch submissions, accepted corrections, supplier-offer packets, or broker-recognized no-go files.
5. First 25,000-60,000 PLN collected from broker-paid case fees, customer-paid portfolio fees, or broker commission share tied to completed switch/no-go outcomes.
6. A rejection log showing at least 40% of inbound cases rejected for missing authority, no reserve-sale/default-supply pain, no broker owner, low value, legal dispute, or supplier choice outside the startup boundary.

6-month POC:
Month 1: sign two broker/adviser routing agreements; build the customer authorization, PPE intake, reserve-sale notice, switch-rejection, and no-go workflow; attach an energy-market process reviewer for boundary checks.
Month 2: process 5-8 customer portfolios covering 50-150 PPE points; complete at least two switch submissions, corrections, supplier-offer packets, or no-go outcomes; collect first paid case fees.
Months 3-4: expand to 4-6 brokers/advisers; handle 20-35 portfolios covering 300-900 PPE points; document repeated rejection/mismatch patterns by DSO/seller/PPE type; prove turnaround under 5 business days for evidence-ready files.
Months 5-6: reach 50-100 paid portfolios or site clusters, 1,000-3,000 PPE points processed, 30-60 completed switch/correction/no-go outcomes, 180,000-500,000 PLN revenue, 60%+ gross margin after reviewers and admin contractors, and at least three repeat broker channels.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to independent energy brokers, ESCOs, energy-procurement advisers, energy-accounting firms, and outsourced CFOs serving multi-site SMEs. The first message is not a generic CSIRE readiness pitch. It asks for live cases: "Do you have clients on reserve/default electricity sale, failed supplier switches, contract-expiry pressure, or PPE/site data mismatches where your team is losing time before the next bill? We take the authorized data and switch evidence queue off your desk while you keep supplier choice and commercial advice."

Economics:
Charge brokers or customers 2,000-6,000 PLN for a small site cluster, 8,000-18,000 PLN for a multi-site urgent reserve-sale exit packet, or 75-250 PLN per PPE point for larger portfolios, with minimum fees. Add optional success fee or broker commission share only when tied to a completed switch, accepted correction, or documented customer exit from reserve/default supply. A customer facing reserve/default electricity prices or a broker at risk of losing a multi-site account has enough current economic pain to support fixed fees if the portfolio is meaningful.

Copy risk:
Energy brokers, electricity sellers, DSO support teams, energy accountants, and energy-procurement consultants can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is not a template alone. The defense is controlling signed customer authorization packets for named site clusters, broker-routed repeat queues, CSIRE/PPE/switch rejection memory by DSO and seller, rapid no-go rules, and an operational library of accepted correction and switch-submission patterns.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the customer authorization, broker routing instruction, named PPE cluster, reserve-sale notice or switch rejection, site-data file, and active switch/correction queue, another party cannot work that exact file without re-obtaining customer authority and reconstructing the case. Brokers can internalize future cases, but not before the startup has executed the first authorized queue and built repeat channel memory across multiple brokers.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is an authorized switch-data and reserve-sale exit rail for named customer portfolios. It produces completed switch submissions, accepted corrections, supplier-offer evidence packets, broker-recognized no-go files, and reserve-sale exit proof. The startup does not sell electricity, recommend tariffs, broker energy contracts, run a generic marketplace, produce a dashboard, or write an advisory report detached from a live switch/payment consequence.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as electricity seller, energy broker of record, legal adviser, URE representative, tariff adviser, claims adviser, or distributor representative. The broker or licensed electricity seller owns supplier selection, price comparison, offer recommendation, contract execution, and customer commercial advice. The startup only structures authorized data intake, status evidence, correction workflow, submission packet, progress log, and no-go evidence. It rejects tariff disputes, legal complaints, regulatory claims, debt disputes, unclear authority, household-consumer cases, and any case where supplier choice or legal interpretation is the core work.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not BatteryFit because it does not qualify PV/battery retrofits for installers. It is not HeatQuiet because it is not a heat-pump noise or complaint packet. It is not Data Act Machine Data Liberation because the controlled source is Polish retail electricity-market switch/PPE/reserve-sale data under CSIRE rollout, not OEM machine telemetry for compressed-air service firms. It is not TraceFaktura because it is not a municipal animal-care event invoice rail. It is not PromoLeak, DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, GreenTender EPREL, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, Supplement Stack Safety, or any Amazon/platform evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Energy brokers and electricity sellers may already be able to do this themselves.
The pain may be transition-heavy and normalize after the CSIRE rollout stabilizes.
Many cases may be low-value, household, or simple enough for the broker to solve without paying.
Some switch failures may be caused by debts, legal disputes, wrong contracts, missing customer authority, supplier risk, or seller choice issues outside the startup's boundary.
Customer authorization and energy-market data handling create privacy and trust friction.
DSO/OIRE/CSIRE responses and switch completion timing are outside the startup's control.
Success attribution can be disputed if the broker or seller would have completed the switch anyway.
The business can decay into generic energy-procurement admin unless it only accepts live reserve-sale/default-supply, failed-switch, or urgent contract-expiry cases.
The unknown part-time founder needs a credible energy-market process reviewer and disciplined secure workflow from the first case.
