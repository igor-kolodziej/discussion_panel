Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

ClaimFoundry Corporate Unclaimed-Property Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:

Search official US and Canadian unclaimed-property programs for Polish and CEE corporate legal names, identify claim records above a strict value threshold before outreach, sign a lawful claim-specific finder/authorization agreement with the apparent corporate owner, prepare the owner evidence package, track official claim IDs, and invoice only after the government or holder pays the company or issues a written payable/approval.

Exact buyer:

Polish and CEE companies, exporters, software vendors, manufacturers, distributors, former US/Canada subsidiaries, acquisition vehicles, and corporate groups whose current or former legal names appear in official US state, Canadian provincial, or NAUPA-linked unclaimed-property records. The first buyer is a CFO, controller, founder, legal ops lead, or group accountant at a company with a public corporate property record or related bundle that appears worth at least USD 25,000 equivalent.

Acute trigger:

The startup approaches with a specific public government-held property lead tied to the company's legal name, former name, branch, acquisition, or historic address. The buyer is not paying for a speculative audit. It is reacting to a named cash asset that may be recoverable from an official state/province program or holder if authority, ownership, identity, and successor documents are assembled correctly.

Control point:

Claim-specific finder and authorization file: partial public record disclosure, owner match evidence, lawful finder/fee agreement, company officer authorization, permitted representative forms where required, state/province claim number, claim submission checklist, official acknowledgement or document request, status log, and success-fee invoice trigger. The company is paid directly by the state, province, or holder; the startup does not custody recovered funds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Search 30-50 official state/province programs and NAUPA-linked sources for at least 1,000 Polish/CEE corporate legal names, former names, branches, acquisitions, and English variants. Build 40-80 candidate corporate property records with source state, owner name, address/entity match, holder type, property type, and listed range where available. Contact 120 CFOs/controllers/legal ops leads/founders with claim-specific evidence. Sign 4-6 claim-specific mandates covering at least USD 200,000 equivalent of listed or expected claim value. Submit at least 3 claim packages and obtain at least 2 official claim IDs, acknowledgements, document requests, payable/approved statuses, or written no-go decisions. Recover or obtain payable approval for at least one claim, even if small, and issue the first success-fee invoice.

6-month POC:

25-40 signed corporate mandates. USD 1.5-4.0M equivalent in listed or expected claim value under signed mandate. 15-25 claims submitted. 8-15 official acknowledgements, payable approvals, recovered payments, or clean no-go decisions. USD 250,000-900,000 recovered or approved/payable. 120,000-500,000 PLN in fees invoiced or collected. Gross margin above 70% after legal-template review, company-record pulls, translations, notarization, apostille/courier costs, and contractor research. A state-by-state playbook for lawful finder contracts, fee limits, required disclosures, document packs, company-name/entity matching, successor evidence, notarization, apostille/translation needs, and claim-status follow-up.

First acquisition mechanism:

Use official state/province search portals, MissingMoney/NAUPA-linked programs, corporate registry data, acquisition histories, old subsidiary names, and address variants to find specific public records before outreach. Send CFO/controller/legal ops outreach that reveals enough to be credible without turning the work into a free self-serve report: source jurisdiction, apparent owner name, partial historic address or holder category, rough value band where public, required owner documents, no-upfront-fee terms, and a clear note that official claiming is free if they choose to do it themselves. Prioritize companies with US/Canada customers, offices, bank accounts, payroll, securities holdings, litigation/settlement histories, dissolved subsidiaries, or acquired entities.

Economics:

No upfront search fee for the first wedge. Charge 10% where state law, forms, or market norms cap or strongly imply that limit. Charge 15-20% only in jurisdictions and property types where lawful and commercially acceptable. Minimum success fee of 8,000 PLN equivalent per submitted claim unless bundled. Optional fixed document-rebuild fee only after trust is established, credited against success fee. Costs are legal-template review, jurisdiction checklist, company registry pulls, translations, notarization, apostille, courier, document retrieval, and contractor research. One USD 50,000 recovered claim at 10% yields roughly 20,000 PLN, so the business only works if low-value records and legal-succession traps are rejected.

Copy risk:

Asset locators, unclaimed-property investigators, law firms, accountants, corporate-recovery firms, and internal finance teams can copy the general workflow. The edge is not software or secret law. It is pre-identifying cross-border corporate claims others ignore, signing claim-specific mandates before full disclosure, respecting state-by-state finder rules, and building a private CEE corporate evidence library for name changes, acquisitions, old addresses, officer authority, translations, notarization, apostille, and state document requests.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

For each specific property record, once the founder has shown a partial record, signed a claim-specific finder/authorization agreement, prepared the evidence checklist, filed the claim, and obtained an official claim ID or acknowledgement, a later asset locator cannot control that same claim without displacing an already-authorized and already-filed recovery process. The control is claim-by-claim, not market-wide. Incumbents can compete for future claims, but they cannot easily take an already-filed mandate with a state response trail and fee trigger.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

The startup does not sell an unclaimed-property search report, database subscription, dashboard, generic corporate audit, or introduction to a finder. It accepts only named public property records where a corporation appears to be the owner, signs a claim-specific recovery mandate, prepares the official owner evidence file, submits or coordinates the claim, tracks official responses, and gets paid only from actual recovered or approved government-held funds.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

No individuals, heirs, deceased estates, consumer claims, probate, family tracing, or inheritance work. No upfront fee and no custody of recovered funds. The company is paid directly by the state/province/holder. Use a lawyer-reviewed agreement and jurisdiction checklist before first paid contract. Respect fee caps, disclosure rules, timing restrictions, investigator forms, registration requirements, notarization rules, and state-specific limits. Reject claims requiring legal representation, contested successor ownership, litigation, tax advice, securities transfer advice, medallion-signature guarantee work outside an approved provider route, or ambiguous ownership. Do not imply that paid help is required; official claiming is free if the owner chooses to handle it directly.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This is not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central, retail chargeback, 3PL, OTA, courier COD, OEM core credit, utility deposit, DRS settlement, KSeF, LC discrepancy, PV compensation, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, or EUDR. The buyer trigger is a pre-identified public government-held property record. The control point is a claim-specific finder/authorization file and official claim ID. The payment event is government or holder return of unclaimed property to the corporate owner.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Owners can claim directly for free, and official programs warn that no paid finder is required. Finder fees are capped or regulated in some states and contracts may require disclosures, forms, timing rules, or registration. Public records often show incomplete values, stale addresses, old names, merged entities, dissolved subsidiaries, or ambiguous owners. Corporate authority evidence can be painful: name changes, acquisitions, signatory authority, tax IDs, officer certificates, board authority, address proof, notarized documents, apostilles, and translations. Claim processing can take months, so 60-day cash recovery is uncertain. Trust friction is high because unclaimed-property finder outreach can look scammy. Asset locators already exist in the US and may have better data, mail infrastructure, and state-specific experience. The market may be one-time and episodic rather than recurring. Many high-value claims may be outside the Warsaw founder's local advantage unless CEE corporate-name matching is genuinely under-mined. Success-fee collection depends on enforceable contracts and owner willingness to pay after the state pays the company directly.
