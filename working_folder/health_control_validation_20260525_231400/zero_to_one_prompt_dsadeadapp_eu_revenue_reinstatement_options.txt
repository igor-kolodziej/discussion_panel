Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DSAApp EU Revenue Reinstatement Option Bank

One-sentence thesis:
Option, acquire, or revenue-share with small app publishers whose iOS or Android apps have non-EU revenue and historical EU demand but whose EU app-store availability, updates, or monetization is blocked by trader-verification status, then restore EU availability and take payment only from recovered EU revenue or a post-restoration app transfer.

Exact buyer:
The first counterparty is a solo or small app publisher, indie studio, micro-SaaS app owner, mobile utility owner, niche B2B app publisher, or small mobile-game owner with an existing iOS or Android app, active non-EU downloads/subscriptions/ads/IAP revenue, historical EU users, EU reviews, EU search demand, or old EU subscription cohorts, but whose EU availability, EU updates, or EU monetization is impaired because App Store Connect, Google Play Console, or another app marketplace requires trader-status verification, public trader contact details, entity/individual identity evidence, address/contact evidence, tax/bank/profile consistency, or platform account corrections. Exclude apps with consumer harm, gambling, adult content, crypto/financial promotion, health/medical claims, children's privacy risk, regulated advice, deceptive subscriptions, malware/adware, copied IP, sanctions, or unresolved account enforcement.

Acute trigger:
The app has already lost or is at immediate risk of losing EU distribution, updates, or monetization because the app-store account is not verified as a trader, has inconsistent trader contact or business identity data, has an incomplete marketplace compliance profile, or cannot complete the store's EU availability requirements. The owner may still earn outside Europe, may have abandoned the EU issue as not worth the distraction, or may be willing to share upside if someone restores a measurable EU payment stream without requiring an upfront acquisition price.

Control point:
The startup controls a signed app-specific option, revenue-share, earnout, or conditional acquisition agreement before doing the work. The controlled file includes the app ID/package name, current app-store status, historical EU download/revenue evidence, non-EU revenue baseline, seller identity and platform-account authority, required trader-verification documents supplied by the seller, public trader-contact workflow, app-transfer or admin handoff route where allowed, before-state EU availability screenshots/exports, submission chronology, platform correspondence, payout split, and seller payout rule. The seller receives no meaningful cash-out beyond a small option fee until EU availability is restored, platform status is accepted, or first restored EU revenue appears.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign two app-owner options or revenue-share agreements where the seller has active non-EU revenue and documented historical EU demand. For at least one app, obtain platform-authorized admin access or a compliant transfer/handoff path, submit or cure the trader-verification file using truthful seller-supplied documents, restore EU availability or obtain a written platform no-go, and capture first restored EU subscription, ad, IAP, or paid download revenue, or a platform acceptance status that permits EU monetization. Collect at least 20,000 PLN equivalent in option fees, restored-revenue share, or post-restoration acquisition spread only if the first app passes; otherwise treat the no-go as paid diligence if the contract allowed it.

6-month POC:
Screen 300-600 small app publishers and app listings for signs of EU-unavailable status, historical EU demand, and current non-EU revenue. Sign 8-15 app options or revenue-share agreements. Attempt 8-12 trader-status restoration files. Restore EU availability or monetization for 4-7 apps and produce 2-4 clean no-go outcomes for accounts with false identity, prohibited content, unresolved enforcement, untransferable ownership, or weak economics. Generate 150,000-500,000 PLN in restored EU revenue share, post-restoration app acquisition spread, fixed no-go fees, or seller-paid success fees. Build a private playbook by app-store, seller type, trader-file defect, platform response pattern, transfer route, no-go reason, and post-restoration revenue quality.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with owner-operated mobile apps where the public app listing, app website, forums, app-acquisition marketplaces, indie-maker communities, GitHub/readme links, support pages, or app-owner posts reveal a small team and likely neglected compliance/admin work. Target apps that remain available outside the EU, still update outside the EU, or still show active non-EU reviews but appear missing, unavailable, or stale in EU storefronts. Outreach is seller-side and cash-first: "If your app lost EU availability or EU monetization because trader verification is a distraction, I will option only the EU recovery path. You keep the app; I do the verified-status and EU reinstatement file under your authority; I get paid only from restored EU revenue or a pre-agreed app transfer after restoration."

Economics:
Avoid upfront purchases. Use a small 1,000-5,000 PLN option/admin fee only for serious files, plus 30-60% of restored EU net revenue for 6-12 months, or a pre-agreed post-restoration acquisition price based on actual restored payout. Reject apps unless historical EU revenue, reviews, search traffic, waitlist, or subscription cohort evidence suggests at least 5,000 PLN/month recoverable EU net revenue or a strategic app acquisition resale path. Costs are mostly document intake, platform-account workflow, app-owner coordination, privacy/consumer-risk screening, light legal boundary review, and technical checks that the app still functions for EU users. The model dies if it becomes generic platform-account consulting at 1,000 PLN per ticket.

Copy risk:
App acquirers, app agencies, app-store consultants, small studios, and marketplace operators can copy the broad idea. The defense is app-specific control: once the founder has a signed option, seller authority, before-state evidence, platform submission chronology, and payout split for a specific app, another party cannot restore and monetize that app without displacing the seller agreement. Over time, the private asset is pattern memory across app-store responses, trader-file defects, seller no-go signals, transfer paths, and post-restoration EU revenue quality.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The controlled object is the named app and its EU payment stream, not a public checklist. After the seller signs an option or revenue-share agreement and grants the platform-authorized work path, a competing agency or app buyer would need to persuade the same seller to break or replace the live agreement while the restoration file is already in motion. App agencies can fix future accounts and app buyers can buy other apps, but they do not own this app's option, submission file, EU before-state, seller-supplied identity documents, payout agreement, or restored revenue split.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell a compliance checklist, trader-status advice, a dashboard, a marketplace of apps, or a platform-support service. It only accepts app-specific option or revenue-share files where restored EU availability and payout movement are the proof. The output is controlled app revenue reinstatement: signed option, platform-authorized evidence submission, accepted status or clean no-go, EU availability before/after, payout tracking, and seller payout under pre-agreed terms.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, tax advice, consumer-law advice, app-review guarantees, privacy compliance certification, financial promotion review, medical or child-safety review, or platform-policy evasion. The app owner remains the truthful trader, publisher, seller, tax party, data controller where applicable, and provider of identity/contact documents. The startup rejects apps with deceptive subscriptions, unsafe content, prohibited categories, copied IP, sanctions, unresolved enforcement, privacy risk, regulated services, health/financial/gambling/adult content, or any request to submit false identity, false address, false contact, or misleading trader information. Any app transfer must follow the app marketplace's own transfer and account rules.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack, REDBlocked Amazon Connected-Device Evidence Rescue Desk, PromoLeak, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, platform evidence desks, or Shopify Ops App Continuity Buyout. The control point is not a compliance response packet, retailer deduction, industrial telemetry file, tender evidence pack, physical salvage lot, or generic app MRR acquisition. The buyer trigger, asset, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: a named mobile app's EU availability and payment stream restored after trader-verification friction, with seller payout tied to actual restored app-store revenue or post-restoration transfer.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many app owners can complete trader verification themselves using official platform instructions, so willingness to share upside may be weak. Platform status may not be blocked only by trader verification; there may be unresolved policy violations, tax/bank issues, privacy defects, deceptive subscriptions, missing support, old SDKs, or abandoned code. App-store transfers and admin handoffs may be restricted, slow, or risky. Historical EU revenue may not return after restoration because rankings, reviews, ads, competition, localization, pricing, or user trust changed. The best apps may not accept earnout-heavy terms, while willing sellers may own low-quality or declining apps. The business can become app-account admin consulting if it accepts cases without revenue evidence. App acquirers and agencies can copy future sourcing. Handling identity documents, account access, contact details, and app analytics creates trust and security friction for an unknown solo founder. Part-time execution is only plausible if intake rejects technical rebuilds, privacy remediation, app-review appeals, regulated categories, and support-heavy apps.
