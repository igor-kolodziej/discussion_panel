## Selected Candidate

**cPanel/Plesk Hosting Continuity Book Buyout**

One-sentence thesis: Buy or option tiny cPanel/Plesk hosting books from retiring solo hosts and web studios, but pay meaningful seller consideration only after customer-approved billing transfer, admin custody, backups, and first-month recurring cash prove continuity.

## Why It Clears Simulation

- The startup controls a named book, not a lead list: customer consents, invoices, control-panel accounts, DNS/registrar records, backups, server inventory, support inbox, runbooks, and seller handoff.
- The first proof is cash movement: at least 30 customers pay the new operator before close.
- The handoff event creates urgency because websites, DNS, email, SSL, backups, renewals, PHP versions, and server security cannot simply be abandoned when a solo host retires.
- It avoids the low-margin CSP/domain-only trap by requiring hosting/control-panel custody plus recurring maintenance revenue, not only licence resale.
- It is not a generic web agency: no new site builds, SEO, design retainers, ad management, or broad MSP work are accepted in the first POC.
- The seller-mediated acquisition path reduces CAC: one retiring micro-host can transfer 30-80 accounts.

## Internal Cap Review

- Concrete 6-month control proof: passes only if customer payment direction, admin access, backups, and first-month cash transfer before close.
- Copy risk: large hosts, MSPs, and web agencies can serve future customers, but cannot copy the exact book after the founder controls the seller option, customer consents, admin custody, and payment direction.
- CAC/payback/margins/cash conversion: passes if seller payout is earnout-heavy and the first month collects enough cash to prove retained MRR before closing.
- Vague partnerships: no pass without signed seller option and customer-approved transfer; referrals alone fail.
- Support/security burden: pass only if support history is mostly renewals, hosting, email, backups, DNS, SSL, PHP updates, and bounded migration work; hacked-site cleanup and bespoke app maintenance are excluded or priced separately.

Simulated score: **88.4 / 100**

## Kill Conditions

- No seller can show clean recurring invoices, customer list, access inventory, and support history.
- Fewer than 30 customers consent and pay the new operator before close.
- Support history is dominated by hacked WordPress sites, bespoke PHP fixes, ecommerce checkout bugs, SEO/design work, or angry underpriced clients.
- Registrar, DNS, control-panel, backup, mailbox, or root access cannot be transferred or rotated cleanly.
- Churn exceeds 20% in the first two months after handoff.
- Gross margin falls below 55% after hosting infrastructure, licences, contractor support, security tooling, backup storage, accounting, and seller earnout.
- The first book cannot produce at least 15,000 PLN monthly recurring gross revenue or equivalent annualized renewals after customer transfer.
- Zero To One treats it as commodity hosting or a web-agency support book rather than a controlled payment/admin continuity acquisition.
