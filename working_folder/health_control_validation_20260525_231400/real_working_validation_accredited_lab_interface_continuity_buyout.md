## Evaluator Summary

Zero To One called this a good niche cash-flow acquisition and one of the better continuity-buyout ideas. It credited the hard control object: exact installed scripts, templates, parsers, mappings, sample exports, customer-specific reporting formats, support history, and customer payment transfer.

## Strongest Objections Captured

1. The right seller may be rare: enough active labs, clean source/config files, clear IP rights, documented scripts, customer transfer approval, and earnout willingness may not align quickly.
2. Old code may be brittle or unmaintainable: VBA, Access, serial-port collectors, compiled binaries without source, hardcoded paths, and old runtimes can require archaeology rather than bounded maintenance.
3. Labs may use the handoff to replace the old scripts with a LIMS vendor, instrument vendor, or internal IT project.
4. ISO 17025 boundaries are dangerous if changes affect calculation logic, rounding, units, report meaning, acceptance criteria, measurement uncertainty, conformity decisions, or certificate content.
5. Support can become custom development unless every contract names exact dependencies and forces changes into paid tickets.
6. Base retainer value may be too low unless emergency fixes and upgrade tickets add meaningful cash.
7. IP ownership may be messy because the lab, seller, instrument vendor, ex-employee, or freelancer may have partial rights.
8. ISO 17025 consultants and assessors may be cautious about a new third-party maintainer touching production scripts.
9. The 60-day proof is seller-dependent; the clean first seller is the hard part.
10. One support book is a useful cash-flow asset, but a company requires repeatable sourcing and a safe operating model.

## Lesson For Next Round

Installed-base control is better than reports, but it still fails when the first seller source is rare and hidden support burden can turn the asset into a custom helpdesk. Round 229 should not continue software-continuity books unless seller discovery is already in hand. Return to hard cash/payment-direction, current accepted claims, or physical/title control where the founder can prove first cash without inheriting an open-ended support obligation.
