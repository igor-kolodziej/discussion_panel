Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DRS Operator Settlement Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
Polish retail chains, fuel-station groups, and franchise clusters now have live deposit-return-system settlement statements where operator credits, deposits, returned-packaging quantities, and handling fees can fail to match POS/RVM/manual-return evidence; a founder can take signed recovery mandates over named outlet-months and get paid only when operators acknowledge, correct, credit, or pay the discrepancy.

Exact buyer:
Polish retail groups, fuel-station franchisees, convenience-store clusters, pharmacy chains selling beverages, and regional grocery chains with 20-300 outlets participating in the Polish deposit-return system after its 1 October 2025 launch. The best buyer has multiple outlets, at least one operator agreement, POS deposit lines, manual-return or RVM logs, monthly operator settlement statements, and a finance/controller owner who sees unreconciled deposit or handling-fee variance.

Acute trigger:
The first operating months of the Polish deposit-return system produce operator settlement statements that do not reconcile to the retailer's own data: deposits collected, deposits returned, returned-package counts, RVM voucher totals, manual-return logs, pickup notes, handling-fee invoices, operator portal statements, or bank credits. The trigger is current cash or credit due, not DRS readiness, label advice, customer education, or generic compliance.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate for named outlet-months; operator agreement and settlement rules; store list; POS deposit-collected and deposit-returned journals; RVM reports or manual-return logs; voucher redemption logs where relevant; packaging pickup/transport notes; handling-fee invoices; operator settlement statements and portal exports; bank credits; filed discrepancy register by operator, outlet, month, EAN/package class, quantity, deposit value, handling fee, baseline amount, claimed amount, evidence, operator response, corrected statement, credit memo, payment, or clean no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Six retail groups or franchise clusters sign recovery mandates covering at least 120 outlets and three settlement months; each mandate identifies a specific operator, outlet-months, settlement statements, and evidence source. The startup loads 500+ outlet-month lines, files 60+ discrepancy items with operators, documents at least 250,000 PLN of claimed deposit/handling-fee variance or missing credits, receives at least three operator acknowledgements or corrected settlement statements, recovers or credits at least 75,000 PLN, invoices 8-15% success fees plus small fixed onboarding fees, and gets one buyer to route a second month before the first engagement closes.

6-month POC:
Process 40-80 retailer/franchise/fuel-group mandates covering 800-2,500 outlets and 6,000+ outlet-month lines; file 500+ discrepancy items across several operators; recover or get operator-recognized credits for 1.5-4.0 million PLN; collect 200,000-600,000 PLN in success fees and onboarding fees; maintain gross margin above 65% after reconciliation contractor time; document an operator-specific discrepancy taxonomy covering RVM/manual-return count mismatch, pickup-to-credit mismatch, missing handling fee, duplicate reversal, delayed bank credit, wrong package class, wrong outlet mapping, voucher cashout mismatch, and portal export inconsistency.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target CFOs/controllers of regional retail chains, fuel-station franchise groups, convenience-store franchise clusters, and accounting/POS implementation firms already dealing with DRS month-end chaos. Pitch: "Send us one operator statement, POS/RVM return logs, and the store list for the first DRS months. If we cannot find a recoverable operator-side variance, you pay only a small onboarding fee. If the operator acknowledges, corrects, credits, or pays, we take a success fee on the credited amount."

Economics:
Charge a 2,500-8,000 PLN onboarding fee per buyer to cover data loading and evidence normalization, then 8-15% of recovered cash, corrected operator credits, or accepted handling-fee adjustments. A 40-outlet group with a 50,000-150,000 PLN settlement variance can pay 5,000-20,000 PLN without procurement friction because the fee is tied to recovered or recognized credits. Contractors reconcile data and prepare evidence; the founder handles buyer qualification, operator claim strategy, fee triggers, and repeat-partner relationships.

Copy risk:
DRS operators, POS integrators, accounting bureaus, retail controllers, RVM vendors, and DRS consultants can copy the workflow. The startup's narrow defense is current signed mandates over named outlet-months, a growing operator-specific discrepancy library, accepted evidence formats, filing language, response timing, credit-memo trigger definitions, and partner channels through POS/accounting firms that do not want to run claim queues themselves.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a retailer signs a mandate and provides operator statements, store-level POS/RVM/manual-return evidence, pickup notes, bank credits, and outlet-month discrepancy rights, the startup controls the active claim queue for those named settlement months. Operators, POS firms, accountants, and consultants can copy the general checklist later, but they cannot instantly control the already-signed buyer mandate, evidence room, discrepancy register, filed claim IDs, operator responses, credit-memo trigger language, or success-fee right on that recovered settlement batch.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell DRS readiness, training, software, operator selection, POS integration, recycling advice, or reports. It accepts only live settlement discrepancies with current operator statements and retailer evidence. The output is a filed recovery queue and monetary outcome: operator acknowledgement, corrected settlement statement, credit memo, bank payment, or clean no-go tied to named outlet-months and fee triggers.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as a DRS operator, waste collector, transporter, recycler, RVM vendor, POS integrator, accountant, tax adviser, legal adviser, cash handler, or packaging custodian. Retailers remain responsible for stores, customer refunds, cash handling, POS configuration, records, and operator relationships. Operators remain responsible for settlement rules, package collection, handling-fee contracts, and credits. Accountants and lawyers handle accounting, tax, contractual disputes, litigation, and legal interpretation. Cases are rejected when records are missing, fraud is suspected, cash handling is unreconciled, packaging counts are physically unverifiable, the retailer caused the variance, or the operator dispute requires legal action rather than evidence-backed settlement correction.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not TraceFaktura because it is not municipal animal-care invoice routing or public-sector payment evidence. It is not KaucjaMark SKU Release, because it does not unblock products, labels, or retail listing readiness; it recovers DRS operator settlement credits after live deposit operations. It is not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, 3PL overbilling, freight audit, BSP ADM recovery, PSP reserve release, CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The buyer trigger, control point, payment event, and proof artifact are specific to Polish DRS operator settlements and returned-packaging/deposit/handling-fee credits.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Retailers and accountants may reconcile DRS statements internally once the first launch chaos settles. Operators may provide portals and support that make outside recovery unnecessary. POS/RVM/manual-return data may be messy, and many apparent discrepancies may be store process errors rather than operator underpayment. The recoverable pool may be too small or too delayed to support high fees. A success fee on corrected credits can be disputed if the retailer was already chasing the issue. Operators, POS vendors, accounting firms, and DRS consultants can copy the claim pack. The startup controls one batch at a time, not a durable monopoly. Part-time execution may be hard during monthly close windows. The business can drift into DRS consulting, POS cleanup, cash variance investigation, waste/logistics disputes, or legal contract arguments unless boundaries are strict.
