Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DIWASS Waste-Shipment Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A broker-routed shipment-release desk for CEE waste exporters, recyclers, scrap traders, and waste brokers whose named cross-border waste loads, customer deliveries, pickup slots, recovery-facility slots, or sale invoices are blocked by the EU Waste Shipment Regulation and DIWASS digital notification/consignment workflow after the 21 May 2026 go-live.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized Polish/CEE waste brokers, recyclers, scrap-metal traders, paper/plastic recyclers, waste-export operators, circular-feedstock traders, and manufacturing groups with recurring cross-border waste or recycled-feedstock shipments. The first buyer has a named load, customer order, recycling/recovery slot, carrier pickup, or invoice currently at risk because route parties, authorities, carriers, customers, or recovery facilities require a correct DIWASS/WSR shipment file. Exclude hazardous-waste specialists already served by large environmental-law teams, medical waste, radioactive waste, illegal export, unresolved waste-code disputes, and cases where the buyer expects the startup to determine legal waste classification.

Acute trigger:
Since the revised EU Waste Shipment Regulation and DIWASS digital process entered application on 21 May 2026, a recurring or current cross-border waste shipment cannot move, be accepted, be invoiced, or be scheduled until the exporter/importer/broker/carrier/recovery facility has the right digital shipment file, Annex VII or notified-shipment documents where applicable, route-party data, contract and recovery-facility details, financial-guarantee evidence where required, consent/authority status where required, and operational responsibility map. The commercial event is a named truck/load/container/order/invoice, not abstract readiness.

Control point:
Signed shipment-release mandate over a specific load or recurring low-risk lane; written buyer authorization; shipment value and deadline; waste code/classification supplied by the responsible operator or qualified adviser; existing consent/notification status where applicable; customer purchase order or sales contract; exporter/importer/broker/carrier/recovery-facility details; recovery operation and facility acceptance evidence; route and border-crossing details; prior shipment history for the same lane; financial-guarantee/provider evidence where required; DIWASS account/provider route; qualified waste-consultant or waste-broker review route for red flags; accepted submission, route-party confirmation, authority response, pickup evidence, or clean no-go. The startup controls the shipment file, evidence requests, route-party tracker, and release-status chronology for that named case. It does not control authority decisions, waste classification, legal compliance, carrier performance, recovery-facility acceptance, or hazardous handling.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign 5 paid live-shipment mandates across at least 3 waste operators or brokers; every case has a named load/order/lane, shipment value, route parties, responsible operator, waste code supplied by customer/adviser, and current movement or invoice deadline. Secure 2 qualified waste-consultant, waste-broker, or environmental-law review partners for classification/consent red flags. Collect at least 80,000 PLN in prepaid triage and release fees. Produce at least 3 concrete outcomes: accepted DIWASS/Annex VII/notification/consignment submission, route-party acceptance, authority response, carrier pickup, recovery-facility acceptance, invoice/payment release, or paid clean no-go because the case needs counsel, corrected classification, new consent, or cannot lawfully move.

6-month POC:
Process 35-70 live shipment-release cases across 12-25 operators or broker channels; collect 500,000-1,400,000 PLN in prepaid shipment fees, lane setup fees, and partner-routed case fees; document 25+ accepted submissions, pickups, route-party acceptances, authority responses, invoice releases, or clean no-go decisions; build a private lane taxonomy by waste stream, route, party role, missing field, DIWASS/provider issue, authority response pattern, carrier/recovery-facility requirement, and accepted packet format; secure 5+ repeat channels among waste brokers, environmental consultants, recycling associations, freight forwarders serving waste shipments, and DIWASS implementation vendors; keep gross margin above 60% after specialist review costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with waste brokers, environmental consultants, recycling trade associations, freight forwarders handling waste loads, and DIWASS/WSR implementation vendors that face overflow questions from smaller operators after the 21 May 2026 go-live. Partner pitch: "When a client has a named cross-border waste load stuck in DIWASS/WSR paperwork, route only the operational shipment file. You keep legal, classification, broker, and system work; I collect route-party facts, evidence, missing fields, and release/no-go chronology under your review." Direct outreach goes to Polish and CEE recyclers and waste traders with visible cross-border paper, plastic, metal, RDF/SRF, recycled-feedstock, or industrial byproduct flows.

Economics:
Charge 4,000-8,000 PLN for a shipment triage/no-go screen; 10,000-25,000 PLN for a single live shipment-release file; 25,000-60,000 PLN for a recurring lane setup covering the first 3-5 shipments; and partner wholesale pricing where a waste consultant or broker keeps margin. The buyer pays because one blocked truck, customer delivery, mill/recovery slot, feedstock sale, or monthly route can be worth far more than the case fee. Capital stays below 100,000 PLN because the startup does not buy waste, finance shipments, provide guarantees, operate transport, or run treatment facilities.

Copy risk:
Waste brokers, environmental consultants, law firms, DIWASS software vendors, carriers, recovery facilities, large recyclers, and in-house compliance teams can copy pieces. The defense is not legal expertise or software. It is rapid control of named shipment cases, partner-routed overflow, low-risk recurring-lane memory, route-party requirement memory, accepted/rejected DIWASS packet formats, and strict clean no-go filtering before incumbents prioritize small CEE operators.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the operator or broker signs the paid mandate, provides the named load/order/lane, authorizes route-party communication, supplies responsible-party waste codes/classification, and gives the DIWASS/provider path and shipment deadline, the startup controls the release file, missing-field tracker, route-party chronology, and outcome evidence for that specific shipment. A larger environmental consultant or software vendor can compete generally, but it must displace an already-authorized case while the load, pickup, customer delivery, or invoice is waiting.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business refuses general WSR readiness reports, dashboards, software resale, marketplace matching, legal opinions, waste-code classification, hazardous-waste advice, and abstract training. It accepts only current named shipments or recurring lanes where the paid outcome is digital submission acceptance, route-party acceptance, pickup, authority response, invoice release, or clean no-go. The deliverable is a shipment-release file: intake, missing-field list, route-party data room, specialist red-flag review, DIWASS/provider submission support, chronology, response wording, and outcome record.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as waste broker, dealer, carrier, recovery facility, exporter of record, notifier, legal adviser, environmental consultant of record, DIWASS software provider, financial-guarantee provider, customs broker, hazardous-waste handler, or authority representative unless a properly licensed/qualified partner is engaged for that role. It does not determine waste classification, consent requirement, Basel status, legality of shipment, treatment standard, or hazardous handling. Responsible operators, licensed waste brokers/dealers, environmental consultants, lawyers, carriers, recovery facilities, competent authorities, and DIWASS providers remain responsible for legal compliance, classification, permits, consent, transport, treatment, and final submission decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not CBAM Import Continuity Desk because it is not carbon-border imports, CN/TARIC embedded-emissions evidence, certificate-price exposure, or customs-broker CBAM memos. It is not TraceFaktura because it is not municipal animal-care event invoicing. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, PromoLeak, BatteryFit, HeritageDoor, BioSignal, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, EUDR, KSeF, Amazon recovery, 3PL overbilling, or FuelEU pooling. The buyer trigger, control artifact, payment event, and proof are materially different: named cross-border waste shipments under WSR/DIWASS, with movement, route-party acceptance, authority response, or invoice release as the proof.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Waste brokers, environmental consultants, law firms, recovery facilities, and DIWASS software vendors may already own trust and workflow. Many cases require legal classification, hazardous-waste expertise, consent, financial guarantees, or authority judgment outside the startup's control. Operators may have too few urgent shipments or too low margin to pay premium fees. Route parties can still reject or delay even a well-assembled file. Software vendors may bundle intake templates and reduce the need for a manual desk. The 21 May 2026 transition may create a short spike rather than a durable business. Access to DIWASS and authority correspondence may be limited to responsible operators. The founder must avoid becoming an unlicensed waste broker, environmental consultant, legal adviser, or shipment representative. Case volume may be lumpy and red flags may force many paid no-go decisions.
