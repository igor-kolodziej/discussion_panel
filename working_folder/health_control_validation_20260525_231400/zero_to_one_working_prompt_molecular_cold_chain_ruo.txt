Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality.

Scored idea:

Molecular Cold-Chain Failure + RUO Reagent Salvage Bank is a Poland/CEE lab-infrastructure control business for genetic, molecular, IVF, biotech, CRO, university spinout, and research labs. It does not handle patient data, diagnose, interpret tests, sell clinical advice, or resell prescription drugs. It owns emergency cold-chain capacity and first-look rights to research-use-only / non-clinical reagent lots that would otherwise be wasted or stranded after freezer failures, distributor overstock, lab closures, project cancellations, or near-expiry events.

Buyer:
- genetic and molecular biology labs;
- IVF and embryology/reproductive labs for non-patient reagent/logistics needs;
- biotech and medtech research teams;
- CROs and university spinouts;
- small diagnostics-adjacent R&D labs that use research-only reagents;
- distributors with overstock, near-expiry, returned-but-unopened, or discontinued RUO lots.

Acute trigger:
- a -80C / -20C freezer failure or LN2 storage gap threatens samples or high-value reagents;
- a lab is moving, closing, downsizing, or losing freezer capacity;
- a distributor or lab has unopened RUO sequencing/PCR/sample-prep/cell-biology reagent lots near expiry;
- a research project ends and leaves expensive RUO reagent stock unused;
- a small lab needs emergency validated cold storage, dry-shipper/dewar/logged courier handoff, or quick liquidation without listing everything manually.

Control point:
The founder owns or leases actual emergency cold-chain assets and signs first-look or consignment rights over specific distressed RUO/research lots:
- one tested used ultra-low-temperature freezer or reserved ULT freezer slots;
- -20C backup freezer capacity if useful;
- dry shippers/dewars/cold boxes, temperature loggers, packaging, and emergency pickup workflow;
- documented temperature logs and chain-of-custody process;
- signed first-look/consignment/salvage agreements with closing labs, upgrading labs, research groups, small distributors, or freezer-service companies;
- title or consignment rights to named unopened RUO/research-only reagent lots, not clinical IVD patient-use stock.

The control point is not "we rent a freezer." It is local emergency cold-chain capacity plus signed access to specific distressed RUO lots and the ability to turn urgent failures/closures into logged storage, dewar movement, and lawful research-only resale/consignment before the value disappears.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 20,000-45,000 PLN for one tested used ULT freezer or reserved third-party ULT capacity plus backup -20C storage, temp monitoring, alarms, and service check;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN for dry shipper/dewar/cold boxes, temperature loggers, courier packaging, rental deposits, and emergency handling equipment;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN for legal/compliance memo: RUO vs IVD boundary, VAT/invoicing, product-label/expiry handling, storage documentation, liability disclaimers, no clinical-use resale, disposal/no-go rules;
- 10,000-30,000 PLN for selective purchase of one distressed unopened RUO reagent lot or option/consignment deposit;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN for targeted lab/distributor/freezer-service outbound and simple inventory/temperature-log system.

Required POC proof:
1. Three paid standby deposits or reserved-access agreements from labs that want emergency cold-chain failure capacity, rescue pickup, or priority ULT/dewar support.
2. One signed first-look or consignment/salvage agreement with a lab, distributor, freezer-service firm, or research group over named RUO/research-only lots or closing-lab cold-chain inventory.
3. One controlled cold-chain asset or committed reserved capacity: tested ULT freezer / ULT slot agreement / dry shipper / temp-log workflow with proof of temperature monitoring.
4. One purchased or consigned unopened RUO/research-only reagent lot with documented source, storage history where available, expiry, label, and no-clinical-use terms.
5. One paid emergency storage/move/rescue job or one paid resale/consignment sale to a research buyer.

Economics:
Revenue types:
- paid standby deposits / reserved cold-chain rescue access for small labs;
- emergency pickup/storage/move fee after freezer failure or lab move;
- daily/weekly ULT or dewar storage rental with temperature logs;
- margin or consignment fee on near-expiry/open-market RUO reagent lots;
- liquidation fee for closing labs/distributors;
- optional disposal coordination fee for no-go lots.

Margins can be strong because distressed RUO/research lots may be acquired cheaply and sold at a discount to research buyers, while emergency cold-chain rescue is priced against reagent/sample loss, project delays, and freezer downtime. The founder's data background helps build a searchable inventory, temperature-log records, expiry/risk grading, and buyer matching, but the asset is physical capacity and signed lot rights.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The foundational asset is titled/reserved cold-chain capacity and signed first-look or consignment rights over specific RUO lots. Service work exists to protect and monetize the asset: logging, storage, QA, routing, and resale. A competitor cannot copy a signed first-look lot or owned emergency freezer/dewar slot by launching a website.

Main risks acknowledged:
- If no labs pay standby deposits, the freezer asset may become dead equipment.
- If lots are clinical IVD or patient-use medical inventory, the regulatory burden rises sharply; first wedge should be RUO/research-only lots with no clinical-use resale.
- Temperature history may be incomplete; many lots must be rejected, discounted, or sold only with clear disclaimers.
- Near-expiry reagents may not resell fast enough.
- Cold-chain equipment can fail and create liability.
- University/public labs may have slow procurement or disposal rules.
- Distributors may already have return/liquidation routes.
- Founder must not overclaim reagent performance or clinical suitability.
- 24/7 emergency promise may be hard part-time unless response SLA is defined realistically.
