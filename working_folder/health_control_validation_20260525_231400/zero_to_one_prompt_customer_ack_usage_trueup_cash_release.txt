Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Customer-Acknowledged Usage True-Up Cash Release Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Convert already customer-acknowledged usage true-ups, annual consumption amendments, seat-count statements, API overage reconciliations, and data-license true-up invoices into collected cash for B2B SaaS, API, and data vendors where entitlement has already been accepted but AP/procurement/payment release is stuck.

Exact buyer:
B2B SaaS, API, data, infrastructure, developer-tool, and usage-priced software vendors with 20-300 employees, 1-20 enterprise customers on usage-based, seat-based, consumption, minimum-commit, annual true-up, or hybrid contracts, and at least one customer-acknowledged true-up above 100,000 PLN equivalent that is accepted commercially but unpaid operationally.

Acute trigger:
The vendor has a signed customer renewal amendment, signed true-up order form, customer-approved usage statement, AP/procurement acknowledgement, agreed overage reconciliation, or customer email accepting the amount, but cash is delayed because the customer AP/procurement process needs PO alignment, invoice evidence, usage tie-out, legal-entity mapping, vendor-master update, tax/invoice correction, procurement note, or internal approval packet.

Control point:
Signed cash-release mandate over named acknowledged true-up receivables; underlying customer contract or order form; customer acknowledgement or signed amendment; usage statement tied to the contract metric; prior invoice and payment history; PO/procurement/AP thread; corrected invoice or credit/rebill package; payment-release tracker; AP acceptance, payment schedule, remittance advice, bank receipt, or written no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five signed mandates over named customer-acknowledged usage true-ups; 1,000,000-3,000,000 PLN equivalent in acknowledged receivables screened; at least 600,000 PLN accepted into release workflow after exclusions; three customer AP/procurement acknowledgements or invoice-acceptance events; two payments scheduled, remitted, or collected above 100,000 PLN each; 50,000-150,000 PLN startup fees collected or invoiced.

6-month POC:
20-45 signed mandates across usage-priced SaaS/API/data vendors; 8,000,000-30,000,000 PLN acknowledged true-up receivables screened; 4,000,000-15,000,000 PLN accepted into release workflow; 2,000,000-8,000,000 PLN paid, scheduled, accepted by AP, corrected into buyer-approved invoice form, or cleanly no-go'd; 300,000-900,000 PLN startup revenue; gross margin above 70% after secure document handling, finance-ops contractors, channel commissions, and limited legal/accounting boundary review.

First acquisition mechanism:
Source through fractional CFOs, RevOps consultants, usage-billing implementation partners, SaaS accountants, billing-platform agencies, VC/PE portfolio operators, and customer-success leaders. Ask only for named true-ups where the customer has already acknowledged the amount through signed amendment, approved statement, order form, PO, or AP/procurement email. Reject "find our underbilling" and "help us argue entitlement" leads.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-15,000 PLN per accepted true-up release file plus 3-8% of cash paid, payment schedule accepted, or buyer-approved invoice value. Minimum single-customer acknowledged receivable is 100,000 PLN and preferred vendor mandate is 300,000+ PLN across multiple acknowledged true-ups. Success fees trigger only on documented AP acceptance, payment schedule, remittance, bank receipt, buyer-approved corrected invoice, or agreed netting, not on identified leakage.

Copy risk:
CFOs, RevOps teams, billing-platform consultants, accountants, AR teams, customer-success leaders, and fractional finance operators can copy. The defense is strict acknowledged-cash intake, signed current mandates, before-state AP/procurement evidence, repeat buyer-side release wording, contract-metric tie-out templates, and a case library of true-up invoice acceptance patterns by customer procurement system and usage model.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the general workflow, but not an already-signed mandate over a named acknowledged receivable, the timestamped before-state AP/procurement hold, the customer acknowledgement, the usage statement, the corrected invoice trail, the release packet, and the success-fee right attached to that specific payment event. The wedge depends on capturing current acknowledged true-up cash through finance and RevOps channels before the vendor gives the same file to internal AR, a billing consultant, or a generic collections process.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a named acknowledged true-up payment file converted into AP acceptance, payment schedule, remittance, bank receipt, buyer-approved corrected invoice, buyer-approved netting, or written no-go. The startup is not selling spend analytics, revenue leakage audits, pricing design, billing software, RevOps consulting, debt collection, dashboards, databases, or introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No legal advice, tax advice, accounting opinion, revenue recognition advice, debt collection, litigation, contract interpretation, customer negotiation strategy, pricing redesign, billing-platform implementation, payment processing, factoring, receivables purchase, holding client funds, or statements that a customer legally owes money. Vendor CFO/counsel decides collectability, customer relationship posture, and invoice authority. Accept only cases where customer-side acknowledgement or signed amendment/order form already exists. Reject ambiguous clauses, disputed usage, weak logs, customer-success waivers, strategic accounts where billing would harm retention, service-credit disputes, delivery disputes, tax-only issues, insolvency, old debt, and any case requiring surprise backbilling.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not PromoLeak, because this is B2B SaaS/API/data usage true-up cash accepted by the customer, not FMCG retailer deductions, promo leakage, retail-media charges, or modern-trade claims. Not Amazon Vendor Central, MDF, retail OTIF, supplier rebate, PartnerCenter, cloud marketplace remittance, KSeF, VoP, factoring reserve, PSP reserve, DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, or TraceFaktura. The distinctive control point is customer-acknowledged usage true-up receivables where entitlement is commercially resolved before intake and the work is payment-release packaging, not recovery audit.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Clean acknowledged true-ups may already be handled by competent CFO, AR, RevOps, or billing teams. Some "acknowledged" amounts may still be delayed for valid reasons such as missing PO, wrong legal entity, tax/invoice errors, budget owner turnover, procurement policy, renewal timing, or customer cash management. Customer AP may ignore an outside coordinator. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if payment would have arrived anyway. Vendors may try to submit ambiguous underbilling disputes despite the intake rule. Sensitive contract, usage, invoice, and customer data creates trust friction. Billing consultants and fractional CFOs can internalize the workflow after seeing the release packet.
