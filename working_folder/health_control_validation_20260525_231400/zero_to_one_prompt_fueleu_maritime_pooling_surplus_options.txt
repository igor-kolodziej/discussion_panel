Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
FuelEU Maritime Pooling Surplus Option Mandates

One-sentence thesis:
Secure signed 2026 reporting-year option mandates over named ships with projected FuelEU Maritime compliance surplus, then sell buyer-deposit-backed pool reservations to deficit ship operators before the 30 April 2027 verifier/database allocation deadline.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized shipping companies, ship managers, charterers with compliance responsibility, and maritime compliance brokers serving vessels above 5,000 GT that call at EU ports and expect a 2026 FuelEU Maritime compliance deficit. The first buyer is not a giant liner company with an internal desk; it is a 2-30 vessel operator, manager, or broker-client group that wants a cheaper and earlier compliance route than paying penalties or shopping for last-minute surplus near the 2027 deadline.

Acute trigger:
The buyer's 2026 voyages, fuel mix, shore-power behavior, or vessel profile indicate a likely FuelEU compliance deficit, while other ships using lower-GHG fuel, shore power, wind-assist, efficient operations, or favorable routes may generate surplus. The 2025 pooling deadline has already passed, so the product is explicitly for 2026 reporting-year forward reservations that can be verified and recorded by the selected verifier by 30 April 2027.

Control point:
Seller-side signed option or exclusive mandate over projected 2026 FuelEU surplus for named ships; responsible company authority; IMO numbers and ship list; no-double-sale covenant for the relevant reporting period; monitoring-plan and verifier status; preliminary compliance-balance calculation; fuel and voyage data availability; buyer-side reservation agreement; buyer deposit or escrow instruction; selected verifier precheck; maritime-law contract review; pool composition worksheet; allocation logic; database submission responsibility map; and clean no-go rules. The startup controls the private option package, buyer reservation, evidence room, and closing workflow for specific named surplus/deficit ships, while accredited verifiers and responsible companies handle official FuelEU database actions.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three surplus-side companies sign option mandates covering at least 8-15 named 2026 reporting-year ships with plausible positive FuelEU balances; two deficit-side buyers or broker-client groups place refundable deposits or escrow instructions totaling at least 80,000-250,000 PLN for named pool reservations; a maritime lawyer reviews the option and no-double-sale structure; an accredited verifier or verifier-facing compliance consultant completes a precheck of the pool data package; every ship has IMO number, responsible company authority, monitoring-plan status, data-access commitment, provisional balance method, and no-double-sale/no-double-pool covenant; and no buyer deposit is released to the startup until the buyer accepts the named package and verifier precheck.

6-month POC:
Control option mandates over 25-60 named surplus ships for the 2026 reporting year; secure 6-12 deficit buyer reservations; collect 250,000-900,000 PLN in option fees, reservation fees, success fees, and spread share without buying balances on the startup balance sheet; complete verifier-prechecked pool packages for at least three multi-company pools; document accepted/rejected surplus quality, buyer deposit conversion, legal no-go causes, verifier objections, and expected penalty-avoidance economics; and continue only if at least one pool package has all parties, selected verifier path, and database allocation workflow ready well before the 30 April 2027 deadline.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start on the surplus side through public fleet data, maritime decarbonization vendors, biofuel/shore-power/wind-assist case references, ship managers, small EU short-sea operators, compliance consultants, and bunker brokers that know which operators may over-comply but lack a monetization process. Pitch surplus owners: "If your 2026 FuelEU position is positive, sign a no-double-sale option over named ships. I bring deposited deficit buyers, pay only if a verifier-prechecked pool package forms, and you keep official database responsibility." On the buyer side, approach small deficit operators and their brokers with named surplus packages rather than a generic pooling introduction.

Economics:
Charge surplus sellers a small success-only placement fee or capture 5-15% of the surplus sale value; charge deficit buyers 10,000-40,000 PLN reservation/setup fee plus 5-15% of savings versus expected penalty or last-minute market price; or earn a spread where seller option price and buyer reservation allow it. The buyer pays because a FuelEU deficit can become a direct penalty or expensive late compliance purchase, while early reserved surplus reduces deadline risk. The seller pays or accepts a spread because otherwise projected surplus may be banked, undersold, or left to larger brokers.

Copy risk:
FuelEU pooling providers, maritime compliance consultants, bunker traders, ship managers, EU ETS/carbon desks, classification societies' advisory arms, and law firms can copy pieces. The defense is not general FuelEU expertise; it is signed private options over named surplus ships, buyer deposits tied to named pool reservations, local CEE/small-operator sourcing, no-double-sale controls, verifier-ready data rooms, and a private memory of which ship profiles create usable surplus without triggering verifier objections.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a surplus company signs an exclusive option over named ships for the 2026 reporting year and a deficit buyer places a deposit or escrow instruction for that named package, the startup controls that private commercial option and reservation workflow. A maritime consultant or broker can source other surplus later, but cannot instantly take the already-optioned named ships or the buyer's deposited reservation without the parties breaching or terminating the option.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell generic FuelEU advice, penalty calculators, monitoring reports, dashboards, database access, a listing board, fuel procurement, ship-management services, or broker introductions. It accepts only named surplus and deficit ships where responsible-company authority, no-double-sale option rights, buyer deposits, verifier precheck, and pool allocation workflow can be assembled into a controlled commercial package before the statutory database deadline.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as an accredited verifier, ship manager, legal adviser, fuel supplier, bunker trader, classification society, database operator, compliance authority, or penalty payer. Responsible shipping companies remain responsible for FuelEU reports, monitoring data, database registrations, pool validation, verifier selection, and compliance. Accredited verifiers handle official verification and database recording. Maritime lawyers review option, escrow, no-double-sale, liability, sanctions, KYC, and governing-law terms. Reject ships without clear responsible-company authority, verifier path, monitoring data, transferability, no-double-pool covenant, sanctions/KYC clearance, or plausible positive/negative compliance balance.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, merchant-acquirer residuals, Shopify app continuity, CleanRIPE, COD courier remittance, 3PL overbilling, RAGProof, FlexMW, REDLab, or PSP reserve release. The buyer, trigger, control point, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: FuelEU Maritime ship-level compliance-balance pooling options and buyer deposits for the 2026 reporting year, not evidence response, retailer deductions, data liberation, product compliance, physical salvage, invoice recovery, appsec, or payment residuals.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The FuelEU pooling market may already be served by specialist maritime compliance firms, bunker brokers, EU ETS/carbon desks, ship managers, and large advisory firms with much better trust and buyer access. A projected 2026 surplus is uncertain until fuel/voyage data is verified in 2027. The startup's option is private commercial control, not direct control inside the FuelEU database. Responsible companies and verifiers may reject third-party option structures. Buyers may not place meaningful deposits before final verified balances exist. Surplus sellers may prefer known brokers or bank the surplus. The founder has weak maritime credibility from Warsaw. Legal, KYC, sanctions, escrow, no-double-sale, liability, and verifier acceptance work can overwhelm a part-time solo founder. If the first packages are not truly exclusive and buyer-deposit-backed, this collapses into generic compliance brokerage.
