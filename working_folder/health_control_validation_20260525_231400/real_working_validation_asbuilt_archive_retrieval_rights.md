## Zero To One Judgment

Zero To One treated this as a differentiated controlled-source archive retrieval business, not a document-scanning service. It accepted the core insight: private design-office archives can contain drawings and technical folders that owners no longer have, and missing plans can delay renovation, fit-out, insurance review, sale, refinancing, legalization, or engineering work.

The idea still failed because release rights are messy, demand is sporadic, municipal or owner-held records may solve many cases, indexing is labor-intensive, and old documents may not be reliable as current as-built truth. The evaluator viewed it as a narrow research/pilot candidate rather than a confirmed idea.

## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Release rights are the core blocker: archive folders may contain copyrighted drawings, confidential correspondence, personal data, contractor pricing, permits, calculations, or contract-restricted documents.
2. Owners may already be legally responsible for holding building documentation, making the archive only a fallback source.
3. Municipal, authority, owner, administrator, or building-permit archives may solve many routine cases.
4. Demand discovery is difficult because buyers search by address and often do not know which architect or engineer held the archive.
5. Indexing old boxes can be labor-intensive due to missing labels, old street names, rolled drawings, multiple revisions, fragile paper, duplicates, and incomplete folders.
6. Retrieved documents may not be valid current as-built truth because buildings may have changed and drawings may be permit, tender, workshop, or partial as-built versions.
7. Retiring architects and engineers may not want to transfer custody because of professional duties, copyright concerns, reputation risk, or successor relationships.
8. Paid retrieval frequency may be too low unless archives are concentrated in high-value commercial, multifamily, MEP-heavy, or renovation-active buildings.
9. Physical storage and handling risks are real: water, fire, mold, deterioration, lost boxes, and scanning damage.
10. Liability boundaries must be strict because buyers may want to use retrieved documents for renovation, insurance, financing, permits, or engineering decisions.

## Useful Narrowing

Zero To One said the best niche is MEP and structural project archives for commercial, mixed-use, multifamily, and premium renovation-heavy buildings in Warsaw, Krakow, Wroclaw, Gdansk, and Poznan.

Only accept an archive if:

- release rights are reviewed;
- archive owner signs custody/search/release terms;
- at least 200 folders can be address-indexed;
- commercial, multifamily, MEP, or structural content is meaningful;
- storage cost is low;
- at least 5 demand-side contacts agree to test paid searches;
- no release occurs without owner authorization.

The better POC metrics are paid retrievals per 100 indexed folders, average revenue per retrieval, no-hit rate, no-release rate, indexing time per folder, and legal/review friction per release.

## Decision

Reject. Do not continue archive-custody ideas unless release rights and demand discovery are materially cleaner than private architect/engineer files.
