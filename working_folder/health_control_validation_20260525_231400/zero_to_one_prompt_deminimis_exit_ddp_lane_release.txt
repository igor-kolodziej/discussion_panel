Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DeMinimisExit DDP Lane Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Convert CEE ecommerce exporters and 3PL client lanes that relied on US low-value de minimis parcel assumptions into broker-cleared DDP or formal/informal-entry shipping lanes, with proof measured by named SKU/order lanes accepted by a licensed broker/provider, first released entries, approved landed-cost terms, or a written no-go.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE DTC brands, Shopify/WooCommerce sellers, marketplace-adjacent brands, ecommerce 3PLs, fulfillment consultants, cross-border parcel agencies, fashion/beauty/accessory/home-goods exporters, and small EU manufacturers that sell into the United States through low-value parcel flows and now face margin shock, clearance friction, returns, carrier refusals, customer cancellations, or 3PL/broker demands for importer, SKU, certificate, landed-cost, and duty/tax data.

Acute trigger:
The seller's US parcel lane no longer works under the old de minimis economics or clearance process. Orders are paused, US checkout promises are wrong, parcels are being returned or delayed, the 3PL or carrier is asking for a new broker/import lane, the brand cannot quote delivered cost, or a broker/fulfillment partner needs SKU-level HTS, country-of-origin, product description, declared value, importer/IOR route, duty/tax handling, certificate flags, and DDP terms before accepting more shipments.

Control point:
Signed lane-release mandate over named US-bound SKU/order lanes; seller order history and US GMV; SKU master with product descriptions, values, origin, supplier/manufacturer data, and known regulated-product flags; 3PL/carrier/broker blocker text; licensed customs broker or DDP/IOR provider route; landed-cost and margin approval by seller; channel-approved shipment instruction; first entry/release tracker; released entries, DDP setup, broker acknowledgement, customer-facing lane restart, or written no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three signed 3PL, agency, freight, or ecommerce-accounting channel agreements; eight prepaid seller lane triages; four accepted lane-conversion files with named SKUs, order lanes, broker route, landed-cost approval, and seller-authorized shipment plan; two live lanes producing first released formal/informal entries, DDP provider acceptance, broker acknowledgement, restarted US checkout/shipping promise, or documented no-go; 60,000-160,000 PLN startup fees collected or invoiced.

6-month POC:
40-90 paid seller lane triages; 18-40 accepted lane conversions; 10-25 live lanes with first released entries, broker/DDP acceptance, restarted US shipping promises, or paid no-go decisions; 400,000-1,200,000 PLN startup revenue; three repeat channels each sending five or more sellers; gross margin above 60% after broker/provider coordination, SKU-data contractors, channel commissions, and limited customs/legal boundary review.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with ecommerce 3PLs, fulfillment consultants, Shopify/WooCommerce agencies, parcel aggregators, DTC accountants, and freight forwarders serving CEE brands that shipped low-value goods to the US. Ask only for sellers with current US orders, order history, named SKUs, written 3PL/carrier/broker friction, and willingness to pay for a lane decision. Do not cold-sell generic tariff readiness to brands without live US order impact.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-12,000 PLN for a paid lane triage and 15,000-45,000 PLN for a conversion file tied to one product family or shipping lane, with urgent fees for lanes above 200,000 PLN annual US GMV. Optional success fee of 1-3% of the first 60 days of restarted US shipped revenue or approved landed-cost lane volume, only when broker/provider acceptance or released entries are documented. No fee is tied to duty avoidance or legal classification conclusions.

Copy risk:
Customs brokers, freight forwarders, ecommerce 3PLs, DDP/IOR providers, trade consultants, Shopify agencies, parcel aggregators, and accountants can copy pieces. The defense is current channel control, signed lane mandates, SKU/order lane evidence, broker/provider response memory, no-go patterns by product family, landed-cost templates, and the fact that many partners want a paid coordinator to prepare seller-side data before the licensed broker or provider will touch the lane.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the general workflow, but not an already-signed lane mandate over named seller SKUs and US order history, the timestamped 3PL/carrier/broker blocker, the prepared SKU master, seller-approved landed-cost decision, broker/provider response trail, and success-fee right attached to that specific restarted lane or no-go decision. The wedge is to capture messy current seller lanes through 3PL and agency channels before the seller hands the same file directly to a customs broker, forwarder, or DDP provider.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a named US-bound ecommerce lane converted into broker/provider acceptance, released entries, DDP setup, approved landed-cost shipping terms, restarted checkout/shipping promise, or written no-go. The startup is not selling customs brokerage, HTS legal opinions, tax advice, duty avoidance, software, dashboards, generic freight brokerage, importer-of-record services, or introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Licensed customs brokers, freight forwarders, IOR/DDP providers, tax advisers, and counsel remain providers of record. The startup does not classify products legally, file customs entries, act as importer of record, provide customs/tax/legal advice, hold client funds, guarantee clearance, arrange duty avoidance, advise sanctions/export controls, or certify product admissibility. Reject regulated or high-risk goods without a qualified provider route, including FDA-sensitive food/supplement/cosmetic/medical goods, CPSC-sensitive children's products, batteries/hazmat, weapons, controlled substances, counterfeit-risk goods, forced-labor traceability concerns, sanctions exposure, unclear country-of-origin, missing supplier data, and cases where the post-de-minimis margin is structurally negative.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not CBAM Import Continuity, because this is US ecommerce export lane conversion after low-value parcel/de minimis disruption, not EU CBAM declarant status, carbon-cost exposure, CN/TARIC mapping, CBAM certificates, or EU import continuity. Not CPSC eFiling, because the buyer trigger is a whole US shipping/economic lane conversion, not only product-safety certificate data. Not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central, retail deductions, KSeF, VoP, EUDR, DORA, REDBlocked, GreenTender, Data Act, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, or BioSignal.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Customs brokers, freight forwarders, 3PLs, DDP providers, and trade consultants are much closer to the decisive lane and may internalize the work. HTS classification, country-of-origin, IOR, duty/tax treatment, product admissibility, PGA flags, forced-labor risk, and certificates can require licensed or legal expertise outside scope. Some sellers will be economically dead after duties and cannot be saved. 3PLs and brokers may refuse third-party-prepared seller files or require direct seller communication. Seller data can be messy, supplier documents weak, and SKUs too low-margin for paid conversion. The disruption may normalize as brokers, platforms, and carriers productize standard lanes. The business can drift into customs brokerage, freight brokerage, tax advice, or tariff-avoidance consulting unless it accepts only live lane decisions with licensed provider routing.
