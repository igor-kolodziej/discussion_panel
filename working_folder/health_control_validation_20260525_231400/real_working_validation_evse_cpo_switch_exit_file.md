## Strongest Objections

1. New CPOs and backend providers can internalize the onboarding checklist if migration pain is frequent.
2. Old CPOs may refuse or delay session exports, payout ledgers, roaming IDs, OCPP endpoint details, firmware/configuration data, and settlement records.
3. Many migration failures are not data-exit problems; they are broken chargers, weak mobile signal, unsupported firmware, SIM/router failures, payment-terminal issues, electrical defects, UDT/metrology gaps, installation records, or warranty disputes.
4. Sites with 6-10 low-use AC chargers may not support the proposed fee unless unpaid revenue, contract pain, or a waiting new operator is present.
5. Charger ownership and authority can be messy because assets may be leased, financed, subsidized, tenant-owned, CPO-owned, or bundled into broader agreements.
6. Data Act leverage may not solve contract, payout, roaming, tariff, payment-terminal, or settlement disputes.
7. Migration success remains outside the startup's control because the new CPO, charger firmware, backend compatibility, OCPP behavior, network access, site visit, payment setup, roaming setup, and warranty status all matter.
8. Payout recovery can become accounting, legal, or contract dispute work.
9. Session history, payout ledgers, tariffs, roaming setup, customer/RFID lists, payment data, and operator contracts create security and trust friction.
10. The business can drift into EVSE repair, CPO brokerage, legal enforcement, charge-point operation, or generic charging consultancy.

## Narrowing Advice From Evaluator

Best beachhead: new CPOs, OCPP backend providers, and EVSE installers that receive messy migration leads from site owners with 10-80 chargers.

Accept only cases with owner-signed mandate, named site and charger cluster, old CPO/backend contract or account, new operator or installer intake needs, charger serial/asset list, session/payout issue or migration deadline, old-operator export request, prepaid fee, technical reviewer route, and no-go authority.

Reject broken hardware, unsafe electrical installation, unclear charger ownership, active litigation, unpaid debts, no new operator, very low-use small AC sites, payment-terminal certification issues, tax/legal disputes, and cases where the startup is asked to act as operator or broker.

## Lesson

Data Act plus current site/account control helps, but it is not enough where new operators and backend providers can internalize the workflow and many real blockers require technical migration, repair, or legal pressure. Do not continue EVSE, charging-data, or operator-switch variants unless the founder controls a pre-existing paid partner queue, a stronger recurring payment stream, or a specific payout/settlement object that incumbents cannot internalize.
