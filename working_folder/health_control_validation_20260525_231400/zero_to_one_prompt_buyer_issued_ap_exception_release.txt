Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ExceptionPay Buyer-Issued AP Release Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Large-enterprise suppliers often have earned, accepted invoice queues stuck not because the customer disputes entitlement, but because AP, procurement, or vendor-master teams issued a written exception requiring one specific non-commercial artifact before remittance; sell a narrow release lockbox that fixes the named exception and captures buyer acceptance, scheduled remittance, payment, or clean no-go.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE exporters, SaaS vendors, industrial suppliers, staffing/project suppliers, agencies, and distributors selling to large multinational, telecom, retail, university, hospital, defense-prime, or public-sector-adjacent buyers through Ariba, Coupa, SAP Business Network, Oracle, EDI, custom supplier portals, or shared-service AP systems. The first buyer has at least 150,000 PLN in accepted or commercially payable invoice value blocked by one written buyer-issued AP/procurement/vendor-master exception.

Acute trigger:
The buyer has issued written AP, procurement, portal, vendor-master, shared-service, or treasury text saying a named invoice queue is payable, accepted, approved, or ready for process, but remittance cannot be released until a specified artifact or portal state is fixed: bank validation, tax form, vendor-master legal name, PO flip, invoice portal state, goods-receipt link, service-entry link, EDI acknowledgment, remittance address, supplier onboarding task, entity evidence, or missing supplier evidence. The startup rejects vague overdue invoices and starts only from the buyer's written exception.

Control point:
Signed lockbox over a named buyer AP exception: supplier officer authorization, buyer-written exception text, invoice list and value, delivery/acceptance evidence, portal or AP ticket path, artifact checklist, before-state screenshots or exports, submission log, buyer response log, after-state accepted/cleared status, scheduled remittance or payment trace, no-go memo, and fee right. The startup works behind the supplier's authorized billing/procurement contact and does not pressure the customer, interpret disputed contract terms, or collect funds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Sign five paid mandates where the supplier provides buyer-issued AP/procurement/vendor-master exception text, named customer, invoice list, invoice value, and supplier officer authorization. Put at least 1,500,000 PLN invoice value under signed mandate. Submit artifact packets or portal corrections for all five. Obtain at least three buyer AP/procurement/vendor-master acknowledgements that the named artifact has been accepted, the exception has cleared, the portal state has changed, or the case has narrowed to a clean no-go. Obtain two scheduled remittances, paid invoices, or written no-go decisions. Collect 50,000-120,000 PLN in fixed release fees and clearly attributed success fees.

6-month POC:
Handle 30-60 paid exception files. Put 8,000,000-25,000,000 PLN invoice value under signed mandate. Produce 20-35 buyer-acknowledged accepted, cleared, scheduled-remittance, paid, narrowed, or clean no-go outcomes. Collect 300,000-900,000 PLN in fees. Establish three repeat referral channels among fractional CFOs, AR/revenue operations consultants, EDI/ERP consultants, procurement-onboarding specialists, B2B SaaS accountants, export advisers, and PE/VC portfolio operators. Maintain gross margin above 65% after secure intake, contractor admin review, translation where needed, and occasional accountant/legal boundary checks.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start through fractional CFOs, outsourced controllers, AR/revenue operations consultants, EDI and ERP implementation partners, supplier-onboarding consultants, SaaS accountants, export advisers, and PE/VC portfolio operators who see invoices stuck in Ariba, Coupa, SAP Business Network, Oracle, EDI, custom portals, and shared-service AP queues. The outreach is specific: "Send only buyer-issued AP exception text for accepted invoices above 150,000 PLN. I fix the named artifact, submit through your official contact/portal path, and return cleared status, scheduled remittance, payment trace, or clean no-go."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-18,000 PLN fixed per exception file up to 500,000 PLN invoice value, 18,000-35,000 PLN for larger or multi-artifact files, and 2-5% success fee only where the before-state exception, submission, buyer acknowledgement, and after-state remittance/payment support attribution. Fixed fees must dominate because the supplier has already earned the money and buyer timing may improve for reasons outside the startup. Costs are secure intake, artifact cleanup, portal/ticket submission support, status reconciliation, contractor admin review, and limited professional boundary review.

Copy risk:
Supplier finance teams, controllers, AR teams, accountants, EDI consultants, ERP consultants, procurement-onboarding specialists, customer-success teams, and buyer AP teams can copy much of the workflow. The defense is brutal intake discipline, active control over exact AP exception tickets, fast turnaround, buyer/portal exception memory, partner channels, and a growing no-go taxonomy. This is not defensible if it becomes generic AR chasing.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
For each accepted file, once the supplier signs the lockbox, pays the fixed fee, provides officer authorization, and routes the buyer-issued exception through the startup's evidence room, the founder controls that exact exception trail: before-state blocker, required artifact, submission log, buyer response chronology, after-state portal/AP status, remittance/payment trace, and fee trigger. Incumbents can compete for future cases, but replacing an already-paid lockbox requires rebuilding the same current-buyer evidence trail and responsibility split.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell AR outsourcing, collections, invoice auditing, a dashboard, a generic portal guide, or introductions to AP contacts. It accepts only named buyer-issued AP exceptions for accepted invoice queues. The output is a resolved payment-state object: cleared exception, accepted artifact, changed portal state, scheduled remittance, paid invoice, narrowed issue, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No debt collection, payment pressure, legal demand letters, contract interpretation, disputed entitlement, factoring, payment custody, receivables purchase, tax advice, sanctions advice, fraud review, or bank-change advocacy. The supplier remains seller of record and communicates through official supplier contacts or portals. Accountants, lawyers, banks, and customer AP/procurement teams remain responsible for tax, legal, banking, fraud-control, and contract decisions. Reject vague overdue invoices, buyer cash-management delays, missing delivery, quality disputes, price disputes, unresolved PO mismatch, customer insolvency, sanctions/UBO uncertainty, suspicious bank changes, legal assignment disputes, fake documents, or any case where the buyer has not issued a specific AP/procurement/vendor-master exception.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, KSeF, LC discrepancy, PV compensation, road-assistance event evidence, or a platform evidence desk. It is adjacent to the previously tested SupplierPortal Invoice Release Desk, but the control point is materially narrower: buyer-issued AP exception text for an already accepted invoice queue, not broad portal cleanup, AR outsourcing, or overdue-invoice chasing.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Clean buyer-issued AP exceptions may be rarer than expected; many overdue invoices hide real disputes, missing delivery proof, PO mismatch, missing goods receipt, quality rejection, tax-form problems, sanctions review, vendor fraud review, or buyer cash management. Buyer AP teams may ignore third-party coordinators and require the supplier's official billing contact to act. Supplier AR, finance, ERP, EDI, and accounting teams can copy the checklist once they see it. Portal variation across Ariba, Coupa, SAP Business Network, Oracle, EDI, and custom AP systems can make delivery manual and margin-heavy. Fraud controls around bank changes and legal-entity changes are dangerous and must be rejected unless buyer instructions are explicit and the supplier acts directly. Success-fee attribution is fragile if the buyer was already going to pay after normal processing. The startup needs high ticket discipline because small invoices make the work low-margin. Sensitive invoices, bank, tax, customer, portal, and company records create trust friction for an unknown founder. The business can degrade into ordinary collections unless intake requires a buyer-written exception, accepted invoice queue, and objective status outcome.
