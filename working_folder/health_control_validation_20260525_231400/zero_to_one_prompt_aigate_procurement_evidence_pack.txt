Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AIGate Procurement Evidence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A 5-business-day deal-release evidence pack for small B2B SaaS vendors whose live enterprise customer, procurement, security, privacy, or vendor-risk questionnaire now has an AI section that is blocking a signed deal, renewal, pilot expansion, or procurement approval.

Exact buyer:
20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors in Europe or selling into Europe that use ordinary embedded AI features, LLM APIs, RAG, AI assistants, summarization, classification, search, support automation, document processing, or agent workflows, and have a live enterprise buyer questionnaire with AI-specific questions. Best first buyers have a deal, renewal, pilot expansion, or procurement approval worth at least 100,000 PLN equivalent blocked by the AI section, but are not selling regulated high-risk decisioning in credit, insurance, HR, biometrics, clinical, law enforcement, or autonomous adverse-decision workflows.

Acute trigger:
The vendor receives a live buyer questionnaire or security/procurement follow-up asking AI-specific questions that the normal SOC2, privacy, and security packet does not answer cleanly: AI feature inventory, whether customer data trains models, model/subprocessor list, data flow to LLM providers, retention and deletion, prompt/log access, human review, output evaluation, hallucination/error handling, AI policy enforcement, model-change control, customer opt-out, incident response, RAG source controls, audit logs, transparency wording, or buyer-specific AI Act/NIST/ISO references. The deal is stalled until the vendor can answer with evidence rather than marketing copy.

Control point:
Signed live-deal evidence mandate with the buyer questionnaire, deal value/deadline, AI feature list, architecture notes, model/subprocessor records, DPA/security documents, customer-data training/no-training evidence, retention/logging settings, eval/monitoring records, human-review workflow, access-control screenshots, RAG/source-control evidence where applicable, incident/change-control notes, answer matrix, evidence-room index, buyer response wording, and no-go/escalation log. The startup controls the active AI questionnaire response packet for that buyer deadline, not legal AI Act compliance or model validation.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five SaaS vendors prepay 8,000-25,000 PLN each for live buyer AI-questionnaire blockers; every case has a named buyer, questionnaire, deal value, and deadline; at least three packs produce buyer-progress outcomes such as procurement accepting the AI section, narrowing follow-up questions, moving to security/legal review, allowing pilot expansion, approving a lower-risk deployment scope, or producing a clean no-go; one SOC2, privacy, fractional-CISO, or AI implementation partner routes a second case; gross margin stays above 65% after contractor AI/security/privacy review; fewer than 25% of accepted leads drift into legal classification, high-risk AI, DPIA, product redesign, or ISO 42001 implementation.

6-month POC:
Deliver 25-50 paid live-deal packs; collect 250,000-900,000 PLN in fees; document 15+ buyer-progress outcomes and 5 clean no-go or out-of-scope decisions; get at least four partners to send repeat cases; maintain sub-5-business-day delivery for evidence-ready vendors; build a private taxonomy of AI questionnaire patterns, accepted answer formats, buyer follow-up objections, no-training proof, model/subprocessor disclosure, RAG/source evidence, eval/monitoring evidence, human-review evidence, and deal-stage outcomes.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target SaaS founders, heads of sales, customer success leads, fractional CISOs, SOC2 consultants, privacy consultants, security-questionnaire outsourcing firms, and AI implementation agencies. Pitch only live blocked revenue: "Send the AI section of the customer questionnaire, your AI feature list, model/subprocessor records, no-training evidence, retention/log settings, eval notes, and deal deadline. I return a buyer-ready answer matrix and evidence room in five business days, or a clean no-go if the request needs legal, high-risk, DPIA, or product work."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-12,000 PLN for an evidence-ready single-product AI section, 15,000-25,000 PLN for multi-feature or RAG/agent products, and 25,000-45,000 PLN for urgent renewal or high-value enterprise deal sprints with deeper review. Contractor review costs are 2,000-8,000 PLN per case from AI security, privacy, or governance reviewers. The buyer pays because a 100,000+ PLN equivalent deal or renewal is already delayed and normal security questionnaires do not cover the new AI-specific evidence.

Copy risk:
Vanta, Drata, Secureframe, SOC2 consultants, AI governance consultants, ISO 42001 firms, privacy lawyers, fractional CISOs, and technical diligence firms can copy pieces. The defense is not generic AI compliance knowledge. It is narrow live-deal response speed, partner-routed urgent cases, accepted/rejected buyer wording memory, evidence-room taxonomy, and staying out of broad compliance implementation.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the SaaS vendor signs the live-deal mandate, prepays, and shares the buyer questionnaire, deal deadline, AI feature inventory, model/subprocessor records, evidence files, and current buyer objections, the startup controls the response packet and evidence room for that procurement event. A GRC platform or consultant would need to displace the already-built answer matrix, evidence index, review notes, and buyer-response workflow while the deal deadline is active.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live enterprise buyer AI-questionnaire blockers tied to a specific deal, renewal, expansion, or procurement deadline. The output is a deal-release artifact used in an active buyer workflow: answer matrix, AI evidence-room index, model/subprocessor table, data-flow map, no-training proof, retention/logging evidence, eval/monitoring summary, human-review evidence, access-control screenshots, RAG/source-control evidence, buyer response wording, and no-go/escalation log. It is not generic AI readiness, legal AI Act compliance, ISO 42001 consulting, model validation, a dashboard, a questionnaire automation app, a marketplace, or brokered introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification opinions, DPIAs, high-risk AI conformity assessment, ISO 42001 implementation, SOC2 certification, privacy legal opinions, model validation, safety certification, clinical/HR/credit/biometric/law-enforcement review, or guarantees of buyer approval. Vendors, lawyers, DPOs, security officers, AI governance specialists, auditors, and enterprise buyers remain responsible for legal compliance, privacy, security, contractual terms, product changes, and procurement decisions. Cases involving regulated high-risk AI, autonomous adverse decisions, clinical data, credit/insurance/HR decisioning, biometrics, law enforcement, unclear training claims, missing evidence, or buyer demands for legal opinions are declined or routed to qualified advisers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle ICT third-party-risk, Article 28/30 DORA evidence, exit plans, subcontracting, audit rights, or regulated-finance operational resilience. It is not the failed AI Model-Risk Renewal Evidence Pack because it excludes regulated-finance model validation and high-risk decisioning. It is not the failed VoiceAI Article 50 Release Pack because it is not voice-agent deployment transparency or Article 50 implementation. It is not REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BESSGuard, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
GRC platforms, SOC2 consultants, privacy lawyers, AI governance firms, and fractional CISOs can add AI-questionnaire templates quickly. Many buyer questions may require legal classification, DPIA, product changes, model validation, or high-risk AI review outside scope. Vendors may lack real evidence for no-training, retention, evals, RAG sources, logging, or human review. Enterprise buyers may not accept a third-party packet from an unknown founder. Sensitive architecture, prompt, log, and customer-data evidence creates trust friction. The product may become generic AI compliance consulting unless it rejects all non-live-deal readiness work.
