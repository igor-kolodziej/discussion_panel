Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
RAGPO Buyer-Condition Release

One-sentence thesis:
A partner-routed AI security release sprint for B2B SaaS vendors whose enterprise PO, pilot expansion, renewal, invoice payment, or production rollout is blocked by a written buyer condition requiring bounded evidence of RAG or agent prompt-injection resistance, source isolation, tool permissions, logging, human review, or lower-risk deployment scope.

Exact buyer:
SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, appsec boutiques, security-questionnaire firms, and AI implementation partners serving 20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors with non-regulated RAG, knowledge-base, document-processing, support-copilot, enterprise-search, or limited tool-using agent features. The end SaaS client must have a named enterprise buyer and a written buyer condition tied to PO release, renewal, pilot expansion, payment, production approval, or security-review clearance.

Acute trigger:
The buyer says in writing that the deal, rollout, invoice, renewal, or pilot cannot progress until the vendor provides bounded evidence for prompt injection, cross-tenant/source leakage, sensitive-source exposure, tool/action permissions, audit logs, human-review workflow, model-provider data handling, hallucination/error handling, or approved lower-risk deployment scope. Generic AI questionnaires without a commercial hold are rejected.

Control point:
Signed partner-routed case with client authorization, buyer condition text, deal/payment/renewal deadline, partner-of-record approval path, non-high-risk AI scope, sandbox or demo environment, synthetic tenant/source setup where needed, RAG corpus/sample documents, tool/action permission map, logging access, model/provider settings, human-review evidence, test limits, no-go rules, and partner-branded buyer response. The startup controls the scoped test plan, traces, evidence room, buyer-condition matrix, pass/rework/no-go log, and response packet for that commercial release event.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three appsec/SOC2/fractional-CISO partners sign routing terms; five to eight live buyer-condition release sprints are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 20,000-70,000 PLN each; every sprint has a written buyer condition, named commercial release path, deadline, and authorized test environment; at least three sprints produce buyer-progress, PO/pilot/renewal/payment progress, accepted lower-risk deployment scope, narrowed follow-up, remediation/no-go decision, or clean formal-review escalation; one partner routes a second case; gross margin stays above 60% after AI security reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Deliver 25-50 partner-routed buyer-condition releases; collect 700,000-2,000,000 PLN in wholesale, direct-bill, and urgent sprint fees; document 20+ buyer-progress or commercial-release outcomes, 8 accepted lower-risk scopes, 10 remediation/no-go decisions, and 5 repeat partners; build a private taxonomy of buyer AI security conditions, accepted trace formats, prompt-injection/source-isolation/tool-permission/logging failures, lower-risk scope language, no-go triggers, and partner-safe caveats.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, appsec boutiques, and AI implementation partners that already support AI-enabled B2B SaaS vendors. Pitch: "If a buyer has written a specific RAG/agent security condition blocking PO, renewal, payment, pilot expansion, or production rollout, route it to me. I run bounded sandbox tests, produce traces and a buyer-condition response, and return pass/rework/no-go. You remain security/privacy owner and route remediation or formal review separately."

Economics:
Charge 15,000-25,000 PLN wholesale for narrow evidence-ready RAG/source-isolation conditions, 30,000-50,000 PLN for multi-feature RAG or limited tool-agent cases, and 50,000-90,000 PLN for urgent high-value commercial release cases. AI security reviewer cost is paid per case. The SaaS vendor pays because the sprint protects a 100,000+ PLN equivalent deal, renewal, invoice, or rollout and answers a written buyer condition that a policy template cannot satisfy.

Copy risk:
AI security vendors, appsec firms, penetration testers, red-team providers, GRC platforms, AI governance firms, and partner internal teams can copy pieces. The defense is narrow written-buyer-condition intake, partner-routed trust, fast bounded test recipes, accepted/rejected buyer evidence memory, lower-risk scope language, no-go discipline, and a trace library focused on procurement/payment release rather than broad AI security.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the partner routes the case, the SaaS client authorizes testing, and the startup receives the written buyer condition, sandbox, source samples, tool-permission map, logging access, deadline, and payment guarantee, the startup controls the active evidence packet for that buyer condition. A broader AI security firm can compete later, but cannot instantly displace the in-flight partner-approved release packet without the vendor changing course.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live written buyer conditions tied to PO, payment, renewal, pilot expansion, or production approval. The output is an operational release artifact: scoped test plan, traces, condition matrix, evidence index, lower-risk deployment note, pass/rework/no-go findings, and partner-branded buyer response. It is not generic AI security consulting, a full red-team service, product remediation, legal compliance, dashboard, database, template pack, marketplace, or expert broker.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification, DPIAs, high-risk AI conformity assessment, SOC2 certification, ISO 42001 implementation, formal penetration-test certification, model validation, safety certification, remediation, production incident response, or guarantees of buyer approval. It declines regulated high-risk AI, clinical data, credit/insurance/HR decisioning, biometrics, law enforcement, autonomous adverse decisions, live production sensitive data without authorization, and buyer demands for formal certification.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle regulated-finance ICT supplier reviews. It is materially different from RAGProbe because it requires a written buyer condition tied to PO/payment/renewal/pilot/production release before intake; RAGProbe accepted broader buyer security evidence asks. It is not AIQ because it produces bounded traces and condition release artifacts, not questionnaire answer packaging. It is not REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CyberBind, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
AI security and appsec firms are natural incumbents. A written buyer condition may require remediation, formal penetration testing, certification, legal AI review, or model validation outside scope. Sensitive access remains a trust barrier. Short bounded tests can create false assurance. Tool-using agent tests require strict sandboxing to avoid side effects. Partners can internalize the workflow after seeing the method. Reviewer costs may compress margin. GRC and AI security tools may automate common checks. Buyer progress is outside founder control.
