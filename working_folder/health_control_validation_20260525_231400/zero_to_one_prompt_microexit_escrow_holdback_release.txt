Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

MicroExit Escrow Holdback Release File

One-sentence thesis:

Small-business sellers often have 50,000-500,000 PLN equivalent trapped in post-close escrow, holdback, transition-payment, or seller-note release conditions; sell a narrow release file that turns contractually due administrative conditions into a buyer/escrow-agent-ready packet while counsel and accountants remain responsible for legal, tax, and commercial advice.

Exact buyer:

Founders, owner-operators, search-fund sellers, micro-SaaS sellers, ecommerce store sellers, agency sellers, small B2B service business sellers, and small acquisition sellers with a named post-close escrow, holdback, transition payment, seller-note tranche, or deferred consideration release due within 90 days. The first buyer has at least 50,000 PLN equivalent at stake, a signed purchase or escrow agreement, release-condition text, and no active indemnity, earnout, tax, fraud, litigation, or working-capital dispute.

Acute trigger:

A release date, post-close milestone, transition checklist, asset-transfer receipt, customer-notice condition, source/domain/account transfer receipt, inventory report, training completion, or time-based escrow maturity is approaching, but the seller has not assembled a buyer/escrow-agent-ready evidence file. Cash is already held or scheduled, but payment may slip because evidence, notices, receipts, signatures, logs, or release instructions are scattered across email, data rooms, accountants, brokers, counsel, platform portals, and founder memory.

Control point:

Signed seller mandate over a named release event plus the release evidence room: release-condition excerpts, release amount, deadline, no-dispute warranty, seller authority, counterparty/escrow contact path, evidence checklist, artifact collection log, release packet, submission record, buyer/escrow/counsel acknowledgement, document-request response log, payment-date/release-instruction record, no-go memo, and fee right. The startup does not hold funds, act as escrow agent, buy receivables, broker M&A, negotiate terms, or provide legal/tax advice.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Sign 3 paid mandates from small business sellers with escrow, holdback, seller-note, or transition-payment release events due within 90 days. Each mandate includes release-condition excerpts, release value, named counterparty or escrow contact path, seller counsel/accountant contact if any, and a no-dispute warranty. Deliver 3 release files and obtain at least 2 buyer, escrow-agent, counsel, or accountant acknowledgements, document-request replies, release instructions, payment dates, partial releases, or clean no-go decisions. Produce at least 1 actual released payment or escrow instruction. Collect 45,000-120,000 PLN in fixed or release-linked fees.

6-month POC:

15-25 paid mandates. 3M-12M PLN equivalent release value under mandate. 10-18 release files submitted. 6-12 objective outcomes: release instruction, payment, partial release, narrowed document request, or clean no-go. 300,000-900,000 PLN fees invoiced or collected. Five repeat referral channels among micro-M&A brokers, search-fund operators, outsourced CFOs, small-business accountants, post-close integration advisers, and boutique deal lawyers. Gross margin above 65% after legal-template review and accountant/counsel boundary checks.

First acquisition mechanism:

Target micro-M&A brokers, search-fund operators, small-business accountants, outsourced CFOs, boutique deal lawyers, online acquisition communities, Shopify/Amazon/agency/SaaS exit advisers, and founders who sold businesses in the last 3-18 months. The first outreach is not "M&A consulting." It asks for one release file: "Do you have escrow or holdback cash due soon where the buyer or escrow agent needs proof of transfer, notice, training, account handoff, inventory, source/domain/platform transfer, or time-based maturity? I assemble the release packet and no-go split; your counsel stays counsel."

Economics:

Charge 12,000-20,000 PLN for a clean time-based release or single-condition file, 20,000-40,000 PLN for multi-asset transition or escrow files, and 40,000-65,000 PLN for urgent multi-counterparty releases above 500,000 PLN. Add a 1-3% release-linked bonus only where buyer/escrow instruction or payment timing can be attributed to the packet. No speculative contingency-only work. Cost is document assembly, secure workspace, coordinator time, optional accountant/counsel boundary review, and templates. Case economics work only if the average fixed fee exceeds 15,000 PLN and legal/accountant review stays below 35% of revenue.

Copy risk:

Lawyers, accountants, M&A brokers, outsourced CFOs, and post-close integration consultants can copy the general workflow. The edge must be narrow, fast, and seller-side: repeated micro-exit condition language, evidence checklists, release packet formats, no-go triage, and referrals from brokers whose clients are too small to justify expensive legal project management.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

For a signed release event, once the seller authorizes the startup, pays the fixed fee, shares the release-condition text, and routes evidence assembly through the startup's release room, the founder controls that specific packet, submission log, counterparty response trail, and fee right. Incumbents can compete for future files, but not easily displace a paid release file already moving through buyer or escrow-agent review.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

It accepts only named post-close payment release files with a release amount, condition text, deadline, seller authority, counterparty path, and objective outcome. The deliverable is a submitted release packet tied to payment or no-go. It does not source deals, broker businesses, negotiate terms, litigate, value companies, provide legal opinions, manage generic post-close integration, or sell software.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

No earnout interpretation, working-capital adjustment, indemnity claim, fraud, breach allegation, valuation dispute, tax clearance, litigation, non-compete enforcement, legal opinion, escrow-agent representation, debt collection, receivables purchase, or negotiation. Seller's lawyer and accountant remain responsible for legal, tax, and commercial advice. The startup does not hold escrow funds, act as escrow agent, receive buyer payments, buy claims, or represent the seller before a court or authority. Reject any file where buyer/seller disagreement, legal interpretation, tax clearance, fraud suspicion, or disputed performance is the main issue.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, KSeF, ABL borrowing-base, true-up receivable, guarantee-line, construction retention, LC discrepancy, or platform evidence rescue. The buyer is a post-close small-business seller, the trigger is contractually held post-close consideration, the control point is a release-condition evidence file, and the payment event is escrow, holdback, transition-payment, or seller-note release.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Lawyers and accountants are the natural owners of post-close escrow and holdback releases. Clean administrative files may be too easy for the seller or broker to handle, while painful files are usually legal or commercial disputes. Purchase agreements are bespoke, and release conditions may require interpretation that the startup cannot provide. Buyer cooperation is outside control, and buyers may use holdbacks as leverage. Escrow agents may only accept instructions from counsel or named parties. Direct seller outreach can look opportunistic after an exit. Micro-M&A deal flow may be too small or hidden unless brokers route cases. Fixed fees may feel high if the seller expects the money anyway. Success-fee attribution is weak if release happens on a maturity date regardless of the packet. Data rooms include sensitive company, employee, customer, contract, and financial records.
