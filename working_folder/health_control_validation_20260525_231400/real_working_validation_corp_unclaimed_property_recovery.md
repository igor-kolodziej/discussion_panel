## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Official claiming is free, and the pitch must disclose that the company can claim directly.
2. Finder rules vary by jurisdiction; some states cap fees, require disclosures, or regulate locator contracts.
3. Outreach that says "we found money for your company" looks like a classic scam pattern.
4. Public records may not show enough value to pre-filter reliably above USD 25,000 equivalent.
5. Corporate ownership evidence can become slow: old names, mergers, dissolved subsidiaries, former addresses, tax IDs, officer authority, board resolutions, notarization, apostille, and translations.
6. Claim processing can take months, so the 60-day proof should not depend on recovered cash.
7. Success-fee collection is exposed because the state pays the company directly.
8. US asset locators and unclaimed-property investigators already have better state-specific experience, mailing workflows, and databases.
9. The market is mostly one-time and weakly recurring.
10. Successor ownership, securities, medallion guarantees, tax, dissolved entities, bankruptcy, and disputed M&A ownership can require lawyers.

## Lesson

Pre-identifying a public cash asset is not enough when the category is scam-adjacent, state rules are fragmented, payout timing is slow, and recurrence is weak. Future claim-recovery candidates need a clearer repeat payment stream, faster neutral-payer acknowledgement, and less trust stigma.
