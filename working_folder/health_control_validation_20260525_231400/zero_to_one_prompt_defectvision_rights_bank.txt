Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DefectVision Industrial Defect-Image Rights Bank.

One-sentence thesis:
Machine-vision AI vendors, inspection integrators, and industrial QA software teams will pay for exclusive inspection access or licensing rights to rare, non-personal, non-health industrial defect-image archives that factories, QA labs, and vision integrators already possess but have never packaged for external model evaluation, demo, or benchmark use.

Exact buyer:
European machine-vision software vendors, AI inspection startups, camera/integrator firms, quality-inspection tool vendors, and industrial automation consultancies building or selling defect-detection systems for non-health, non-defense, non-safety-critical manufacturing categories such as packaging, labels, textiles, metal surfaces, molded plastic parts, ceramics, glass, SMT/PCB assembly, coatings, CNC tool wear, additive manufacturing, and general industrial surface defects. The buyer is the product lead, founder, dataset lead, sales engineering lead, or CTO who needs credible defect examples for model evaluation, demos, pre-sales benchmarks, or internal test sets.

Acute trigger:
The buyer is trying to sell or validate an inspection model but lacks enough real negative examples: rare defect classes, messy factory images, rejected-part photos, before/after QA examples, and labeled pass/fail evidence. Public datasets are too generic, synthetic defects do not convince customers, and the buyer's own pilots do not yet contain enough failures. The buyer wants inspection access to a real archive before committing to a paid license, not a marketplace subscription.

Control point:
The startup signs exclusive options or limited licensing mandates over named, source-controlled defect-image archives from factories, QA labs, machine-vision integrators, inspection service firms, or retiring quality consultants. The controlled asset is the rights file: source authority statement, archive description, excluded customers/products, non-personal-data screen, trade-secret redaction plan, permitted use terms, sample index, label/metadata quality notes, buyer inspection-room access, deposit terms, final license scope, and no-go list for restricted categories.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, secure two source-side archive options covering at least 5,000 usable candidate images across at least three defect classes each, with written authority to show a redacted sample to buyers. Secure at least two buyer inspection deposits of 5,000-15,000 PLN each for access to sample indexes and a controlled review room. At least one buyer must convert to a paid limited evaluation license, paid benchmark license, or written no-go citing dataset quality. No speculative archive cleanup happens before source rights and buyer deposits are signed.

6-month POC:
Control 8-12 source archives across at least four non-health industrial categories, build redacted indexes for 50,000-150,000 candidate images, obtain 12-20 buyer inspection deposits, close 5-8 paid licenses, and collect 150,000-400,000 PLN revenue. Keep at least 60% gross margin after source revenue share, redaction, metadata cleanup, legal template review, secure data room, and sample QA. Reject categories with personal data, medical/IVD/clinical use, defense/weapons, product-safety certification, customer-identifying secrets that cannot be redacted, or source contracts that block external licensing.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with machine-vision integrators, QA labs, NDT/inspection service firms, packaging/print inspection consultants, and factories that already use visual inspection but have old rejected-part photos or abandoned POC data. The first source pitch is: "I do not need your trade secrets or customer names. I option a redacted defect-image archive only if buyers place inspection deposits." The first buyer pitch is: "I control a named archive of real industrial defect images with source permission. You can inspect labels and sample quality under NDA before licensing."

Economics:
Charge 5,000-15,000 PLN for controlled inspection access, 20,000-80,000 PLN for a limited evaluation or benchmark license, and 50,000-150,000 PLN for broader category-specific licensing where rights and quality justify it. Sources receive a revenue share or fixed license fee only after buyer money arrives. Costs are legal template review, source screening, redaction, secure data room, metadata cleanup, and limited QA contractors. The founder does not build models, certify inspection accuracy, sell production training data as guaranteed, or handle regulated product-safety decisions.

Copy risk:
Factories, integrators, inspection firms, machine-vision vendors, and data brokers can copy the general idea. The defense is transaction-specific control: exclusive source options over named archives, buyer deposits before cleanup, source-specific metadata knowledge, and category-by-category archive quality memory. Once a source archive is under exclusive option and a buyer has paid for inspection, competitors cannot license that same archive during the option window.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder holds the signed source option, redaction rights, permitted-use terms, sample index, secure inspection room, buyer deposit, and license negotiation for a named archive, another vendor cannot control that same archive before the current option or inspection window closes. Incumbents can source future archives, but not the exact controlled dataset rights already under option.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell consulting, model-building, dashboards, public dataset access, generic introductions, a marketplace, or reports about AI. It controls named source-side defect-image rights and sells inspection access or limited licenses to specific archives. The proof artifact is a signed archive option, redacted sample index, buyer deposit, controlled inspection room, license agreement, and paid source/buyer transaction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No human health, clinical, IVD, patient, biometric, genetic, defense, weapons, law-enforcement, facial, employee-monitoring, consumer-surveillance, or safety-certification datasets. No personal data unless independently reviewed and removed. No trade-secret disclosure without source approval. No promise that a dataset will train a production model, certify inspection performance, satisfy a customer audit, or be representative of all processes. Buyers must evaluate fitness for their own use. Source companies warrant authority only to the extent reviewed in the source agreement. Legal counsel reviews template rights, confidentiality, permitted use, data-room terms, and exclusions before paid licensing.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from BioSignal and SomnoConsent-style health-data concepts because it excludes health, clinical, biometric, patient, and regulated medical data. It is distinct from Data Act Machine Data Liberation because it does not request machine-generated operational data from OEMs under statutory access rights; it controls voluntary source-side image archive options. It is distinct from DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, Supplement Stack, and health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Industrial defect data may be too process-specific for third-party buyers. Buyers may prefer collecting data from their own pilots. Factories may refuse release because of trade secrets, customer confidentiality, brand risk, or fear of exposing quality problems. Source authority may be unclear if integrators collected images under customer contracts. Archive quality may be poor: weak labels, duplicates, inconsistent lighting, unknown camera setup, missing metadata, unlabeled defect severity, no ground truth, and nonrepresentative examples. Data buyers may demand broader rights than sources will allow. Inspection deposits may be hard before buyers see enough samples. The business can become bespoke data brokerage unless source exclusivity, buyer deposits, and category memory compound.
