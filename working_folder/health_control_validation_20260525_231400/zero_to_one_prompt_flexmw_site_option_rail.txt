Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
FlexMW Site Option Rail

One-sentence thesis:
Pre-sign neglected mid-size Polish industrial, logistics, cold-storage, water/wastewater, food-processing, HVAC, and commercial-load sites into exclusive flexibility options with meter-data authorization, then route only aggregator-accepted kW/MW to licensed DSR/flexibility aggregators for bounty, option, or revenue-share cash.

Exact buyer:
The paying buyer is a licensed or established DSR/capacity-market/flexibility aggregator, energy services company, balancing-service provider, or aggregator partner that needs qualified sites for Polish capacity-market, DSR, ancillary-service, balancing, congestion, or commercial flexibility programs. The source site is a mid-sized Polish facility with controllable load, backup generation, refrigeration, pumps, HVAC, compressed air, process scheduling, water/wastewater assets, batteries, or other operational flexibility that is too small or neglected for direct enterprise-sales focus.

Acute trigger:
Aggregators need additional qualified flexible load and site options for current or upcoming capacity-market, balancing, ancillary-service, or commercial flexibility programs, while many mid-sized facilities have eligible load but no owner, energy manager, or broker pushing enrollment. The site trigger is a high-energy-cost review, ESG/efficiency project, operations-capex planning, or CFO interest in non-core revenue without buying equipment.

Control point:
Signed site exclusivity and data-release option over a named facility; 15- or 60-minute meter data where available; load/process inventory; curtailment-window notes; operational no-go limits; contact authorization; authorization to submit the site to named aggregators; preliminary baseline/flexibility estimate; aggregator-specific qualification checklist; aggregator bounty or option agreement; and a rule that the startup earns only when an aggregator accepts, deposits, or pays for the named site's kW/MW opportunity.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Ten facilities sign 60-90 day exclusivity and meter-data authorization covering at least 3 MW of preliminary curtailable or shiftable load; two aggregators sign bounty, referral, option, or revenue-share terms for accepted sites; at least five sites provide usable interval data and operational flexibility notes; at least three sites receive written aggregator qualification, site-visit request, preliminary accepted kW/MW estimate, bounty approval, or option deposit; at least 40,000 PLN is collected from aggregator bounties, option deposits, or first accepted-site payments; and no site is counted without signed exclusivity and data rights.

6-month POC:
Control 40-80 signed site options totaling 15-40 MW preliminary flexibility; deliver 20-30 aggregator-accepted sites; collect 200,000-700,000 PLN in bounty, option, and revenue-share payments; prove at least three aggregators accept multiple sites; keep average founder sourcing/prequalification time under five hours per accepted site; maintain a private map of site types, load shapes, curtailment windows, baseline issues, aggregator acceptance criteria, rejection reasons, revenue ranges, and owner objections; and keep all dispatch, market operations, metering settlement, balancing responsibility, and performance obligations with the aggregator.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of cold stores, food processors, water/wastewater operators, logistics warehouses, refrigerated warehouses, municipal utilities, commercial HVAC-heavy buildings, plastics/packaging plants, compressed-air-heavy manufacturers, and facilities with backup generators or batteries. Outreach: "If you can pause, shift, or reduce specific loads without hurting operations, I can prequalify the site for DSR/flexibility revenue. You sign only a short exclusivity/data authorization. Aggregators handle market participation, dispatch, settlement, and compliance; I get paid only if your site is accepted."

Economics:
Aggregator-side revenue comes from accepted-site bounties, option deposits, or 10-25% of the startup's share of first-year aggregator/site revenue, not from site consulting fees. A mid-sized site accepted for 250 kW-1 MW can justify several thousand to tens of thousands of PLN in bounty or expected revenue-share value if aggregator programs pay enough. Founder costs are sourcing, site screening, meter-data handling, technical prequalification by a contractor, and legal templates. No hardware, energy trading, or balance-sheet commitment is required.

Copy risk:
Aggregators, energy brokers, ESCOs, auditors, utilities, and energy consultants can source sites directly. The defense is narrow transaction control: signed site exclusivity, meter-data authorization, aggregator-specific prequalification packet, and accepted-site bounty/payment terms before competitors see or control that facility. The startup is not trying to beat aggregators at market operations; it controls neglected site options earlier and cheaper.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a site signs exclusivity and meter-data authorization and the startup submits a named facility to an aggregator under bounty/option terms, competitors cannot enroll the same load during the exclusivity window without the site breaching the signed option. They can source other sites, but not the already controlled facility, interval data, operations contact, and accepted-site payment stream.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell energy audits, DSR advice, software, dashboards, facility reports, or a marketplace of leads. It controls a signed site option and meter-data authorization, routes that exact facility through aggregator qualification, and gets paid only when the aggregator accepts, deposits, or pays for the named site's flexibility.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not trade energy, act as a licensed supplier, become the DSR aggregator of record, provide balancing services, guarantee revenue, operate dispatch, certify baselines, install metering, advise on legal/regulatory eligibility, or assume performance penalties. Aggregators, energy suppliers, BRPs/BSPs where relevant, metering providers, site owners, lawyers, and technical contractors remain responsible for market participation, dispatch, settlement, metering, contracts, compliance, operational safety, and performance obligations. Reject sites with unsafe curtailment, unclear authority, missing data, critical-care load, regulated public-service constraints without qualified review, or operations teams unwilling to define no-go windows.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not GridSlot Host Site Option Lockbox, GridFund inverter options, white-certificate cashout, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, CBAM, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, Supplement Stack, Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL, OTA, RMA, PSP reserve, merchant-acquirer residuals, Shopify app buyout, or any evidence desk. The distinct control object is a signed operating-site flexibility option with meter-data authorization and aggregator-accepted kW/MW payment, not a grid-connection right, energy-efficiency certificate, regulatory evidence packet, recovery claim, app subscription, or physical asset lot.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Aggregators already have sales teams and may not need or pay an unknown scout. Big attractive sites may already be enrolled. Mid-sized sites may not understand DSR, may distrust exclusivity, or may refuse meter-data access. Site flexibility can be technically weak after real baseline, process, penalty, or operations review. Aggregator acceptance and market rules are outside founder control. The business can collapse into low-margin lead generation unless exclusivity and bounty terms are real. Part-time execution is difficult if site walkthroughs, data cleanup, technical screening, and owner education consume time. Revenue may be delayed until aggregator contracts or market delivery rather than paid on site acceptance.
