Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
PatternServe Horeca Serviceware Lot Bank

One-sentence thesis:
Premium hotels, restaurants, event venues, and caterers often need exact discontinued plate/bowl/cup patterns to keep a coherent service, but closure and refresh lots are split across auctions and small sellers; a founder can control compact exact-pattern lots and resell them only after buyer deposits.

Exact buyer:
Premium independent hotels, boutique hotel groups, restaurant groups, wedding/event venues, caterers, and serviced hospitality operators in Poland/CEE/EU that already use a named ceramic/porcelain/stoneware pattern and need exact replacements or expansion.

Acute trigger:
The buyer has an upcoming season, event calendar, opening, refurbishment, VIP service standard, brand audit, or banquet contract and lacks enough matching plates, bowls, cups, saucers, or serving pieces because the manufacturer discontinued the pattern; official replacement MOQ/lead time is too high; the buyer inherited a pattern from a previous operator; breakage has reduced full-service counts; mixed substitutes look visibly inconsistent; or a new room/event capacity requires more place settings.

Control point:
The founder controls titled or optioned exact-pattern lots: maker, series, pattern, color, backstamp, size, and SKU where identifiable; counted pieces by type; condition grade and chip/crack/scratch breakage allowance; photo/video proof; packing and breakage reserve; seller no-split agreement; buyer deposit for full or partial lot before final purchase where possible.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days: four signed supplier-side options or titled lots totaling at least 600 usable pieces across two to four named patterns; three buyer deposits of 2,000-10,000 PLN for exact-pattern replacements; one paid handoff of at least 100 pieces; no purchase above 20,000 PLN without buyer deposit coverage; documented gross margin after breakage reserve, packing, shipping, and unsold residue.

6-month POC:
20-35 controlled lots; 4,000-8,000 pieces processed; 20-40 buyer transactions; 180,000-600,000 PLN revenue; 40-60% gross margin after acquisition, cleaning, grading, breakage reserve, and shipping; at least 8 repeat buyers or hospitality procurement/restoration/refurbishment partners; inventory turns under 45 days on accepted patterns.

First acquisition mechanism:
Supply: monitor hotel/restaurant closures, auctions, insolvency listings, venue refurbishments, catering-company refreshes, liquidation sellers, and premium hospitality dealers; contact hotel operations managers and caterers before refresh/closure lots are split; offer fast pickup/purchase for coherent exact-pattern lots with counted pieces.

Demand: build a target list of 150 premium venues and caterers with visible tableware patterns from menus, social media, banquet photos, hotel breakfast photos, and supplier references; send a concrete offer only when a controlled pattern matches their current service; ask for exact pattern gaps and deposits, not general interest.

Economics:
Illustrative lot: acquire 300 coherent pieces at 5-18 PLN per usable piece; spend 2-5 PLN per piece on cleaning, grading, packing, and breakage allowance; sell at 18-70 PLN per piece depending on maker, pattern, condition, rarity, and buyer urgency; target 40-60% gross margin after residue.

The buyer value is not cheap plates. It is avoiding visible mismatch, event-service degradation, long manufacturer lead times, and full pattern replacement.

Copy risk:
Copy risk is real from hospitality dealers, auction buyers, tableware distributors, event-rental firms, and online replacement marketplaces.

The defense is controlling coherent named-pattern lots before they are split and matching them to existing venues with buyer deposits, not listing random used plates.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the category, but they cannot sell a specific lot once the founder has title or exclusive option, no-split agreement, counted condition packet, buyer deposit, and packing/handoff plan.

If the founder cannot secure exact-pattern demand before or immediately after lot control, the business should be killed.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It is a titled compact inventory bank. The first proof is controlled exact-pattern serviceware plus buyer deposits and paid handoff. It is not hospitality consulting, restaurant design, procurement advice, an auction marketplace, a dashboard, or a brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No claims of food-safety certification beyond normal used-serviceware condition disclosure. No cracked, unsafe, heavily chipped, unknown-contamination, or non-cleanable pieces. No antique lead-glaze/unsafe decorative wares for food service unless sold only as decorative with explicit terms. No speculative bulk storage beyond deposit-backed lots. Clear breakage/DOA terms and inspection window. No brand-counterfeit or unclear ownership lots.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not HeritageDoor: this is compact hospitality serviceware for operating venues, not architectural salvage for pre-war residential renovation.

Not hotel FF&E: this excludes furniture, fixtures, and bulky hotel liquidation; only compact exact-pattern serviceware with buyer deposits qualifies.

Not StainedGlass, StormTile, or FacadePatch: no installer acceptance, code, monument, or building-system risk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Replacement tableware dealers and online marketplaces already exist. Many venues can switch patterns, buy new, or accept near matches. Exact-pattern demand is private and hard to discover before sourcing. Condition grading, chips, glaze wear, dishwasher wear, scratches, and breakage can destroy margin. Shipping fragile lots creates breakage and dispute risk. Buyer urgency may be lower than assumed unless a major event, opening, or brand standard is imminent. ACV may be too low unless lots are large and premium. The business could become ordinary used tableware dealing unless it enforces exact-pattern, buyer-deposit discipline.
