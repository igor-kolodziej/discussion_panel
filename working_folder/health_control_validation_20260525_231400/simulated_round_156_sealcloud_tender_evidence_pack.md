Date: 2026-05-29
Real browser gate: working Zero To One >=85, fresh Zero To One >=85

## Search Frame

Round 155 showed that cash recovery is still capped when the recoverable pool is ambiguous. This round tests a live procurement gate with a strong external forcing function: the European Commission's 2026 sovereign-cloud procurement and Cloud Sovereignty Framework/SEAL scoring have turned cloud sovereignty from policy language into concrete public-sector tender evidence.

The lead candidate serves cloud/SaaS/hosting/managed-service providers whose public-sector or regulated-enterprise tender is blocked by sovereignty evidence: jurisdiction, control, operations, supply chain, technological openness, security, environmental criteria, compliance, subcontractors, data residency, support location, key management, exit/portability, and provider evidence.

Source notes checked before simulation:

- European Commission, 17 April 2026: sovereign-cloud tender awarded for up to EUR 180 million over 6 years; eligibility required SEAL-2 level, and the framework measures eight sovereignty objectives including strategic, legal, operational, environmental, supply-chain, technological openness, security, and EU-law compliance.
- European Commission, 10 October 2025: the tender established a benchmark for applying sovereignty in practice and describes the Cloud Sovereignty Framework as a public-sector reference point.
- European Commission Cloud Sovereignty Framework PDF: tender specifications can indicate minimum SEAL levels and require supporting evidence from tenderers.

## Lead Candidate

**SEALCloud Procurement Evidence Lane**

### Internal Simulated Score

88 / 100

### Why It Clears Simulation

- The timing is unusually sharp: the Commission's April 2026 sovereign-cloud award gives the market a visible benchmark and language for tenders.
- The buyer pain is current economic access: tender, framework, renewal, or regulated-enterprise procurement can be delayed or lost if sovereignty evidence is not buyer-ready.
- The artifact is concrete and repeatable: SEAL/objective matrix, evidence index, jurisdiction map, support/operations map, subcontractor/supply-chain table, key-management summary, exit/portability evidence, cloud-provider evidence pack, gap/no-go split, and buyer response wording.
- It is not legal advice or architecture migration; it organizes existing evidence and flags legal/security/cloud-architecture decisions for advisors.
- The proof is measurable within 60 days: paid live tender cases, accepted/progressed submissions, buyer-narrowed follow-up, repeat partner route, and gross margin.

### Internal Objections

- Public-sector cloud sovereignty may become a consulting/law-firm/bid-adviser market quickly.
- If the provider is structurally non-sovereign, no evidence pack can fix the architecture.
- Some cases require legal positions on non-EU law exposure, ownership/control, and contractual safeguards.
- Small providers may not see enough live tenders; the first target must include cloud providers, MSPs, SaaS vendors, and bid/procurement advisers already receiving requests.

### Gate Decision

Advance to working Zero To One validation because the simulated score is strictly above 87 and the candidate has a live procurement gate with stronger timing than ordinary evidence-desk variants.
