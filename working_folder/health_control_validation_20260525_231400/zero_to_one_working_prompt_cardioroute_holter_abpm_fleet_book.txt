Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, and patient-data businesses.

Scored idea:

CardioRoute Holter/ABPM Fleet-Book Buyout buys or options a retiring, overloaded, or under-digitized Polish Holter ECG / ABPM micro-operator's existing B2B fleet-book. The acquired asset is not a clinic, telemedicine app, diagnostic report business, patient data business, or marketplace. It is the physical and administrative continuity layer around ambulatory cardiology diagnostics: titled Holter ECG and ABPM devices, cuffs, cables, chargers, docks, shipping cases, software stations where transferable, service/certificate files, CE/instruction files, cleaning logs, accessory compatibility map, booking phone/domain/mailbox, clinic contacts, deposit/payment terms, route/courier notes, and seller-endorsed customer handoff.

Licensed clinics and doctors remain provider of record. CardioRoute does not diagnose, interpret ECG/BP recordings, prescribe, advertise medical claims to patients, modify reports, or sell patient data. During the POC it only supplies tested device capacity and admin/rental continuity to clinics whose paid Holter/ABPM slots are delayed by device failure, overflow, missing accessories, cleaning-cycle bottlenecks, or courier/admin failure.

Buyer:
- small private cardiology clinics;
- POZ/private primary-care clinics with cardiology referrals;
- occupational-medicine clinics serving aging workers;
- local diagnostic clinics that already sell paid Holter/ABPM slots but have device overflow or downtime;
- clinic groups where 50-60+ and pre-elderly patients create recurring cardiac and blood-pressure monitoring demand.

Acute trigger:
Paid Holter/ABPM appointment slots are canceled, delayed, or capped because a recorder, cuff, cable, charger, dock, software station, cleaning cycle, courier route, or admin handoff breaks. The clinic loses appointment revenue and patient trust. The clinic does not need a new consumer telemedicine platform; it needs working local devices and a reliable admin/rental overflow layer.

Control point:
The acquired asset is immediate custody of working local devices plus signed clinic payment flow before closing:
- title or conditional title to 6-10 working Holter ECG and ABPM devices;
- cuffs, cables, chargers, electrodes/accessories, shipping cases, cleaning supplies, docks, and compatibility map;
- serial files, service history, CE/instruction files, cleaning logs, and maintenance notes;
- software/readout station access only where legally and technically transferable;
- booking phone/domain/mailbox and clinic contact list;
- clinic deposit/payment terms, route/courier notes, and seller-mediated customer handoff;
- prepaid continuity/overflow agreements before final close.

A competitor can buy random devices, but cannot instantly acquire this exact clinic route, seller trust, device serial history, accessory compatibility map, booking phone/domain continuity, and prepaid clinic payment flow.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option fee to the seller;
- 35,000-60,000 PLN reserved for acquisition/option of 6-10 working devices and accessory kits, paid meaningfully only after prepaid clinic cash clears;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN legal/device-transfer/insurance/contract review;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN cleaning, testing, accessory replacement, courier cases, labels, booking workflow, and basic fleet tracking;
- remaining seller consideration structured as earnout from retained rental gross margin.

Required POC proof:
1. One signed conditional acquisition option under 100,000 PLN over a named micro-operator's fleet-book covering devices, accessories, service files, booking phone/domain/mailbox, route notes, clinic contacts, seller transition support, and payment/deposit handoff.
2. Six to ten working Holter/ABPM devices checked by serial number, accessories, service history, CE/instruction files, cleaning logs, software-transfer status, and legal/technical usability.
3. Five clinics or occupational-medicine buyers sign continuity/overflow/rental agreements before close.
4. At least 25,000 PLN prepaid rental/deposit cash clears from those clinic buyers before close.
5. One live week of overflow or failure-cover rentals is completed without founder access to diagnostic interpretation or patient-report content.
6. Written boundary: clinics/doctors remain provider of record; CardioRoute only supplies tested device capacity, accessory readiness, pickup/drop-off/admin flow, and fleet continuity.
7. Kill if the first acquisition is only hardware without prepaid clinic flow, or if device/software transfer is blocked, or if clinics expect the founder to handle reports/interpretation.

Economics:
The business is acquisition-led, not ad-led. The first customers are inherited through seller handoff. The first cash comes from prepaid clinic overflow/loaner agreements before closing. Clinics pay because a missed Holter/ABPM slot means lost revenue, staff friction, rescheduling, and patient dissatisfaction. The founder starts with asset utilization and deposit discipline rather than building a patient-facing brand.

Initial pricing logic:
- monthly clinic continuity/overflow access fee;
- per-device weekly rental fee for Holter/ABPM overflow;
- deposit for devices and accessories;
- rush accessory/cuff/cable replacement fee;
- paid pickup/drop-off/courier route;
- seller earnout only from retained rental gross margin.

Gross-margin logic:
The startup controls scarce local working devices and clinic payment flow. Gross margin is driven by utilization, deposits, repeated clinic rentals, accessory control, and strict exclusion of diagnosis/reporting. It should not buy devices speculatively; it only closes after prepaid clinic cash proves the book is real.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The foundational asset is acquired custody of a local B2B fleet-book: titled Holter/ABPM devices, serial/service files, accessories, booking phone/domain/mailbox, clinic route notes, seller handoff, deposit/payment flow, and prepaid clinic rental agreements. Service work exists only to keep the acquired fleet available and moving. It is not a patient acquisition funnel, not a diagnostic interpretation service, and not a generic device marketplace.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- Do not advertise medical claims to patients or operate as a diagnostic clinic.
- Clinics/doctors remain provider of record and handle patient consent, fitting instructions, recordings, interpretation, and reports.
- CardioRoute should avoid patient-identifiable recordings and report content during the POC.
- Device transfer, distribution/rental obligations, instructions, labeling, cleaning, service files, insurance, and contract terms need legal/device review.
- If a software station or cloud readout path is non-transferable, exclude it or require the clinic/seller to retain provider-of-record access.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Strong incumbents and tele-Holter operators exist.
- Clinics may choose to buy devices rather than rent.
- Device service, cleaning, CE/instruction, software-license, or accessory issues may kill the acquisition.
- If founder touches recordings/reports, the idea becomes regulated healthcare operations and should be rejected.
- If clinic prepayments fail, the acquired book is weak.
- If utilization is low, device ownership ties up capital.
- If the seller has only hardware and no clinic route/payment flow, the idea is just a small equipment rental shop.
