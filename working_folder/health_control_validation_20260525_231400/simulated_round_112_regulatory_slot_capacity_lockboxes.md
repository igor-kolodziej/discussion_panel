## Simulated Pass Candidate

### Smart Tachograph G2V2 LCV Slot Bank - 91

One-sentence thesis: a buyer-deposit-backed slot bank that secures authorized smart tachograph version 2 installation capacity and device availability for Polish/CEE light commercial fleets with 2.5-3.5 tonne vans that must be equipped before the 1 July 2026 cross-border transport deadline.

Buyer: Polish and CEE courier subcontractors, express freight operators, small international transport companies, trades/logistics firms, and marketplace-delivery operators running vans above 2.5 tonnes and up to 3.5 tonnes in international transport or cabotage for hire or reward. Best first buyers have 5-80 vans, cross-border revenue in July/August, and no internal transport-compliance team.

Acute trigger: from 1 July 2026, in-scope light commercial vehicles used in international goods transport or cabotage must be equipped with a smart tachograph version 2 and drivers must use driver cards/records. A missed installation risks cancelled cross-border jobs, roadside fines, vehicle downtime, or subcontractor delisting. The date is close enough that authorized workshop slots and device availability are the scarce current asset.

Transferable control point: dated authorized workshop installation slots, confirmed device/fitment availability, VIN/vehicle register, fleet deposit, installation quotation, workshop acceptance, customer payment direction, and post-install certificate/calibration handoff for named vehicles. The startup controls a slot calendar tied to VINs and deposits, not a generic lead list.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:

- 3-5 authorized tachograph workshop slot-block agreements covering at least 60 named van installation slots before and immediately after 1 July 2026.
- Written confirmation of G2V2 device and fitment availability for the accepted vehicle models.
- 30-50 fleet-paid deposits tied to named VINs, dates, and workshops.
- At least 10 completed installs or workshop-issued installation/calibration certificates.
- At least 40,000-100,000 PLN collected in slot/admin margin, deposits, or fleet coordination fees.
- Signed customer terms stating that authorized workshops remain installer/calibrator/provider of record and fleets remain responsible for legal scope and driver compliance.

First acquisition mechanism: call authorized tachograph workshops and ask for dated LCV G2V2 capacity they can sell in blocks if the founder brings named VINs and prepaid fleets. In parallel, contact courier subcontractor groups, fleet accountants, transport associations, van leasing brokers, cross-border marketplace-delivery operators, and small international freight firms with a concrete offer: "send your VIN list and deposit by date X; receive a named workshop slot and device-fitment confirmation before the July deadline."

Gross margin and payback logic: the workshop charges the fleet for installation/calibration/device work or sells a wholesale slot/device bundle. The startup earns a 350-900 PLN per-vehicle coordination margin, 5-10% device/slot spread, or 2,500-8,000 PLN fleet scheduling fee for multi-vehicle batches. A 50-van first block can produce 25,000-60,000 PLN gross margin quickly with low inventory risk if deposits precede workshop commitment. The buyer pays because one delayed van or lost cross-border route can cost more than the slot margin.

Copy risk: authorized workshops, telematics providers, fleet compliance firms, transport accountants, and tachograph equipment distributors can copy the general offer. The defense is deadline speed, named slot/device/VIN control, payment-before-slot discipline, workshop capacity already blocked, and direct access to fragmented courier/LCV fleets that are not being proactively scheduled.

Why incumbents cannot copy before control is secured: once the startup controls a dated workshop slot block, matching G2V2 device availability, named VIN list, fleet deposits, and payment direction, competitors cannot use that specific capacity unless the workshop or fleet breaks the booking. The asset is perishable and date-specific, but that is the point: before 1 July 2026, the scarce unit is not advice, it is a named install slot.

Why this is not a service/report/app/dashboard/marketplace/generic broker: the startup does not sell compliance advice, a checklist, telematics software, a marketplace of workshops, or a dashboard. It sells controlled installation capacity for named vehicles: dated slot, device-fitment confirmation, deposit, workshop handoff, and installation/certificate status. The proof is completed installs and certificates for VINs before the deadline.

Reasons this could still fail:

- Authorized workshops may not reserve capacity for an unknown founder.
- Workshops and telematics distributors can sell directly to fleets.
- Many serious fleets may already have slots booked before the founder starts.
- The deadline spike is short-lived; recurring revenue after July must come from calibration/download/calendar books or future transport-compliance slot events.
- Fitment can be messy by van model, device availability, wiring, workshop authorization, and calibration requirements.
- Driver-card and driving-time compliance can create legal/admin scope creep.
- Part-time founder must avoid day-of operational chaos and only sell prepaid named slots.
- Margin per van may be too thin if the founder cannot secure block pricing or fleet scheduling fees.

Decision: advance this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
