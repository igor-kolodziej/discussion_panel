Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: HotelRoom FF&E Package Lot Bank

One-sentence thesis:
Option or title complete matched hotel-room FF&E packages from renovation and closure deadlines, preserve them as room-equivalent lots, and sell them against buyer deposits to budget accommodation operators that need coherent rooms faster and cheaper than new procurement.

Exact buyer:
Budget hotel, hostel, serviced-apartment, student-housing, worker-accommodation, or FF&E installer buyer in Poland/Central Europe with a concrete 10-50 room opening/refit deadline.

Acute trigger:
The seller has a renovation, lease-exit, liquidation, or brand-refresh deadline and must clear room furniture quickly. The buyer has an opening, refit, or seasonal occupancy deadline and cannot wait for new FF&E procurement or tolerate mismatched one-off used furniture.

Control point:
A written first-refusal or purchase option on an exact hotel FF&E lot before it is split at auction or scrapped, with a room-by-room inventory appendix, pickup window, and buyer deposits referencing the same package. The founder then takes title by bill of sale or uses an escrowed pickup/payment agreement for the first lot.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. One or two seller first-refusal or purchase-option agreements covering exact hotel room FF&E lots.
2. Inventory appendices with room count, item list, dimensions, condition grades, photos, excluded soft goods, safety/fire labels where relevant, pickup deadline, and access rules.
3. Buyer reservation agreements and deposits for exact room-equivalent packages, ideally covering at least 10-20 rooms before pickup.
4. One completed bill of sale/title transfer or escrowed pickup transaction for the first paid lot.

6-month POC:
Month 1: source 20-40 hotel renovation, closure, and FF&E installer contacts; inspect 3-5 candidate lots; reject any lot with unclear title, unsafe goods, missing package coherence, or no buyer-deposit path.
Month 2: sign the first seller option and pre-sell a 10-20 room slice to one buyer with deposit and inspection rights.
Month 3: complete pickup/title transfer and delivery or buyer pickup; document landed cost, buyer acceptance, and gross margin.
Months 4-6: repeat with 2-3 additional lots, build a private buyer list of operators and installers, and keep the rule that no large lot is acquired without signed buyer reservations or a very small capital exposure.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with Warsaw, Krakow, Gdansk, Wroclaw, and regional tourist-city hotel renovation contractors, hotel facility managers, insolvency/closure liquidators, property managers, and FF&E installers. Offer sellers a fast whole-room-package removal option before public liquidation fragments the lot. Offer buyers a photographed, inspected, room-count package with clear pickup/delivery terms and deposit-backed reservation.

Economics:
The first proof should avoid warehouse dependence by matching a 10-30 room slice before pickup. Seller pricing is clearance/disposal-driven because bulky FF&E blocks renovation schedules. Buyer pricing is replacement-cost and deadline-driven because matching room packages are hard to assemble quickly from random used listings. A realistic first proof can target 25-45% net gross margin after inspection, handling, transport, damaged-item allowance, and payment fees. Buyer deposits should fund most pickup cash; the founder should keep first-lot exposure well under 100,000 PLN.

Copy risk:
Hotel liquidators, auction houses, used-furniture dealers, FF&E installers, and buyers can source similar goods. The defense is not general sourcing know-how. The defense is control of the exact matched lot, the removal window, and buyer deposits before the lot is fragmented, scrapped, or sold item-by-item.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset:
Once the seller option or title covers the exact room packages and buyer deposits reference the same inventory appendix, a later competitor cannot recreate that coherent lot. They can only compete for future hotel closures or offer substitutes that may not match the buyer's room count, look, dimensions, or deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The founder is not selling a lead list, matching buyers with sellers, or producing a liquidation report. The business controls or owns exact physical goods, takes condition/removal coordination risk, collects buyer deposits, and completes titled package resale or escrowed pickup transactions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Exclude mattresses, bedding, towels, upholstered goods, electrical fixtures, and soft goods unless condition, hygiene, fire/safety labels, and buyer acceptance are explicit. Do not represent goods as certified, code-compliant, or suitable for a regulated accommodation use unless documentation exists. Buyers inspect and accept condition before final payment. The founder does not provide architecture, engineering, fire-safety certification, installation certification, lending, insurance, or hospitality operating advice.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, or BioSignal because it is not a compliance evidence desk, platform unblock desk, or investor diligence pack. It is closest in structure to the confirmed HeritageDoor physical-lot pattern, but the asset and buyer are materially different: commercial hotel-room FF&E packages for budget accommodation operators and installers, not pre-war interior door suites for premium renovation authenticity. The control point is the exact matched room-package lot plus deposits, not documents or expertise.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Used hotel FF&E may be too common, which weakens scarcity and pricing power.
Mattresses, upholstery, curtains, and electrical items may create hygiene, fire-safety, condition, or warranty problems.
Logistics, stairs/elevators, storage, damage, and transport can consume the margin.
Buyer demand may be lumpy because dimensions, brand standards, color, and condition must fit.
Existing liquidators, FF&E dealers, and hotel contractors may already control the best lots.
The idea may become labor-heavy and operational instead of a high-margin owner-earnings business unless the founder keeps strict deposit and lot-size discipline.
