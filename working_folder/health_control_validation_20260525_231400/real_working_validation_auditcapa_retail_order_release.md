## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Incumbents already do this or can do it easily.
2. Many nonconformities are not evidence problems; they require real remediation, equipment repair, pest-control changes, cleaning validation, retraining, infrastructure work, traceability fixes, or process redesign.
3. The startup must not become the food-safety decision-maker.
4. SMETA/labor cases are sensitive and should be rejected unless qualified specialists own the judgment.
5. Buyers, certification bodies, auditors, Sedex workflows, and customer QA teams control acceptance.
6. Eight prepaid mandates in 60 days is too aggressive for an unknown part-time founder.
7. Pricing only works when a real commercial blocker exists.
8. Attribution can be disputed if the supplier's own QA team or consultant did the core work.
9. Scope creep into audit prep, HACCP, procedure writing, training, root-cause analysis, retailer negotiation, or certification strategy would kill margins.
10. The 6-month target is too high without strong partner channels.

## Lesson

Formal audit deadlines and buyer/order consequences still do not pass when the founder controls only an evidence room and the natural experts can copy the workflow. Avoid more audit/CAPA evidence variants unless the founder controls a current payment stream or an exclusive case book rather than a closeout process.
