## Zero To One Summary

It failed because the cleanest insurer-approved payouts may not need the founder: if payment is truly approved and near-term, the claimant can often wait, use ordinary factoring, or coordinate directly through the broker/contractor. ## Strongest Objections

1. If the payout is approved, the customer may simply wait.
2. Delayed cases may be delayed because they are not clean: liens, mortgagee consent, VAT, contractor invoices, beneficiary disputes, fraud checks, setoff, or coverage reservations.
3. Assignment may be generally possible but blocked by policy, beneficiary, lien, or insurer procedure in the specific case.
4. Insurer acknowledgement is the whole deal and may take long enough to kill the value.
5. Factoring firms and banks own the clean version.
6. Discount economics may not work and may look unattractive to claimants.
7. Contractor channels may create conflicts.
8. Fraud and double-payment risk are serious.
9. The founder may drift into claims handling or insurance/legal advice.
10. Capital and trust are constrained under the 100,000 PLN/part-time founder setup.

## Useful Learning

Generic debtor-acknowledged receivables are not enough. Zero To One is rejecting claims finance unless the payout is both hard-controlled and underserved by existing factors, banks, brokers, and contractors. The next search should favor controlled transaction streams or channel-owned cases where the founder controls current demand before incumbents can see it.
