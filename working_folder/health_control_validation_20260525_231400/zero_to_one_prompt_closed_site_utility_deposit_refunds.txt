Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Closed-Site Utility Deposit Refund Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
Recover already-held refundable deposits, overpayments, and credit balances from electricity, gas, water, district-heat, telecom, waste, and meter-service suppliers for multi-site operators after store closures, relocations, legal-entity changes, supplier switches, or meter closures, accepting only account-evidenced clean balances and taking a fee from actual cash or credit returned.

Exact buyer:
Franchise groups, restaurant chains, gym chains, clinic groups, small retail chains, service chains, multi-site warehouses, and franchise operators with 10-200 historical or recent Polish/CEE sites and weak closure administration. The first buyer has at least 20 closed, relocated, assigned, or supplier-switched sites from the last 36 months and a plausible 100,000+ PLN pool of deposits, overpayments, or supplier credits across utility and service accounts.

Acute trigger:
The operator has closed or reassigned premises, switched suppliers, terminated meters, changed legal entities, or inherited a messy site ledger, while refundable deposits, estimated-billing overpayments, final-bill credits, telecom account credits, meter-service deposits, or supplier account balances remain unrecovered. The cash is already held by suppliers; the pain is trapped cash and messy closure admin, not future savings.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate over named sites and supplier accounts; site closure list; old and current legal entity names; supplier account numbers; contracts, invoices, final bills, meter reads, closure protocols, handover protocols, supplier-switch documents, payment proof, deposit terms, telecom line closure evidence, waste-service closure evidence, customer bank account confirmation, supplier support tickets, debtor acknowledgements, refund/credit tracker, and clean no-go ledger.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign five multi-site mandates; screen at least 100 supplier accounts; obtain at least 10 supplier acknowledgements of refundable balances, credit notes, valid no-go, or required missing proof; get at least 50,000 PLN refunded, credited, or formally approved for refund; and collect at least 15,000 PLN in screened-ledger and success fees. Do not count speculative candidate balances; count only supplier-acknowledged or cash/credit-returned amounts.

6-month POC:
Run 20-35 multi-site mandates; screen 600-1,500 supplier accounts; recover, credit, or formally approve 350,000-1,200,000 PLN of deposits/overpayments/credits; collect 80,000-250,000 PLN in fees; achieve 70%+ gross margin; maintain average screened account handling time below 35 minutes after intake; document recovery rates by supplier type, account age, evidence type, closure scenario, legal-entity mismatch, and debtor response; get at least five operators to submit a second account batch.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to multi-site operators visible through store-closure notices, franchise networks, restructuring announcements, franchisee forums, commercial real-estate vacancy changes, retail/restaurant/gym/clinic chains, and accountants serving franchise groups. The hook is a no-speculation cash cleanup: "Send your closed-site account ledger; we only pursue refundable balances with account evidence and supplier acknowledgement, and you pay mainly from returned cash."

Economics:
Charge a 2,500-8,000 PLN screened-ledger fee depending on account count plus 12-25% of actual refunded or credited cash. Minimum target account batch is 20 closed or switched sites and at least 100,000 PLN plausible recoverable deposits/credits. The work is mostly account matching, document chasing, supplier ticketing, closure evidence assembly, and follow-up, so gross margin can exceed 70% if cases with active disputes, missing account authority, or tiny balances are rejected early.

Copy risk:
Accountants, utility brokers, energy consultants, telecom admins, property managers, and internal finance teams can copy pieces. The defense is account-level mandate control over a messy historical closure ledger, supplier-specific accepted evidence memory, debtor acknowledgement tracking, and a pay-from-recovered-cash model for operators whose internal teams have already failed to clean up the backlog.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the operator signs the mandate and provides the site/account ledger, supplier account numbers, closure evidence, bank details, and authority letters, the startup controls the recovery workflow and fee right for that batch. Competitors can offer generic utility audits, but they cannot pursue the same supplier tickets, debtor acknowledgements, and payment-direction requests without replacing an already-authorized account-level mandate.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is not a utility audit, energy procurement service, savings dashboard, closure checklist, marketplace, or broker introduction. It accepts only named closed/switching supplier accounts and produces cash movement, supplier acknowledgement, credit note, formal refund approval, or clean no-go. Revenue is tied to screened ledgers and actual recovered or credited money.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not handle disputed damage claims, landlord fights, lease disputes, insolvency, litigation, debt collection, energy brokerage, supplier switching, tariff advice, tax advice, accounting treatment, or legal interpretation. Do not take custody of client funds. Suppliers refund directly to the customer or credit the customer's account. Cases with active disputes, missing authority, fraud concerns, entity-ownership fights, or supplier refusal requiring legal escalation are rejected or routed to the customer's accountant/lawyer.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak because it does not recover retailer promotional deductions or trade-spend leakage. It is distinct from Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL overbilling, PSP reserve, carrier credit, and platform recovery ideas because the debtor is a utility/service supplier holding closure-related deposits or overpayments, not a marketplace, carrier, PSP, or logistics vendor. It is distinct from TraceFaktura because it is private multi-site supplier-account cash recovery, not municipal event routing.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The recoverable pool may be much smaller than the gross account list after final bills, offsets, landlord-paid accounts, franchisee-owned accounts, stale balances, and normal supplier refunds are removed; suppliers may be slow or require customer-side signatures; old entity names, mergers, franchisee ownership, and missing invoices can block authority; many operators may already have accountants or property managers who should handle this; small balances can destroy margin; the business may become manual support-ticket labor; repeatability depends on finding operators with many closures or messy historical accounts; success-fee attribution can be disputed if a supplier would have refunded anyway.
