Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
PFAS Destruction Claim-Risk Diligence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
An investor-paid claim-risk diligence memo for climate, water, chemical, infrastructure, and deeptech investors evaluating startups that claim PFAS destruction, mineralization, remediation, sorbent regeneration, AFFF disposal, sludge treatment, ion-exchange regeneration, plasma/electrochemical/thermal destruction, or PFAS-free treatment breakthroughs.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE and EU-adjacent climate/deeptech funds, water-tech investors, corporate venture teams at utilities/chemicals/waste companies, industrial innovation teams, accelerators, venture studios, and scout programs evaluating PFAS destruction or remediation startups before seed, pilot, strategic partnership, or project-finance decisions.

Best first buyers see repeated water, waste, chemical, remediation, PFAS, or industrial-cleanup dealflow and need a fixed-format technical-commercial screen before paying for a full expert panel, lab validation, engineering diligence, or legal/environmental review.

Acute trigger:
A live investment, accelerator selection, corporate pilot, or strategic partnership decision is pending and the startup claims it can destroy, mineralize, permanently remove, regenerate, or safely dispose of PFAS in water, landfill leachate, AFFF, sludge, industrial wastewater, membranes, resins, spent sorbents, contaminated soils, or concentrated waste streams. The investor must decide whether the claim is plausible, overclaimed, only concentrating PFAS, missing analytical proof, ignoring byproducts, too energy-intensive, not scalable, or dependent on permitting/handling assumptions.

Control point:
Paid diligence mandate for one live deal or pilot candidate with:
- investor-provided deck, data room, pilot data, test reports, customer claims, techno-economic claims, and unit economics where available;
- public claims, patents, papers, grant summaries, conference materials, and regulatory references;
- matrix-specific claim map: water, leachate, foam, sludge, resin, sorbent, soil, concentrate, or industrial wastewater;
- destruction-versus-capture distinction;
- analytical-method and detection-limit review;
- mass-balance and byproduct question list;
- energy, reagent, throughput, waste-handling, and permitting-risk screen;
- reviewer notes from a PFAS/water/chemistry/environmental-engineering reviewer when needed;
- IC-ready claim-risk memo and evidence request list.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three paid live-deal or pilot diligence memos at 7,500-25,000 PLN each.
2. At least two buyers are investors, corporate venture teams, industrial innovation teams, or accelerators evaluating real PFAS/water/remediation opportunities.
3. At least one memo is used in an IC, partner meeting, accelerator selection, pilot/no-pilot decision, diligence call, term-sheet condition, evidence request, or rejection.
4. One PFAS/water/analytical chemistry reviewer and one environmental/process-engineering reviewer are available on paid per-case terms.
5. A repeat taxonomy of claim risks: destruction vs concentration, incomplete mass balance, weak detection limits, matrix mismatch, ignored short-chain PFAS, byproducts, spent media disposal, unrealistic energy cost, lab-to-field scale gap, regulatory/permitting uncertainty, chain-of-custody weakness, and customer-pilot overclaiming.
6. One buyer requests a second memo, retainer screen, or portfolio/pipeline review.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build two public teardown samples using public PFAS destruction/remediation claims and public technical references. Build a one-page investor evidence request checklist for PFAS destruction/remediation startups. Recruit one PFAS/analytical chemistry reviewer and one environmental/process-engineering reviewer.

Month 2: build a target list of 60 climate/deeptech/water funds, corporate venture teams, utility innovation teams, waste/chemical strategics, PFAS accelerators, and EU/CEE industrial innovation programs. Send targeted outreach offering a 72-hour fixed-format claim-risk memo for one live PFAS deal or pilot candidate.

Month 3: close three paid live-deal memos. Deliver IC-ready memos with claim-risk map, evidence gaps, analytical-method questions, pilot-data concerns, unit-economics stress points, scale/permitting risks, and a decision recommendation: proceed, proceed with conditions, request more evidence, delay, reject, or treat claim as unproven.

Months 4-6: complete 8-20 paid memos, collect 100,000-350,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, secure 2-3 repeat buyers or retainer screens, and build a private PFAS claim-risk graph by technology, matrix, evidence provided, failure mode, and decision impact.

First acquisition mechanism:
Buyer pitch:
"Send one live PFAS destruction/remediation deal before IC or pilot approval. I return a 72-hour claim-risk memo: what the startup has actually shown, whether it destroys or only concentrates PFAS, what analytical evidence is missing, what byproducts/mass-balance questions remain, what unit economics break, and what evidence request you should send before proceeding."

Partner pitch:
"You keep full legal, lab, engineering, and environmental diligence. I provide the fast pre-diligence screen that tells the investor whether the claim is plausible enough to justify deeper expert spend."

Economics:
Charge 7,500-12,000 PLN for a narrow public/deck-based memo, 12,000-18,000 PLN for data-room and pilot-data review, 18,000-25,000 PLN for a corporate-pilot or multi-technology comparison, and 20,000-50,000 PLN/month for repeated deal screening after proof. Reviewer cost is 1,500-6,000 PLN per memo depending on depth. The buyer pays because one weak PFAS claim can waste a seed check, pilot budget, corporate partnership, or project-finance process.

Copy risk:
Environmental consultants, PFAS labs, expert networks, engineering firms, water-tech advisers, corporate technical teams, and university experts can copy pieces. The defense is narrow early-stage investor workflow, fixed IC-ready language, 72-hour turnaround, repeated claim-risk taxonomy, and a private database of PFAS claim patterns across startups before full technical diligence.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once an investor sends the live deal, pays for the memo, shares the data room, and routes IC questions through the pack, the startup controls that specific decision artifact: claim-risk map, evidence request list, reviewer notes, and decision language. A full consultant can be hired later, but the first paid decision memo is already embedded in the investor's live workflow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live PFAS destruction/remediation investment, accelerator, or pilot decisions. The output is an IC/pilot decision artifact: claim-risk map, evidence gap list, analytical-method questions, mass-balance/byproduct concerns, scale and unit-economics stress test, reviewer notes, and decision recommendation. It is not startup-paid validation, lab testing, environmental consulting, legal advice, engineering design, a dashboard, database, marketplace, or expert-call brokerage.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify PFAS destruction, run lab tests, validate environmental claims, provide legal or regulatory advice, design treatment systems, sign off on permits, approve disposal, provide engineering opinions for operations, or handle samples. Investors, labs, environmental consultants, engineers, lawyers, regulators, corporate buyers, and startup operators remain responsible for formal diligence, testing, legal/regulatory conclusions, environmental decisions, pilot design, procurement, and investment decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack. BioSignal screens wearable, EEG, sleep, stress, fatigue, recovery, longevity, and digital-biomarker claims for healthtech investors. PFAS Destruction Claim-Risk screens environmental chemistry and remediation claims for climate/water/industrial investors: destruction vs capture, analytical detection, mass balance, byproducts, matrix transferability, energy cost, waste handling, permitting, and pilot scale. The buyer segment, technical evidence objects, reviewer bench, claim failure modes, and decision criteria are materially different.

It is not DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Specialist PFAS consultants, labs, expert networks, and environmental engineering firms may already be credible diligence providers.
- A solo Warsaw founder lacks default technical authority unless senior reviewers are attached.
- Deal volume may be too small unless the buyer has repeated PFAS/water/remediation dealflow.
- Many startups will not provide enough data to distinguish destruction from concentration or to assess mass balance, byproducts, and detection limits.
- The memo can become generic environmental consulting if not tied to live investor or corporate-pilot decisions.
- Reviewer cost may eat margin if each memo needs deep chemistry/process review.
- Investors may prefer one-off expert calls over a fixed memo.
- Corporate pilot buyers may need formal engineering, legal, and environmental diligence rather than a pre-diligence screen.
