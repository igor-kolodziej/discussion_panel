## Zero To One Judgment

Zero To One treated this as a strong micro-acquisition / continuity-book idea and agreed it is better than a generic EDI or KSeF consulting shop because it acquires an existing paid production dependency: live mappings, credentials, retailer profiles, message flows, runbooks, support history, and customer payment rights.

It still failed because the first book may hide undocumented scripts, fragile credentials, one-off mappings, retailer-specific exceptions, emergency support obligations, and bespoke ERP cleanup. The evaluator viewed the idea as practical but operationally risky for an unknown part-time founder.

## Strongest Objections Captured

1. The exact seller profile may be scarce: small enough to sell cheaply, organized enough to transfer cleanly, trusted enough for handoff, and not already absorbed by a larger EDI provider.
2. Support history may reveal bespoke ERP/accounting cleanup rather than reusable continuity maintenance.
3. Credentials, certificates, mailboxes, VPNs, retailer portals, and EDI provider accounts may be non-transferable or unsafe without a serious access-control transition.
4. Customer consent may be slower than expected unless the seller gives a strong warm handoff and customers prepay before close.
5. The handoff event can trigger customers to compare larger EDI providers, ERP vendors, retailer-recommended platforms, MSPs, or internal IT.
6. KSeF helps the pitch but can pull the business into tax, legal, invoice-content, and accounting-workflow interpretation.
7. Part-time operation is risky when PO, ASN, invoice, or certificate incidents need fast response.
8. Seller IP may be unclear across customer-owned scripts, prior employers, ERP vendors, platform providers, open-source packages, or subcontractors.
9. A large retailer format/certificate/KSeF change can create a burst of simultaneous support work.
10. A six-customer book can prove the handoff mechanism but may still be too small for durable owner earnings.

## Useful Narrowing

Zero To One said the best version is:

- a micro-EDI maintainer supporting 10-30 Polish/CEE retail suppliers;
- repeated order, ASN/despatch advice, invoice, and remittance flows to a few large retailers;
- documented mappings, active recurring invoices, low custom ERP cleanup, and 3-6 month seller warm handoff;
- 6+ prepaid continuity contracts and at least 35,000 PLN collected before close;
- one EDI contractor secured from day one.

Even with a strong six-month proof, Zero To One estimated the score would rise only to 80-83. This remains below the current gate.

## Decision

Reject. Do not continue generic connector, EDI, or digital-admin book buyouts unless the control point includes current cash that is stronger than support continuity and the operating burden is structurally bounded.
