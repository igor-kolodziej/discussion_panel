Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Insolvency Dividend Claim Option Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Option or buy small admitted, non-disputed insolvency and restructuring distribution claims from SME creditors, then convert them into cash through trustee/supervisor acknowledgement, downstream distressed-claim buyer deposits, or near-term distribution payments.

Exact buyer:
The supply side is Polish/CEE SME creditors, landlords, distributors, service providers, and B2B vendors holding admitted non-disputed bankruptcy, restructuring, arrangement, or distribution claims that are too small, slow, or administratively annoying for management or normal counsel to prioritize. The economic buyer is either the startup for small clean claims, a downstream distressed-claim buyer for packaged claim options, or a creditor that accepts a discounted cashout instead of waiting for a distribution or arrangement instalment. The first buyer/seller profile excludes consumers, employees, disputed claimants, sanctions-risk parties, and claims with unresolved objections or litigation.

Acute trigger:
The creditor has a named admitted claim included in a claim list, arrangement, approved plan, distribution plan, or trustee/supervisor acknowledgement, but expected cash is delayed, administratively uncertain, or too small for ordinary legal follow-up. The creditor wants immediate discounted cash, a clean no-go, or an assigned buyer before the next distribution, instalment, creditor update, year-end cleanup, management change, or accounting close.

Control point:
Signed option or cession over a named admitted claim; claim-list, arrangement, distribution-plan, or trustee/supervisor proof; creditor identity and authority; source invoice/proof-of-debt file; proceeding reference; expected category and dividend or instalment path; payment-account update; lawyer-reviewed transfer terms; no-go checklist; trustee/supervisor/debtor acknowledgement or written no-objection where required; and downstream buyer escrow/deposit or founder purchase only after the acknowledgement path is credible.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, screen at least 60 public/professional leads and 25 creditor files; sign six options or cessions over admitted non-disputed claims with at least 500,000 PLN face value and at least 120,000 PLN expected distribution or plan-instalment value; obtain two proceeding-side acknowledgements, payment-direction confirmations, or written no-objection responses; close at least one cash conversion through founder purchase, downstream buyer assignment, or actual first distribution/instalment; and collect at least 25,000 PLN gross spread, origination fee, option fee, or realized distribution margin.

6-month POC:
Source 200-350 creditor files; verify 60-90 as potentially admitted and transferable; sign 20-35 options or cessions; control 2,000,000-5,000,000 PLN face value with 400,000-1,200,000 PLN expected distribution or plan-instalment value; close 8-15 cash conversions through purchases, downstream assignments, or distributions; generate 150,000-450,000 PLN gross margin before legal and sourcing costs; maintain legal/review cost below 25% of gross margin; and document repeatable no-go reasons by proceeding type, claim category, acknowledgement path, timing, setoff risk, and buyer appetite.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a creditor lead list from public insolvency/restructuring notices, creditor lists where accessible, professional networks, accountants, lawyers, receivables managers, landlords, distributors, and suppliers known to have exposure to bankrupt or restructured debtors. The initial outreach is creditor-specific: "If your admitted claim is included in the proceeding and you prefer discounted cash or a clean transfer/no-go rather than waiting through administration, I can option or buy it after transferability review and proceeding-side acknowledgement." In parallel, secure two downstream distressed-claim buyers or small funds willing to escrow deposits for pre-screened admitted claim packages.

Economics:
Revenue comes from claim-control spread, not consulting. The desk buys or options small admitted claims at 45-75% of expected distribution value when the payment horizon and acknowledgement path are credible; resells packaged claims to a downstream buyer at a lower discount; or holds selected small claims to first distribution where the timeline is credible. Small fixed diligence fees are charged only to downstream claim buyers, not to creditors. The 100,000 PLN budget covers legal templates, counsel review, sourcing, small pilot purchases, and deposits; the startup does not need to buy every claim because signed options can control the first package before capital is exposed.

Copy risk:
Insolvency lawyers, restructuring advisers, distressed-debt funds, factoring firms, and debt purchasers can copy the structure. The defense is narrow small-claim sourcing, fast option paperwork, admitted-claim intake discipline, proceeding-specific acknowledgement memory, and control over exact signed claim options or cessions before larger buyers see the files.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the creditor signs an option or cession over a named admitted claim, and the desk has the source file, proceeding reference, acknowledgement request, payment-direction trail, and downstream buyer deposit or purchase path, competitors cannot control that exact claim without displacing the signed transfer or waiting for the option to expire. They can pursue other claims, but not the same controlled claim package.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not sell insolvency advice, claim-filing help, monitoring software, a creditor database, a marketplace, or introductions. It accepts only named admitted non-disputed claims where a signed option or cession can be obtained and converted into cash through purchase, resale, distribution, or plan instalment. No signed claim control means no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not provide legal advice, act as insolvency counsel, pursue litigation, file disputed claims, represent consumers or employees, handle sanctions-risk parties, handle client funds, or collect debts outside the controlled transfer structure. Reject claims with objections, setoff risk, security-priority disputes, employee/consumer status, missing admission proof, unclear creditor authority, pending appeals, fraud concerns, litigation, or no credible payment horizon. Use licensed counsel for transfer terms, local-law review, priority questions, acknowledgement requirements, AML/sanctions checks, and any proceeding-specific communication. Use escrow, lawyer trust account, or direct creditor payment where needed; do not hold funds informally.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, carrier claims, supplier rebate recovery, KSeF invoice release, insurance payout lockbox, grant reimbursement release, and other recovery mandates because the controlled object is a signed option or cession over an admitted insolvency or restructuring distribution claim, not a disputed invoice, platform case, public payment claim, rebate, insurance claim, or evidence packet. It is distinct from HeritageDoor and physical-lot ideas because no physical asset is bought or moved.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Clean admitted claims may simply be worth waiting for, while problematic claims may be adverse-selection traps. Assignment or option acknowledgement may be slow, lawyer-owned, or procedurally blocked. Distribution timing and percentage can change. Distressed-debt buyers and insolvency lawyers may already know the attractive claims. Small creditors may distrust discounted cashout offers. Good claims may be too expensive after legal review and sourcing. The 100,000 PLN budget limits purchase capacity. Some claims may hide setoff, priority, secured-creditor, tax, employee, or appeal issues. The desk can drift into legal services, debt collection, or brokerage if it accepts anything other than admitted, transferable, non-disputed claims with a clear cash-conversion path.
