## Strongest Objections Captured

- The real asset is customer inertia during a fragile provider transition, not durable ownership of SMS traffic.
- The handoff moment creates churn risk because customers may choose a known CPaaS provider once they notice the provider is changing.
- SMS resale gross margin is structurally exposed to upstream CPaaS and carrier pricing, failed-message fees, segment mix, fraud controls, and support costs.
- OTP/login traffic is trust-sensitive and poorly matched to an unknown part-time solo founder without deep incident-response, DPA, abuse-monitoring, fallback-routing, and uptime credibility.
- Sender IDs, templates, routes, and configurations are weaker control points than the thesis assumes; they are often permissions, registrations, or provider-specific settings rather than hard transferable assets.
- Clean seller supply may be rare; willing sellers may be exiting thin-margin, repriced, technically outdated, support-heavy, concentrated, or grey-route-contaminated books.
- Customer concentration and working-capital timing can destroy the economics even if the headline traffic volume looks attractive.
- Marketing traffic contamination is a major kill risk because it brings consent, complaint, churn, and deliverability exposure.

## Reviewer Guidance

The evaluator considered the acquisition structure smarter than generic CPaaS resale because payment is conditional on live traffic, billing transfer, and retained margin. However, it recommended narrowing, if pursued at all, to low-risk ecommerce/logistics/order-notification books rather than fintech or security-sensitive OTP/login flows.

## Search Lesson

Direct billing transfer and sender-identity continuity are still too weak when the underlying business is a commodity resale spread with high support and trust sensitivity. Future rounds should look for harder current-cash control where the buyer cannot immediately re-platform to a larger known provider at the handoff moment.
