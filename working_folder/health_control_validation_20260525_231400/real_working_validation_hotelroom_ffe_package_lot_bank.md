## Zero To One Judgment

Zero To One scored this as a real but operationally heavy physical-lot arbitrage business. It accepted that the best part is the control point: exact matched room-equivalent lots with buyer deposits before pickup, not generic used furniture.

It rejected the idea as a confirmed candidate because used hotel FF&E is not scarce enough, logistics are punishing, and buyer fit is too narrow. The evaluator treated the model as likely to become a furniture moving/liquidation operation rather than a high-owner-earnings controlled-asset business.

## Strongest Objections Captured

- Used hotel FF&E is common; existing hotel clearance firms, liquidators, auction houses, and used-furniture dealers already serve the market.
- Logistics can destroy margin: access, stairs/elevators, labor, parking, wrapping, transport, damage, missing parts, storage, disposal, and leftover handling can turn theoretical gross margin into near-zero profit.
- Buyer demand is lumpy and fit-sensitive: room dimensions, bed sizes, style, condition, color, brand standards, safety expectations, and delivery deadlines must all match.
- Mattresses, upholstery, curtains, soft goods, and electrical items create hygiene, odor, fire-label, cleaning, and safety objections. Excluding them reduces package completeness.
- Renovation/closure urgency can create impossible pickup windows for a part-time solo founder.
- Existing liquidators and FF&E installers may control the best lots before a new entrant sees them.
- Storage is a trap; the model only works as seller-site to buyer-site direct transfer.
- "Room-equivalent" packages may fall apart under inspection if item counts, condition, or missing parts do not align.
- Pricing power is weak against cheap new furniture, local carpenters, direct hotel clearances, OLX/Facebook lots, and auction channels.
- Scaling requires repeated sourcing, inspection, transport, condition grading, buyer matching, and damage/leftover handling.

## Useful Narrowing

Zero To One said the best version would be much narrower:

- deposit-backed 10-20 room hotel casegoods packages for budget operators;
- no mattresses, soft goods, electricals, or warehouse dependence;
- exact photographed/countable inventory;
- buyer deposit before seller payment;
- pickup and delivery prepriced;
- founder capital at risk below 30,000-40,000 PLN on the first lot.

Even after narrowing, it estimated the idea would only rise to 70-73 if a first profitable package closes. This is well below the threshold.

## Decision

Reject. Do not keep mining generic commercial FF&E lots unless the asset becomes materially scarcer than used hotel furniture and logistics/storage are structurally solved by the control point itself.
