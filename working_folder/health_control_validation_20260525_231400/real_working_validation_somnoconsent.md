Core judgment:

- The control point is real if secured: exclusive lab mandates plus fresh explicit patient consent plus buyer deposits.
- The idea is stronger than a sleep-data marketplace and is closer to a rights-conversion company.

## Strongest Objections

1. The control point is real but hard to secure fast.
   - A private sleep lab must trust an unknown founder enough to run commercial reconsent outreach through its patient channel.

2. Patient reconsent may underperform.
   - The model assumes 50-150 explicit consents from 500-1,000 former patients.
   - Patients may react poorly to commercially framed AI/data reuse unless messaging is very careful.

3. Buyer deposits are the hardest proof point.
   - A 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable inspection deposit is a good filter, but medtech buyers may need legal, privacy, clinical, cybersecurity, regulatory, and procurement review even for feasibility access.

4. Dataset quality may shrink sharply.
   - EDF export quality, missing channels, scorer variability, old scoring criteria, incomplete metadata, and weak outcome linkage could reduce 1,200 archived nights to a much smaller usable cohort.

5. Pseudonymisation does not remove GDPR risk.
   - The idea correctly avoids relying on pseudonymisation alone, but rich biosignal health records remain legally sensitive.

6. Lab ownership/control may be incomplete.
   - The lab may control patient contact and medical records, but commercial reuse can be constrained by medical-record rules, vendor contracts, physicians/scorers, insurer/NFZ/private-care arrangements, and privacy policies.

7. Ethics and reputational risk are high.
   - Labs may fear patient backlash around old sleep studies being reused for AI companies.

8. First buyer selection is narrow.
   - Large medtech is slow, startups may lack budget, academic buyers may not pay, and CROs may require more mature compliance.

## Pivot Implications

Do not continue trying to rescue SomnoConsent as the primary candidate unless the control proof becomes much simpler:

- one lab instead of two;
- one trusted sleep physician/lab advisor already attached;
- one written buyer spec before patient outreach;
- one paid feasibility deposit before building the full consent machine.

The next pivot should keep sleep/biosignal fit but move from "commercial data-rights conversion" to a faster regulatory/workflow bottleneck where the founder can reserve testing capacity, route payments, and produce cash in 60 days.
