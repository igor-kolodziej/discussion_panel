## Evaluator Judgment

## Strongest Objections

1. The aviation supplier pool may be too small for enough recurring volume.
2. Aviation customers may already use established aviation cyber consultants, ISO firms, GRC providers, law firms, or internal supplier-risk teams.
3. Unknown-founder trust is weak because suppliers would share sensitive security, access, architecture, incident, subcontractor, hosting, and aviation-interface evidence.
4. Some requests will require real remediation, not evidence packaging: incident response, BCP/DR, access logs, MFA, vulnerability management, subcontractor oversight, audit rights, ISO/SOC evidence, or security monitoring.
5. Legal and contractual scope creep is likely around clauses, audit rights, incident notification, and commitments.
6. Not all suppliers are directly Part-IS scoped, so the pitch must be "your regulated customer needs supplier/interface evidence," not "you must comply with Part-IS."
7. The structure is close to DORA/KSC evidence packs; differentiation must be aviation-specific or it becomes generic cyber questionnaire support.
8. Partner channels may keep the work rather than refer it.
9. The first cases may be hard to find because there is no public signal of a live Part-IS supplier blocker.
10. Customer-progress outcomes may be hard to attribute.
