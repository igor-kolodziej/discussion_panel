## Strongest Objections Captured

1. If payment is already scheduled, value capture is weak; vendors may resist paying a high fee for money that appears to be arriving anyway.
2. "Assignment" language creates unnecessary legal and accounting friction around receivable transfer, anti-assignment clauses, customer consent, notice formalities, derecognition, lender/factoring covenants, and payment rights.
3. If the startup advances cash, the business becomes financing with credit risk and a materially different regulatory/legal profile.
4. Success-fee attribution is weak because the customer had already provided a payment date.
5. Clean debtor-scheduled files may be rare; the painful cases usually remain blocked by PO, vendor-master, tax, entity, procurement, budget, service-credit, legal, or netting issues.
6. Enterprise AP may ignore a third-party coordinator, pushing the work back into behind-the-scenes AR support.
7. Fractional CFOs, SaaS accountants, RevOps consultants, billing implementation partners, and outsourced AR teams can internalize the workflow.
8. The work can degrade into generic AR follow-up: confirm payment date, check portal, follow up, classify no-go.
9. No-go outcomes are hard to monetize when the vendor expected payment anyway.
10. The better timing is earlier in the process: turning customer acknowledgement into an AP/payment path, not monitoring already scheduled payments.
