Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AIQ Partner Ticket Book

One-sentence thesis:
A white-label AI-questionnaire overflow desk for SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, privacy/security questionnaire firms, and AI implementation agencies that already control trusted SaaS client relationships but need fast partner-branded answers and evidence rooms for live enterprise buyer AI sections.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, virtual security teams, privacy/security questionnaire outsourcing firms, DPO/privacy consultants, and AI implementation agencies serving 20-150 person B2B SaaS vendors with embedded AI features. The payor is either the partner on a wholesale basis or the SaaS client through partner-approved direct billing. Best first partners already have 3-20 SaaS clients and are receiving AI-specific questionnaire sections that they do not want to handle as legal AI Act classification, ISO 42001, or deep model-validation work.

Acute trigger:
The partner's SaaS client has a live enterprise customer, procurement, security, privacy, or vendor-risk questionnaire with an AI section blocking a signed deal, renewal, pilot expansion, or procurement approval. The partner can answer normal SOC2/security/privacy questions but lacks a repeatable AI evidence workflow for model inventory, model/subprocessor disclosure, customer-data training/no-training proof, retention/logging settings, RAG source controls, eval/monitoring evidence, human review, AI incident path, opt-out, and buyer-specific response wording.

Control point:
Signed 90-day partner routing agreement or priority-lane mandate covering AI-questionnaire overflow tickets, partner-branded delivery terms, client authorization workflow, evidence intake checklist, payment/revenue-share terms, escalation/no-go boundaries, and current ticket ledger. For each ticket, the startup controls the partner-routed AI answer matrix, evidence-room index, review notes, no-go/escalation log, and buyer-response packet under the partner's trusted client relationship.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three SOC2/fractional-CISO/privacy/security-questionnaire partners sign written routing agreements; at least twelve live AI-questionnaire tickets are prepaid or partner-guaranteed at 6,000-20,000 PLN each; every ticket has a named SaaS client, buyer questionnaire, deal value/deadline, and evidence-access authorization; at least eight tickets produce partner-accepted delivery, buyer-progress outcome, narrowed follow-up, approved lower-risk deployment scope, or clean no-go; two partners route a second ticket; gross margin stays above 65% after AI/security/privacy reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Secure 8-12 active routing partners; deliver 50-100 partner-routed tickets; collect 400,000-1,200,000 PLN in wholesale, direct-bill, and success-linked fees; document 30+ buyer-progress outcomes and 10 clean no-go or out-of-scope decisions; get at least five partners to send 5+ tickets; keep sub-5-business-day turnaround for evidence-ready tickets; build a private partner memory base of AI questionnaire patterns, buyer follow-ups, accepted answer formats, evidence gaps, no-training proof, model/subprocessor disclosures, RAG/source controls, eval evidence, and legal/high-risk escalation triggers.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of 150 SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, security questionnaire outsourcing firms, DPO/privacy consultants, and AI implementation agencies serving B2B SaaS. Pitch only overflow economics: "Keep your client relationship. When a buyer AI section appears, route it to me white-label. I return your partner-branded answer matrix, AI evidence-room index, no-go/escalation notes, and buyer wording in five business days. You keep high-value security/privacy/legal work; I only handle bounded AI-questionnaire evidence tickets."

Economics:
Charge partners 5,000-8,000 PLN wholesale for evidence-ready single-product tickets, 10,000-16,000 PLN for multi-feature, RAG, or agent tickets, and 16,000-25,000 PLN for urgent renewal or high-value deal sprints. Direct-bill SaaS clients 8,000-25,000 PLN where the partner prefers referral fees. Reviewer costs are 2,000-8,000 PLN per ticket from AI security, privacy, or governance reviewers. The partner pays or routes payment because the ticket protects the partner's client relationship, avoids template embarrassment, and lets the partner keep broader advisory work.

Copy risk:
SOC2 consultants, fractional CISOs, privacy lawyers, GRC platforms, AI governance consultants, and AI implementation agencies can copy pieces. The defense is signed current-ticket routing, fast white-label delivery, partner-specific answer memory, accepted/rejected buyer wording, evidence-room taxonomy, no-go discipline, and the startup becoming the behind-the-scenes AI questionnaire bench before partners hire their own.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a partner signs the 90-day routing agreement, forwards a current AI-questionnaire ticket, authorizes client evidence access, and guarantees or approves payment, the startup controls the active ticket workflow, evidence room, answer matrix, and buyer-response packet for that partner's client deadline. Another consultant would need to displace the partner-approved white-label bench while the client deal is already waiting.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only partner-routed live AI-questionnaire tickets tied to a specific SaaS customer deal, renewal, expansion, or procurement deadline. The output is a partner-branded deal-release artifact used in an active buyer workflow: AI answer matrix, evidence-room index, model/subprocessor table, data-flow map, no-training proof, retention/logging evidence, eval/monitoring summary, human-review evidence, access-control screenshots, RAG/source-control evidence, buyer response wording, and no-go/escalation log. It is not generic AI readiness, legal compliance, ISO implementation, model validation, SaaS, a dashboard, a marketplace, template sale, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, AI Act classification opinions, DPIAs, high-risk AI conformity assessment, ISO 42001 implementation, SOC2 certification, privacy legal opinions, model validation, safety certification, clinical/HR/credit/biometric/law-enforcement review, or guarantees of buyer approval. Partners, vendors, lawyers, DPOs, security officers, AI governance specialists, auditors, and enterprise buyers remain responsible for legal compliance, privacy, security, contractual terms, product changes, and procurement decisions. Cases involving regulated high-risk AI, autonomous adverse decisions, clinical data, credit/insurance/HR decisioning, biometrics, law enforcement, unclear training claims, missing evidence, or buyer demands for legal opinions are declined or routed back to the partner's qualified advisers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle ICT third-party-risk, Article 28/30 DORA evidence, exit plans, subcontracting, audit rights, or regulated-finance operational resilience. It is not the failed AIGate direct procurement pack because the control point is partner-routed ticket-book ownership and white-label billing authority, not direct cold sales to SaaS vendors. It is not the failed AI Model-Risk Renewal Evidence Pack because it excludes regulated-finance model validation and high-risk decisioning. It is not the failed VoiceAI Article 50 Release Pack because it is not voice-agent deployment transparency or Article 50 implementation. It is not REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BESSGuard, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Good SOC2 consultants and fractional CISOs can internalize the workflow once they see the first few tickets. Partners may resist exclusivity or avoid routing client work to an unknown subcontractor. Vendors may still lack real evidence for no-training, retention, evals, RAG sources, logging, or human review. Enterprise buyers may ask for legal classification, DPIA, product fixes, model validation, or high-risk AI analysis outside scope. Sensitive architecture and log access creates trust friction even through partners. The business may collapse into low-margin white-label questionnaire labor unless it retains urgent AI-specific accepted-answer memory and strict no-go boundaries.
