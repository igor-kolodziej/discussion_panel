## Simulated Pass Candidate

### Grid-Connection Claim-Risk Diligence Pack - 89

One-sentence thesis: a fixed-format paid diligence memo for investors, developers, buyers, and lenders evaluating CEE land, data-center, battery, EV charging, industrial, and renewable projects whose valuation depends on claimed grid connection capacity, queue position, upgrade cost, energization date, or DSO/OSD progress.

Buyer: small infrastructure funds, real-estate developers, data-center/AI infrastructure buyers, battery-storage developers, EV charging networks, industrial-site buyers, project-finance lenders, search funds, family offices, and corporate development teams considering a deal where "secured MW," "connection conditions," "grid capacity," "transformer availability," "queue position," "DSO approval," or "ready-to-energize" is material to price.

Acute trigger: buyer is about to pay an option deposit, sign an LOI, enter exclusivity, approve development spend, submit IC memo, close an acquisition, lend against a project, or buy a site/project whose economics depend on the seller's grid-connection claim. A wrong claim can destroy project value or delay cash flow for years.

Transferable control point: prepaid live-deal memo with seller data-room documents, DSO/OSD letters, connection conditions, grid-connection agreement drafts, capacity/voltage point evidence, upgrade-cost documents, queue/permit correspondence, site maps, single-line diagrams where available, seller claims, evidence request list, and engineer/advisor review of flagged issues. The startup controls the buyer's decision artifact for that specific deal.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:

- 3 prepaid live-deal diligence memos from at least 2 buyer types.
- One electrical/grid engineer or energy-project advisor on paid per-review terms.
- Evidence checklist covering DSO/OSD documents, connection status, capacity, timeline, cost, validity, transferability, dependency, and missing documents.
- At least 10 deal documents reviewed across 3 projects.
- At least 2 memos used in a real decision: no-bid, repricing, deeper diligence, revised conditions precedent, seller evidence request, or IC/LOI language.
- First 30,000-80,000 PLN collected in memo fees.

First acquisition mechanism: target investors, developers, brokers, lawyers, project-finance advisors, EV/CPO teams, battery developers, data-center site scouts, and industrial real-estate buyers with a narrow offer: "before you pay for a site/project marketed with grid capacity, send the connection documents and seller claims; I return a claim-risk memo with red/yellow/green status, missing evidence, and buyer questions in 5 business days."

Gross margin and payback logic: price 6,000-12,000 PLN for a light claim-risk screen, 15,000-25,000 PLN for a full memo with engineer review, and 30,000+ PLN for urgent multi-site batches. One avoided bad deposit, repriced project, or tightened condition precedent can justify the fee. Contractors are paid only for flagged engineering review; the founder standardizes evidence extraction and decision memo structure.

Copy risk: grid engineers, energy lawyers, technical advisors, due-diligence firms, DSO consultants, and developer teams can do pieces of this. The defense is narrow claim-risk packaging, 5-business-day speed, small-deal pricing, local CEE grid-document pattern memory, and refusal to become broad engineering or legal diligence.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: once the startup controls the buyer's paid live-deal memo, seller documents, evidence checklist, reviewer notes, missing-document list, and IC/LOI language, competitors cannot influence that specific decision without the buyer switching. The asset compounds through local DSO/OSD wording, connection-document patterns, seller-claim variants, missing-evidence patterns, and deal outcomes.

Why this is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: the output is a paid decision artifact inside a live capital-allocation workflow, not market research, a dashboard, a marketplace, or brokered expert call. It answers whether a specific grid-capacity claim is supported enough for the buyer's next deal action, what evidence is missing, and what conditions or questions should be attached before money moves.

Reasons this could still fail:

- Buyers may already use engineering, legal, or grid advisors.
- An unknown solo founder has weak credibility without a named grid engineer.
- Some documents require deep technical or legal diligence beyond a fast memo.
- Sellers may withhold DSO/OSD documents or provide vague claims.
- Local grid processes vary by country, DSO/OSD, voltage level, project type, and transferability.
- The product can drift into engineering signoff, legal advice, valuation, or project development.
- Deal flow may be relationship-driven and slow.
- Memos are useful but may not create enough repeat owner earnings unless a channel sends recurring deals.

Decision: advance this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
