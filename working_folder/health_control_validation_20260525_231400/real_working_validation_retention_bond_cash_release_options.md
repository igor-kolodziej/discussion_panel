## Strongest Objections

1. Contract wording may kill most cases; without explicit substitution rights, the desk only asks the beneficiary politely.
2. Beneficiary discretion is a hard bottleneck; beneficiaries can reject wording, reject providers, delay review, or prefer retained cash.
3. Guarantee providers may require collateral, blocked credit lines, personal guarantees, or other security that eliminates the liquidity benefit.
4. Clean files may be rarer than expected because construction retentions often involve defects, punch lists, penalties, delays, setoff, missing protocols, or general-contractor liquidity issues.
5. Guarantee brokers, insurance brokers, banks, surety providers, and construction lawyers already sit close to the workflow.
6. Unlicensed insurance/guarantee brokerage risk is real if the founder compares, places, or receives fees around guarantee products.
7. Beneficiaries may prefer cash because it is already held and simpler than guarantee wording, expiry, provider risk, and claim formalities.
8. Success fees may be squeezed when the actual licensed broker or lawyer appears to own the valuable part of the work.
