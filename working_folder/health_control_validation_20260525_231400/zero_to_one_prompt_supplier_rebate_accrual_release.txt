Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Supplier Rebate Accrual Release Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
Recover, reconcile, and release contractually owed supplier/manufacturer rebates, retrospective discounts, volume bonuses, price-support credits, and year-end credit notes for CEE distributors and dealer networks where purchase ledgers and supplier program terms show a named payable rebate period but cash or credit has not landed.

Exact buyer:
CEE building-materials, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, industrial MRO, tools, packaging, office-equipment, B2B consumables, and specialty wholesale distributors or dealer groups with 5-80 suppliers, meaningful annual purchases, and at least 150,000 PLN in visible delayed, short-paid, or unreconciled supplier rebates/credits. Exclude health, pharma, insurance, lending, tax, regulated financial products, and retailer-promotion deduction cases.

Acute trigger:
Quarter-end or year-end closes, supplier statement mismatch, CFO cash-pressure review, owner sale diligence, distributor margin squeeze, buyer group settlement, supplier rep turnover, or a delayed credit memo reveals that a supplier owes or may owe retrospective discount, annual bonus, volume rebate, price-protection credit, marketing support credit, or stock-support credit for a named period.

Control point:
Signed recovery/reconciliation mandate over named suppliers, rebate periods, and customer legal entities; supplier agreement, rebate letter, program terms, price-support note, or commercial correspondence; purchase ledger and invoices; return and credit-note ledger; SKU/vendor-code mapping; threshold and tier calculation; supplier statement or missing-statement log; claim submission or reconciliation ticket; supplier acknowledgement, revised statement, credit note, payment, or clean no-go tracker; and fee right tied to cash/credit actually acknowledged or recovered after the mandate.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign five distributor/dealer mandates; screen at least 20 supplier-period files; file or update at least 12 supplier reconciliation requests; obtain at least 300,000 PLN in supplier-acknowledged, credited, paid, revised-statement, or clean no-go outcomes; collect at least 40,000 PLN in screened-ledger and success fees; and get at least two customers to submit a second supplier period.

6-month POC:
Run 25-50 mandates across 12-25 distributors/dealer groups; screen 150-300 supplier-period files; recover, credit, formally acknowledge, or cleanly no-go 4,000,000-12,000,000 PLN of rebate/credit value; collect 500,000-1,500,000 PLN in fees; maintain gross margin above 70%; build a private playbook by supplier type, rebate wording, purchase-ledger evidence, threshold issue, SKU mapping issue, return offset, statement mismatch, payment timing, and accepted claim format; show that at least 40% of customers submit a second batch.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to distributor CFOs, owners, buying-group admins, and finance controllers in sectors where supplier rebates are common but admin is messy: electrical/plumbing/HVAC wholesalers, building-materials distributors, MRO/tool dealers, packaging distributors, office-equipment dealers, and industrial consumables wholesalers. Triggered lists come from dealer networks, distributor associations, supplier partner pages, owner-sale listings, restructuring signals, and firms advertising many supplier brands. The first message asks for one rebate period and one supplier statement mismatch, not a broad audit.

Economics:
Charge 6,000-18,000 PLN for a screened supplier-period ledger depending on number of suppliers and purchase volume, plus 8-18% of supplier-acknowledged or recovered cash/credit generated after the mandate. Only accept files where visible annual supplier-rebate exposure exceeds 500,000 PLN or the delayed/short-paid balance exceeds 150,000 PLN. Reject purely discretionary relationship bonuses, undocumented promises, tiny balances, active litigation, and cases where the supplier relationship owner refuses the reconciliation.

Copy risk:
Accountants, ERP consultants, procurement teams, buying groups, rebate-management software vendors, and distributor finance teams can copy pieces. The defense is a narrow mandate over specific supplier periods, supplier-specific accepted evidence memory, fast purchase-ledger reconstruction, cross-functional finance/procurement execution, and pay-from-recovered-cash economics for distributors whose internal teams have already failed to close the rebate ledger.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the distributor signs the mandate and provides program terms, purchase ledger, supplier statements, account authority, and correspondence for a named supplier period, the startup controls that reconciliation file, supplier ticket, evidence packet, acknowledgement tracker, and fee right. A consultant or accountant can offer a generic review, but cannot pursue the same supplier-period claim without displacing the already-authorized mandate and supplier communication path.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product does not sell rebate software, dashboards, procurement benchmarking, supplier negotiation, generic audits, or broker introductions. It accepts only named supplier-period files with existing purchase evidence and produces cash payment, credit note, revised supplier statement, formal supplier acknowledgement, or clean no-go. The output is a released or formally resolved rebate accrual, not a report.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not negotiate new supplier terms, advise on tax/accounting treatment, litigate, collect debts, threaten suppliers, handle customer funds, finance receivables, or pursue cases based only on verbal promises. Do not accept cases involving health/pharma pricing, regulated finance, insurance commissions, active legal disputes, fraud allegations, sanctions, insolvency, or retailer promotion deductions. The customer remains commercial relationship owner; suppliers pay or credit the customer directly; accountants decide recognition and tax/accounting treatment.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak because PromoLeak is retailer-side promotional deduction/trade-spend leakage where retailers short-pay suppliers. This is supplier/manufacturer rebate and retrospective-credit cash owed to distributors or dealer groups after purchases. The buyer, debtor, payment direction, evidence file, and economic trigger are different. It is distinct from Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL recovery, PartnerCenter, closed-site utility refunds, and carrier credits because the controlled artifact is a contractual supplier rebate period and purchase-ledger reconciliation, not a platform, logistics, utility, or partner-portal support case.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many rebates are discretionary or relationship-sensitive rather than cleanly payable; supplier account managers may resist third-party involvement; procurement or sales teams may fear damaging supplier relationships; ERP purchase data may be messy across SKU codes, returns, substitutions, merged entities, buying groups, and late invoices; suppliers may offset rebates against returns, warranty credits, marketing funds, debt, or future pricing; accountants and finance teams may claim they already own the process; success-fee attribution can be disputed; after one recovery, the customer may internalize the checklist; rebate-management software and buying groups can copy if the category proves valuable.
