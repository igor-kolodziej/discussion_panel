Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Issued White-Certificate Cashout Options

One-sentence thesis:
A cashout desk that options, buys, or takes exclusive sale mandates over already-issued Polish energy-efficiency certificate property rights from hotels, factories, housing cooperatives, public-lighting retrofit owners, and ESCO-orphaned projects, then monetizes those rights through a licensed broker/TGE-member path and proves value with first cash settlement.

Exact buyer:
Supply-side seller: a holder of already-issued or already-registered Polish white-certificate/property-right claims from a completed energy-efficiency project who has not monetized them because the ESCO, energy auditor, facility manager, housing cooperative board, hotel/factory owner, or public-lighting contractor never completed the broker/TGE sale path. Economic buyer/payor: obligated energy-market participants or market buyers purchasing energy-efficiency certificate rights through the proper exchange/broker/offtaker route. The startup's immediate customer is the right holder who wants cashout without learning the market process.

Acute trigger:
The project is already completed and the certificate/right exists or is close enough to registration to verify, but cash is still trapped. The holder needs cash for loan repayment, retrofit budget closure, annual financial reporting, ESCO settlement, public-lighting project closeout, cooperative budget approval, or simply wants to avoid price/expiry/administrative risk. The founder rejects application-stage projects and acts only when the right, decision, account statement, registry status, or broker-verifiable title path exists.

Control point:
Signed exclusive purchase option, assignment path, or sale mandate over a specific issued/registered white-certificate property right; URE decision or registry evidence; right-holder identity and title documents; account/registry statement or broker-verifiable ownership evidence; no prior pledge, lien, double-sale, ESCO claim, or consultant fee dispute; board/owner authority to sell; broker/TGE-member execution path; market quote or buyer/offtaker indication; sale instruction; settlement statement; and first cash proceeds. The startup controls the seller relationship and the exact right under option/mandate while licensed brokers or TGE members execute any trading that requires licensing.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three signed exclusive options or sale mandates over already-issued or broker-verifiable white-certificate rights from hotels, factories, housing cooperatives, lighting retrofits, or ESCO-orphaned projects; each file has title evidence, URE/registry evidence, owner authority, no known pledge/dispute, estimated toe volume, and net-sale estimate; one legal/broker memo confirms the startup can use a purchase-option, mandate, or title-acquisition structure without acting as an unlicensed exchange broker or investment adviser; one licensed broker/TGE-member or qualified market intermediary agrees to execute verified trades; at least one right receives a binding bid, market sale, or signed offtake; first cash settlement or escrowed proceeds lands within 60 days; the founder earns either a transparent success fee or spread after all broker, legal, tax, and right-holder costs.

6-month POC:
Control 15-30 issued-right cashout files; close 6-12 cash settlements; generate 500,000-3,000,000 PLN gross right-holder cashout value depending on certificate volume and market price; collect 50,000-250,000 PLN in success fees, spreads, or option gains; maintain gross margin above 60% after legal checks, broker/intermediary costs, travel, title review, and admin; reject at least half of inbound files if they are application-stage, title-unclear, disputed, pledged, expired, uneconomic, or consultant-owned; build a private map of auditors, ESCOs, facility managers, cooperatives, project types, registry status, right volumes, sale timing, buyer/offtaker routes, and failed-title patterns.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build lists of energy auditors, ESCOs, lighting retrofit contractors, facility managers, housing cooperatives, hotel groups, factories, hospitals, schools, and municipal/public-lighting contractors that completed efficiency retrofits but may not have monetized certificates. Ask only for issued or broker-verifiable rights: "If a white-certificate right exists from a completed project and nobody has converted it into cash, I can option it, verify title, route sale through a proper broker/TGE-member path, and pay only from cash settlement or an agreed purchase spread." Start with orphaned ESCO/auditor files where the holder has a right but no working market process.

Economics:
For small holders, charge a 8-15% success fee on net cash settlement after broker/intermediary costs. For larger clean rights, buy at a discount to a conservative broker-indicated net price or sign a minimum-price sale mandate plus upside split. Do not spend meaningful capital before title, registry status, broker route, and market bid are verified. The 100,000 PLN budget covers legal/broker setup, title checks, travel, admin, small option deposits, and possibly one small right purchase; it should not be used to speculate on price movements.

Copy risk:
ESCOs, energy auditors, energy traders, brokers, TGE members, obligated parties, and energy-law firms can copy pieces. The defense is not secret law or a platform; it is finding neglected issued rights before incumbents bother with them, signing exclusive options/mandates, building source relationships with auditors/ESCOs/facility managers, proving fast cash settlement, and refusing application-stage consulting. Once the startup controls a specific right under a signed option or mandate, competitors cannot sell that same right without displacing the legal holder relationship.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After the right holder signs the exclusive option or mandate and the startup verifies title, registry evidence, owner authority, broker route, and sale instruction, the founder controls the specific monetization path for that issued right. An energy auditor, ESCO, broker, or trader can market a similar service, but cannot monetize that exact right unless the holder breaks the signed control arrangement or the startup fails title/broker execution.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell energy audits, certificate applications, compliance reports, dashboards, trading screens, introductions, or a marketplace. It controls specific issued property-right cashout files through signed options, title acquisition, or exclusive sale mandates and gets paid from actual settlement. Application-stage files and "maybe we can get certificates" projects are rejected.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, energy audit opinions, URE application services, financial/investment advice, market-price guarantees, tax advice, licensed brokerage, or exchange trading unless it has the required authorization. Licensed brokers, TGE members, tax advisers, energy lawyers, auditors, accountants, and right holders remain responsible for regulated trading, tax treatment, legal authority, accounting, and formal filings. The startup must verify title, authority, registry status, pledge/lien/dispute status, expiry/validity, consultant/ESCO claims, and broker route before accepting any file.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, CBAM Import Continuity, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, or a marketplace/platform release desk. It is a cashout option over an already-issued tradable energy-efficiency property right, not a compliance evidence pack, product listing rescue, investor memo, physical salvage suite, import/customs desk, or deduction recovery mandate.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Natural incumbents are strong: ESCOs, energy auditors, energy brokers, TGE members, traders, energy lawyers, and obligated parties already understand the market. Many holders may not actually own a clean right because of ESCO contracts, consultant fees, pledges, board approvals, title defects, expiry, prior sale commitments, or application-stage confusion. The founder must avoid unlicensed brokerage or investment advice. Deal sizes may be too small unless rights volumes are large. Liquidity and price can move before settlement. Public-sector or cooperative sellers may need slow board/procurement approvals. Sellers may prefer the original ESCO/auditor or a known broker once they realize the asset has value. A pure success fee can produce lumpy cash flow, while buying rights creates price and title risk. The whole idea fails if issued-right holders are rare, if incumbents already cash them out routinely, or if legal/broker structure makes the startup an unnecessary middleman.
