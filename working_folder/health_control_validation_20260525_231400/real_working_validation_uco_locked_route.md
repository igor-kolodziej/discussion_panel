## Strongest Objections Captured

- UCO collection is a real market, but structurally looks like commodity route aggregation rather than a defensible startup.
- Existing Polish/Warsaw operators already offer restaurant pickup, payment, barrels, KPO/documentation, contracts, and national coverage.
- The likely spread is too thin after site payments, collector/provider fees, containers, admin, route coordination, quality losses, buyer deductions, and working capital.
- Restaurant exclusivity is fragile because sites can switch for more cash, faster pickup, fresh-oil discounts, better containers, or lower hassle.
- Compliance boundaries are risky: waste collection, transport, storage, processing, documentation, and operator-of-record duties must stay with registered/authorized parties, and the startup can blur the line quickly.
- Quality risk can erase margin through water, food residue, mixed waste, unsafe storage, fraud, rejection, or buyer downgrades.
- UCO fraud scrutiny is real in EU biofuel supply chains, raising the documentation and buyer-trust bar.
- Part-time founder fit is poor because the business needs site visits, barrel placement, training, pickup coordination, complaints, ticketing, weighing, invoices, reconciliation, and contamination disputes.

## Evaluator's Best Narrowing

The evaluator said the only plausible POC is one controlled food-service cluster, such as a mall food court, ghost kitchen building, hotel group, catering commissary, or franchisee group with 10-30 locations. Minimum proof would be:

- one downstream buyer commitment with exact accepted-oil price formula, quality deductions, payment timing, minimum volume, and rejection-risk allocation;
- one registered collector/provider agreement naming operator-of-record, documentation responsibility, container ownership, and spill/transport liability;
- 20-30 kitchens in one dense cluster;
- two paid pickup cycles;
- actual gross margin above 20% after all costs.

Even with that proof, the evaluator revised only to 68 / 100.

## Search Lesson

Physical route control is not enough when the underlying stream is a competed commodity and the founder controls the hardest, lowest-margin layer. Avoid UCO/waste-route branches unless the route is already captive through one central operator and the buyer price/collector role are both pre-signed before any restaurant acquisition.
