## Strongest Objections

- Responsibility matrices and TPSP evidence packs are recognizable artifacts; large vendors already publish them.
- Many buyers will need formal PCI work, remediation, SAQ/ROC, or QSA assessment, not just a response pack.
- Unknown founder credibility is a real blocker for PCI wording used with customers, QSAs, and acquirers.
- QSAs, PCI consultants, GRC platforms, and security consultants can copy the offer.
- Partner channel may be harder than assumed because QSAs may avoid referral risk or keep the work.
- Written acknowledgments and scope notes carry legal/contractual risk.
- Cold outbound will be low-yield unless trigger-based.
- Annual refresh may be too weak for durable LTV.
- Five-business-day turnaround can be operationally dangerous if the vendor's architecture, policies, data flow, or prior statements are messy.

## Reviewer Suggested Narrow Version

Only sell:

Emergency PCI v4.0.1 TPSP customer evidence response for payment-adjacent SaaS vendors with a live customer/QSA/acquirer/procurement blocker.

Day-60 pass criteria would require:

- 3 prepaid vendors.
- 5 live request threads under NDA.
- 2 explicit acceptances from customer, QSA, acquirer, or procurement.
- 1 referral from a PCI consultant/QSA/fractional CISO/payment agency.
- 1 case where revenue release is attributed to the pack.

## Lesson

The DORA-like evidence-pack pattern does not automatically pass in adjacent compliance regimes. The evaluator wants a stronger structural moat than accepted wording and partner referrals when QSAs/consultants can copy the artifact. Avoid more generic customer-evidence packs unless the gate is unusually sharp and incumbents cannot credibly internalize it.
