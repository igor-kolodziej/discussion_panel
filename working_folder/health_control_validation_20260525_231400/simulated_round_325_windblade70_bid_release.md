## Official Timing Notes

- Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/718 was published on 23 March 2026 and lays down minimum environmental sustainability requirements for public procurement procedures involving certain net-zero technologies under Regulation (EU) 2024/1735.
- The implementing regulation covers onshore and offshore wind technologies and requires wind turbine blades to have a recyclability rate of at least 70%, calculated as relative weight of recyclable material and demonstrated at the latest on completion of contract execution.
- The requirements apply to covered public procurement procedures launched on or after 30 June 2026.

## Search Frame

Recent failures show that generic evidence packs fail when the buyer could ask a consultant, lawyer, broker, or internal team. This round only considers tender cases where a new mandatory requirement creates a bid-or-no-bid artifact for a named turbine model, named blade, named tender, and named bid deadline. The controlled unit is the live bid file, OEM blade evidence request, EN 45555-style recyclability calculation support, reviewer issue list, clarification text, and bid/no-bid decision, not a sustainability report.

The best case is not a giant OEM already bidding its own turbine. The buyer is a smaller EU/CEE wind EPC, repowering developer, municipal energy company partner, public-utility contractor, turbine reseller/representative, or procurement adviser that is bidding or supporting a public wind procurement and needs to prove whether its proposed turbine/blade package can survive the new 70% blade-recyclability clause.

## Why It Clears Simulation

- The acute trigger is unusually concrete: covered public wind procurements launched on or after 30 June 2026 must handle a specific 70% blade-recyclability requirement.
- The product is tied to a named bid deadline, not general sustainability readiness.
- Contract values are large enough to support 18,000-45,000 PLN fixed fees for urgent bid evidence.
- A bid team can pay before final tender outcome because the artifact helps decide whether to bid, clarify, substitute turbine model, request OEM evidence, or no-go.
- Case memory can compound by tender wording, turbine model, blade family, OEM response pattern, EN 45555 calculation format, reviewer objections, and accepted clarification language.
- The founder does not certify recyclability. Qualified blade/LCA/recycling reviewers issue technical issue-spotting; the OEM/manufacturer remains source of truth.

## Internal Cap Notes

- This barely clears simulation. It should fail if early tenders are too sparse, if OEMs provide sufficient evidence directly, or if only major OEMs with internal bid teams face the requirement.
- Incumbent risk is high: wind consultancies, procurement lawyers, OEM bid teams, engineering firms, and LCA advisers can copy the visible workflow.
- The six-month POC only works if acquisition starts from exact live tender clauses and specific proposed turbine/blade models, not from generic education.
- Reject any case requiring legal tender advice, engineering certification, blade BOM verification, recycling technology certification, project design, warranty advice, or authority representation.
