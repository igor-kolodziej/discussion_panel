## Selected Candidate

Idea name: TwoCodePay Buyer-Issued Exception Release Queue

Simulated score: 88.1/100

### Thesis

Suppliers with large accepted-payable invoices stuck behind buyer-issued AP/portal exceptions will pay a narrow release fee to a founder who only takes two clean exception families, controls the supplier mandate and portal contact, and turns buyer exception text into cleared status or a binary no-go.

### Strict Control Logic

Accepted cases must satisfy all of these:

- The buyer has already accepted the underlying work or delivery, or has otherwise confirmed the invoice is payable after a specific process correction.
- The buyer has issued exact exception text/code before intake.
- The exception belongs to one of two families:
  - missing service entry, goods receipt, work confirmation, delivery acceptance, or PO-line acceptance after accepted work/delivery;
  - invoice/EDI/portal-state correction where the buyer already confirms the invoice is processable/payable after correction.
- The supplier signs a mandate covering that invoice queue, portal/ticket access through a named contact, release-fee rights, and before-state evidence capture.

Rejected cases:

- collections, disputed debt, buyer cash delay, litigation threats, price disputes, PO exhaustion, quality disputes, sanctions/UBO/vendor-master/bank-change, tax advice, fraud/compliance reviews, and cases where no buyer-issued exception exists.

### 60-Day Proof

- 4-5 paid supplier mandates.
- 1m-2m PLN accepted-payable invoice value under mandate.
- 3 buyer acknowledgements, cleared exception statuses, scheduled remittance notices, or explicit no-go reasons.
- 1-2 paid releases or remittances.
- 40k-100k PLN collected setup/release fees or fee invoices tied to released cash.

### Economics

- Intake requires a minimum queue, likely 150k-250k PLN accepted-payable value.
- Fee structure: 3k-8k PLN triage/setup per queue plus 3-7% of released invoice value, capped where needed for enterprise acceptability.
- Owner earnings can work if the founder stays inside two exception families and avoids long-tail AR chasing.
- Cash conversion is credible because the buyer has already issued the failure state and the founder only works on current invoices with release paths, not speculative claims.

### Why This Clears Simulation

The candidate is ugly, narrow, and cash-linked. It uses an existing buyer-issued exception as the routing artifact, not a founder-generated report. The first control proof is signed mandate plus live exception queue, and the first outcome is cleared status/remittance/no-go, not interest. It also responds directly to prior evaluator objections against broad AP chasing by refusing most AR failure states.

### Main Simulation Risks

- The market may still view this as ordinary AP/AR follow-up.
- Suppliers may expect their own finance team or the buyer's portal support to fix these issues.
- A narrow two-code scope may reduce TAM and make acquisition episodic.
- If buyer-side AP portals are opaque, the founder may not reliably cause release inside 60 days.
- Incumbents can copy the pattern if they see it, though only after the founder has already controlled specific invoice queues.

## Gate Decision

Simulated score is strictly above 87, so this candidate advances to real working-chat validation.
