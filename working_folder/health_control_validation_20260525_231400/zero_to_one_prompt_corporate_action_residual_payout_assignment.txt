Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Corporate-Action Residual Payout Assignment

One-sentence thesis:
Corporate and professional holders with already-announced corporate-action cash sitting at an issuer, depositary, trustee, broker, or paying agent will pay for a narrow mandate/assignment desk that gets payor acknowledgement and routes the named payout.

Exact buyer:
Microfunds, family offices, founder holding companies, old SPVs, dormant ESOP administrators, small investment companies, and corporate legal or finance teams that can point to a named corporate-action, redemption, liquidation, squeeze-out, delisting, merger, dividend, or bond-redemption payout that is already payable but not collected.

Acute trigger:
A transaction, fund wind-down, audit, entity cleanup, broker account closure, cap-table cleanup, year-end review, or cash need reveals that a payable corporate-action amount remains stranded because identity, bank, broker, entity, tax, signatory, or legacy paperwork is incomplete.

Control point:
A narrow collection mandate or receivable assignment over one named payable corporate-action amount, backed by issuer notice, depositary statement, trustee notice, broker record, corporate-action notice, or other payor evidence, followed by written acknowledgement or accepted payment-instruction handling from the issuer, depositary, trustee, broker, or paying agent.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five signed holder mandates or receivable assignments over named payable amounts above 50,000 PLN each; two written acknowledgements or accepted instruction paths from a payor, broker, depositary, trustee, or paying agent; one payout, credit, or regulated escrow receipt; and a paid file fee or success-fee invoice on the first recovered amount.

6-month POC:
Source cases through boutique corporate-law firms, M&A accountants, fund administrators, family-office accountants, ESOP administrators, small brokers, and issuer-service providers. Accept only corporate or professional holders with event evidence and a named payable amount. Use counsel-reviewed mandate and assignment templates, no-custody payment directions into holder accounts or regulated escrow, and a 30-day close/kill workflow: accepted instruction, requested correction list, or no-go. Target 5-10 signed files, 3 acknowledged payor instructions, 2 payouts, and 25,000-75,000 PLN collected.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to professional channels that already see stale payout cleanup: boutique corporate-law firms after M&A or squeeze-outs, accountants cleaning old SPVs and family-office entities, fund administrators winding down vehicles, ESOP administrators, and small brokers with orphaned corporate-action cash. The initial offer is a no-broad-search cleanup sprint for named B2B payouts, not retail heir finding.

Economics:
Minimum named payout 50,000 PLN. Charge a 3,000-8,000 PLN paid file fee for accepted clean files plus 10-20% of recovered cash, or only a 12-25% success fee for larger files. Expected gross margin 60-80% after counsel/notary/reviewer costs because the product is document control, payor acknowledgement, and payout routing, not litigation or securities work. No principal is committed unless a discounted receivable purchase is used after payor acknowledgement.

Copy risk:
Brokers, transfer agents, lawyers, fund administrators, accountants, and issuer-service providers can copy the workflow. The defense is narrow control over specific files: signed holder mandate or assignment, complete event evidence, payor acknowledgement, and an accepted payment instruction before a generalist decides to internalize the work.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Incumbents can serve future cases, but they cannot redirect or recover a specific named payout once the holder has signed the mandate or receivable assignment, the event evidence is organized, and the payor, broker, trustee, depositary, or paying agent has acknowledged the instruction path. The startup does not own a broad market; it controls individual acknowledged payout files.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell analysis, search results, introductions, monitoring, software, or a marketplace. It only accepts named payable corporate-action cash where the holder signs a mandate or assignment and the outcome is payor acknowledgement and recovered cash or a clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No securities advice, investment recommendations, securities trading, consumer heir-hunting, unclaimed-property mass claiming, inheritance disputes, litigation, tax advice, regulated custody, issuer-of-record work, broker-of-record work, or paying-agent work. Licensed counsel reviews templates and flagged legal questions. Payments go to the holder account or regulated escrow unless a legally reviewed receivable purchase is used after payor acknowledgement.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack Safety, or HeatQuiet. It is closest in structure to recovery-mandate ideas, but the control point is different: a named corporate-action cash entitlement with a mandate or assignment and payor acknowledgement, not retailer deductions, platform recovery, regulatory evidence, import continuity, or municipal routing.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Paying agents, brokers, or depositaries may reject third-party mandates or receivable assignments. Professional channels may not reveal enough named stranded payouts. Many cases may require legal, tax, inheritance, sanctions/KYC, or securities advice. Existing lawyers, brokers, transfer agents, and fund administrators may internalize the work. Unknown-founder trust is hard because the files involve money and ownership records. Timelines may be slower than 60 days. Ticket sizes may be too small or too episodic. Fraud and identity risk may force bank-like compliance. The wedge may become bespoke legal admin rather than a repeatable company.
