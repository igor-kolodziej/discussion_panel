Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Atlassian Data Center App Continuity Buyout.

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or option tiny Atlassian Marketplace Data Center apps whose vendors are exiting support during the official Data Center wind-down, then monetize the exact listing, app key, source, support history, and customer renewal stream through customer-prepaid continuity and migration-support contracts before closing.

Exact buyer:
The economic seller is a one-to-five-person Atlassian Marketplace partner with a paid Jira or Confluence Data Center app, 10-150 active enterprise customers, low public profile, weak cloud roadmap, and owner fatigue from the Data Center sunset.

The economic payor is an existing enterprise Data Center customer whose internal Jira or Confluence workflow, audit record, macro content, reporting view, workflow condition, admin automation, or compliance process depends on that exact app until migration or retirement.

Acute trigger:
Atlassian Data Center is winding down through 2026-2029, and customers must either migrate, replace, or maintain current Data Center workflows during a multi-year transition. A small vendor may stop support, stop compatibility releases, fail to publish a credible cloud path, or be unable to answer enterprise procurement and security renewal requests. The customer pain is not new app shopping; it is that the customer already depends on this app and cannot have it abandoned before its migration window closes.

Control point:
Seller-signed option over the exact Marketplace app listing, app key, source repository, build and release pipeline, license and sales history, support inbox, issue history, docs domain, trademarks where needed, and customer-contact handoff. Atlassian Marketplace app-transfer support path and proof-of-transfer package are prepared before close. Customer-signed continuity orders or renewal commitments for the current installed app are secured before meaningful seller payout.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One seller option over a named paid Data Center app with at least 10 active enterprise customers and clean source/release access. App transfer checklist prepared with seller admin approval, proof-of-transfer draft, partner account details, repo/build credentials inventory, IP assignment draft, and support-history export. Five existing customers sign continuity or migration-support orders totaling at least 80,000 PLN, conditional on the transfer completing and excluding bespoke migration projects. One compatibility, security, or support-maintenance release plan is reviewed by an Atlassian contractor before close. Seller payout is earnout-heavy and starts only after customer payments or Marketplace renewal cash routes to the new owner.

6-month POC:
Close one app transfer or operate under a signed management/option structure approved by seller counsel and Marketplace transfer path. Retain at least 70% of contacted renewal-value from the first customer cohort. Collect 180,000-500,000 PLN from continuity orders, Marketplace renewals, and bounded migration-support add-ons. Ship two low-risk compatibility/security/support releases. Resolve 15 support tickets without bespoke customer development becoming more than 35% of delivery time. Source two additional seller options, but only advance a second if first-app support margin exceeds 55%.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of small paid Jira and Confluence Data Center apps with sparse release activity, no credible cloud migration page, old owner/operator signals, low review volume but visible enterprise use cases, and support commitments that look hard to maintain through 2028.

Seller outreach: "If Data Center wind-down makes your app support burden unattractive, I can option the app, preserve your customers, and pay you from transferred renewal/continuity cash. No cash-heavy acquisition before customers confirm they need continuity."

Customer proof outreach, with seller intro: "The current vendor is considering a supported continuity handoff for the exact app you already run. We are collecting paid continuity commitments only from customers that need updates/support through their Data Center migration window. No rewrite, no broad consulting, no unsupported fork."

Economics:
Customer continuity order: 15,000-60,000 PLN per enterprise account for 12 months of bounded maintenance, security-fix handling, compatibility releases, and migration-readiness support for the existing app. Seller economics: small option fee plus 20-40% earnout on retained renewal/continuity cash for 12-24 months. Contractor cost: Atlassian developer/security reviewer paid only after seller option and customer proof. Gross margin target: 55-75% if the app is technically bounded and support history shows reusable maintenance. Cash conversion: customer payments and Marketplace renewal cash precede or fund seller payout.

Copy risk:
Atlassian partners, Marketplace consolidators, app vendors, migration consultants, and enterprise IT consultancies can copy the category. They cannot copy the exact app listing, app key, installed customer base, support history, customer trust trail, and seller endorsement once the startup has a signed option and customer continuity orders for that app.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The founder is not selling a generic Data Center migration service. The controlled object is the named Marketplace app and its current renewal/support stream. Once the seller signs the option and customers sign continuity orders tied to that exact app, competitors would need to replace the installed workflow, convince the seller to breach or abandon the option, or persuade customers to migrate under deadline pressure. That is possible long term, but not before the first continuity cash is captured.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The acquired object is an operating Marketplace app payment stream: listing, app key, source, releases, customer licenses, support inbox, documentation, and renewal cash. The founder buys or operates the app and maintains bounded continuity for existing paying customers. The startup does not sell a report, dashboard, marketplace, generic brokered introduction, or net-new custom Atlassian consulting.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Exclude apps touching payments, regulated health data, law-enforcement workflows, secrets management, privileged identity, destructive automation, or customer production data beyond normal app operation. Exclude apps with unclear IP, copied code, unresolved security incidents, forged reviews, fake installs, sanctions risk, or unavailable seller admin approval. Do not promise Atlassian approval, customer migration success, security certification, or indefinite support past the Data Center sunset. Customer data-processing terms, IP assignment, Atlassian partner-account transfer, vulnerability handling, credential rotation, and release signing are reviewed before closing. Bespoke cloud migration, large rewrites, customer-specific workflow rebuilds, and 24/7 critical incident response are separate no-go or partner-routed work.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not MaintainerExit because there is no free-riding open-source package; customers pay through Marketplace licenses or continuity orders for a commercial app they already use. This is not Shopify Ops App Continuity because the buyer is enterprise Atlassian Data Center customers under a sunset window, not low-ARPU Shopify merchants; the controlled artifact includes official Marketplace app key/listing, enterprise install base, source/release chain, and support history. This is not OneStack Critical Connector because it is not a custom ERP connector book; it is a platform Marketplace app with a formal transfer path and many installed enterprise app instances. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, RAGProbe, AIQ, AppExchange retest, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Seller sourcing may be very hard: good apps are expensive, bad apps have support debt, and small vendors may prefer to sunset quietly. Enterprise customers may already plan to migrate off Data Center or replace the app, reducing willingness to prepay continuity. Atlassian Marketplace transfer, partner account, tax, payouts, licenses, DPAs, and app listing details can slow or block transfer. Technical debt may be the reason the owner is exiting. Enterprise customers may not trust an unknown new owner with app maintenance. Atlassian partners and app consolidators can buy attractive apps faster if the target is visible. Data Center sunset creates a finite horizon; the POC must pay back before the market winds down. Support can drift into custom Jira/Confluence administration, cloud migration consulting, or critical incident work. A single acquired app is a cash-flow asset, not a company, unless seller sourcing and bounded support repeat.
