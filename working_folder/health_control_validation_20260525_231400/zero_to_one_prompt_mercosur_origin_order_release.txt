Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Mercosur Origin Order Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
A customs-broker-routed origin-evidence desk for Polish and CEE exporters/importers whose first EU-Mercosur tariff-preference orders, shipments, landed-cost quotes, distributor onboardings, or customer purchase decisions are blocked until a truthful rules-of-origin file, supplier-declaration trail, product/HS mapping, and broker-reviewed preference claim can be assembled after the 1 May 2026 provisional application.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE machinery, industrial equipment, automotive component, chemical, plastics, packaging, agri-food, wine/spirits, olive oil, specialty food, pharmaceutical-adjacent non-regulated goods, and B2B component exporters selling to Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, or Uruguay, plus importers buying Mercosur-origin goods where tariff preference affects landed cost. The first buyer has a named order, distributor/customer request, shipment, RFQ, price renegotiation, or import preference claim with visible tariff savings or order value above 150,000 PLN. Exclude controlled goods, sanctions cases, veterinary/phytosanitary holds, quota allocation fights, legal origin rulings, tariff classification disputes, and products where a customs broker or lawyer must decide the legal position.

Acute trigger:
Since the EU-Mercosur interim trade agreement started provisional application on 1 May 2026, a Mercosur customer, importer, distributor, freight forwarder, customs broker, or buyer asks for preference-origin evidence before confirming a purchase order, accepting landed-cost pricing, releasing a shipment, claiming reduced duty, onboarding a supplier, or approving the first order under the new agreement. The commercial event is a named order, shipment, quote, distributor onboarding, or duty-saving claim, not abstract market-entry advice.

Control point:
Signed order-release mandate over the named Mercosur order/shipment/preference claim; customer or importer request text; product list; HS code and tariff-preference value supplied by the customs broker/importer; invoice/pro-forma/order value; bill of materials or production description supplied by the exporter; supplier declarations and material origin evidence; product-specific rule checklist prepared under broker review; statement-on-origin or certificate path where applicable; direct-transport/non-alteration evidence; broker/red-flag review route; customer-facing preference-origin file; clean no-go log; and order/shipment/preference outcome tracker. The startup controls the evidence room, missing-document chase, customer-ready origin file, broker review packet, and fee right for the named order. It does not classify goods, issue legal origin opinions, file customs declarations, act as exporter/importer of record, or guarantee customs acceptance.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign three customs-broker, freight-forwarder, export-adviser, or chamber/referral routes for live EU-Mercosur origin files; take six prepaid order-release cases at 8,000-28,000 PLN each; every case has a named order/shipment/RFQ, visible tariff-preference value, product list, customer/importer request, and broker/importer owner for HS/origin final decisions. Produce at least four documented outcomes: customer accepts the origin file for pricing/order progress, broker approves the evidence packet for submission, preference claim is filed, shipment moves, distributor onboarding progresses, order is confirmed, or paid clean no-go because origin cannot be supported. Collect at least 80,000 PLN within 60 days.

6-month POC:
Process 40-80 live EU-Mercosur origin/order-release cases across 10-25 exporters/importers and 5-8 broker/forwarder/adviser routes; collect 500,000-1,400,000 PLN in prepaid case fees and broker-wholesale fees; document 25+ customer acceptances, broker-approved files, confirmed orders, preference claims, shipment progress events, distributor onboardings, or clean no-go outcomes; build a private product-rule and evidence-gap taxonomy by HS family, product-specific rule, BOM problem, supplier-declaration gap, customer wording, broker red flag, and accepted file format; keep gross margin above 65% after broker/origin reviewer costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with customs brokers, freight forwarders, chambers of commerce, export advisers, industry associations, and Polish/CEE exporters that already sell or quote into Brazil, Argentina, Paraguay, or Uruguay. Partner pitch: "When a client has a first Mercosur order or shipment where tariff preference depends on messy origin evidence, route the evidence assembly file to me. You keep classification, legal, and customs decisions. I collect supplier declarations, BOM/production facts, direct-transport evidence, missing documents, and customer-ready proof under your review." Direct outreach targets exporters advertising Mercosur/South America sales, trade-fair participation, distributor searches, or machinery/industrial/agri-food exports.

Economics:
Charge 8,000-12,000 PLN for a triage/no-go origin-evidence screen, 15,000-28,000 PLN for a named order/shipment origin-release file, and 30,000-60,000 PLN for multi-product first-order packs or distributor onboarding batches. Broker review fees are passed through or wholesale-priced. The fee is rational when a 15,000-28,000 PLN file helps confirm a 150,000-2,000,000 PLN order, preserve margin after tariff preference, satisfy a distributor, or avoid a wrong unsupported preference claim.

Copy risk:
Customs brokers, freight forwarders, chambers, trade advisers, export consultants, law firms, in-house trade teams, and ERP/global-trade tools can copy pieces. The defense is not customs law. It is fast control of named first-order cases, broker-routed overflow, messy supplier-declaration chase, product-rule evidence memory, customer-ready file formats, and clean no-go discipline for SMEs that brokers do not want to handhold document-by-document.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the exporter/importer signs the mandate, pays the case fee, provides the named order/shipment/RFQ, customer/importer request, product list, supplier contacts, BOM/production facts, and chosen broker review route, the startup controls the evidence file, missing-document chase, customer-ready preference packet, no-go record, and fee right for that order. A broker or consultant can compete generally, but must displace an already-authorized file while the order, pricing, shipment, or distributor onboarding is waiting.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business refuses generic Mercosur opportunity memos, market-entry reports, export strategy, customs brokerage, legal opinions, dashboards, tariff databases, and broker introductions without a live order/shipment/preference request. It accepts only named tariff-preference commercial blockers and produces an origin-evidence release file: product list, rule/evidence checklist under broker review, supplier-declaration trail, missing-proof log, customer-facing packet, and order/preference/no-go outcome record.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as customs broker, freight forwarder, exporter of record, importer of record, legal adviser, tax adviser, customs declarant, chamber authority, tariff-classification adviser, origin-certifying body, sanctions adviser, or trade-finance provider. It does not determine HS classification, legal origin status, customs value, quota eligibility, controlled-goods status, or final preference eligibility. Exporters, importers, customs brokers, freight forwarders, chambers, customs authorities, and counsel remain responsible for classification, legal origin decisions, customs filings, transport, certificates, and tariff claims. Cases involving sanctions, controlled goods, veterinary/phytosanitary controls, quota fights, suspected false supplier declarations, or legal origin disputes are declined or routed to qualified advisers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not CBAM Import Continuity Desk because it is not carbon-border imports, embedded-emissions evidence, CBAM quarterly reporting, certificate-price exposure, customs-broker CBAM memos, or import continuity under EU climate rules. It is not PromoLeak, DORA, REDBlocked, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, BatteryFit, HeritageDoor, BioSignal, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, TraceFaktura, EUDR, F-Gas, ICS2, DIWASS, Amazon recovery, 3PL recovery, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The buyer trigger, control artifact, payment event, and proof are materially different: named EU-Mercosur tariff-preference orders or shipments with broker-reviewed origin evidence and customer/importer acceptance.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Customs brokers and freight forwarders may already handle rules-of-origin evidence. SMEs may not have enough Mercosur order volume in the first six months. Many cases require legal classification, product-specific rule interpretation, supplier-origin verification, quota knowledge, or customs decisions outside startup control. Customer/importer acceptance does not guarantee customs acceptance. Supplier declarations can be missing, false, or too slow. Exporters may resist paying before the first order is certain. The 1 May 2026 opportunity may create interest but not enough urgent blockers. Broker channels may compress margin or internalize the checklist. Multi-product origin files can become slow BOM/supplier-chasing work. The founder must avoid unauthorized customs brokerage, legal origin opinions, and liability for unsupported preference claims.
