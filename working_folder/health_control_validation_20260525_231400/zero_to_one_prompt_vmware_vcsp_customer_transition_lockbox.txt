Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
VCSPExit Customer Transition Lockbox.

One-sentence thesis:
Small European VMware cloud/service providers, MSPs, and reseller-adjacent operators disrupted by Broadcom/VMware partner-route changes can option their named customer renewal and transition queues to a lockbox operator that secures customer-approved payment direction, authorized-provider handoff, and first transition fees before any seller payout.

Exact buyer:
The acquisition/seller target is a small European VMware cloud service provider, MSP, VAR-adjacent operator, or hosting provider with 10-100 VMware-dependent business customers, current renewal/support/entitlement/migration confusion, and weak or lost ability to transact future VMware business through its old channel.

The economic payor is the existing customer that needs continuity for VMware-based hosted workloads, on-prem support, support entitlement access, renewal decisioning, license inventory cleanup, or migration away from the old provider. A second payor can be an authorized Broadcom/VMware partner, migration partner, or alternative platform provider that pays for a qualified, customer-approved transition order.

Acute trigger:
The old provider can no longer reliably handle the customer's next VMware support, renewal, cloud-service continuation, entitlement, or migration path, or no longer wants to carry the workload under the changed Broadcom/VMware partner environment. The customer has a current support expiry, hosted workload renewal, entitlement/download/support-access issue, budget deadline, or migration deadline. The seller has relationship access but not a clean future path; the customer has urgency but does not want a cold replacement search.

Control point:
Seller-signed option over a named customer transition queue plus customer-approved transition files: customer list, current invoices, contract/renewal dates, VMware environment summary, entitlement or support status where available, open support/portal issues, current service scope, customer contact permission, old-provider warm handoff, authorized-provider or migration-partner quote path, customer-approved payment direction for transition/admin work, and a fee right before seller payout.

The startup does not sell VMware licenses unless properly authorized, does not act as Broadcom/VMware partner of record, does not provide legal licensing opinions, and does not operate production infrastructure. Authorized providers remain seller/provider of record for licenses or replacement services.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One seller option over a named transition queue with at least 15 current VMware-dependent customers and no upfront seller payout beyond a small diligence fee. Five customers sign transition/admin orders or payment-direction acknowledgements before close. One authorized provider or migration partner signs a receiving-route agreement for those customers. At least 50,000 PLN in customer transition/admin fees, authorized-partner fees, or first-month managed transition cash is collected before seller earnout starts. At least one customer receives an accepted renewal/support/entitlement path, authorized-provider quote, migration plan, or clean no-go decision.

6-month POC:
Option 2-3 seller queues. Move 20-50 customers into signed transition/admin orders or authorized-provider handoffs. Collect 250,000-900,000 PLN in transition/admin, referral, or first-month service fees. Produce 15-35 objective outcomes: active authorized-provider quote accepted, renewal/support path clarified, support entitlement restored or documented no-go, migration project sold, customer payment direction transferred, or old-provider service safely wound down. Keep gross margin above 55% after contractor, partner, and seller earnout costs. Reject customers needing emergency production rescue, legal licensing fights, unsupported piracy, sanctions issues, or open infrastructure incidents.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of small VMware cloud/service providers, MSPs, and VAR-adjacent operators in Poland/CEE and Europe that appear affected by Broadcom/VMware channel disruption, customer uncertainty, or owner fatigue. Outreach to sellers:

"If your VMware customer base has renewal/support/migration pressure and your old partner route is no longer attractive, I can option the transition queue, protect your customers, and pay you from customer-approved transition cash. No customer count is valued until the customer approves the handoff, payment direction, and authorized-provider or migration route."

Customer outreach happens only with seller introduction:

"Your current provider is organizing a controlled VMware continuity handoff. We will inventory your renewal/support/entitlement/migration state, route license/provider-of-record work to an authorized provider, and give you either an accepted path or a no-go/migration decision. You pay only for a named transition order, not a generic audit."

Economics:
Seller payout: no material upfront payment; 20-40% earnout on collected transition/admin fees, authorized-partner referral fees, or retained first-month service cash for 6-18 months.

Customer pricing: 5,000-15,000 PLN for a clean renewal/entitlement/authorized-provider handoff file; 15,000-40,000 PLN for multi-cluster or hosted-workload transition planning; 40,000-90,000 PLN only for a customer cluster where partner/migration/provider revenue is already committed.

Authorized-provider economics: 10-25% referral or implementation-origin fee where lawful and disclosed, or wholesale transition subcontract fee if the authorized provider keeps the customer relationship.

The business does not depend on success-fee-only work. Customer or partner cash must precede seller earnout.

Copy risk:
Authorized Broadcom/VMware partners, MSPs, cloud migration firms, VARs, and licensing consultants can copy the category. They cannot copy the exact seller-endorsed customer queue, customer permission trail, transition file, and payment direction once the startup has a signed option and customers have approved the handoff. The durable risk is that authorized partners learn the playbook and source stranded books directly.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The controlled object is not generic VMware consulting. It is the named old-provider customer transition stream: seller endorsement, customer permission, renewal/support dates, entitlement facts, current invoices, payment-direction approvals, authorized-provider quote path, and transition cash. Once that file and payment path are signed, a competitor would need to convince the old provider or customer to unwind an active handoff under deadline pressure.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup is buying or optioning a current transition payment stream and operating the handoff room. It does not merely introduce buyers and sellers, sell a report, build a dashboard, or provide generic VMware advice. Every accepted case must include a named seller queue, customer authorization, current renewal/support/migration trigger, receiving provider or no-go route, payment direction, and objective outcome.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not resell VMware/Broadcom products without authorization. Do not represent yourself as Broadcom, VMware, or an authorized partner unless that is formally true. Do not provide legal licensing opinions, audit-defense advice, antitrust advice, software-use-rights opinions, or contract interpretation. Do not take production administrator access or operate customer infrastructure. Do not promise pricing, support restoration, entitlement correction, partner acceptance, migration success, or Broadcom approval.

Authorized partners, customer counsel, customer IT owners, and licensing specialists remain responsible for license sale, legal interpretation, production changes, and migration execution. Reject cases involving piracy, unsupported download access, sanctions, disputed ownership, active security incident, unpaid vendor litigation, fraud, or emergency production outage.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because the trigger is not regulated-finance ICT third-party-risk evidence or bank renewal questionnaires. It is not REDBlocked because there is no Amazon product-compliance evidence response. It is not BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, RAG/AI security evidence, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, Marketplace KYBC, or an AP/KSeF payment-release desk.

It is not Atlassian Data Center App Continuity because it does not buy a Marketplace app, source code, app key, listing, or installed app customer base. It controls an old provider's VMware customer transition payment stream and routes license/provider-of-record work to authorized parties.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The exact seller profile may be rare. Good customer books may already be acquired or approached by authorized VMware partners. Customers may not trust an unknown transition operator and may prefer a large authorized partner directly. The work can become generic MSP migration consulting, VMware licensing advice, or emergency infrastructure support. Broadcom/VMware rules, customer contracts, and partner terms may restrict referral fees, customer transfers, or representation. Existing MSPs and authorized partners can internalize the playbook after seeing one seller queue. Customers may use the disruption to leave VMware entirely, delay decisions, or renegotiate with incumbents. Some seller books may contain angry customers, undocumented environments, unpaid invoices, unsupported versions, or security debt. The six-month window is tight without existing MSP/channel relationships. The opportunity may be a time-limited arbitrage around a current channel disruption rather than a durable company.
