## Validator Summary

Zero To One called this a good payment-release / go-live evidence desk for commercial EV charging sites where the installer has done physical work but final acceptance is stuck on operational proof: charger serials, OCPP/backend visibility, RFID/payment tests, load management, photos, handover documents, electrical protocols, warranty paperwork, and punch-list closure.

The idea failed because many blocked sites are not evidence problems. They require real technical work such as SIM/router fixes, firmware updates, OCPP configuration, load-management tuning, payment-terminal work, UDT inspection, electrical protocol correction, warranty/OEM action, or charger repair. The desk can document release, no-go, or rework, but it cannot make a broken site acceptable.

## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Many blockers require actual technical work rather than evidence assembly.
2. Good installers, CPOs, OEMs, and distributors already have commissioning checklists and portals.
3. Buyer, CPO, landlord, fleet, or general-contractor acceptance remains outside founder control.
4. Legal and technical boundaries are thin around electrical certification, UDT, metrology, fire safety, building closeout, and payment-terminal readiness.
5. The work can become low-margin punch-list chasing across electricians, backend providers, SIM vendors, OEM portals, and property managers.
6. OEM and backend platforms can automate much of the serial/status/session/warranty evidence over time.
7. Public charging adds AFIR/payment-readiness complexity outside the startup's scope.
8. The first channel must trust an unknown outsider with buyer communications around payment release.
9. Part-time execution may clash with urgent fleet go-live or retention release deadlines.
10. Backend and charger-brand fragmentation can slow early delivery.

## Lesson

Do not continue commissioning/payment-release passport variants unless the founder controls a signed economic gate with repeated site volume before the round begins. Evidence assembly around installed infrastructure still fails when the actual blocker is technical repair, certification, or buyer acceptance.
