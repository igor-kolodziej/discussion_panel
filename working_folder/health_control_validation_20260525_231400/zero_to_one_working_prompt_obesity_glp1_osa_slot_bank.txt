Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, patient-data businesses, and prescription-drug businesses without asset/payment-flow control.

Scored idea:

Obesity/GLP-1 Sleep-Apnea Clearance Slot Bank options an underused home sleep-apnea testing/poligraphy micro-operator or sleep-lab capacity book and reserves a defined block of HSAT/poligraphy device nights plus licensed sleep-physician/pulmonologist interpretation slots for private obesity, metabolic, bariatric, diabetes, and GLP-1 clinics. Clinics prepay diagnostic bundles for high-BMI patients who need fast comorbidity workup, anesthesia/bariatric readiness, CPAP pathway documentation, or treatment-risk screening. The startup does not sell or advertise GLP-1 medicines, prescribe, diagnose, interpret studies, or give treatment advice.

Licensed medical providers remain providers of record and issue reports. The startup controls titled/leased device-night capacity, physician-read slots, old sleep-lab phone/domain handoff, clinic deposits, pickup/return logistics, and reporting SLA workflow.

Buyer / payer:
- private obesity/metabolic/diabetes clinics using GLP-1s as part of physician-led care;
- bariatric surgery clinics and anesthesia-prep pathways;
- CPAP shops or sleep clinics that want faster diagnostic intake;
- 50-60+ overweight patients paying privately through clinics for fast OSA workup;
- underused sleep-lab/poligraphy operator owner who wants guaranteed volume or succession.

Acute trigger:
Obese or GLP-1-treated patients often have suspected sleep apnea or cardiometabolic comorbidity. A clinic may lose follow-up revenue, surgery readiness, or care continuity when sleep testing takes weeks. The urgent buyer is the clinic, not the drug seeker: it needs fast device availability and physician-read reports without building sleep diagnostics internally.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is:
- option/handoff over underused HSAT/poligraphy operator devices, phone/domain/mailbox, pickup-return process, and referral book;
- titled or exclusive-use HSAT/poligraphy device-night capacity for 60 days;
- signed physician/pulmonologist interpretation slots with 48-72h SLA;
- obesity/metabolic clinic prepaid bundle or minimum-volume deposit;
- device logistics: pickup/return, cleaning, sensor replacement, patient instructions provided by provider, report delivery to clinician;
- clinic payment routing and monthly settlement file;
- no Rx sale, no prescription guarantee, no diagnosis by startup, no patient-facing medication claims.

The hard control is: reserved diagnostic nights + physician-read slots + clinic prepaid bundle + old sleep-lab/operator handoff.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 10,000-25,000 PLN option/revenue-share over an underused HSAT/poligraphy operator/device book;
- 15,000-35,000 PLN device rental, deposit, or purchase for 8-12 HSAT/poligraphy units, only with clinic deposits;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN prepaid physician-read capacity for 40-60 studies;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN legal/GDPR/medical-device/clinic-contract review;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN logistics, cleaning, sensor stock, packaging, phone/CRM/payment workflow.

Required proof:
1. Signed option/handoff over one underused sleep-testing operator or sleep-lab capacity book.
2. Exclusive-use capacity for at least 80 device nights in the next 60 days.
3. Signed interpretation SLA for at least 40 physician/pulmonologist reads.
4. Two obesity/metabolic/bariatric clinics sign prepaid bundles or minimum-volume deposits.
5. 25,000+ PLN prepaid clinic deposits before buying/renting additional devices.
6. 30 completed studies in 60 days with reports issued by licensed providers directly to clinic/patient.
7. Gross margin above 35% after device, physician read, logistics, sensors, cleaning, patient support, returns, seller/operator earnout, and admin.
8. No GLP-1/Rx advertising, no prescribing, no diagnosis by startup, no CPAP/device medical advice by startup.

Economics:
Revenue comes from clinic-paid diagnostic bundle spread, device-night logistics/admin margin, rush-slot fee for clinics, volume commitment revenue from obesity/metabolic/bariatric clinics, and later CPAP-shop or sleep-lab handoff fee where lawful and patient-choice-preserving.

Payback logic:
The founder does not open a clinic. The founder options existing underused sleep-test capacity, locks physician reads, secures clinic deposits, completes 30 paid studies, and only then buys/leases more devices. The proof is clinic deposit flow and report-throughput capacity, not consumer ads.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
This is not GLP-1 sales, wellness coaching, or a sleep app. The asset is reserved diagnostic capacity plus prepaid clinic demand and licensed provider reads. The startup does logistics and slot custody; clinicians own diagnosis and treatment.

Copy risk:
Sleep labs, obesity clinics, and telemedicine providers can copy the workflow. The wedge exists only if the startup controls a specific underused device/reader capacity block, old operator handoff, and prepaid clinic bundle before competitors react. If there is no exclusive capacity or clinic deposit, the score falls below 82.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- No GLP-1/Rx sale, advertising, reservation, delivery, prescribing, or prescription guarantee.
- Licensed providers handle diagnosis, interpretation, reporting, and treatment decisions.
- Startup minimizes patient data and avoids report interpretation.
- Device and report logistics must be reviewed for medical-device, provider-of-record, GDPR, and insurance boundaries.
- Any GLP-1-related clinic wording must be clinic/provider-led and not medicine promotion.

Main risks acknowledged:
- OSA testing capacity may not be scarce enough.
- Clinics can internalize this or contract labs directly.
- Positive patients need diagnosis/treatment/CPAP pathways that the startup cannot control.
- Patient data and report custody must be minimized.
- Device logistics, cleaning, failed returns, and unreadable studies can destroy margins.
- GLP-1 linkage must not become medicine advertising or prescription facilitation.
