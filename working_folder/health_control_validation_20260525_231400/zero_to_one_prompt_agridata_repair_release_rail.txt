Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AgriDiag Data-Release Repair Rail.

One-sentence thesis:
Independent Polish/CEE agricultural repair shops will pay for a white-label Data Act machine-data release rail when a named tractor, combine, sprayer, baler, telehandler, or connected implement repair is stalled by missing OEM portal data, fault history, service history, telematics export, implement/controller records, or repair-context evidence that the machine owner can lawfully authorize for sharing.

Exact buyer:
The first buyer is an independent agricultural machinery repair shop, mobile mechanic, precision-ag technician, or small machinery service firm that already serves farms but lacks reliable access to OEM/dealer portals and connected-machine data. The end customer is a farmer, contractor, or machinery owner with a named machine and a live repair, pre-season service, post-season fault investigation, implement/controller mismatch, recurring fault, high-value repair quote, or service-history question. The startup sells through the shop, not cold to farmers.

Acute trigger:
A repair or service decision is stuck because the independent shop cannot see or organize the machine data needed to finish a credible diagnosis, quote, no-go, or repair plan. Examples include recurring fault codes, machine-hour/service history gaps, telematics event history, implement/controller mismatch records, remote diagnostic exports, software/configuration evidence, sensor/controller logs, or a dealer/OEM portal response. This is not same-hour harvest emergency repair. The first scope is 2-7 business day cases where a data-release packet can change the repair path, stop guesswork, support a repair quote, prevent unnecessary parts, or route the owner back to an OEM dealer with a clean no-go.

Control point:
The startup controls a shop-routed, machine-specific data-release case file:

- signed repair-shop partner mandate;
- machine-owner authorization to request and share connected-product and related-service data with the named shop;
- machine identity: make, model, serial/VIN/PIN where applicable, controller/implement details, owner proof, and service context;
- live repair ticket, quote request, recurring fault note, or service-history question;
- OEM/dealer/app/portal request trail;
- exported data, screenshots, CSV/PDF/API files, or written refusal/narrowing response;
- data-field map and exception log;
- mechanic decision log showing how the data was used or why it was insufficient;
- repair/no-go/dealer-escalation outcome and paid case record.

The mechanic, owner, OEM dealer, and qualified technician remain responsible for diagnosis, repair, safety, warranty, software updates, and machine operation.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days:

1. Sign 3 independent agri repair shops or mobile technicians to route eligible machine-data repair tickets.
2. Close at least 8 prepaid machine-specific cases at 1,500-4,500 PLN each, paid by the shop or passed through to the machinery owner.
3. Each accepted case must have owner authorization, named machine identity, a written repair/service question, a current shop ticket, and a specific data request path.
4. Produce at least 5 completed data-release packets: export, screenshot set, data-field map, written refusal, or no-go.
5. Produce at least 2 hard commercial outcomes: repair quote accepted, repair completed, unnecessary part avoided, owner escalated to OEM dealer with documented reason, service-history dispute resolved, or paid no-go accepted.
6. Reject cases requiring emergency same-day repair, safety certification, illegal access, software bypass, warranty argument, legal threat, or unsupported diagnostic claims.

6-month POC:
Six-month proof is 6-10 shop partners, 40-80 paid machine-specific cases, 120,000-350,000 PLN collected, at least 20 repair/no-go/dealer-escalation outcomes, 2 shops routing 5 or more cases each, gross margin above 60% after mechanic-facing operations and reviewer support, and a reusable request/outcome library by OEM, model, controller, data field, request wording, response time, refusal reason, and repair use.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with independent agri mechanics and service shops, not farmers. The outreach message is: "When a repair is stuck because the dealer portal, app, or machine data is missing, I handle the owner authorization, Data Act request trail, export room, field map, and no-go record under your brand. You keep the repair and customer relationship." Target shops serving John Deere, Claas, New Holland, Case IH, Fendt, Massey Ferguson, Deutz-Fahr, Valtra, JCB, Manitou, Amazone, Horsch, Lemken, Krone, Kuhn, and similar connected machines and implements, but accept only tickets where the mechanic already has a real customer and repair context.

Economics:
Initial pricing is 1,500-4,500 PLN per machine-specific data-release packet, with a 600-1,000 PLN paid triage for uncertain cases. Shops can mark up or pass through the fee. Higher-ticket bundles cover fleets or repeated faults across the same model. The buyer pays because one avoided wrong part, faster quote, cleaner dealer escalation, or completed repair can be worth more than the packet. The founder's costs are structured intake, authorization, data-request operations, secure file handling, data-field mapping, and limited reviewer support. No inventory, no repair tools, and no machine service capital are required.

Copy risk:
OEM dealers, larger repair shops, farm-tech consultants, precision-ag platforms, and legal advisers can copy parts of the workflow. The defense is not a secret legal template. It is shop-routed case flow, owner authorizations, OEM/model-specific data-request paths, response timing memory, refusal patterns, exported data-field maps, mechanic-facing packet formats, and repeated outcomes across multiple independent shops. A competitor cannot take over a specific paid repair ticket once the shop, owner authorization, request trail, and data room are controlled.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup does not claim monopoly over agricultural machine data. It controls specific paid repair-ticket files. Once a shop routes a named machine case and the owner authorizes the startup's request path, the controlled object is the exact case: machine ID, repair question, authorization, data request, response trail, data room, mechanic decision log, and fee right. OEM dealers can still service the machine, but they do not own the independent shop's customer relationship or the founder's cross-shop request/outcome library.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It is not a dashboard or marketplace. It is a case-controlled repair-release rail attached to a paid shop ticket and owner authorization. It does not sell generic Data Act advice, farmer education, a legal demand letter, or a data room template. The output is an operational packet that either gives the mechanic usable machine data, documents a no-go/refusal, or routes the owner to the correct OEM/dealer path with evidence.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not diagnose machines, perform repairs, certify safety, bypass software, alter firmware, unlock tools, defeat access controls, provide legal advice, threaten OEMs, make warranty claims, operate equipment, or handle same-hour harvest emergencies. Mechanics and qualified service providers make repair decisions. Owners authorize data access and remain responsible for lawful sharing. Legal counsel handles disputes, trade-secret/security objections, warranty interpretation, or formal enforcement. Cases involving personal/location data are minimized and handled through written authorization, secure storage, and deletion rules. Any request involving illegal access, safety-critical bypass, emissions tampering, software circumvention, forged ownership, or disputed title is rejected.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is adjacent to the confirmed Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, but the buyer, trigger, payment event, and proof artifact are different. The confirmed idea serves compressed-air service firms and energy auditors producing partner-branded compressor energy-savings evidence memos. AgriDiag serves independent agricultural repair shops working on named machine repair tickets. The trigger is stalled repair or service evidence, not energy-efficiency sales. The proof is a machine-specific repair data packet, mechanic decision log, repair/no-go/dealer-escalation outcome, and paid shop ticket, not a compressor savings memo, formal audit lead, or white-certificate/grant readiness file.

Strongest anticipated objections:
1. OEM dealers may already own the decisive trust, tooling, service-data access, warranty path, and repair expertise.
2. Data Act requests may be too slow for urgent harvest downtime, making the product useful only for a narrower pre-season/post-season or non-emergency subset.
3. Some needed information may be repair manuals, diagnostic tool access, security access, software, or service documentation rather than connected-product data available under the Data Act.
4. Independent mechanics may have aftermarket diagnostic tools, dealer relationships, or informal data sources already.
5. OEMs may respond slowly, narrowly, or with trade-secret/security objections.
6. Mechanics may not want an outsider in the repair flow unless the packet clearly saves time or unlocks revenue.
7. Farmer authorization and data privacy are nontrivial because machine data can reveal location, field activity, yield, working patterns, fuel use, and business-sensitive farm operations.
8. The business may be seasonal and case volume may be lumpy.
9. The workflow can drift into legal enforcement, right-to-repair advocacy, warranty disputes, software bypass, or unsafe repair advice.
10. It may be judged too close to the existing Data Act machine-data confirmed idea unless repair-ticket control proves a distinct paid wedge.
