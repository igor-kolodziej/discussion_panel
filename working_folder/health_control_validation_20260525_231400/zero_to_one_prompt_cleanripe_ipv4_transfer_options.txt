Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CleanRIPE IPv4 Transfer Option Bank

One-sentence thesis:
A Polish/CEE IPv4 transfer-option desk that secures exclusive options or sale mandates over specific clean RIPE-region `/24` IPv4 blocks from underusing SMEs, legacy holders, closing LIRs, and infrastructure-light companies, then closes buyer-deposit-funded transfers or leases to hosting, MSP, VPN, email, cybersecurity, SaaS infrastructure, and network buyers that need clean routable IPv4 space quickly.

Exact buyer:
Hosting companies, managed-service providers, VPN and security-network operators, email/deliverability infrastructure providers, SaaS infrastructure teams, regional ISPs, datacenter operators, and cloud/network resellers that need a clean RIPE-region `/24` or adjacent IPv4 block with verifiable registry holdership, routing history, blacklist status, route/RPKI readiness, and transfer path. The seller side is Polish/CEE companies or legacy holders with unused, underused, or non-core IPv4 resources that can legally transfer or lease the block.

Acute trigger:
The buyer needs clean IPv4 space for a customer deployment, new ASN/peering setup, hosting/VPS growth, VPN/security-node expansion, email infrastructure, cloud cost reduction, M&A/network migration, geolocation/reputation separation, or urgent replacement of abused/dirty leased space. The seller is a company that is closing, merging, shrinking infrastructure, sitting on unused allocations, tired of LIR/admin fees, or unaware that a clean `/24` can be monetized without running network operations.

Control point:
Signed seller-side exclusive option or sale mandate for a named IPv4 block, RIPE Whois/holdership evidence, transfer-eligibility precheck, 24-month restriction check where applicable, seller authority/KYC file, route/RPKI/ROA status, abuse-contact and reputation report, blacklist and hijack-history screen, current route-object/geolocation notes, seller payout terms, buyer qualification, buyer deposit or escrow instruction, receiving LIR or sponsoring LIR path, and transfer/lease closing checklist. The startup controls the specific numbered block option, diligence room, buyer reservation, and closing workflow before any broad marketplace sees the asset.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three seller-side exclusive options or sale mandates over specific clean RIPE-region `/24` blocks or equivalent small transferable IPv4 resources; two qualified buyers place refundable or escrowed deposits for named blocks; one transaction submits a RIPE transfer request or closes a compliant lease with receiving/sponsoring LIR path documented; at least 60,000 PLN in buyer deposits, escrowed funds, success fees, or first-year lease prepayments is controlled before final seller payout; every block has seller authority, transfer eligibility, reputation, blacklist, routing, RPKI/route-object, and abuse-contact diligence completed; no deal proceeds if title, authority, sanctions/KYC, reputation, or permitted-use checks fail.

6-month POC:
Source 15-30 seller-qualified IPv4 blocks or small resource lots; sign 8-12 exclusive options or sale mandates; close 4-8 transfers or annual leases; collect 150,000-600,000 PLN in spreads, success fees, lease setup fees, and managed closing fees; document buyer deposit before seller payout in every completed deal; build a private seller map of Polish/CEE legacy holders, closing LIRs, underannounced/unused prefixes, inactive routing history, and clean-block buyer demand; reject abusive VPN/spam/fraud buyers and dirty-title seller cases; keep founder capital exposure below 100,000 PLN by using options, escrow, and buyer deposits rather than speculative block purchases.

First acquisition mechanism:
Use RIPE, BGP, routing, and public company data to identify Polish/CEE holders of underused, inactive, or non-core IPv4 resources. Contact owners with a concrete offer: "I can find a qualified buyer or annual lessee for your unused clean IPv4 block without you running a sales process. You sign a short exclusive option; I verify transfer eligibility and reputation; buyer funds escrow or deposit before your payout." On the buyer side, approach small hosting providers, MSPs, datacenter operators, VPN/security operators, and email infrastructure teams with named clean blocks rather than a generic brokerage pitch.

Economics:
Charge seller or buyer a 3,000-15,000 PLN managed closing fee on small `/24` transactions, or capture a 10-25% spread where the option price and buyer reservation allow it. For leases, charge a setup fee plus 10-25% of first-year lease value or a monthly management spread, while making the seller and buyer responsible for acceptable-use, abuse handling, routing, and network operations. The buyer pays because clean IPv4 is immediately useful and the fee is small relative to deployment delay, dirty-block replacement, failed email/VPN/customer rollout, or continued cloud IPv4 costs. The seller accepts because the founder brings a qualified buyer, diligence, and escrow without requiring the seller to become an IPv4 market expert.

Copy risk:
IPv4 brokers, IP leasing platforms, datacenter brokers, network consultants, LIRs, hosting companies, and domain/infrastructure brokers can copy pieces. The defense is not a marketplace; it is control of specific Polish/CEE seller options before public listing, clean-block diligence, buyer deposits tied to named blocks, local owner outreach in Polish/CEE, and a private map of underused resource holders.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs an exclusive option or sale mandate for a named prefix and the buyer places a deposit or escrow instruction for that same block, the startup controls that specific numbered asset and closing workflow. Existing IPv4 brokers can find other blocks or compete on the next seller, but they cannot instantly copy or displace the exact block already under option, diligence, and buyer reservation.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not list random IPv4 supply or sell market reports. It accepts only named blocks where the seller signs exclusivity, transfer/lease eligibility can be checked, and a buyer deposit or escrowed reservation can be secured before seller payout. The product is a controlled transaction asset: a specific clean prefix with option documents, diligence file, buyer reservation, escrow/transfer checklist, and close/no-go decision. It is not a dashboard, generic marketplace, SEO brokerage, infrastructure consulting, managed hosting, spam-delisting service, or network-operations business.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as RIPE NCC, LIR of record unless separately and properly structured, ISP, network operator, abuse desk, legal adviser, tax adviser, sanctions/KYC authority, escrow provider, or guarantor of routing/reputation forever. RIPE NCC, LIRs, sponsoring LIRs, buyers, sellers, escrow providers, lawyers, accountants, and network operators remain responsible for registry transfer, contractual terms, tax, compliance, routing, acceptable use, abuse handling, and ongoing network operations. The startup rejects spam, fraud, sanctions, unclear-title, hijacked, disputed, or materially blacklisted blocks.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, BESSGuard, RAGProbe, CyberBind, or any health/Rx/clinical route. It is also not a generic physical-lot salvage idea: the controlled unit is a registry-recognized numbered internet resource with seller exclusivity, buyer deposit, and transfer/lease closing evidence.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The IPv4 secondary market is mature and has existing brokers with reputation, buyer lists, escrow process knowledge, and RIPE/LIR experience. Sellers may already know the value of their blocks and choose known brokers. Buyers may fear abuse history, dirty reputation, hijacking, unclear title, or bad geolocation. A solo founder may struggle to establish trust with network operators and resource holders. RIPE transfer restrictions, LIR/sponsoring requirements, contractual title, sanctions/KYC, tax, and escrow terms can slow deals. IPv4 pricing can be volatile and IPv6 adoption limits long-term upside. Leasing can create abuse-handling and reputation problems if the buyer is not carefully screened. The first proof must be signed exclusivity plus buyer deposit; a list of possible underused prefixes is not enough.
