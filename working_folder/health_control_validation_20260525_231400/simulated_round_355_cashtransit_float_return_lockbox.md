## Search Posture

The latest failures keep punishing evidence desks, portal-release work, assumed partner queues, and asset books whose first seller or buyer is hidden. This round tests a narrower current-cash control point: money already held or reconciled by a cash-in-transit, smart-safe, cash-management, or change-float provider after a site closure, provider switch, retailer insolvency cleanup, franchise handoff, or cashless conversion.

The selected candidate is not cash handling, cash transport, debt collection, factoring, insurance adjustment, accounting advice, or payment services. It accepts only corporate retailers, fuel stations, franchise groups, hospitality groups, parking operators, amusement operators, cash-heavy service chains, or insolvency administrators where a named provider already holds, reports, or owes a returnable balance and will acknowledge a written refund path.

## Selected Candidate

**CashTransit Float Return Lockbox**

One-sentence thesis: Multi-site cash businesses with closed sites, cashless conversions, CIT provider switches, smart-safe shutdowns, or insolvency cleanup will pay to recover provider-held cash balances, change-fund residues, cassette deposits, sealed-bag exceptions, and settled overages when the founder controls the mandate, site ledger, provider acknowledgement, and refund instruction.

### Why It Clears Simulation

The decisive difference from recent failed recovery desks is that the startup accepts only balances already held, reported, or acknowledged by a cash-management counterparty. The founder is not arguing entitlement, chasing unpaid invoices, auditing a broad AP ledger, or telling a debtor to change preferred economics. The clean case starts with a provider-side statement, closure ledger, site list, or account balance trail.

The control proof is concrete:

- corporate mandate over named closed/switched sites and provider accounts;
- site-level safe/CIT/change-fund/cassette/deposit-bag ledger;
- CIT, smart-safe, bank, processor, or cash-management provider statement or response;
- accepted refund instruction or deficiency list;
- first returned balance, account credit, or paid no-go;
- fee right tied to returned cash or acknowledged balance.

The buyer pays because branch closures, franchise transfers, cashless conversion, provider switches, and insolvency cleanups create annoying balance tails that are too small for senior finance teams and too messy for ordinary AP. A 10,000-25,000 PLN fee can make sense if 80,000-500,000 PLN is tied up across many sites and the work converts stale cash into a visible refund.

### Internal Cap Review

- Concrete 6-month control proof: passes. A signed mandate, carrier/provider statement, accepted refund path, and first refund are measurable.
- One vendor or consultant copy risk: partially passes. CIT providers and accountants can do the work, but cannot copy the specific closed-site mandate and provider acknowledgement once controlled.
- CAC/payback/margins/cash conversion: passes only for multi-site accounts with at least 75,000 PLN expected recoverable balances, not single-store cash bag disputes.
- Vague partnerships/goodwill: passes only if intake requires written provider statements, account IDs, site lists, and named refund contacts.
- Regulated authority: passes only with no cash handling, no armored transport, no custody of funds, no debt collection, no insurance adjustment, no legal/accounting advice, and no AML/suspicious-cash remediation.

## 6-Month POC

1. Build a target list of 150 multi-site retailers, fuel-station groups, franchise administrators, hospitality groups, parking operators, amusement/cash-terminal operators, insolvency administrators, and retail finance consultants.
2. Accept only cases with a named CIT/smart-safe/cash-management provider, site list, closure/switch event, provider statement or reconciliation trail, and expected recoverable balance above 75,000 PLN.
3. Sign 5-8 paid mandates covering at least 40 sites and 750,000 PLN of candidate provider-held balances.
4. Obtain 3-5 provider acknowledgements, accepted refund instructions, deficiency lists, account credits, refunds, or clean no-go responses.
5. Deliver first actual returned cash, account credit, or offset within 60 days.
6. Target 120,000-400,000 PLN revenue in 6 months with gross margin above 65%, because the work is ledger reconstruction, provider correspondence, site-pack assembly, and refund tracking rather than field cash handling.

## Kill Conditions

- Providers refuse to acknowledge balances or refund paths except through the retailer's existing treasury team.
- Most candidate balances are already reconciled, too small, disputed, or written off.
- The work becomes overage/shortage dispute advocacy, suspected theft/fraud review, AML remediation, insurance claim support, or legal/accounting advice.
- Retailers will not pay fixed fees or success fees because they expect their CIT provider or accountant to fix the tail.
- Average recoverable balance per mandate is below 75,000 PLN.
- First provider acknowledgement takes longer than 30 days in most clean cases.
- Chain closures and provider switches are too infrequent to create repeat acquisition flow.
- Finance teams refuse to share safe/CIT account ledgers, site lists, provider statements, closure records, or bank details with an unknown founder.
