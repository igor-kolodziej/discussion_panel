# Round 46 Candidate Funnel: Health/Biosignal Hard Assets

Created: 2026-05-25 Europe/Warsaw

## Filter

This round rejects:

- generic clinics, care coordination, dashboards, or reports;
- direct-to-consumer prescription medicine sale or advertising;
- unlicensed pharmacy activity;
- health-data resale without explicit consent/control;
- clinic-capacity routing unless the startup controls payment flow or title.

It prioritizes:

- inventory title;
- salvage/consignment rights;
- acquired customer books/phones/domains;
- patient order execution custody;
- payment/claim/control flow that can be proven inside 60 days.

## Candidates

### 1. NeuroSpare EEG/PSG Donor Bank

Buyer: private EEG, EMG, neurophysiology, and sleep labs; small hospitals; occupational/clinical research sites.

Acute trigger: a headbox, amplifier, cable loom, patient box, photic stimulator, PSG input module, or legacy laptop/software dongle fails and the lab loses revenue because OEM parts are slow, expensive, or discontinued.

Control point: founder owns title to a tested donor inventory of exact legacy EEG/PSG/EMG SKUs plus signed first-look/consignment rights from closing or upgrading labs.

Two-month proof:

- buy 8-15 high-confidence donor SKUs under 60,000 PLN;
- sign one biomedical engineer / electrical-safety tester;
- sign two first-look salvage or consignment agreements with small labs upgrading equipment;
- collect one paid emergency loaner/resale/rental deposit from a lab;
- keep MDR Article 14/23 distributor and replacement-part evidence file.

First acquisition mechanism: scrape used-equipment listings and call private labs, sleep centers, neurology practices, biomedical-service shops, and liquidation sellers for obsolete but still-used Natus, Nihon Kohden, Cadwell, Compumedics, Lifelines, and similar components.

Gross margin/payback logic: buy donor modules for hundreds to a few thousand PLN; sell/rent urgent replacements at 2-5x when a lab is down; charge rush courier, testing certificate, and loaner fee. One line-down transaction can recover a meaningful part of inventory cost.

Copy risk: OEM/service incumbents can provide official parts for supported systems, but cannot instantly copy already-owned lots of obsolete exact components or signed salvage options.

### 2. NFZ Monthly Medical-Consumables Book Roll-Up

Buyer: 50-60/pre-elderly and elderly patients using monthly reimbursed continence, stoma, catheter, diabetes-pump infusion-set, wound-care, or similar medical-device consumables; also adult children who manage repeat orders.

Acute trigger: a small medical shop owner is retiring or under-digitized, and patients need continuity for recurring monthly e-zlecenie execution.

Control point: option/acquire the shop phone, domain, patient reorder calendar, supplier terms, starter inventory, courier workflow, and patient-authorized monthly order execution rights. The startup does not prescribe; authorised clinicians issue e-zlecenia and an NFZ-contracted shop/apteka realizes orders.

Two-month proof:

- signed option on one retiring/under-digitized medical shop book;
- 50 patient re-authorizations / reorder consents;
- 25 shipped monthly reimbursed orders;
- at least 15,000 PLN gross profit pipeline or equivalent recurring monthly order margin evidence;
- clean GDPR/medical-data transfer notices and patient choice language.

First acquisition mechanism: call small independent medical shops, orthopedic shops, stomia/continence distributors, and NFZ-contracted points whose owners are 55+ or whose websites/phone flow are poor.

Gross margin/payback logic: recurring monthly order flow with supplier margin plus patient co-pay on premium products. Payback comes from inherited patient book rather than paid ads.

Copy risk: large e-commerce pharmacies and medical shops can compete, but they cannot copy a signed local phone/book/customer-transition packet once acquired.

### 3. EEG Lab Closure Referral-Book Option

Buyer: neurologists, psychiatry clinics, occupational medicine providers, and patients who need EEG/EMG slots.

Acute trigger: a private neurodiagnostic lab owner retires or upgrades out of legacy service; referral doctors need continuity.

Control point: option/acquire the lab phone number, domain, referral-doctor list, waitlist, old device inventory, and transition endorsement. Contracted technician and neurologist remain responsible for testing/interpretation.

Two-month proof: option one closing lab, 20 paid bookings transferred, one contracted technician, one reading neurologist, and one inherited referral-doctor list.

Risk: this is closer to a clinical service acquisition than a hard inventory arbitrage; licensed professionals own authority.

### 4. Hearing-Aid Renewal Cycle Book

Buyer: 50-60/pre-elderly hearing-impaired adults and older patients using NFZ/private hearing-aid cycles.

Acute trigger: independent audioprosthetist retires with a patient base approaching renewal/repair/battery/accessory cycles.

Control point: acquire patient book, phone/domain, fitting notes, appointment calendar, repair cases, and existing stock/loaner devices, with patient re-consent.

Two-month proof: 100 patient re-consents, 20 repair/renewal appointments, 10 deposits or completed sales.

Risk: Amplifon/Geers/Audika and other chains already own much of the market; fittings are trust-heavy and clinical/service-led.

### 5. GLP-1 Cold-Chain Failure Claim Desk

Buyer: licensed pharmacies, clinics, and legitimate wholesalers handling GLP-1/insulin cold-chain incidents.

Acute trigger: a courier temperature excursion, suspected falsified pen, batch recall, or returned cold-chain product creates unrecoverable inventory loss and documentation burden.

Control point: assigned courier/insurer/wholesaler claim plus lawful quarantine/destruction evidence custody. No resale of Rx medicines.

Two-month proof: one pharmacy/clinic agreement, three assigned incidents, one courier/insurer acknowledgement, one recovered payment.

Risk: incident frequency may be too low; pharmacies/wholesalers already have GDP SOPs; controlled Rx handling creates liability.

### 6. Legal Nicotine/Vape Age-Gate Payment-Risk Lane

Buyer: PSPs, marketplaces, and legal nicotine/vape retailers.

Acute trigger: payment provider or marketplace faces underage-sale, excise, product-label, or prohibited-advertising risk.

Control point: PSP risk queue and merchant settlement hold workflow, not the products themselves.

Two-month proof: one PSP/marketplace pilot, 50 merchant reviews, 5 settlement/risk actions, paid compliance/risk fee.

Risk: not strongly genetics/EEG/biosignal; copyable by compliance/fraud vendors unless payment-flow access is exclusive.
