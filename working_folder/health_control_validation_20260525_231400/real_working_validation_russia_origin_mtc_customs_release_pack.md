## Strongest Objections

1. Brokers may already handle MTC/sanctions-origin requests as normal customs support. The only plausible wedge is overflow when the broker does not want to become a supplier-evidence factory.
2. The product sits close to sanctions legal advice. The startup can say evidence is present/missing/inconsistent and should escalate, but cannot say the import is lawful, the origin is acceptable, customs must release it, or the evidence is legally sufficient.
3. Many cases will be impossible because suppliers cannot prove melt/pour/input origin or provide upstream MTCs.
4. Product/CN scope can drag the founder into customs classification advice; the founder must use only codes supplied by broker/importer and stop if classification is disputed.
5. Customs acceptance can vary by Member State, broker, product, and authority practice, so case memory is useful but cannot create guarantees.
6. Supplier RFIs can be slow; a fast pack is only realistic when documents already exist or supplier contact is responsive.
7. Suspicious routes, false declarations, sanctioned-party concerns, or pressure to "make documents work" create reputational risk and must be rejected.
8. It is close to the confirmed CBAM broker-first import continuity idea. The distinction is real but narrow, and the same brokers may see both as special document exceptions.
9. The moat is modest because brokers, trade-compliance consultants, sanctions lawyers, and large forwarders can copy the workflow.
10. Case volume may be episodic unless one broker sends repeated cases.
