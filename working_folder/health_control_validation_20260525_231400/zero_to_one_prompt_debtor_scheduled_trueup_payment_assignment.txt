Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:

Debtor-Scheduled True-Up Payment Assignment

One-sentence thesis:

B2B SaaS, API, data, and infrastructure vendors can turn accepted enterprise true-up receivables into near-term cash by assigning only debtor-scheduled, non-disputed payment files where customer/AP has already confirmed the payment route or date before the startup accepts the case.

Exact buyer:

20-500 employee B2B SaaS, API, data, AI-infrastructure, usage-priced software, and enterprise software vendors with accepted usage, seat, data-volume, minimum-commit, co-term, or renewal true-up receivables above 150,000 PLN equivalent.

Acute trigger:

A named customer has accepted the true-up and AP/procurement has issued a written scheduled-payment confirmation, buyer-portal approved-for-payment record, or signed payment-direction acknowledgement, but the vendor still needs the cash before quarter close, board reporting, lender covenant reporting, acquisition diligence, runway planning, or a renewal-expansion milestone.

Control point:

The startup accepts only a named payment file with: vendor mandate, debtor/AP written payment-date or buyer-portal approved-for-payment evidence, invoice/order form/true-up statement, no-dispute certificate, vendor-approved customer contact path, action log, and fee right tied to scheduled cash received or a dated clean no-go. It does not discover underbilling, argue contract entitlement, finance the receivable, become reseller/payee of record, move customer money, or collect disputed debt.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

Three signed vendor mandates covering at least 1.5M PLN equivalent in debtor-scheduled accepted true-up payments; each mandate must include debtor-side written pay-date, buyer-portal approved-for-payment record, or signed payment-direction acknowledgement before intake. First proof is at least 500,000 PLN cash received by vendors or cleanly no-goed with debtor-side reason codes, plus at least 35,000 PLN collected in setup, success, or no-go fees.

6-month POC:

Ten accepted payment files from at least six vendors; 5M PLN equivalent in named debtor-scheduled receivables under mandate; at least 2.5M PLN cash received by vendors or cleanly no-goed; at least 250,000 PLN revenue collected; two repeat channel partners; average case cycle under 30 days; gross margin above 65%; fewer than 20% of accepted files degrade into disputed usage, legal collection, tax-only correction, or generic AR follow-up.

First acquisition mechanism:

Warm outbound to billing-migration partners, usage-pricing consultants, RevOps firms, fractional SaaS CFOs, SaaS accountants, PE/VC portfolio operators, and enterprise billing implementation partners with one narrow ask: "Do you have accepted true-ups where the customer/AP side has already scheduled payment in writing, but the vendor still needs controlled release follow-through before close?"

Economics:

8,000-25,000 PLN fixed setup/no-go fee per named payment file, plus 1.5-5% success fee when cash arrives by the scheduled date or after documented AP-release action. Costs are secure evidence handling, finance-ops review, AP follow-through through the vendor-approved billing owner, and no-go classification. No capital advance, no escrow, no debt purchase, no debt collection, and no payment-services role.

Copy risk:

Internal CFO, AR, RevOps, customer-success, billing, and collections teams can do much of the work. Billing-migration partners, fractional CFOs, ERP/billing consultants, SaaS accountants, outsourced AR providers, and PE/VC operators can copy the checklist if they see enough cases.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

The startup only claims control after a vendor signs the mandate and debtor-side payment-date or payment-direction evidence is already captured for a named receivable. Once assigned, the exact file includes the receivable, scheduled-payment proof, approved contact path, before/after action log, fee right, and no-go boundary. Incumbents can copy future workflow, but they cannot retroactively control the specific assigned payment file once the mandate and evidence room are live.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

It does not sell analysis, dashboards, software, introductions, outsourced AR hours, debt collection, or underbilling discovery. It accepts only named debtor-scheduled payment files and produces one of three outcomes: cash received by the vendor, dated debtor-side no-go, or rejected intake before work expands.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

Reject disputed usage, ambiguous contract terms, surprise backbilling, informal waivers, legal settlements, old debt, insolvency, tax-only issues, customer relationship conflicts, anything requiring legal demand letters, and any case where the startup would become payee, reseller, factor, lender, escrow, payment institution, or debt collector. Customer contact must go through the vendor-approved billing owner unless the debtor/AP side has explicitly authorized third-party coordination.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

This is not PromoLeak because it is not retail promotion, deduction, chargeback, or trade-spend recovery. It is not the failed Amazon Vendor Central recovery idea because there is no platform deduction queue or opaque marketplace decision. It is narrower than prior true-up recovery variants because debtor-side scheduled-payment or payment-direction evidence is mandatory before intake.

Strongest anticipated objections:

Clean debtor-scheduled true-up files may be rare; competent SaaS CFOs and AR teams should already manage many approved payment files; AP scheduled payment does not guarantee cash because PO, vendor-master, tax, budget, netting, or legal-entity issues may remain; enterprise AP may ignore an outside coordinator; success-fee attribution can be contested if payment would have arrived anyway; partners can internalize the workflow; sensitive contracts, usage statements, invoices, AP records, and customer threads create trust friction; the business can degrade into generic AR follow-up if intake discipline slips.
