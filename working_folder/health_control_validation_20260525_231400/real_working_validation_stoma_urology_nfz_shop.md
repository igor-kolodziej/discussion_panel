## Zero To One Summary

Zero To One scored the idea as a strong grounded cash-flow acquisition with a real control point, but not high enough for the real gate. It agreed the wedge is better than a caregiver marketplace because it starts from an NFZ-contracted transaction node plus an active patient book.

The score stayed at 80 because transferability, margins, working capital, and health-data compliance can kill the economics, especially for an unknown part-time founder under 100,000 PLN.

## Strongest Objections

1. NFZ contract / transaction-right transfer is the gating risk.
   - The control point depends on lawfully acquiring or controlling the entity/enterprise that realizes NFZ e-zlecenia.
   - Asset purchase alone may not transfer NFZ agreement, eZWM process, staff authorization, or operating status.
   - Acquiring the whole legal entity reduces transfer risk but brings inherited liabilities.

2. Patient book may not transfer.
   - Patients and caregivers may trust the old owner, local shop, stoma nurse, or hospital discharge source.
   - The decisive test is current-month reordering through the new operator, not historical patient count.

3. Margins may be thin.
   - Supplier cost, courier, packaging, call handling, returns, stockouts, co-pay administration, working capital, and seller earnout can compress contribution margin.

4. Working capital can trap the founder.
   - Spending 60,000-80,000 PLN upfront may leave too little for inventory, supplier deposits, legal work, courier issues, and settlement timing gaps.

5. Online incumbents are a real ceiling.
   - The acquired book is defensible only if patients value continuity, phone support, e-zlecenie help, product familiarity, and reliable reorder execution.

6. Health-data/GDPR risk is non-trivial.
   - The business handles sensitive data around product categories, ostomy/urology status, medical-device orders, caregiver contacts, and PESEL/e-zlecenie data.

7. Product support can drift into medical advice.
   - Patients will ask clinical product-choice questions; the operator must route medical questions to authorised professionals.

## Pivot Implications

Do not advance NFZ shop buyout unless the founder can first prove:

- clean acquisition/control of the contracted entity or legally safe enterprise transfer;
- 100+ active recurring patients verified by recent order history;
- 40+ patient/caregiver reauthorizations;
- 30+ shipped orders under the new workflow;
- first NFZ settlement or strong accountant/NFZ process confirmation;
- contribution margin above 30-35% after all costs and earnout;
- supplier terms that prevent working-capital stress.

Next pivot should avoid patient-book transfer and medical-service authority entirely. Priority: hard EEG/EMG/PSG donor inventory title plus signed first-look rights to closing/upgrading lab equipment, where the startup owns the scarce part/loaner asset rather than trust-heavy patient flow.
