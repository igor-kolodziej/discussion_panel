## Strongest Objections

- Customs brokers and freight forwarders can internalize the work because live declaration support is already their job.
- The 50-tonne threshold cuts out many SMEs and narrows the accessible urgent market.
- Many high-pain cases are clean no-go cases if the importer is above threshold and lacks timely authorised-declarant status or application evidence.
- Legal/customs boundary risk is high: the startup must avoid acting as declarant, representative, tax adviser, legal adviser, or CBAM authority interpreter.
- The promised 48-72h turnaround only works for evidence-ready cases; many files will need portal action, importer decisions, supplier data, or authority processing.
- The first-wave urgency may normalize quickly once brokers standardize document-code, threshold, and status workflows.
- It is close to the already confirmed CBAM Import Continuity Desk despite a live-release framing.
- Mistakes are expensive because the output influences customs handling and import release.
- An unknown part-time Warsaw founder likely needs a named customs/CBAM reviewer to be credible.
- Case volume may be episodic rather than a repeatable acquisition engine.

## Evaluator's Best POC

The strongest version would sign two broker routing agreements, close five paid live shipment cases, produce two release/progress outcomes plus one clean no-go, get one broker to route a second case, and keep turnaround under 72 hours only for evidence-ready files.

## Lesson

Live customs release pressure is stronger than a memo, but not enough when the natural incumbent is the broker/customs specialist and the founder cannot control the legal filing, authorization, or release outcome. Do not continue CBAM variants unless the startup controls a signed economic gate or payment stream that brokers cannot simply absorb.
