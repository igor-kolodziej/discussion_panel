Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
StainedGlass Suite Options

One-sentence thesis:
Complete pre-war stained-glass, etched-glass, fanlight, stairwell, and transom suites are destroyed or scattered during renovations; the startup options named coherent suites before removal and sells only buyer-deposit-backed, measured, conservation-mapped suites to boutique hotels, restaurants, heritage apartment developers, and interior architects.

Exact buyer:
Boutique hotel and restaurant developers, heritage apartment developers, interior architects, and premium renovation investors in Poland/CEE who need a coherent decorative glass suite for a named project before design freeze, opening, sale photography, or construction close.

Acute trigger:
A restoration or fit-out project wants authentic pre-war decorative glass, but individual panels disappear quickly when buildings are renovated, demolished, or stripped. The buyer needs a coherent suite with dimensions, condition, provenance, removal feasibility, and deposit-secured availability before committing the interior design or marketing promise.

Control point:
The startup controls a specific named suite, not a catalog. Control means a signed seller/building-owner option or title-transfer agreement covering exact panels, removal permission, authority chain, price, hold period, and no-speculation terms; a buyer deposit or escrowed conditional purchase before removal; and a measured suite packet that prevents fragmentation.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, prove one signed seller option over a complete suite, one contractor removal/condition quote, one measured panel schedule with photos/dimensions/damage map/provenance notes, and one buyer deposit or conditional purchase agreement tied to that exact suite. No suite is removed, bought, stored, or transported speculatively without buyer money or a buyer-paid hunt mandate.

6-month POC:
Option 4-6 coherent decorative-glass suites before demolition or renovation fragmentation. Secure 2-3 buyer deposits or buyer-paid search mandates. Close 1-2 transfers with conservation-safe removal and documented title/authority chain. Build a request file of at least 20 architects/developers/restaurateurs specifying dimensions, style, budget, and deadline. Kill if most leads are random panels, buyer deposits do not appear before removal, or restoration/logistics consume the spread.

First acquisition mechanism:
Contact demolition contractors, renovation architects, stained-glass restorers, antique-salvage operators, property administrators, boutique-hotel architects, and heritage developers with a specific offer: "I option complete decorative-glass suites before they are broken up and bring deposit-backed buyers; I do not buy random panels." Use photo examples, a one-page removal/authority checklist, and fast site visits by a contractor/restorer for qualifying suites.

Economics:
Charge a buyer-paid search/measurement fee of 3,000-7,000 PLN for project-specific hunts, or earn a 15-35% gross spread/success fee on a completed suite transfer. Target suite sale prices of 20,000-120,000 PLN depending on coherence, size, style, provenance, and project fit. First six months target 60,000-180,000 PLN gross margin from 1-2 closed suites plus buyer-paid hunts, with storage avoided by requiring buyer deposits before removal.

Copy risk:
Stained-glass restorers, antique dealers, salvage dealers, and architects can copy the category, but they cannot copy a specific optioned suite once the startup has seller authority, a hold period, measured condition proof, and buyer deposit. The operational wedge is speed and discipline around complete-suite control before panels are fragmented, not generic antique dealing.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a seller option names the panels, hold period, removal rules, price, and authority chain, a later dealer cannot legally sell that exact suite without the seller breaking the option. Once a buyer deposit is tied to the measured suite, the founder controls the transaction sequence and can reject speculative storage or random-panel brokering. Incumbents can compete for future suites, but not for the already optioned lot and deposit-backed buyer stream.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The asset is the controlled physical suite plus option/deposit sequence. The measured packet is only the proof artifact for a named suite. The startup is not listing random salvage, matching introductions, restoring glass, running a marketplace, or selling design advice; it earns only when it controls a named suite and moves it to a deposit-backed buyer under bounded removal/title terms.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify heritage compliance, provide conservation opinions, perform removal, insure transport, install glass, authenticate beyond documented provenance, or advise on monument-law/building-permit obligations. Licensed contractors, restorers, carriers, insurers, lawyers, and building authorities remain responsible for removal, conservation, transport, installation, permits, and legal interpretation. Reject monument-protected, disputed-title, unsafe-removal, stolen/provenance-unclear, or condominium-contested cases.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is close to HeritageDoor Suites, so the distinction must be strict. HeritageDoor controls complete pre-war interior door suites for apartments. StainedGlass Suite Options controls decorative glass/fanlight/transom/stairwell suites for project-specific visual feature or restoration use. The buyer trigger is design/opening/sale photography for a glass feature, the payment event is buyer deposit before conservation-safe removal, and the proof artifact is a measured glass condition/provenance/removal map rather than a door fit/hardware schedule.

Strongest anticipated objections:
This may still be too close to the HeritageDoor idea. Supply may be too sparse for a repeatable company. Restorers and salvage dealers may have better seller access and trust. Buyer deposits may be hard before inspection and restoration quotes. Conservation status, owner authority, condominium consent, or monument restrictions can block removal. Condition risk is high because lead came, cracked glass, prior repairs, bowing, paint loss, and missing pieces can erase value. Fragile transport, crating, insurance, and restoration estimates can eat margin. Many buyers may prefer new custom glass rather than salvaged suites.
