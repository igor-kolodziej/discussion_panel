Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Cloud Marketplace Remittance Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
Recover missing, short, held, misallocated, duplicate-deducted, or incorrectly mapped AWS Marketplace, Microsoft commercial marketplace, and Google Cloud Marketplace private-offer payouts for small ISVs by taking signed mandates over named remittance discrepancies and charging only for screened ledgers plus actual recovered, released, corrected, or cleanly no-go'd cash.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized B2B SaaS vendors, security tools, data products, DevOps tools, AI infrastructure vendors, and cloud-native ISVs selling through AWS Marketplace, Microsoft commercial marketplace, or Google Cloud Marketplace, especially those using private offers, channel private offers/reseller offers, marketplace committed-spend programs, multi-year billing schedules, non-USD payouts, or multi-entity payout/tax profiles. The first buyer has a visible marketplace payout, invoice, private offer, disbursement report, revenue statement, bank trace, support case, or customer/order mapping problem worth at least 80,000 PLN equivalent, but lacks a dedicated marketplace finance-ops person.

Acute trigger:
The seller expected marketplace cash that did not arrive, arrived short, was held, was routed to the wrong payout entity, was reduced by an unexplained fee/tax/withholding/channel share, was tied to the wrong private-offer billing schedule, was not mapped to the expected customer/order, was marked abandoned/released/pending, or is stuck in a marketplace support loop while board cash, commission payments, reseller settlement, revenue close, audit reconciliation, or runway planning depends on the answer.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate for a named remittance queue; client authorization to review marketplace payout and offer evidence; read-only or exported AWS/Microsoft/Google marketplace reports; private-offer IDs, order IDs, offer acceptance data, billing schedules, customer/account identifiers where allowed, reseller/channel-private-offer documentation, payout/disbursement statements, revenue reports, tax/withholding/fee lines, bank traces, support-case IDs, correspondence chronology, prior finance reconciliation, and client-approved escalation wording. The startup controls the recovery file and filed marketplace/support claim chronology, not the marketplace account, buyer contract, tax position, or seller-of-record decision.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three signed recovery mandates covering named cloud marketplace remittance discrepancies with at least 500,000 PLN equivalent in candidate missing/short/held payout value; at least 24,000 PLN in prepaid screened-ledger fees; complete evidence ledgers for all accepted cases; marketplace support cases or escalations filed under client authorization for each case; at least one recovered, released, corrected, credited, or cleanly no-go'd payout above 80,000 PLN equivalent; first success-fee invoice issued only on recovered/released/corrected value; and no case accepted without visible marketplace reports, offer/order identifiers, and client authorization.

6-month POC:
Source 25-40 candidate ISV payout queues from cloud GTM agencies, marketplace operations freelancers, SaaS CFO groups, cloud alliance consultants, micro-SaaS M&A operators, and founder communities; accept 10-15 cases with visible value and document-ready evidence; process at least 4,000,000 PLN equivalent in candidate discrepancies; recover, release, correct, or cleanly no-go at least 1,500,000 PLN equivalent; collect 250,000-500,000 PLN in screened-ledger and success fees; document win/loss reasons by marketplace, private-offer type, payout issue, report artifact, and support path; and continue only if at least three referral channels send a second qualified case and gross margin stays above 65%.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start from cloud marketplace agencies, AWS/Azure/GCP private-offer consultants, SaaS finance operators, cloud alliance freelancers, SaaS CFO communities, and founders whose products publicly list on hyperscaler marketplaces. Partner pitch: "When a seller has already closed a private offer but finance cannot reconcile or collect the marketplace payout, send the recovery queue to us. You keep GTM/setup work; we only take named payout discrepancies with report artifacts and seller authorization." Direct seller pitch: "If Marketplace says you sold it but finance cannot find, reconcile, or collect the payout, I turn the exact reports, offer IDs, bank traces, support cases, and channel documents into a recovery file and chase only recoverable cash."

Economics:
Charge 8,000-20,000 PLN prepaid for a screened recovery ledger and evidence reconstruction, then 12-25% of actual recovered, released, corrected, or newly payable value, with a minimum recovered-value threshold per case. No success fee on speculative claim value, future bookings, sales pipeline, or amounts merely identified as probably due. Buyer ROI is credible when a single private-offer payout, reseller split, annual subscription, or marketplace disbursement issue is 80,000-1,000,000+ PLN equivalent and internal finance or support loops have stalled. Founder capital stays under 100,000 PLN because the work is document reconstruction, support-case management, partner referral, and specialist review, not claim purchase or fund movement.

Copy risk:
Cloud marketplace operations consultants, cloud GTM agencies, accountants, marketplace platforms, alliance teams, reseller/channel partners, and hyperscaler support can copy pieces. The defense is transaction-specific control and repeated payout-case memory: once the startup has the signed mandate, reports, offer IDs, support-case chronology, client-approved escalation wording, and success-fee economics for a named remittance queue, another provider cannot recover the exact same value without displacing the authorized file. The compounding asset is issue-pattern memory by marketplace, private-offer structure, channel path, report field, tax/fee line, support route, and accepted no-go reason.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup enters only after the seller signs over a named recovery mandate and exports the precise marketplace payout, offer, order, support, bank-trace, and reconciliation evidence. A cloud GTM consultant can help with future private offers, and an accountant can reconcile books, but neither can instantly own the existing support chronology, filed recovery case, client-approved escalation path, and success-fee claim over that named missing payout unless the seller switches providers.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell marketplace setup, co-sell strategy, listing optimization, generic reconciliation software, dashboards, accounting cleanup, tax advice, or introductions. It accepts only named remittance discrepancies where the buyer prepays for a screened ledger, authorizes a recovery mandate, provides marketplace reports and offer/order evidence, and pays success fees only on actual recovered, released, corrected, or cleanly no-go'd value. The paid artifact is money recovered or a defensible no-go on a specific payout queue, not a report about possible leakage.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not give tax, accounting, legal, securities, revenue-recognition, or audit opinions; does not move funds; does not change payout bank accounts; does not create or accept private offers; does not negotiate buyer contracts; does not represent itself as AWS, Microsoft, Google, reseller, tax adviser, auditor, or seller of record; does not access production customer data beyond required marketplace reports; and does not pursue cases involving fraud, sanctions, disputed buyer nonpayment, fake documents, or unclear account authority. The client remains marketplace account owner and decision-maker; accountants, tax advisers, lawyers, auditors, cloud marketplace consultants, and reseller/channel partners remain responsible for their own domains.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central Recovery Mandate, PartnerCenter Incentive Claim Release, OneStack, KSeF payment release, VoP payment exception release, PSP reserve release, carrier credit recovery, or COD courier recovery. The buyer, trigger, control point, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: cloud marketplace seller remittance from AWS/Microsoft/Google private-offer payout reports and support cases, not retail deductions, generic incentives, bank payments, ecommerce courier remittances, or regulated-finance evidence.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Recoverable discrepancies may be rare because many apparent gaps are normal billing cycles, marketplace fees, taxes, withholding, buyer nonpayment, offer terms, reseller/channel shares, refunds, exchange-rate effects, payment thresholds, or report timing. Cloud marketplace consultants, marketplace ops agencies, SaaS accountants, cloud alliance teams, and platform support may already solve the best cases. Marketplace support outcomes can be opaque and slow. Seller-console and finance-data access creates trust friction for an unknown founder. Success fees may be hard to attribute if the platform would have paid anyway. The EU/CEE ISV pool with large enough marketplace payout errors may be too small. Many sellers may need marketplace setup or revenue ops consulting rather than cash recovery. The workflow could become bespoke report reconciliation unless strict visible-value and evidence rules are enforced.
