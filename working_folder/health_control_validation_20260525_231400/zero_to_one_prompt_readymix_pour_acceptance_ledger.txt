Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ReadyMix Pour Acceptance Ledger.

One-sentence thesis:
Small ready-mix concrete plants and concrete pump operators will pay for a mandatory live pour-evidence rail when it helps them get invoices, accessorial charges, rejected-load disputes, waiting time, and quantity/material questions accepted faster than scattered paper tickets, driver WhatsApps, batch records, and site memory.

Exact buyer:
Owner-operated and small regional ready-mix concrete plants, concrete pump operators, and concrete delivery operators serving small and mid-sized contractors, builders, developers, industrial sites, farms, renovation crews, and municipal/private works where payment depends on delivery tickets, pour quantities, waiting time, rejected loads, material evidence, and site acceptance.

Acute trigger:
The plant or pump operator loses cash, margin, or admin time when a customer questions whether a pour happened as billed, whether the delivered cubic meters match the invoice, whether truck waiting time or pump overtime is payable, whether a rejected load was customer-caused, whether slump/temperature/arrival time/site conditions were documented, whether material batch/delivery tickets support the invoice, or whether a later crack/defect complaint can be separated from basic delivery acceptance.

Control point:
A paid, signed all-pour routing mandate inside one plant's or pump operator's workflow for at least two named contractors, job sites, or customer accounts. During the pilot, every new eligible pour for those named accounts must be captured through ReadyMix first, before invoice preparation. The controlled evidence packet includes delivery ticket, batch or mix ID, truck/driver, scheduled and actual arrival/departure time, site photos, before/after or pour-location photos, customer work-order/job reference, waiting/pump/overtime notes, rejected-load reason if any, material ticket, site signature or customer acknowledgement where available, and lab cube/sample/slump/temperature references only when the plant already normally records them.

60-day signed/prepaid proof:
One prepaid 60-day pilot signed by a ready-mix plant or pump operator for at least 8,000-18,000 PLN.
Two named contractors, job sites, or customer accounts placed under all-pour routing.
At least 30 live pours captured before invoice preparation.
At least two customer-specific invoice attachment packets generated.
At least one customer invoice packet accepted, or one waiting-time/rejected-load/accessorial charge paid using the packet.
At least 10,000 PLN collected in pilot and packet fees before expanding.
No broad software build before the proof: mobile capture, structured storage, OCR, PDF packet generation, and manual QA are enough.

6-month proof:
Three plants or pump operators signed.
150+ live pours captured.
At least 1,000,000 PLN of underlying invoiced pour value routed through the evidence packets.
At least five customer invoice packets accepted without major rework.
At least three accessorial, rejected-load, waiting-time, or quantity disputes resolved or avoided using the live packet.
At least 50,000 PLN revenue collected with 65%+ gross margin after contractors/manual QA.
One repeatable target list built from local concrete plants, pump operators, public permit/project signals, contractor directories, and supplier referrals.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of 80-120 small concrete plants and pump operators around Warsaw, Mazowieckie, Lodzkie, Slaskie, Wielkopolskie, and Malopolskie from public websites, maps, supplier directories, local construction listings, and industry directories. Prioritize plants that appear independent, sell to many small contractors, show phone/email ordering rather than advanced customer portals, offer pumping or small-load delivery, and publish terms around waiting time, rejected loads, or delivery. Outbound with a narrow offer: "For two named customers or jobs, every pour gets captured into the invoice packet before billing, so you stop reconstructing proof from driver tickets and WhatsApp when someone questions the invoice."

Economics:
Price the first 60-day pilot at 8,000-18,000 PLN for two named customers/jobs and one plant or pump team.
After proof, charge 1,500-4,000 PLN per month per plant plus 20-80 PLN per captured pour or 300-1,500 PLN per dispute/accessorial packet.
Gross margin should be 65-80% in the POC because the product is mobile forms, OCR, document assembly, storage, and manual QA, not capital equipment.
Payback for the buyer can come from one accepted waiting-time, rejected-load, pump-overtime, quantity, or defect-related payment event, or from reduced invoice-prep/admin time across repeated pours.
Founder cash conversion is prepaid pilot first, then monthly retainer and packet fees; no receivable funding, no inventory, no legal claim purchase.

Copy risk:
Concrete batch software, construction ERPs, fleet tracking, delivery-ticket systems, and QC tools are substitutes. They do not automatically control the plant's customer-specific payment packet for disputed small pours, especially where the current workflow is paper tickets, phone orders, driver photos, and WhatsApp. Once ReadyMix is mandated for named customers, a competitor cannot copy the exact live source ledger, photos, timestamps, signatures, waiting notes, and customer-specific acceptance memory already captured.

Why incumbents cannot copy before control is secured:
The first proof does not require a market-wide software launch. It requires a plant owner to mandate a two-customer all-pour workflow for 60 days and prepay because their own invoice evidence is painful. A batch-system vendor, ERP reseller, or large concrete group can add features later, but they cannot retroactively create the live evidence packet for already-captured pours or own the specific small plant workflow once the founder has become the invoice packet operator for named customers.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, marketplace, or generic broker:
ReadyMix does not clean old documents, write claims, list concrete suppliers, sell a dashboard, broker plants to contractors, or provide engineering opinions. It controls live event capture before the invoice exists and turns each pour into a source evidence object for payment acceptance. The product is the routed event ledger plus customer-specific invoice packet memory.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify concrete quality, perform engineering analysis, provide legal advice, decide structural defect liability, or override plant/customer contract terms. Slump, temperature, lab cube, sample, and batch records are included only when the plant already normally records them or qualified plant/lab personnel provide them. Technical disputes requiring an engineer, lab, lawyer, or expert witness stay with the plant, customer, or qualified third party. The startup handles operational evidence capture and packet assembly only.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, PromoLeak, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, parcel claims, heritage-door inventory, or the animal-care TraceFaktura idea. The closest analogue is TraceFaktura because both use live event routing before invoice preparation, but this version has a different buyer, event object, payment trigger, and control point: private concrete producers/pump operators, pour delivery tickets, material/batch evidence, waiting/rejected-load charges, and construction invoice acceptance rather than municipal animal-care events.
