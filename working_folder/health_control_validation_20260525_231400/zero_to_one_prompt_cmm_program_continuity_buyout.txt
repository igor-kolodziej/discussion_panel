Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
CMM Program Continuity Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Option or acquire a retiring or overloaded CMM programmer/metrology microshop's live inspection-program continuity book, then transfer customer-approved support ownership, exact program custody, report-format history, and prepaid continuity contracts before close so precision suppliers can keep shipment, first-article, PPAP/FAI, audit, and customer-acceptance reporting moving.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE precision-machining, plastics, sheet-metal, electronics, toolmaking, and component suppliers whose shipments, first-article inspections, PPAP/FAI packages, customer scorecards, serial-production releases, or audit responses depend on accepted CMM measurement programs and repeatable dimensional-inspection report exports.

The first customers already rely on a small external CMM programmer, metrology freelancer, quality microshop, or retiring specialist for exact programs and report formats. They are not shopping for generic quality consulting.

Acute trigger:
A retiring, overloaded, or one-person CMM programmer/metrology microshop supports customer-specific inspection programs. If that maintainer disappears or stops responding, a supplier can lose the ability to:
- regenerate accepted dimensional reports;
- update programs after drawing or characteristic revisions;
- recover after CMM, probe, controller, or metrology-software changes;
- reproduce prior report formats for customer audits;
- support FAI, PPAP, serial-production release, or shipment documentation;
- answer customer quality questions quickly enough to protect shipment or payment.

Control point:
The startup signs an option or acquisition agreement over one named microshop's live CMM program continuity book, including:
- CMM program files and version history;
- customer, part-family, machine, controller, probe, and software map;
- drawing/characteristic mapping where transferable;
- fixture, probe, setup, and alignment notes;
- accepted report templates and export formats;
- prior measurement reports and change tickets where customer-authorized;
- calibration and traceability document references where relevant;
- support inbox, phone/domain, renewal calendar, and seller handoff;
- customer-approved admin, access-control, and payment transfer;
- named CMM programmer/metrology contractor on per-ticket terms for technical work.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Signed option over one named CMM/metrology microshop's support book covering transferable files, runbooks, customer list, support history, seller handoff, and earnout.
2. IP, confidentiality, customer-data, assignment, and access-control review completed before close.
3. At least six supplier customers approve transfer of support owner, support inbox, access control, and payment direction.
4. At least 40,000 PLN clears before close through 3-month prepaid continuity contracts or annual support deposits.
5. Exact program custody is established in a versioned repository or secure customer-approved evidence room.
6. At least one live drawing revision, software/probe change, report-export issue, or audit-support ticket is resolved by the new owner and accepted by the customer.
7. At least one accepted inspection report, FAI/PPAP measurement attachment, or shipment-support report is regenerated from the acquired program workflow.
8. Support history shows repeatable maintenance rather than bespoke open-ended metrology consulting.

6-month POC:
Month 1: identify 30-50 retiring, overloaded, or one-person CMM programmers, metrology freelancers, quality microshops, small inspection labs, and CAD/CMM specialists in Poland/CEE. Screen only for sellers with live recurring support, transferable program artifacts, documented customers, and willingness to use an option plus earnout.

Month 2: sign one option covering the support book, transferable files, seller handoff, and earnout. Complete IP/confidentiality/access-control review. Secure one named CMM programmer or metrology contractor for inherited tickets.

Month 3: secure six customer approvals and at least 40,000 PLN prepaid before closing. Move program custody into a versioned repository or secure evidence room with customer-approved access rules. Resolve one live inherited ticket and regenerate one accepted inspection report or shipment-support report.

Months 4-6: close or extend the option only if support history proves reusable. Maintain 10-20 inherited customers, collect 120,000-300,000 PLN in continuity, ticket, and transition fees, keep gross margin above 55% after contractor support, seller earnout, security, and legal amortization, and document recurring patterns by machine/software/report type.

First acquisition mechanism:
Seller pitch:
"Your CMM customers depend on exact programs, report formats, setup notes, and your memory. I buy or option the continuity book with an earnout, keep you visible during handoff, move files into a controlled repository, and require customer prepayment before close so you are not handing the book to an unfunded successor."

Customer pitch:
"Your current CMM programmer is retiring or overloaded. I am not replacing your quality team or certifying measurements. I am preserving the exact accepted program/report workflow you already use, with controlled files, a named CMM contractor, support ownership, and prepaid continuity before the handoff breaks."

Economics:
Charge 2,000-6,000 PLN per customer per month for continuity coverage, 5,000-20,000 PLN for drawing-revision/program-update tickets, and 10,000-30,000 PLN for transition/repository hardening on inherited customers. Contractor cost is per ticket or fixed retainer for a named CMM programmer. Six customers prepaying 40,000 PLN funds diligence and transition; a 15-30 customer book can create meaningful owner earnings if scope stays on inherited programs and accepted report workflows.

Copy risk:
Metrology labs, Zeiss/Hexagon/Mitutoyo resellers, CMM programmers, quality consultants, OEM service teams, and large suppliers can create or replace CMM programs. The defense is not generic metrology skill. It is seller-endorsed custody of exact accepted programs, report formats, setup notes, support history, customer trust, and prepaid continuity contracts before competitors see the handoff risk.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder controls the seller option, customer-approved support transfer, exact program files, setup notes, accepted report formats, support inbox, renewal/payment rights, seller handoff, and prepaid continuity contracts, a competitor cannot recreate those exact live inspection workflows without winning a replacement project from each supplier and rebuilding programs, report formats, setup knowledge, and customer trust under time pressure.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell generic metrology consulting, inspection reports, CMM programming from scratch, software, dashboards, databases, or expert introductions. It acquires an existing paid production dependency and its recurring payment stream: inherited CMM programs, accepted report formats, support history, and customer-approved continuity contracts. Technical service work exists only to preserve and update the acquired book under strict scope.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify measurements, operate the customer's CMM, approve product conformity, make safety or regulatory claims, replace the customer's quality manager, or guarantee customer acceptance. The supplier remains responsible for measurement execution, equipment calibration, quality decisions, customer submission, and product release. The CMM contractor performs only scoped program/report-maintenance work on customer-approved artifacts. No controlled defense data, clinical data, patient data, or regulated diagnostic workflow is accepted in the first POC.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not Lab Interface Continuity Buyout because it does not handle clinical/lab-result data, LIMS interfaces, diagnostic workflows, genetic data, or health-data security. It is not Retail EDI Order-To-Cash Continuity because the controlled artifact is an accepted dimensional-inspection program/report workflow rather than order, ASN, invoice, or remittance messaging. It is not Factory Control Spares because there is no speculative inventory or emergency spare-parts bank. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, or Supplement Stack Safety.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- The right seller may be scarce: small enough to buy, organized enough to transfer, trusted enough for customer handoff, and not already absorbed by a larger metrology provider.
- Program files, report templates, prior reports, drawings, fixture notes, and customer data may have unclear ownership or confidentiality restrictions.
- Customers may prefer an established metrology lab, CMM reseller, OEM service team, or in-house programmer after the handoff becomes visible.
- Hidden support history may reveal bespoke programming, fixture problems, measurement-method disputes, or quality consulting rather than repeatable continuity.
- Part-time operation is risky if a shipment, audit, PPAP, or drawing-revision ticket needs fast response.
- Incorrect program changes can create scrap, false rejects, false accepts, or customer-quality disputes.
- Customer acceptance is outside the founder's control, and a regenerated report may still fail if the underlying measurement setup or calibration is wrong.
- The first book may be too small for durable owner earnings unless it expands to adjacent inherited metrology books.
