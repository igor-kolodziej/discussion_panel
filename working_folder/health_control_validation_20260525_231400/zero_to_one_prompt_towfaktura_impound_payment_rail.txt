Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
TowFaktura Impound Payment Rail

One-sentence thesis:
A current-invoice payment acceptance rail for Polish municipal/police towing and impound-storage contractors whose invoices or reimbursement batches are delayed because removal orders, photos, timestamps, GPS, storage ledgers, tariff calculations, owner-release records, abandonment/scrap status, or municipal billing evidence are incomplete or inconsistent.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized towing, roadside-assistance, vehicle-removal, and impound-storage contractors with active contracts or recurring work for municipalities, municipal guards, police, road authorities, or public entities. Best first buyers have current unpaid, rejected, corrected, or at-risk invoice batches tied to vehicle removal and storage events.

Acute trigger:
The contractor has already performed removal/towing/storage work but payment is delayed, reduced, challenged, or administratively risky because the gmina/public payer, municipal guard, police path, road authority, or owner-payment flow needs a complete event file: official removal instruction, vehicle and location photos, timestamps, GPS/route, license/VIN where lawful, impound entry and exit, storage days, owner notice/release/payment, abandonment/scrap transfer status, tariff schedule, and invoice line mapping.

Control point:
Signed payment-acceptance mandate over a current invoice batch with the contractor's active public contract, removal-order templates, current event register, unpaid/rejected invoices, impound ledger, photos, GPS/time evidence, storage entry/exit logs, owner release/payment records, tariff schedule, correspondence with public payer, correction log, and payment/no-go status. The startup controls the evidence packet and payment-acceptance workflow for the current batch, not towing operations or legal entitlement.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three contractors with active municipal/police/public towing or impound-storage work sign prepaid mandates; at least 150 current or recent events or 100,000 PLN in unpaid/at-risk invoices are reviewed; ten current event packets are built from official removal order, photos, GPS/time, impound entry/exit, storage-days calculation, tariff mapping, owner payment/release or municipal billing path, and invoice correction notes; at least five invoices are accepted, corrected, paid, escalated with clean evidence, or cleanly no-go'd; one contractor submits a second batch; gross margin above 60% after document work, field-data cleanup, and reviewer costs.

6-month POC:
Process 1,000+ towing/impound events across 6-10 contractors; collect 150,000-450,000 PLN in fees; document 500,000+ PLN in accepted/paid/corrected/no-go invoice value; get at least three contractors to submit repeat monthly batches; keep turnaround under 7 business days for complete events; build a private taxonomy of removal-order gaps, photo/GPS issues, storage-day disputes, tariff mapping errors, owner-release paths, abandoned-vehicle statuses, scrap-transfer blockers, and payer-specific correction patterns.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of towing/impound contractors from municipal contract notices, public procurement results, roadside-assistance directories, impound yards, and local towing associations. Pitch only current cash: "If your vehicle-removal or storage invoice batch is unpaid, corrected, or risky because event evidence is scattered, send the removal orders, photos, GPS/time, impound ledger, storage days, owner release/payment records, tariff schedule, and payer notes. I return a payment-acceptance packet and correction log for the current batch."

Economics:
Charge 3,000-7,000 PLN for a small correction batch, 8,000-18,000 PLN for 50-150 event batches, and 18,000-35,000 PLN for urgent large municipal batches. Optional 3-8% success fee only where legally and contractually clean, but fixed prepaid batch fees remain the core. The buyer pays because current invoices, storage reimbursement, or contractor cash conversion are already at stake.

Copy risk:
Contractors, dispatch systems, municipal officers, accountants, and towing software vendors can copy parts. The defense is narrow payer-specific evidence taxonomy, repeat batch workflow, correction pattern memory, and contractor trust around current invoice acceptance. Copy risk is high if contractors already keep clean event records or if payers accept simple spreadsheets.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the contractor signs the batch mandate, prepays, and shares the active contract, event register, unpaid invoices, removal orders, photos, GPS/time logs, impound ledger, tariff schedule, owner-release/payment records, and payer correspondence, the startup controls the current payment-acceptance packet and correction workflow for that invoice batch. Another provider must displace the evidence packet and payer-specific correction log while cash is already delayed.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only current towing/impound invoice batches with performed events and payment acceptance friction. The output is a payment artifact used in an active billing workflow: event packet, evidence index, tariff-line mapping, storage-day ledger, correction log, payer response file, and no-go notes. It is not dispatch software, towing operations, debt collection, legal advice, a dashboard, marketplace, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, debt collection, towing services, public-procurement interpretation, vehicle ownership decisions, lien/enforcement advice, scrap/disposal authorization, police/municipal representation, or guarantees of payment. Contractors, public payers, police/municipal guards, road authorities, lawyers, accountants, impound operators, and vehicle owners remain responsible for legal entitlement, towing authorization, tariffs, notices, disposal/scrap decisions, and payment decisions. Disputed legality, owner litigation, accidents, damage claims, fraud, forged evidence, unlawful towing, data-protection violations, or vehicle-title questions are declined or routed to qualified advisers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is adjacent to TraceFaktura only as an event-to-invoice rail, but it is materially different in buyer, trigger, proof artifact, and payment path. TraceFaktura handles municipal animal-care invoices. TowFaktura handles vehicle removal, towing, impound storage, owner-release/payment, abandoned-vehicle status, and tariff evidence for current towing/impound invoice batches. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, EPRBlocked, ButtonCellBlocked, or any health/Rx route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Contractors may already have adequate paper/photo/dispatch workflows. Public payers may not withhold enough current cash to justify fees. Municipal and police procedures vary by jurisdiction. Some cases may be legal disputes over towing authority, vehicle damage, owner notice, storage entitlement, or scrap/disposal status. Field data can be messy or missing after the event. Optional success fees may be awkward. Towing contractors may have thin margins and low willingness to pay. The idea may be viewed as a TraceFaktura variant unless payment friction is more acute and repeat monthly batches are proven.
