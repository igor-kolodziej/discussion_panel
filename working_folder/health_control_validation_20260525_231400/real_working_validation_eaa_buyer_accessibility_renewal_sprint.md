## Zero To One Judgment

Zero To One treated this as a strong, timely buyer-blocker sprint idea with a real regulatory catalyst. It agreed that the best version is not a generic accessibility audit: it activates only when a named buyer blocks a renewal, procurement, rollout, or release and produces scoped fixes, before/after evidence, issue disposition, and customer-ready response artifacts.

The idea still failed because accessibility agencies, automated tools, UX shops, dev shops, auditors, and VPAT/ACR consultants are abundant. The evaluator also saw major risk in formal-audit requirements, deep redesign scope, legal-compliance boundaries, founder credibility, and remediation sprawl.

## Strongest Objections Captured

1. Accessibility agencies and automated tools are abundant, so the founder cannot win as another accessibility audit provider.
2. Some buyer requests require deep redesign, design-system changes, complex data-grid work, document-flow remediation, mobile architecture changes, or third-party widget replacement rather than a sprint.
3. Enterprise buyers may require formal audits, recognized accessibility specialists, procurement-approved vendors, legal signoff, or the customer's own retest.
4. EAA legal interpretation risk is real; the startup must not answer whether the vendor is legally compliant.
5. Unknown-founder credibility is weak without a named accessibility tester, credible front-end contractor, sample redacted pack, secure workflow, and clear QA process.
6. Vendor willingness to pay depends on an explicit commercial blocker; general accessibility interest will not support 12,000-35,000 PLN sprint fees.
7. Defect remediation can sprawl unless the sprint caps exact journeys, defect count, fix/retest limits, and out-of-scope decisions.
8. Case memory may not compound deeply because each product has a different UI, framework, component library, and release pipeline.
9. Partner channels may keep the work internally after seeing the method.
10. Gross margin may be lower than expected because good accessibility testers and front-end contractors are not cheap.

## Useful Narrowing

Zero To One said the best version is a "5-10 business day accessibility buyer-unblock sprint" for Polish/CEE B2B SaaS vendors with live accessibility requests from banks, insurers, payment firms, telecoms, transport, booking, or ecommerce customers.

Only accept cases with:

- named buyer request or defect list;
- affected journeys defined;
- staging/product access;
- vendor decision-maker;
- tester and front-end contractor availability;
- prepaid fee;
- no promise of legal compliance or buyer acceptance;
- no full-product audit inside the sprint.

It suggested starting pricing of 10,000-16,000 PLN for five-business-day triage and buyer response, 18,000-30,000 PLN for ten-business-day scoped remediation, and 35,000+ PLN only for multi-flow urgent renewal work.

Even with proof of 10 paid sprints, 6 buyer-progress outcomes, 150+ defects triaged, gross margin above 55%, founder handling below 8 hours per sprint, and one partner sending 3+ cases, Zero To One estimated the score would rise only to 82-85. That remains too uncertain for the current gate.

## Decision

Reject. Do not continue buyer-blocker evidence/remediation sprints unless the next candidate controls a harder payment stream, assigned claim, titled asset, direct payment direction, or scarce buyer-deposit asset beyond a prepaid service ticket.
