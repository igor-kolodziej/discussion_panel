## Zero To One strongest objections

- Lawyers and accountants are the natural owners of escrow, holdback, and post-close release conditions because the release terms sit inside purchase agreements, escrow agreements, and post-close documents.
- Clean administrative cases may be too easy for the seller, broker, lawyer, or accountant to handle, so a 12,000-40,000 PLN fee may be hard to justify.
- Valuable cases often become disputed matters involving indemnity, earnouts, working-capital adjustments, tax issues, fraud allegations, buyer objections, or commercial leverage.
- Escrow agents generally act under the escrow agreement and party instructions; a third-party packet may help preparation but may not trigger release without buyer consent, counsel instructions, or formal release forms.
- Buyer leverage is outside the startup's control, and forcing release would cross into legal dispute or negotiation.
- Deal flow is hidden inside brokers, lawyers, accountants, acquisition communities, and recent seller networks.
- Fees may be too high relative to smaller holdbacks; the minimum release value likely needs to be 150,000-250,000 PLN rather than 50,000 PLN.
- Success-fee attribution is weak when a maturity date would have triggered release anyway.
- Sensitive data-room access creates high trust friction for an unknown solo founder.
- Purchase agreements are bespoke, limiting repeatability.

## Useful narrowing from evaluator

The best version would target only small-business sellers with objective, non-disputed administrative post-close release conditions due within 90 days, referred by brokers, accountants, or boutique M&A lawyers. It should reject earnout interpretation, working-capital adjustments, indemnity claims, buyer objections, tax clearance, fraud allegations, escrow-agent disputes, and any case requiring negotiation rather than evidence assembly.

## Gate Decision

Do not fresh-validate. Continue to Round 330.
