Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
OriginLock Amazon EU/UK COO Restriction Release Desk.

One-sentence thesis:
Amazon EU/UK sellers with live Seller Central country-of-origin notices, missing-COO compliance flags, or pending listing restrictions will pay an agency-routed desk to reconcile SKU-level origin evidence and submit accepted COO updates or clean no-submit decisions before revenue-producing listings are restricted.

Exact buyer:
Primary channel buyer: Amazon account-health, marketplace-compliance, VAT/EPR, and seller-operations agencies that serve EU/UK cross-border FBA and MFN sellers.

Economic buyer: Amazon EU/UK sellers with 50-500+ ASINs/SKUs, meaningful FBA inventory or monthly sales at risk, and a written Seller Central notice, compliance-dashboard flag, case ID, upload queue, or listing-restriction warning tied to missing, inconsistent, or unsupported country-of-origin information.

Acute trigger:
The seller has a current Amazon notice, compliance dashboard queue, case message, restriction warning, or blocked listing state saying COO information is missing, inconsistent, or required for EU/UK cross-border sales. The buyer is not buying generic customs advice; the buyer is trying to prevent or release named Amazon listings and FBA/MFN revenue from restriction.

Control point:
Seller-signed or agency-routed mandate over a named ASIN/SKU batch, the source-document set for those SKUs, Seller Central case IDs or compliance-dashboard rows, batch update files, pre-submission origin ledger, submission screenshots or exports, Amazon acknowledgement or follow-up, and a no-submit list for SKUs without truthful support.

The startup controls the operating file and case chronology, not the legal truth of origin and not Amazon's final decision.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One agency-routed paid batch or two direct paid batches covering at least 100 ASIN/SKU rows with written Amazon COO notices or dashboard flags, at least 50,000 PLN equivalent inventory or 100,000 PLN monthly GMV at risk, and a prepaid fixed fee before work starts.

Proof artifacts by day 60: seller mandate, source-document intake, SKU-level COO evidence ledger, batch upload or Seller Central update record, Amazon acknowledgement or narrowed follow-up, and a clean no-submit list for unsupported SKUs. At least one batch must show accepted updates, listing restriction avoided/released, or a written Amazon case status improvement; no-submit outcomes count only if they prevent false origin submission.

6-month POC:
Close 8-15 paid batches through 2-4 agencies or serious direct sellers. Process 1,500-8,000 ASIN/SKU rows. Collect 120,000-400,000 PLN revenue. Produce at least 6 accepted-update, restriction-avoided, restriction-released, narrowed-follow-up, or clean no-submit outcomes. Keep gross margin above 60% after contractors and agency margin. Reject at least 25% of inbound rows or cases where origin support is missing, false, legally ambiguous, or outside scope.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of Amazon account-health, EPR/VAT, compliance, and seller-operations agencies serving EU/UK cross-border sellers. Offer a white-label or named escalation desk:

"Your next Seller Central COO notice or ASIN batch with missing origin rows goes to us. We reconcile invoices, manufacturer declarations, product/SKU lists, customs/shipping evidence, and Seller Central rows into a truthful COO update file in 72 hours for evidence-ready cases. You keep the seller relationship and margin. We do not make customs-law or preferential-origin opinions."

In parallel, monitor public seller forums and agency content for sellers reporting COO notices, but do not sell direct unless the seller has a real written Amazon notice and a material SKU batch.

Economics:
Triage: 2,000-4,000 PLN for a small live-notice batch up to 50 rows.

Main sprint: 8,000-25,000 PLN for a 100-500 ASIN/SKU batch with available source documents and a live Amazon notice or dashboard queue.

Large catalog sprint: 25,000-60,000 PLN only when a serious seller or agency has 500+ rows, meaningful FBA inventory or GMV at risk, and clean document access.

Agency margin: 25-35%. Contractor cost is mainly spreadsheet/data reconciliation, document extraction, supplier follow-up templates, and QA. The founder handles acceptance rules, no-submit decisions, and case chronology. Cash conversion is prepaid fixed fee; no success-fee-only work.

Copy risk:
Amazon agencies, VAs, customs brokers, freight forwarders, VAT/EPR advisers, and seller-operations teams can copy routine COO update work. The startup must avoid routine attribute cleanup and own only live restricted or at-risk batches where SKU-origin evidence, batch QA, no-submit discipline, and Amazon case memory matter.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder has a paid agency-routed ASIN/SKU batch, seller mandate, document set, Seller Central case IDs, batch upload file, and before-state restriction proof, the immediate case is controlled for that seller and deadline. Competitors can copy the category later, but they cannot take the exact active operating file without the agency or seller moving the case away mid-deadline. The narrow defense is fast execution, agency trust, and accumulated SKU-evidence patterns across repeated COO notices, not a permanent legal monopoly.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The output is not a generic report or dashboard. It is an operating release file for named Amazon listings: source-document intake, SKU-level COO ledger, batch Seller Central update, case chronology, accepted/narrowed/rejected status, and no-submit decisions. The startup is not brokering customs advisers, listing services, or seller introductions; it performs the batch restriction-release workflow under seller or agency authority.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not provide customs-law opinions, preferential-origin eligibility, duty/tariff advice, tax advice, VAT representation, legal certificates of origin, substantial-transformation analysis, or false-origin remediation. Do not handle cases involving sanctions, counterfeit goods, forged invoices, fake manufacturer declarations, intentional mislabeling, product-safety noncompliance, VAT fraud, customs evasion, or disputed importer-of-record facts.

If origin is legally ambiguous, the output is a no-submit decision or referral to customs counsel/broker. The startup may reconcile documents and prepare Seller Central update files only from source evidence the seller is authorized to use. Seller remains responsible for truthfulness and any legal origin position.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not REDBlocked because it does not involve RED, CE, GPSR, EN 18031, product-safety evidence, connected baby monitors, indoor cameras, or cybersecurity/product-compliance dossiers.

This is not Amazon Vendor Central Recovery because it does not recover 1P deductions, shortages, chargebacks, co-op deductions, or remittance credits. It targets Seller Central COO listing restrictions and revenue-at-risk catalog rows.

This is not Marketplace KYBC Disbursement Release because it does not involve seller identity, UBO, bank, VAT, address verification, or held payouts. It is a SKU-level origin evidence and listing-restriction release workflow.

It is not DORA, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, KSeF/AP release, RAG/AI security evidence, trade-credit claim release, or a generic Amazon evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Routine COO fixes may be simple enough for sellers, VAs, Amazon agencies, or catalog teams to solve with bulk uploads. True country-of-origin determination can become customs-law work that the founder cannot safely perform. Source documents may be weak, inconsistent, fake, stale, or not tied to exact SKUs. Amazon may accept, reject, or re-open cases opaquely. Agencies can internalize the workflow after seeing a few batches. Customs brokers, freight forwarders, VAT/EPR firms, and Amazon consultants may already own the seller relationship. Low-value sellers will not justify the fee. The enforcement spike may fade after catalogs are updated. Direct acquisition may attract sellers seeking shortcuts or false-origin submissions. The moat depends on repeated agency case flow and disciplined no-submit outcomes, not on the basic idea of adding COO data.
