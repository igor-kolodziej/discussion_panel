Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Merchant Acquirer Residual Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Acquire or option small existing residual-commission books from retiring or exiting POS/payment-terminal/acquiring agents only when the licensed acquirer, PSP, or master agent confirms assignment or agent-of-record transfer and the first current-month residual payment is redirected before meaningful seller payout.

Exact buyer:
The economic seller is a small Polish/CEE payment-terminal, ecommerce-gateway, POS, fiscal-device, or merchant-acquiring agent with 100-800 active merchants and monthly residual commissions from one or more licensed acquirers, PSPs, payment gateways, or master agents. The payor is the acquirer/PSP/master agent that already pays residual commissions. End merchants stay contracted with their existing licensed payment provider.

Acute trigger:
The agent is retiring, exiting payments, consolidating into another business, losing energy for compliance/support/admin, or too small to interest a large ISO/acquirer buyout. The residual stream may decay if nobody handles the successor handoff, merchant-contact update, renewal calendar, terminal/gateway issue triage, and acquirer relationship. The seller wants liquidity or earnout while the acquirer wants merchant retention.

Control point:
Signed seller option or residual-purchase/revenue-share agreement; 12-24 months of residual statements; merchant-count and churn report; payor/acquirer/master-agent contract; written payor consent to assignment, subagent appointment, agent-of-record transfer, or direct residual-payment instruction; exclusion list for high-risk merchants, active disputes, fraud flags, terminal debt, clawbacks, and pending chargebacks; noncompete/non-solicit; seller handoff; and first residual payment to the startup or escrow before significant seller consideration is paid.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Two sellers sign conditional options over residual books; one acquirer/PSP/master agent gives written approval for assignment, agent-of-record transfer, or direct residual payout to the startup; one book includes at least 200 active merchants, 12 months of statements, and at least 15,000 PLN monthly gross residual before seller payout; at least 10,000 PLN current-month residual is paid to the startup or escrow; seller payout is limited to verified post-transfer residual cash; and total startup cash at risk stays below 100,000 PLN.

6-month POC:
Control 2-4 residual books with 500-1,500 active merchants and 35,000-120,000 PLN monthly gross residual; complete at least three acquirer/master-agent approved transfers or subagent appointments; collect 200,000-500,000 PLN gross residual during the period; keep merchant churn below 8% annualized after transfer; keep support escalation below 10% of merchants per quarter; maintain gross margin above 75% before seller earnout; and prove at least two repeatable seller channels such as POS installers, fiscal-device service firms, payment consultants, small ecommerce-gateway integrators, accountants serving merchants, and master-agent networks.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a list of small Polish/CEE POS installers, fiscal-device service firms, ecommerce payment consultants, payment-terminal sales agents, local merchant-service agents, and POS/ecommerce integrators. Outreach: "If you have a monthly residual commission book from acquirer/terminal/gateway merchants and want to exit without abandoning merchants, I buy only after the acquirer or master agent confirms residual transfer and the first residual payment lands. You receive an earnout from retained residuals; merchants stay with the licensed provider."

Economics:
Pay sellers 3-8x verified trailing monthly gross profit, mostly as earnout from post-transfer residuals. Example: a 250-merchant book paying 18,000 PLN monthly gross residual is optioned with 10,000-25,000 PLN upfront only after payor approval, then 40-90% of monthly residual is shared with the seller until the agreed multiple is reached. The startup earns from retained residual margin, not consulting fees. Capital is used for legal/payment-contract review, seller options, data room, security, and small verified upfronts, not for merchant-funds custody.

Copy risk:
Acquirers, ISOs, payment consultants, POS dealers, fiscal-device firms, ecommerce gateways, and master agents can buy or internalize residual books. The defense is transaction-specific control: written payor approval, transferred agent-of-record or commission assignment, exact merchant/residual ledger, seller handoff, noncompete, and current residual cash movement before competitors see or control that book.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs the option, the acquirer/master agent approves the transfer, and residual payment direction changes for the named merchant book, competitors cannot collect or redirect that exact current residual stream without displacing the signed seller agreement, payor acknowledgement, merchant ledger, handoff, and payment instruction. They can pursue future sellers, but they cannot copy the already assigned residual stream.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup buys or options an existing recurring payment stream and closes only when the payor acknowledges the transfer and current residual cash moves. It does not sell payment advice, build a dashboard, broker merchant introductions, process card payments, hold merchant funds, run a marketplace, or sell a report. The proof is payor-approved residual assignment or agent-of-record transfer plus first residual cash.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide merchant acquiring, payment processing, fund custody, settlement, lending, chargeback representation, PCI certification, KYC/AML decisions, tax advice, or legal advice. Licensed acquirers, PSPs, gateways, banks, terminal providers, fiscal-device service providers, merchants, accountants, and lawyers remain responsible for regulated payment services, merchant contracts, settlement, underwriting, KYC, PCI, chargebacks, terminal leases, fiscal-device obligations, tax, and legal decisions. Reject books with prohibited/high-risk merchants, unresolved fraud or chargeback spikes, unclear payor consent, undisclosed terminal debt, non-transferable contracts, merchant funds custody, active regulatory issues, or support obligations that require payment-provider licensing.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PSP Reserve Maturity Release Mandate, Amazon Vendor Central Recovery Mandate, PromoLeak, 3PL recovery, OTA recovery, RMA credit recovery, COD courier remittance recovery, approved carrier credit, DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, or a payment-terminal hardware lot. The distinct control object is an acquirer/PSP/master-agent paid residual commission stream, not merchant trapped funds, platform deductions, regulatory evidence, physical assets, or card-processing itself.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Acquirer or master-agent contracts may prohibit assignment or require the buyer to become an approved agent. Residual statements may be opaque, clawback-prone, repriced, concentrated, or too small. Good agents may already have relationships with acquirers, ISOs, or POS dealers who will pay more. Some books may require field terminal support, PCI/KYC help, fiscal-device work, chargeback triage, merchant complaints, or payment-provider credibility beyond a passive residual assignment. Merchant churn may spike when the seller exits. The model may be a small financial roll-up rather than a venture-scale company unless repeat seller sourcing and acquirer-approved transfer mechanics are proven.
