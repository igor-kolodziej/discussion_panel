Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DefectLot AOI Rights Bank

One-sentence thesis:
Option or acquire rights-cleared industrial visual-inspection defect image lots from factories, QA labs, and machine-vision integrators only after machine-vision AI vendors or inspection integrators reserve named defect categories with deposits, then license bounded data lots with metadata for model evaluation, sales proof, and POC preparation.

Exact buyer:
Machine-vision AI vendors, automated optical inspection vendors, industrial QA software companies, inspection-system integrators, and factory automation firms selling visual inspection into electronics, metal parts, plastic injection molding, packaging print, coating, welding, surface-finish, assembly, and general manufacturing QA workflows.

The first buyer has a live factory POC, RFP, paid pilot, sales demo, benchmark request, or model-evaluation need where synthetic examples, public datasets, and clean marketing demos are not enough. The buyer needs real defect and near-defect examples for a named category such as solder bridge, missing component, scratch, crack, flash, short shot, sink mark, coating blister, print misregistration, label defect, surface corrosion, burr, chip, stain, dent, wrong assembly, or false reject.

Acute trigger:
A machine-vision vendor or integrator is trying to win or de-risk a paid industrial inspection project, but the target customer will not yet provide enough real defect examples, the vendor's demo set is too generic, or the buyer asks for evidence that the model has seen realistic defects and near-misses. The vendor is not buying a dashboard or market research. It is paying because a specific POC, RFP, benchmark, paid pilot, or enterprise sales process needs rights-cleared real examples before the vendor can credibly scope, test, or sell.

Control point:
The controlled unit is a rights-cleared defect data lot, not a consulting memo:
- source-side option or license over a named image/video archive from a factory, QA lab, inspection contractor, or machine-vision integrator;
- source authority statement covering who created the images, who may license them, and what customer/product identifiers must be removed;
- defect taxonomy, non-defect controls, capture method, inspection station type, approximate material/process category, label owner, and metadata fields;
- confidentiality scrub removing personal data, customer names, product brands, drawings, serials, defense/medical/safety-critical context, and trade secrets where needed;
- buyer reservation, deposit, or conditional license order for named defect classes before meaningful source payout;
- sample acceptance file with image count, label quality, permitted use, license scope, warranty disclaimers, and rejection rules.

The founder controls source rights, buyer reservation, metadata QA, delivery file, and license payment for the named lot. The founder does not own the buyer's model, the source factory, or any product certification outcome.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Four source-side options or licenses over non-personal, non-regulated industrial defect image archives, each with source authority, permitted-use scope, confidentiality scrub terms, and metadata fields.

Three buyer reservations or deposits from machine-vision vendors, AOI vendors, inspection integrators, or QA software companies for named defect classes and minimum lot quality.

One paid delivery of a rights-cleared pilot lot with at least 500 usable images or clips, defect labels, non-defect controls, capture notes, acceptance criteria, and signed license terms.

At least 45,000 PLN collected in buyer deposits, pilot license fees, or conditional license orders before paying more than 20,000 PLN in source fees.

Every accepted lot must exclude personal data, defense, medical-device production, automotive-safety parts, child-safety parts, product certification claims, customer-confidential drawings, and any source that cannot sign a clean authority statement.

6-month POC:
Build a source list of 150 factories, QA labs, inspection contractors, AOI integrators, machine-vision installers, metrology consultants, and retiring quality engineers with historical defect archives.

Build a buyer list of 120 machine-vision AI vendors, AOI vendors, inspection integrators, industrial QA software firms, and automation houses that sell into electronics, plastics, metals, packaging, coating, and general manufacturing.

Control 12-20 source lots under option or license, deliver 6-10 paid rights-cleared lots, collect 250,000-800,000 PLN in license revenue, keep gross margin above 70% after source payouts, metadata QA, image scrubbing, contractor review, and legal-template cost, and reject at least half of potential source archives for weak rights, weak labels, sensitive content, poor image quality, or no buyer reservation.

By month six, at least three buyers must buy a second lot or request a new defect class, and at least three sources must provide a second archive or referral.

First acquisition mechanism:
Buyer-side outreach:
"If an industrial inspection POC is stuck because you do not have enough real examples of a named defect class, reserve a rights-cleared pilot lot. I only source and license data after you define the defect class, minimum quality, metadata needs, and permitted-use terms."

Source-side outreach:
"If your factory, lab, or inspection team has old defect images that are not customer-confidential, not personal data, and not safety-regulated, I option only named rights-cleared lots. You receive a source fee only when a buyer reserves that category and the lot passes rights and metadata QA."

Start with machine-vision integrators, AOI consultants, industrial QA freelancers, metrology labs, and quality managers because they understand which defect examples are valuable and may hold archives that are currently not monetized.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-10,000 PLN for a buyer reservation and sample search, credited against the first license. Charge 15,000-40,000 PLN for small pilot lots, 40,000-90,000 PLN for larger or more specialized rights-cleared lots, and 8,000-25,000 PLN for follow-on defect-class refreshes.

Sources receive 20-40% of net license revenue or a fixed source fee after buyer acceptance. Contractor costs cover metadata QA, de-identification, duplicate removal, label review, and rights-file administration. The model works only if no speculative source purchase is made before buyer reservation and if at least 70% gross margin remains after source and QA costs.

Copy risk:
Machine-vision vendors, data brokers, synthetic-data vendors, QA consultants, integrators, and large inspection companies can copy the general idea. The defense is not the phrase "defect data." It is signed control over specific rights-cleared source lots, buyer-reserved defect classes, label-quality memory, source trust, accepted metadata formats, and a growing library of what industrial buyers actually accept for POC preparation.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After the founder signs a source option, source authority file, confidentiality scrub plan, and buyer reservation for a named defect class, another vendor cannot license that same image lot, metadata, and acceptance package without replacing the source agreement or persuading the buyer to abandon the active reserved lot. A competitor can source other data later, but it cannot copy the controlled source-rights lot already under option and buyer deposit.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not sell model training, model validation, QA consulting, computer-vision software, dashboards, benchmarking reports, a defect-image marketplace, or introductions between factories and AI vendors. It options and licenses specific rights-cleared data lots only when a buyer has reserved named defect classes. The deliverable is a licensed asset package: image files, labels, metadata, source authority, permitted-use scope, scrub log, acceptance criteria, and invoice.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify model performance, guarantee factory acceptance, provide safety validation, issue quality-system certification, perform product certification, sell regulated production data, certify AI systems, or claim a dataset is sufficient for deployment. Buyers remain responsible for model development, validation, customer-specific testing, and product claims.

Reject personal data, employee images, customer names, product brands, serials, drawings, source code, defense production, medical-device production, automotive-safety parts, aviation, railway safety, food-safety critical inspection, child-safety products, confidential customer programs, export-controlled data, or archives where source authority is unclear. Use counsel-reviewed license templates, source warranties, buyer disclaimers, and takedown/withdrawal terms.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, RAGProbe, AIQ, or a platform evidence desk.

It differs from BioSignal because it is not investor diligence or a memo about healthtech claims. It differs from Data Act Machine Data Liberation because it does not request machine telemetry from OEMs for energy savings; it controls licensed industrial defect image lots for machine-vision vendors. It differs from HeritageDoor because the asset is a rights-cleared digital lot, not physical architectural salvage. It differs from generic AI consulting because the controlled asset is source-rights data plus buyer-paid license delivery.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Factories may not have clean rights to license historical images, or they may refuse because defect images reveal customer products, process weaknesses, or trade secrets. Buyers may prefer their own customer-domain data and treat third-party defect lots as only weak demo material. Data quality can collapse if labels are inconsistent, defect classes are too broad, images lack non-defect controls, capture conditions differ from the buyer's target line, or metadata is missing. Machine-vision integrators may source examples directly from their own customers. Synthetic-data vendors and public datasets can reduce willingness to pay for some defect classes. The market may fragment by material, process, lighting, camera type, defect definition, and factory context. Legal templates and source warranties must be serious enough to avoid selling data the source had no right to license. The business can become a marketplace or low-value dataset catalog unless every lot is buyer-reserved, rights-cleared, and acceptance-tested.
