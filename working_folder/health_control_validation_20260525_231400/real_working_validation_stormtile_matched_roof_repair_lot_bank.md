## Strongest Objections

1. Buyers may not pay premium prices often enough. Premium matched lots work for urgent leaks, storm repairs, heritage constraints, visible mismatch risk, and special fittings, but many owners will tolerate mismatch, use new close-enough tiles, swap hidden-area tiles, custom order, reroof, or buy cheap singles.

2. Existing salvage and heritage suppliers are real substitutes. The founder is not creating the category; the only wedge is controlling the exact lot needed for a specific repair with matching notes and fast delivery.

3. Material fitness is risky. Old tiles can fail on freeze-thaw damage, water absorption, microcracks, hidden delamination, salt contamination, prior storage, and breakage. The startup cannot imply roof-performance fitness without testing and roofer approval.

4. Matching is harder than the prompt implies. Profile alone is not enough: thickness, hook geometry, overlap, curvature, weathering, color, batch, frost condition, ridge/hip angle, fastening, underlay, and ventilation constraints can all matter.

5. Inventory risk can quietly kill the business. Obscure profiles, rare demand, too-small quantities, missing fittings, high breakage, storage costs, transport costs, bad searchability, or vanished buyer urgency can turn a lot into dead stock.

6. A part-time founder is poorly matched to fast physical sourcing. Valuable lots often require inspection, pricing, pickup, pallets, transport, and storage within days.

7. Storage and handling are not trivial. Tiles are heavy, fragile, space-consuming, and exposed to weather. Sorting and palletizing are labor-heavy.

8. Clear title and provenance can be messy. Demolition, estate, construction-site, and heritage-property material needs seller identity, source address, right to sell, no stolen material, no protected-property issue, handover document, invoice/receipt, and pre-pickup photos.

9. Conservation approvals can slow sales. For protected buildings, conservator approval can be required, which moves approval outside the startup and lengthens the cycle.

10. The moat is lot-specific, not company-wide. Once a lot and deposit are controlled, the transaction is defensible, but roofers and salvage yards can copy the general behavior.
