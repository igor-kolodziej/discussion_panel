Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
DORA Subcontractor Change Release Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A vendor-side, event-scoped release pack for SaaS and software-product vendors whose bank, insurer, payment-institution, leasing, or fintech customer has blocked a cloud migration, hosting change, subprocessor addition, support-provider change, or material ICT subcontractor change until DORA-style subcontractor, exit, security, incident, location, and continuity evidence is acceptable.

Exact buyer:
20-200 person Polish/CEE SaaS, fintech-infrastructure, payments, KYC/AML, fraud/risk/scoring, workflow, analytics, claims, leasing, document, and B2B software vendors with existing regulated-finance customers and a current customer blocking or threatening a go-live, production change, renewal milestone, invoice, or expansion because a material ICT subcontractor or subprocessor change has not been accepted.

Acute trigger:
The vendor needs to change AWS/Azure/GCP region or account, add a model/API provider, move hosting, replace an MSP/support vendor, add a subcontracted development/support team, move support outside the EU, add an incident/monitoring provider, or change a critical SaaS dependency. A regulated financial customer asks for change notification, subcontractor evidence, data-location summary, access-control summary, exit/data-return story, BCP/DR impact, incident-support summary, subprocessor/subcontractor terms, or management approval before allowing production use or paying the next milestone.

Control point:
The startup accepts only one named customer change case after the vendor prepays and grants evidence-room access. The controlled case file includes the live customer request, change description, current and proposed subcontractor/subprocessor details, contracts/DPA/SLA snippets, data-flow and location evidence, security/BCP/DR artifacts, exit/data-return/deletion notes, reviewer issue log, final customer-ready change packet, and customer response/outcome record.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three prepaid live vendor change cases at 15,000+ PLN each; one customer-accepted change notification, consent, or no-objection; one clean no-go or rework decision where the startup refuses unsafe wording; one partner referral from a DORA/cyber/legal/fractional-CISO boutique; at least 45,000 PLN collected before broad template building.

6-month POC:
Complete 8-12 paid change-release cases; document 5+ accepted/no-objection/rework/no-go outcomes; get 2+ partners routing repeat cases; collect 200,000-350,000 PLN revenue; maintain gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs; keep fewer than 30% of accepted cases from becoming broad contract negotiation, cloud engineering, remediation, or legal-advice projects.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of Polish/CEE software vendors with visible bank, insurer, payment, leasing, fintech, or regulated-finance customers and public cloud/SaaS/subprocessor-heavy products. Send a narrow trigger message to CTOs, COOs, security leads, and founders: if a regulated-finance customer is blocking a cloud, subprocessor, support-provider, hosting, MSP, model-provider, or critical dependency change, the pack turns existing evidence into a customer-ready change response in 7 business days. In parallel, recruit DORA/cyber/legal/fractional-CISO boutiques that want to keep high-value advisory and legal work while referring urgent evidence-assembly overflow.

Economics:
Charge 15,000-35,000 PLN fixed fee for a 7-business-day change-release pack depending on urgency, buyer complexity, and artifact set. Pay security/cloud/legal reviewers only for flagged issue-spotting sections, typically 2,000-8,000 PLN per case. The vendor pays because a bank/customer go-live, renewal milestone, expansion, invoice, or production change is blocked and the fee is small relative to the regulated-finance relationship. A repeat annual or event-based refresh can price at 6,000-15,000 PLN when the same customer asks what changed.

Copy risk:
DORA consultants, cyber boutiques, law firms, ISO/SOC consultants, GRC vendors, cloud consultancies, and fractional CISOs can copy the format. The defense is not secret templates. The defense is narrow case selection, vendor-side timing, partner referral relationships, accepted/rejected customer-response memory, and a private map of which change evidence regulated financial customers actually accept, reject, escalate, or convert into legal/security/remediation work.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a vendor prepays for a named bank-customer change case and grants evidence-room access, the startup controls the work queue, issue log, change packet, reviewer routing, customer-response record, and post-response learning for that specific case. Competitors can offer broad DORA advice, but they do not have that vendor's live customer request, evidence room, change detail, reviewer flags, or accepted/rejected response history unless the vendor switches mid-case.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
No live regulated-finance customer change blocker means no sale. The deliverable is a customer-ready change-release packet tied to a named cloud/subprocessor/subcontractor event and a recorded customer outcome. It is not DORA readiness, a generic register audit, a legal memo, a dashboard, software, a template library, a marketplace, a brokered introduction, or open-ended consulting.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide DORA certification, legal advice, contract negotiation, formal audit, cloud migration engineering, security remediation, penetration testing, official outsourcing classification, or bank-side register reporting. Vendor management, legal, security, cloud, and executive owners make final decisions. Legal and security reviewers only perform bounded issue spotting on flagged sections. Cases requiring legal opinions, contract redlines, formal audit, remediation, false evidence, unresolved data-location risk, critical outsourcing classification disputes, or customer-specific prohibited subcontracting are declined or routed to the vendor's advisors.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is close to the confirmed DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack, but the unit, trigger, payment event, and proof artifact are different. The confirmed idea handles broad vendor-side regulated-finance renewals or procurements blocked by DORA/ICT evidence questionnaires. This idea accepts only a named material cloud/subprocessor/subcontractor/support-provider change case where an existing regulated-finance customer blocks go-live, production change, invoice, expansion, or milestone until the change is accepted, no-objected, or cleanly rejected. It does not handle general annual renewal questionnaires, bank-side registers, generic DORA readiness, or procurement evidence unrelated to a specific vendor change event.

Strongest anticipated objections:
DORA consultants, cyber boutiques, law firms, cloud consultants, GRC platforms, and fractional CISOs may already handle this. Many cases may need real legal advice, contract negotiation, security remediation, cloud architecture, or formal customer-specific risk assessment rather than evidence assembly. Banks may have their own forms, timelines, and approval committees, so customer acceptance remains outside the startup's control. Vendors may lack real evidence for data location, subcontractor terms, exit, BCP/DR, incident support, or access control. The idea may be too close to the already-confirmed DORA vendor evidence pack unless the company rejects broad renewals and accepts only named change-release cases. Sensitive contracts, security documents, outsourcing details, and customer correspondence create trust and handling burdens for an unknown part-time founder.
