Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
F-Gas Authorisation Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Importers of heat pumps, air-conditioning units, refrigeration equipment, and other non-pharma pre-charged HFC equipment will pay for a founder-controlled transaction desk that locks quota-holder authorisations or delegated authorisations in the F-gas Portal against named shipments, purchase orders, or customs-release blockers.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE importers, distributors, HVAC wholesalers, heat-pump importers, refrigeration-equipment importers, private-label equipment buyers, and customs-broker-routed importer clients that import non-pharma pre-charged equipment containing HFCs. The first buyer has a named shipment, purchase order, supplier invoice, product model list, refrigerant charge data, customs broker, and commercial consequence above 150,000 PLN if sufficient F-gas quota authorisation or delegation is not secured in time. Exclude metered dose inhalers, pharma, medical devices, clinical products, consumer one-off imports, illegal imports, and cases where the importer lacks valid portal registration or a lawful importer-of-record structure.

Acute trigger:
The importer or its customs broker discovers that a shipment, production order, seasonal reseller allocation, customs release for free circulation, or supplier dispatch is at risk because the HFCs in pre-charged equipment are not covered by sufficient quota authorisations or delegated authorisations from a quota holder or authorisation manager in the F-gas Portal. The buyer is not buying general F-gas advice; it is paying because goods cannot be placed on the EU market or released on time without a valid authorisation path.

Control point:
Signed transaction mandate over a named importer and named shipment/order; importer deposit or escrow instruction; equipment model list and refrigerant-charge/tCO2e calculation supplied by the importer or broker; written quote or option from a quota holder or authorisation manager for a defined tCO2e quantity; F-gas Portal registration/status checklist; portal acceptance or pending-acceptance record; buyer/seller payment sequence; broker-ready authorisation evidence file; and written no-go rules. The startup controls the matched importer demand, quota-holder option, portal transaction trail, payment condition, and fee right for that exact authorisation/delegation transaction.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Two quota holders or authorisation managers sign option or reservation terms for defined non-pharma pre-charged-equipment authorisation quantities. Three importers sign shipment-specific mandates and pay deposits or escrowed reservation fees for named orders totaling at least 1,500 tCO2e of requested coverage and at least 500,000 PLN in shipment value. At least one authorisation or delegated authorisation is accepted, pending acceptance, corrected, or cleanly rejected in the F-gas Portal with written reason. At least one case produces customs-broker progress, supplier dispatch progress, release-readiness progress, or paid clean no-go. At least 50,000 PLN is collected in case fees, reservation fees, and transaction spread without the founder taking unsold quota inventory risk.

6-month POC:
Close 20-45 importer cases through 4-8 quota holders, authorisation managers, customs brokers, HVAC distributors, and equipment importers. Process 8,000-25,000 tCO2e of matched authorisation or delegation demand. Produce 12+ accepted portal authorisations/delegations, shipment-release/procurement-progress outcomes, or clean no-go decisions. Collect 350,000-1,100,000 PLN in fees and spread with gross margin above 55% after referral, escrow, portal-admin, F-gas reviewer, and legal-boundary costs. Build a private transaction memory by product type, refrigerant, tCO2e calculation pattern, quota-holder terms, portal acceptance issue, importer registration gap, broker wording, payment sequence, and no-go reason.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with customs brokers, HVAC/refrigeration distributors, F-gas consultants, and small equipment importers that already see shipment blockers but do not want to source quota authorisations one by one. Parallel outreach goes to quota holders and authorisation managers with unused or under-monetized authorisation capacity. Partner pitch: "When an importer has a named pre-charged-equipment shipment blocked by authorisation coverage, route the transaction. You keep customs, legal, and importer-of-record work. I match the importer deposit to a quota-holder option, track the F-gas Portal acceptance trail, and deliver broker-ready authorisation evidence or a clean no-go."

Economics:
Charge 6,000-15,000 PLN for narrow transaction setup and portal-trail management, 15,000-45,000 PLN for urgent shipment/order lockbox cases, plus a transparent spread or success fee on matched authorisation quantities where commercially and legally permitted. Buyer deposits or escrow fund any quota-holder commitment before the founder pays nonrefundable consideration. A 300,000-1,500,000 PLN equipment shipment can justify a 15,000-45,000 PLN transaction fee if it avoids delay, demurrage, cancelled reseller allocation, or lost seasonal sales. The founder avoids inventory risk by refusing unmatched authorisation purchases.

Copy risk:
Quota holders, authorisation managers, F-gas consultants, customs brokers, refrigerant distributors, HVAC import specialists, and large importers can copy pieces or transact directly. The defense is transaction-specific control: once the startup has the importer mandate, deposit, equipment/tCO2e file, quota-holder option, portal acceptance trail, and payment sequence for a named shipment, a competitor cannot instantly access that exact matched authorisation transaction without displacing both sides under deadline pressure.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Incumbents can offer general F-gas help, but they cannot copy a specific optioned authorisation quantity, importer deposit, matched shipment file, and portal transaction trail already reserved for a named importer. The controlled object is not information; it is the matched authorisation/delegation path and payment sequence between a quota holder and an importer before a shipment or order deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell a dashboard, lead list, generic F-gas compliance report, quota marketplace, or customs advisory memo. It accepts only named shipment/order cases with importer authorization, quota-holder option terms, deposit or escrow, and a portal transaction path. The output is an accepted or pending F-gas Portal authorisation/delegation, broker-ready transaction evidence, shipment/procurement progress, or paid clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as importer of record, customs declarant, customs broker, legal adviser, F-gas authority, auditor, verifier, quota holder, refrigerant seller, equipment distributor, or medical/pharma product adviser. Importers, quota holders, authorisation managers, customs brokers, legal counsel, auditors, and authorities remain responsible for lawful import structure, product classification, portal registration, declarations of conformity, labelling, customs declarations, reports, audits, and regulatory conclusions. Reject cases involving metered dose inhalers, pharma, medical devices, consumer grey-market imports, invalid importer registration, suspected illegal HFC supply, sanctions, false refrigerant data, missing supplier charge data, disputed importer authority, or requests to misstate tCO2e calculations.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not CBAM Import Continuity Desk because this handles F-gas Portal quota authorisations for pre-charged HFC equipment, not embedded-carbon reporting, CBAM declarant readiness, CN/TARIC emissions exposure, or supplier emissions data. Not Data Act Machine Data Liberation because there is no industrial telemetry or machine-data access. Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack, PromoLeak, Amazon recovery, retailer chargeback recovery, or any health/Rx/clinical route. It is also distinct from the failed F-Gas Import Licence Continuity Desk because the controlled object is a matched authorisation/delegation transaction with deposit and quota-holder option, not an import evidence packet.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Quota holders and authorisation managers may prefer selling directly to importers or through existing F-gas consultants. The F-gas Portal already has official paths and market participants may not need a middle desk. Customs brokers and import specialists may internalize the workflow once they see it. Portal acceptance timing, correction windows, and authority questions may not fit urgent shipment deadlines. Many blockers may be due to invalid importer registration, wrong product classification, missing declaration of conformity, labelling, supplier data gaps, or illegal supply risk rather than lack of authorisation. Transaction spreads may be compressed if authorisation prices are transparent. Trust is nontrivial because both sides must share shipment, quota, pricing, and portal-status information. Large importers already have quota-holder relationships. Small importers may lack enough shipment value to pay. The model can drift into customs/legal/F-gas compliance advice unless the transaction boundary is strict.
