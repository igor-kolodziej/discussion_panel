Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
PackPlate Reorder Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Option or buy a retiring print broker, prepress shop, or packaging reorder desk's active source-file book, then transfer customer-approved packaging reorder payment direction around dielines, print files, proofs, plate/die references, supplier specs, and current repeat orders.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized food, cosmetics, pet-food, ecommerce, boutique retail, industrial, and CPG companies that repeatedly order the same labels, cartons, sleeves, pouches, shippers, printed bags, paper packaging, or product inserts through a retiring print broker, prepress operator, or packaging coordinator. The source seller has active customer files, current reorder history, supplier routes, proofs, dielines, plates/dies/cylinder references where relevant, and invoice history.

Acute trigger:
The print broker or prepress operator who holds the source files, approved proofs, supplier specs, order history, and customer communication path is retiring, overloaded, ill, or closing. Customers need repeat packaging orders without recreating artwork, finding old dielines, re-proofing colors, chasing plate/die references, onboarding new suppliers, or delaying SKU replenishment. The seller wants a low-friction handoff and earnout instead of shutting down or manually moving every customer.

Control point:
Signed seller option over source files, dielines, print-ready artwork, proofs, color targets, SKU/version history, plate/cylinder/die references, supplier specifications and pricing, supplier contacts, customer approvals, current orders, invoices, phone/domain/mailbox, support notes, and seller endorsement; customer reauthorization for reorder communication and payment direction; production remains with existing printers/converters unless the customer approves a change.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, option one retiring print broker/prepress/packaging reorder book; verify at least 40 active SKUs with source files, proofs, specs, order history, and supplier route; obtain 20 customer reauthorizations and payment-direction approvals; convert at least 150,000 PLN of current reorder value; collect at least 35,000 PLN gross margin before seller payout; and complete 10 reorder jobs with fewer than 5% rework or color/spec dispute.

6-month POC:
Option or acquire 2-4 small packaging source-file books; control 150-400 active SKUs; obtain 75-160 customer reauthorizations; convert 900,000-3,000,000 PLN of repeat reorder value; collect 180,000-650,000 PLN gross margin; maintain realized gross margin above 15% after printer/converter costs, shipping, rework, seller earnout, admin, and file cleanup; convert at least 20 larger accounts into annual source-file custody, SKU-version, and reorder-continuity retainers; and track reauthorization rate, reorder gross margin, rework rate, file-rights defects, supplier bypass, and support minutes by seller book.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to retiring print brokers, prepress operators, small packaging coordinators, label brokers, boutique paper-packaging shops, packaging designers who manage repeat orders, estate liquidators, and small print businesses closing because the owner no longer wants quoting, proofing, and reorder admin. The seller offer is low-upfront with earnout on retained gross margin. Customer handoff is seller-endorsed and specific: "Your existing files, proofs, suppliers, and reorder path continue; production provider does not change unless you approve it; you approve payment direction before any seller payout."

Economics:
Revenue comes from 8-20% gross margin on repeat packaging orders after printer/converter costs, 1,500-5,000 PLN source-file cleanup or SKU version-control fees for messy accounts, and 2,000-12,000 PLN annual source-file custody/reorder continuity retainers for larger customers. Seller payout is mostly earnout on retained gross margin and customer cash collected, keeping upfront exposure below 100,000 PLN. The startup does not need packaging machinery because production remains with existing printers and converters.

Copy risk:
Printers, converters, design agencies, print brokers, packaging consultants, and customers can copy pieces or route future jobs elsewhere. The defense is control of exact source files, dielines, proofs, plate/die/cylinder references, supplier specs, current orders, seller endorsement, customer reauthorizations, and payment direction before a competitor can reconstruct or win that exact reorder path.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs the option and customers reauthorize the source-file custody and reorder/payment path, a printer or broker cannot instantly reproduce that exact file history, proof library, SKU version map, supplier route, current order queue, and customer handoff without the customer deciding to rebuild or migrate. Competitors can win future redesigned packaging, but they cannot immediately control the acquired active reorder book.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business is an acquisition-led packaging reorder book built around controlled source files, proofs, supplier specs, and customer-approved payment direction. It does not sell generic design, compliance reviews, print consulting, dashboards, marketplaces, lead lists, or broker introductions. It accepts only repeat packaging reorder jobs tied to acquired files and reauthorized customers. No source-file book and no customer payment transfer means no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not certify food, cosmetics, supplement, chemical, product-safety, labeling, barcode, legal, regulatory, or advertising compliance. Do not create new package claims or approve label content. Customers remain responsible for legal and regulatory content, printer-ready approvals, barcodes, claims, ingredient/allergen data, recycling marks, and product information. Reject accounts where source-file rights are unclear, files belong to the customer or printer without transfer permission, regulated claims are changing, versions are uncontrolled, or repeat production requires new design/compliance work. Use written customer proof approval and production provider acknowledgement before print.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from MachinePlate, CIJ consumables, commercial cleaning consumables, route books, software connector books, and physical-lot ideas because the controlled asset is a packaging source-file and proof/spec reorder book with current repeat orders and payment transfer. It is distinct from PromoLeak, Amazon recovery, KSeF invoice release, DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, HeritageDoor, and other confirmed ideas because there is no claim recovery, evidence response, regulated import/release, marketplace ticket, or salvage resale.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Printing and packaging are crowded and relationship-driven. Customers may own the source files or may be able to ask the printer directly. Printers and converters may bypass the startup once the handoff is visible. Seller files may be messy, obsolete, poorly named, missing fonts/links, unclear in rights, or not truly print-ready. Current reorder volume may be too low or lumpy. Gross margin can disappear through quote chasing, proofing, color disputes, shipping, rework, minimum order quantities, rush fees, and seller earnout. Food, cosmetics, pet-food, chemical, and supplement labels can pull the business into compliance risk. Customers may use the transition to consolidate with a direct printer or agency. The business may become ordinary print brokerage unless most revenue comes from repeat orders tied to controlled source files and payment direction.
