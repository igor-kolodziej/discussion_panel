## Materially Different Candidate

`Provider-Led GLP-1 Employer Metabolic Cohort Book`

This acquires/options a benefits broker or occupational-health micro-operator's employer wellness/admin book and uses it to sell a private metabolic-risk cohort to employers. The founder controls employer deposits, cohort admin, reserved licensed-clinic capacity, aggregate reporting, scheduling, and non-clinical operations.

The founder does not prescribe, sell, advertise, reserve, dispense, deliver, or transport prescription medicines. Licensed obesity/diabetes/endocrinology clinics remain provider and clinical merchant of record. Licensed pharmacies handle prescription medicine dispensing and pharmacy pickup. Clinical decisions, medicine choice, eligibility, monitoring, contraindications, side effects, and discontinuation are handled only by licensed clinicians.

Public positioning must avoid prescription-drug names and drug-specific promises. Internally, the clinical pathway may include GLP-1/GIP pharmacotherapy only when independently prescribed by the licensed provider.

## Buyer

- employers with 200-1,000 employees aged 40-60+;
- logistics, manufacturing, call centers, retail chains, and sedentary office employers;
- HR/benefits leads seeing employee demand for obesity medications and unsafe self-sourcing;
- benefits brokers and occupational-health brokers who already sell private care add-ons.

## Acute Trigger

Employees are already asking about pharmacological weight-loss pathways, self-sourcing drugs online, or struggling with cardiometabolic risk. Employers want a lawful private-care benefit that reduces unsafe self-treatment and gives a structured medical path without seeing individual health data.

## Control Point

The acquired/optioned asset and POC control stack:

- signed employer book or broker-introduced employer access;
- employer deposits and cohort payment flow before clinic rollout;
- reserved licensed clinic consultation/lab slots;
- provider-of-record agreement and clinic merchant-of-record payment architecture;
- pharmacy pickup and cold-chain education SOP, without startup handling medicines;
- sharps/waste education workflow and licensed waste contractor only for clinic-generated waste where applicable;
- aggregate-only employer reporting templates;
- DPA/privacy model separating employer from individual health data;
- call-routing/admin, scheduling, reminders, and cohort operations.

The hard proof is not a landing page or app. It is paid employer cohort deposits plus signed clinic capacity and real licensed-provider visits.

Required proof:

2. Two employer cohort contracts or paid pilots.
3. At least one employer pays `15,000+ PLN` deposit before launch; target `30,000+ PLN` total deposited/prepaid.

6. At least 15 clinically appropriate treatment starts or care plans where the clinician decides the pathway; no startup prescribing or medicine influence.
7. No public Rx drug advertising, no public drug names, no patient acquisition based on a named prescription product.
8. Employer sees only aggregate utilization/operations reporting; no individual health data.
9. Written pharmacy pickup/cold-chain education SOP and clinic waste-handling guardrail; startup does not touch medicines.

## Economics

Revenue:

- `12,000-25,000 PLN` employer setup/cohort fee;
- `180-300 PLN` per participant per month for non-clinical cohort operations;
- optional one-time scheduling/admin fee per licensed-provider visit;
- clinic bills clinical care directly or through legally reviewed provider-of-record/merchant-of-record model;
- startup revenue is admin/cohort operations, not medicine margin.

Gross-margin logic:

The POC is not capital-heavy if employers pay deposits and the clinic owns clinical delivery. Contribution margin can be `50-65%` on employer admin/cohort fees after coordinator, scheduling, privacy, and reporting costs. One 50-person employer cohort at `15,000-25,000 PLN` setup plus monthly admin can pay back POC legal/admin costs if clinic fees are passed through or separately billed.

## Why This Is Not A Service/Report/Dashboard/Marketplace

The startup controls signed employer cohort payment flow and reserved licensed-provider capacity. It is not selling advice, drug access, a weight-loss app, a report, or a patient marketplace. The proof is employer deposit plus clinic capacity plus completed licensed-provider visits.

## Copy Risk

Large private medical networks can copy obesity-care benefits. The defense is not proprietary medicine access. The defense is acquired broker/employer access, privacy-compliant cohort operations, prepaid employer contracts, local clinic capacity, and a narrow "safe pharmacotherapy pathway without Rx advertising" playbook.
