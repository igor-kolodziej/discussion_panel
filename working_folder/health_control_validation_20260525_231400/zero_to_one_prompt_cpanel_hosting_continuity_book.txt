Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
cPanel/Plesk Hosting Continuity Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or option tiny cPanel/Plesk hosting books from retiring solo hosts and web studios, but pay meaningful seller consideration only after customer-approved billing transfer, admin custody, backups, and first-month recurring cash prove continuity.

Exact buyer:
The payors are small Polish/CEE businesses whose websites, email, DNS, SSL, backups, renewals, PHP/runtime settings, and light hosting support are currently handled by a retiring solo host, small web studio, local hosting reseller, or freelancer. The seller is a micro-hosting operator with 30-120 recurring SME accounts that are too small or old-fashioned for larger MSPs to pursue carefully, but too business-critical to abandon.

Acute trigger:
A solo host or small web studio is retiring, closing, moving upmarket, or trying to stop low-ticket hosting support. Customers face continuity risk around website uptime, business email, DNS, domain renewals, SSL, backups, PHP/runtime versions, billing records, and forgotten admin credentials. The handoff is a churn point unless one operator takes custody of the book, obtains customer approvals, rotates access, and collects the next billing cycle before final seller payout.

Control point:
Signed option or conditional asset purchase over one named hosting book; seller-mediated customer continuity notices; customer-approved billing transfer; transferred or rotated registrar, DNS, control-panel, mailbox, backup, server, billing, and support access; server and account inventory; recent invoices and payment status; support history; backup verification; old-owner transition availability; and first-month cash collected by the new operator before meaningful closing cash is paid.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One signed 60-90 day exclusivity/option agreement over a named cPanel/Plesk hosting book; full account inventory and last 12 months of invoices/support history; transfer notices sent by the seller; at least 40 customer transfer consents; at least 30 customers paying the new operator before close; registrar/DNS/control-panel/server/backup access rotated or moved for the transferred accounts; one live support or renewal ticket resolved under the new operator; at least 15,000 PLN monthly recurring gross revenue or equivalent annualized renewals under transferred payment direction; no large seller payout until these events happen.

6-month POC:
Complete one book transfer with 50+ retained customers, churn below 20%, 55%+ gross margin after hosting infrastructure, contractor support, licences, backup storage, security tooling, accounting, and seller earnout; collect at least 90,000 PLN gross revenue over six months from hosting, maintenance, renewal, migration, and security-hardening fees; resolve at least 30 ordinary support or renewal tickets without turning into bespoke web development; document a second seller pipeline with two signed options or serious term sheets.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of 150-250 Polish/CEE solo hosts, cPanel/Plesk resellers, small web studios, local WordPress agencies, and freelancers showing old client portfolios, hosting pages, reseller plans, or maintenance offers. Contact owners with a direct succession offer: "If you want to stop hosting support without abandoning clients, I will option the book, send continuity notices through you, collect first-month customer payments, and pay you mostly from retained recurring cash rather than asking you to run a sales process." Also source sellers through accounting offices, registrar/hosting communities, web freelancer groups, and small agencies that no longer want support-heavy legacy accounts.

Economics:
Initial customer pricing is inherited where possible, then normalized after continuity: 80-250 PLN/month for simple hosting/mail maintenance, 300-800 PLN/month for higher-touch business sites, 300-1,500 PLN one-off migration/security/runtime cleanup, and annual renewal packages for domains, SSL, backups, and email. Seller payout is earnout-heavy, for example 1-3 months retained gross margin upfront after transfer proof plus 10-25% of retained gross margin for 12 months. The POC stays under 100,000 PLN because meaningful payment waits for customer cash and admin transfer. Payback can occur from retained MRR and cleanup fees rather than speculative acquisition spend.

Copy risk:
Large hosts, MSPs, web agencies, freelancers, and registrar resellers can offer hosting and support. They cannot copy the specific book once the seller signs an option, sends customer handoff notices, customers approve payment transfer, admin access is rotated, backups are verified, and the new operator receives the next billing cycle. The advantage is not hosting technology; it is controlled transfer of an ignored but live payment/admin book.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The first asset is a named seller's active customer book and access estate: invoices, support inbox, DNS, registrar, control-panel accounts, backups, server list, renewal calendar, customer approvals, and seller trust at the handoff moment. A larger host can market to those SMEs later, but cannot instantly obtain the seller-mediated approvals, access inventory, payment direction, and transition context once the book is optioned and customers have paid the new operator.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell new web design, SEO, ad management, generic MSP projects, hosting comparison, leads, dashboards, or advisory reports. It acquires and operates a specific recurring payment/admin continuity book. The proof is transferred access, customer consent, first-month cash, retained MRR, and resolved continuity tickets.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No customer funds are held in escrow. No email-content review, privacy/legal advice, SEO claims, ad management, custom app rebuilds, or security guarantees are included. Hacked sites, malware cleanup, custom PHP development, ecommerce checkout repairs, legal-content updates, and heavy migrations are scoped separately or rejected during diligence. Customer data access is minimized, credentials are rotated, backups are verified, processor terms are updated where needed, and customers remain free to move to any provider. The startup does not become a domain registrar unless it already has proper reseller terms; domains stay with registrars or approved resellers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, or TraceFaktura. It is closest to prior unconfirmed digital-admin and connector book buyouts, but the control point is different from a domain-renewal calendar or custom connector book: it requires customer-approved billing transfer plus cPanel/Plesk hosting/admin custody, backups, support history, first-month cash, and seller-mediated continuity before close.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Commodity hosting is crowded and customers can move to known providers. Tiny books may hide underpriced support, angry clients, hacked WordPress sites, stale PHP, weak backups, missing credentials, unclear registrar ownership, unpaid invoices, insecure plugins, mailbox deliverability problems, and old custom code. A part-time founder may struggle with urgent outages, email failures, malware, migrations, and security incidents. Larger hosts and MSPs can win customers after the handoff. Seller records may be messy or overstated. The first book may be too small for owner earnings after infrastructure, contractor, support, security tooling, accounting, churn, and seller earnout.
