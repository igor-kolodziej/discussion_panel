## Zero To One Summary

The evaluator credited the discipline of selling only against active professional repair demand and deposits. It also agreed that vintage/discontinued watch parts can be hard to source, that exact parts unlock paid repairs, and that retiring watchmaker lots can be under-monetized when sellers do not want cataloging work.

The idea failed because the market is not empty. Specialist watch-parts suppliers, caliber-search suppliers, eBay sellers, estate buyers, forums, and private watchmaker networks already serve the need. The startup would need rare supply access, cheap acquisition, and trusted horology expertise very quickly.

## Strongest Objections

1. Supply sourcing is harder than the thesis admits; good retiring watchmakers often know their drawers are valuable or already have dealer/watchmaker relationships.
2. Cheap available lots may be cheap because they are generic, incomplete, corroded, quartz-heavy, dead inventory, or impossible to identify profitably.
3. Existing suppliers already sell vintage parts by make and caliber, including large international inventories.
4. Authentication, originality, compatibility, condition, counterfeit, and provenance mistakes can destroy buyer trust.
5. The founder has no horology credibility and would need a paid watchmaker expert immediately.
6. Cataloging unsorted lots is slow, expert-heavy, and error-prone.
7. Many parts will be low-value or slow-moving, making realized margin much weaker than headline rare-part examples.
8. Professional watchmakers may prefer known suppliers and may not prepay until trust is built.
9. Poland alone is too small; EU-wide shipping and buyer acquisition would be required.
10. The likely outcome is a profitable specialist parts desk, not a scalable or highly defensible company.

## Evaluator's Best Narrowing

The best first niche would be Polish/CEE independent watchmakers repairing vintage Omega, Longines, Atlantic, Doxa, Tissot, Certina, Poljot, Raketa, Blonie, and common ETA/AS/FHF/Landeron/Valjoux-family movements. The evaluator advised against starting with Rolex, Patek, AP, or other high-fraud/restricted-provenance categories.

The better sequence would be:

- collect 100-200 exact active requests from professional watchmakers before buying large lots;
- buy or option only lots that overlap known requests;
- build a private compatibility/substitution map from paid searches;
- become the buyer of choice for retiring watchmakers and estates in Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Baltics, and Germany.

## Lesson

Compact physical inventory is not enough. The supply side must be less professionalized and the founder must not need rare craft credibility to identify value. Future physical-control rounds should only advance if the founder can evaluate the asset cheaply or buyer deposits prove value before expert cataloging work.
