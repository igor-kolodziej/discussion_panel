Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
AuditCAPA Retail Order Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
CEE food, beverage, and food-packaging suppliers lose purchase orders, supplier approval, or certificate continuity when BRCGS, IFS, SMETA, retailer, or customer audits leave nonconformities unresolved; a founder can run a prepaid, evidence-only closeout desk for live audit CAPA files where the responsible site has already fixed or can prove the issue and the first proof is buyer, auditor, certification-body, or portal acceptance.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE food manufacturers, private-label suppliers, beverage suppliers, food-packaging converters, and consumer-goods suppliers with 20-500 employees that sell to retailers, brands, food-service groups, exporters, or marketplaces and have a live BRCGS, IFS, SMETA, retailer, or customer audit nonconformity blocking a purchase order, supplier approval, certificate issuance, certificate continuity, audit grade, customer onboarding, shipment, or payment milestone. The buyer has a named QA/technical manager and a written audit report, corrective action plan, or customer CAPA request.

Acute trigger:
A buyer, retailer, certification body, auditor, Sedex/SMETA workflow, or customer portal requires corrective-action evidence by a deadline or before order release. The supplier has already implemented the fix or has existing evidence, but the file is scattered across QA records, photos, pest-control logs, training records, maintenance work orders, cleaning records, traceability tests, lab CoAs, calibration records, supplier approvals, management review notes, procedure revisions, or contractor invoices. The trigger is current commercial blockage, not routine audit preparation.

Control point:
Signed mandate over a named audit/CAPA file; audit report or nonconformity list; buyer/customer/certification-body deadline and consequence; QA owner authorization; evidence owner map; source documents; photo/video evidence where appropriate; procedure/training/log revisions; contractor/lab records; corrective-action narrative; root-cause and preventive-action text supplied or approved by the responsible site; submission checklist; portal/upload or email submission record; follow-up register; and fee triggers tied to closure acceptance, narrowed follow-up, certificate progress, buyer approval, PO/shipment/payment release, or clean no-go.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Eight suppliers sign prepaid live CAPA closeout mandates at 7,000-25,000 PLN each; every case has a written buyer/auditor/certification-body/customer request, a deadline, named nonconformities, evidence owners, and a commercial consequence above 75,000 PLN. At least five files are submitted to the relevant buyer, auditor, certification body, Sedex/SMETA workflow, or portal; at least three produce closure acceptance, narrowed follow-up, certificate progress, buyer approval, PO release, shipment/payment progress, or written clean no-go; at least 80,000 PLN is collected; one QA consultant, auditor-adjacent reviewer, or retail-supplier adviser routes a second case.

6-month POC:
Process 35-70 live CAPA/order-release files through 6-10 QA consultants, retail-supplier advisers, certification-prep consultants, and direct supplier referrals; collect 350,000-900,000 PLN in fixed case fees and urgent closeout fees; document 20+ accepted closures, buyer approvals, certificate-progress events, PO/shipment/payment releases, narrowed follow-ups, or clean no-go outcomes; maintain gross margin above 60% after QA/food-safety reviewer and document contractor time; build a private taxonomy by scheme, retailer, nonconformity type, evidence object, accepted proof format, deadline, reviewer pattern, and no-go reason.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target QA consultants, food-safety consultants, retail-supplier advisers, certification-prep consultants, packaging consultants, and export managers who already see suppliers panic after audits but do not want to run evidence rooms and portal follow-up. Pitch: "When your supplier client has already fixed the issue but the BRCGS/IFS/SMETA/retailer CAPA file is scattered and blocking an order or certificate, route the case. We assemble the evidence, closure narrative, submission register, and follow-up file. You keep the food-safety advice, audit-prep relationship, and technical judgment."

Economics:
Charge 7,000-12,000 PLN for narrow single-audit evidence closeout files, 12,000-25,000 PLN for urgent retailer or certification-body blockers, and 25,000-45,000 PLN for multi-site or multi-buyer CAPA packages. Referral partners keep 20-30% if they source and review the case. The supplier pays because a blocked private-label order, customer approval, export listing, certificate, or shipment can be worth 75,000-1,000,000+ PLN, while the startup does bounded document reconstruction and submission tracking rather than full QA remediation.

Copy risk:
Food-safety consultants, QA consultants, certification-prep advisers, internal QA teams, technical writers, audit-prep firms, Sedex/SMETA consultants, and retailer-compliance specialists can copy the workflow. The defense is live case routing through consultants who do not want operational evidence chasing, a growing acceptance library by scheme/retailer/nonconformity, fast closeout templates, no-go discipline, and control of specific in-flight CAPA files once the supplier signs the mandate and pays.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a supplier signs a mandate and prepays for a named CAPA file, the startup controls the evidence room, source-document map, submission register, buyer/auditor/certification-body correspondence log, follow-up dates, and fee trigger for that exact audit nonconformity set. A consultant or internal QA team can copy the general process later, but they cannot instantly control the already-paid evidence file, response sequence, uploaded packet, accepted proof format, or partner-routed follow-up for the live blocked order or certificate.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell routine audit preparation, a dashboard, a consultant marketplace, a document library, or generic food-safety advice. It accepts only live audit/CAPA blockers with written nonconformities, a deadline, source evidence, a responsible QA owner, and a buyer/order/certificate consequence. The output is a submitted closeout package and tracked counterparty outcome: accepted closure, narrowed follow-up, certificate progress, buyer approval, PO/shipment/payment progress, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as certification body, auditor, food-safety consultant of record, legal adviser, labor adviser, technical manager, site inspector, pest-control provider, lab, maintenance provider, or supplier QA department. The supplier remains responsible for product safety, legality, site practices, root-cause truth, actual corrective actions, staff training, records, and management approval. Certification bodies, auditors, buyers, retailers, Sedex/SMETA approved auditor companies, labs, contractors, lawyers, and food-safety experts remain responsible for certification, audit judgment, acceptance, legal/labor advice, testing, and technical conclusions. Cases are rejected when the fix is not actually implemented, evidence would be misleading, the nonconformity involves worker harm or legal/labor violations needing specialists, the issue requires infrastructure/process remediation before submission, or the buyer wants certification/legal guarantees.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because the buyer is not an ICT vendor answering bank third-party-risk requests. It is not REDBlocked, MoCRA, CPSC eFiling, EPRBlocked, HazmatBlocked, or any Amazon/platform product-evidence desk. It is not TraceFaktura, CBAM, GreenTender EPREL, Data Act, PromoLeak, BioSignal, Supplement Stack, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, HeritageDoor, KSeF AP release, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The buyer trigger, proof artifact, and payment event are specific to live audit nonconformity closeout and retailer/order/certificate release.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Food-safety consultants, QA consultants, certification-prep firms, internal QA teams, Sedex/SMETA advisers, and retailer-compliance specialists may already do this. Many nonconformities require real operational remediation, training, equipment, lab work, or site changes before any truthful evidence can be submitted. Certification bodies, auditors, retailers, and customer QA teams control acceptance and may reject the packet. The founder needs credible QA/food-safety reviewers from day one. Some SMETA and labor-related issues are sensitive and should not be handled by a document desk. The 60-day proof may be too aggressive because evidence collection and reviewer feedback are time-compressed. Success attribution can be disputed if the supplier would have closed the NC anyway. The workflow can drift into audit prep, certification consulting, legal/labor advice, food-safety remediation, or broad QA outsourcing unless boundaries are strict.
