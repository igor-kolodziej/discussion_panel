Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: Retail EDI Order-To-Cash Continuity Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Option or acquire a retiring or overloaded micro-EDI integrator's live retail-supplier order-to-cash maintenance book, transfer customer-approved admin/payment rights, and keep production EDI/KSeF-adjacent order, ASN, invoice, and remittance flows alive under prepaid continuity contracts.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE SME suppliers, distributors, and challenger brands selling to large retailers, marketplaces, wholesalers, or buying groups through existing EDI/order-to-cash flows. The first customers are already paying a small EDI maintainer for exact production mappings, not shopping for a new ERP implementation. The seller is a one-person or small-team EDI freelancer, retired ERP/retail-integration specialist, overloaded micro-MSP, or tiny integration shop with recurring support invoices and no clean successor.

Acute trigger:
A broken EDI/order-to-cash flow can block or delay purchase orders, order responses, despatch advice/ASN, goods-receipt matching, invoice acceptance, KSeF-to-retailer handoff, remittance matching, deductions, or payment. Poland's 2026 KSeF transition increases invoice-flow change pressure, but the buyer pays because an existing production order-to-cash dependency must not break.

Control point:
Signed seller option plus source scripts, mappings, AS2/SFTP/API/mailbox credentials, certificates, GLN/partner profiles, retailer-specific runbooks, support history, renewal calendar, customer contacts, seller endorsement, customer-approved admin transfer, customer-approved payee transfer, and prepaid continuity contracts.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. One signed option or acquisition agreement with a named micro-EDI vendor covering code, mappings, credentials, runbooks, support inbox, customer list, renewal rights, seller handoff, and earnout.
2. Clean IP/assignment review and data-processing/security terms for message handling.
3. At least 8 supplier customers approve transfer of support owner, access, and payee.
4. At least 6 supplier customers prepay 3 months or one annual continuity term, with at least 35,000 PLN cleared before close.
5. Credentials/certificates are rotated or placed under customer-approved access control.
6. At least one live production EDI/order-to-cash incident or change ticket is resolved under the new owner.
7. At least one current message batch is monitored or processed under new custody: order, order response, ASN/despatch advice, invoice, remittance, or retailer status.
8. Support history proves reusable maintenance, not bespoke ERP cleanup; target contribution margin above 55% after contractor support, security ops, legal amortization, and seller earnout.

6-month POC:
Month 1: identify 30-50 micro-EDI maintainers and retail-integration freelancers, sign one seller option, and review customer list, support history, IP, and access-transfer feasibility.
Month 2: secure 8 customer approvals, 6 prepaid continuity contracts, credential/certificate custody, and at least 35,000 PLN cleared before closing payment.
Month 3: resolve one live production ticket, monitor or process at least one current order-to-cash message batch, and document access logs, backup/rollback, and incident boundaries.
Months 4-6: retain at least 10 paying customers, collect 60,000-120,000 PLN in continuity/transition fees, keep average support below 4 hours per customer per month, keep gross contribution above 55%, and build case memory by retailer, message type, mapping issue, certificate/access issue, KSeF invoice handoff issue, and win/loss reason.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target one-person EDI freelancers, retired ERP/Comarch/ECOD/GS1/retail integration specialists, small integration shops, and overloaded micro-MSPs serving suppliers that sell to Polish and CEE retail chains. Offer a 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option plus seller earnout only after customer prepayments clear. Use customer prepayment before close as the truth serum that the book is live and transferable.

Economics:
Charge 1,500-4,000 PLN per supplier per month for continuity, monitoring, certificate/access maintenance, mapping-change support, and emergency order-to-cash incident response. Charge 5,000-15,000 PLN transition or KSeF compatibility sprints only for inherited customers and only when scope is bounded. Six customers prepaying at least 35,000 PLN funds diligence and transition. A 20-40 customer book can produce meaningful owner earnings if support stays reusable and net-new implementation, ERP cleanup, tax advice, and accounting process redesign are excluded.

Copy risk:
Comarch, ECOD/EDI providers, ERP vendors, GS1/EDI consultants, MSPs, and freelancers can replace or build flows. The defense is not generic EDI skill. It is seller-endorsed custody of exact production mappings, credentials, retailer profiles, support history, current customer approvals, and prepaid customer contracts before competitors see the handoff risk.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder controls the seller option, customer-approved admin transfer, credentials, runbooks, support inbox, renewal/payment rights, and prepaid continuity contracts, a competitor cannot recreate those exact live production flows without winning a replacement project from each customer. Customers can switch later, but the immediate continuity lane and payment stream are controlled for the mandate period.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The founder is not selling a KSeF report, EDI dashboard, or new integration marketplace. The business acquires an existing paid production dependency and its recurring payment stream. Service work exists only to maintain the acquired book under strict scope and to protect already-live order-to-cash flows.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The company does not provide tax advice, legal advice, accounting advice, or guarantee KSeF compliance. The customer, accountant, ERP owner, or tax advisor remains responsible for invoice content, VAT treatment, and formal filing decisions. The startup maintains transport, mapping, credential, certificate, monitoring, and message-delivery continuity under customer-approved access. It does not impersonate the taxpayer, issue invoices without authorization, alter commercial truth, or accept broad ERP/accounting cleanup inside the continuity fee.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, or Data Act. It is adjacent to prior unconfirmed connector-buyout ideas, but materially narrower: live retail EDI order-to-cash flows, retailer message profiles, KSeF invoice handoff pressure, current customer prepayment, and acquired recurring continuity payment rights. It is not a generic ERP connector marketplace or KSeF implementation shop.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The exact seller profile may be scarce or messy.
Customers may prefer a larger established EDI provider once the old maintainer exits.
IP, credentials, certificates, customer contracts, or data-processing terms may be non-transferable.
Support history may reveal bespoke ERP/accounting cleanup rather than reusable continuity maintenance.
KSeF scope may drift into tax, legal, or accounting advice.
Retailer EDI providers and ERP vendors can replace customers over time.
Part-time operation is risky if order-to-cash incidents require fast response.
The first book may be too small to prove repeatable acquisition supply or durable owner earnings.
