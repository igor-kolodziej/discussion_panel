## Lead Candidate: ShowComms Intercom Fleet Lockbox

### Buyer

Small and mid-sized AV rental firms, theatre/venue technical managers, broadcast/event production companies, conference AV firms, and touring production suppliers in Poland and CEE that already use a named production-intercom ecosystem but cannot justify buying a larger fleet until a show, festival, convention, broadcast, or corporate event requires it.

### Acute Trigger

A dated production needs more compatible intercom packs than the buyer owns. The rider, event plan, broadcast run-of-show, stage manager plan, or production safety workflow requires additional beltpacks, base stations, headsets, chargers, batteries, antennas, cables, and spare units that work with the buyer's existing system. If the extra packs are not secured, the buyer subhires at expensive last-minute rates, loses margin, disappoints the production manager, or cannot accept the job.

### Control Point

The controlled asset is a titled, tested, serial-numbered fleet of compatible production-intercom hardware, purchased or optioned from a retiring/upgrading AV rental firm, theatre, broadcaster, venue, or event supplier before the units are split or sold as singles:

- base station or master unit where relevant;
- beltpacks or wireless packs;
- headsets;
- chargers, batteries, antennas, cables, spare clips, power supplies, cases;
- firmware/configuration notes where needed;
- serial list, photos, condition grades, battery-health notes, and test log;
- buyer deposits or signed subhire/resale bookings tied to dated events before final purchase.

### 60-Day Proof

1. One signed option or bill of sale over a coherent intercom fleet of at least 20 compatible packs/units with accessories.
2. Bench test and condition log from a named AV technician or rental-house engineer.
3. Three signed rental/resale reservations or deposits from AV buyers for named event dates or fixed delivery windows.
4. At least 40,000 PLN in deposits, rental commitments, or resale margin tied to the exact serial-numbered fleet.
5. First paid subhire/resale invoice and handoff record before total capital exposure exceeds 100,000 PLN.

### Economics

The founder should not buy random AV gear. The first transaction must be deposit-backed. A retiring/upgrading rental house may sell a coherent older fleet below replacement value because it wants one buyer, fast pickup, and no single-unit listing/support burden. The startup earns either:

- resale spread on a controlled lot sold to a rental firm or venue;
- short-term subhire fees from dated events before resale;
- a margin on splitting only after full-fleet deposits prove demand.

Target first-lot economics: 45,000-90,000 PLN acquisition/control cost, 20,000-60,000 PLN gross spread or subhire margin within six months, and 50%+ expected gross margin on incremental subhire once the fleet is tested and cased.

### Copy Risk

AV dealers, rental houses, OEM distributors, theatres, broadcasters, and production suppliers already understand intercom gear. The business is not defensible as a generic used-gear dealer. It is defensible only around a specific controlled fleet plus signed event/rental deposits: once the founder controls the exact serial-numbered fleet and deposits, competitors cannot fill those same dated bookings with that same compatible lot.

### Why It Is Not A Service Or Broker

The startup owns or options the gear, documents condition, takes title/condition risk, collects deposits for exact serial-numbered units, and invoices a rental/resale transaction. It does not sell production advice, crew staffing, RF coordination, a rental marketplace, an equipment database, or generic introductions. Buyer/rental houses remain responsible for show design, crew operation, RF coordination, insurance, and production safety.

### Boundaries

Do not accept mission-critical broadcast/tour obligations requiring 24/7 technical support unless a rental-house partner is contracted. Avoid gear with unclear title, stolen-risk flags, missing accessories, unsupported batteries, firmware locks, region/frequency noncompliance, unsafe chargers, or untestable defects. The first fleet should be equipment that can be bench tested, insured/shipped simply, and handed to professional AV buyers who already know how to operate it.

### Duplicate Risk

This is not HeritageDoor or the failed hotel FF&E lot. The controlled asset is compact, serial-numbered, functional event hardware with dated buyer deposits, not bulky furniture or architectural salvage. It is not the failed industrial-spares route because buyers are not asking the founder to repair production lines under emergency technician trust; they are professional AV/rental buyers reserving a compatible fleet for named events.
