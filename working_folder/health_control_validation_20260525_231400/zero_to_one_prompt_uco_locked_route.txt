Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
UCO Locked Route With Recycler Prepay

One-sentence thesis:
Lock dense used-cooking-oil collection routes from restaurants and commercial kitchens only after named sites sign exclusive collection direction, the legal BDO/KPO collection chain is handled by registered parties, a downstream recycler/feedstock buyer gives a purchase commitment or prepay, and the first paid pickup proves cash movement.

Exact buyer:
The cash buyer is a biodiesel feedstock buyer, UCO aggregator, recycler, or processor that pays for documented used cooking oil with acceptable quality and traceability. The controlled supply comes from restaurant groups, ghost kitchens, catering commissaries, hotel kitchens, food courts, mall restaurant clusters, franchisees, and independent Horeca operators in tight pickup radii that generate recurring used cooking oil but have unreliable, unpaid, opaque, or ad hoc collection.

Acute trigger:
Food sites have recurring used cooking oil that is being underpaid, collected unreliably, exposed to theft or contamination, or handled through informal cash pickups that create admin and traceability risk. Downstream buyers need documented, repeatable, quality-controlled UCO supply rather than random spot barrels. The founder intervenes when a site cluster wants cleaner pickup, clearer payment, sealed containers, BDO/KPO documentation, and predictable collection without managing multiple collectors.

Control point:
Signed exclusive collection direction from named sites covering pickup location, estimated liters per month, container custody, contamination rules, photo/weight/ticket proof, payment terms, minimum duration, and right to route the oil to the startup's selected registered collector and recycler buyer. The startup also controls a downstream purchase order or prepay formula from a recycler/feedstock buyer, the pickup schedule, route book, BDO/KPO documentation trail where applicable, site contact list, and first paid pickup settlement. Licensed or registered waste collection/transport/storage duties stay with the proper collector/provider of record.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, the founder signs 25-40 kitchens in one or two dense Warsaw-area clusters with exclusive UCO collection direction for at least six months, expected supply of at least 4,000-8,000 liters per month, documented container and contamination rules, and site consent to route pickups through the selected collector/provider. A recycler or feedstock buyer signs a purchase commitment or prepay formula for accepted oil from those sites. A registered collector/provider confirms the legal collection and documentation process. The first pickup batch is weighed or ticketed, supported by site evidence and BDO/KPO trail where required, accepted by the buyer, and paid to the startup or escrow before any meaningful seller/route consideration is paid.

6-month POC:
Build one route with 40-80 active kitchens, 8,000-18,000 liters per month, at least 90% scheduled pickup completion, less than 5% rejected or downgraded oil, documented traceability for each pickup, and positive cash conversion from buyer payment before supplier/site payouts where possible. Gross margin after collector fees, containers, route coordination, quality losses, admin, and contractor support should exceed 25-35% on the spread or produce at least 15,000-35,000 PLN monthly gross profit. Prove route retention, repeat buyer payments, and a second cluster sourced through the same signed-route-before-pickup playbook.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with clusters where one operator controls multiple kitchens or where kitchens are physically dense: food courts, ghost kitchen buildings, franchise groups, hotel groups, catering commissaries, malls, and restaurant streets. Offer sites a reliable container/pickup/payment package and clean documentation, not a generic recycling pitch. Only sign locations that meet minimum expected liters, have oil storage discipline, accept contamination rules, and agree to exclusivity. In parallel, pre-negotiate the recycler/feedstock buyer formula and registered collector/provider role before committing to route economics.

Economics:
Revenue comes from the spread between downstream buyer payment for accepted documented UCO and the cost of site payments, collector/provider fees, containers, routing, admin, quality losses, and contractor support. The 100,000 PLN budget covers containers, route setup, legal/provider setup, first contractor operations, quality checks, admin tooling, and working capital. The model avoids buying trucks or building a processing plant. It only scales routes where buyer price, collector cost, site payment, and contamination loss leave cash-positive unit economics before expansion.

Copy risk:
Existing UCO collectors, waste companies, biodiesel feedstock traders, restaurant suppliers, and recyclers can approach the same kitchens or offer better prices. The defense is not generic recycling knowledge. It is control of a signed dense route, site exclusivity, container custody, documentation history, accepted quality record, downstream buyer commitment, and first paid pickup settlement before a competitor can assemble the same cluster.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
After the founder has signed site-level exclusive collection direction, installed or assigned containers, scheduled pickup through the registered collector/provider, tied the route to a recycler purchase commitment, and completed the first paid pickup with traceability evidence, a competitor cannot own that exact route, site permissions, pickup history, quality record, and payment stream without inducing the sites to breach or wait out the contract. They can compete for future routes, but they cannot retroactively control the current signed cluster and accepted oil flow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup controls a recurring physical supply route and payment stream. It does not sell consulting, recycling advice, compliance reports, software dashboards, restaurant lead lists, marketplace matching, or broker introductions. The proof is signed site collection direction, downstream buyer commitment or prepay, legal collection/provider chain, accepted pickup evidence, and buyer cash received.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not pretend to be a licensed waste collector, transporter, storage site, processor, biodiesel producer, or certification body unless it obtains the required status. Registered/licensed collectors, transporters, storage operators, processors, and buyers remain provider of record for their regulated roles. The startup must reject sites requiring informal cash handling, undocumented pickups, mixed waste, stolen oil, contaminated barrels, unclear ownership, unsafe storage, or false documentation. It must keep BDO/KPO or equivalent records where applicable, maintain data and contract hygiene, and avoid making environmental certification claims it cannot substantiate.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, 3PL recovery, OTA recovery, RMA recovery, FieldAction, KSeF, cyber-insurance, AI-security, white-certificate cashout, payment residuals, Shopify app buyout, or physical bulky-lot liquidation. The control point is a dense signed UCO collection route plus downstream paid offtake, not evidence response, claim recovery, software MRR, municipal paperwork, or generic physical inventory.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Used cooking oil is a commodity with existing collectors and thin spreads. Restaurants may break exclusivity if someone pays more in cash. Incumbents can outbid once the route is visible. Collection reliability, contamination, water content, theft, barrel custody, route density, and rejected loads can destroy margin. Legal waste handling and documentation may be more complex than a part-time founder can manage. Downstream buyers may pay slowly or downgrade oil. The 6-month proof may become logistics-heavy field work rather than a scalable company. The route may be defensible only while contracts hold, and the startup may need operational density across many kitchens before owner earnings are meaningful.
