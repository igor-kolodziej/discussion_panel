Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
OrphanMold Last-Run Parts Bank

One-sentence thesis:
Option or buy specific orphaned production tooling from retiring/closing molders, rubber shops, tool shops, or small OEM suppliers only after securing a buyer deposit or PO for legacy non-safety replacement parts, then run a last batch or transfer the tool with documented title, sample, and first-article boundaries.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized OEMs, industrial maintenance firms, equipment distributors, MRO suppliers, agricultural-machinery dealers, appliance/service-parts distributors, HVAC/electrical/packaging-equipment suppliers, and niche B2B brands that still need non-safety legacy plastic/rubber/simple metal parts but whose old mold/tool owner is retiring, closing, bankrupt, or planning to scrap the tool.

First categories exclude medical, aviation, automotive safety, pressure-containing, food-contact, electrical safety-critical, child-safety, structural, and certified parts. Start with low-liability non-safety replacement parts: covers, guards, knobs, handles, clips, guides, brackets, grilles, spacers, feet, trays, strain reliefs, cable clips, louver parts, packaging machine covers, and simple equipment accessories.

Acute trigger:
A tool shop, molder, rubber shop, small manufacturer, or repair-parts supplier is closing or retiring and wants to scrap or sell old molds/tools, while at least one OEM/distributor/MRO buyer still needs a last run or wants to secure the tool before parts disappear. The buyer has a current PO, shortage, service obligation, dealer backorder, warranty/support commitment, or installed-base spare-part need.

Control point:
The startup does not buy random tooling. It controls only cases with:
- written option or purchase agreement over a specific mold/tool, fixture, pattern, die, or production file;
- seller proof of right to transfer, tool photos, part samples, process notes, and any drawing/BOM/material information available;
- buyer deposit, PO, or conditional purchase agreement for a last-run batch or tool transfer before final tool purchase;
- quoted production partner or current seller willing to run first articles/batch;
- first-article/sample acceptance path and documented boundaries excluding safety-critical or regulated uses.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three signed tool options from retiring/closing small molders, rubber shops, tool shops, or OEM suppliers.
2. Two buyer deposits or conditional POs for named legacy non-safety parts before final tool purchase.
3. One production partner quote for each accepted tool.
4. One first-article/sample run completed or scheduled with buyer acceptance criteria.
5. 40,000-120,000 PLN in deposits, POs, or signed conditional orders.
6. Tool title/option files with photos, samples, seller chain-of-title statement, and no-regulated/safety-critical boundary.
7. Gross margin target above 45% on at least one first batch after tool option/purchase, sampling, production, shipping, and founder time.

6-month POC:
Month 1: contact 80 retiring/closing/low-successor molders, rubber shops, tool shops, small manufacturers, repair-parts suppliers, and liquidation sources; contact 80 OEMs, industrial distributors, MRO firms, agricultural machinery dealers, appliance service-parts distributors, and HVAC/electrical/packaging-equipment suppliers; ask only for named orphaned tooling with current or recent buyer demand.

Month 2: sign three tool options, verify seller right to transfer, photograph and identify the parts/tools, obtain production quotes, and secure two buyer deposits or conditional POs before buying any tool.

Month 3: complete or schedule one first-article/sample run with explicit buyer acceptance criteria, document all no-go cases, and close or abandon each option based on paid demand, tool condition, title, and production feasibility.

Months 4-6: control 8-12 tool options, buy only 3-5 tools with buyer-backed demand, complete 3+ first batches or tool transfers, collect 150,000-350,000 PLN revenue, keep gross margin above 45%, and build a tool-demand map by part type, buyer segment, tool condition, seller type, production partner, sampling issue, and gross margin.

First acquisition mechanism:
Seller-side pitch:
"Before you scrap or store old molds and fixtures, send photos of tools that still made parts for a real customer in the last few years. I option only named tools, verify buyer demand, and either pay for the tool, pay you to run a last batch, or release the option. No random inventory buyout."

Buyer-side pitch:
"If a legacy non-safety part is disappearing because the old molder/tool shop is closing, send the part, supplier, and current need. I secure the tool only if you sign a deposit/conditional PO. You get a last-run batch or documented tool transfer without paying for a full reverse-engineering/new-tool project first."

Economics:
Option fees are 1,000-5,000 PLN per tool while buyer demand is verified. Tool purchase is 5,000-40,000 PLN only after buyer deposit, PO, or distributor commitment. First batches are 100-2,000 units depending on part, quoted by the current shop or a partner molder/rubber shop.

Revenue is 10,000-60,000 PLN for a low-volume last-run batch, 20,000-100,000 PLN for a tool transfer plus first batch to an OEM/distributor, and 30-60% gross margin when the tool is cheap because the seller otherwise planned to scrap/store it and the buyer pays for continuity and avoided reverse-engineering/new-tool cost.

Copy risk:
Tool shops, molders, OEMs, industrial distributors, and spare-parts brokers can copy the concept. The defense is transaction-specific: once the founder controls the exact tool option/title and buyer PO/deposit, competitors cannot produce the same part cheaply without the mold or a costly reverse-engineering/new-tool process. The compounding asset is a network of retiring shops and OEM/distributor buyers, plus tool-level demand memory.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder has the written tool option or title, seller chain-of-title file, samples/photos/process notes, buyer deposit or conditional PO, and production quote, another party cannot access that exact mold/tool or buyer-backed last-run transaction unless the seller or buyer breaks the signed option/order. Incumbents can make a new tool or reverse-engineer, but that is slower and more expensive for the named part.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup must own or option the tool before taking buyer money and must either run the last batch through the current shop/production partner or transfer the tool under a documented sale. It does not sell reports, run a tooling directory, introduce buyers to sellers, or build a marketplace. The controlled unit is tool title/option plus buyer payment plus batch/transfer.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup excludes medical, aviation, automotive safety, pressure-containing, food-contact, electrical safety-critical, child-safety, structural, certified, regulated, IP-disputed, or high-liability parts. The buyer remains responsible for fit-for-purpose use, installation, certification, end-customer claims, and any regulated use. The seller must state transfer rights and disclose known restrictions. The startup does not fabricate origin, ownership, certification, material, or performance claims. When title, IP, safety, or buyer authority is unclear, the option is killed or escalated to counsel/technical review.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not Legacy Line-Control First-Call Spares Retainer or Factory Control-Spares Donor Bank. Those were titled spare-part inventory or emergency module banks and failed because buyers can search incumbents and wrong inventory strands capital. This idea controls production tooling and only buys after buyer deposit/PO, reducing random inventory risk.

It is not HeritageDoor Suites because there is no architectural salvage, premium renovation buyer, or matched historical interior asset. It is not a marketplace, dashboard, broker, recovery mandate, evidence desk, Amazon/RED, DORA, CBAM, Data Act, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, PromoLeak, GreenTender, or BioSignal.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Clean transferable tooling with real buyer demand may be rare.
- Tool ownership and IP rights can be messy, especially when the tool was paid for by an OEM or prior customer.
- Tool condition may be unknown until a test run, and repair cost can destroy margin.
- Buyer deposits may be hard to secure before a first article exists.
- Production partners may not want to run old tools or may require setup/minimums that exceed the budget.
- Part quality, material substitution, shrinkage, tolerances, color, finish, and fit can create rework.
- The business can become bespoke manufacturing operations rather than a repeatable control play.
- Incumbent molders, OEMs, distributors, and repair-parts firms can copy the search behavior if they see margin.
