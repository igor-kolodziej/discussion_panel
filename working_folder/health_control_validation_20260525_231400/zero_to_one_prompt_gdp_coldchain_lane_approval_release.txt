Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: GDP Cold-Chain Lane Approval Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Build a prepaid live-case desk for small Polish/CEE cold-chain logistics providers whose pharma, CRO, biotech, wholesaler, or specialty-care customer blocks a named route, lane, tender, renewal, subcontractor approval, or shipment program until the provider submits a bounded GDP temperature-control lane evidence file built from actual route, logger, packaging, vehicle, SOP, and escalation artifacts.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized Polish/CEE GDP couriers, specialty cold-chain logistics providers, pharma 3PL subcontractors, depot operators, temperature-controlled packout providers, and logistics arms of distributors with a named pharma, CRO, biotech, wholesaler, manufacturer, or specialty-care customer asking for route/lane temperature-control and GDP evidence before approving a customer contract, renewal, tender, subcontractor role, or shipment program.

Acute trigger:
A customer QA, procurement, or supply-chain owner blocks lane approval because the provider has not produced enough evidence around route risk, temperature band, logger calibration, vehicle or fridge qualification, thermal packaging, SOPs, staff training, deviation/excursion handling, subcontractors, handoff points, seasonal assumptions, or dry-run/test-run data. The buyer is paying because a real customer contract, tender, renewal, subcontractor approval, or shipment program is stuck now.

Control point:
Prepaid live lane approval case with the exact customer QA/procurement request and deadline, named route/lane or shipment program, vehicle/fridge/packaging/logger IDs, calibration certificates where available, route risk notes, handoff points, transport mode, lane timing, SOPs, training records, deviation/excursion process, subcontractor list, escalation contacts, lane dry-run or logger records when needed, GDP/cold-chain reviewer issue spotting for flagged sections, customer-ready lane file, and clean no-go/escalation notes. The startup controls the live lane evidence file and status workflow, not the shipment, customer QA decision, or regulated logistics role.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three prepaid live lane approval cases at 12,000-35,000 PLN each; at least two include real logger or dry-run/test-run evidence rather than only SOP paperwork; one named GDP/cold-chain QA reviewer on paid per-case terms; one customer QA/procurement progress outcome such as lane accepted, conditional approval, tender/customer next step, shipment program unblocked, or written clean no-go; at least 50,000 PLN collected; and a repeatable case taxonomy by product temperature band, route type, packaging/loggers, customer request wording, evidence gaps, reviewer flags, and customer acceptance/no-go reason.

6-month POC:
Handle 12-25 prepaid lane approval cases; collect 180,000-600,000 PLN in triage, lane-file, urgent, and multi-route fees; document at least eight customer-progress outcomes or clean no-go decisions; secure two repeat referral channels from GDP consultants, cold-chain packaging vendors, logger/calibration providers, pharma QA freelancers, or specialty logistics operators; keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer, logger rental, admin, and template costs; keep standard lane-file turnaround under seven business days when inputs are complete; and reject cases that require the startup to transport medicines, certify GDP compliance, validate packaging, approve QA release, handle patient data, or invent missing evidence.

First acquisition mechanism:
Build a target list of 80 Polish/CEE specialty cold-chain couriers, pharma 3PL subcontractors, depot operators, cold-chain packaging resellers, temperature-monitoring vendors, GDP consultants, and logistics providers that advertise pharma, biotech, clinical-trial, specialty-care, or temperature-controlled shipping. Partner pitch: "When a small provider has a real customer lane approval blocked by GDP temperature-control evidence, route the live case to me. I assemble the lane file, logger/dry-run evidence map, reviewer flags, and customer-ready proof while you keep the regulated logistics or QA work." Direct pitch: "If a pharma/CRO/customer QA team will not approve your route until you prove temperature-control and GDP evidence, send the exact request, lane, vehicle/packaging/logger IDs, certificates, SOPs, and route notes. I return a submit/cannot-submit/no-go lane approval file."

Economics:
Charge 6,000-10,000 PLN for paid lane triage and submit/cannot-submit/no-go decision, 12,000-22,000 PLN for a standard lane approval file, and 25,000-35,000 PLN for urgent multi-route or tender-critical packs with logger dry runs and reviewer issue spotting. The fee is credible only when a material customer contract, tender, renewal, subcontractor approval, or shipment program is blocked. Expected gross margin is 60-70% after GDP reviewer, logger rental, admin, and template costs if cases are tightly filtered.

Copy risk:
GDP consultants, large 3PL QA teams, packaging vendors, temperature-monitoring vendors, validation firms, pharma logistics specialists, and customer QA teams can copy pieces. The defense is not a GDP checklist. It is live lane-case control, prepaid customer deadlines, actual logger/test-run files, CEE provider focus, reviewer-bounded no-go discipline, and accumulated customer-request/outcome memory by route type, temperature band, packaging/loggers, and evidence gap.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the provider signs the prepaid lane mandate, gives the exact customer QA request, lane details, logger/vehicle/packaging evidence, and deadline, the startup controls the live evidence room, dry-run/logging evidence map, reviewer flags, customer-ready packet, and outcome trail for that specific lane approval. A GDP consultant or large 3PL can build a similar workflow later, but cannot instantly own the already-authorized lane file and customer deadline unless the provider switches mid-case.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The unit is a named customer lane approval case with prepayment, route evidence, logger/test-run artifacts, reviewer flags, and a customer QA outcome. The startup does not introduce logistics providers to pharma customers, run shipments, sell monitoring hardware, build a dashboard, or write generic GDP reports. It assembles and controls the live lane evidence file for a current approval blocker.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not sell, prescribe, dispense, store, transport, import, export, or advertise medicines. It does not hold wholesale distribution authorization, certify GDP compliance, validate packaging, approve QA release, handle patient data, or interpret legal obligations. Licensed wholesalers, distributors, manufacturers, logistics providers, depots, QA owners, GDP advisers, and customers remain responsible for regulated activities, contracts, shipments, product quality, release decisions, and final approvals. Cases involving patient-specific data, clinical advice, blood/plasma, controlled substances, falsified evidence, unclear authorization, or medicine handling by the startup are rejected.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PharmaFridge Cold-Chain Compliance Book Buyout, which tested recurring pharmacy fridge/logger calibration renewal books and failed in prior testing. This candidate is customer-contract lane approval for logistics providers, with route-specific logger evidence and customer QA progress as proof. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, Part-IS Aviation, KSC/NIS2, CPSC eFile, EUDR, or Amazon/marketplace evidence. The buyer, trigger, controlled artifact, and payment event are materially different: a named pharma cold-chain transport lane or shipment program blocked by customer QA approval.

Strongest anticipated objections:
GDP consultants and established pharma 3PL QA teams may already own buyer trust. A lane approval can require real GDP qualification, packaging validation, customer audit, route testing, or QA signoff outside the founder's scope. Small logistics providers may not have enough existing evidence to produce a credible file. Customer QA teams may ignore a third-party packet or ask for direct audits. Sensitive product, route, security, and customer information creates trust friction. Reviewer and logger costs can compress margin. Case flow may be lumpy because pharma logistics is relationship-driven. The work can drift into regulated logistics, GDP certification, packaging validation, shipment operations, or legal/quality advice unless no-go boundaries are strict.
