Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Non-Clinical Lab Interface Continuity Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Acquire or option retiring micro-vendor books that maintain live non-clinical lab interfaces for food, water, environmental, construction-materials, industrial QA, materials, and calibration labs, then take over only the existing production connectors with customer-approved admin transfer and prepaid continuity contracts before close.

Exact buyer:
Non-clinical ISO/IEC 17025-type testing and calibration labs, including food safety labs, water labs, environmental labs, construction-materials labs, industrial QA labs, materials testing labs, and calibration labs that rely on fragile custom connectors maintained by a retiring freelancer, local MSP, tiny LIMS integrator, or former employee.

Best first labs have live production connectors for instrument exports, LIMS/LIS-like imports, certificate-of-analysis delivery, customer portal uploads, billing exports, sample status feeds, regulator/client report uploads, or ERP/accounting handoffs. They do not handle human clinical diagnostics, human genetic data, patient results, veterinary diagnostics, forensics, medical-device software, or clinical interpretation.

Acute trigger:
The old micro-vendor or freelancer wants to retire, stop answering support, sell the book, or hand off maintenance. The lab is not buying a new LIMS project; it needs continuity for already-running connectors that keep samples, results, certificates, customer portals, invoices, and reporting flows moving.

Control point:
Signed option or asset-purchase agreement over a real seller book with:
- source code, scripts, mappings, templates, runbooks, deployment notes, and version history;
- support inbox, ticket history, customer contacts, renewal dates, and recurring invoices;
- customer-approved admin transfer for each accepted lab;
- credential handoff followed by credential rotation, least-privilege access, backups, and access logs;
- prepaid continuity contracts from labs before close;
- one scoped production ticket resolved under the new owner before final payment;
- strict exclusion of clinical, diagnostic, human health, genetic, veterinary diagnostic, forensic, medical-device, accreditation-decision, and result-interpretation scope.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. One signed option over a named seller book with 5-15 non-clinical lab customers.
2. Clean IP, assignment, DPA/processor, and customer contract review for the specific book.
3. Five lab customers sign customer-approved admin transfer or new continuity contracts.
4. At least 25,000 PLN prepaid transition/support cash clears before final acquisition close.
5. Credentials are rotated, access logs established, backups verified, and a support escalation path documented.
6. One live production ticket is resolved under the new operator without touching result interpretation, certificate signoff, method validation, or accreditation decisions.
7. Support history shows mostly reusable maintenance, monitoring, mapping, routing, formatting, upload, and compatibility work rather than bespoke open-ended projects.

6-month POC:
Month 1: identify 40 possible sellers: retiring lab IT freelancers, tiny LIMS/interface integrators, local MSPs with lab customers, former employees maintaining scripts, and small vendors supporting non-clinical labs. Reject any book involving clinical diagnostics, patient data, genetic data, veterinary diagnosis, forensics, or medical-device software.

Month 2: sign one option over a seller book. Run diligence on code ownership, customer contracts, DPAs, credentials, ticket history, support hours, connector scope, backup state, deployment notes, and whether mappings only transport/format/rout data rather than changing scientific meaning.

Month 3: seller introduces customers. Secure five customer-approved admin transfers or new continuity contracts and 25,000+ PLN prepaid support before close. Rotate credentials, establish access logs/backups, and resolve one scoped ticket.

Months 4-6: complete the first acquisition only if the prepayment and support-history tests pass. Stabilize 5-15 labs, collect 60,000-180,000 PLN in fees, keep contribution margin above 55% after contractor support/security/legal amortization/seller earnout, and build a repeatable seller diligence checklist. Begin outreach to a second seller only after average support stays under 6 hours/lab/month.

First acquisition mechanism:
Seller pitch:
"You keep your reputation and earn an exit instead of abandoning lab customers. I only close if your customers approve the admin transfer, prepay continuity, credentials are rotated, and one live ticket is resolved. You get an upfront payment plus earnout tied to retained prepaid customers."

Lab pitch:
"Your existing connector keeps running. The seller introduces me, I take over only the current production interface, rotate credentials, document the runbook, add backups/logging, and keep support bounded. I do not replace your LIMS, advise on accreditation, validate methods, sign certificates, or change result meaning."

Economics:
Charge labs 1,500-5,000 PLN/month for continuity support depending on connector count and response SLA, plus 5,000-20,000 PLN for transition, documentation, credential rotation, monitoring, or scoped patches. Pay the seller mainly through earnout from retained customers, with upfront cash only after at least 25,000 PLN customer prepayment clears. Use contractors for code review, security hardening, and emergency patches. The acquisition must fit under 100,000 PLN by making customer prepayment and seller earnout the financing mechanism.

Copy risk:
LIMS vendors, instrument vendors, local IT MSPs, lab automation integrators, and software freelancers can copy pieces. The defense is seller-mediated custody of the existing production book: code, mappings, deployment notes, credentials, ticket history, customer trust, recurring invoices, and customer-approved transfer. Competitors can sell a replacement project, but they do not already own the inherited connector files and seller handoff.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder signs the seller option, receives the asset inventory, gets customer-approved transfer, rotates credentials, collects prepaid continuity contracts, and resolves one ticket, the founder controls the specific production connector book and payment stream. Another provider must displace the inherited code, runbooks, access, support inbox, seller introduction, customer approvals, and prepaid continuity workflow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell net-new lab integrations. It acquires or options already-running non-clinical lab connector books before they are abandoned. The controlled asset is the seller-mediated production book: code, mappings, runbooks, credentials, support history, customer-approved admin transfer, and prepaid continuity contracts. It is not a LIMS, dashboard, marketplace, database, generic MSP, implementation agency, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not handle human clinical diagnostics, human health data, genetic data, veterinary diagnostic workflows, forensics, medical-device software, diagnostic interpretation, method validation, accreditation advice, certificate signoff, scientific result approval, or regulated lab decisions. It handles only transport, routing, mapping, formatting, upload, monitoring, backups, compatibility, and bounded maintenance for non-clinical lab interfaces. Lab management, authorized signatories, quality managers, accreditation bodies, LIMS vendors, instrument vendors, and customers remain responsible for method validity, result content, certificates, accreditation, scientific interpretation, and regulatory submissions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, BioSignal, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, or any health/Rx/clinical route. It is also not the earlier clinical Lab Interface Continuity Buyout because this version excludes patient, genetic, diagnostic, veterinary diagnostic, forensic, and medical-device workflows. The control point is a non-clinical production connector book with customer-approved admin transfer and prepaid continuity.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- The exact seller profile may be rare: a tiny vendor with transferable IP, clean contracts, supportable code, and 5-15 paying non-clinical lab customers.
- Existing connectors may be bespoke chaos rather than reusable maintenance.
- Labs may not prepay unless the seller-mediated continuity risk is immediate and credible.
- Part-time production support is still risky if sample flows, certificates, uploads, invoices, or customer reports break.
- LIMS vendors, instrument vendors, MSPs, or integrators can pitch replacement projects.
- Credential, DPA, processor, security, backup, and incident-response obligations are serious even without health data.
- Unit economics collapse if support exceeds 6 hours per lab per month or if urgent fixes require senior developers frequently.
- Customer contract assignment and admin transfer may be slower than source-code transfer.
- Some connectors may accidentally alter scientific meaning through unit conversion, reference limits, flags, calculations, or certificate formatting; those must be rejected or escalated.
