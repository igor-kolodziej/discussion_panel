# Simulated Round 76: SteriLink Autoclave Uptime + Validation Book Buyout

Date: 2026-05-26

## Why This Pivot

The focused health/genetics/biosignal/Rx-adjacent run keeps failing when the control point is a document, partner relationship, patient book, or generic service.

This pivot keeps the health-adjacent/50-60 clinic market but shifts to hard B2B uptime control:

- titled loaner equipment;
- serial/certificate calendars;
- acquired technician/customer book;
- prepaid clinic uptime retainers;
- one completed paid loaner/service/validation event inside 60 days.

## Candidate

`SteriLink Autoclave Uptime + Validation Book Buyout`

Acquire or option a small autoclave/sterilizer service technician's clinic book serving dental, podology, aesthetic-medicine, tattoo/piercing, dermatology, and small private procedure clinics in Warsaw/Mazowieckie. Control the phone/domain/mailbox, device serial records, validation/calibration/inspection certificate archive, due-date calendar, clinic contacts, service history, consumable/seal/filter stock, and a small titled loaner-autoclave fleet.

The startup does not perform medical procedures, sterilize instruments for clinics, certify devices itself, or issue legal/compliance opinions. Qualified service, validation, calibration, and inspection providers remain provider of record. The startup owns continuity: calendar, customer book, loaner units, parts stock, logistics, and payment flow.

## Buyer

- Dental clinics, orthodontic offices, implant/prosthetic practices.
- Podology, dermatology, aesthetic medicine, permanent makeup, tattoo/piercing, and small procedure rooms.
- Clinic managers and owners whose revenue depends on sterile instrument availability.

## Acute Trigger

A clinic's autoclave fails, is waiting for parts/service, lacks a valid validation/check record, has an expired certificate/calendar item, or cannot run enough sterile loads for scheduled procedures. Downtime can cancel dental, podology, aesthetic, dermatology, tattoo, and procedure visits.

The buyer pays for continuity: a same/next-day compliant loaner pathway, service coordination, certificate calendar custody, consumable readiness, and no missed due dates.

## Control Point

The acquired asset is a live service/customer book plus titled equipment:

- service phone/domain/mailbox and WhatsApp history;
- clinic device serials, brands/models, locations, service notes, failure history;
- due-date calendar for validation, inspections, calibration, maintenance, and consumables;
- certificate/archive records;
- customer-approved transfer or new direct continuity contracts;
- titled loaner autoclaves with service/validation records;
- baskets, trays, seals, filters, printer rolls, indicators, and basic consumable stock;
- partner service/validation/calibration/inspection providers;
- prepaid monthly/annual uptime retainers and paid loaner deployments.

This is not a marketplace or BHP/legal newsletter. The founder controls a clinic uptime account book and physical loaner capacity.

## 2-Month Proof

Required proof:

1. Signed option/LOI over one technician or micro-service book with at least 20 active clinic devices in the last 12 months.
2. Customer-approved transfer or new contract for at least 5 clinics.
3. Three titled loaner autoclaves under 60,000 PLN total, with condition/service records and legal role reviewed.
4. Due-date/calendar and certificate archive reconstructed for at least 20 clinic devices.
5. At least 30,000 PLN collected or prepaid across retainers, loaner deployments, consumable packs, and due-date renewals.
6. One completed paid loaner deployment or emergency continuity event.
7. One completed paid validation/calibration/service routing batch through qualified providers.
8. 50%+ gross margin after service provider, transport, legal, insurance, parts, storage, and seller earnout.
9. Written boundary: startup is not the certifying body, medical provider, sterilization processor, or legal advisor.

## First Acquisition Mechanism

- Identify retiring/overloaded autoclave technicians, dental-equipment service micro-firms, small hygiene/sterilization validation coordinators, and dental equipment resellers with neglected service calendars.
- Offer an earnout: seller receives 15-25% of collected gross profit from transferred clinics for 12 months.
- Close only if at least 5 clinics approve direct billing/payment transfer before purchase.
- Buy loaner units only after clinic retainer demand or an emergency deployment is signed.

## Economics

Revenue:

- 500-1,500 PLN/month per clinic uptime retainer, depending on device count and loaner access.
- 1,500-4,500 PLN emergency loaner deployment / continuity event.
- 400-1,500 PLN service/validation/calendar renewal coordination fee where appropriate.
- 200-1,000 PLN consumable/seal/filter/indicator packs.
- 6,000-18,000 PLN annual multi-chair clinic package.

Gross margin:

- 50-65% possible if the founder owns calendar/payment/logistics and routes technical/certification work externally.
- One emergency loaner plus 5 prepaid retainers can produce meaningful cash inside 60 days.

## Why This Fits The User's Focus

This is health-adjacent and 50-60/soon-elderly-adjacent without patient trust transfer. Dental, podology, dermatology, aesthetic, and minor-procedure clinics serve older and middle-aged patients, but the buyer is the clinic, not the patient.

It avoids direct prescription, GLP-1, addictive-drug, treatment, diagnosis, or patient-data handling.

## Copy Risk

Incumbents exist: dental-equipment distributors, autoclave service providers, calibration/validation firms, and larger clinic-equipment companies.

The defensible wedge is the acquired micro-book plus same/next-day loaner fleet and due-date calendar custody. Distributors can sell new units, but they may not own the exact local service history, clinic due dates, technician handoff, and small-clinic trust.

Copy risk is high if there is no acquired book or loaner fleet. Reject any version that starts as cold-sold service coordination from zero.
