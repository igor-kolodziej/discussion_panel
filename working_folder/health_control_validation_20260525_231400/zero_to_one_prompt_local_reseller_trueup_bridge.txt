Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Local Reseller-of-Record True-Up Bridge

One-sentence thesis:
Become the temporary named reseller/payee for already-accepted B2B SaaS, API, data-feed, or developer-tool true-ups and renewals that are blocked only because the enterprise customer cannot pay the original vendor directly through its vendor-master, PO, VAT, currency, or local-invoice process.

Exact buyer:
Small or foreign B2B SaaS/API/data/developer-tool vendors selling to EU or CEE enterprise customers where the business owner at the customer has already accepted a renewal, usage overage, data-feed fee, or true-up, but procurement/AP cannot pay the vendor directly on time. The best first buyers are founder-led vendors, channel-light vendors, and specialist data/API suppliers with 150,000-1,000,000 PLN equivalent payments stuck behind local supplier onboarding or PO routing.

Acute trigger:
A current accepted payment is at risk of slipping or being lost because the customer requires a local/EU reseller invoice, an approved vendor record, a different currency/VAT route, a named PO to an already onboarded supplier, or a back-to-back procurement route before quarter-end, year-end, renewal cutoff, or service-continuity deadline. The startup rejects cases where the underlying commercial amount, usage, renewal, or service acceptance is still disputed.

Control point:
The startup controls the transaction only after two documents exist for a named payment: a vendor-signed back-to-back reseller/order authorization and a customer-issued PO or written payment-direction confirmation naming the startup as reseller/payee for that specific accepted true-up, renewal, overage, or data/API invoice. The startup then invoices the customer and pays the vendor under back-to-back terms after customer cash clears or under a tightly bounded no-advance schedule.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, obtain one vendor authorization, one customer PO or payment-direction confirmation naming the startup, one startup invoice to the customer, one matching vendor invoice to the startup, first customer payment received or formally scheduled under no-advance terms, first vendor remittance, and retained fee/spread. A valid POC case must have at least 150,000 PLN routed value or at least 15,000 PLN retained gross margin/fee, and must not involve lending, factoring, regulated payment services, medical data, consumer claims, sanctions exposure, legal representation, or unresolved tax authority.

6-month POC:
Close 8-15 routed transactions from 4-8 vendors, route 2-6 million PLN of accepted payments, retain 150,000-450,000 PLN gross margin/fees, show that at least three source channels repeatedly produce cases, and maintain zero customer-payment defaults by using customer-cash-first or no-advance back-to-back terms. Kill the POC if fewer than two payments clear in the first 90 days, if most opportunities lack written customer acceptance, if customers insist on established VARs, or if legal/accounting review says the model creates payment-services, factoring, product-liability, or tax exposure the founder cannot bound.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with RevOps, fractional CFO, SaaS founder, data-vendor, cloud-partner, and enterprise-procurement communities. The outreach is not "we help with collections"; it asks for one very specific pain: accepted enterprise true-ups or renewals where the customer wants to pay but cannot route payment to the vendor before a deadline because of supplier onboarding, local invoice, VAT/currency, or PO constraints. Intake requires the vendor to show customer acceptance and procurement blockage before any work starts.

Economics:
Charge a fixed setup/admin fee of 5,000-20,000 PLN plus a 2-8% transaction spread or success fee on routed cash, depending on urgency and liability. Gross margin can be high because there is no inventory, no software build, and no delivery team; main costs are contract templates, accounting/VAT review, KYC/sanctions screening, invoicing, and part-time operations support. Cash conversion is protected by customer-cash-first or no-advance back-to-back terms; the startup avoids paying the vendor before customer funds clear unless a specific case has separately approved credit protection.

Copy risk:
Large VARs, cloud distributors, SoftwareOne/Crayon/SHI/TD SYNNEX-style resellers, procurement BPOs, RevOps consultants, fractional CFOs, and marketplace private-offer teams can copy the mechanism. The risk is especially high if customers already have a preferred reseller catalog or if the vendor can quickly move the transaction through an existing channel partner.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup does not compete on generic reseller status. It only accepts named transactions where the vendor has already authorized the startup for that order and the customer has already issued, or is ready to issue, a PO/payment direction to the startup. Once the startup is named on the PO and invoice path, a later reseller must restart procurement, re-paper the vendor authorization, and persuade both sides to change a live payment route. The defensible control is the specific current payment stream, not a general channel relationship.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup is the named reseller/payee for a specific accepted payment and earns from routed cash. It does not sell a report, dashboard, database, advisory memo, introduction, or software workflow. It also does not broker unsigned opportunities: without a vendor authorization and customer PO/payment direction, there is no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No lending, factoring, debt collection, escrow, regulated payment services, legal representation, tax advice, medical/health data, consumer claims, sanctions-risk goods, high-risk regulated products, or product categories where reseller-of-record duties cannot be bounded. Licensed lawyers, accountants, tax advisors, and compliance professionals remain responsible for their own regulated work. The startup uses standard resale/order paperwork, customer-cash-first terms, KYC/sanctions checks, and explicit product/service responsibility limits. If a case requires the startup to certify the product, become provider of record, handle patient/consumer regulated data, or advance material cash before customer payment, it is rejected.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak because it is not recovering retailer promo leakage or deductions; it routes already-accepted B2B software/data/API payments through a customer-approved reseller PO. It is distinct from DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack and REDBlocked because it is not an evidence-response product. It is distinct from Round 280's acknowledged true-up payment release because the controlled object is a live customer-issued PO/payment stream to the startup as reseller/payee, not an AP release file for the original vendor's invoice.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Enterprise customers may insist on an established VAR or already approved catalog supplier rather than a new Warsaw solo founder. Vendors may not trust an unknown reseller with customer relationships or payment flow. Existing cloud distributors and procurement resellers may already solve the clean cases. Legal, VAT, reseller liability, data-processing, sanctions, and product responsibility boundaries may consume more effort than expected. The startup may be forced to take credit risk if vendors demand payment before customer funds clear. Many "blocked" payments may actually hide unresolved commercial disputes, weak customer acceptance, or customer budget problems. Success fees may compress once the workaround is understood. Repeatability may be limited if each customer procurement route is bespoke.
