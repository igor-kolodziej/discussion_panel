Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time. Cap below 85 without a hard control point securable in 6 months. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, margins, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible.

Idea name: OneStack Critical Connector Lockbox Buyout.

Exact buyer: Polish/CEE e-commerce merchants that depend on one neglected local connector between one storefront family and one ERP/accounting family, starting with PrestaShop or WooCommerce merchants using one Subiekt GT/nexo, Comarch ERP Optima, or enova365 order, invoice, stock, correction, or COD reconciliation connector. The economic buyer is the merchant owner, operations lead, or accountant who already treats the connector as business-critical.

Acute trigger: A small connector author or micro-agency is tired of maintaining a paid support tail while customers face a near-term PHP, storefront, ERP, KSeF invoice, warehouse/status, or COD reconciliation update that could break invoicing, stock sync, order export, correction invoices, or settlement reconciliation. Customers do not want a generic software project; they want the exact dependency they already use to keep working.

Control point: The startup does not buy a random plugin first. It signs a conditional option over one exact connector asset and closes only after customers prepay continuity. The option covers source code, update/build pipeline, license server or update channel if any, support inbox/history, customer list, issue history, install/version map, domain/docs, seller endorsement, and the right to collect renewal/support payments. Customers must approve payment transfer to the founder/new entity before close.

Exactly what is signed, titled, assigned, or prepaid within 60 days:

- signed seller option with a small option fee and earnout, not a large upfront acquisition;
- anonymized support history and install/version map reviewed before the option becomes meaningful;
- proof that at least 70% of recent tickets are reusable connector-core maintenance, not bespoke store cleanup;
- 15-25 named merchants sign 12-month continuity contracts at 2,500-4,000 PLN each;
- 45,000-70,000 PLN total customer prepayment or escrowed payment clears before acquisition close;
- customers explicitly approve the new payee/support owner and the limited support scope;
- the seller transfers source/update/support assets only after the prepayment threshold is hit;
- one small patch, compatibility update, or support ticket is completed under the founder's control before month 2 ends.

6-month proof:

- one closed connector succession with code/update/support assets transferred;
- 45,000-70,000 PLN customer prepayment before close and 80,000-150,000 PLN total booked annual continuity revenue by month 6;
- at least 15 paying merchants remain active after handoff;
- first patch or compatibility update shipped to at least 8 merchants;
- support load stays under 0.8 founder/contractor hours per merchant per month after the first transition month;
- at least 70% gross margin after contractor support, hosting, legal, option fee amortization, and seller earnout;
- no more than 20% of tickets are bespoke store/theme/server/accounting cleanup unless separately paid from an escalation menu;
- a second seller pipeline with at least two qualified one-stack connector authors, but no second acquisition unless the first support economics are proven.

First acquisition mechanism: Find tired Polish micro-agencies, freelancers, and plugin authors with 30-100 named active merchants in one narrow stack. Do not target payment gateways, shipping labels, parcel lockers, or obvious commodity plugins. Preferred first assets are boring operational connectors: PrestaShop/WooCommerce to Subiekt, Comarch ERP Optima, or enova365 for order export, warehouse stock, invoice/correction invoice, B2B account export, COD reconciliation, or fulfillment status sync. The seller gets a small option fee, public/emailed endorsement script, and earnout from collected customer renewals, so the seller helps convert active customers before transfer.

Economics: Charge 2,500-4,000 PLN per merchant per year for continuity maintenance, with paid escalation for emergency fixes, PHP migrations, ERP mapping cleanup, custom store conflicts, accounting reconciliation, KSeF-related invoice changes, or server/store debugging. Initial proof requires 45,000-70,000 PLN prepaid before close, so the founder does not spend the 100,000 PLN cap acquiring bad code. The first acquisition should pay back from prepaid customer cash. Long-term owner earnings are credible only if one core patch/update benefits many merchants and support creep is priced separately.

Copy risk: BaseLinker, official integrations, ERP integrators, freelancers, and agencies are substitutes. The wedge is not that nobody can build connectors. The wedge is that the founder controls one exact installed dependency with source code, update channel, issue history, seller endorsement, install/version map, and prepaid customer continuity contracts before competitors know the asset is for sale. Incumbents can copy the category but cannot instantly inherit the specific customer-approved payment stream and support history.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, marketplace, or generic broker: The startup acquires a live software asset and its customer-approved payment stream only after customer cash proves demand. It does not sell consulting, discovery, a dashboard, connector marketplace, or generic e-commerce integration services. The founder must reject any asset where customers mainly need bespoke store repair, accounting cleanup, or custom integration work.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- no access to customer production credentials until customer consent and support scope are signed;
- no payment processing or PSP role;
- no legal/tax/accounting advice and no KSeF compliance guarantee;
- customer data, support inboxes, credentials, and logs transfer only under DPA/security terms;
- inherited code/IP must have written ownership or assignment from the seller and contractors;
- support scope excludes custom store/theme/server work unless separately quoted;
- no acquisition closes if IP, license rights, customer consent, data transfer, or reusable support history is unclear.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

- Not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack: this is not evidence response for regulated-finance renewals; it is a customer-prepaid acquisition of one operating connector asset and payment stream.
- Not REDBlocked: no Amazon/Allegro listing compliance, no marketplace evidence desk, no account-health agency.
- Not BioSignal Claim-Risk: no healthtech investor diligence or biosignal claims.
- Not PromoLeak: no FMCG deduction recovery or retail chargeback audit.
- Not KSeF/e-Delivery Accounting Lockbox from prior failures: this avoids accounting-service labor and does not sell broad KSeF cleanup; KSeF is only one possible update trigger for an existing connector dependency.
