Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
GLP-1 Telehealth Revenue-Quality Diligence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
Sell fixed-scope revenue-quality and regulatory-exposure diligence packs to investors, PE/search-fund buyers, strategic acquirers, lenders, and corporate development teams evaluating GLP-1 telehealth, obesity-clinic, pharmacy-dependent subscription, or weight-loss platform businesses before an investment, LOI, acquisition, financing, or rollout decision.

Exact buyer:
Healthcare, consumer-health, software-enabled-services, and lower-middle-market investors; PE/search-fund buyers; strategic acquirers; venture lenders; corporate development teams; and rollup operators with a live deal or financing decision involving a GLP-1 telehealth company, obesity-care clinic group, DTC weight-loss subscription brand, pharmacy/provider-network platform, or software-enabled clinic whose valuation depends on GLP-1 revenue quality, retention, CAC payback, supply continuity, pharmacy/provider contracts, refund/chargeback behavior, or conversion from compounded to branded medication pathways.

Acute trigger:
The buyer is about to sign an LOI, wire capital, approve an acquisition, underwrite debt, expand a strategic partnership, or approve a rollout based on claims that GLP-1 revenue is durable, compliant, profitable, and operationally scalable. The target may claim strong subscription retention, low CAC payback, high gross margin, stable pharmacy fulfillment, legal compounded-drug supply, branded-drug conversion, safe provider-of-record operations, low refund/chargeback risk, strong adherence, or defensible patient demand. FDA GLP-1 compounding and shortage-related policy changes make old revenue and margin assumptions suspect, so the buyer needs a fast decision memo before capital moves.

Control point:
Signed prepaid diligence mandate for one live capital or procurement decision, plus buyer-authorized access to the specific non-patient or de-identified evidence needed for that decision: target deck, dataroom materials, aggregate revenue cohorts, acquisition-channel cohorts, refund/chargeback exports, gross-margin bridge, pharmacy and provider contracts or summaries, pharmacy-of-record and prescriber-network structure, customer support/refund policies, ad funnels and claims, subscription terms, fulfillment SLAs, branded-vs-compounded product mix, legal/compliance memos already prepared by counsel, pharmacy/vendor dependency map, quality/safety escalation process, and target-approved management Q&A. The startup controls the diligence file, evidence-request list, red-flag matrix, reviewer notes, and buyer-ready decision memo for that active deal.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three prepaid live-decision diligence packs at 15,000-45,000 PLN each. Each case must have a named investor/acquirer/lender/procurement buyer, a live LOI/investment/financing/acquisition/rollout decision, and written permission to review non-patient or de-identified business evidence. At least one pack must be used in an investment committee memo, LOI term change, earnout or holdback request, legal diligence scope, lender covenant/advance decision, valuation adjustment, procurement scorecard, evidence request, pause, or kill decision. Every pack includes a revenue-quality bridge, CAC/payback review, refund/chargeback screen, pharmacy/provider continuity map, compounded-to-branded exposure analysis, legal/clinical no-go referral list, and strongest unanswered evidence requests.

6-month POC:
Complete 8-14 paid diligence packs, collect 180,000-500,000 PLN in fees, maintain gross margin above 60% after healthcare regulatory, pharmacy-operations, CAC/subscription, and legal-boundary reviewers, and prove decision impact in at least five cases. Build a private benchmark library of GLP-1 telehealth failure modes: revenue dependent on now-risky compounding assumptions, hidden refund/chargeback rates, weak recurring retention after first fill, CAC payback distorted by affiliate incentives, pharmacy/provider contract fragility, branded-drug conversion margin collapse, patient-support cost undercounting, subscription cancellation friction, adverse event escalation weakness, and legal/compliance issues that require counsel.

First acquisition mechanism:
Targeted written outreach to 80 investors, PE/search-fund buyers, healthcare acquirers, venture lenders, corporate development teams, and transaction advisers already looking at telehealth, obesity care, DTC health, clinic rollups, pharmacy-enabled services, or GLP-1 growth assets. Send a redacted sample teardown using public claims from visible GLP-1 telehealth businesses and a concrete offer: "If a GLP-1 target is asking you to underwrite subscription revenue, pharmacy supply, compounding continuity, branded conversion, or CAC payback, I turn the dataroom and de-identified business evidence into a decision pack in five business days: credible, overclaimed, legal-review-needed, terms-adjust, or kill."

Economics:
Charge 15,000-22,000 PLN for a fast revenue-quality screen, 25,000-35,000 PLN for a full diligence pack with cohort, CAC, pharmacy/provider continuity, refund/chargeback, and claims review, and 40,000-45,000 PLN for urgent acquisition or lender-underwriting cases. Reviewer costs should stay at 4,000-14,000 PLN per case only when healthcare regulatory, pharmacy-operations, legal-boundary, or subscription-growth review is needed. Buyers pay because a weak GLP-1 revenue-quality claim can distort valuation, earnout structure, debt underwriting, pharmacy/vendor diligence, CAC assumptions, or acquisition go/no-go by far more than the diligence fee.

Copy risk:
Healthcare diligence firms, FDA/regulatory lawyers, pharmacy consultants, QoE providers, subscription-growth diligence firms, PE operating partners, and strategy consultants can copy pieces. The defense is narrow live-decision focus, fast GLP-1-specific evidence requests, repeated failure taxonomy, buyer decision language, and a private benchmark library of revenue-quality and regulatory-exposure patterns during a major GLP-1 market transition. The company should not compete as a generic healthcare consultant, legal adviser, clinic operator, or pharmacy consultant.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the buyer signs the mandate, shares the dataroom, authorizes evidence requests, and uses the pack in a live capital or procurement decision, the startup controls that specific diligence file and decision artifact before the deadline. Another provider would have to replace the evidence-request list, cohort bridge, pharmacy/provider continuity map, CAC/refund/chargeback review, reviewer notes, unanswered questions, and buyer-ready memo before the decision clock runs out. Incumbents can compete for future diligence work, but they cannot instantly displace the active paid mandate without the buyer restarting diligence.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live investment, acquisition, financing, procurement, or rollout decisions where a GLP-1 telehealth or obesity-care revenue claim is being underwritten. The output is a decision artifact tied to current capital movement: revenue-quality bridge, evidence-request list, pharmacy/provider continuity map, CAC/payback analysis, refund/chargeback screen, compounded-to-branded exposure, reviewer red flags, and decision memo. It is not patient care, prescribing, pharmacy brokerage, drug advertising, generic market research, dashboard software, a clinic marketplace, implementation consulting, or brokered introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not prescribe, dispense, sell, resell, advertise, recommend, or deliver prescription drugs. Licensed clinicians, pharmacies, outsourcing facilities, labs, and healthcare entities remain provider, prescriber, dispenser, lab, seller, and operator of record. The startup does not diagnose, treat, interpret patient clinical data, validate clinical outcomes, give medical advice, give legal opinions, determine FDCA/state-law compliance, handle patient communications, or process patient-level health data unless a qualified legal/clinical structure is explicitly established. It uses non-patient, aggregate, de-identified, redacted, or target-approved business evidence wherever possible. Cases requiring patient-level clinical review, legal compounding opinions, pharmacy enforcement advice, claims adjudication, adverse-event medical judgment, or healthcare regulatory conclusions are routed to qualified counsel/clinicians or rejected.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack. BioSignal evaluates biosignal, wearable, EEG, sleep, fatigue, recovery, longevity, remote-monitoring, and digital-biomarker claims for healthtech capital allocators. GLP-1 Telehealth Revenue-Quality Diligence evaluates obesity-care and GLP-1 telehealth business-model claims: revenue durability, CAC/payback, pharmacy/provider continuity, compounded-to-branded exposure, refund/chargeback behavior, subscription economics, and legal/clinical no-go flags in live investment, acquisition, lender, or rollout decisions. It is not Supplement Stack Safety because it does not advise consumers, interpret labs, recommend supplements, or handle patient health decisions. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, PromoLeak, or any drug sales, Rx delivery, or clinical workflow.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Healthcare transaction diligence, FDA counsel, pharmacy consultants, QoE firms, and PE operating partners already serve serious buyers. A solo unknown founder needs reviewer credibility before any buyer trusts a GLP-1 diligence memo. Targets may refuse access to pharmacy contracts, provider agreements, cohort data, refund/chargeback files, CAC by channel, customer-support data, or legal memos. Many conclusions require healthcare regulatory lawyers, pharmacy experts, or clinicians, not a generalist founder. The product risks becoming legal advice, medical judgment, patient-data review, pharmacy compliance work, or generic market commentary if boundaries are weak. Some buyers may only trust known healthcare diligence firms. GLP-1 market conditions can change quickly, making the benchmark library perishable. The first proof must show a real IC, LOI, financing, valuation, earnout, legal-diligence, or no-go impact, not praise for a public teardown.
