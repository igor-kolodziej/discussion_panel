## Simulated Pass Candidate

### EAA Buyer Accessibility Renewal Sprint - 90

One-sentence thesis: a fixed-scope buyer-unblock sprint for Polish/CEE B2B SaaS and software-product vendors whose bank, insurer, telecom, transport, ecommerce, booking, or fintech customer renewal/procurement is blocked by a live European Accessibility Act, EN 301 549, WCAG, VPAT/ACR, or accessibility-defect request.

Buyer: 20-150 person Polish/CEE B2B SaaS or software-product vendors that supply user-facing digital components, portals, checkout flows, booking flows, mobile apps, self-service tools, document workflows, or customer communications to EU consumer-service companies now exposed to EAA accessibility pressure. The economic buyer is the founder, COO, CTO, product lead, or enterprise sales owner trying to preserve a live renewal, procurement, rollout, or strategic logo.

Acute trigger: a named enterprise customer sends an EAA/accessibility questionnaire, EN 301 549/VPAT request, WCAG defect list, procurement hold, renewal condition, release-blocking Jira ticket, or accessibility issue list tied to a live renewal/procurement/rollout. The vendor needs a buyer-acceptable answer and visible fixes before the customer deadline.

Transferable control point: prepaid sprint with live buyer request, product/staging access, affected user journeys, defect list or questionnaire, accessibility tester, front-end/dev contractor, answer/evidence owner, before/after artifacts, and customer-submitted response. The startup controls the sprint queue and evidence artifact for that specific renewal blocker.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:

- 3 prepaid live buyer-unblock sprints from at least 2 vendors.
- One accessibility specialist/tester and one front-end contractor on paid per-sprint terms.
- Vendor authorization to review the named product journey and prepare the buyer response/evidence pack.
- A scoped affected-flow inventory for at least 5 journeys across the first cases.
- At least 30 accessibility defects triaged into fixed, customer-accepted workaround, owner-approved remediation plan, out-of-scope legal/product decision, or no-repro.
- At least 2 customer-progress outcomes: renewal/procurement moves forward, buyer accepts the issue disposition, release blocker is downgraded, buyer narrows follow-up, or rollout gate reopens.
- First 40,000-90,000 PLN collected in sprint fees.

First acquisition mechanism: build a target list of Polish/CEE software vendors with public customers in banking, insurance, payments, telecom, transport, ecommerce, booking, and fintech. Outbound message asks only for live buyer accessibility blockers, not generic audits. Partner outreach goes to boutique accessibility testers, UX agencies, security/DORA advisors, enterprise-sales consultants, and software houses that see buyer questionnaires but do not want urgent remediation sprints.

Gross margin and payback logic: charge 12,000-20,000 PLN for a 5-business-day triage and buyer-response sprint, 20,000-35,000 PLN for a 10-business-day remediation sprint with contractor fixes, and 40,000+ PLN for multi-flow urgent renewal sprints. Tester and front-end contractor costs are scoped before kickoff. The buyer pays because the fee is small compared with a delayed or lost 100,000+ PLN renewal, enterprise rollout, or strategic logo.

Copy risk: accessibility agencies, UX shops, dev shops, automated accessibility tools, auditors, and in-house product teams can do pieces of the work. The defense is narrow vendor-side live buyer-blocker focus, local Polish/CEE outreach, fast paid sprint packaging, customer-response evidence memory, and combined tester-plus-remediation capacity. It should not compete as a broad accessibility consultancy.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: once the startup controls the paid live buyer ticket, affected product journeys, staging access, defect list, tester notes, contractor patch queue, buyer response wording, and submitted evidence, competitors cannot work that exact renewal blocker without the vendor switching mid-deadline. Each case adds accepted wording, recurring defect patterns, customer-question variants, and remediation snippets by buyer type and product flow.

Why this is not a service/report/app/dashboard/marketplace/generic broker: the output is not an audit PDF, accessibility score, monitoring dashboard, or brokered expert call. The proof is customer-facing progress on a named buyer blocker: fixed issues, before/after test artifacts, buyer-ready ACR/VPAT/EN 301 549 response sections where appropriate, issue-disposition table, staged screenshots/videos, and customer acceptance or narrowed follow-up. The startup sells an urgent renewal sprint, not general compliance readiness.

Reasons this could still fail:

- Accessibility agencies and automated tools are abundant.
- Some buyer requests require deep product redesign rather than a sprint.
- EAA legal interpretation, declarations, or conformance claims can drift into legal/compliance advice.
- Unknown founder credibility depends on a named accessibility tester and credible contractor bench.
- Buyers may accept only formal audits or established accessibility vendors.
- Vendors may not pay until a customer explicitly threatens renewal/procurement.
- Defect remediation can sprawl if the affected user journey is not tightly scoped.
- Case memory may not compound if every product and customer request is bespoke.

Decision: advance this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
