## Fresh Validator Summary

The fresh evaluator scored the idea **68 / 100**.

The score collapsed because the evaluator treated the "lockbox" as a case contract artifact, not a durable moat. ## Strongest Objections Captured

1. **The lockbox is not a moat.** It improves cash collection for a specific case, but labs, lawyers, consultants, and large converters can sell the next PFAS release workflow.
2. **Credibility is the bottleneck.** A known lab or food-contact consultant may beat a better workflow because customers trust them with sensitive orders and sample chains.
3. **Labs and consultants are already moving into the space.** The wedge must be commercial release orchestration, not testing coordination.
4. **Buyer acceptance remains outside control.** Retailers, QSRs, brands, and co-packers can reject the path, require a different lab, demand a legal opinion, or change criteria.
5. **No harmonised EU testing methodology creates risk.** Testing uncertainty helps demand but makes promises dangerous.
6. **Best cases may be too complex for a fast solo lockbox.** Grease barriers, coatings, adhesives, inks, laminates, recycled paper, molded fiber, and multi-component packs create sample-identity and evidence-matching problems.
7. **The POC is too optimistic for a part-time founder.** The required monthly case volume, channel building, reviewer coordination, and lab coordination are aggressive.
8. **Gross margin can be fragile.** Expert review, legal boundary checks, rework, supplier evidence gaps, logistics, insurance, translation, lab coordination, and buyer-response iterations can consume the case fee.
9. **It may become a transition spike.** After 2026, buyer requirements, lab workflows, and supplier declarations may standardize.
10. **No-go monetization is hard.** Clients prefer paying for release over paying for "do not produce" unless avoided scrap/rejection is explicit in the mandate.
