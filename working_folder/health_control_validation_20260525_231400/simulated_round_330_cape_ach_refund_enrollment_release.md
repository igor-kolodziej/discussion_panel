## Why This Branch

Round 300 CAPEClaim scored 82 in working validation. The evaluator accepted the current-cash trigger and CAPE reality but capped the idea because customs brokers, lawyers, and importers already own eligibility, entry screening, and filing.

Round 330 strips the product down to the narrower administrative choke point that remains after the broker or importer has already identified a refund path: electronic refund enrollment, ACE importer sub-account access, ACH bank authorization, Form 5106/email cleanup, refund-monitoring reports, and proof that otherwise payable refunds are not delayed by missing refund rails.

This is still risky and adjacent to Round 300. It only advances because the control point is materially narrower:

- no customs classification;
- no protest strategy;
- no eligibility opinion;
- no CAPE filing as an unauthorized broker;
- no tariff-law advice;
- no refund custody;
- proof is ACE/ACH refund readiness, accepted declaration monitoring, CBP refund report status, and first electronic refund trace.

## Source Check

CBP guidance says electronic refund enrollment is managed through ACE and ACH refund authorization, requires proper ACE Portal/importer access, and uses refund-monitoring reports. Trade-law summaries of CAPE Phase 1 state that IEEPA refunds are issued electronically, and that payment to eligible importers can be delayed until ACH banking information is provided in ACE.

## Lead Candidate

### Idea Name

CAPEACH Electronic Refund Enrollment Rail

### Thesis

IEEPA refund money can be delayed even after the importer or customs broker has a refund path if the importer lacks ACE importer sub-account access, valid Form 5106 email/contact data, ACH refund authorization, bank information, refund report monitoring, or a clean internal owner for the electronic refund rail; sell a narrow setup-and-proof rail that converts broker/importer-ready refund batches into traceable electronic refund readiness without giving customs or legal advice.

### Exact Buyer

CEE/EU brands, distributors, and manufacturers that acted as U.S. importer of record for IEEPA-duty entries, plus small and mid-sized U.S. customs brokers serving such importers, where there is already a broker, trade adviser, or importer owner for CAPE eligibility and filing.

### Acute Trigger

The buyer has IEEPA refund exposure and a broker/importer path, but payment can be delayed because ACE access, importer sub-account permissions, ACH refund enrollment, U.S. bank/refund recipient data, Form 5106 contact data, or REV refund reporting is missing or unowned.

### Control Point

Signed refund-readiness mandate over named IOR accounts and refund rail artifacts: ACE access checklist, importer sub-account status, Form 5106 contact/email status, ACH refund authorization status, bank-data confirmation record, broker/IOR filing path, CAPE declaration ID/status if available, REV refund-monitoring exports or screenshots, exception log, and payment/accepted-status trace.

### 60-Day Proof

- Six signed IOR or broker-routed mandates.
- Four paid refund-readiness setup files completed.
- Three ACE/ACH enrollment or bank-data correction outcomes.
- Two CAPE declaration/refund status traces through ACE reports.
- One electronic refund payment, refund date, or written refund-readiness confirmation tied to a before-state defect.
- 60,000-160,000 PLN equivalent collected in fixed setup fees and success fees.

### Economics

Charge USD 1,500-4,000 for an IOR refund-readiness sprint, USD 500-1,500 for broker-routed secondary importer files, and 1-3% of refunds only when a before-state refund-rail defect is fixed and an after-state refund, payment date, or accepted refund-monitoring status is documented. Fixed fees must dominate because refund entitlement belongs to the importer and CBP may pay valid refunds after proper setup.

### Internal Score

88.0 / 100.

The score clears the simulation gate only because the product has current cash, official electronic payment mechanics, binary proof artifacts, no refund custody, and a narrower boundary than the previously failed CAPE batch desk. It remains fragile: brokers and trade teams are close, the wave may be temporary, and legal/customs questions must be rejected immediately.

### Kill Criteria

- Fewer than four paid refund-readiness files in 60 days.
- No completed ACE/ACH setup or bank-data correction.
- No CAPE/refund report trace.
- More than 25% of accepted cases require eligibility, protest, drawback, reconciliation, classification, or legal refund-rights judgment.
- Brokers say they will do all enrollment/status work themselves.
- Gross margin below 60%.
- Any request for startup custody of refund proceeds or unauthorized customs filing.

Advance Round 330 to real working-chat Zero To One validation because the simulated score is strictly greater than 87 and the prompt materially narrows the failed CAPE batch idea to the electronic refund rail, not customs eligibility or filing.
