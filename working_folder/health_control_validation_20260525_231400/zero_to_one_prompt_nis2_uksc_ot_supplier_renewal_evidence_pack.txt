Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
NIS2/UKSC OT Supplier Renewal Evidence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A vendor-side renewal evidence pack for small Polish/CEE OT, SCADA, telemetry, maintenance, remote-access, monitoring, and industrial software suppliers whose customer renewals or procurements are blocked by new NIS2/UKSC supply-chain cybersecurity evidence requests from essential or important entities.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized OT/industrial technology vendors, SCADA integrators, telemetry providers, PLC/remote-access maintenance firms, industrial monitoring software vendors, water/wastewater automation suppliers, food/cold-chain monitoring vendors, municipal utility contractors, and industrial service companies selling recurring support, monitoring, or software to Polish essential/important entities outside regulated finance.

Best first buyers have 10-150 employees, 2-10 customers in water, wastewater, energy, district heating, transport/logistics, food production, chemical, waste management, manufacturing, telecom-adjacent infrastructure, or municipal utility operations, and a live customer request for supply-chain cybersecurity evidence, contract updates, remote-access controls, incident notification, subcontractor disclosure, or maintenance-access procedures.

Acute trigger:
A Polish essential or important entity under the amended National Cybersecurity System Act / NIS2 implementation pauses renewal, procurement, framework agreement, site access, remote maintenance, or paid support because the OT supplier cannot answer the customer's new supply-chain cybersecurity request. The customer asks for evidence around remote access, privileged accounts, VPN/MFA, patching, backups, incident contacts, subcontractors, logging, change windows, asset boundaries, emergency access, service continuity, vulnerability handling, and secure maintenance procedures.

Control point:
Signed renewal-evidence mandate with:
- the live customer NIS2/UKSC/supply-chain cybersecurity request;
- renewal, procurement, framework, service, or support value and deadline;
- OT service description and site/customer boundary;
- remote-access, VPN, MFA, privileged-account, service-account, and emergency-access evidence;
- patching, backup, restore, logging, monitoring, incident contact, change-management, and vulnerability-handling evidence;
- subcontractor/subprocessor/vendor-tool list;
- service-continuity and support-hours evidence;
- site-access and maintenance-window procedure evidence;
- "answerable now / technical remediation / customer decision / legal review / unsafe to claim" split;
- customer-specific response matrix and evidence room.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid renewal-evidence packs at 12,000-35,000 PLN each.
2. Each case has a live customer request from an essential/important entity and renewal/procurement/support value above 100,000 PLN or a strategic logo at risk.
3. At least one pack is submitted to a customer security/procurement/OT owner in a renewal, procurement, site-access, or support-continuity workflow.
4. At least one case produces customer acceptance, renewal progress, procurement progress, remote-access/site-access continuation, remediation scoping, or a clean no-go before the vendor wastes more sales time.
5. One OT/cybersecurity reviewer and one legal/contract reviewer are available on paid per-case terms for flagged sections.
6. A repeat taxonomy of customer blockers: unclear service boundary, weak remote access, missing MFA, shared service accounts, undocumented emergency access, no incident contact, no subcontractor list, no patch/change process, missing backup/restore evidence, weak logging, and vague maintenance responsibilities.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a strict intake checklist for NIS2/UKSC OT supplier customer requests; recruit one OT cybersecurity reviewer and one legal/contract reviewer; build a source/artifact library around Polish UKSC/NIS2 materials, customer request patterns, remote-access evidence, incident contacts, subcontractor registers, and response-matrix templates.

Month 2: map 80 Polish/CEE OT suppliers and industrial service vendors with likely essential/important customers: water/wastewater automation, municipal utilities, district heating, transport/logistics, food/cold-chain monitoring, energy/utility maintenance, industrial telemetry, SCADA service, and remote-access support firms. Send targeted outbound based on live customer evidence requests, not generic readiness.

Month 3: close three prepaid live cases. Accept only vendors with a real customer request, contract/support value above 100,000 PLN or strategic logo, existing evidence folders, named customer deadline, and a CTO/operations/security owner available for working sessions. Deliver customer-specific response matrix, evidence index, answer/gap split, remediation handoff, executive memo, and no-go/escalation log.

Months 4-6: complete 8-18 paid packs, collect 150,000-450,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewers, secure 2-3 repeat channels from OT cybersecurity firms, industrial automation advisers, legal boutiques, municipal utility advisers, or industrial service networks, and build a private library of accepted OT supplier evidence by customer type, sector, and blocker.

First acquisition mechanism:
Buyer pitch:
"If a water utility, energy/district-heating operator, food plant, transport/logistics customer, or other essential/important entity is pausing renewal or site access because of NIS2/UKSC supplier cybersecurity questions, send the customer request and your existing remote-access, patching, backup, incident, subcontractor, and maintenance evidence. I return a buyer-ready response matrix in 7 business days: answerable now, evidence attached, remediation needed, legal review needed, and unsafe-to-claim items."

Channel pitch:
"You keep security, legal, remediation, and audit work. I take urgent vendor-side evidence collation and response packs when an OT supplier has a live customer renewal blocked by NIS2/UKSC supply-chain cyber requests."

Economics:
Charge 12,000-18,000 PLN for a narrow single-customer response pack, 18,000-28,000 PLN for multi-site or remote-access-heavy OT supplier packs, 28,000-35,000 PLN for urgent multi-stakeholder renewal/procurement cases, and 6,000-15,000 PLN for annual refreshes. Reviewer cost is 2,000-8,000 PLN per case depending on flagged OT/cyber/legal sections. The vendor pays because a 100,000+ PLN renewal, strategic utility logo, site-access right, or remote-support contract is blocked by customer evidence, not because they want broad NIS2 consulting.

Copy risk:
Cybersecurity consultants, OT security firms, IEC 62443 advisers, MSPs, law firms, integrators, and customer security teams can copy pieces. The defense is strict vendor-side live-request focus, Polish/CEE OT customer pattern memory, sector-specific response matrices, fast evidence-room assembly, and partner overflow from firms that prefer higher-value remediation, audits, legal work, and architecture projects.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the vendor signs the mandate, prepays the case, shares the live customer request, contract deadline, OT service description, remote-access evidence, maintenance procedures, incident contacts, subcontractor list, and reviewer notes, the startup controls the active renewal evidence file. Another provider must displace the customer-specific response matrix, evidence room, answer/gap split, remediation handoff, and customer answer workflow under deadline pressure.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live OT supplier renewal, procurement, support-continuity, site-access, or remote-maintenance blockers tied to a named essential/important customer request. The output is a renewal artifact used in an active customer process: response matrix, evidence index, remote-access/control evidence, subcontractor register, incident/contact evidence, remediation handoff, executive memo, and no-go/escalation log. It is not broad NIS2 readiness, a cyber audit, IEC 62443 certification, penetration testing, legal advice, MSP support, dashboard, database, marketplace, or brokered consultant introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, NIS2/UKSC compliance certification, cyber audit opinions, penetration testing, IEC 62443 certification, OT architecture approval, managed security operations, incident-response service, or guarantees of customer approval. OT/cyber advisers, lawyers, customer security teams, auditors, vendors, and essential/important entities remain responsible for legal interpretation, controls, remediation, formal assurance, procurement approval, and operational cybersecurity decisions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack. DORA handles ICT third-party-risk and operational-resilience evidence for software vendors selling to regulated finance. NIS2/UKSC OT Supplier Renewal Evidence handles supply-chain cybersecurity evidence for OT/SCADA/telemetry/remote-maintenance suppliers selling to essential/important entities outside finance: water, wastewater, district heating, transport/logistics, food/cold-chain, municipal utilities, industrial operations, and infrastructure. The trigger, law, buyer sectors, control artifacts, reviewer skill, and operational evidence are materially different.

It is not REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, BioSignal, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Cybersecurity consultants, OT security firms, law firms, MSPs, and IEC 62443 advisers can provide broader and more credible support.
- The idea is structurally adjacent to DORA because it is another vendor-side regulated-buyer evidence pack.
- Some customers may demand real remediation, audits, penetration tests, or certifications rather than evidence collation.
- A solo Warsaw founder needs OT/cyber reviewer credibility from day one.
- Many small OT suppliers may have weak controls, shared passwords, informal remote access, and missing documentation, producing gap/no-go packs rather than renewal progress.
- Customer approval remains outside the founder's control.
- The Polish NIS2/UKSC rollout may create consulting noise but not enough urgent paid vendor-side blockers immediately.
- The work can become generic cybersecurity consulting unless it stays tied to live customer renewal/procurement requests.
