## Selected Candidate

**Closed-Site Utility Deposit Refund Mandate**

### Why This Advanced

This is not a speculative audit. The first accepted cases require a closed account, site-level supplier ledger, contract/deposit evidence, meter-closure or supplier-switch proof, and supplier acknowledgement of a refundable balance or credit. The buyer is a multi-site operator with enough closures to make one mandate economically meaningful. The founder does not move funds, litigate, sell energy, provide legal advice, or pursue disputed claims.

### Control Point

- Signed mandate over named closed sites and supplier accounts.
- Site/account ledger with electricity, gas, water, district heat, telecom, waste, and meter-service accounts.
- Closure/assignment/switch/handover protocols.
- Invoices, final bills, meter reads, deposit terms, bank account confirmation, entity-name mapping, and supplier tickets.
- Supplier acknowledgement of refundable deposit, overpayment, credit note, or valid no-go.
- Fee right tied to actually recovered/credited amounts plus fixed screened-ledger fee.

### 60-Day Proof

- 5 signed multi-site mandates.
- At least 100 supplier accounts screened.
- At least 10 debtor/supplier acknowledgements of refundable balance, credit, or valid no-go.
- At least 50,000 PLN refunded, credited, or formally approved for refund.
- At least 15,000 PLN collected in fees.

### Economics

- 2,500-8,000 PLN screened-ledger fee per operator depending on account count.
- 12-25% success fee on actual refunded/credited cash.
- Initial target: operators with 10-200 recent closed/relocated sites and at least 100,000 PLN plausible deposits/credits across utilities.
- Gross margin target above 70% because the work is document collection, account matching, supplier ticketing, and follow-up, not legal work or field service.

### Internal Caps Applied

- Capped below 93 because the hidden recoverable pool may be smaller than site/account count implies.
- Capped below 91 because suppliers may be slow and customer records may be messy.
- Not capped below 87 because the proof artifact is current cash movement or debtor acknowledgement, buyer data is non-sensitive compared with health/security, regulation is low, and one mandate can control a batch of accounts rather than a single small claim.
