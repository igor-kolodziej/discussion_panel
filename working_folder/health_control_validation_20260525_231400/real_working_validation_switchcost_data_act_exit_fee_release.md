## Zero To One Summary

The evaluator confirmed that the Data Act switching-charge wedge is real. Until 12 January 2027, switching charges for data-processing services must be limited to costs directly linked to switching, and from 12 January 2027 switching charges are prohibited.

The idea failed because many fees may be valid or outside the switching-charge bucket, applicability is legally sensitive, the 2026 transition window is short, and the natural channel partners are also likely competitors.

## Strongest Objections

1. Providers can argue fees are standard service fees, early termination penalties, premium migration support, professional services, custom export, extended retrieval, or direct switching costs.
2. The strongest transitional argument is time-compressed because the full no-switching-charge rule starts 12 January 2027.
3. Applicability to specific SaaS, hosting, managed-service, archive, and bespoke arrangements can require legal interpretation.
4. Providers can slow-walk, refuse third-party engagement, ask for counsel-to-counsel correspondence, or require account-owner-only communication.
5. MSPs, law firms, migration consultants, FinOps firms, and procurement advisers can absorb the workflow.
6. Technical migration can remain the bottleneck even if fees are reduced.
7. Case values may be uneven; SMEs may not have enough disputed charge value to justify the fee.
8. Export-delay cases can become migration project management rather than a clean charge-release desk.

## Evaluator's Better Narrowing

"Provider-exit charge and export-release desk for EU SMEs leaving hosting, backup, archive, storage, and mid-market SaaS providers."

Recommended constraints:

- Accept only written quotes, invoices, fee schedules, support tickets, or export-delay records.
- Require a current renewal, migration, acquisition, or cost-reduction deadline.
- Exclude cases below 25,000 PLN disputed charge or below 100,000 PLN commercial consequence.
- Use a tech lawyer and cloud/migration reviewer before provider-facing wording.
- Continue only if at least 3 of 5 first cases produce provider-written outcomes.

## Lesson

Do not continue Data Act switching/exit-fee variants for this search. They are real cash-control ideas, but the validator sees legal ambiguity, short transition timing, provider discretion, and channel conflict as hard ceilings. The confirmed machine-data idea remains materially stronger because it creates a partner-side operational use case, not only a charge dispute.
