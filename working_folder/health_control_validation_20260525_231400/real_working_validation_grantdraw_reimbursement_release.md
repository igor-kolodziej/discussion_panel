## Validator Summary

It accepted that reimbursement delay creates real working-capital pain and that “payment-request evidence assembly and correction loop” is sharper than grant advisory or grant writing.

A document desk cannot cure those without becoming a grant lawyer, accountant, auditor, or procurement adviser.

## Strongest Objections Captured

- Grant consultants already own much of the payment-request and settlement workflow.
- Many blocked payment requests are not packaging problems; they are genuine eligibility, procurement, VAT, deliverable, state-aid, conflict-of-interest, payroll, participant-evidence, or audit issues.
- Program rules vary too much across innovation, regional, energy, training, agriculture, fisheries, NGO, and public-sector grants.
- Success fees on public money are sensitive and should generally be avoided or reviewed carefully.
- Authority acceptance and payment timing remain outside the founder’s control.
- Files include sensitive invoices, payment proofs, supplier contracts, payroll records, participant data, procurement documents, project photos, serial numbers, and internal records.
- Beneficiaries may blame the founder when the file reveals old mistakes.
- Direct cold outreach to public award lists is likely slower than warm consultant/accountant channels.
- Pricing fails if the startup becomes low-status document chasing for broad, chaotic cases.
- A signed file gives temporary control, but competent grant consultants can copy the workflow.

## Lesson

Post-award cash-release is better than grant readiness, but it still fails when the actual bottleneck often belongs to grant consultants, accountants, procurement advisers, or authority discretion. Do not continue public-funds payment-file variants unless the founder controls a more binary accepted claim or payment direction.
