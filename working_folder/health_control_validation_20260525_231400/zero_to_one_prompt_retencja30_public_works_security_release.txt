Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections. Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time. Idea name: Retencja30 Public-Works Security Release Mandate. One-sentence thesis: Recover due-but-unreturned Polish public-procurement performance securities for small contractors; proof is cash refunded or a bank/insurance guarantee released. Exact buyer: Polish contractors with repeated public contracts and retained cash securities, bank guarantees, or insurance guarantees after final acceptance or warranty/defect-liability expiry. Acute trigger: A statutory/contractual return date has passed or is within 45 days: 70% after accepted performance, or retained 30% after warranty/defect-liability expiry. Cash, collateral, guarantee limit, or bonding capacity is still tied up. Control point: Contract-specific signed release mandate plus final acceptance protocol, warranty/defect-liability expiry proof, security instrument or cash-deposit proof, authority contact, contractor bank account, and dated release-request package. 60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof: Five contractors sign mandates covering 20 securities. At least 2 million PLN equivalent in cash securities, guarantee exposure, or collateral is assigned. Three authorities issue release/refund/guarantee-return approvals. Two cash refunds or guarantee releases complete. First success fees are invoiced after release. 6-month POC: Use BZP/TED/local tender records to find repeated contractors and likely due security dates. Process 120-200 assigned files for 25-40 contractors. Release 8-15 million PLN equivalent. Generate 300,000-900,000 PLN revenue from setup fees, release fees, and portfolio sweeps. First acquisition mechanism: Contract-specific outbound to owners/CFOs/bid managers: "This public contract appears to have a performance-security return date due or approaching. If cash, a bank guarantee, or an insurance guarantee is still tied up, I recover or release it on a mandate-backed success fee. No litigation, no procurement consulting, no disputed claims." Economics: 1,500-4,000 PLN setup per portfolio, credited against first success fee. 5-9% success fee on recovered cash. 1,500-5,000 PLN per released guarantee, or 5-15% of verified annual fee/collateral savings. Target 70-85% gross margin. A 100,000 PLN refund or freed guarantee line can justify the fee. Copy risk: Contractors, accountants, lawyers, bid consultants, bond brokers, and procurement advisors can do this. The wedge is proactive public-record targeting plus mandate control over overlooked release dates; lawyers are too expensive for undisputed administrative releases, and banks/insurers benefit from guarantees staying outstanding. Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: Once the contractor signs a mandate and the startup files the release request, the startup controls the correspondence trail, evidence packet, deadline follow-up, and success-fee right for that security. Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: The deliverable is cash returned or guarantee/collateral released on named public contracts. Payment is tied to completed release, not a dashboard, report, procurement advisory, marketplace, or introduction. Boundaries and legal/provider limits: Only undisputed administrative release requests with documented acceptance and warranty/defect dates. No litigation, legal opinions, disputed claim negotiation, or authority setoff challenges. Disputed, penalized, defective, setoff, insolvency, or litigation cases are declined or referred to counsel. Contractor remains party to the contract and recipient of returned cash. Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas: Not TraceFaktura or municipal invoice rails: this is not invoice documentation or municipal service settlement. It is statutory/contractual security release after accepted public contracts or warranty expiry. Not PromoLeak, CBAM, DORA, REDBlocked, HeritageDoor, or BatteryFit. Strongest anticipated objections: Competent contractors may already track large securities. Remaining unreturned securities may be small, disputed, or low value. Authorities may return automatically. Public records may not reveal acceptance and warranty dates. Legal edge cases can leak in. Success fees may look expensive for a simple release letter. The business may be cash-flow service unless public-record targeting and authority-specific release memory compound.
