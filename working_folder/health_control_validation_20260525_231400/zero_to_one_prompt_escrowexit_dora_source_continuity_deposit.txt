Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EscrowExit DORA Source-Continuity Deposit Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A 7-10 business day escrow-deposit execution desk for small SaaS and software-product vendors whose bank, insurer, payment-institution, leasing, or fintech customer is blocking a renewal, procurement, audit response, migration approval, or contract signature until the vendor can show source-code escrow, build/recovery artifact deposit, exit-support evidence, or step-in continuity proof tied to DORA/ICT third-party-risk expectations.

Exact buyer:
20-150 employee SaaS and software-product vendors serving EU regulated-finance customers, especially KYC/AML, fraud, payment infrastructure, lending/leasing workflow, claims workflow, document workflow, risk analytics, reconciliation, reporting, and operational SaaS vendors. The vendor has a live regulated-finance customer request that specifically asks for source-code escrow, executable artifact escrow, build/recovery instructions, exit support, data-return/deletion evidence, step-in continuity evidence, disaster-recovery evidence, or software continuity proof before renewal, procurement approval, audit closure, migration approval, or contract signature. Exclude vendors needing full DORA readiness, broad security remediation, legal negotiation, product rebuild, or a generic questionnaire response.

Acute trigger:
A named bank, insurer, payment institution, leasing group, or regulated fintech writes that the vendor must provide escrow/deposit, exit-continuity, build/recovery, source-access, executable-artifact, runbook, or step-in evidence before a commercial event moves. The vendor is not buying abstract DORA readiness; it is paying because a regulated-finance customer has tied renewal, procurement, audit closure, signature, migration approval, or continued use to a concrete continuity artifact.

Control point:
Signed escrow-deposit execution mandate; live customer request text; chosen qualified escrow provider or counsel-approved escrow route; source-code, dependency, build, infrastructure-as-code, executable artifact, and release package scope; readme/runbook owner; build or recovery dry-run boundary; customer-facing receipt format; secure transfer checklist; hash/checksum list; version tag; third-party license caveat list; secrets-exclusion check; release-condition/legal review boundary; data-return/deletion evidence pointer where requested; and a buyer-ready deposit receipt plus gap/no-go log. The startup controls the active deposit package, escrow-provider handoff, technical packaging checklist, buyer proof pack, and fee right for that commercial blocker. It does not act as escrow agent, lawyer, auditor, penetration tester, formal certifier, or production SRE.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three software escrow providers, boutique tech/privacy law firms, or DORA/cyber advisers sign referral or execution routes for source-continuity deposit cases. Six vendors sign paid live-blocker mandates at 18,000-55,000 PLN each. Every case has a written regulated-finance customer request, chosen escrow/counsel route, management authorization, source/build/runbook owner, and commercial consequence above 150,000 PLN. At least four cases produce signed escrow-provider acceptance, deposit receipt, verified package transfer, buyer-ready receipt, customer-progress acknowledgement, or paid clean no-go because source/build continuity is not deposit-ready. At least 150,000 PLN is collected within 60 days, and no case is counted unless the proof artifact goes beyond a memo into a dated escrow/deposit or no-go outcome.

6-month POC:
Deliver 30-55 source-continuity deposit cases; collect 750,000-2,000,000 PLN in fixed execution fees; document 20+ signed deposits, buyer-progress outcomes, audit/renewal/procurement progress outcomes, or clean no-go decisions; secure 6+ repeat routing partners among software escrow providers, DORA advisers, boutique tech/privacy law firms, fractional CISOs, and SaaS procurement-response consultants; build a private taxonomy of regulated-finance escrow request wording, accepted receipt formats, build/runbook gaps, release-condition escalation triggers, source-package defects, third-party dependency pitfalls, secrets/configuration exclusions, and customer objections; keep gross margin above 60% after technical reviewer and legal-boundary costs.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target DORA advisers, software escrow providers, boutique technology law firms, privacy/cyber firms, fractional CISOs, and procurement-response consultants. Partner pitch: "When a regulated-finance customer asks your SaaS vendor client for source escrow, build/recovery deposit, exit continuity, or step-in proof, route the deposit execution file to me. You keep legal, security, and DORA advisory work. I package the source/build/runbook artifacts, coordinate the escrow-provider handoff, produce the buyer-ready receipt/gap log, and return deposit or no-go within 7-10 business days." Direct outreach targets software vendors with public regulated-finance customers and visible DORA/ICT-supplier exposure.

Economics:
Charge 18,000-28,000 PLN for simple source escrow deposit packaging where source ownership and build instructions are clean, 30,000-45,000 PLN for multi-component SaaS deposits with infrastructure-as-code/runbook/data-export evidence, and 45,000-80,000 PLN for urgent strategic-customer renewals with multiple repositories, environments, or artifact types. Escrow-provider and legal review fees are quoted separately or passed through. The vendor pays because a 25,000-60,000 PLN deposit execution is rational when a 150,000-2,000,000 PLN regulated-finance customer renewal, procurement, or signature is blocked.

Copy risk:
Software escrow providers, law firms, DORA/cyber consultancies, SRE/DevOps firms, fractional CISOs, ISO/SOC consultants, and procurement advisers can copy pieces. The defense is not escrow law or generic DORA knowledge. It is fast vendor-side packaging for live regulated-finance blockers, accepted bank receipt formats, source/build/runbook defect memory, partner routing, no-go discipline, and a private library of what small SaaS vendors must fix before an escrow deposit is buyer-credible.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the vendor signs the mandate, pays the fixed fee, shares the bank's exact request, chooses an escrow/counsel route, and authorizes source/build/runbook packaging, the startup controls the active deposit package, gap log, escrow-provider handoff, and buyer-ready proof for that renewal deadline. An escrow provider, law firm, or DORA consultant can compete generally, but it must displace the already-authorized source-continuity file while the regulated-finance customer is waiting.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business refuses broad DORA readiness, generic questionnaires, contract negotiation, legal opinions, software development, security audits, SaaS migration, dashboards, marketplaces, and escrow introductions without a live blocker. It accepts only regulated-finance customer requests that require a source/build/recovery/exit-continuity deposit artifact. The deliverable is a dated deposit execution file: source/artifact scope, technical package checklist, secrets-exclusion log, build/runbook evidence, escrow-provider transfer, deposit receipt or no-go log, and buyer-ready proof for the named commercial event.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as escrow agent, provide legal advice, draft or negotiate release clauses, certify DORA compliance, provide SOC/ISO certification, audit security controls, perform penetration testing, guarantee build reproducibility, operate production systems, hold production secrets, or promise bank acceptance. Escrow providers, law firms, DORA advisers, security officers, auditors, SRE/DevOps contractors, and vendor management remain responsible for their domains. Cases requiring major engineering remediation, unresolved IP ownership, contractor-rights disputes, secret leakage, unlicensed third-party code, formal assurance, source-code review, litigation, or bank-specific contract negotiation are no-go or routed to qualified providers.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack because the control object and proof are materially different. The confirmed DORA idea handles broad vendor-side DORA/ICT questionnaires, Article 28/30 evidence, subcontractor lists, exit-plan wording, BCP/DR summaries, and evidence-room organization. EscrowExit accepts only requests requiring a signed source/build/recovery/exit-continuity deposit artifact and produces escrow-provider acceptance, deposit receipt, source/build/runbook package, or clean no-go. It is not REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, KSeF, RAGProbe, AIQ, CyberBind, AppExchange, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Software escrow providers and law firms may already own the natural workflow. Some banks may only accept large recognized escrow providers or counsel-led documentation. Many vendors will discover their source/build/runbook package is not deposit-ready because of missing scripts, undocumented dependencies, hardcoded secrets, unclear contractor IP, proprietary third-party code, missing IaC, fragile deployments, or lack of reproducible build. A deposit receipt may not satisfy the bank if the bank wants full exit testing, step-in rights, audit rights, BCP/DR proof, or contract clauses. The idea may be treated as a DORA add-on rather than a separate company. Handling source code, architecture, build instructions, and dependency lists creates serious trust and security burden. Partner channels can internalize the checklist. The 7-10 business-day delivery promise may fail when engineering cleanup is needed.
