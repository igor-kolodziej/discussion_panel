Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ABMClean Corporate Device Lot Release.

One-sentence thesis:
SMEs, schools, and small groups with Apple refresh lots will pay to turn clean-title but MDM/ABM/Activation-Lock-risk device piles into buyer-deposit resale lots through lawful source-admin release, serial manifests, erasure proof, and refurbisher acceptance.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE SMEs, private schools, language-school groups, clinics only for non-patient devices, agencies, retailers, warehouses, and professional services firms with 30-500 retired company-owned MacBooks, iPads, iPhones, or mixed Apple fleets after a refresh. The economic buyer is the IT manager, finance/admin owner, school operations head, MSP, or accountant who wants resale value or lease-return penalty avoidance. The downstream payer is a refurbisher or broker who deposits against a clean serial lot after release proof.

Acute trigger:
A company has a refresh lot sitting in storage or facing lease-return deadline, but resale value is blocked by unknown Apple Business Manager status, MDM enrollment, Activation Lock risk, missing serial manifest, uncertain erasure proof, or buyer refusal to price the lot before release status is verified. The company wants cash or penalty avoidance within weeks, not a generic IT asset audit.

Control point:
The startup accepts only source-side lots where the company or school already controls legal title and has administrator authority over Apple Business Manager, MDM, Apple Business Essentials, or the relevant admin route. The controlled asset is the serial-numbered release file: title statement, admin authority, serial manifest, supervised release/wipe workflow, erasure evidence, exception list, buyer grading notes, refurbisher deposit/offer, and clean/no-go status for every device.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign two paid source-side lots covering at least 120 devices total. Each lot must include a company title statement, admin authority confirmation, serial manifest, written scope excluding unauthorized unlocks, contractor release/wipe runbook, erasure proof, exception list, and one refurbisher deposit, conditional purchase order, or written offer tied to the cleaned lot. Seller payout or startup success fee is tied to devices that are lawfully released, wiped, and accepted or explicitly no-go'd.

6-month POC:
Process 600-1,200 devices across 8-15 refresh lots from at least five source companies, schools, MSPs, accountants, or refurbisher channels. At least 70% of devices must be released/wiped/accepted or cleanly no-go'd within 10 business days of admin access. At least five lots must produce cash sale, lease-return penalty reduction, or refurbisher accepted offer. Gross margin must stay above 50% after contractor handling, secure logistics, erasure tooling, insurance, exception work, and channel fees.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with three channels: Apple-focused MSPs that do not want low-margin asset cleanup, refurbishers who see promising lots rejected for ABM/MDM uncertainty, and accountants/admin offices at schools or SMEs with refresh assets on the books. Outreach asks for one source-controlled lot with title and admin access, not stranded third-party devices. The first offer is a paid serial-lot release sprint: 3,000-6,000 PLN intake plus 50-250 PLN per successfully released or no-go'd device, or a resale spread only when a refurbisher deposit is already signed.

Economics:
Pricing is 50-250 PLN per device plus a lot intake fee, or 10-25% of verified resale uplift where a refurbisher's pre-clean and post-clean offers are documented. A 100-device MacBook/iPad lot can support 10,000-30,000 PLN gross revenue if cleaning changes resale value or prevents lease-return penalties. Upfront capital stays low because the founder does not buy devices speculatively; refurbisher deposits or source-company fees fund contractor handling and logistics.

Copy risk:
ITAD providers, Apple MSPs, refurbishers, device brokers, and corporate IT teams can do parts of this workflow. The defense is strict source-side control and case memory: title/admin intake, release sequencing, serial exception taxonomy, erasure proof, refurbisher offer patterns, and trusted channels that send lots before devices become mixed with questionable ITAD stock.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder has the source-company title statement, admin mandate, serial manifest, supervised release workflow, and refurbisher deposit for a named lot, competitors cannot control that exact batch before the cash event. Incumbents can pursue future lots, but not the current serial-numbered lot after source admin authority, buyer deposit, and exception workflow are locked.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It does not sell generic ITAD, device unlocking, a resale marketplace, a device database, an app, or advisory hours. It accepts only clean-title source-controlled lots and produces a cash-linked release artifact: title/admin file, serial manifest, release/wipe proof, erasure record, exception/no-go list, buyer offer/deposit, and accepted resale or lease-return outcome.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No unauthorized unlocking, no bypassing Activation Lock, no devices without source-company title and admin authority, no consumer lost-property cases, no police/government/high-security cases, no patient/customer data handling, no purchase of questionable lots, and no promise that Apple, MDM, lease company, or buyer will accept a device. Qualified ITAD or MSP contractors perform physical handling, erasure, and platform operations under written authorization. Any device with unclear title, missing admin authority, suspicious provenance, employee dispute, or data-risk concern is rejected or no-go'd.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from the previously failed ABM/MDM release mandate because it does not chase original owners on behalf of refurbishers or ITAD buyers. It starts only with the original source company or school that already controls title and admin authority. It is distinct from HeritageDoor and physical-lot ideas because the value is not aesthetic scarcity; it is lawful release of serial-numbered corporate devices into resale cash. It is distinct from DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, PromoLeak, GreenTender, TraceFaktura, Supplement Stack, and health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
ITAD providers and Apple MSPs may already solve this for good companies. The opportunity may be mostly messy lots that should be rejected. Companies may lack clean admin access even if they own devices. Refurbisher deposits may wait until physical inspection and grading. Activation Lock/MDM workflows have scam-adjacent optics and require strict provenance controls. Data erasure and chain-of-custody errors can create serious liability. A source company may choose a full-service ITAD vendor instead of a narrow release desk. One lot proves transaction skill, not a repeatable company, unless MSP/accountant/refurbisher channels repeatedly send source-controlled lots.
