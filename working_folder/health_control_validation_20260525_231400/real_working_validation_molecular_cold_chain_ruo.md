## Zero To One Summary

Zero To One agreed that the idea has a tangible control point: freezer/dewar capacity, temperature logs, first-look or consignment rights over named RUO lots, and emergency storage/move workflow.

It scored only 70 because the two revenue streams have different problems:

- emergency cold-chain rescue has urgent but sporadic demand;
- RUO reagent salvage may have margin but has buyer-trust, expiry, storage-history, and compliance friction.

The best version is not a "RUO bargain warehouse"; it is paid cold-chain continuity infrastructure with documented RUO salvage as a secondary layer. It becomes attractive only if labs prepay access or upstream partners reliably feed high-quality distressed lots.

## Strongest Objections

1. Standby deposits may be hard to sell.
   - Labs agree freezer failure is painful, but may still rely on insurance, neighboring labs, distributors, service companies, university infrastructure, or improvisation.

2. Emergency demand is real but lumpy.
   - A freezer failure is urgent but unpredictable; assets may sit idle unless channels from service firms, lab movers, distributors, incubators, and closures are secured.

3. RUO reagent resale has a trust problem.
   - Buyers need proof of unopened status, storage history, authenticity, expiry, label integrity, source rights, and procurement usability.

4. RUO / IVD / clinical-use boundary is a trap.
   - The first POC must stay with RUO/research-only stock and avoid clinical IVD patient-use inventory.

5. Human samples are a separate high-risk category.
   - Patient-linked samples, embryos, gametes, or clinical materials create biosafety, ownership, consent, chain-of-custody, and insurance risk.

6. Cold-chain liability can wipe out margin.
   - Terms need liability caps, no clinical-use warranty, no reagent-performance warranty, rejection rules, and insurance review.

7. Part-time founder conflicts with emergency promise.
   - A realistic SLA may be same-business-day / scheduled rescue, not full 24/7.

8. Public/university labs may be slow buyers.
   - Early buyers would need to be private biotech, CRO, IVF-adjacent research teams, small molecular labs, or distributors.

## Pivot Implications

Do not keep brute-forcing inventory-only variants unless the demand side is prepaid or assigned before purchase.

A viable version would first prove:

- 3+ paid standby deposits;
- 1 paid rescue/storage/move job;
- 1 signed first-look channel from freezer service, lab closure, distributor, or incubator;
- 1 clean RUO consignment sale with documented storage history;
- gross margin above 40% after equipment, logging, courier, rejects, and liability reserves.

The next pivot should prioritize payment/receivable/claim custody where cash is already owed or formally reserved, not speculative future inventory turns.
