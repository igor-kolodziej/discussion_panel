Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
VirtualCard Payment-Direction Switch Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Preserve margin for B2B suppliers whose already-approved enterprise invoices are being routed through fee-bearing virtual-card, procurement-card, or AP-payment-network rails by controlling the customer AP payment-method switch until a fee-free bank-transfer/ACH/SEPA remittance is acknowledged or received.

Exact buyer:
Polish, CEE, and EU B2B suppliers, industrial distributors, SaaS vendors, agencies, manufacturers, and service firms that sell to large enterprise customers and have approved invoices or recurring customer accounts currently paid through virtual cards, procurement cards, or AP-payment networks that impose a 1.5-3% fee haircut or delay payment if the supplier refuses the card route. Best first buyers have at least 50,000 PLN equivalent monthly invoice volume per affected customer account and visible gross-margin pain.

Acute trigger:
The supplier has a named approved invoice, remittance schedule, or recurring customer account where the buyer's AP process is pushing payment through a virtual-card, procurement-card, card-network, dynamic early-pay, or fee-bearing AP-network route. The supplier either accepts an immediate card/payment-network fee haircut or waits while trying to obtain a direct bank-transfer, ACH, or SEPA path. The pain is current cash and current margin, not a forecasted optimization project.

Control point:
Signed supplier mandate for named customer accounts; approved invoice list; remittance evidence; AP portal/payment-method status; buyer AP contact path; supplier-approved payment-method change request; buyer AP acknowledgement, vendor-master update, portal ticket, payment-method confirmation, remittance schedule, or first no-card-fee payment. The startup never receives or moves funds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Eight supplier mandates covering at least twenty named enterprise buyer accounts; at least 1,000,000 PLN of monthly or scheduled approved invoice volume under switch review; ten buyer AP acknowledgements or portal payment-method cases opened; five customer accounts switched, scheduled, or confirmed for bank-transfer/ACH/SEPA payment; at least one first fee-free remittance received; 25,000-60,000 PLN collected through setup fees and switch/savings-linked fees.

6-month POC:
Control 40 suppliers and 120 buyer-account payment routes; move 20,000,000-50,000,000 PLN annualized approved invoice volume away from avoidable card/AP-network fees; document 400,000-1,000,000 PLN annualized supplier margin preserved; collect 150,000-400,000 PLN in fees; build a private buyer/AP-network/payment-method switch taxonomy by buyer policy, portal path, accepted evidence, remittance timing, rejection reason, and fee-free route.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target CFOs, AR leads, outsourced finance teams, and accountants serving B2B suppliers. The outreach asks for one concrete file, not a discovery project: "Send the enterprise customers currently paying your approved invoices through virtual card or fee-bearing AP-network routes. I get paid only when customer AP acknowledges a fee-free route, schedules bank-transfer/ACH/SEPA payment, or the first no-card-fee remittance arrives."

Economics:
Charge 1,500-5,000 PLN per buyer-account switch setup after a written path is confirmed, plus 15-25% of first-year avoided card/AP-network fees capped by buyer account. For larger recurring accounts, charge a fixed 10,000-25,000 PLN switch fee. A supplier with one 500,000 PLN monthly customer account and a 2% card haircut can justify a meaningful fee from annualized margin preserved. Founder work is structured AR/AP case control; contractors handle portal operations, buyer-account research, and secure document handling.

Copy risk:
Supplier AR teams, accountants, outsourced CFOs, AP consultants, and procurement-payment specialists can copy the workflow. Enterprise buyers and AP networks can also harden policies. The defense is narrow control of signed supplier mandates, exact buyer-account routes, AP case wording, payment-method switch evidence, approved invoice registers, and remittance outcomes before rivals know which buyer accounts are movable.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the supplier mandate, approved invoice list, buyer account, AP portal case, customer AP thread, payment-method evidence, and fee agreement for a specific buyer-account route, another provider cannot act on that same payment-direction switch without displacing the active mandate and re-collecting sensitive AR/customer evidence from the supplier.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The controlled object is a named payment-direction switch file for approved invoices and buyer accounts. The output is customer AP acknowledgement, a vendor-master/payment-method update, a fee-free remittance schedule, or the first bank-transfer/ACH/SEPA payment without the virtual-card/AP-network fee. There is no analytics report, generic AR consulting, dashboard, supplier marketplace, or payment broker role.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No funds custody, no factoring, no loans, no claims financing, no payment execution, no legal threats, no customer contract renegotiation, and no advice on tax, accounting, card-surcharge legality, sanctions, fraud, or banking compliance. Reject disputed invoices, buyer insolvency, fraud flags, suspicious bank changes, contract disputes, new bank-account risk, requests to misrepresent terms, and cases where buyer AP provides no written route.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not PromoLeak because it does not recover retailer deductions, promotions, retail media charges, shortage claims, or remittance leakage. Not VoP payee-name mismatch because the issue is fee-bearing payment method, not beneficiary-name verification. Not KSeF, AP portal, unapplied cash, credit memo, or true-up recovery. Not Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL, OTA, freight, PSP reserve, card-settlement claim, or marketplace payout recovery. Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, or health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Enterprise buyers may refuse to switch because virtual cards and AP networks create rebates, working-capital benefits, or standardized AP policy. Suppliers may be able to do this themselves once shown the request path. Some cases will be ordinary late payment, disputed invoice, contract problem, fraud-control concern, or buyer cash-management delay rather than a payment-method problem. Customer relationships can be damaged if outreach is clumsy. The moat is operational and account-specific, not deep technology. Success-fee attribution can be disputed if the buyer would have switched anyway. The best cases may cluster around a small number of buyers and networks, making case supply uncertain. The founder must avoid legal, accounting, surcharge, banking, and payment-advice boundaries.
