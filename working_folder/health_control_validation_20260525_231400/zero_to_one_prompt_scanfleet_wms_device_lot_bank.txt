Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
ScanFleet WMS Device Lot Bank

One-sentence thesis:
Warehouses standardize operations around exact rugged scanner models, cradles, batteries, MDM images, WMS apps, and worker training; a founder can control lease-end or site-closure exact-model fleets before they are split by refurb dealers and sell deposit-backed replacement lots to 3PLs, manufacturers, and distributors.

Exact buyer:
3PLs, ecommerce warehouses, manufacturers, wholesalers, and retail distribution centers in Poland/CEE/EU with an existing WMS/MDM setup standardized on a named rugged handheld scanner model and accessories.

The best first buyers have 40-500 warehouse devices already deployed and need 20-80 more of the exact model or accessory set.

Acute trigger:
The buyer needs exact devices because peak season or a new shift/site is within 30-90 days; OEM lead time or new-device budget is too slow; changing model would require WMS app testing, Android/OS/firmware checks, MDM enrollment changes, cradle/mount/battery changes, barcode-scanning profile changes, worker retraining, or site training changes; existing devices are failing faster than planned; or a 3PL has won a new customer and needs identical scanners for a go-live.

Control point:
The founder controls titled or optioned compact hardware lots before fragmentation: 20-200 exact-model rugged scanners from one lease-end, site-closure, integrator refresh, or corporate standardization event; serial-number list; model/SKU/accessory list; chargers, cradles, batteries, pistol grips, vehicle mounts, and docks where present; MDM/account wipe and ownership-clean statement; basic functional test sheet; photo/video inventory; and buyer deposit plus acceptance terms before final title transfer when possible.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days: two signed supplier-side options over exact-model lots totaling 80-200 devices; one physically controlled lot of at least 30 devices; serial list, accessory list, wipe/test sheet, and photo pack; three buyer deposits of 5,000-20,000 PLN for full or partial lots; one paid handoff to a buyer or buyer's integrator; and no speculative purchase above 35,000 PLN without buyer deposit coverage.

6-month POC:
6-10 controlled lots; 350-700 devices processed; 8-15 deposit-backed buyer transactions; 250,000-800,000 PLN revenue; 35-55% gross margin after acquisition, testing, batteries, shipping, and DOA reserve; at least 4 repeat buyers or integrators; inventory turns under 45 days for accepted models.

First acquisition mechanism:
Supply: call leasing companies, refurbishers, 3PL site-closure managers, warehouse integrators, barcode equipment installers, insolvency administrators, and corporate IT/operations teams. Offer fast purchase or option for unsplit exact-model lots with accessories. Pay more than bulk refurb scrap only when serials and accessories are coherent.

Demand: target warehouses and integrators publicly using Zebra, Honeywell, Datalogic, or similar rugged scanners. Message operations/WMS/IT owners with exact available model and accessory list. Require deposits before acquisition or before splitting a lot. Sell lots, not one-off devices, unless singles are leftover residue.

Economics:
Illustrative lot: acquire 60 exact scanners with mixed accessories at 350-900 PLN per working device equivalent; spend 80-180 PLN per unit on wipe, test, battery sorting, and packing; sell working devices with accessories at 850-1,800 PLN per unit depending on model, condition, accessories, and urgency; target 35-55% gross margin after DOA reserve.

The buyer's value is avoiding a forced device-model change that would delay a go-live, peak season, WMS acceptance, customer onboarding, or shift expansion.

Copy risk:
Copy risk is real from refurb dealers, barcode hardware resellers, warehouse integrators, leasing liquidators, and ecommerce equipment brokers.

The defense is not selling "used scanners." It is controlling coherent exact-model lots before they are split, matching them to live WMS/MDM compatibility demand, and taking deposits before title transfer.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
They can copy the idea, but they cannot sell a specific lot once the founder has the signed supply option or title, serial-number inventory, accessory bundle, wipe/test evidence, buyer deposit, and no-split terms with the seller.

If the founder fails to secure first-look supply and deposit-backed buyers, the idea has no moat and should be killed.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The company takes title or exclusive option over compact, serial-numbered hardware lots and sells them under deposit-backed terms. It is not a buyer-seller introduction marketplace, IT consulting, scanner repair service, WMS implementation, rental desk, or refurb catalog.

The first product is a controlled lot with serials, accessories, wipe/test proof, and buyer deposit.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No payment terminals or PIN pads. No devices with unresolved ownership, lease, theft, MDM lock, carrier lock, or asset lien. No buyer data or warehouse data handling. No security bypass, account unlocking, firmware circumvention, or MDM removal without written owner authority. No promise of OEM warranty. No unsupported safety-critical, medical, aviation, or regulated devices. Clear DOA return window only for tested working units.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not HeritageDoor: this controls compact serial-numbered warehouse devices, not pre-war architectural suites.

Not the failed industrial-spares branch: the buyer is not buying emergency single spares for a machine failure; the buyer is buying a coherent exact-model fleet to avoid WMS/MDM retraining and go-live disruption.

Not hotel FF&E: the lot is compact, high-value per cubic meter, serial-numbered, testable, and deposit-backed before acquisition.

Not POS terminals: payment-processing devices are explicitly excluded.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Used rugged-device refurb dealers already exist and may have better supply, testing, warranty, and buyer trust. Compatibility can still be messy: OS version, firmware, scanner engine, battery health, cradles, charging pins, MDM history, Android Enterprise enrollment, WMS app support, and regional SKUs. Strong buyers may prefer new devices with OEM warranty, financing, and vendor support. Weak buyers may be price-shopping on Allegro, eBay, or refurb catalogs rather than paying for coherent lots. Supply may be inconsistent because clean lease-end lots may already go to established refurb channels. Testing, battery replacement, DOA returns, and accessory matching can eat margin. Inventory risk returns if deposits are not enforced. The business may become a used-equipment dealer unless it sticks to exact-model WMS fleet lots with buyer deposits.
