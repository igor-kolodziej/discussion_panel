## Lead Candidate

**EES CarrierInterface Boarding-Check Release Desk**

### Internal Simulated Score

88.4 / 100

### Why It Clears Simulation

- The timing is live: the carrier obligation started 2026-04-10, not a distant readiness story.
- The interface has web portal and mobile app access, so a small carrier can be onboarded without a full enterprise API project.
- The first proof is operational: a carrier account, named routes, staff workflow, and completed pre-departure checks, not a memo.
- It targets small international coach, ferry, and niche air carriers that may be below the attention of large airline IT vendors.
- The founder can run the first cases part-time through written onboarding, staff scripts, route dry runs, log review, and departure-day support.
- It avoids legal advice and passenger-data custody by keeping carriers as the operator and using only official interface outputs.

### Internal Objections

- Carriers can use official eu-LISA/Frontex guidance and support directly.
- Large airlines and ferry operators already have IT/compliance teams or travel-technology vendors.
- The window could be short if carriers adapt quickly after the first few departures.
- Some carriers may see this as internal operations work and resist paying.
- A refused passenger, wrong check, or misunderstood output can create reputational and legal pressure.
- The startup must avoid immigration advice, carrier-liability advice, passenger eligibility decisions, and data-controller responsibilities.
- If the buyer is not a repeat route operator with multiple staff and departures, the ticket size may be too small.

### Gate Decision

Advance to working Zero To One validation because the simulated score is strictly above 87 and the candidate has a live official operational control point with 60-day proof through completed carrier route checks and paid operator adoption.
