## Zero To One Summary

Zero To One agreed NeuroSpare has a real physical control point: titled donor inventory, first-look rights over closing/upgrading lab lots, serial/compatibility records, biomed checks, and emergency rental/resale terms.

It scored only 72 because the market may be too thin, lumpy, and compatibility-sensitive for strong owner earnings under the founder constraints. The business risks becoming a slow-moving pile of obsolete medical hardware unless demand is pre-reserved before inventory purchase.

## Strongest Objections

1. Demand frequency may be too low.
   - Failures happen, but not necessarily often enough across a small Polish/CEE installed base, on components already controlled, with buyers willing to pay urgently.

2. Wrong inventory destroys the model.
   - Used systems can be commercially useless if the installed base moved on, software license is non-transferable, key cables are missing, revisions differ, or the part is not failure-prone.

3. Software and dongles may matter more than hardware.
   - Useful assets may be licensed workstations, dongles, service passwords, or software versions rather than amplifiers alone.

4. Compliance is real even without patient care.
   - Selling/renting/making medical devices available creates MDR distributor/economic-operator obligations.

5. "Tested working" is insufficient.
   - Buyers need serial number, model/revision, firmware/software compatibility, electrical safety test, known-good system test, photos, accessories, exclusions, return/rental terms, and no-clinical-performance guarantee.

6. Authorized distributors/service firms cap upside.
   - NeuroSpare only wins where the system is obsolete, unsupported, too slow officially, or one local component saves a scheduled testing week.

7. Part-time founder conflicts with emergency SLA.
   - Labs want fast response, invoice, courier, compatibility confirmation, and sometimes after-hours logistics.

8. Standby retainers may be hard to sell.
   - Labs may agree risk exists but refuse to pay before failure.

## Pivot Implications

Do not buy random neurodiagnostic inventory. A viable version would require:

- 40-60 lab/biomed interviews;
- top 5 exact systems/components named by buyers;
- 3 written standby/reservation agreements before expensive inventory;
- one first-look agreement from a closing/upgrading lab;
- legal/MDR memo;
- one paid rental/resale/deposit.

Next candidate: Molecular Cold-Chain Failure + RUO Reagent Salvage Bank, which keeps hard inventory/first-look control but expands demand beyond a small neurodiagnostic installed base and avoids clinical-device compatibility as the main bottleneck.
