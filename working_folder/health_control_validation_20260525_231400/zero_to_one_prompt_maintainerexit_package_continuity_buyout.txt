Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
MaintainerExit Package Continuity Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Buy or option exact open-source package maintainer rights only after dependent B2B software companies prepay for continuity, so the startup controls the trusted package namespace, release channel, maintainer endorsement, and support payment stream before the maintainer exits.

Exact buyer:
B2B SaaS companies, software-product teams, agencies with retained product clients, DevOps teams, API-heavy startups, and vertical software vendors that already rely on a specific small but production-relevant open-source package, SDK wrapper, parser, connector, CLI tool, protocol adapter, or framework plugin.

Acute trigger:
The maintainer is retiring, unresponsive, burned out, or explicitly wants out while dependent companies face a current runtime, framework, API, security, build, browser, package-manager, or buyer-procurement update. The buyer does not need generic open-source support; it needs the exact package it already uses to keep publishing trusted releases under the known namespace.

Control point:
Signed package-rights option or transfer covering GitHub/GitLab repository administration, package-registry namespace and publishing rights for npm, PyPI, Maven, Composer, Packagist, Docker Hub, Terraform Registry, or similar where relevant, release-signing or maintainer keys rotated through a secure handoff, domain/docs/site and issue tracker, changelog, CI, test suite, dependency map, security advisory history, open pull requests, contributor notes, maintainer endorsement and transfer announcement, sponsor/support contact list where legally transferable, and prepaid dependent-company LTS or continuity contracts before meaningful seller payment.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One maintainer signs an option over a package with meaningful production dependents and clean transfer authority. Registry transfer or co-maintainer handoff is rehearsed or completed without publishing-risk ambiguity. Eight dependent companies sign 12-month continuity/LTS contracts or prepaid support blocks totaling at least 60,000 PLN before closing cash is paid to the maintainer. One named senior contractor reviews the repo, CI, tests, release process, dependency risk, and first scoped maintenance plan. First maintenance release, security patch, runtime/framework compatibility release, or clean no-go advisory ships under the controlled namespace. Scope excludes regulated, crypto/security-critical, payment-processing, health/patient, destructive-infra, or classified packages in the first POC.

6-month POC:
Control one to three package namespaces with clean maintainer transfers; convert 20-45 dependent companies into paid continuity contracts; collect 180,000-450,000 PLN in annualized prepaid or contracted revenue; ship at least six scoped maintenance, compatibility, security, or no-go releases/advisories; keep gross margin above 65% after contractor review and maintenance work; reject packages where paying dependents, tests, transfer authority, or maintenance scope are weak; and document the repeatable playbook for package selection, maintainer transfer, paying-dependent conversion, release handoff, security triage, and support boundaries.

First acquisition mechanism:
Mine GitHub/GitLab issues, package registries, dependency graphs, sponsorship pages, maintainer posts, deprecation notices, stale security advisories, and company usage signals for packages with production dependents but tired maintainers. Contact maintainers with a no-speculation offer: the startup will line up dependent-company prepayments first, then pay the maintainer via option fee and earnout from collected continuity contracts. Contact dependent companies with a concrete offer: pay for continuity of the exact package they already use, not a fork or generic support retainer.

Economics:
Charge dependent companies 2,500-12,000 PLN per year for package continuity, LTS patch access, supported-version notices, priority issue triage, buyer-safe maintenance notes, and optional private compatibility patches. A package with 15 paying dependents at 6,000 PLN per year creates 90,000 PLN ARR. Seller payout is an earnout from collected contracts, not speculative purchase. Gross margin can stay above 65% if only packages with small, bounded maintenance surfaces and existing tests are accepted.

Copy risk:
HeroDevs, Tidelift, Snyk, GitHub, OpenCollective, foundations, dev agencies, security firms, and larger user companies can offer open-source support or fork the code. The defense is package-specific control: once the startup controls the trusted namespace, release channel, maintainer endorsement, issue history, and prepaid support stream, competitors cannot instantly publish trusted official releases for that package. They can fork, but the buyer's production dependency and procurement evidence usually prefer the recognized package lineage.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the maintainer signs the package-rights option, dependent companies sign paid continuity contracts, and the package registry handoff is rehearsed or completed, the startup controls the active namespace, release channel, maintainer endorsement, first continuity revenue, and package-specific support queue. A competitor can support other packages or propose a fork, but it cannot instantly become the official maintainer or collect the already-signed continuity contracts for this package.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not sell generic coding hours, dependency reports, vulnerability scans, dashboards, introductions, or a marketplace of maintainers. It buys or options the exact package continuity asset and closes only when dependent companies prepay for that package's official continuity stream.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, security certification, formal penetration testing, software warranty, regulated compliance attestation, malware analysis for unknown packages, escrow, insurance, or fiduciary custody of community funds. Counsel reviews IP/assignment/license issues where needed. Security reviewers handle vulnerability-sensitive releases. Customers remain responsible for their own dependency risk, code integration, deployment, testing, and production acceptance. Reject packages with unclear maintainer authority, disputed IP, malware, abandoned but widely security-critical auth/crypto/payment code, healthcare/patient-data libraries, license contamination, sanctions/export-control issues, toxic community disputes, or no paying dependent companies.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not OneStack Critical Connector Lockbox or Retail EDI continuity. Those buy installed customer-specific connectors and support books. MaintainerExit controls a public package namespace and release channel used across multiple dependent companies, with no customer-specific ERP cleanup unless separately contracted. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, RAGProbe, AIQ, AppExchange, or any recovery/evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Free-riding may kill conversion because many companies use open-source packages without paying. The best packages may already have foundations, corporate maintainers, HeroDevs/Tidelift-style support, or a larger user company willing to take over. Maintainer authority and IP can be messy across contributors, licenses, trademarks, old employers, and registry ownership. Security trust is a serious barrier for an unknown founder controlling release rights. Package users may prefer to fork, migrate, or pin versions rather than pay. Support can sprawl into bespoke integration, customer debugging, or feature work. Contractor review may reveal weak tests, fragile CI, poor code quality, or hidden security debt. A public community may resist commercial takeover. The first package may be too small to prove repeatable owner earnings.
