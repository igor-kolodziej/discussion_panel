Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, patient-data businesses, and prescription-drug businesses without asset/payment-flow control.

Scored idea:

Controlled-Substance In-Person Compliance Conversion Desk buys or options the nonclinical call-routing/admin book of a distressed or transitioning telemedicine clinic/operator that previously handled prescription categories requiring tighter examination controls, and converts that flow into lawful in-person visit capacity for licensed clinics. The startup controls existing patient-contact permissions, clinic phone/domain/mailbox routing, compliant room-day blocks, doctor-session capacity, appointment deposits, record-transfer admin, and completed-visit payment flow.

The startup does not advertise or sell opioids, cannabis, stimulants, benzodiazepines, psychotropics, or any prescription medicines. It does not prescribe, guarantee prescriptions, advise on dosing, triage dependence/withdrawal, or decide treatment. Licensed medical providers and clinics remain providers of record and decide clinically after lawful examination.

Buyer / payer:
- telemedicine/pain/cannabis/psychiatry clinics that need to convert existing remote patient flow into compliant in-person workflows;
- licensed clinics with doctor capacity but weak admin/call conversion;
- existing patients who need lawful continuity visits, not guaranteed medicines;
- clinic owners who want to avoid fines, complaints, and reputation loss from noncompliant remote prescribing.

Acute trigger:
Rules and enforcement pressure around controlled/restricted prescription categories make remote-only prescription renewal risky. Clinics with old telemedicine call books need in-person visit conversion, records transfer, room scheduling, consented outreach, and provider-of-record workflow. If they do not convert quickly, revenue, patient continuity, and compliance are at risk.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is:
- signed option/revenue-share over existing clinic call/admin book, phone/domain/mailbox, CRM queue, and consented reactivation workflow;
- compliant exam-room day blocks in Warsaw and 1-2 regional cities;
- licensed doctor-session capacity under clinic/provider-of-record contracts;
- appointment deposits or prepaid clinic conversion bundle;
- record-transfer/check-in workflow with patient consent;
- scripts that say "lawful in-person medical visit" and never promise prescriptions;
- clinic settlement file and completed-visit payment flow;
- no public drug advertising, no prescription guarantee, no drug sale.

The hard control is: existing clinic admin/call flow + in-person room/doctor slots + appointment deposits + completed-visit payment flow.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN option/revenue-share over one transitioning clinic/operator admin book and phone/mailbox routing;
- 10,000-20,000 PLN legal/medical-advertising/GDPR/records/provider-contract review;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN room-day blocks and check-in/admin staffing;
- 15,000-30,000 PLN prepaid provider/clinic sessions or minimum guarantees;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN call routing, CRM, appointment deposits, and settlement workflow.

Required proof:
1. Signed clinic/operator handoff over existing admin/call flow, not cold patient leads.
2. Legal memo approving outreach scripts, medical-advertising boundary, data roles, records transfer, and provider-of-record structure.
3. Two licensed clinic/provider contracts covering in-person visits, clinical responsibility, deposits, cancellations, and settlement reporting.
4. 6 booked doctor sessions and 3 room-day locations.
5. 100 consent-compliant reactivation calls/messages to existing clinic patients without drug promises.
6. 40 completed in-person visits in 60 days.
7. 25,000+ PLN collected as clinic conversion fees, appointment deposits, or completed-visit admin revenue.
8. No prescription guarantee, no public controlled-drug advertising, no medication sale, no dosing advice, no dependence/withdrawal triage by startup.

Economics:
Revenue comes from clinic-paid conversion/admin fee per completed visit, setup fee for compliant in-person slot conversion, room/doctor scheduling margin where lawful and disclosed, monthly retainer for clinic phone/admin conversion workflow, and later roll-up of distressed telemedicine admin books.

Payback logic:
The founder does not launch a drug clinic. The founder options an existing admin/call book, secures legal scripts and provider contracts, books rooms and doctor sessions, converts 40 lawful in-person visits, and proves payment flow. Closing payment to the seller is earnout-based from completed compliant visits.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
This controls phone/admin flow, room capacity, provider-session capacity, deposits, and completed-visit settlement. It is not a compliance dashboard, lead-gen marketplace, or prescription-drug business.

Copy risk:
Clinics can build their own admin teams and room networks. The wedge exists only if the founder controls an existing clinic call book, phone/domain routing, and near-term in-person capacity before the clinic loses revenue. If there is no acquired admin flow, the score falls below 80.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- No sale, resale, reservation, advertising, delivery, or brokerage of prescription drugs.
- No prescription guarantees.
- Licensed providers and clinics remain providers of record and make all clinical decisions.
- Outreach scripts avoid drug names/promises and say only that an in-person medical visit is available.
- GDPR/medical-record consent and controller/processor roles must be reviewed.
- Counsel must approve medical-advertising, data-transfer, and provider-contract structure before outreach.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Legal/reputation risk is high.
- Patient outreach may be considered medical advertising or improper inducement if handled badly.
- Clinics/providers can internalize if they already have rooms/doctors/admin staff.
- Existing patient data transfer and consent must be clean.
- Providers may refuse the category or demand high fees.
- Any public wording around cannabis/opioids/stimulants/benzos can kill compliance.
