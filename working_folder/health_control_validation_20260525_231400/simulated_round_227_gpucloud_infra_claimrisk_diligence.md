## Advanced Candidate

Idea name: GPUCloud Infra Claim-Risk Diligence Pack

One-sentence thesis: Provide a buyer/lender-paid claim-risk diligence pack for live GPU-cloud, neocloud, AI data-center, or GPU-backed financing deals where the transaction depends on hardware title, power/cooling readiness, utilization, customer contract quality, and gross-margin claims being true.

Exact buyer:

- Private credit funds, PE funds, family offices, venture debt funds, infrastructure lenders, data-center investors, AI-infrastructure SPVs, strategic acquirers, and search funds underwriting small-to-mid GPU-cloud, AI hosting, neocloud, model-serving, high-performance-compute, or GPU-backed collateral deals.
- Best first buyers are not hyperscalers. They are smaller capital allocators that can lose money on one bad 10-100 million PLN equivalent transaction and cannot justify a Big Four or full engineering diligence team for every first-pass deal.

Acute trigger:

- A live loan, lease-financing, SPV investment, GPU-lot purchase, acquisition, revenue-share contract, refinancing, or covenant package depends on claims about GPU inventory, title, serials, delivery status, vendor financing, colocation/power contract, cooling density, network fabric, utilization, customer concentration, take-or-pay terms, resale value, support/warranty, and gross margin.
- The buyer is deciding whether to issue a term sheet, fund a draw, close an acquisition, set covenants, require escrow, reprice, or kill the deal.

Control point:

- A paid diligence mandate over a named live transaction and buyer-approved data room.
- Evidence set: GPU purchase invoices, serial list, photos, warranty/registration status, liens/security interests, vendor financing docs, lease terms, delivery/installation acceptance, colocation or site contract, power allocation, rack density, cooling notes, network architecture, customer contracts, order forms, usage logs, billing exports, cloud marketplace or Stripe invoices where relevant, support tickets, incident logs, utilization dashboards, depreciation/resale assumptions, and borrower/operator explanation.
- Reviewer bench: GPU hardware/infra reviewer, cloud/FinOps/utilization reviewer, data-center power/cooling reviewer, and finance reviewer used only for red flags and memo sections.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

1. Three paid live diligence mandates from investors, lenders, acquirers, or AI-infra SPV sponsors, each tied to a named transaction and decision deadline.
2. 90,000-220,000 PLN collected in fixed diligence fees before delivery.
3. At least two data rooms include GPU serial/invoice/warranty evidence, customer contract evidence, power/colo evidence, and utilization/billing logs.
4. At least one output changes a deal action: lower valuation, additional collateral covenant, escrow/holdback, no-go, delayed funding, amended utilization covenant, required title cleanup, or required customer concentration disclosure.
5. Zero operator-paid promotional reports, zero investment recommendations, zero legal opinions, zero valuation fairness opinions, and zero handling of client funds.

6-month POC:

- Complete 12-25 live claim-risk screens for funds, lenders, acquirers, and SPV sponsors.
- Collect 450,000-1,400,000 PLN in fixed diligence fees.
- Produce 8+ documented decision effects: term-sheet changes, no-go decisions, holdbacks, covenant additions, collateral/title cleanup, repricing, or customer-contract risk flags.
- Build a private claim-risk taxonomy by GPU type, serial/title pattern, power/cooling bottleneck, utilization metric, customer contract structure, resale assumption, warranty issue, vendor financing pattern, lien/collateral issue, and margin bridge.
- Keep gross margin above 65% after reviewer fees and secure data-room tooling.

First acquisition mechanism:

- Start with AI-infrastructure lawyers, private-credit advisers, data-center consultants, GPU financing brokers, and small PE/search-fund communities that see deals but do not want to staff full technical diligence.
- Opening pitch: "When a GPU-cloud or AI-infrastructure deal depends on hardware title, utilization, power, customer contracts, and gross-margin claims, I run a fixed-fee claim-risk screen. I do not value the company, give legal advice, or recommend the investment. I test the claims the lender or buyer is about to fund."

Economics:

- Fixed screen: 25,000-45,000 PLN for a narrow 5-business-day red-flag screen.
- Deep screen: 55,000-95,000 PLN for serial/title/utilization/customer-contract/power and margin bridge review.
- Reviewer cost target: 8,000-30,000 PLN per screen depending on depth.
- Gross margin target: 65-75% after reviewer bench, secure data room, insurance, and admin.
- Payback is credible because one bad GPU collateral, power, utilization, or customer-contract assumption can change millions of PLN equivalent in loan sizing, purchase price, holdback, or covenant structure.

Copy risk:

Technical diligence firms, data-center consultants, Big Four teams, cloud FinOps consultants, GPU brokers, lawyers, accountants, and infrastructure advisers can copy pieces. The defense is a narrow and fast claim-risk taxonomy for small-to-mid AI infrastructure deals where full diligence is too slow or expensive but unchecked claims are dangerous.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:

Once the buyer prepays and grants access to a named data room, the founder controls the exact claim map, evidence request list, reviewer routing, red-flag log, decision memo, and feedback from that live transaction. A larger adviser can compete for the next deal, but cannot inspect that specific data room or influence that decision without being engaged by the buyer.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:

It is not AI consulting, a GPU marketplace, financing brokerage, hardware brokerage, valuation report, dashboard, or generic technical diligence. It accepts only live buyer/lender/acquirer mandates where a specific financing or acquisition decision depends on named GPU-infrastructure claims. The artifact is a transaction-control file: claim ledger, evidence tie-out, red-flag memo, no-go list, covenant/holdback suggestions for the buyer's advisers, and decision-effect record.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- No investment advice, valuation fairness opinion, legal opinion, tax/accounting opinion, lien perfection, securities advice, regulated lending, broker-dealer activity, client-fund custody, hardware custody, or warranty certification.
- Lawyers handle security interests, liens, contract interpretation, IP, investment terms, and regulatory issues.
- Accountants handle revenue recognition, audit, and tax.
- Engineers/operators remain responsible for safety, electrical, cooling, network, and uptime representations.
- The pack rejects classified/defense-sensitive data, sanctioned parties, crypto-mining collateral without lawful documentation, unclear asset authority, fake invoices, undisclosed liens, customer-data access beyond aggregated logs, and promotional operator-paid "diligence."

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

- Not BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack: this is not healthtech, biosignal, medical claim, clinical evidence, wearable data, or patient/clinical-data diligence.
- Not AI Voice-Agent Revenue-Claim Diligence Pack or AI Micro-SaaS Diligence: the asset under review is GPU infrastructure, power/cooling, hardware title, utilization, customer contracts, and collateral economics, not software ARR, call containment, or LLM app behavior.
- Not DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, PromoLeak, or any platform evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:

1. Established technical diligence, data-center engineering, cloud FinOps, accounting, and legal advisers can serve serious buyers.
2. A Warsaw solo founder lacks default credibility unless the reviewer bench is named, the sample memo is strong, and the buyer is small-to-mid market.
3. Operators may not share enough real utilization, customer contracts, financing, or power data before the buyer pays.
4. GPU utilization and margin claims can be noisy, manipulated, or non-comparable across training, inference, batch, reserved, idle, and burst workloads.
5. Hardware title, liens, security interests, and customer contracts require lawyers; the founder must stay in claim-risk and evidence tie-out.
6. Deal flow may concentrate among bigger funds that already use known advisers.
7. Reviewer costs can eat margin if every case needs deep data-center, hardware, legal, cloud, and finance review.
8. AI infrastructure timing could cool quickly if GPU supply, price, or financing sentiment changes.
9. The business is not defensible unless outcome memory and referral channels compound quickly.
10. If buyers treat it as a nice-to-have memo rather than a term-changing artifact, kill it.
