## Reviewer Diagnosis

The reviewer accepted that the regulatory trigger is real: 2.5-3.5t LCVs used for international goods transport/cabotage for hire or reward need smart tachograph version 2 from 1 July 2026.

The idea still failed because it looks like a strong deadline-arbitrage or emergency logistics trade, not a durable company. Demand may be real, but the reviewer did not believe a part-time unknown Warsaw solo founder can reliably control installation capacity before workshops, device suppliers, leasing brokers, fleet advisors, and tachograph providers capture the same panic.

## Strongest Objections

- Likely not a company, mostly a June-August 2026 timed arbitrage.
- Workshops are the true control point; if capacity is scarce they can sell directly.
- Deposit-first cash conversion may be weaker than stated if device/workshop exposure exceeds customer deposits.
- Demand may be overestimated because the rule excludes many vans: domestic-only, own-account, under 2.5t, not hire/reward, already fitted, or shifted to other configurations.
- Part-time execution is mismatched with operational load: VINs, vehicle mass, route/use attestation, workshop confirmations, device availability, calibration certificates, payment timing, cards, cancellations, and documentation.
- The price spread can disappear as workshops raise prices or fleets call direct.
- Liability leaks into the business even if the startup says it is only coordinating capacity.

## Useful Reviewer Suggestions

The reviewer said the strongest version would be much narrower:

Emergency G2V2 retrofit capacity for Polish/CEE cross-border courier vans serving Germany/Benelux, sold only after enforceable workshop slots and device availability are confirmed.

Non-negotiable proof would require:

- 2+ approved workshops signing written slot blocks with cancellation terms.
- At least 20 VIN-specific paid deposits from real fleets.
- Customer deposit terms covering workshop/device exposure.
- A clear per-vehicle margin matrix.
- A contract template that avoids legal-advice and compliance-guarantee liability.

## Lesson

Even with a real statutory deadline and cash deposits, perishable workshop-slot arbitrage is not enough for this evaluator unless the founder controls something more durable than dated capacity. Avoid similar deadline-slot ideas unless the startup can own a recurring post-event payment stream or a legally transferable asset, not just coordinate scarce capacity.
