Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: AsBuilt Archive Retrieval Rights

One-sentence thesis:
Acquire or option retiring architect, engineer, MEP, and small design-office project archives, index them by address and project, and sell owner-authorized retrieval packages of original drawings, as-built plans, calculations, permits, and technical folders when buildings need missing documentation for renovations, tenant fit-outs, insurance, sale, refinancing, legalization, or engineering work.

Exact buyer:
Property owners, facility managers, boutique developers, building administrators, architects, MEP engineers, fire-safety consultants, insurers' survey follow-up teams, and renovation firms responsible for commercial, mixed-use, multifamily, and premium residential buildings where original project documents are missing or incomplete. Best first buyers have a named address and a current documentation blocker.

Acute trigger:
A renovation, tenant fit-out, change of use, building sale, refinancing, fire or insurance review, energy retrofit, legalization, warranty claim, or engineering investigation is delayed because the owner or project team lacks original floor plans, structural drawings, MEP drawings, as-built updates, calculations, permit folders, material specs, commissioning records, or old project correspondence.

Control point:
Written archive acquisition, custody, consignment, or exclusive retrieval agreement from a retiring architect, engineer, MEP designer, or small design office; box/file inventory; address index; copyright/release/scan terms where available; data-handling rules; owner-authorization workflow; and paid retrieval deposits for named addresses. The startup controls the source archive before it is discarded, fragmented, sold as waste paper, stored in an inaccessible basement, or forgotten.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Two written archive options or custody agreements with retiring design offices, engineers, MEP designers, or estate/clear-out sellers.
2. At least 200 project folders or drawing sets indexed by address, year, designer, discipline, and document type.
3. Release rules documented for every archive: who may receive copies, what owner authorization is needed, what copyright limits apply, and what cannot be released.
4. Five paid retrieval deposits from owners, architects, facility managers, or renovation firms for named addresses.
5. At least two delivered retrieval packages with source-document scans and provenance notes.
6. First 15,000-50,000 PLN collected in retrieval, scan, rush, or archive-search fees.

6-month POC:
Month 1: contact 80 retiring architects, engineers, MEP designers, small design offices, building administrators, document-storage vendors, insolvency/estate clear-out sellers, and specialist renovation firms; sign the first archive option or custody agreement; build the indexing and release workflow.
Month 2: control two archives, index at least 200 project folders, and run demand outreach to 100 architects, facility managers, boutique developers, property administrators, and insurers' survey follow-up contacts using a named-address search offer.
Month 3: secure five paid retrieval deposits, deliver two source-document retrieval packages, and document no-hit, no-release, owner-authorization, copyright, and document-quality failure reasons.
Months 4-6: control or option 1,000+ project folders across at least five source archives, complete 20+ paid retrievals, collect 80,000-200,000 PLN in fees, keep gross margin above 60% after scanning/indexing labor, and prove that at least three demand channels send repeat named-address searches.

First acquisition mechanism:
Call retiring architects, structural engineers, MEP designers, small design offices, building administrators, insolvency/estate clear-out firms, document storage vendors, and specialist renovators. The supply offer is: "do not throw away or split old project archives; I pay, remove, index, and only release documents under written rules to documented owners or their authorized project teams." Demand outreach goes to architects, facility managers, boutique developers, insurers' survey follow-up contacts, property administrators, and renovation firms with a named-address search offer.

Economics:
Charge 500-1,500 PLN for address search/no-hit or light retrieval, 2,000-6,000 PLN for a standard drawing/folder retrieval, 6,000-15,000 PLN for rush multi-discipline retrieval, scanning, and provenance pack, and custom pricing for large commercial folders. Archive acquisition and scanning costs are low relative to the delay cost of re-surveying, opening walls, commissioning fresh measurements, delaying a tenant fit-out, delaying a sale, or reconstructing missing technical documentation. The same archive can produce many paid retrievals over years.

Copy risk:
Municipal archives, building owners, original architects, scanning firms, facility managers, and design offices can solve some cases. The defense is source custody over specific private archives, address indexing, release discipline, and paid retrieval memory. Competitors can build their own archives, but they cannot retrieve documents from an archive the startup already controls.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has physical or digital custody, address index, release terms, owner-authorization workflow, and paid retrieval request for a named building, competitors cannot access those specific source documents unless the archive owner or requester switches. The compounding asset is controlled archive depth by city, designer, building type, discipline, address, and retrieval outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is owner-authorized retrieval of controlled source documents from specific archives. The index is internal; the output is scans, copies, and provenance notes from original project folders, not a research report, marketplace listing, dashboard, or generic building-document database. No archive custody or release right means no sale.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify engineering correctness, provide architectural or engineering opinions, provide legal advice, make permit representations, alter drawings, claim documents are current as-built truth, or release documents without documented authorization and release rights. Architects, engineers, lawyers, owners, insurers, and authorities remain responsible for technical, legal, permitting, and compliance decisions. The startup only preserves, indexes, searches, scans, and releases source documents under written rules.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not HeritageDoor because the asset is controlled project documentation, not physical architectural salvage, doors, hardware, or interior suites. The buyer trigger is a missing-document blocker for renovation, fit-out, insurance, sale, or engineering work, not a premium aesthetic desire for matched historical continuity. The proof artifact is a paid source-document retrieval package, not a reserved physical suite. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity, Data Act Machine Data Liberation, GreenTender EPREL, Supplement Stack Safety, BatteryFit, or HeatQuiet.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Archive ownership, copyright, confidentiality, and release rights can be messy.
Municipal or owner-held records may already solve many cases.
Retiring architects may not want to transfer archives or may have contractual duties to clients.
Indexing old boxes can be labor-intensive.
Demand is hard to trigger because owners may not know which designer held the archive.
Some documents may be outdated, incomplete, or not valid as as-built evidence.
Professional liability boundaries must be strict; the startup cannot certify engineering correctness.
Paid retrieval frequency may be too low unless enough archives are controlled.
