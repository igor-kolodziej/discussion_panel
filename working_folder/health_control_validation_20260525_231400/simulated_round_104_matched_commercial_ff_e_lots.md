## Simulated Pass Candidate

### HotelRoom FF&E Package Lot Bank - 89

One-sentence thesis: option or title complete matched hotel-room FF&E packages from renovation and closure deadlines, preserve them as room-equivalent lots, and sell them against buyer deposits to budget accommodation operators that need coherent rooms faster and cheaper than new procurement.

Buyer: budget hotel, hostel, serviced-apartment, student-housing, worker-accommodation, or FF&E installer buyer with a concrete 10-50 room opening/refit deadline.

Acute trigger: a seller must clear rooms before renovation/lease exit/closure, while a buyer must furnish rooms before launch or peak-season occupancy.

Transferable control point: a written first-refusal or purchase option on an exact lot, with a room-by-room inventory appendix and pickup window, followed by title/bill of sale once buyer deposits cover the committed package.

Exactly what is signed/titled/prepaid inside 60 days:
- Seller first-refusal or purchase option for one exact hotel FF&E lot.
- Inventory appendix listing room count, item count, dimensions, condition grade, photos, excluded soft goods, fire/safety labels where applicable, pickup deadline, and access rules.
- Buyer reservation agreement and deposit for exact room-equivalent packages.
- Bill of sale/title transfer or escrowed pickup agreement for the first completed lot.

First acquisition mechanism: start with Warsaw/Krakow/Gdansk/Wroclaw hotel renovation contractors, facility managers, insolvency/closure liquidators, and FF&E installers. Offer a fast removal plan and a whole-room-package bid before the lot is fragmented through auctions.

Gross margin and payback logic: the first proof should avoid warehouse dependence by matching a 10-30 room slice before pickup. Seller pricing is clearance/disposal-driven; buyer pricing is replacement-cost and deadline-driven. A realistic proof can target 25-45% net gross margin after inspection, handling, and transport, with buyer deposits funding most pickup cash.

Copy risk: hotel liquidators, used-furniture dealers, FF&E installers, and buyers can source similar goods. The defense is not general sourcing know-how; it is control of the exact matched lot, the removal window, and buyer deposits before the market splits the package.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset: once the seller option/title covers the exact room packages and buyer deposits reference the same inventory appendix, a later competitor cannot recreate that coherent lot. They can only compete for future hotel closures.

Why this is not a service/report/app/dashboard/marketplace/broker: the founder is not selling a lead list or arranging introductions. The business controls or owns exact goods, takes condition/removal coordination risk, collects deposits, and completes titled package resale.

Reasons this could still fail:
- Used hotel FF&E may be too common, making scarcity weaker than in heritage-building components.
- Mattresses, upholstery, curtains, and electrical fixtures can create hygiene, fire-safety, or warranty objections.
- Logistics and storage can eat the spread if buyer pickup is not locked before removal.
- Liquidators and FF&E dealers already have relationships with hotels and may outbid a solo founder.
- Buyer taste, dimensions, and brand standards can make demand lumpy.

Decision: advance only this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
