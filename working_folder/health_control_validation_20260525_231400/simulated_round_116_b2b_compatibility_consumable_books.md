## Simulated Pass Candidate

### CIJ Date-Coder Consumable Reorder Book Buyout - 88

One-sentence thesis: buy or option a retiring niche continuous-inkjet/date-coder service reseller's live reorder book, exact printer compatibility map, supplier terms, phone/mailbox, and remaining consumable inventory, then transfer customer-approved recurring orders for ink, makeup fluid, wash, filters, nozzles, printheads, and cartridges before closing.

Buyer/payor: food, beverage, cosmetics, household chemicals, pharma packaging, cable, construction-products, and industrial manufacturers using continuous inkjet or similar date/lot coding equipment on production lines. Best first customers are small and mid-sized plants that already buy consumables from the retiring reseller because the reseller knows their exact printer model, fluid compatibility, reorder rhythm, and emergency substitution limits.

Acute trigger: production lines need date, batch, expiry, lot, barcode, or traceability coding every day. Consumables are recurring and compatibility-specific; wrong ink/makeup fluid or missing filters can create print failures, quality holds, wasted batches, or line downtime. The buyer is not being sold prevention; they are already buying these exact consumables.

Transferable control point: seller option covering customer list, last-24-month order history, printer model/serial/SKU map, compatible consumable SKUs, supplier terms, phone/mailbox, reorder calendar, current stock, seller endorsement, and customer-approved payment direction. The founder closes only after customers prepay current-month or next-cycle orders under the new payee.

Exactly what is signed/titled/assigned/prepaid inside 60 days:

- One signed seller option or earnout LOI over a retiring CIJ/date-coder consumable reseller book.
- 20 customer approvals for payment and reorder transfer.
- Printer compatibility map covering at least 60 installed printers or coding lines.
- Titled inventory or supplier back-to-back orders for the exact SKUs needed in the first cycle.
- 50,000-120,000 PLN in prepaid current-month or next-cycle customer orders before final close.
- First 15 orders fulfilled with fewer than 2 compatibility or delivery exceptions.
- Seller warm handoff by phone/email to every transferred customer.

First acquisition mechanism: find small CIJ/date-coder service shops and consumable resellers through Google Maps, Allegro/OLX/B2B listings, industry directories, packaging-service forums, retiring technician networks, and plant-maintenance referrals. The seller pitch is: "I will not value your old contacts; I will pay for live customers only after they approve payment transfer and place current orders." Customer outreach is seller-led and order-specific, not cold selling.

Gross margin and payback logic: consumables can support 25-45% gross margin where compatibility, availability, and emergency delivery matter. The first book target is 20-40 active customers ordering 3,000-15,000 PLN/year each, with 80,000-300,000 PLN annual gross revenue and expansion through exact-SKU stock discipline. Acquisition is paid as a small upfront plus earnout from transferred gross profit, keeping the 100,000 PLN budget safe.

Copy risk: OEMs, authorized distributors, MRO distributors, packaging integrators, and online suppliers can sell many consumables. The defense is not exclusive chemistry; it is current customer-approved payment transfer, exact compatibility memory, seller endorsement, emergency stock for named printers, and refusal to buy stale contacts. Once current orders and printer/SKU maps transfer, competitors cannot capture those specific orders without the customer switching.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: after the founder controls the seller option, phone/mailbox, order history, compatibility map, current inventory, supplier terms, and customer-approved next orders, competitors cannot act on that payment stream unless customers leave. The compounding asset is installed-base compatibility memory by plant, printer model, fluid type, failure history, reorder rhythm, substitute risk, and delivery outcome.

Why this is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: the business is an acquired recurring consumable order stream with titled stock/back-to-back supplier orders and customer-approved payment direction. The proof is fulfilled paid orders for exact compatible consumables, not a report, dashboard, lead list, service visit, or marketplace listing.

Reasons this could still fail:

- OEMs and authorized distributors may recapture customers.
- Some third-party fluids or substitutes may create warranty, quality, or compatibility risk.
- Retiring sellers with clean order books may be rare.
- Customers may approve handoff but then switch when the seller leaves.
- Supplier terms and stock availability may be worse than assumed.
- Plant buyers may demand emergency support or printer repair beyond consumables.
- Gross margin can collapse if products are price-transparent online.
- A part-time founder must avoid becoming a 24/7 line-down printer technician.

Decision: advance this candidate to real Zero To One validation.
