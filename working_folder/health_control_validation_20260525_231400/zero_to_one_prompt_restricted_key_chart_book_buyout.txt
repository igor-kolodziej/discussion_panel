Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Restricted-Key Chart Continuity Book Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Option or buy the restricted-key customer book of a retiring commercial locksmith, then transfer only customer-authorized key/cylinder reorder continuity, chart custody, restricted blank access, and payment direction while qualified locksmiths perform cutting, pinning, and hardware work.

Exact buyer:
Property managers, office buildings, warehouses, schools, campuses, factories, logistics sites, retail chains, housing associations, coworking operators, small hotels, and facility managers already using restricted or master-key mechanical systems installed or maintained by a small commercial locksmith. The source seller is a retiring or overloaded locksmith with key charts, bitting records, authorization lists, restricted blank routes, customer order history, and current reorder invoices.

Acute trigger:
The locksmith who controls the key charts, master-key hierarchy, restricted blank source, and customer authorization workflow is retiring, ill, overloaded, or exiting. Buildings still need authorized duplicate keys, replacement cylinders, rekeys after tenant/staff changes, broken cylinder replacements, additional restricted blanks, and continuity without paying for full rekeying or recreating charts from scratch.

Control point:
Signed seller option over key charts, bitting records, master-key hierarchy, restricted blank stock, cylinder/core records, customer authorization lists, customer contracts and invoices, phone/domain/mailbox, supplier/manufacturer route, seller endorsement, and support history; customer reauthorization for controlled key/cylinder orders and payment direction; secure chart custody; access logs; dual-control release rules; named qualified locksmith contractor route; and no-go rules for unauthorized duplication, unclear authority, compromised systems, or security-sensitive sites outside scope.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign one seller option; verify at least 30 active commercial/building sites with restricted key charts; obtain 10 customer reauthorizations for controlled key/cylinder ordering and payment transfer; complete 50 authorized key, restricted-blank, cylinder, or core orders through a qualified locksmith route; collect at least 25,000 PLN before seller payout; and establish secure chart custody, dual-control access, customer authorization checks, and documented no-go rules.

6-month POC:
Option or acquire 2-3 retiring commercial locksmith books; cover 100-250 active building/customer sites; obtain 60-120 customer reauthorizations; complete 400-1,200 authorized key, restricted-blank, cylinder, core, or rekey-subset orders; collect 180,000-650,000 PLN revenue; maintain gross margin above 45% after locksmith contractor, seller earnout, secure custody, insurance, and admin costs; convert at least 15 larger sites or property managers into annual key-control continuity retainers; and document reauthorization rate, support minutes, no-go reasons, order type, margin, and customer retention by seller book.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to retiring commercial locksmiths, locksmith estates, small security hardware shops, property-manager referrals, door-hardware wholesalers, restricted-key system reps, facility managers, and locksmith associations. The source offer is a low-upfront option plus earnout on retained customers and fulfilled authorized orders. Demand is seller-mediated: the old locksmith endorses the handoff, customers reauthorize key/cylinder orders and payment direction, and the startup accepts only authorized continuity orders under existing chart records.

Economics:
Revenue comes from authorized restricted-key orders, replacement cylinders/cores, small rekey subsets, chart custody/admin fees, and annual key-control continuity retainers. Target 80-250 PLN gross margin per restricted key/order line, 300-1,500 PLN gross margin per cylinder/core/rekey subset, and 1,500-6,000 PLN annual retainers for larger property managers or campuses. Seller payout is mostly earnout on collected orders and retained customers, keeping upfront cash below 100,000 PLN. The founder does not need to be the locksmith if qualified locksmith contractors perform cutting, pinning, hardware, and site work under strict authorization.

Copy risk:
Commercial locksmiths, security integrators, door-hardware suppliers, and property managers can copy pieces or buy future books. The defense is control of specific installed-system charts, authorization records, restricted blank routes, seller endorsement, customer reauthorizations, and current payment direction before competitors see or acquire that exact book.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs the option and transfers the charts, authorization records, restricted blank route, customer handoff, and current order/payment path, another locksmith cannot service the same restricted systems with the same chart custody unless the customer rekeys, reconstructs records, or persuades the seller/customer to unwind the transfer. Competitors can sell new systems or win future rekey projects, but they cannot instantly control the acquired chart book and reauthorized order flow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business is an acquisition-led continuity book around installed restricted-key systems. It does not sell a locksmith marketplace, key-management app, security audit, report, lead list, or generic introductions. It accepts only customer-authorized orders tied to acquired charts, restricted blank access, and transferred payment direction. No chart custody and customer reauthorization means no case.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not perform unauthorized duplication, emergency lockouts, covert entry, security consulting, alarm/CCTV/access-control admin transfer, law-enforcement-sensitive work, safe/deposit-box work, high-security government/hospital/data-center sites, or any order without documented customer authority. Do not promise system security. Qualified locksmith contractors perform cutting, pinning, hardware work, and site work. Use encrypted chart storage, access logs, dual-control release, customer identity/authority checks, secure destruction rules, insurance, and immediate no-go for compromised systems, unclear title, suspicious requests, missing authorization, or seller books with poor record integrity.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from fiscal-device, qualified-signature, tachograph, connector, EDI, route-book, and consumable buyouts because the acquired asset is restricted-key chart custody and installed mechanical key-system continuity, not a provider-issued certificate, regulated service calendar, software support book, or commodity reorder route. It is distinct from physical-lot ideas because the main asset is an operating authorization/chart book plus current payment direction, not inventory resale. It is distinct from recovery mandates and evidence desks because there is no claim or document submission.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Security trust is the main obstacle. Sellers may be unable or unwilling to transfer charts and authorization records. Customers may refuse reauthorization or use the handoff to rekey with a known locksmith. Restricted blank/keyway access may depend on manufacturer or locksmith approvals outside founder control. Commercial locksmith incumbents may be the natural buyers of clean books. The founder must manage sensitive chart custody without being a locksmith. Unauthorized duplication, compromised systems, stolen blanks, insider misuse, unclear customer authority, and poor access logs create serious downside. Support can creep into emergency lockouts, hardware troubleshooting, access-control systems, site visits, and security advice. Per-order ticket sizes may be too low unless larger property managers and recurring sites convert to retainers.
