Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
SEALCloud Procurement Evidence Lane

One-sentence thesis:
A 7-business-day cloud-sovereignty procurement evidence pack for EU/CEE SaaS, cloud, hosting, and managed-service providers whose public-sector, regulated-enterprise, or framework tender is blocked by SEAL-like cloud sovereignty evidence requests.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE cloud infrastructure providers, hosting providers, managed-service providers, sovereign-cloud resellers, cloud-native software vendors, and B2B SaaS vendors bidding for public-sector, regulated-enterprise, EU-institutional, municipal, education, research, or critical-sector contracts where the buyer asks for cloud sovereignty, data sovereignty, operational sovereignty, jurisdictional exposure, supply-chain transparency, technological openness, security, environmental, subcontractor, support-location, key-management, exit/portability, or Cloud Sovereignty Framework/SEAL-style evidence.

Acute trigger:
A tender, procurement questionnaire, framework qualification, renewal, buyer security review, regulated-customer onboarding, or public-sector RFI/RFP asks the provider to prove or map sovereignty controls: EU jurisdiction and ownership/control posture, exposure to non-EU law, data residency, operations and support location, subcontractors/subprocessors, supply-chain transparency, technological openness and portability, key management, auditability, security certifications, incident/support process, environmental evidence, compliance with EU laws, exit/data-return/deletion, and alignment with minimum sovereignty assurance levels. The provider has scattered contracts, cloud-provider statements, security policies, architecture diagrams, subprocessor lists, support/process notes, and certifications, but no buyer-ready evidence pack.

Control point:
Prepaid live procurement case with the exact tender/RFI/questionnaire/renewal request, submission deadline, buyer context, cloud service scope, architecture and data-flow notes, provider contracts, subprocessor/subcontractor list, hosting-region evidence, support/operations evidence, key-management evidence, security/compliance artifacts, exit/portability materials, cloud-provider sovereignty statements, and buyer-approved boundaries. The startup controls the evidence index, sovereignty-objective matrix, SEAL-style response map, gap/no-go split, reviewer notes, buyer response wording, and versioned evidence room for that active procurement event.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three providers or one provider plus two bid/procurement partner channels sign prepaid live-tender cases at 15,000-45,000 PLN each; every case has a named buyer or tender, written sovereignty evidence request, submission deadline inside 30 days, and existing evidence folder; at least two cases produce tender/procurement progress outcomes such as accepted submission, buyer-narrowed follow-up, moved-to-next-stage framework review, cleared security questionnaire section, or clean no-go on missing architecture/control truth; one cloud, security, or procurement partner routes a second case; gross margin stays above 60% after specialist review costs.

6-month POC:
Deliver 20-40 paid SEALCloud evidence packs; collect 500,000-1,500,000 PLN in case fees and urgent tender fees; document 15+ buyer-progress outcomes, 8 no-go/remediation decisions, and 5 repeat customer or partner routes; build a private taxonomy of sovereignty requests by buyer type, sovereignty objective, SEAL-like criterion, cloud provider, jurisdiction issue, key-management pattern, support-location pattern, subcontractor/supply-chain gap, exit/portability gap, and accepted response wording; keep delivery under 7 business days for evidence-ready cases; keep fewer than 25% of cases drifting into legal opinion, architecture migration, cloud implementation, or bid-writing beyond scope.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target EU/CEE cloud providers, hosting providers, MSPs, sovereign-cloud resellers, public-sector SaaS vendors, cloud procurement advisers, security boutiques, and bid consultants. Pitch only live procurement blockers: "If a public-sector, regulated, or framework buyer asks for cloud sovereignty, SEAL-style, data/jurisdiction, support-location, key-management, subcontractor, or exit evidence, send the exact request and existing artifacts. I turn it into a buyer-ready evidence index, sovereignty matrix, response pack, and gap/no-go list in seven business days. No legal opinions, no architecture migration, no generic sovereignty readiness."

Economics:
Charge 15,000-25,000 PLN for a narrow buyer questionnaire or framework evidence pack, 25,000-45,000 PLN for a full tender response pack covering multiple sovereignty objectives, and 45,000-80,000 PLN for urgent multi-service or consortium evidence packs. Pay cloud/security/procurement/legal-boundary reviewers only for flagged sections. The buyer pays because the pack protects a tender, framework qualification, renewal, public-sector contract, or regulated-customer deal where the revenue at risk is materially larger than the fee.

Copy risk:
Cloud consultants, MSPs, bid consultants, security consultancies, law firms, hyperscalers, sovereign-cloud providers, privacy consultants, and GRC tools can copy pieces. The defense is live tender specificity, fast evidence-room assembly, local EU/CEE public-sector and regulated-buyer wording memory, sovereignty-objective mapping, accepted/rejected response patterns, and partner routes from providers and bid/security consultants that do not want to staff this narrow SEAL-style evidence workflow.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the provider signs the paid live case, shares the tender or buyer request, evidence folder, architecture scope, cloud-provider statements, subcontractor files, and submission deadline, the startup controls the versioned evidence room, response matrix, reviewer notes, and buyer-ready wording for that procurement event. A cloud consultant, law firm, MSP, or bid shop would need to displace an active tender evidence process while the submission clock is already running.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only prepaid live procurement, tender, framework, renewal, or buyer-review blockers with written sovereignty evidence requests and submission deadlines. The output is a buyer-ready procurement evidence artifact: sovereignty-objective matrix, SEAL-style response map, jurisdiction/support/subcontractor/key-management/exit evidence index, gap/no-go split, reviewer notes, and response wording for the active buyer event. It is not generic cloud consulting, legal advice, bid writing, architecture migration, cloud implementation, certification, SaaS, dashboard, database, marketplace, or brokered introduction.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, procurement-law advice, sovereignty certification, SEAL certification, cloud architecture redesign, migration implementation, security audit, formal compliance attestation, ISO certification, DORA/NIS2 compliance opinion, GDPR transfer-impact opinion, or guarantees of tender acceptance. The provider, lawyers, DPOs, cloud architects, security officers, procurement advisers, auditors, hyperscalers, sovereign-cloud partners, and buyers remain responsible for legal conclusions, technical architecture, final claims, contracts, security controls, and submission decisions. Cases where the provider lacks the underlying architecture/control truth are marked remediation/no-go rather than papered over.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because it does not handle regulated-finance ICT third-party-risk questionnaires, Article 28/30 DORA evidence, exit plans, subcontracting, audit rights, or bank/insurer supplier renewals. It is not GreenTender EPREL because it does not handle lighting-product energy labels, EPREL entries, or public lighting tenders. It is not Data Act Machine Data Liberation because it does not demand machine data access from OEMs. It is not CBAM, REDBlocked, BioSignal, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BESSGuard, AIGate, RAGProbe, NIS2/KSC OT supplier evidence, CMMC, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Cloud consultants, law firms, MSPs, hyperscalers, security firms, privacy advisers, bid consultants, and sovereign-cloud providers may already own buyer trust. Some providers are structurally unable to meet sovereignty requirements, so no evidence pack can fix non-EU ownership/control exposure, support operations, subcontractor dependencies, key-management gaps, or portability weaknesses. The market may be early outside EU institutions and a few public-sector tenders. Legal and procurement boundaries are sensitive because buyers may ask for conclusions about jurisdiction, non-EU law exposure, contractual controls, and compliance. Tender acceptance remains outside founder control. Public-sector sales cycles may be slow. Case memory may be useful but not a deep moat if frameworks and templates spread quickly. The business can drift into bid writing, legal memo work, cloud migration, security auditing, or generic "sovereign cloud readiness" unless it accepts only live evidence blockers.
