Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EVCharge Commissioning Payment Release

One-sentence thesis:
Commercial EV charger installations are increasingly paid only after the site is demonstrably backend-visible, load-managed, meter/billing-ready, photographed, tested, and handover-complete; the startup controls named final-payment, warranty-activation, fleet go-live, or CPO acceptance holdbacks by producing buyer-approved release/no-go files for installed sites.

Exact buyer:
EV charger installation companies, charger distributors, fleet depot project managers, commercial landlords, and small CPOs in Poland/CEE with installed commercial AC/DC charging sites where final payment, retention, warranty activation, go-live, or CPO/customer acceptance is blocked by commissioning and handover evidence.

Acute trigger:
A site has chargers physically installed, but the buyer, CPO, fleet operator, landlord, distributor, or general contractor will not release final payment, warranty activation, handover acceptance, go-live, or retention because evidence is incomplete: charger serials, OCPP/backend visibility, firmware/settings, RFID/payment test, load-management behavior, MID/meter evidence where relevant, photos, as-built notes, electrical test protocol, handover documents, issue log, or rework trail.

Control point:
The startup controls a signed site-release mandate over named installed chargers and a named blocked payment/go-live/warranty event. Control includes site address, charger model/serials, installer and buyer contacts, amount held or commercial consequence, exact acceptance wording, source evidence owners, permitted communications, versioned issue log, no-go/rework authority, and fee terms. The startup does not install chargers or certify electrical safety; it controls the closeout evidence file and acceptance trail.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, prove two signed site-release mandates covering at least 100,000 PLN of final payment, retention, warranty, or go-live value; one prepaid fixed fee; complete charger serial/site evidence; backend/OCPP visibility or written blocker status; electrical contractor/source-document owner identified; and one accepted release, scheduled payment, warranty activation, go-live approval, or paid no-go/rework decision.

6-month POC:
Complete 12-25 paid site-release/no-go cases across 4-8 installers, distributors, fleets, CPOs, or landlords. Screen at least 1,500,000-5,000,000 PLN of final-payment, warranty, retention, or go-live value. Produce at least 8 payment/go-live/warranty/acceptance outcomes, 6 paid no-go/rework decisions, 180,000-500,000 PLN revenue, 60%+ gross margin after contractor/reviewer costs, and a reusable evidence taxonomy by charger brand, backend, site type, acceptance wording, and failure mode.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target installers and distributors first, not broad property owners. Outreach: "When a commercial charging site is installed but final payment, retention, warranty activation, or CPO go-live is blocked by commissioning evidence, I build the release/no-go file, chase the exact source artifacts, and preserve the buyer acceptance trail. I do not do electrical work, UDT/metrology/legal signoff, charger repair, backend migration, or CPO operations."

Economics:
Charge 4,000-12,000 PLN for a small commercial AC site, 15,000-40,000 PLN for fleet/depot/DC/multi-site handover release, and a small 1-3% success fee only where final-payment or retention release attribution is documented. Reject cases below 50,000 PLN of held payment/go-live value unless the buyer supplies repeated sites. Variable costs are contractor evidence review, secure file handling, site-photo QA, backend/OCPP log review, and source chasing. No software build before repeated evidence patterns appear.

Copy risk:
Installers, charger distributors, CPOs, OEM portals, OCPP platforms, electrical contractors, and project managers can copy checklists. The narrower control point is a signed mandate over a named installed site, exact charger serials, exact final-payment/warranty/go-live blocker, buyer acceptance wording, and release/no-go trail. Case memory compounds around which evidence objects buyers accept by brand, backend, site type, fleet/CPO workflow, and failure mode.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup holds the signed site-release mandate, buyer/installer communication authority, evidence room, issue log, fee terms, and exact payment/go-live/warranty queue, an incumbent can compete for future sites but cannot take over that named closeout file without the parties revoking the mandate. The startup is paid for controlling that specific blocked handover and turning it into accepted release, scheduled payment, warranty activation, go-live, or paid no-go/rework.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It is not an installation company, commissioning engineer, OCPP dashboard, marketplace, warranty administrator, grant consultant, repair service, or broker. The commercial unit is a named installed-site payment/warranty/go-live holdback with signed authority and a release/no-go outcome. The packet exists only to move or reject a specific acceptance, payment, retention, warranty, or go-live event.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide electrical certification, UDT decisions, building-permit closeout, metrology/legal advice, fire-safety signoff, charger repair, installation work, payment-terminal certification, grid-connection decisions, warranty promises, or guarantees of buyer/CPO acceptance. Licensed electricians, installers, distributors, OEMs, backend providers, metrology providers, CPOs, lawyers, and buyers remain responsible for technical, legal, safety, warranty, and commercial decisions. Reject unsafe installs, disputed workmanship claims, missing licensed test protocol, fire/metrology/legal disputes, fraud, falsified photos/logs, and cases where actual repair or configuration work is the main blocker.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
It is not HeatQuiet because there is no heat-pump noise, neighbor complaint, acoustic risk, or distributor noise workflow. It is not the old heat-pump commissioning-passport idea because the asset and gate are EVSE-specific: charger serials, OCPP/backend visibility, RFID/payment tests, load management, fleet/CPO go-live, final payment, and warranty activation. It is not BESSGuard because commercial chargers have higher site volume, clearer backend evidence, and a narrower closeout/payment gate than batteries.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Installers and CPOs already have commissioning checklists and OEM portals. Many blocked cases require real electrical repair, backend configuration, SIM/router fixes, firmware updates, load-management tuning, meter/payment-terminal work, or warranty/OEM action. A packet cannot force the buyer or CPO to accept the site. Unknown founder trust is weak around final payments and technical evidence. The workflow may collapse into project management, punch-list chasing, or low-margin admin. Some sites require licensed electrical, UDT, metrology, fire, or building decisions outside scope. OEMs and backends can automate evidence collection. Part-time execution may not fit urgent go-live deadlines.
