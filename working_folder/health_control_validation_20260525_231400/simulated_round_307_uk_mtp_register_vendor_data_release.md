## External Timing Check

Official timing supports a current UK financial-services third-party-data trigger:

- The FCA published PS26/2 on 18 March 2026 for operational incident and third-party reporting. Its third-party reporting rules require firms to notify the FCA of new or significant changes to material third-party arrangements, maintain a register for those arrangements, and submit it annually. The rules apply from 18 March 2027. Source: https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps26-2-operational-incident-third-party-reporting
- The FCA outsourcing and operational-resilience page says firms are expected to manage outsourcing and third-party providers in a risk-based and proportionate way, with greater risk management when dependence on outsourced and third-party service providers increases. Source: https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/outsourcing-and-operational-resilience
- The UK regulators' critical third-party policy statement says CTP final rules took effect from 1 January 2025 and that firms remain responsible for resilience and management of third-party suppliers under existing outsourcing and operational-resilience rules. Source: https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps24-16-operational-resilience-critical-third-parties-uk-financial-sector

This round is not a DORA retest. The candidate is a UK-regime field-data response product for supplier-side renewal/onboarding blockers created by FCA/PRA material third-party arrangement reporting and operational-resilience data collection. The artifact is a customer-specific material-third-party register data pack, not a DORA Article 28/30 evidence pack.

## Internal Cap Check

- Concrete 6-month control proof exists: prepaid cases with live UK regulated-customer requests, exact material-third-party field requirements, evidence room, buyer-ready field pack, buyer response/progress, no-go logs, and annual refresh intent.
- The first proof is commercial: a renewal, onboarding, customer change approval, or procurement process moves because the vendor returns the specific material-third-party register data pack.
- The trigger is current but not immediately expiring: final rules are published in 2026 and apply in 2027, giving UK regulated customers a reason to start collecting vendor data before the first annual submission cycle.
- Copy risk is high. DORA, cyber, legal, ISO/SOC, GRC, and UK operational-resilience advisers can copy the workflow. This caps the idea unless the product is anchored to exact customer requests and repeated accepted field mappings.
- Duplicate risk with DORA is serious. The candidate only survives simulation because the control point and artifact are materially narrower: UK FCA/PRA material-third-party arrangement register fields, not EU DORA Article 28/30 ICT supplier evidence. If the real evaluator sees this as just another DORA-style evidence pack, it should fail.
- It avoids legal conclusions: the startup maps vendor-provided facts to customer-requested fields and flags "needs counsel/customer/legal owner"; it does not classify materiality, interpret FCA/PRA rules, negotiate contracts, or advise regulated firms.
- It is not a dashboard or generic questionnaire service if intake is limited to written UK regulated-customer data requests tied to a current commercial event.
