Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
WeldRepair Access Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Independent welding-equipment repair shops and industrial maintenance firms will pay to release current paid repair jobs when an OEM, importer, or distributor blocks access to the exact spare part, repair information, diagnostic route, board, software parameter, or service documentation needed to finish the job.

Exact buyer:
Polish and CEE independent welding-equipment repair shops, welding suppliers with service departments, industrial maintenance firms, welding rental fleets, and factory maintenance contractors that already have customer-approved repair jobs on professional welding equipment but are blocked by OEM/importer spare-part or repair-information access.

Acute trigger:
A factory, contractor, rental fleet, welding school, or fabrication shop has a professional welding machine down or unusable. The independent repairer has inspected it and the customer wants repair, but the job is stuck because the repairer cannot obtain the exact board, module, torch/component, service manual, parameter file, diagnostic instruction, repair information, or importer response needed to complete or cleanly abandon the repair. The EU right-to-repair rules must be transposed by 31 July 2026, and welding equipment is already a high-value repair-relevant product category under EU ecodesign repair/spare-part logic.

Control point:
A signed repair-access mandate over one named repair job or repair batch, including customer authorization, repairer diagnosis, model and serial evidence, photos, needed part/information list, existing supplier correspondence, quote or work order, safety boundary notes, manufacturer/importer/distributor contact route, and authority to assemble and send the access request through repairer-approved channels.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days the founder must have three repair-shop partners, at least 12 named blocked welding-equipment repair jobs or 120,000 PLN of quoted repair value under mandate, prepaid access-release or no-go fees from the repair shops, at least four manufacturer/importer/distributor written responses, and at least two objective outcomes: part ordered, repair information supplied, diagnostic route identified, paid no-go/refusal record accepted by the customer, repair completed, or replacement/resale decision made.

6-month POC:
Handle 40-80 repair-access cases from 8-15 independent repair shops or maintenance firms, prove 600,000-1,500,000 PLN of quoted repair value under mandate, achieve 20+ objective outcomes, collect 180,000-500,000 PLN in fixed case fees and outcome fees, maintain gross margin above 60% after a welding-service reviewer and admin support, and build a private access-route library by OEM/importer, model family, part category, request wording, response time, accepted evidence, no-go reason, and repairer follow-up.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with independent welding-equipment repair shops, welding suppliers with service desks, industrial maintenance contractors, and welding rental fleets. The first outreach asks for live blocked repair jobs, not general compliance interest: "If you have a paid welding-machine repair stuck because the OEM, importer, or distributor will not provide a part, service route, board, parameter, diagnostic instruction, or repair information, send the serial, diagnosis, and request trail. I build the access packet and return part/info access, a repair route, customer-accepted no-go, or a documented refusal."

Economics:
Charge 1,500-4,000 PLN prepaid for one blocked repair-access file, 5,000-15,000 PLN for a multi-machine batch, and a 5-12% outcome fee only when a released part/information path lets the repairer invoice a repair that was otherwise blocked. Reject low-value repairs below 8,000 PLN unless bundled. The repair shop remains the repair provider and captures the main repair margin; the startup monetizes access-release, evidence assembly, repeat request memory, and fast no-go decisions.

Copy risk:
Repair shops, welding distributors, OEM service centers, industrial maintenance firms, and equipment dealers can chase parts and repair information themselves. The defensible wedge is the signed queue of named blocked repair jobs across multiple independent repairers, repeat manufacturer/importer request memory, model/part response history, request templates, no-go/refusal evidence, and fast routing before each repairer has to rediscover the path alone.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a repair shop signs the mandate and prepays for a named serial/model repair-access file, the founder controls that request trail, required part/information list, customer authorization, manufacturer/importer correspondence, and no-go/outcome chronology for that job. Another operator can copy the general idea, but cannot complete that exact repair-access file without the repair shop switching, restating the diagnosis, and reopening the access route.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The accepted unit is a current paid repair job with a specific blocked part, information route, diagnostic route, or documented no-go decision. The deliverable is not advice or a database; it is part/information access, a repair route, a written refusal/no-go record, or a released repair outcome tied to that job. The startup does not broker repair customers, sell software, run a marketplace, certify repairs, or perform the repair.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not repair equipment, certify electrical safety, alter safety systems, provide CE conformity decisions, give legal opinions, litigate right-to-repair disputes, bypass locks, reverse engineer firmware, source counterfeit parts, or act as OEM-authorized service. Repair shops remain responsible for diagnosis, workmanship, safety testing, customer communication, warranty boundaries, and final repair decisions. Cases are rejected if they involve medical devices, elevators, payment terminals, lithium battery pack rebuilds, unsafe improvisation, stolen-risk flags, warranty fraud, active litigation, unclear ownership, or requests to alter safety-critical functions.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk because it does not request machine operating data for compressed-air energy audits; it releases blocked industrial repair jobs by obtaining spare parts, repair information, diagnostic routes, or documented no-go outcomes for professional welding equipment. It is distinct from BatteryFit and HeatQuiet because it is not PV/heat-pump retrofit qualification or heat-pump noise complaint evidence. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, PromoLeak, CBAM, GreenTender, Supplement Stack Safety, KSeF, or an Amazon/platform evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Repair shops may already know how to chase parts and information, and OEMs/importers may ignore a third party. The EU right-to-repair timing may not translate into fast practical access for professional welding equipment. Some repairs are blocked because parts are obsolete, unsafe, proprietary, or genuinely unavailable. Case values may be too small unless focused on expensive equipment and batch jobs. Repairers may not want to prepay for admin help before seeing the first result. Manufacturer responses may be slow enough to miss customer deadlines. The founder must avoid drifting into repair advice, legal claims, safety certification, or counterfeit/grey-market sourcing. If repair shops internalize the request templates after a few cases, durability depends on multi-OEM response memory and repeat partner queues.
