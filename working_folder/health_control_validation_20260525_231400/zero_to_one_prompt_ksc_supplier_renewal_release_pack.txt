Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
KSC Supplier-Renewal Release Pack

One-sentence thesis:
Polish and CEE software, MSP, OT, SCADA, industrial IT, remote-maintenance, and specialist integration vendors will pay for a 7-business-day buyer-ready KSC/NIS2 supplier-security release pack when a named renewal, onboarding, PO, payment, or remote-access continuation is blocked by a written cybersecurity evidence request from a Polish critical-sector customer.

Exact buyer:
The buyer is a 20-200 person Polish or CEE vendor that sells software, managed IT, industrial IT, OT/SCADA support, remote maintenance, monitoring, maintenance-management, hospital/municipal/utility workflow software, or specialist integration services to customers in sectors such as water and sewage, district heating, energy, transport, healthcare, public administration, telecom, digital infrastructure, industrial production, or municipal services. The economic buyer is the founder, COO, CTO, security owner, or commercial lead responsible for preserving a renewal, onboarding, support contract, PO, payment, or remote-access permission. Exclude broad custom software houses without recurring product/service contracts, vendors without a live written blocker, vendors trying to claim compliance they do not have, and cases requiring urgent incident response or real security remediation before any honest answer can be submitted.

Acute trigger:
A customer newly covered by, preparing for, or operationalizing KSC/NIS2 supplier-security duties sends a written supplier questionnaire, vendor-risk request, procurement portal task, remote-access review, maintenance-provider review, subcontractor request, incident-contact request, vulnerability-management request, backup/BCP request, access-control request, MFA/logging request, software-update request, or secure-development request before approving renewal, onboarding, PO activation, vendor-master status, payment release, or continued remote support access. The vendor has a real commercial deadline and existing evidence scattered across policies, cloud/admin screenshots, MSP contracts, access-control exports, backup-test notes, incident contacts, vulnerability scans, subprocessor lists, SDLC notes, prior questionnaires, customer contracts, and support procedures.

Control point:
The startup controls a customer-specific release file after the vendor signs a paid mandate and gives the live customer request, named customer and contract/PO context, evidence-folder access, responsible internal owner, response deadline, customer portal/email submission path, and authority to assemble the answer pack. The controlled file includes the original request, evidence index, KSC/NIS2 supply-chain topic map, answerable-now responses, evidence attachments, reviewer notes on risky sections, unsafe-to-answer list, remediation-needed split, legal/security escalation split, submission chronology, customer acknowledgement, follow-up/narrowing response, accepted no-go where applicable, and commercial outcome record.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, close 3 prepaid live cases at 12,000-28,000 PLN each or paid triage plus conversion, all tied to a named renewal, onboarding, PO, payment, or remote-access blocker. Each accepted case must include the actual customer request, contract or PO value context, named response deadline, evidence-folder access, officer approval, and a written rule that the startup will not invent unsupported controls or certify compliance. Submit at least 2 customer-facing packs. Produce at least 1 hard commercial outcome: accepted questionnaire, narrowed follow-up, remote-access continuation, vendor-master progress, renewal/PO movement, payment process movement, or accepted no-go that saves the vendor from making a false claim. Collect at least 40,000 PLN in prepaid fees and keep one named cyber/security reviewer available for flagged sections.

6-month POC:
Build a target list of 250-400 Polish/CEE vendors with public customer references in water, district heating, energy, transport, healthcare, public administration, telecom, digital infrastructure, industrial production, and municipal services. Close 12-20 paid release packs, submit 10 or more customer-facing packs, achieve 6 or more hard commercial outcomes or accepted no-go decisions, collect 180,000-450,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer and secure-handling costs, and produce a reusable private library of request wording, evidence objects, accepted answer structures, no-go reasons, buyer pushbacks, reviewer flags, and renewal outcomes by sector and customer type.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with vendors whose websites, case studies, procurement references, LinkedIn posts, public tenders, or integration pages show customers in Polish or CEE critical sectors. Send precise written outbound: "If a water utility, district-heating company, hospital, transport operator, telecom, municipal body, or industrial customer has sent you a KSC/NIS2 supplier-security request that is blocking renewal, onboarding, payment, or remote access, I turn your existing evidence into a buyer-ready response pack in 7 business days. I do not certify compliance or invent controls." In parallel, recruit referral partners among ISO 27001 advisers, SOC 2 advisers, fractional CISOs, boutique cyber firms, IT lawyers, MSPs, and procurement consultants who want to keep remediation/advisory work but refer urgent response-pack overflow.

Economics:
Charge 3,000-5,000 PLN for paid triage only when the request may be ineligible, and 12,000-28,000 PLN for a fixed-scope release pack. Larger multi-customer renewal sprints can price at 35,000-70,000 PLN. Reviewer cost should be 1,000-4,000 PLN per case for flagged sections rather than a full audit. The buyer pays because a 12,000-28,000 PLN pack is small compared with a 100,000-1,000,000 PLN renewal, maintenance contract, PO, or critical customer relationship. The model dies if it accepts open-ended remediation, generic readiness work, low-value questionnaires, or customers without a live commercial blocker.

Copy risk:
Cybersecurity consultants, ISO 27001 firms, fractional CISOs, GRC platforms, MSPs, law firms, procurement consultants, and in-house security teams can copy generic NIS2 questionnaire help. The defense is narrow case control, speed, local buyer-request memory, partner routing, and a private library of accepted and rejected response patterns for Polish/CEE critical-sector customer requests. The startup must not compete on broad NIS2 readiness; it must own urgent vendor-side release files tied to named renewals, POs, payments, and remote-access continuations.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a vendor signs the paid mandate and gives the live customer request, evidence folder, commercial deadline, responsible owner, and submission path, the controlled object is the specific release case: the customer request, evidence index, risky-answer split, reviewer notes, submission chronology, follow-up history, and fee right. A consultant can sell a future readiness project, but cannot take over the already assembled case without replacing the vendor's active mandate and response timeline. The case library also improves with every buyer pushback and accepted answer, especially in Polish-language and bilingual customer workflows.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
It is not a generic NIS2 readiness assessment, legal memo, cyber audit, compliance dashboard, vendor database, marketplace, or brokered introduction. It only accepts a named commercial blocker and produces a buyer-submitted release file or explicit no-go tied to a renewal, onboarding, PO, payment, vendor-master, or remote-access decision. The proof is not advice consumed internally; it is the customer-facing response trail and the resulting buyer acknowledgement, narrowed follow-up, renewal movement, remote-access continuation, or accepted no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, certify KSC or NIS2 compliance, perform penetration testing, operate security systems, provide managed security, handle incident reporting, negotiate contract clauses, guarantee buyer acceptance, guarantee renewal/payment, or write unsupported statements. The vendor remains responsible for truthful answers, control ownership, customer relationship, legal decisions, and security remediation. Legal counsel handles contract and statutory interpretation. Qualified cyber/security reviewers handle flagged technical sections. Reject incident response, active breach, classified/security-sensitive public-sector material, personal-data-heavy evidence, operational technology safety decisions, false claims, forged screenshots, and requests to hide missing controls.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack. DORA serves vendors selling to regulated-finance customers under DORA/ICT third-party-risk and Article 30-style financial-sector evidence requests. KSC Supplier-Renewal Release Pack serves vendors selling to Polish/CEE non-financial critical-sector and public/industrial customers operationalizing KSC/NIS2 supply-chain security. The buyer trigger, regulatory frame, customer sectors, proof artifacts, and request patterns are different: KSC/NIS2 supplier-security questionnaires, remote-access reviews, maintenance-provider evidence, incident-contact evidence, vulnerability/backups/access-control evidence, and critical-sector procurement blockers rather than bank/insurer DORA renewal packs.

Strongest anticipated objections:
1. Cybersecurity consultants, ISO advisers, fractional CISOs, MSPs, law firms, and GRC platforms already serve NIS2/KSC demand.
2. Many vendors will not have real evidence for MFA, backups, logging, vulnerability management, incident handling, access reviews, SDLC, supplier oversight, or remote-access security.
3. Buyer acceptance is outside the startup's control, and some customers may require full remediation, audit, certification, or contractual commitments.
4. The timing may be uneven because Polish KSC/NIS2 customer processes are still maturing and may not yet create enough paid supplier-side urgency.
5. An unknown solo founder faces trust friction handling security documentation, customer requests, admin screenshots, contracts, and operational evidence.
6. Partner channels may capture the economics or internalize the workflow after seeing it.
7. It may be seen as too close to DORA unless the first cases prove distinct non-financial critical-sector request patterns and repeatable Polish/CEE customer response memory.
8. The business can degrade into generic security-questionnaire outsourcing or remediation consulting unless intake rejects low-value, vague, or unsupported cases.
