Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
COD Courier Remittance Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
A Polish ecommerce cash-recovery desk that signs mandates with high-COD sellers, reconciles courier-collected cash-on-delivery shipments against bank deposits and carrier remittance statements, files evidence-backed recovery cases for late, missing, short-paid, wrongly netted, or orphaned COD amounts, and earns only from recovered remittances or credited balances.

Exact buyer:
Polish ecommerce sellers, Allegro/marketplace merchants, D2C brands, cash-heavy niche shops, and 3PL-served sellers that ship significant domestic cash-on-delivery volume through InPost, DPD, DHL, GLS, Poczta Polska, broker platforms, or multi-carrier aggregators. The first buyer has 500+ COD shipments per month, multiple courier accounts or broker accounts, messy remittance exports, and at least 20,000-150,000 PLN of suspected delayed, missing, short-paid, or unreconciled COD cash in the last 3-12 months.

Acute trigger:
The courier or broker collected payment from recipients but the seller cannot match expected COD amounts to bank deposits. Common blockers include late remittance beyond expected window, split bank transfers, missing parcel IDs in transfer titles, netted COD fees, returned parcels still showing collected, delivered parcels without remittance, remittance statement gaps, wrong COD amount on label, duplicate shipment IDs, courier/broker account migration, charge corrections, or support tickets that die without a documented resolution. The seller wants cash recovered, not a dashboard.

Control point:
Signed recovery mandate plus authorized access to shipment exports, COD amount fields, delivery/payment status, courier/broker statements, bank-deposit exports, label purchase records, return statuses, remittance schedules, support tickets, claim forms, proof of delivery/payment where available, courier account identifiers, seller bank account evidence, and success-fee terms. The startup controls the live COD remittance discrepancy register, evidence packets, courier/broker recovery tickets, status tracking, and success-fee right for that mandate period.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three sellers or two sellers plus one 3PL/ecommerce-accounting partner sign COD recovery mandates; each seller provides at least 3 months of shipment exports, remittance statements, and bank deposits; the startup builds discrepancy registers totaling at least 100,000 PLN in candidate late/missing/short-paid COD remittances; 30-100 evidence-backed courier or broker cases are filed; at least one courier/broker acknowledges a mismatch, issues a correction, pays a missing remittance, credits a seller balance, or documents a no-go reason; the startup invoices or collects the first success fee from recovered or credited cash; gross margin stays above 60% after data-cleanup and support costs.

6-month POC:
Sign 8-15 seller mandates and 2-4 3PL/accounting/referral partners; process 50,000-250,000 COD shipments; identify 500,000-2,000,000 PLN in candidate remittance discrepancies; recover or credit 100,000-400,000 PLN in seller cash/balances; collect 25,000-120,000 PLN in success fees, cleanup fees, and partner-wholesale fees; document win/loss rates by courier, broker, shipment status, remittance age, amount band, return status, transfer-title pattern, and support-ticket path; continue only if recovered cash is not mostly one-off cleanup and at least three sellers route a second month of COD reconciliation.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target sellers and service partners closest to COD pain: ecommerce accountants, 3PLs, Baselinker/IdoSell/Shoper operators, Allegro agencies, shipping brokers, and D2C sellers in categories where customers still use cash-on-delivery. Pitch only current cash recovery: "Send three months of COD shipment exports, carrier remittance statements, and bank deposits. I find parcel-level collected-but-not-matched cash, file evidence-backed remittance cases, and charge only on recovered or credited money. I do not sell a dashboard or parcel-claim service."

Economics:
Charge 15-30% of recovered or credited COD cash, plus optional 1,000-5,000 PLN setup only for badly formatted exports or multi-account migrations. A seller with 40,000 PLN of recovered COD cash can justify a 6,000-12,000 PLN success fee. The founder's work is data reconciliation, evidence packet assembly, courier/broker case filing, and status tracking. Capital need is low because the courier already collected the cash and the client owns the remittance claim.

Copy risk:
Shipping software, Baselinker-style integrations, accounting firms, 3PLs, courier brokers, ecommerce agencies, courier account managers, and seller finance teams can copy pieces. The defense is seller-mandated active discrepancy queues, parcel-level remittance recovery memory by courier/broker, partner referrals from accountants/3PLs who do not want reconciliation labor, and first-cash proof before expanding.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the seller signs the recovery mandate, provides shipment/remittance/bank data, and the startup files evidence-backed COD remittance cases, the startup controls that seller's live discrepancy register, courier/broker ticket trail, and success-fee right for the mandate period. A shipping tool or accountant can build a similar workflow later, but cannot instantly act on the specific filed recovery queue without the seller switching authority.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell a reconciliation dashboard, shipping-rate comparison, parcel-claim service, or consulting report. It accepts only signed recovery mandates with real COD shipment, remittance, and bank data. The output is recovered cash, credited seller balance, courier/broker correction, or documented no-go reason. The proof is first recovered/credited remittance and paid success fee, not a prettier mismatch spreadsheet.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, debt collection litigation, factoring, payment services, courier brokerage, bank-account control, tax/accounting advice, consumer refund handling, or false claims. The seller remains account owner and approves filings. Couriers, brokers, banks, accountants, lawyers, 3PLs, and sellers remain responsible for contracts, bank records, accounting treatment, taxes, refunds, customer disputes, and final claim decisions. The startup rejects cases that require litigation, fraud allegations, consumer disputes, or claims unsupported by shipment/remittance/bank evidence.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak because it does not handle retailer promotion leakage, trade deductions, retail-media charges, or modern-trade remittances. It is not Amazon Vendor Central Recovery because the debtor path is domestic courier/broker COD remittance, not Amazon 1P Vendor Central deductions. It is not a parcel lost/damage claim service because the trigger is collected COD cash not matching remittance, not damage valuation or lost-parcel compensation. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, HeritageDoor, RAGProbe, CyberBind, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many sellers already reconcile COD through shipping software, accounting exports, carrier statements, Baselinker-style workflows, or internal finance. The discrepancy pool may be too small after returns, failed deliveries, bank timing, netted fees, and data-format issues are cleaned up. Couriers and brokers may reject third-party filings or require the seller to act directly. Recoveries may be slow, small, and support-ticket-heavy. High-COD sellers may be low-margin and reluctant to pay setup fees. Data access across multiple courier accounts, broker accounts, stores, 3PLs, and bank accounts can be messy. The business may become low-margin reconciliation labor unless it filters for high-volume sellers with material unreconciled cash and fast courier response paths.
