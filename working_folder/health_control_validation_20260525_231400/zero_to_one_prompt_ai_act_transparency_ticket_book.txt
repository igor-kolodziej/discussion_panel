Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name: AI Act Transparency Retrofit Ticket Book.

One-sentence thesis: acquire prepaid, agency-routed implementation and evidence tickets for B2B SaaS, contact-center, ecommerce, marketing-tech, and web-product vendors whose enterprise or public-sector buyers are blocking launch, renewal, or procurement over AI chatbot disclosure, AI-generated-content transparency, AI-use inventory, provider/deployer boundary evidence, or AI questionnaire answers.

Exact buyer: 20-300 person EU/CEE software vendors, contact-center/chatbot implementers, ecommerce vendors, marketing-tech agencies, and B2B service firms with customer-facing AI chatbots, AI assistants, AI-generated content workflows, or embedded AI features. The payer is the vendor or implementation agency whose buyer asks for AI transparency evidence before contract signature, renewal, rollout, or campaign launch.

Acute trigger: a live buyer, procurement team, public-sector customer, security reviewer, privacy reviewer, brand client, or legal/procurement workflow asks questions such as:

- Where do users see that they are interacting with AI?
- What AI-generated or manipulated content is labeled?
- What chatbot, assistant, content-generation, translation, summarization, or recommendation workflows use AI?
- What data is sent to model providers?
- Who is provider, deployer, reseller, processor, or subprocessor for each AI feature?
- What user notice, escalation, human handoff, logging, retention, and opt-out language exists?
- What evidence can be attached to the procurement file before launch or renewal?

The product focuses on current transparency and buyer-risk evidence. It does not rely on the delayed high-risk AI system deadline as the main urgency.

Control point: the founder controls value only when all of these are present:

- a live buyer questionnaire, procurement request, launch checklist, campaign approval request, or renewal blocker;
- a prepaid ticket from the vendor or its web/privacy/cyber/marketing agency before delivery starts;
- written agency referral or revenue-share terms where the agency routed the case;
- access to the relevant chatbot/content/AI workflow screenshots, product settings, privacy/security answers, subprocessor/model-provider notes, user-facing notices, and buyer deadline;
- a fixed output scope: implementation checklist, user-notice copy, screenshot/evidence packet, AI-use inventory, buyer-answer matrix, residual-risk list, and advisor-review notes;
- reusable accepted/rejected answer memory from buyer responses, not generic AI Act training;
- explicit boundary that lawyers, DPOs, security leads, or customer counsel approve legal positions; the founder does not certify compliance or provide legal advice.

6-month proof:

1. Sign referral/payment-split terms with 3 web agencies, privacy boutiques, cybersecurity questionnaire consultants, or marketing-tech agencies that already serve B2B SaaS and ecommerce vendors.
2. Collect 20 anonymized live buyer/procurement AI-transparency questions or campaign approval checklists.
3. Close 3 prepaid tickets at 8,000-24,000 PLN each.
4. Deliver within 7 business days per ticket: AI-use inventory, disclosure/user-notice edits, screenshot packet, buyer answer matrix, residual-risk list, and advisor-review handoff.
5. Get buyer or agency evidence that at least 2 packets were accepted, advanced to the next procurement step, or unblocked launch/renewal/campaign approval.
6. Build a case-memory file of accepted wording, rejected wording, required screenshots, common buyer objections, and scope boundaries by buyer type.
7. Prove 60%+ gross margin after contractor review, privacy/cyber/legal-adjacent specialist review, project management, and agency referral share.

First acquisition mechanism:

Agency pitch:
"When a client has an AI chatbot, AI content workflow, or AI feature blocked by a buyer questionnaire or launch review, route the live ticket. I turn the workflow screenshots, notices, model-provider notes, subprocessor details, and buyer questions into a buyer-ready transparency evidence packet. You keep the client relationship and receive a referral/revenue share."

Vendor pitch:
"Send the live buyer AI questions and the relevant workflow screenshots. In 7 business days you get the answer matrix, disclosure/user-notice edits, AI-use inventory, evidence screenshots, residual-risk list, and items for your counsel/DPO/security lead to approve. This is for active procurement or launch blockers, not generic AI policy."

Economics:

- Price: 8,000-24,000 PLN per live ticket depending on number of AI workflows, buyer questions, and implementation changes.
- Contractor/reviewer cost: 2,000-7,000 PLN per ticket.
- Agency referral share: 10-25%.
- Target gross margin: 60%+.
- Payback: one paid ticket funds the manual workflow; three tickets prove whether case memory and agency routing reduce delivery time.
- Cash conversion: payment upfront or 70% upfront / 30% on delivery. No unpaid audits or free readiness reviews.

Copy risk:

Privacy lawyers, DPOs, cyber consultants, web agencies, accessibility agencies, and AI consultants can copy parts of this. The wedge is not "AI Act advice." It is fast, agency-routed, prepaid response to live buyer blocks, with reusable accepted/rejected procurement answer memory and implementation evidence across real chatbot/content/AI workflows. Once an agency signs referral terms and a client prepays a current ticket, competitors cannot copy that case before delivery.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, marketplace, or generic broker:

The startup does not sell generic AI policy, training, a dashboard, a marketplace, or readiness consulting. It owns a ticket book of prepaid live buyer blockers and produces implementation-linked evidence: actual workflow inventory, notice edits, screenshot proof, buyer-answer matrix, and advisor handoff. The compounding asset is accepted-case memory from real buyer reviews.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- No legal advice, compliance certification, AI Act conformity assessment, high-risk classification opinion, DPIA, or security audit.
- Lawyers, DPOs, security leads, or customer counsel approve legal interpretations and final commitments.
- The vendor remains responsible for product behavior, user notices, data processing, logs, retention, and model-provider contracts.
- The agency remains client owner where it referred the case.
- The founder does not handle sensitive personal data unless the vendor's DPA/controller-processor structure is explicit; screenshots and configuration evidence are minimized and redacted where possible.
- High-risk AI questions are triaged to legal counsel; the product only organizes evidence and implementation items for approval.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed and unconfirmed ideas:

- Not DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack: this is AI transparency and buyer launch/procurement evidence for AI workflows, not ICT third-party risk under regulated-finance DORA questionnaires.
- Not REDBlocked Amazon Connected-Device Evidence Rescue Desk: no Amazon listing, RED, CE, GPSR, or connected-device evidence.
- Not BioSignal Claim-Risk Diligence Pack: not investor diligence and not health/biomarker claims.
- Not CBAM, EUDR, GreenTender, HeatQuiet, TraceFaktura, or PromoLeak: no import emissions, deforestation, EPREL lighting tenders, heat-pump noise disputes, municipal event billing, or retailer deduction recovery.
- Not a broad AI Act consultancy: it activates only on prepaid live buyer/procurement/campaign tickets with implementation evidence and buyer-response memory.
