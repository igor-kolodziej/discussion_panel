Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
FIFA Training-Reward Recovery Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
Small Polish and CEE football clubs lose or delay FIFA training compensation and solidarity money because player registration history, Electronic Player Passport review, FIFA Clearing House onboarding, KYC, allocation statements, and payment follow-through are admin-heavy; a solo founder can take signed club mandates over named former players and convert missed or stuck training-reward events into allocation statements, onboarding acceptance, payment progress, and success-fee invoices.

Exact buyer:
Small professional, semi-professional, academy, and youth football clubs in Poland and CEE that trained players between ages 12-23 and may be entitled to FIFA training compensation or solidarity contribution when a former player signs a first professional contract, moves internationally, or is transferred for compensation. The best first buyer is a club with former players now visible in professional leagues, a weak admin office, incomplete player-registration archives, and at least one current or recent FIFA Clearing House / Electronic Player Passport / allocation / onboarding event.

Acute trigger:
A former player transfer, first professional registration, domestic/international transfer trigger, provisional Electronic Player Passport review, allocation statement, FIFA Clearing House onboarding email, KYC request, rejected document, payment delay, or club suspicion that a former player moved without the training club receiving its training reward. FIFA Clearing House now centralises training-reward payments based on final EPP and allocation statements, so the key moment is not abstract legal education; it is correcting or supporting the club's official registration/payment path before money is missed, delayed, or misallocated.

Control point:
A club-signed mandate over named former players and named FIFA Clearing House / EPP / training-reward events; club authority letter; player registration periods; old registration cards or federation records; academy agreements where relevant; club identity/KYC documents; bank/payment details submitted by the club; TMS/FCH correspondence supplied by the club; provisional EPP review notes; allocation statement where available; document-submission log; deadline tracker; sports-law escalation rule; and success-fee trigger tied to allocation statement, onboarding acceptance, payment release, or clean no-go. The startup controls the active case file and document chronology, while FIFA, the club, member association, lawyers, and FCH remain the official decision/payment actors.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five clubs sign limited mandates covering named former players and FCH/EPP events; at least 20 former players are screened against public transfer/professional-registration signals and club-provided registration histories; at least five concrete EPP/FCH/onboarding/payment events are opened or already active; two cases produce measurable official progress such as corrected registration history, submitted EPP response, allocation-statement clarification, accepted FCH onboarding/KYC, payment scheduled, or clean no-go; one club receives a training-reward payment or signs a success-fee invoice against an official allocation/payment event; at least 25,000 PLN is collected through fixed dossier fees, retainers, or success fees; disputed sports-law cases are routed to qualified counsel.

6-month POC:
Sign 20-40 club mandates; screen 300-800 former players; open or support 40-80 named FCH/EPP/training-reward cases; obtain 15-30 official progress outcomes; produce 5-12 allocation/payment events or paid clean no-go outcomes; collect 150,000-600,000 PLN from fixed case fees, club retainers, and success fees; build a private former-player monitoring and registration-evidence map for Polish/CEE clubs; maintain strict boundaries that the startup does not litigate, represent clubs before FIFA dispute bodies, hold client funds, act as player agent, or issue legal opinions.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with Polish and CEE clubs whose former academy players are publicly visible in transfer news, professional registration announcements, Transfermarkt-style databases, league rosters, national-team youth records, and international transfer reports. Send a precise offer to club boards, academy directors, and admins: "I will screen your former players for FIFA training-reward triggers, organize your registration evidence, support EPP/FCH document steps, and invoice only a small dossier fee plus success fee when an allocation/payment event progresses. I do not litigate, act as agent, or touch the money."

Economics:
Charge 1,500-4,000 PLN for a former-player trigger screen and registration-evidence audit for one club; 2,000-6,000 PLN per active EPP/FCH case file; 8-18% success fee on training-reward amounts paid or newly allocated where attribution is clean; optional 1,000-3,000 PLN monthly monitoring retainer for clubs with many alumni. FIFA Clearing House pays clubs directly; the startup invoices the club after an agreed official milestone or payment. Costs are founder research, document operations, translation, federation-record requests where allowed, secure intake, and sports-law review only for disputed or uncertain cases.

Copy risk:
Sports lawyers, football agents, club administrators, member associations, federation officials, and specialist sports consultants can copy parts. The defense is signed mandates over named club/player cases, public-transfer monitoring focused on Polish/CEE small clubs, registration-evidence cleanup memory, EPP/FCH process memory, and trust with under-administered clubs that are too small for expensive sports-law firms unless money is clearly at stake.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a club signs the mandate, provides player registration history, grants authority over the active document file, and ties the success fee to a named EPP/FCH/allocation/payment event, another adviser cannot control that specific case without persuading the club to restart the file, duplicate sensitive registration work, and risk missing the official review/payment timeline. Sports lawyers can handle disputes, but the startup controls the administrative payment-path file until the case either progresses, gets paid, or is routed to counsel.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only club-authorized named former-player training-reward cases tied to FIFA Clearing House, EPP, allocation, onboarding, or payment events. The output is not a generic report or dashboard; the proof is official case progress, allocation clarification, accepted onboarding/KYC, payment release, or paid clean no-go. It does not sell player leads, transfer gossip, legal opinions, litigation, agent services, or a marketplace of clubs and lawyers.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as a player agent, football intermediary, lawyer, FIFA representative, payment institution, lender, debt collector, litigation funder, or holder of client funds. It does not file disputed legal claims, interpret FIFA entitlement in contested cases, negotiate transfer contracts, advise players, or contact buying/selling clubs as legal representative. Disputed entitlement, contract interpretation, federation conflict, CAS/FIFA proceedings, sanctions, AML, tax, and legal questions are routed to qualified sports counsel, accountants, the club, the member association, FIFA, or FCH. FIFA Clearing House pays the club directly.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, retailer chargeback recovery, freight audit, OTA settlement recovery, or any platform evidence desk. The buyer is a football club or academy, the trigger is a FIFA training-reward/FCH/EPP event, the control point is a club mandate plus player-registration evidence, and the proof artifact is official EPP/allocation/payment progress. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, BatteryFit, or a health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
FIFA Clearing House automation may already identify many training-reward events, reducing the need for a third-party operator. Strong clubs, agents, sports lawyers, and federations may already manage valuable claims. Small clubs may have messy or missing player registration records. Entitlements are episodic and lumpy rather than recurring. Many public transfer signals will be false positives. Clubs may resist paying success fees if they believe FIFA would have paid anyway. EPP corrections and disputed eligibility can become sports-law work. FCH onboarding, KYC, AML, and sanctions checks may block payment for reasons outside the founder's control. Some clubs may be low-budget, political, or slow to provide documents. The business must prove that the founder can source under-administered but valuable cases before sports lawyers, agents, or club officials capture them.
