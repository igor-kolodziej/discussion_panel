Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
EVSE CPO Switch Exit File Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Property, fleet, hotel, retail, and parking-site owners with installed EV chargers need an owner-authorized exit file to leave an underperforming charge-point operator or backend provider without losing charger identity, OCPP configuration, session history, payout evidence, tariff/roaming setup, warranty records, and migration handoff needed by the next operator.

Exact buyer:
Primary buyer: Polish/CEE site owners with 6-80 installed AC/DC chargers, including fleet depots, hotels, retail parks, parking operators, office parks, logistics sites, residential developers, and property managers.

Channel buyer: new charge-point operators, EVSE installers, OCPP backend providers, and EV charging service companies that want to onboard installed chargers but do not want to spend unpaid weeks reconstructing the old operator's account, device, session, tariff, payout, and configuration state.

Best first buyer has a named existing CPO/backend contract, visible uptime or payout frustration, a contract-end or operator-switch deadline, and a new operator willing to accept a clean exit file.

Acute trigger:
The site owner wants to switch CPO/backend provider, renegotiate a bad revenue-share contract, sell/refinance the site, recover unpaid charging revenue, or hand the chargers to a new operator. The switch is blocked because the owner lacks a clean charger register, serial/asset list, OCPP endpoint/configuration, firmware/backend status, SIM/payment-terminal identifiers, roaming IDs, tariff setup, RFID/user list where relevant, session history, payout statements, pending settlement items, warranty documents, and old-operator handoff trail.

Control point:
The startup controls the owner-signed data/export and operator-switch mandate for a named site and charger cluster. The controlled file includes charger asset register, site ownership/authority, old CPO/backend account details, OCPP/backoffice export request, session and payout ledger, tariff/roaming configuration, SIM/payment-terminal inventory, warranty and installer records, new-CPO intake checklist, data-room access log, migration/no-go tracker, and first migrated charger or accepted no-go proof.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Two site-owner mandates covering at least 20 installed chargers.
2. One new-CPO, OCPP backend, or EVSE installer routing agreement saying what exit-file evidence is needed before onboarding.
3. Two old-operator/backend export requests submitted with owner authorization.
4. One complete exit file with charger register, session/payout ledger, configuration inventory, and new-CPO handoff packet.
5. At least five chargers migrated, accepted for migration, or cleanly no-go'd with written reason.
6. First 30,000-80,000 PLN collected from fixed site fees, new-operator onboarding fees, payout-recovery share, or migration-success fees.
7. Rejection log showing at least 40% of inbound sites rejected for broken hardware, unclear ownership, legal dispute, no new operator, low charger count, no payout/session data, or technical repair as the real blocker.

6-month POC:
Month 1: sign two site-owner mandates and one new-CPO/backend/installer route; build the exit-file schema, authorization wording, secure data room, rejection rules, and OCPP/EVSE contractor bench.
Month 2: complete two exit files covering 20-60 chargers; produce one migrated/accepted/no-go outcome; collect first paid fees.
Months 3-4: process 8-15 sites and 120-350 chargers; document repeated export, payout, session, roaming, tariff, and OCPP configuration blockers by old operator/backend; secure two repeat routing channels.
Months 5-6: process 25-50 paid site clusters and 400-1,200 chargers; produce 150+ migrated, accepted-for-migration, payout-reconciled, or clean no-go charger outcomes; collect 250,000-700,000 PLN revenue; keep gross margin above 60% after OCPP/EVSE contractors; prove that at least three new-CPO/backend/installer partners route repeat sites.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to new CPOs, EVSE installers, OCPP backend providers, property managers, hotel groups, fleet depot owners, retail parks, and parking operators using a specific operator-switch message: "If a site wants to leave its current CPO/backend but cannot hand you a clean charger register, OCPP/session/payout export, tariff/roaming setup, and warranty file, we produce the owner-authorized exit file and migration/no-go packet so you can decide quickly."

Economics:
Charge 6,000-12,000 PLN for a small site exit file, 15,000-35,000 PLN for multi-site or DC-charger clusters, or 400-1,200 PLN per charger with minimum fees. Add a capped success fee only for recovered old-operator payouts or completed migration batches. New operators can also pay onboarding fees when the file converts an otherwise messy installed-site lead into a migration-ready account. The economic value is avoided revenue leakage, faster CPO switch, recovered payout evidence, and reduced unpaid onboarding labor for the new operator.

Copy risk:
Old CPOs, new CPOs, backend providers, EVSE installers, OCPP consultants, lawyers, and property managers can copy pieces of the workflow. The defense is owner authorization over the exact site account, structured charger/session/payout export memory, new-CPO intake standards, repeated old-operator/backend patterns, and a growing library of migration/no-go reasons. The old operator often has no incentive to make exit easy; the new operator wants the site but may not want unpaid reconstruction work.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the owner-signed mandate, charger cluster, old-CPO account, export request, payout/session ledger, configuration inventory, and new-CPO handoff path, competitors cannot work that exact site without re-obtaining owner authority and rebuilding the exit file. The startup's control is over the specific site-account migration and payment/session truth, not over generic EV charging advice.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The product is a controlled owner-authorized EVSE exit file that changes operator-switch readiness for a named charger cluster. The output is a charger register, OCPP/backend export, session/payout ledger, configuration inventory, new-CPO handoff packet, migration tracker, payout-recovery evidence, and migrated/accepted/no-go result. It is not a dashboard, marketplace, lead-gen list, generic EV charging consultancy, commissioning service, or software subscription.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not act as electricity seller, charge-point operator, payment-service provider, electrical contractor, metrology/UDT certifier, warranty authority, lawyer, or Data Act enforcer. Qualified EVSE/OCPP contractors handle technical checks. Lawyers handle disputed contracts or formal legal enforcement. New CPOs handle operator acceptance, tariffs, roaming, customer terms, payments, and ongoing operations. Reject sites where the real blocker is broken hardware, electrical defects, unsafe installation, active litigation, unpaid debts, unclear site ownership, missing consent, payment-terminal certification, tax questions, or regulatory compliance outside data/export handoff.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not Data Act Machine Data Liberation because the controlled object is an EV charger site-account/operator-switch exit file and charging-revenue/session stream, not compressed-air machine telemetry for energy-savings evidence. It is not BatteryFit or HeatQuiet because it does not sell PV/battery retrofit qualification or heat-pump noise evidence. It is not TraceFaktura, DORA, REDBlocked, PromoLeak, CBAM, GreenTender EPREL, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, Supplement Stack Safety, or any Amazon/platform evidence desk.

Strongest anticipated objections:
New CPOs and backend providers may decide to build this internally once they see repeated migration pain.
Old CPOs or backend providers may delay, refuse, or provide incomplete exports unless legal pressure is applied.
Some sites are not data-exit problems; they may have broken chargers, bad wiring, missing UDT/metrology status, unsupported firmware, SIM failures, payment-terminal defects, or bad installation records.
Site owners may lack authority if chargers are leased, financed, subsidized, tenant-owned, or bundled into a broader property contract.
Data Act rights may not cleanly solve every OCPP, payout, roaming, or configuration export request.
Values may be too small for sites with only a few low-use AC chargers.
Session and payout data may be commercially sensitive and require strong security, NDA, retention, and access controls.
Migration success remains outside the startup's control because the new operator, hardware, firmware, network, payment setup, and site access all matter.
The business can drift into EVSE repair, operator brokerage, legal dispute handling, or charging-network operations unless intake boundaries are strict.
