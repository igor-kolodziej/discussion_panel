Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
SwitchCost Data Act Exit-Fee Release Mandate

One-sentence thesis:
A current-money Data Act switching-charge desk for EU cloud, hosting, backup, archive, SaaS, and managed-service customers whose migration, renewal exit, acquisition diligence, or cost-reduction project is blocked by a provider's quoted export fee, switching charge, data-release delay, or migration-support charge that may exceed what is defensible under the EU Data Act's data-processing-service switching rules.

Exact buyer:
EU SMEs and mid-market companies, MSP clients, ecommerce operators, SaaS customers, regulated-but-not-bank-heavy businesses, private-equity portfolio companies, and IT consultancies managing migrations away from cloud, hosting, backup, archive, collaboration, ticketing, CRM, ERP-lite, log-management, or other data-processing services. The first buyer has a named provider, live contract or renewal, current migration project, written exit/export quote or invoice, support ticket, data-release plan, and a commercial reason to switch now. Exclude hyperscaler enterprise migrations already owned by major FinOps firms, disputes requiring litigation, public-sector mega-projects, health/patient-data systems, and cases where the startup would perform the technical migration.

Acute trigger:
The customer is trying to leave, downsize, replatform, bring data in-house, switch providers, satisfy acquisition diligence, avoid auto-renewal, move an archive, or complete an MSP handoff. The incumbent provider quotes switching or export fees, charges for extraction tools, withholds or delays exports, invoices migration-support work, refuses standard-format delivery, or applies unclear data egress/transfer charges. The buyer pays because a current invoice, provider ticket, migration deadline, auto-renewal, M&A diligence item, or customer commitment is stuck now.

Control point:
Signed recovery/release mandate over the named provider exit; customer authorization; current contract and amendment stack; renewal/termination notice; provider quote, invoice, or fee schedule; support-ticket history; data categories and export scope; technical migration owner; requested export format; direct-cost explanation requested from provider; Data Act switching-charge issue log; counsel escalation boundary; provider correspondence tracker; revised-charge/credit/export-delivery/no-go ledger; and customer-approved response wording. The startup controls the active charge/export-release file, provider evidence packet, deadline tracker, and fee right for that specific exit event. It does not control the provider, technical migration, legal interpretation, or final payment decision.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Five paid live mandates at 8,000-25,000 PLN each with written provider quotes, invoices, support tickets, or export-delay evidence tied to a current migration or renewal exit. At least two MSPs, IT consultants, cloud-migration advisers, or boutique tech/privacy law firms sign referral routes. At least three cases produce provider-written outcomes: reduced switching charge, withdrawn export fee, credit note, revised invoice, accepted export schedule, completed standard-format export, migration deadline progress, or paid clean no-go because the fee is contractual/defensible or the case requires counsel-led dispute work. The startup collects at least 70,000 PLN within 60 days and records only provider-confirmed cash/export outcomes, not estimated savings.

6-month POC:
Process 35-70 live exit-fee/export-release cases; collect 450,000-1,300,000 PLN in setup, release, and outcome fees; document 25+ provider-confirmed outcomes or clean no-go decisions; prove at least 8 repeat referral partners among MSPs, migration consultants, boutique privacy/tech law firms, private-equity operating partners, and cloud-cost advisers; build a private taxonomy of provider fee wording, direct-cost explanations, export formats, contract clauses, ticket paths, successful reduction language, no-go triggers, and data-processing-service categories; keep gross margin above 60% after legal/cloud reviewer cost.

First acquisition mechanism:
Target MSPs, IT migration consultants, cloud-cost advisers, boutique tech/privacy law firms, PE operating partners, and data-archive/export vendors. Partner pitch: "When your customer is leaving a cloud, hosting, backup, archive, or SaaS provider and the provider quotes an exit/export/switching charge or stalls the data release, route the charge-release file to me. You keep the migration and legal work. I reconstruct the provider fee, request the direct-cost basis, package the Data Act switching-charge issue, track the ticket, and return provider-confirmed reduction, export progress, or no-go." Direct outreach targets companies publicly hiring for migrations, vendor exits, cost-reduction programs, or PE-backed system consolidations.

Economics:
Charge 8,000-12,000 PLN for screened exit-fee triage and provider-response package, 15,000-25,000 PLN for one-provider charge/export release, and 25,000-60,000 PLN for urgent multi-system or acquisition-deadline exits. Add 8-18% success fee only on provider-confirmed credit notes, revised invoices, withdrawn fees, cash refunds, or separately confirmed charge reductions above the fixed fee. The buyer pays because a 15,000-40,000 PLN file is rational when a provider is charging 50,000-500,000 PLN for export/switching work or when a migration/acquisition deadline is blocked.

Copy risk:
Cloud migration firms, MSPs, FinOps consultants, law firms, privacy consultants, procurement advisers, data-archive vendors, and provider account teams can copy pieces. The defense is not generic Data Act knowledge. It is partner-routed live exit-fee cases, provider-specific fee wording, direct-cost response memory, export-format paths, successful reduction language, no-go discipline, and a growing ledger of provider-confirmed charge/export outcomes.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the customer signs the mandate, shares the provider quote/invoice/ticket, authorizes the response path, and pays the case fee, the startup controls the current exit-charge file, provider correspondence tracker, evidence packet, and fee right for that exact migration deadline. A law firm, MSP, or FinOps consultant can compete generally, but it must displace an already-authorized provider response file while the customer's renewal, migration, or diligence clock is running.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business refuses generic Data Act readiness, contract review, cloud migration, FinOps dashboards, SaaS procurement, litigation, and provider introductions. It accepts only live provider exits with written charge/export friction and a current commercial deadline. The deliverable is a charge/export-release file: contract/fee evidence map, direct-cost request, provider ticket chronology, customer-approved response wording, counsel-escalation flag, revised-charge/export/no-go ledger, and outcome proof for the named exit.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, interpret contracts as counsel, litigate, threaten regulators, perform cloud migration, handle production credentials, move customer data, operate as MSP, advise on privacy transfer law, or guarantee provider reduction/export acceptance. Lawyers handle legal interpretation and dispute escalation. MSPs, migration consultants, internal IT, and cloud providers handle technical migration and data transfer. The customer remains responsible for termination notices, contract decisions, data protection, security, business-continuity risk, and final payment choices. Cases involving health/patient data, criminal/legal discovery, suspected breach, insolvency, sanctions, illegal content, or active litigation are declined or routed to qualified counsel.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk because it does not handle connected-product or industrial-machine telemetry, compressed-air systems, OEM/controller export playbooks, logger fallback, energy-savings memos, or independent service-company channels. It uses a different Data Act chapter, buyer, trigger, proof, and economics: cloud/data-processing-service switching charges, export delays, provider tickets, revised invoices, credit notes, and migration-release outcomes. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, KSeF, cloud marketplace remittance recovery, cyber-insurance evidence, AIGate, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Law firms, MSPs, migration consultants, FinOps firms, and procurement advisers may already own the workflow. Many fees may be contractually valid direct costs or standard service/termination fees outside the switching-charge argument. Large cloud customers may already have provider programs, account teams, or enterprise counsel. Small SaaS exits may be too low-value to support the fee. Providers may slow-walk responses or refuse third-party correspondence. Applicability of the Data Act's data-processing-service rules can be legally sensitive and counsel-dependent. The January 2027 no-switching-charge deadline may make 2026 cases transitional and time-limited. Customer data exports create security, privacy, and continuity concerns even if the startup does not move data. The work can drift into legal disputes, cloud migration, FinOps, contract negotiation, or generic vendor-exit consulting if live charge/export mandates are not enforced.
