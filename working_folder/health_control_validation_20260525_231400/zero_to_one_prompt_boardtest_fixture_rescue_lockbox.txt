Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
BoardTest Fixture Rescue Lockbox

One-sentence thesis:
Option or take custody of exact electronics board test-fixture packages stranded at exiting EMS/contract manufacturers or retiring test engineers, then transfer them to the authorized OEM or new EMS only after buyer deposit, ownership proof, and one accepted handoff or test-run proof.

Exact buyer:
Small and mid-sized electronics OEMs, industrial IoT makers, appliance/control-board brands, machinery electronics suppliers, medical-adjacent non-clinical electronics makers, automotive-adjacent non-safety electronics suppliers, and new EMS providers whose repeat PCB assembly build, production transfer, warranty/service run, or customer shipment is blocked because an existing ICT bed-of-nails fixture, functional-test jig, programming jig, adapters, harnesses, scripts, golden unit, fixture CAD/BOM, or test runbook is held by an old EMS, closing supplier, retiring test engineer, or liquidation site. Safety-critical aerospace, automotive safety, implantable/clinical, defense-classified, and regulated final-release cases are excluded unless the OEM and qualified provider own all validation decisions.

Acute trigger:
The old EMS, test engineer, or small electronics supplier is closing, refusing ongoing support, being acquired, liquidating, retiring, or scrapping fixtures, while the OEM or new EMS has a live PO, repeat build, transfer deadline, warranty-support run, customer shipment, or line restart that would require either rescuing the old fixture package or spending weeks and much more money rebuilding and revalidating it.

Control point:
Seller-side custody agreement, option, or title path over a named fixture package; OEM written ownership or release authorization; fixture photos and serial/asset tags where present; board/product identifiers; fixture CAD/BOM where transferable; ICT/FCT/programming scripts; adapters, probes, cables, nests, harnesses, power supplies, dongles, golden unit/control board, pass/fail logs, runbook, version notes, and last-known-good test evidence; buyer-side deposit or escrow instruction; qualified electronics/test engineer inspection; chain-of-custody file; data/IP/confidentiality review; no-certification/no-warranty terms; and handoff acceptance criteria. The startup controls the exact physical/digital fixture package and buyer reservation, not the customer's production validation or product-quality decision.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Two seller-side fixture custody or option agreements covering named board-test packages from exiting EMS/test-engineer sources; two OEM or new-EMS buyer deposits or escrow instructions totaling at least 60,000-180,000 PLN for named fixture rescue/transfer packages; written OEM ownership or authorization for every package; one qualified test engineer inspection per accepted package; chain-of-custody photos and file inventory; at least one package transferred to the buyer or new EMS with a documented powered-on inspection, dry run, golden-unit run, or accepted handoff checklist; no seller payout before buyer deposit and ownership/authorization review; and gross margin above 50% after inspection, transport, legal/IP review, data handling, and seller option costs.

6-month POC:
Source 20-40 candidate fixture packages from EMS closures, retiring test engineers, small electronics suppliers, liquidation sources, and OEM transfer pain; accept only 8-15 packages with clean ownership/authorization and buyer deposits; complete 5-10 paid transfers; collect 300,000-900,000 PLN in rescue fees, option spreads, transfer fees, and inspection/handoff fees; document rebuild-cost avoidance, time saved, rejected ownership cases, fixture condition failures, missing-script failures, license blockers, buyer acceptance criteria, and repeat channels; and continue only if at least three channels produce a second qualified case and no package requires the startup to certify board quality or perform production release.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start from CEE electronics clusters, EMS closure/liquidation signals, small contract manufacturers, test-fixture builders, retiring electronics test engineers, PCB assembly brokers, industrial electronics OEMs, and new EMS onboarding teams. Seller-side pitch: "Do not scrap or auction customer-specific board-test fixtures blindly. I option only named packages with OEM authorization and buyer money, inventory the fixture/scripts/golden unit, and pay only after the authorized buyer accepts the transfer path." Buyer-side pitch: "If a repeat board build or EMS transfer is blocked because the old fixture, scripts, adapters, or golden unit are stranded, I secure the exact package before it disappears and hand it to your chosen EMS/test engineer under a documented custody file."

Economics:
Charge buyers 15,000-45,000 PLN for simple fixture custody release and handoff, 45,000-120,000 PLN for multi-fixture or script-heavy packages, and 5,000-20,000 PLN for inspection/inventory/no-go packages where the rescue is rejected. Seller option costs are 1,000-10,000 PLN per package until buyer deposit clears, with seller payout or success share only on accepted transfers. Buyer ROI comes from avoiding a 40,000-250,000 PLN rebuild, weeks of test development, delayed EMS transfer, missed shipment, or field-service parts shortage. Founder capital stays under 100,000 PLN because no package is purchased without buyer deposit or escrow instruction.

Copy risk:
EMS providers, electronics test engineers, test-fixture builders, production-transfer consultants, liquidators, and OEM sourcing teams can copy pieces. The defense is transaction-specific control: once the startup has seller custody/option, OEM authorization, chain-of-custody inventory, qualified inspection, and buyer deposit for a named fixture package, another provider cannot obtain that exact physical/digital package without displacing signed parties. The compounding asset is a map of EMS exits, retiring test engineers, fixture-builder contacts, accepted handoff checklists, rejected ownership patterns, and OEM/new-EMS transfer pain.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
The startup controls the exact package before broad salvage or rebuild: the physical fixture, harnesses, adapters, scripts, golden unit, runbook, custody file, OEM authorization, and buyer deposit are all tied to named boards and a named production-transfer event. A test engineer or EMS can rebuild later, but it cannot instantly possess the already-optioned fixture package or collect the buyer deposit unless the seller or buyer switches out of the signed custody workflow.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell test engineering, fixture design, production validation, QA certification, consulting, software, listings, or introductions. It accepts only named fixture packages where seller custody/title, OEM authorization, buyer deposit, qualified inspection, file inventory, and handoff acceptance can be controlled. The paid artifact is a transferred production asset package and custody file, not a memo or matching service.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not certify products, validate production tests, approve boards for shipment, provide safety-critical engineering signoff, reverse-engineer firmware, bypass license terms, handle classified/defense data, or alter test scripts except through the buyer's qualified provider. OEMs, new EMS providers, qualified electronics/test engineers, quality managers, and legal/IP owners remain responsible for production validation, product quality, safety, compliance, customer acceptance, firmware rights, and final release. Reject cases with unclear ownership, no OEM authorization, disputed IP, unavailable scripts, missing golden unit where required, safety-critical validation, export-control/classified data, medical/clinical final-release dependency, or buyer unwillingness to deposit against the exact package.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM Import Continuity Desk, Data Act Machine Data Liberation Desk, GreenTender EPREL Bid Packs, Supplement Stack Safety, HeatQuiet, OrphanMold, CMM Program Continuity, Factory Control Spares, OneStack, CleanRIPE, ShowComms, UCO Locked Route, FuelEU pooling, Amazon Vendor Central recovery, or any health/Rx/clinical route. The buyer, trigger, control point, payment event, and proof artifact are materially different: exact electronics board-test fixture custody and transfer for a live production handoff, not tooling last-run manufacturing, metrology support, evidence response, physical salvage resale, software payment-stream buyout, or claim recovery.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many fixtures may already belong to the OEM, so the startup may be unnecessary if the OEM can demand return directly. Ownership, IP, firmware image, license, script, and customer-data rights may be unclear or disputed. A fixture can be useless if probes, harnesses, power supplies, scripts, golden units, dongles, or last-known-good logs are missing. Qualified test engineers, EMS providers, or fixture builders may be the real trust owners. Buyer deposits may be hard before the package is inspected. Some fixtures are cheaper to rebuild than rescue. The founder must avoid certifying product quality or becoming responsible for faulty tests. Case flow may be irregular because EMS closures and painful transfers are episodic. Incumbent EMS providers and test-fixture builders can offer rebuilds, while liquidators may not cooperate with a tiny option workflow.
