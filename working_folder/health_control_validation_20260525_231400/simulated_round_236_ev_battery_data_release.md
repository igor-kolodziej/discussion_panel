## Source Timing Notes

Official EU Data Act materials say the Data Act became applicable on 12 September 2025 and gives individuals and businesses rights to access and share data generated by connected products, including cars and industrial machinery. The official Commission news page explicitly names cars among connected devices whose data can support aftermarket services.

This round tries to use that already-active data-access right in a high-value, non-clinical, cash-linked setting: used EV lease-end, trade-in, remarketing, warranty, and dealer purchase decisions where battery state-of-health, warranty, charging, and error-history evidence materially affects price or payment.

## Internal Objections Before Real Gate

- Duplicate risk is real with both BatteryFit and Data Act Machine Data Liberation. This must be positioned around EV used-asset payment events and VIN-level buyer files, not PV battery retrofit sales or industrial machine data access.
- Existing battery-certification, inspection, and used-car appraisal providers are strong substitutes.
- OEMs may resist, narrow, delay, or provide incomplete connected-vehicle data.
- OBD/diagnostic tests may already solve many cases, weakening the Data Act angle.
- Buyer willingness depends on EV value spread, fleet batch size, and whether the packet changes actual sale/lease-end price.
- Vehicle data can include personal trip/location data; the product must minimize and exclude irrelevant personal data.
- The founder cannot certify safety, warranty eligibility, or battery condition; reviewers can only assemble a buyer-ready evidence file.
