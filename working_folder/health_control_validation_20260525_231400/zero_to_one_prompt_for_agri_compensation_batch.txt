Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
FOR Agri Compensation Batch Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Run producer-group and cooperative batch operations for Polish Agricultural Protection Fund compensation applications when farmers sold agricultural products to an insolvent buyer and were not paid, with fees tied to filed, accepted, status-advanced, or paid compensation claims.

Exact buyer:
Polish producer groups, agricultural cooperatives, farm accountants, grower clusters, and authorized administrators representing multiple agricultural producers with unpaid invoices from one insolvent buyer that bought, stored, treated, or processed agricultural products. The best first buyer has many member claims from one buyer insolvency event, documented delivery/invoice records, and enough unpaid value to justify a batch fee.

Acute trigger:
New rules have applied since 13 May 2026 and add a second annual application window for Fundusz Ochrony Rolnictwa compensation, with applications possible from 1 February to 31 March and from 1 July to 31 August. A producer group or co-op has members who were not paid by an insolvent agricultural buyer and must assemble invoice, delivery, producer, product, unpaid-balance, authority, and bank files before the window closes.

Control point:
Signed mandate from the producer group, cooperative, accountant, or authorized administrator; member authorizations where required; named insolvent buyer event; invoice and delivery records; product, quantity, date, due-date, unpaid balance, producer identity, bank details, and supporting files; KOWR/FOR application tracker; deficiency response log; status evidence; and compensation payment tracker. The startup does not collect the compensation funds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Three group/co-op/administrator mandates; at least 80 producer/invoice files screened for one or more named insolvent buyers; thirty compensation applications filed or prepared for the next application window; two accepted/status-advanced applications, deficiency narrowings, or official no-go outcomes; 40,000-100,000 PLN collected in batch fees and success-linked receivables; one agri accountant or compensation-program reviewer retained.

6-month POC:
Control 10-20 group or administrator mandates; screen 500-1,500 producer/invoice files; file or prepare 250-800 applications; document 80+ accepted, status-advanced, payment, deficiency-narrowing, or official no-go outcomes; collect 180,000-600,000 PLN revenue; secure three repeat producer-group, co-op, or accountant channels; maintain gross margin above 60%; kill if most cases are one-off low-value individual applications or if official free help and local advisers remove willingness to pay.

First acquisition mechanism:
Start with producer groups, cooperatives, agri accountants, grower associations, and clusters affected by a named insolvent buyer. The outreach asks for one concrete unpaid-buyer event, not general grant help: "If one buyer insolvency left many members unpaid, I will assemble the FOR compensation batch, file/status-track the applications, and get paid on filed, accepted, status-advanced, or paid outcomes."

Economics:
Charge 2,000-6,000 PLN for group intake and eligibility triage, 150-400 PLN per producer/invoice file in a batch, plus 2-5% of compensation collected above a threshold, capped and disclosed. For producer groups with many unpaid invoices from one buyer, the fee is justified by completeness, speed, status tracking, deficiency response, and avoiding missed application windows. Avoid low-value individual claims.

Copy risk:
Agricultural advisers, accountants, producer groups, lawyers, chambers, and public support channels can copy the process. The defense is event-specific batch control: signed group mandate, member authorities, shared buyer-insolvency file, invoice/delivery dataset, application tracker, deficiency memory, and status/payment follow-through for a named buyer event.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the startup has the group mandate, member authorizations, named insolvent-buyer file, invoice/delivery dataset, application tracker, deficiency log, and fee agreement, another provider cannot process that same batch without displacing the active administrator and re-collecting sensitive producer and invoice evidence from the group.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The controlled object is a named producer-group compensation batch for one or more insolvent-buyer events. The output is filed applications, accepted or status-advanced applications, deficiency-narrowing responses, official no-go outcomes, or compensation payments. It is not a form guide, farm advisory subscription, legal claim, debt collection, dashboard, database, marketplace, or referral broker.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
No litigation, insolvency representation, debt collection, legal advice, tax advice, farm subsidy consulting, loan or factoring, compensation-fund custody, guarantee of payment, or claim purchase. The agricultural producer remains applicant and recipient of funds. Lawyers, accountants, producer-group authorities, and official channels handle legal, tax, insolvency, and entitlement decisions. Reject unclear buyer insolvency, disputed delivery, missing invoices, uncertain producer authority, fraud flags, duplicate claims, low-value individual cases, and any request to alter records.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Not PSE PV compensation because this is agricultural unpaid-buyer compensation under the Agricultural Protection Fund, not electricity redispatch compensation. Not TraceFaktura because it is not municipal event-to-invoice routing. Not public grant applications generally because it accepts only producer-group batches tied to named insolvent buyers and unpaid sale invoices. Not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, or health/Rx/clinical routes.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Public support channels, chambers, agricultural advisers, accountants, lawyers, and producer groups may already help members with FOR applications. Individual claims may be too low value. The process may look like public form-filling rather than a defensible company. Legal and insolvency edge cases can arise around buyer status, product eligibility, delivery proof, unpaid amount, duplicate claims, assignments, or disputed transactions. Farmers and groups may resist success fees for statutory compensation. Application windows are seasonal. A Warsaw solo founder may lack agricultural trust without an agri accountant or producer-group channel. Official acceptance and payment timing remain outside the startup's control.
