## Strongest Objections

1. Law firms and AI governance consultants may own buyer trust. The founder can assemble release evidence, but legal counsel must approve final legal positions.
2. The founder cannot decide provider/deployer obligations; the pack can only map evidence and issue-spotting questions.
3. Some customers will demand product changes rather than evidence. Disclosure may not fire in the right channel, human handoff may be unclear, or logs may not prove the claim.
4. Article 50 guidance is still evolving, so buyer questions may be vague or inconsistent.
5. Many AI voice/chat vendors may solve simple cases internally by adding disclosure copy and exporting test logs.
6. Customer acceptance remains outside founder control; the right success metric is progress or narrowed follow-up, not guaranteed approval.
7. Scope can drift into high-risk classification, DPIA, conformity assessment, ISO 42001, model-risk review, privacy analysis, contract clauses, legal memos, and full AI governance programs.
8. Voice AI can touch sensitive sectors, which must be excluded from the first POC.
9. Test calls, recordings, transcripts, scripts, IVR flows, and complaint logs create privacy and confidentiality handling risk.
10. The moat is moderate: buyer wording, accepted disclosure placements, test-log formats, handoff evidence, deployer instructions, redacted sample packs, and gap-split language are useful but copyable.
