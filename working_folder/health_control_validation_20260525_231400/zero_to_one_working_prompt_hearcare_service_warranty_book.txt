Evaluate this business idea from 0-100 and include the strongest objections or caveats behind the score.

Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, uniquely produced, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, and patient-data businesses without asset/customer-flow control.

Scored idea:

HearCare Service/Warranty Book Buyout acquires or options a retiring independent audioprosthetist or hearing-aid shop's existing aftercare, consumables, warranty, and repair service book serving 50-60+ and elderly customers.

It is not a clinic, not a diagnostic provider, not medical advice, not a patient-data marketplace, and not a generic e-commerce store. The founder does not perform audiology, diagnose hearing loss, fit medical devices, alter hearing-aid settings, claim clinical provider status, or advertise medical claims to patients during the proof. Licensed audioprosthetist/audiologist partners and manufacturer repair channels handle regulated fitting/clinical/service tasks. The startup controls the aftercare/customer-flow asset around devices already sold by the retiring shop.

Buyer:
- existing hearing-aid users aged 50-80;
- families helping older parents maintain devices;
- customers whose local independent hearing-aid shop is closing or whose audioprosthetist is retiring;
- customers needing warranty handling, repair, loaner device, earmold replacement, batteries, filters, domes, tubes, wax guards, cleaning, charger help, and renewal reminders.

Acute trigger:
When a hearing aid, earmold, charger, receiver, tube, dome, filter, or battery/consumable supply fails, the user cannot communicate normally and often cannot rebuild device history from scratch. The retiring shop controls device serials, warranty status, service history, earmold files, manufacturer contacts, consumable preferences, appointment history, and trusted phone/domain/mailbox. Without a handoff, customers drift to chains or lose continuity.

Control point:
The acquired asset is the aftercare and warranty service book:
- customer consents and seller-endorsed handoff;
- phone/domain/mailbox and appointment channel;
- customer contacts, device serials, model history, warranty and repair calendars;
- earmold files, impression files/records where transferable, size/fit records, and service notes;
- manufacturer repair-channel notes and wholesaler/service terms where transferable;
- consumable purchase history for batteries, filters, domes, tubes, wax guards, cleaning kits, and chargers;
- small loaner device/accessory inventory and replacement-part inventory;
- recurring aftercare, repair handling, consumables, and renewal reminder flow;
- prepaid aftercare/repair/consumables commitments before close.

A chain or competitor can sell new hearing aids, but cannot instantly copy this exact device-serial book, warranty history, earmold records, service notes, phone/domain continuity, seller trust, loaner shelf, and prepaid aftercare flow.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable/creditable option fee to the retiring shop owner;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN legal/consent/data-transfer/manufacturer-warranty review;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN licensed audioprosthetist/audiologist partner retainer, repair-channel setup, and service SOP;
- 10,000-25,000 PLN loaner devices, consumables, repair float, earmold logistics, phone/domain/mailbox transfer, and CRM cleanup;
- closing payment only after prepaid customer aftercare/consumables/repair cash clears, with remaining seller consideration structured as earnout from retained gross margin.

Required POC proof:
1. One signed conditional acquisition option under 100,000 PLN over a named retiring independent audioprosthetist/shop service book covering customer handoff, phone/domain/mailbox, device serials, warranty records, service history, earmold files/records where transferable, consumable purchase history, loaner inventory, supplier notes, and seller transition support.
2. Clean consent/data-transfer and manufacturer/service-channel review: no unusable customer book, no non-transferable warranty blocker that kills the deal, no false clinical/provider claims.
3. Licensed audioprosthetist/audiologist partner agreement covering fitting/regulated service boundaries, customer service days, and repair triage.
4. At least 75-150 active service customers verified, with device serials, warranty/repair records, and recent interaction or purchase history.
5. At least 25 customers prepay aftercare, repair handling, consumables, loaner access, or service membership before close.
6. At least 25,000 PLN prepaid cash clears before final close.
7. One completed repair/loaner/earmold/consumables service cycle through the licensed partner/manufacturer channel.
8. Explicit boundary: founder handles acquired customer-flow administration, inventory, booking, reminders, logistics, and non-clinical support; licensed providers handle fitting, audiology, settings, and regulated tasks.

Economics:
The business is acquisition-led, not ad-led. The first customers are inherited through seller handoff. The first cash comes from prepaid aftercare/consumables/repair/loaner commitments before closing. Hearing-aid users have recurring needs for batteries, filters, domes, tubes, wax guards, cleaning, charger help, earmolds, repairs, warranty handling, and eventual replacement/upgrade timing.

Initial pricing logic:
- annual aftercare/repair-handling membership;
- consumables subscription or prepaid pack;
- loaner-device access fee and deposit;
- repair logistics/handling fee through manufacturer channels;
- earmold replacement coordination fee through licensed partner;
- renewal/reminder and appointment support;
- seller earnout only from retained customer gross margin.

Gross-margin logic:
The startup controls the aftercare book and recurring consumable/service flow while licensed partners handle regulated tasks. Gross margin comes from consumables, repair handling, loaner fees/deposits, aftercare memberships, and retained upgrade/referral flow. It should not pay meaningful acquisition consideration unless prepaid customer cash proves the book is active.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The foundational asset is an acquired customer/device aftercare book: device serials, warranty calendar, earmold/service records, phone/domain/mailbox, seller handoff, loaner inventory, consumable purchase history, and prepaid aftercare commitments. It is not a generic online store or listing marketplace; it owns continuity for devices already in customers' ears.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- Do not diagnose hearing loss, fit hearing aids, alter settings, or provide clinical advice unless performed by licensed/qualified professionals.
- Do not make false medical-device/provider claims.
- Customer consent and data-minimization are mandatory.
- Manufacturer warranty and repair-channel rules must be reviewed before promising transfer.
- Public wording should focus on aftercare continuity, repair logistics, consumables, and licensed-partner support rather than clinical outcomes.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Large chains and hearing-aid retailers can compete and may have stronger manufacturer terms.
- Manufacturer warranty/service rights may not transfer cleanly.
- Customer consents and device records may be incomplete.
- B2C trust may be harder than B2B renewal.
- If the founder cannot secure a licensed audioprosthetist/audiologist partner, the idea fails.
- If customers refuse prepaid aftercare/consumables, the book is weak.
- If the acquired shop has only stale goodwill and no active service/consumables flow, score should collapse.
