## Search Frame

Recent cash-recovery ideas kept failing because the decisive payment action stayed inside a buyer, platform, broker, insurer, customs filer, lender, or claims administrator. This round tests current payment streams where the customer has already authorized recurring collection and the first proof is not a report: it is a corrected collection file and a successful debit batch through the licensed bank/PSP.

The lead candidate is not a billing migration project, payment consulting, PSP brokerage, or debt collection. It only accepts live recurring-revenue businesses with a current failed or at-risk SEPA direct-debit collection cycle, documented payer mandate data, owner authorization, approved bank/PSP path, payer notice or re-consent route where required, and a near-term collection batch.

## Candidate Advanced To Real Validation

Idea name: SEPA Direct-Debit Collection Rescue Lockbox

One-sentence thesis: rescue live failed or at-risk SEPA direct-debit collection cycles for subscription and membership businesses by taking authorized operational control of the mandate register, migration/re-consent work, bank/PSP batch file, and first successful collection run.

Exact buyer: EU subscription or membership businesses with 100-5,000 recurring payers and at least 50,000 PLN/EUR equivalent of monthly direct-debit collections, such as gyms, fitness chains, coworking operators, language schools, private clubs, small ISPs, local SaaS, property-service subscriptions, and other non-regulated recurring billers. The first buyer has a current failed batch, provider migration, creditor-ID change, billing-system cutover, or return-code spike threatening the next collection cycle.

Acute trigger: A recurring SEPA direct-debit batch has failed, is about to fail, or cannot be submitted because the old PSP/bank/billing tool is closing, the creditor identifier or bank account is changing, mandate references do not map, payer notices or re-consents are incomplete, return codes spiked, or a billing-system migration broke the mandate file before the next monthly collection.

Control point: Signed collection-continuity mandate from the biller; current payer mandate register; old and new creditor identifiers where relevant; payer notice/re-consent plan where required; new PSP/bank onboarding status; return-code ledger; corrected mandate-reference mapping; test file; production debit file; batch-submission acknowledgement; settlement report; and the first successful collection batch through the licensed bank/PSP. The startup never holds customer funds.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:

- Three signed biller mandates, each tied to a current failed or at-risk SEPA collection cycle.
- At least 150,000 PLN/EUR equivalent of current monthly collections under rescue.
- Two corrected mandate registers and collection files accepted by a bank/PSP/billing platform.
- One successful recollection or migration batch with settlement into the biller's account.
- 25,000-75,000 PLN collected in setup and current-cash recovery fees, excluding unpaid success-fee receivables.

6-month POC:

- 20-40 screened billers.
- 8-15 accepted rescue mandates.
- 2,000-12,000 payer mandates migrated, repaired, re-consented, or cleanly no-go'd.
- 1,000,000-5,000,000 PLN/EUR equivalent in collection batches submitted after repair.
- 250,000-1,200,000 PLN/EUR equivalent in current collections saved, recollected, or prevented from failing.
- 120,000-350,000 PLN revenue from setup fees, urgent batch fees, and capped collection-rescue fees.
- Gross margin above 65% after payment-ops contractor review, secure file handling, customer communications, and partner fees.

First acquisition mechanism: Target PSP migration partners, billing-system implementers, accountants serving subscription businesses, gym/franchise operators, coworking operators, and small ISPs. The offer is specific: "If your next SEPA direct-debit run is blocked by a provider switch, creditor-ID change, failed return-code spike, or broken mandate migration, I take one authorized rescue batch from register to submitted file to settlement report. No funds custody, no PSP resale, no debt collection."

Economics: Charge 4,000-12,000 PLN/EUR setup for accepted live rescue cases plus 3-8% of the first saved or recollected batch value, capped per biller. For urgent batches above 100,000 PLN/EUR, add a rush fee. The biller pays because one missed collection cycle creates immediate cash-flow damage, churn, manual invoicing, support tickets, and reputational issues with payers.

Copy risk: PSPs, banks, billing platforms, accountants, payment-operations consultants, and internal finance teams can copy the workflow. The defense is temporary but concrete control of the exact current batch, mandate mapping, return-code memory, migration/re-consent scripts, partner playbooks, and repeated rescue cases before the next collection deadline.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: once the founder has the biller's signed mandate, current mandate register, return-code ledger, payer communications, corrected batch file, PSP/bank acknowledgement, and deadline calendar, another operator cannot act on that exact collection cycle without the biller switching mid-rescue. The first asset is the current collection batch and mandate file, not a generic checklist.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: The unit of work is a live authorized collection cycle that ends in a submitted debit file, bank/PSP acknowledgement, settlement report, or clean no-go. There is no case without a named failed or at-risk batch, payer mandate data, authorized collection path, and near-term cash movement. The startup does not sell software, a migration report, a PSP directory, or payment-provider introductions.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:

- Licensed banks, PSPs, and billing platforms remain the payment processors and funds handlers.
- The biller remains merchant/creditor of record and responsible for payer contracts, mandate validity, customer communications, refund decisions, complaints, chargebacks, accounting, and legal compliance.
- The startup does not hold funds, initiate unauthorized debits, provide legal advice, act as a PSP, debt collector, factoring provider, credit provider, or merchant of record.
- Reject consumer credit, insurance, gambling, medical/health subscriptions, adult content, charities with donor-sensitive issues, suspected fraud, sanctions risk, high chargeback risk, ambiguous payer authorization, and any case requiring legal interpretation of mandate validity.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:

- Not PromoLeak, Amazon Vendor Central, Retail OTIF, 3PL overbilling, COD remittance, or other recovery mandates; the cash event is a current direct-debit collection batch, not a disputed deduction or platform claim.
- Not KSeF/AP payment release; no enterprise buyer AP action is required.
- Not DORA, REDBlocked, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, TraceFaktura, HeatQuiet, BioSignal, or HeritageDoor; the buyer, trigger, control point, and proof artifact are payment-stream continuity.

Strongest anticipated objections:

- PSPs, banks, billing tools, and accountants are natural owners and can internalize the workflow.
- Many failed batches are caused by invalid mandates, payer churn, insufficient funds, cancelled authorizations, or bad customer data, not fixable migration defects.
- Handling payer bank data, mandate references, and customer communications creates GDPR, fraud, and security trust friction.
- Payer notices and re-consents can take longer than the collection deadline.
- A part-time unknown founder may be too slow for urgent monthly collection windows.
- Success-fee attribution can be disputed if the next batch would have recovered naturally.
- The opportunity may be episodic around migrations rather than recurring enough to build a durable company.

## Internal Simulation Score

Simulated score: **88.3 / 100**

Rationale:

- First proof is current cash movement, not a packet, report, or dashboard.
- The founder can operate without funds custody by routing through the biller's PSP/bank.
- The trigger is acute: one missed collection cycle directly hits cash flow.
- The batch/mandate file is a concrete control object with a deadline and a measurable outcome.
- Capital needs are low, and payment-ops contractors can review files without becoming PSPs.
- Main weaknesses are natural incumbent proximity, data-security trust, and whether clean failed-batch cases are dense enough. It clears simulation because the control object is a live recurring payment stream with direct authorization and first-batch settlement proof.

## Internal Kill Criteria

Kill if any of the following occurs:

- Fewer than 20% of screened billers have a live failed or at-risk batch above 50,000 PLN/EUR.
- PSPs or banks refuse third-party operational preparation even under biller authorization.
- More than 35% of accepted cases are invalid payer authorization, churn, insufficient funds, or legal/contract ambiguity rather than fixable migration or file defects.
- Average collected fee per rescue is below 8,000 PLN/EUR after setup, secure handling, and contractor review.
- A second batch from the same or referred biller does not materialize within 90 days.
- Any case creates funds-custody, debt-collection, unauthorized-debit, or sensitive-regulated-merchant exposure.

## Decision

Advance to working-chat Zero To One validation because simulated score is strictly above 87 and the proof object is a live recurring payment stream with first successful collection, not a service report or generic migration checklist.
