Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
VoiceAI Article 50 Release Evidence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A 7-business-day vendor-side release evidence pack for EU/CEE and EU-facing AI voice-agent, customer-service AI, sales-call AI, call-center automation, and conversational-AI vendors whose customer go-live, renewal, expansion, or tender is blocked by EU AI Act Article 50 transparency, AI-interaction disclosure, human-handoff, test-log, and deployer-instruction evidence requests.

Exact buyer:
EU/CEE and EU-facing B2B AI voice-agent, customer-service AI, sales-call AI, call-center automation, BPO automation, and conversational-AI vendors with live enterprise, regulated-industry, public-sector, or BPO customer deployments.

The best first buyer is a 10-100 person AI voice/chat vendor with a current customer deployment, live procurement/security/legal review, and a written request for AI transparency evidence before launch, renewal, expansion, or tender award.

Acute trigger:
A customer launch, renewal, expansion, or tender step is blocked by a written request such as:
- prove callers/users are informed they interact with AI;
- show where disclosure appears in call scripts, IVR, chat UI, emails, transcripts, or help pages;
- document when and how human handoff occurs;
- show logs, test calls, or screenshots proving disclosures fire;
- identify provider/deployer responsibilities without legal overclaiming;
- provide deployer/customer instructions for their own transparency obligations;
- confirm synthetic voice/content boundaries, complaint routing, and escalation rules.

Control point:
Vendor-side live release pack tied to one customer deployment or tender:
- buyer request text, questionnaire, contract clause, tender language, or go-live blocker;
- product flow, call scripts, IVR/chat UI, emails, transcripts, and disclosure copy;
- test-call/test-chat logs, screenshots, or recordings where customer-authorized;
- human-handoff rules and escalation evidence;
- deployer instruction sheet;
- provider/deployer responsibility map for issue spotting only;
- model/vendor disclosure and known limitations;
- synthetic voice/content boundary notes;
- incident and complaint routing note;
- gap split: safe to ship, product change needed, legal review needed, customer/deployer decision needed, unsafe to answer.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid live release packs at 10,000-30,000 PLN each.
2. At least two packs tied to written enterprise, regulated-industry, public-sector, or BPO customer blockers.
3. At least one launch, renewal, pilot expansion, tender step, or security/procurement review progresses after the pack.
4. One AI governance/legal issue-spotting reviewer on paid per-case terms.
5. One reusable evidence checklist across AI voice disclosure, human handoff, test logs, deployer instructions, provider/deployer role split, complaint routing, and synthetic content boundaries.
6. One redacted sample pack showing a buyer-ready response without legal overclaiming.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a narrow Article 50 voice/chat evidence checklist from EU AI Act transparency sources, draft guidelines, and buyer-question patterns; recruit one AI governance/legal issue-spotting reviewer; build a target list of 60 EU/CEE AI voice/chat vendors, call-center AI vendors, BPO automation providers, and AI implementation agencies.

Month 2: close three prepaid live release packs only where a written customer blocker exists. For each case, ingest the buyer request, scripts, product flows, test calls/chats, disclosure copy, handoff rules, screenshots/logs, and existing customer-facing instructions. Deliver a buyer-ready release pack with gap split and response language.

Month 3: document whether at least one pack changes a real commercial workflow: go-live proceeds, customer security/procurement review advances, tender step progresses, renewal blocker clears, customer requests a product change, or legal reviewer narrows the final open issues.

Months 4-6: complete 10-20 paid packs, collect 150,000-400,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer costs, sign one agency or implementation-partner routing relationship, and build a private taxonomy of recurring Article 50 voice/chat disclosure blockers and accepted buyer-response patterns.

First acquisition mechanism:
Buyer pitch:
"If an enterprise, BPO, public-sector, or regulated customer is blocking your AI voice/chat go-live because of AI disclosure, handoff, test-log, deployer-instruction, or Article 50 transparency questions, send the written blocker, scripts, product flow, and test evidence. I return a buyer-ready release pack in 7 business days: what you can safely show now, what needs a product change, what needs legal review, what the deployer must decide, and what should not be claimed."

Partner pitch:
"You keep legal advice, AI governance program design, ISO work, and implementation. I handle urgent vendor-side release packs when your AI voice/chat client has one customer go-live blocked by concrete Article 50 transparency evidence questions."

Economics:
Charge 10,000-18,000 PLN for a narrow single-deployment release pack, 18,000-30,000 PLN for multi-flow voice/chat packs with reviewer pass and redacted sample responses, and 25,000-60,000 PLN/month for repeated vendor or implementation-partner queues. Reviewer cost is 2,000-7,000 PLN per case depending on legal/governance depth. The vendor pays because one delayed enterprise go-live, BPO rollout, public-sector tender, or renewal can cost far more than the pack.

Copy risk:
AI governance consultants, law firms, ISO 42001 consultants, GRC platforms, implementation agencies, and in-house product/legal teams can copy pieces. The defense is narrow release-case memory for AI voice/chat deployments, repeated buyer question wording, test-log/disclosure evidence patterns, partner routing, redacted sample packs, and fast vendor-side packaging before larger advisers engage.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a vendor assigns a live customer deployment blocker, prepays for the release pack, shares scripts/product flows/test evidence, and routes the customer response through the startup under a go-live or tender deadline, another provider must displace an active release workflow. The controlled unit is the live customer blocker, evidence room, response wording, product-flow gap map, reviewer notes, and commercial deadline.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live customer deployments, tenders, renewals, or expansion blockers with written AI transparency evidence requests. The output is a release artifact used in procurement/security/legal review: disclosure evidence map, handoff evidence, test-log pack, deployer instruction sheet, gap split, and response language. It is not generic AI Act consulting, a dashboard, a database, legal advice, an ISO program, a software product, or an expert-call broker.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not provide legal advice, certify AI Act compliance, classify high-risk status, perform conformity assessment, provide ISO certification, approve product release, determine deployer/provider obligations as a legal conclusion, or guarantee customer acceptance. Legal counsel and qualified AI governance advisers handle legal conclusions. The vendor remains responsible for product changes, customer representations, contracts, deployment, and compliance decisions. The first POC excludes healthcare, mental health, legal advice, credit, insurance underwriting, employment/recruiting decisions, biometric/emotion-recognition, law enforcement, migration, education grading, children-directed systems, and other high-risk or sensitive deployments.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not DORA because the buyer is an AI voice/chat vendor facing AI transparency release blockers, not an ICT vendor facing regulated-finance third-party-risk questionnaires. It is not REDBlocked because there is no Amazon ASIN, connected-device RED/CE/GPSR evidence request, baby monitor, or camera. It is not Data Act, BioSignal, CBAM, GreenTender, PromoLeak, TraceFaktura, HeritageDoor, BatteryFit, HeatQuiet, Supplement Stack Safety, or any health/Rx/clinical route.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- AI governance consultants and law firms may own the buyer trust relationship.
- Article 50 guidelines and codes are still evolving, so buyers may ask vague questions or wait for clearer standards.
- Many vendors will treat this as a product/legal task they can solve internally with templates.
- The founder cannot certify compliance or make legal role/classification conclusions.
- Customer acceptance is outside founder control; a pack may still trigger legal redlines or product changes.
- AI voice/chat vendors may need actual implementation work, not evidence packaging.
- Sales is hard because the founder must find vendors with live blockers, not general AI Act anxiety.
- The moat may be modest unless repeated buyer question patterns and release evidence examples accumulate quickly.
