Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Russia-Origin MTC Customs Release Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A broker-routed current-shipment evidence pack for Polish/CEE importers whose iron/steel-containing goods are blocked or delayed by EU Russia-sanctions origin-proof requests, turning invoices, product/CN data, mill test certificates, supplier statements, and gap/no-go notes into a broker-ready release file without giving legal advice or acting as customs declarant.

Exact buyer:
Primary buyer/channel: Polish/CEE customs brokers, freight forwarders, courier customs teams, and trade-compliance boutiques serving SME importers of iron/steel-containing goods.

Economic beneficiary: SME importers of steel/iron products, steel-containing components, machinery parts, fasteners, frames, brackets, tools, fittings, and industrial goods whose shipment, customs clearance, customer delivery, or production schedule is delayed because a broker, customs office, carrier, or buyer asks for proof that relevant iron/steel inputs do not originate in Russia.

Acute trigger:
A current shipment, customs declaration, carrier customs query, broker exception, buyer delivery promise, or warehouse release is blocked by a request for mill test certificates, supplier statements, origin of melt/pour/input evidence, product/CN mapping, or documentation showing that listed iron/steel products processed in a third country do not incorporate Russian-origin inputs.

Control point:
The startup controls a paid current-shipment case only when all of these are present:
- current written broker/customs/carrier/customer query or hold;
- importer authorization and broker/forwarder route;
- invoice, packing list, CN/HS/product description, supplier contact path, and existing mill test certificates or origin documents;
- prepaid fixed case fee;
- agreement that the customs broker/importer remains declarant and legally responsible for sanctions/origin statements;
- right to retain anonymized case taxonomy: product type, CN/HS family, evidence requested, MTC gaps, supplier response, customs/broker outcome, and no-go reason.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Two broker/forwarder routing agreements or recurring referral understandings for Russia-origin iron/steel evidence exceptions.
2. Eight prepaid current-shipment cases at 4,000-12,000 PLN each.
3. At least three different importers and at least two brokers/forwarders represented.
4. At least six completed case packs: product/CN scope map, MTC/evidence checklist, supplier RFI, gap/no-go register, broker-ready response file, and escalation boundary note.
5. At least two shipment-progress outcomes: broker accepts file for declaration, carrier/customs query progresses, customs request is satisfied, buyer/customer delivery hold is lifted, or shipment is cleanly killed before demurrage/storage grows.
6. One trade-compliance/customs reviewer on paid per-case terms for flagged cases.
7. 40,000-80,000 PLN collected with more than 60% gross margin after reviewer and translation costs.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a list of 80 Polish/CEE customs brokers, freight forwarders, courier customs teams, industrial import advisers, and trade-compliance boutiques that handle steel/iron-containing goods; recruit one trade-compliance/customs reviewer; build a sample redacted MTC gap pack using dummy shipment data; and contact brokers with a current-exception-only offer.

Month 2: close two broker/forwarder routing agreements or recurring referral understandings and eight prepaid current-shipment cases. Deliver product/CN scope maps, MTC gap registers, supplier RFIs, broker-ready response files, and no-go/escalation notes.

Month 3: document outcomes and kill reasons: accepted for declaration, customs/carrier query progressed, buyer hold lifted, supplier evidence missing, legal/sanctions escalation required, classification dispute, sanctioned-party concern, suspected circumvention, or low-value shipment.

Months 4-6: complete 25-40 prepaid shipment cases, collect 150,000-350,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60%, get at least one broker sending five or more repeat cases, and build case memory by CN/HS family, product type, supplier country, MTC fields, missing-origin pattern, broker wording, response outcome, and no-go reason.

First acquisition mechanism:
Broker pitch:
"When a current shipment is stuck because customs, DHL/FedEx, a broker desk, or a buyer asks for non-Russian iron/steel input proof, send the query and the shipment docs. I assemble the MTC/origin evidence file, supplier RFI, gap register, broker-ready response, and no-go/escalation note. You remain the broker/declarant. I do not give legal advice or make origin declarations."

Importer pitch through broker:
"If your production delivery or customer order is blocked by a request for mill certificates or proof that steel inputs are not Russian, this is a fixed-fee current-shipment pack. We work only from your shipment documents and supplier evidence; if the file is not supportable, we produce a clean no-go/escalation note before storage and delay costs grow."

Economics:
Charge 4,000-6,000 PLN for a simple one-supplier, one-shipment MTC gap pack; 7,000-12,000 PLN for multi-line or multi-supplier shipment files; and 15,000-25,000 PLN monthly lane support for repeated importers or broker accounts with five or more recurring shipments.

The importer pays because the alternative is delayed release, demurrage/storage, missed production or customer delivery, emergency supplier chasing by internal staff, or a failed shipment. The broker pays or resells because a clean file protects the client relationship without turning the broker into a sanctions-origin document factory.

Copy risk:
Customs brokers, freight forwarders, trade-compliance consultants, sanctions lawyers, and large logistics firms can copy generic checklists. The defense is not legal knowledge. The defense is broker-first current exception routing, product/CN evidence patterns by shipment type, supplier RFI wording that gets usable MTC/origin responses quickly, known no-go patterns, accepted or pushed-back broker/customs response files, and repeat importer lane memory.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once a broker or importer routes a current shipment, grants document access, signs the paid case, and starts supplier evidence collection through the startup workflow, a competitor cannot access that shipment file or response clock without the importer switching mid-exception. The exact asset is the live case file: query, docs, supplier trail, MTC gaps, broker wording, and release/no-go outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup does not sell sanctions readiness, legal advice, a dashboard, a supplier database, origin certification, customs declaration filing, or broker introductions. It accepts only current shipment or release-block cases with a written query and prepaid fee. The output is a broker-ready current-shipment case file: evidence index, supplier RFI, MTC/origin gap register, response draft, and no-go/escalation note.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not give legal advice, sanctions opinions, customs classification decisions, origin determinations, certificates, declarations, or clearance guarantees. The importer, broker, customs representative, supplier, and qualified trade/legal advisers remain responsible for declaration, legal interpretation, final origin statements, sanctions compliance, and customs communications. Suspected circumvention, missing evidence, false supplier statements, sanctioned parties, high-risk routes, or requests to fabricate or alter documents are rejected or escalated.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Closest confirmed idea: CBAM Import Continuity Desk.

This is materially different from CBAM because CBAM is carbon-border import continuity, authorised CBAM declarant readiness, emissions/default-value exposure, supplier emissions evidence, and future CBAM certificate cost. This idea is Russia-sanctions iron/steel input origin proof for current shipment release, using mill test certificates, supplier-origin evidence, and customs/broker queries. The proof artifact is an MTC/origin evidence release file, not a CBAM exposure memo. The payment event is current customs/carrier/broker shipment progress or clean no-go under sanctions-origin proof, not CBAM reporting/readiness.

This is still close to CBAM because both use broker-first import documents and steel-related imports. It should be killed if brokers say the same paid CBAM relationship would naturally absorb MTC/sanctions-origin exceptions as a minor add-on rather than a separate paid lane.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- Customs brokers may already handle MTC/sanctions-origin requests as normal customs support.
- The idea is close to legal sanctions compliance and customs-origin interpretation, where an unknown founder has low trust.
- Some cases will be impossible because suppliers cannot or will not provide credible MTCs, melt/pour evidence, or non-Russian input statements.
- Classification and product-scope questions may drag the case into customs advice.
- Brokers, freight forwarders, and trade-compliance consultants can copy the workflow if volume appears.
- Case volume may be episodic unless repeat brokers route many exceptions.
- The market may treat this as a temporary sanctions friction rather than a durable company.
- The CBAM Import Continuity Desk may already own the same broker relationship and absorb this as an adjacent document exception.
