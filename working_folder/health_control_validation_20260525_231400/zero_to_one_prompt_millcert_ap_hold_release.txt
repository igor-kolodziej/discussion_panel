Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
MillCert AP Hold Release Desk

One-sentence thesis:
Release unpaid industrial invoice batches for metal fabricators, machining suppliers, pressure-equipment subcontractors, rail/energy component suppliers, and industrial OEM suppliers when customer AP or QA has written that payment is held until mill certificates, heat numbers, CoC/CoA files, inspection records, NDT reports, welding logs, or batch traceability are matched to the delivered goods.

Exact buyer:
Polish/CEE metal fabricators, machining suppliers, industrial component suppliers, steel/aluminium processors, pressure-equipment subcontractors, rail/energy component suppliers, and OEM subcontractors with named unpaid invoices above 75,000 PLN where the customer has issued written AP/QA hold language tied to missing or mismatched material certificates, heat/lot traceability, inspection evidence, or certificate packets.

Acute trigger:
Delivered goods are accepted physically or commercially close to accepted, but customer AP or QA refuses invoice acceptance, payment release, milestone payment, or final acceptance because the certificate/traceability file is incomplete, mismatched, or not mapped to the invoice, PO, drawing, heat number, batch, serial, weld/NDT report, or delivery lot.

Control point:
Signed invoice-release mandate over named unpaid invoices and customer hold text; PO, invoice, delivery note, packing list, drawing/revision, material batch, heat number, lot/serial map, upstream supplier contacts, mill test certificates, CoC/CoA, inspection records, NDT reports where already performed, welding/traceability logs where relevant, customer response matrix, supplier certificate chase file, customer-ready packet, payment-status tracker, and clean no-go record where source truth is missing.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
Within 60 days, sign eight prepaid invoice-release mandates; process at least 15 invoice batches above 75,000 PLN each; obtain at least 10 customer acceptances, payment releases, partial releases, narrowed follow-ups, or clean no-go outcomes; get at least 500,000 PLN of invoice value accepted, released, partially released, or formally no-go'd; and collect at least 60,000 PLN in fixed and release fees.

6-month POC:
Run 50-100 invoice-release files across 20-40 suppliers; cover 6,000,000-18,000,000 PLN of held invoice value; produce 40+ customer acceptances, payment releases, partial releases, narrowed follow-ups, or paid clean no-go outcomes; collect 500,000-1,500,000 PLN in fees; maintain gross margin above 65% after certificate reviewers and supplier-chasing contractors; build a private taxonomy by customer hold wording, material family, heat/lot mismatch, certificate source, supplier type, accepted packet format, and no-go reason.

First acquisition mechanism:
Outbound to CFOs, owners, sales directors, and quality managers at Polish/CEE industrial suppliers that sell to rail, energy, machinery, construction-steel, pressure-equipment, and OEM customers. Trigger lists come from factoring/AR stress, public tenders, supplier directories, industrial clusters, ISO/EN certifications, and sales teams complaining about "payment stuck in quality." Partner routes include factoring brokers, industrial accountants, QA consultants, NDT providers, and export-document agents who see held invoices but do not want to chase certificates.

Economics:
Charge 5,000-12,000 PLN for triage and missing-source map per invoice batch, 12,000-35,000 PLN for an urgent customer-ready traceability release file, and optional 2-6% success fee only when customer AP/QA hold text and payment release attribution are explicit. Reject invoice batches below 75,000 PLN unless bundled. Reject cases where the true issue is quality failure, contract dispute, unpaid supplier debt, missing tests that were never performed, or a customer using documentation as a pretext for cash delay.

Copy risk:
Internal QA teams, quality consultants, document controllers, NDT providers, industrial accountants, and customer-service teams can copy pieces. The defense is live AP-hold routing, fast invoice-to-heat/lot mapping, customer-specific accepted packet memory, supplier certificate chase discipline, and control over named unpaid invoice batches before the customer's payment run.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the supplier signs the mandate and provides the invoice batch, customer hold text, PO/delivery records, batch map, supplier contacts, and certificate files, the startup controls that payment-release file, response matrix, customer-ready packet, supplier-chase path, and fee right. A QA consultant can compete generally, but cannot resolve the same invoice batch without replacing the already-authorized AP-hold file owner.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The business does not sell quality consulting, certification, audits, dashboards, document software, broker introductions, or generic compliance packets. It accepts only named unpaid invoice batches with written AP/QA hold text and produces customer acceptance, payment release, partial release, narrowed follow-up, or clean no-go.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
Do not create or alter certificates, invent source truth, backdate documents, certify product quality, perform NDT/testing, provide engineering signoff, give legal advice, collect debts, threaten customers, handle funds, or pursue quality disputes. Missing upstream certificates, failed tests, quality defects, contract disputes, customer insolvency, sanctions, falsified documents, or required new testing become no-go or are routed to qualified labs, engineers, lawyers, or the customer's normal dispute process.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is distinct from PromoLeak because it is not retailer deduction or promotional leakage. It is distinct from IMDS/PPAP release because it is payment release on named unpaid invoice batches, not automotive material database acceptance or launch documentation. It is distinct from LC discrepancy and Russia-origin MTC customs release because the customer AP/QA hold is a private buyer payment event, not bank/customs document acceptance. It is distinct from TraceFaktura because it is industrial supplier invoice release, not municipal event routing.

Strongest anticipated objections:
Many holds are real quality disputes or buyer cash-delay excuses rather than document gaps; missing mill certificates or heat traceability may be impossible to recreate truthfully; internal QA should already own this; customer acceptance remains outside founder control; customer-specific documentation rules vary heavily; upstream suppliers can be slow or unresponsive; certificate handling creates liability if documents are mismatched or false; the buyer may refuse an outside coordinator between QA and AP; margins collapse if each file needs deep metallurgical, welding, NDT, or pressure-equipment review; once a packet format is learned, suppliers may internalize the checklist.
