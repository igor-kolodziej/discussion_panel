## Zero To One Summary

Zero To One agreed the control-point design is concrete: assigned PSP/acquirer receivable with debtor acknowledgment or controlled payment direction.

It scored only 67 because this is commercially dangerous under the founder constraints: high-risk factoring has legal, underwriting, adverse-selection, PSP-cooperation, and capital-loss risk. The target customer is risky by definition, and a 100,000 PLN balance sheet is too small for distressed receivable losses.

## Strongest Objections

1. PSP/acquirer acknowledgment is the whole business.
   - Without it, the asset may be a disputed future hope rather than a receivable.

2. Spread is too small for risk.
   - A 50,000 PLN receivable at 10% fee gives 5,000 PLN gross, which can disappear into legal review, AML/KYC, contract review, accounting, collection, and loss reserves.

3. Adverse selection is severe.
   - Clinics with frozen funds may have refund, chargeback, misleading advertising, prescription-drug promotion, weak consent, gray-market, or fraud flags.

4. 100,000 PLN is too little balance sheet.
   - One bad 50,000-80,000 PLN deal can damage runway.

5. PSPs may refuse cooperation.
   - Anti-assignment, setoff, chargebacks, fraud review, rolling reserves, and termination can destroy practical control.

6. Legal-finance setup is heavy.
   - Assignment, factoring/lending boundary, AML/KYC, tax/VAT/accounting, and data minimization are not lightweight.

7. Defensibility is weak.
   - Clean deals can be copied by factors; dirty deals may be unacceptable risk.

## Pivot Implications

Do not pursue with capital. If ever revisited, start with a no-advance success-fee collection or clinic-paid underwriting deposit, not a receivables purchase.

The next pivot should use the one pattern Zero To One has repeatedly scored highest: inherited B2B continuity software/workflow with prepaid contracts before close. Narrow it into genetics/lab/biosignal infrastructure rather than consumer health, clinical care, inventory, or distressed financing.
