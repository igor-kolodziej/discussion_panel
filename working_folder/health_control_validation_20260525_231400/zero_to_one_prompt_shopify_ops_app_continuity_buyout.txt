Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Shopify Ops App Continuity Buyout

One-sentence thesis:
Option and acquire a tiny but active Shopify operational utility app only after the app listing, source, support history, merchant subscription billing, seller handoff, and first post-transfer payout prove that a current payment stream can move to the founder without becoming custom Shopify consulting.

Exact buyer:
The payor is the existing base of Shopify merchants already paying 10-80 USD/month for a boring operational app used in store administration, such as order tagging, packing slips, delivery notes, local invoice exports, inventory alerts, B2B price lists, return labels, product-feed cleanup, metafield bulk edits, or simple back-office workflow utilities. The acquisition seller is a solo or two-person app developer who is tired, underpricing support, facing API/privacy/platform maintenance, or wants to exit a small app that is too small for professional SaaS buyers but too useful for merchants to lose.

Acute trigger:
The app developer is retiring, moving jobs, losing interest, facing a Shopify API version update, app review/privacy update, support backlog, broken webhook, billing migration, or required maintenance that threatens delisting, bad reviews, churn, or merchant workflow breakage. Merchants do not want a new app search or data migration because the app already sits inside their order, inventory, invoice, label, feed, or admin workflow.

Control point:
A signed seller option over one named Shopify app covering source code, repository history, app listing, app store/admin ownership transfer where allowed, billing/subscription records, merchant install list, support inbox/history, privacy/app-review artifacts, domain/helpdesk/docs, changelog, app credentials, contractor/IP assignments, and seller-endorsed merchant continuity notice. Seller payout is mostly earnout from retained gross profit after the listing, app admin, support channel, customer notification, and first Shopify/app subscription payout move to the founder/new owner.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
One signed option or earnout LOI over a named Shopify app with at least 80 active paying merchants, 12-month payout history, monthly recurring revenue of 15,000-45,000 PLN equivalent, gross margin above 75% before founder time, churn below 4% monthly, and no single merchant above 10% of MRR. The founder receives read-only app store/admin, repo, support, billing, and analytics access for diligence; a Shopify developer audits the code/update burden; at least 30 merchants receive seller-endorsed continuity notice and 15 confirm they will remain through transfer; app ownership/admin transfer path is confirmed; first post-transfer or escrowed subscription payout of at least 10,000 PLN equivalent is received or contractually routed before seller payout; and the first bugfix or platform-update patch is shipped without breaking merchant stores.

6-month POC:
Acquire or option one app; retain at least 80% of starting MRR for six months; collect 90,000-250,000 PLN equivalent in subscription revenue; keep gross margin above 60% after contractor support, Shopify developer patches, hosting, support tooling, seller earnout, and app-review/privacy maintenance; keep support below 25 minutes per merchant per month; ship two platform/API/privacy maintenance updates; recover or improve the app store rating; document one expansion or price-normalization cohort; and source a second seller lead under the same no-upfront-multiple, payout-after-transfer structure.

First acquisition mechanism:
Find small Shopify apps with 50-300 reviews or visible niche installs, low but real pricing, solo-founder support footprints, stale changelogs, delayed responses, narrow operational workflows, or public founder fatigue. Source through the Shopify App Store, Indie Hackers, Acquire-style listings, founder forums, developer agencies, Shopify Partner directories, GitHub footprints, and direct emails. Seller pitch: "I do not buy an idea or stale code. I option only active subscription revenue; you get an earnout after merchants stay, the app admin/listing/source transfers, support is handed over, and the first payout clears." Merchant message is seller-led: "The app is continuing under a new maintainer; no migration, no new install, no pricing surprise, and support remains inside the existing workflow."

Economics:
Pay a small option fee of 5,000-20,000 PLN plus 20-35% of retained gross profit for 12-24 months, rather than an upfront SaaS multiple. Target apps with 15,000-45,000 PLN equivalent monthly recurring revenue, 75%+ gross margin before founder time, low hosting cost, and bounded support. Contractor budget covers code audit, first patch, Shopify API/privacy maintenance, documentation cleanup, support migration, and emergency fixes. Revenue comes from the existing app subscriptions, not new consulting. Upside comes from raising underpriced tiers only after retention is proven, adding one paid support tier, and acquiring a second similar app.

Copy risk:
Shopify agencies, app studios, micro-SaaS buyers, app marketplaces, and competing app developers can buy or build similar apps. The defense is not generic Shopify expertise. It is control of a specific installed app listing, reviews, app store search position, merchant subscriptions, billing history, support history, seller-endorsed continuity notice, source/update path, and first post-transfer payout before professional buyers notice or before merchants churn.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the founder signs the option, receives app/repo/support/billing access, confirms the transfer path, sends seller-endorsed merchant continuity notices, and routes the first post-transfer subscription payout, competitors cannot control that exact listing, installed merchant base, reviews, billing relationship, support history, and update channel unless they buy the same seller or convince merchants to churn. They can build substitutes later, but they cannot retroactively own the current app-store payment stream.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup acquires or options an existing Shopify app payment stream and maintains the installed operational utility. It does not sell Shopify development, migration services, app acquisition advice, dashboards, app-marketplace listings, merchant lead lists, reports, or broker introductions. The proof is ownership/control transfer of a named app listing and source, merchant retention, and subscription payouts under the new owner.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup avoids checkout, payments, tax/legal determination, medical/Rx, regulated finance, advertising pixels, sensitive customer-data enrichment, high-risk marketing tracking, and apps requiring 24/7 mission-critical incident response. Merchants remain responsible for store operations, lawful product sales, tax/accounting treatment, customer communications, privacy notices, and Shopify compliance. Shopify remains the platform and billing/app-review authority. The startup must not access merchant data beyond the minimum app scope, must rotate credentials, update data-processing terms, preserve logs, honor deletion requests, avoid credential sharing, and reject apps with unclear IP, copied code, unresolved security issues, fake reviews, policy violations, or privacy-risky data flows.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
This is not OneStack, LabOps Connector, MailDomain, OTP Sender-ID, or other failed book-transfer ideas because the controlled asset is a public app-store listing with installed merchant subscriptions and platform billing, not a custom connector, admin renewal book, messaging route, or service/customer list. It is not DORA, REDBlocked, BioSignal, HeritageDoor, TraceFaktura, BatteryFit, PromoLeak, CBAM, Data Act, GreenTender, Supplement Stack, HeatQuiet, Amazon Vendor Central, 3PL, OTA, RMA, field-action recovery, KSeF, cyber-insurance, AI-security, or physical-lot ideas.

Strongest anticipated objections:
The best small apps may already be bought by micro-SaaS buyers or app studios. Cheap available apps may have bad code, fake reviews, poor retention, unresolved policy issues, privacy risk, platform-deprecation risk, or hidden support load. Shopify app transfer and billing continuity may be more complex than assumed. Merchants can churn at the handoff moment if trust drops or competitors offer better apps. One app may be too small to justify founder time. Support can turn into custom Shopify development. A platform API/privacy change can require urgent engineering work. Seller earnout alignment may be hard if the seller is disengaged. The 100,000 PLN budget is only safe if upfront payment is tiny and the first payout clears before meaningful seller consideration.
