Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections.

Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time.

Idea name:
Part-IS Aviation Supplier Evidence Pack

One-sentence thesis:
A vendor-side 7-business-day evidence-response pack for Polish/CEE aviation software, IT, maintenance, training, airport-ops, ground-handling, and specialist outsourced-service suppliers whose aviation customer renewal, procurement, access, or contract update is blocked by EASA Part-IS information-security interface or supplier-risk evidence requests.

Exact buyer:
20-200 person Polish/CEE and EU-adjacent aviation software, IT, operational technology, maintenance-planning, CAMO, MRO workflow, EFB support, training/LMS, simulator-support, ground-handling, airport-ops, aerodrome, AIS/data, and specialist outsourced-service vendors selling to airlines, Part-145 MROs, CAMOs, ATOs, FSTD operators, aerodromes, ANSPs, aviation authorities, and aviation groups.

The best first buyers have 2-10 aviation customers, no large compliance team, and a live request from an aviation customer that matters commercially.

Acute trigger:
A regulated aviation customer sends a live procurement, renewal, audit, access, contract-update, or supplier-risk request tied to EASA Part-IS / information-security interface risk. The request asks for supplier controls, incident reporting, access control, subcontractor oversight, data-flow, BCP/DR, security ownership, monitoring, audit/access rights, or evidence that the supplier can support the customer's Part-IS obligations.

Control point:
Prepaid live customer-request case with:
- exact customer questionnaire, clause request, audit item, access renewal, or procurement blocker;
- vendor evidence room: security policy, access-control notes, incident process, BCP/DR, subcontractor list, architecture/data-flow, hosting locations, SLA, support model, vulnerability/patch process, and prior customer answers;
- aviation/customer context: service used, data/systems touched, remote access/interface, safety-impact boundary, and contracted activity notes;
- buyer-ready response pack and gap split;
- aviation cyber/Part-IS reviewer for flagged sections;
- no legal advice, no Part-IS certification, no customer-side ISMS implementation, and no claim that the supplier itself is Part-IS compliant unless it actually is in scope and advised.

60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof:
1. Three prepaid live aviation supplier evidence cases at 12,000-30,000 PLN each.
2. At least two different aviation customers represented by the incoming requests.
3. One aviation cybersecurity / Part-IS reviewer on paid per-case terms.
4. Two customer-progress outcomes: questionnaire accepted for next procurement step, renewal not blocked on supplier evidence, access restored or extended, customer security/procurement closes the immediate evidence gap, or clause negotiation moves to legal without the vendor's evidence pack being the blocker.
5. 50,000-90,000 PLN collected.
6. Reusable case taxonomy: customer type, service/interface, evidence requested, source artifact, unsafe answer, remediation/legal review needed, accepted wording, and buyer pushback.

6-month POC:
Month 1: build a target list of 80 Polish/CEE/EU aviation vendors with visible airline, MRO, CAMO, ATO, FSTD, airport, ANSP, aerodrome, ground-handling, aviation authority, or aviation-group customers; recruit one aviation cybersecurity / Part-IS reviewer; build a redacted sample response pack from dummy customer request wording; and contact vendors with a live-request-only offer.

Month 2: close three prepaid cases from vendors with current aviation customer requests. Build the evidence index, questionnaire/answer pack, supplier interface map, subcontractor/access/incident/BCP evidence summary, and gap split.

Month 3: secure at least two customer-progress outcomes and document no-go cases: no live customer request, too legal, needs full Part-IS implementation, needs ISO 27001/SOC work first, no evidence room, no customer consequence, or no budget.

Months 4-6: complete 10-18 paid live customer-request cases, collect 150,000-350,000 PLN, keep gross margin above 60% after reviewer cost, get at least one aviation cyber/compliance partner or customer-side adviser sending two cases, and build case memory by customer type, service/interface, evidence requested, accepted wording, buyer pushback, and remediation/legal escalation pattern.

First acquisition mechanism:
Vendor pitch:
"If an airline, MRO, CAMO, ATO, FSTD operator, airport, ANSP, or aviation group is asking for Part-IS supplier evidence before renewal, access, contract update, or procurement, send the exact request and your existing evidence folder. I turn it into a buyer-ready response pack in 7 business days: what you can safely answer now, what needs technical/security remediation, what needs legal review, what needs management approval, and what should not be answered without adviser input."

Partner pitch:
"You keep the high-value Part-IS, aviation cyber, ISO, audit, remediation, and legal work. I handle urgent vendor-side evidence collation and customer-response overflow for smaller aviation suppliers that have a live buyer blocker but are not ready for a broad consulting project."

Economics:
Charge 12,000-18,000 PLN for a single live customer evidence request, 20,000-30,000 PLN for a 7-business-day renewal/procurement rescue with reviewer pass, and 6,000-12,000 PLN for annual refresh or changed-since-last-review update. Reviewer costs are scoped per case. The fee is justified only when a material aviation renewal, procurement, access right, or strategic customer is at risk.

Copy risk:
Aviation cyber consultants, Part-IS consultants, ISO 27001 firms, aviation compliance advisers, law firms, and larger GRC vendors can copy the surface checklist. The defense is vendor-side speed, Polish/CEE aviation supplier focus, live customer-request routing, bilingual evidence packaging, aviation-interface case memory, and accepted/pushed-back wording by customer type and service/interface.

Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream:
Once the vendor routes a live aviation customer request, signs the prepaid case, grants evidence-room access, and uses the startup's response pack under the procurement or renewal clock, another provider must displace an active evidence workflow. The controlled unit is the exact customer request, vendor evidence folder, aviation service/interface map, reviewer notes, response wording, and customer progress or pushback outcome.

Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker:
The startup accepts only live aviation customer-request cases with prepaid fees and named evidence folders. The output is a buyer-ready response pack for a specific renewal, procurement, access, audit, or contract-update blocker, not a Part-IS readiness report, legal memo, certification, dashboard, marketplace, broad GRC app, or brokered expert call.

Boundaries and legal/provider limits:
The startup does not give legal advice, certify Part-IS compliance, implement the customer's ISMS, perform formal audit, provide ISO/SOC certification, make final security commitments, negotiate contract clauses, or represent the vendor to aviation authorities. The vendor remains responsible for truthfulness, management-approved statements, security controls, customer communications, legal interpretation, remediation, and contractual commitments. Aviation cyber, Part-IS, legal, ISO, audit, and remediation specialists handle flagged issues where needed.

Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas:
Closest confirmed idea: DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack.

This is materially different from DORA because DORA serves software vendors selling to regulated finance customers under ICT third-party-risk pressure. This serves aviation suppliers/vendors responding to EASA Part-IS information-security interface and contracted-activity evidence requests. Buyer/customer context, source rules, evidence map, service interfaces, terminology, reviewer bench, and procurement channels are aviation-specific. The proof artifact is an aviation Part-IS supplier evidence response pack, not a DORA Article 28/30/ICT supplier evidence pack.

It should be killed if the market says DORA/cyber firms naturally absorb aviation Part-IS supplier requests without a separate aviation-specific wedge.

Strongest anticipated objections:
- The aviation supplier pool may be much smaller than financial-sector DORA vendors.
- Aviation customers may handle Part-IS supplier oversight internally and may not send many urgent vendor-side evidence requests yet.
- Aviation cyber/Part-IS consultants, ISO firms, aviation compliance advisers, and law firms can copy the offer.
- Unknown founder credibility is weak without a named aviation cyber/Part-IS reviewer.
- Some requests may require full Part-IS implementation, ISO 27001, formal audit, contract negotiation, or remediation rather than an evidence pack.
- Supplier evidence may not exist or may reveal real control gaps that cannot be packaged safely.
- Handling aviation security and access evidence creates trust and confidentiality barriers.
- The idea may be too close structurally to the already-confirmed DORA Bank Renewal Evidence Pack unless aviation-specific requests, wording, and partner channels repeat.
