# Real Working Validation - Round 330

## Candidate

CAPEACH Electronic Refund Enrollment Rail

## Gate

- Simulated score: 88.0
- Working Zero To One score: 69
- Active working gate: >=85
- Decision: FAIL

## Zero To One Summary

Zero To One treated the idea as a narrow customs-refund operations rail. It accepted the factual base: CAPE Phase 1 for IEEPA refunds is live in ACE, and electronic refund handling can require ACE importer access, ACH refund authorization, U.S. bank information, Form 5106-linked records, and refund-monitoring workflows.

The score stayed low because the candidate is mostly administrative readiness after the real customs-value question has already been solved. That makes it legally cleaner than the broader CAPE refund batch desk, but also less valuable, easier for customs brokers to internalize, and likely temporary.

## Strongest Objections

- Customs brokers handling CAPE refunds should already know how to check ACE access, ACH refund enrollment, Form 5106 contact data, and refund-monitoring reports.
- The high-value work is entry eligibility, CAPE declaration logic, tariff treatment, liquidation/reliquidation, protest preservation, and customs/legal interpretation, all outside the startup's scope.
- IEEPA refund operations may create a temporary 2026 campaign, not a durable company.
- Public CBP guidance makes the setup appear self-service, even if operationally annoying.
- Trust friction is high because the workflow touches ACE records, importer records, bank refund information, CBP reports, broker exports, and refund status data.
- Success-fee attribution is weak if CBP would have paid after normal enrollment.
- Brokers can use the founder once, then assign junior staff or automate the checklist.
- Edge cases rapidly become customs/legal work involving protest timing, eligibility, liquidation status, importer rights, surety disputes, drawback, reconciliation, or refund entitlement.
- Banking and refund-recipient corrections create fraud, authority, ownership, insolvency, and internal-control risk.
- CEE/EU importer acquisition is likely slow unless U.S. customs brokers route repeat clusters.

## Evaluator's Best Version

Broker-channel validation only:

- named importer of record;
- expected refund above USD 50,000 or broker-routed batch;
- broker/importer already owns CAPE eligibility and filing;
- clear ACE/ACH/Form 5106/refund-monitoring defect;
- importer authorization;
- fixed setup fee paid;
- no refund custody and no customs/legal interpretation.

The evaluator's corrected 60-day proof was 2-3 broker/importer mandates, 2-4 setup files, 2-3 objective ACE/ACH/Form 5106/refund-monitoring outcomes, one refund-status trace or payment-date confirmation, and 25,000-75,000 PLN collected with no success-fee reliance.

## Gate Decision

Do not fresh-validate. Continue to Round 331.
