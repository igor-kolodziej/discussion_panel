Score this business idea 0-100 and include strongest objections. Founder context: unknown Warsaw solo founder, contractors allowed, max 100,000 PLN, 6-month POC, part-time. Idea name: ShowComms Intercom Fleet Lockbox. One-sentence thesis: buy or option a coherent, tested production-intercom fleet from a retiring or upgrading AV rental firm, theatre, venue, broadcaster, or event supplier, then rent or resell the exact serial-numbered fleet only after AV buyers place deposits for dated shows or fixed delivery windows. Exact buyer: small and mid-sized AV rental firms, theatre and venue technical managers, conference AV firms, broadcast and event production suppliers, and touring production companies in Poland and CEE that already use a named intercom ecosystem but need extra compatible packs for upcoming events. Acute trigger: a show, convention, festival, broadcast, corporate event, or theatre run has a rider or production plan requiring more compatible intercom beltpacks, base units, headsets, chargers, batteries, antennas, cables, and spares than the buyer owns; without them the buyer must expensive-subhire, lose margin, disappoint the production manager, or decline the job. Control point: title or purchase option over one coherent compatible intercom fleet with serial list, photos, accessory list, condition grades, bench-test log, battery notes, firmware/config notes where needed, cases, and signed buyer deposits or bookings tied to exact event dates or delivery windows. 60-day signed/titled/assigned/prepaid proof: one signed option or bill of sale over at least 20 compatible packs/units with accessories; bench test and condition log from a named AV technician or rental-house engineer; three signed rental/resale reservations or deposits from AV buyers; at least 40,000 PLN in deposits, rental commitments, or resale margin tied to the exact fleet; first paid subhire or resale invoice before total capital exposure exceeds 100,000 PLN. 6-month POC: control 1-2 coherent intercom fleets, complete at least 6 paid rentals/resales, recover capital on the first fleet, show 20,000-60,000 PLN gross spread or subhire margin, keep failed/returned units below 10%, prove at least two repeat AV buyers, and document which systems/accessories sell fastest. First acquisition mechanism: call retiring or upgrading AV rental houses, theatres, broadcasters, venues, production suppliers, insolvency/asset sellers, and installers with a specific offer to buy the whole intercom fleet quickly if serials/accessories/testability are clear; in parallel ask AV rental firms and production buyers for dated deposits on compatible fleet needs before closing the purchase. Economics: acquire a coherent older fleet for 45,000-90,000 PLN because the seller wants one buyer and no single-unit listing/support burden; earn resale spread, dated subhire fees, or split-lot margin only after deposits; target 50%+ gross margin on incremental subhire once tested and cased. Copy risk: AV dealers, rental houses, OEM distributors, theatres, broadcasters, and production suppliers already understand intercom gear, so the model is not defensible as a generic used-gear dealer. Why incumbents cannot copy before the founder controls the specific asset/account/case/claim/lot/payment stream: once the founder controls the exact serial-numbered fleet, accessory set, test log, and booking/deposit calendar, competitors cannot fill those same dated bookings with that same compatible lot unless the seller or buyer breaks the agreement; the defensibility is lot-specific and date-specific. Why it is not a service, report, app, dashboard, database, marketplace, or generic broker: the startup owns or options gear, takes title and condition risk, documents units, collects deposits for exact serial-numbered hardware, and invoices rental or resale transactions; it does not sell production advice, crew staffing, RF coordination, a marketplace, or introductions. Boundaries and legal/provider limits: buyer/rental houses remain responsible for show design, crew operation, RF coordination, insurance, and production safety; reject mission-critical jobs needing 24/7 support unless a rental-house partner is contracted; reject unclear title, stolen-risk flags, unsupported batteries, firmware locks, region/frequency problems, unsafe chargers, or untestable defects. Duplicate risk against existing confirmed ideas: not HeritageDoor because the asset is compact serial-numbered event hardware with dated bookings, not architectural salvage; not REDBlocked/DORA/Data Act/CBAM/PromoLeak/TraceFaktura because there is no evidence desk, platform appeal, regulatory packet, recovery mandate, or municipal invoice rail; not the failed hotel FF&E lot because logistics are compact and demand is compatibility/event-date specific rather than generic furnishing. Strongest anticipated objections: existing AV rental houses and used-gear dealers already trade this equipment; serious buyers may prefer trusted rental partners with support, insurance, and replacements; coherent used fleets may be rare or overpriced; condition, battery health, firmware, missing accessories, region/frequency constraints, and dead units can destroy margin; part-time execution may be too slow around event dates; a single fleet is a transaction not a company unless repeat seller sourcing and buyer bookings compound.
