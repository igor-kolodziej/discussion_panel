Founder constraints: Warsaw-based data scientist with neuroscience/data/business background; unknown solo founder, possibly with a few contractors; maximum 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; low personal-brand/camera fit; part-time start.

Please cap the score below 85 if the idea lacks a concrete control point that can be secured, reserved, owned, routed, prepaid, assigned, or credibly proven during a 2-month POC/POV cycle by an unknown solo founder under these constraints. Cap below 87 if CAC, payback, gross margin, cash conversion, or owner earnings are not credible. Weight profitability, willingness to pay, CAC, margins, payback, retention, repeatability, cash conversion, and defensibility more heavily than originality. Penalize generic clinics, apps, reports, dashboards, marketplaces, coaching, patient-data businesses, and prescription-drug businesses without asset/payment-flow control.

Scored idea:

TrialDevice ePRO/Biosignal Fleet-Book Buyout acquires or options a small clinical-trial equipment/admin operator's device fleet and customer book serving Polish/Central European research sites, SMOs, CROs, and investigator-initiated studies.

It provides ready-to-deploy ePRO tablets/phones, actigraphy/sleep wearables, Bluetooth BP scales/cuffs, CO monitors for smoking studies, medication-adherence packaging accessories, and biosignal capture kits for obesity/metabolic, addiction, sleep, psychiatry, neurology, and 50-60+ workforce-health trials.

It is not a patient recruitment business, not a clinical-data interpretation product, not a medical AI tool, not a prescribing platform, and not a generic hardware store. Sponsors/CROs/sites remain responsible for protocol design, consent, data ownership, clinical interpretation, adverse-event management, and regulated trial conduct. The startup controls titled device inventory, MDM/provisioning, chain-of-custody, helpdesk/admin book, replacement logistics, and prepaid rental flow.

Buyer:
- small CROs and SMOs running Poland/CEE studies;
- private research sites and investigator-initiated trial teams;
- obesity/metabolic, addiction/smoking, sleep, psychiatry, neurology, and aging-worker research projects;
- medtech/biotech startups needing pilot data capture without buying a device fleet.

Acute trigger:
Study startup is delayed because the site lacks configured tablets/phones, actigraphy devices, CO monitors, BP devices, chargers, SIMs, MDM enrollment, participant labels, return kits, helpdesk scripts, or replacement stock. Global eCOA/ePRO vendors are expensive and slow for small local studies, while sites do not want to buy and manage fleets for short pilots.

Control point:
The acquired/optioned asset is:
- titled fleet of study tablets/phones, wearables, actigraphy/sleep devices, BP cuffs/scales, CO monitors, chargers, SIM/router kits, cases, labels, and spares;
- MDM/provisioning account and device image library where transferable;
- device chain-of-custody and return/cleaning logs;
- existing customer book from a small trial admin/equipment rental operator, SMO side business, or study coordinator service;
- sponsor/site contacts, quote templates, helpdesk scripts, courier/replacement SOPs, and rental agreements;
- prepaid rental deposits and per-study device allocation calendar;
- data-export handoff rules where sponsor/CRO owns study data and the startup only provisions devices and logistics.

The hard control is owned device capacity plus MDM/admin custody plus prepaid study rental flow.

Two-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- 5,000-15,000 PLN option/revenue-share handoff for an existing study-device/admin book or SMO/CRO coordinator side book;
- 25,000-55,000 PLN used/refurbished phones/tablets/wearables/CO monitors/BP devices, chargers, SIMs, cases, labels, return kits, spares, and MDM setup;
- 10,000-20,000 PLN legal/GDPR/device-rental/clinical-trial boundary review;
- 5,000-15,000 PLN study coordinator contractor/helpdesk/admin setup;
- 5,000-10,000 PLN courier/replacement float and insurance.

Required POC proof:
1. Signed fleet/book option or revenue-share handoff from a small trial-device/admin operator, SMO, CRO coordinator, or research-services micro-operator.
2. At least 30 owned/provisioned devices under startup custody, with serials, MDM status, cleaning/return SOPs, and replacement stock.
3. Two signed paid pilots from CRO/site/SMO/biotech buyers, at least one in obesity/metabolic, addiction/smoking, sleep, psychiatry, neurology, or 50-60+ population research.
4. At least 30,000 PLN prepaid rental/setup deposits before final fleet expansion.
5. One deployment of at least 15 devices completed with return/replacement workflow tested.
6. Clear no-clinical-interpretation/no-patient-recruitment/no-prescribing boundary.
7. Sponsor/CRO/site remains controller/owner of trial data; startup is only a device logistics/provisioning processor or no-data device lessor where possible.
8. Gross margin above 45% after device amortization, MDM, SIM/courier, support, cleaning, replacement reserve, and seller earnout.

Economics:
- 120-250 PLN per device/month rental for tablets/phones/wearables;
- 300-800 PLN setup/provisioning fee per device batch;
- 2,500-8,000 PLN per-study startup kit fee;
- replacement/failed-device SLA fee;
- return logistics and spare pool fee;
- optional helpdesk/admin retainer.

Payback logic:
The founder does not buy the full fleet before demand. Employer/clinic wellness failed because deposits were soft; here, expansion only follows CRO/site prepaid rental deposits. Used/refurbished standard devices can pay back after 4-8 rental months, faster with setup fees. High-touch medical interpretation and prescribing are excluded.

Why this is not a service/report/dashboard/database/marketplace:
The startup owns titled devices, MDM/provisioning, return logistics, serial-level custody, replacement stock, and signed study rental flow. It is not selling a dashboard or coordinating generic care. The POC only passes if devices are deployed and rental cash is prepaid.

Copy risk:
Large eCOA vendors, CROs, and sites can provide devices. The wedge is underserved small/local studies that need speed, Polish-language helpdesk scripts, local replacement stock, and lower-friction deployment. Copy risk is reduced by acquiring an existing small book/fleet and requiring prepaid study deposits before expansion.

Regulatory and data guardrails:
- Do not recruit patients, prescribe, diagnose, interpret, or advise on clinical outcomes.
- Do not sell a medical AI or clinical interpretation tool.
- Use sponsor/CRO/site protocol and consent; startup should not create new clinical data use.
- Sponsor/CRO/site remains data controller; startup either avoids study-data access or acts under a narrow processor agreement.
- Use devices only where protocol/site/CRO accepts them.
- Do not make medical-device performance claims beyond lawful device documentation and intended use.
- Avoid trial workflows that require global vendor-only validated eCOA systems or sponsor-mandated equipment.

Main risks acknowledged:
- Existing global eCOA/ePRO vendors and CROs can compete.
- Study-device requirements may be protocol-specific and not accept generic devices.
- Data-protection and device-validation requirements can become heavier than expected.
- If the founder must handle identifiable health/trial data, compliance cost rises.
- Device loss, non-return, breakage, SIM costs, support load, and cleaning can reduce margin.
- If there is no existing fleet/book to option, cold-start CRO sales may exceed 60 days.
