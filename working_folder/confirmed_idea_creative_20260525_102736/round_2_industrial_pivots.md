# Round 2 Industrial Pivots

## Why Pivot

Round 1 failed because all six ideas had real pain but operational, copyable control points. The strongest surviving pattern was industrial downtime control, but generic spare custody and used-parts trading were too copyable.

Round 2 therefore pivoted toward harder industrial assets:

- retiring or micro-integrator archive and customer succession rights;
- verified customer-owned restore vaults;
- exclusive obsolete spare option books;
- municipal OT continuity.

## Candidate: Integrator Succession RestoreRail

Combined version:

The startup signs exclusive succession/earnout agreements with retiring or micro automation integrators for PLC/HMI/robot project archives, commissioning-laptop images, cable/software notes, customer-machine mapping, and warm customer introduction rights. It then sells those customers a verified restore vault: files are indexed, projects are opened/verified where possible, passwords/release rights are documented, QR asset map and spare BOM are created, one offline restore drill is done, and obsolete spare gaps are filled through exclusive consignment/option rights from plants/integrators.

Pricing:

- 12,000-25,000 PLN setup per plant or line cluster.
- 1,000-3,000 PLN/month continuity retainer.
- 15-30% margin or commission on spares.

2-month POC:

- 1 signed exclusive integrator succession option covering at least 30 project archives and at least 10 customer-machine mappings.
- 2 paying plants at at least 12,000 PLN setup each.
- 1 verified restore drill.
- 50 obsolete spares optioned or consigned.
- 1 on-call automation contractor.

Primary control point:

Assigned/exclusive access to otherwise inaccessible integrator archives, customer introduction rights, customer-specific verified restore vault, and optional spares rights.

Simulated score: 78/100.

Reason failed:

- Control point is real only if the succession agreement is enforceable, exclusive, assignable, and includes archive custody, introductions, password/license status, and spares rights.
- Founder-market fit and CAC remain weak because the sale is industrial and trust-heavy.
- Setup price must be higher to absorb contractor time, travel, verification, and integrator payout.
- Local integrators and OT firms can copy the service layer; the hard-to-copy part is each acquired archive/customer bundle, which compounds slowly.

## Candidate: Exclusive Obsolete Automation Spares Option Book

The startup signs exclusive 60-180 day consignment/option rights over named PLC/HMI/drive SKUs from factories, integrators, and used-machine dealers, tests and photographs the parts, then sells emergency stock through exact-part SEO, Allegro, and outbound to maintenance teams and integrators.

Simulated score: 64/100.

Reason failed:

- Radwell, ADEGIS, Gal-Industry, Automa.Net, Allegro/eBay, and repair houses already serve the market.
- SKU-level options are better than scraping marketplaces but still fragmented and temporary.
- Testing credibility and warranties are hard for an unknown founder.

## Candidate: Municipal Water/Wastewater OT Continuity Pack

Small utilities buy a setup covering PLC/HMI/SCADA backups, asset mapping, spare BOM, emergency call tree, and restore simulation.

Simulated score: 55/100.

Reason failed:

- Public utility sales are slow, trust-heavy, and relationship-driven.
- Local automation contractors can bundle this into maintenance.
- Founder fit is weak without a known OT practitioner or public-sector route.

## Decision

No Round 2 candidate cleared the simulated `>87` gate. Do not contact real Zero To One with this round.
