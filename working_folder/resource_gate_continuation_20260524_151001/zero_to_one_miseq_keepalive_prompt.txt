Founder constraints: Warsaw data scientist with neuroscience, data science, and business-analysis background; unknown solo founder; max 100,000 PLN starting capital; 2-month POC/POV; part-time start; no celebrity, privileged introductions, or influencer distribution.

Idea: MiSeq KeepAlive Parts Mandate.

MiSeq KeepAlive secures exclusive options or salvage-right mandates over named retired, dead, or soon-to-be-decommissioned Illumina MiSeq RUO, MiniSeq, iSeq, and older HiSeq instruments in Poland/CEE/EU, then sells tested, serial-numbered replacement modules and first-call part reservations to small genetics labs, university sequencing cores, and independent service technicians trying to keep validated legacy workflows alive through EOL transitions.

Buyer: small molecular genetics labs, university sequencing cores, ecology/microbiology sequencing groups, veterinary genetics labs, and independent Illumina service technicians using or servicing older instruments where replacing a platform would require new budget approval, assay revalidation, staff retraining, and delayed sample runs.

Acute trigger: a pump, flow-cell stage component, laser/optics module, touch screen, onboard computer, valve, power supply, fluidics element, barcode/scanner, or board fails on an older sequencing instrument after the OEM has announced EOL/supply transition or no longer wants to support that model as a first-priority platform. The buyer needs a specific tested part from the same model family fast enough to avoid replacing a still-useful sequencer.

Primary control point: signed exclusive 90-180 day option or salvage-right mandate over a named instrument or parts lot. Example: Seller A, MiSeq RUO serial/model, status: dead optics but fluidics intact, location, ownership proof, accessory list, software PC present/not present, exclusive parts-harvest mandate for 120 days, no competing resale/service sale during mandate, option fee or revenue share. This is assigned control over named EOL instrument modules before they reach generic surplus dealers.

2-month POC under 100,000 PLN:
- Identify 60-100 EU labs, university cores, liquidators, refurb sellers, and service technicians with older Illumina instruments or upgrade/decommission signals.
- Secure 3-5 signed salvage-right mandates/options over named dead or retired instruments, paying small option fees only after ownership and model details are verified.
- Acquire or option 1-2 low-cost dead instruments/parts lots within 20,000-60,000 PLN total.
- Contract one sequencing-instrument service technician for part-identification, basic module testing, and compatibility triage.
- Publish a private serial-numbered parts availability sheet to a narrow buyer list, not a public marketplace.
- Collect at least 5 paid first-call reservations or part-search deposits from labs/service technicians tied to specific model families and modules.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: retiring labs and university cores often treat dead instruments as storage burden or liquidation clutter. Small option fees and revenue-share mandates are attractive when the alternative is scrap, storage, or low-value auction. A data-science founder can systematically map EOL notices, lab publications, equipment listings, and buyer part requests; the technical gap is contractor-fillable by one independent service technician during the POC.

Why incumbents cannot copy with one contract: the OEM is structurally disincentivized to create a secondary keep-alive market because it wants migration to current platforms and official support lifecycles. Generic equipment resellers sell whole instruments and do not maintain a verified, module-level, demand-matched inventory for specific EOL failures. Independent service technicians have trust but fragmented supply and limited working capital. Once MiSeq KeepAlive signs a mandate over a named dead instrument, that exact parts source cannot be copied.

Economics: supply option fee 500-3,000 PLN per named instrument or revenue share; dead instrument/parts lot acquisition 5,000-40,000 PLN depending on model/completeness; buyer first-call reservation/search deposit 1,000-5,000 PLN creditable to purchase; part/module sale 2,000-20,000 PLN depending on module scarcity and urgency. One successful module sale can repay a small option/acquisition; a whole dead instrument can yield multiple sellable modules if compatibility testing works.

Kill criteria: no signed salvage-right mandate over at least three named instruments/lots within 45 days; no paid buyer reservations/search deposits for exact model families/modules within 60 days; technician says modules cannot be tested or reused reliably enough to sell; most available instruments are priced near refurbished-working value; buyers require OEM-only parts or refuse third-party parts even for research-use workflows.
