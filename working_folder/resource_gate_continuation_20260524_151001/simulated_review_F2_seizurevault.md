# Simulated Zero To One Review: F2 SeizureVault Rare Event EEG Options

## Score

**82 / 100**

## Diagnosis

SeizureVault starts from a real scarcity: usable ictal EEG events are much more valuable than generic EEG volume, and event density can create a named-asset control point. The idea is better than a report, desk, or routing service because the startup would option specific archives before buyer demand is exposed. The score is capped because the 2-month proof depends on two uncertain things at once: small private practices having enough verified seizure-event density, and those practices having a legally workable path for commercial secondary use of health data. If either fails, the business becomes a slow clinical-data brokerage with long sales cycles and weak founder leverage.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier 2: conditional named-asset option control.**

The strongest asset is an exclusive option over named archives with verified seizure-event segments. It is not Tier 1 until the founder can show signed options, a legal/DPO route, and a buyer inspection deposit for specific event bundles. Without that, the control point is only a sourcing claim.

## Strongest Copy Risk

An established clinical-data company, CRO, hospital consortium, or seizure-device company can contract directly with epilepsy monitoring units and produce a more authoritative dataset once buyer willingness to pay is proven. The startup’s only defense is pre-optioned named event-rich archives plus extraction/annotation know-how; that defense is fragile if the archives are not legally clean or event-dense.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **Legal basis may cap the business below venture quality.** EEG seizure events are health data; commercial reuse may require strict controller/processor structures, consent analysis, anonymization proof, ethics review, or patient-level permissions.
- **Supply may be illusory.** Private practices may have many routine EEGs but few true ictal events; event-dense archives usually sit in hospitals or epilepsy monitoring units, which are slower and more political.
- **Buyer universe is narrower than PSG.** Seizure-detection and neurostimulation buyers exist, but they are fewer than sleep/wearable/respiratory buyers, and many will require clinical provenance, labels, and validation quality that a small archive may not provide.
- **Sales cycles may be too long.** CROs, pharma, and regulated device companies may inspect but delay payment until legal, data-quality, and clinical-governance concerns are resolved.
- **Retention is not automatic.** Buyers may need one or two validation datasets, not recurring subscriptions, unless SeizureVault keeps adding rare subtypes, demographics, device formats, and longitudinal outcomes.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

The gross margin could be attractive if the founder secures event bundles cheaply and sells high-value validation access: option fees of several thousand PLN per archive could support 30,000-150,000 PLN licenses. Cash conversion is the concern. First buyers will likely require heavy trust work, legal review, sample inspection, and clinical provenance before payment. CAC could be tolerable if the founder targets a short list of seizure-AI, ambulatory EEG, neurostim, and CRO buyers directly, but payback is not proven until at least one buyer pays an inspection deposit for a named event bundle rather than merely signing an LOI.

## Best Move

**Narrow / research further, not pursue as the leading finalist yet.**

The best move is to merge the scarcity insight into a more legally and commercially accessible wedge: either event-dense archives with signed options and paid buyer inspection deposits inside 60 days, or abandon it in favor of the broader PSG archive rights bank where buyer density, use cases, and event volume are stronger. For F2 to advance, the POC must prove all three facts quickly:

1. At least 50-150 verified event candidates exist in small/private archives.
2. At least 3 archive holders sign exclusivity over named assets with a credible legal route.
3. At least 2 buyers pay refundable inspection deposits, not just express interest.

If those are not proven under 100,000 PLN in 2 months, kill this shape.
