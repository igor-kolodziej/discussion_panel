# Strict Simulated Zero To One Review: F3 Orphan Dental Implant Compatibility Bank

## Score

**87 / 100**

## Diagnosis

This is one of the stronger resource-first candidates because it starts from a real named physical inventory problem: older dental implants can remain clinically salvageable while the exact prosthetic component, scan body, driver, screw, or CAD reference disappears from normal distribution. The economic pain is credible because the alternative can be explantation, grafting, months of delay, and a much larger treatment plan for a 50-60-year-old working patient. The score is capped below 90 because the control point is only powerful if the inventory is sealed, traceable, legally distributable under MDR obligations, and accepted by dentists; without those proofs it becomes risky medical-device gray-market resale. The 2-month POC can prove supply and paid searches, but may not fully prove clinician adoption and regulatory safety.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier B / hard physical inventory with regulatory-condition risk.**

Owned sealed lots and signed options over discontinued components are materially stronger than service, routing, or expertise. The control point is weaker than Tier A because a large dental distributor or implant specialist could copy the category if it accepts the liability and because many found lots may be unusable if traceability, packaging, lot data, or CE/MDR status is incomplete.

## Strongest Copy Risk

The strongest copy risk is a dental distributor, implant-lab network, or manufacturer rep quietly adding an orphan-parts desk backed by existing dentist trust and compliant distribution infrastructure. They may not want to cannibalize current implant systems, but if the economics are proven, one motivated specialist can source similar lots and sell with more credibility.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **MDR traceability risk:** Unsealed or poorly documented components cannot be used; distributor obligations, complaint handling, storage records, and traceability must be treated seriously from day one.
- **Dentist adoption risk:** The dentist bears clinical risk. Many may prefer a new treatment plan to using a part sourced by an unknown startup.
- **Bypass risk:** If the founder reveals the supplier or exact inventory source too early, dentists/labs can bypass the platform.
- **Inventory toxicity:** The founder may buy hundreds of SKUs with little demand or missing compatibility context.
- **Small repeatability problem:** Individual patients have acute pain, but repeat revenue depends on dentists repeatedly sending searches and the inventory map compounding.
- **Trust problem:** The business must look like a compliant niche distributor, not opportunistic e-commerce.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

The economics can work. Deadstock components bought in drawers or liquidation lots may cost tens to low hundreds of PLN per unit and sell for several hundred to a few thousand PLN when they prevent a large replacement plan. Search and reserve deposits improve cash conversion and reduce inventory speculation. CAC can be efficient if acquisition starts through exact implant-system searches, dental-lab calls, and a narrow group of rescue-oriented dentists rather than broad consumer marketing. Payback is plausible within two months only if the founder collects dentist/patient deposits before scaling inventory.

## Best Move

**Narrow, not confirm yet.**

The threshold version should stop trying to be a universal orphan-implant bank and become a legally conservative **non-implantable compatibility-and-tool bank first**: scan bodies, drivers, torque adapters, lab analogs, CAD/library assets, and only fully sealed/traceable abutment or screw components when a regulatory consultant approves the category. The required POC proof is: signed options over named traceable drawers, 10 paid search deposits for exact implant systems, written clinician willingness from 5 dentists, and a short MDR distributor-obligation memo. If those four proofs exist, this could become a strong company; without them it should not go to real validation.
