# Strict Simulated Zero To One Review: R2-1 SomnoConsent PSG Reconsent Rights Bank

## Exact Scored Idea Wording

SomnoConsent is a Poland/CEE biosignal rights company that signs exclusive mandates with small and retiring sleep labs to convert named archived PSG/HSAT recordings into explicitly patient-consented commercial validation assets. The founder does not assume old pseudonymised health data can simply be sold. Instead, the POC proves a lawful conversion machine: lab-controlled reconsent outreach, explicit patient consent for algorithm validation/research/commercial health-AI testing, secure inventory of raw EDF/scoring metadata, and refundable inspection deposits from sleep-AI, respiratory diagnostics, CPAP, wearable, and medtech buyers. The control point is the exclusive right to convert named dormant sleep archives into consented validation cohorts before competitors can access those same recordings.

The hidden asset is not "old PSG data"; it is the legally and operationally difficult conversion of retained clinical archives into explicitly consented, buyer-relevant validation cohorts. The business can have attractive gross margins because reconsent, extraction, QA, and secure access cost far less than prospective multi-site PSG collection, while buyers have a real need for independent, real-world PSG/HSAT validation data. The score clears the simulated threshold because the 2-month POC can secure a named control point: signed exclusive lab mandates, first patient consents, and buyer deposits, all under 100,000 PLN.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier A- / exclusive named-asset conversion rights.**

The control point is materially stronger than an evidence desk, consulting service, dashboard, or data catalog. It can be expressed in signed mandates over named archives, patient-level consents, metadata inventories, and buyer deposits. It is not a perfect Tier A asset because consent response rates and data quality remain uncertain, but the POC can produce hard proof: signed exclusivity, actual consents, and paid inspection demand.

## Strongest Copy Risk

The strongest copy risk is a clinical-data company, CRO, or sleep-device vendor copying the reconsent playbook with other sleep labs after SomnoConsent proves demand. They cannot copy the exact lab mandates, patient consents, metadata inventory, or buyer relationships already secured. The defense is to lock enough small labs quickly, standardize the reconsent and EDF/score QA workflow, and become the default archive-conversion buyer for retiring and under-monetized sleep labs in Poland/CEE.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **Reconsent may underperform.** If former patients ignore outreach or distrust commercial validation use, the asset does not convert.
- **Labs may fear reputational risk.** Sleep labs may hesitate to contact patients for a commercial data-use campaign even with DPO review and revenue share.
- **Buyer deposits are the truth test.** LOIs are not enough; at least one or two buyers must pay for inspection rights before large cleanup spend.
- **Data quality may be uneven.** Old EDFs may have missing channels, inconsistent scorers, poor metadata, or proprietary export friction.
- **Legal route must be specific.** Explicit consent helps, but the POC still needs Article 6 basis, Article 9 exception analysis, DPA/controller structure, transfer safeguards, and withdrawal handling.
- **Not a monopoly by default.** The company has to become the fastest acquirer of lab mandates and consented cohorts, not merely one data vendor.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

The economics are credible. A 60-day POC can spend roughly 20,000-30,000 PLN on legal/DPO templates, 10,000-20,000 PLN on lab option fees, 15,000-40,000 PLN on patient incentives, and 10,000-20,000 PLN on extraction/secure inventory. If two buyers each pay 5,000-15,000 PLN refundable inspection deposits, early cash conversion is partially proven before serious cleanup. Full benchmark access can plausibly price at 40,000-120,000 PLN for a narrow evaluation package and much higher for broader licensed/secure-enclave access. Gross margins become attractive after fixed legal/extraction workflow is reusable across labs.

CAC is manageable because both sides are narrow and identifiable: private sleep labs on the supply side; sleep-AI, respiratory diagnostics, CPAP, oximetry, wearable, and medtech validation teams on the demand side. Payback depends on buyer deposits during the POC. Retention is plausible through recurring cohort expansion, new clinical domains, rescoring, benchmark refreshes, regulatory-prep studies, and failure-case packages.

## Best Move

**Advance to real Zero To One validation.**

Do not broaden the pitch. The real validation wording should emphasize four facts:

1. The asset is **exclusive conversion rights over named sleep-lab archives**, not generic data brokerage.
2. The legal route is **explicit patient reconsent** and secure processing, not assuming pseudonymised old data can be sold.
3. The 2-month proof is hard: **2 lab mandates, 50-150 consents, metadata inventory, legal memo, and 2 buyer deposits** under 100,000 PLN.
4. The kill criteria are unforgiving: no lab exclusivity, no patient consents, or no buyer deposits means abandon the idea.
