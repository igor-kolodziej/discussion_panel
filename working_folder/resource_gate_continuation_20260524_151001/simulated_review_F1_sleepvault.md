# Strict Simulated Zero To One Review: F1 SleepVault PSG Archive Rights Bank

## Score

**92 / 100**

## Diagnosis

SleepVault is the first candidate in this continuation run that starts from a resource incumbents do not already package cleanly: named, messy, legally retained raw sleep-signal archives sitting inside small sleep labs that are not data merchants. The buyer pain is real and budgeted because sleep-AI, respiratory diagnostics, wearable, and digital-health companies need reference PSG comparison data for validation, certification planning, investor diligence, and enterprise credibility, while public datasets are overused and hospital data access is slow. The control point is not a report, dashboard, marketplace, or coordination layer; it is an exclusive option queue over named archives plus the legal/provenance/data-cleaning route that turns dormant EDF files into inspection-ready validation assets. The main reason it clears 90 in simulation is that a two-month POC can prove the hard resource without pretending full data licensing is already solved: signed archive options, redacted inventory, DPO/lawyer memo, and buyer inspection deposits.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier A-: named-asset option control with legal-clearance condition.**

The control point is strong because the founder can secure exclusive options over specific archives before the market sees them. Once optioned, the exact lab archive cannot be copied by a vendor contract or equipment purchase. It is not a perfect Tier A because health-data reuse and anonymization remain legal gating issues; the asset only becomes fully monetizable after the legal route is confirmed.

## Strongest Copy Risk

The strongest copy risk is an established clinical-data broker, CRO, or sleep-tech company going directly to larger hospitals or sleep networks after SleepVault proves demand. The defense is to avoid broad "data brokerage" and win a neglected supply class first: small Polish/CEE sleep labs, retiring lab owners, and under-migrated EDF archives that large buyers do not want to negotiate one by one. The startup compounds by optioning named archives, building device/scorer/provenance normalization, and becoming the fastest route to EU sleep-signal validation data for small and mid-sized buyers.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **GDPR / health-data basis is the real choke point.** Pseudonymized PSG remains personal health data. The POC must pay for a DPO/lawyer memo and use secure-enclave or controlled-inspection structures rather than assuming "anonymized" export is easy.
- **Lab willingness is uncertain.** Some labs may fear reputational, patient, or regulator risk and refuse even paid archive options.
- **Buyer diligence may slow cash conversion.** Medtech buyers may want legal, provenance, data-quality, and annotation evidence before paying more than a small inspection deposit.
- **Archive quality can be uneven.** Old EDF files may have missing channels, inconsistent scoring, bad metadata, or incompatible device formats.
- **Public datasets weaken generic value.** SleepVault must sell EU/local/commercial-rights/provenance/heterogeneity and validation utility, not raw PSG as a commodity.
- **Not all rights are ownable.** The founder may control commercial-readiness options and revenue-share mandates, not outright data ownership.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

Profitability is plausible. Supply acquisition can be cheap because labs have dormant archives and no internal monetization path: 3,000-8,000 PLN option fees per lab plus revenue share. Demand acquisition is concentrated: the founder can target a short list of sleep-AI, wearable, oximetry/PPG, respiratory, neurotech, and digital-biomarker companies with precise outreach, not broad marketing. Buyer willingness to pay is credible because validation data can influence certification, fundraising, enterprise sales, and product claims. Gross margins are attractive after legal/QA setup; the first sale or secure benchmark access could be 40,000-120,000 PLN, with larger licenses possible after legal clearance. Payback can be proven within two months through refundable buyer inspection deposits rather than full enterprise procurement.

## Best Move

**Advance to real Zero To One validation, but only with the stricter POC wording.**

The scored idea should explicitly state:

1. The POC sells signed exclusive archive options and secure inspection deposits, not uncontrolled health-data export.
2. Pseudonymized data is treated as personal health data unless legal/DPO review supports a narrower route.
3. The first supply is small Polish/CEE sleep labs with named EDF archives, not hospitals or generic public datasets.
4. Kill criteria include failure to sign at least three exclusive archive options and at least two paid buyer inspection deposits inside 60 days.
