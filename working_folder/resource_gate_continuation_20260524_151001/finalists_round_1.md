## F1. SleepVault PSG Archive Rights Bank

**Idea:** SleepVault is a Poland/CEE biosignal data-rights company that secures exclusive options over named archived raw polysomnography and home sleep test recordings from small, retiring, or under-monetized sleep labs, then turns legally cleared archives into a secure validation benchmark for sleep-AI, respiratory diagnostics, wearable, and digital-health companies.

**Buyer:** EU/US sleep-AI, oximetry/PPG, wearable, respiratory diagnostics, and medtech companies that need independent PSG comparison data before certification, investor diligence, clinical validation design, or enterprise sale.

**Acute pain:** Public sleep datasets are useful but overused, demographically imperfect, and not always commercially/regionally suitable. Companies claiming sleep staging, apnea detection, arousal detection, or respiratory-event estimation need raw reference signals and scored labels from real clinical workflows, but negotiating one-off hospital data access is slow.

**Control point:** Signed exclusive commercial-readiness options over named archives at specific sleep labs: e.g. "Lab A, 2016-2024, 1,400 EDF nights, consent/legal status to be reviewed, metadata inventory, no competing commercial data option for 12 months." The founder does not buy anonymous generic data; he controls a queue of named archives and their cleanup/provenance rights before competitors.

**2-month POC under 100,000 PLN:** Spend 15-30k PLN on lawyer/DPO route memo and templates; pay 3-8k PLN option fees to 4-8 small labs for 90-180 day exclusivity over named archives; inventory 300-800 redacted nights locally/securely without uncontrolled raw export; obtain 2-3 buyer LOIs or refundable inspection deposits of 5-15k PLN for a secure sample data room after legal clearance. Kill if no lab signs exclusivity or no buyer deposits.

**Why an unknown founder can secure it:** Small sleep labs are not data businesses, have legally retained archives, face storage/migration burden, and lack the time/legal/data-engineering skill to monetize old EDF files. The founder offers small upfront option fees, no patient-care disruption, no raw export before legal review, and a revenue share on otherwise dormant assets.

**Why incumbents cannot copy with one contract/process/equipment purchase:** The asset is not equipment or a vendor relationship; it is fragmented named archives with local provenance, device heterogeneity, scorer labels, and legal-chain documentation. Once optioned, the same archive is unavailable. Large medtech vendors can negotiate hospital studies, but they are slow and conflicted; broad data brokers lack sleep-lab relationships and signal cleanup depth.

**Economics:** Option cost 3-8k PLN per lab plus legal/cleanup; first benchmark access priced 40-120k PLN per buyer for secure evaluation, or 150-400k PLN for broader licensed data rights once legal route is proven. Gross margin is high after extraction/QA/legal review. Repeat revenue comes from additional buyers, new archives, rescoring, and EU validation packages.

**Main risk:** Health data legal basis and anonymization may not permit commercial reuse in the expected way; some archives may be pseudonymised personal data requiring strict processor/controller structures or EHDS-like access rather than simple licensing.

## F2. SeizureVault Rare Event EEG Options

**Idea:** SeizureVault secures exclusive options over named, legally clear EEG archives containing rare ictal seizure events from small neurology/EEG practices and retiring clinicians, then sells event-dense validation sets to seizure-detection, wearable EEG, neurostimulation, and pharma/CRO buyers.

**Buyer:** EEG seizure-detection startups, ambulatory EEG vendors, neurostimulation companies, and CROs that need real ictal events rather than mostly-normal EEG.

**Control point:** Exclusive options over verified rare event segments, not generic EEG volume. Each option names the practice/archive, approximate event count, recording system, annotation status, and legal readiness route.

**2-month POC:** Pay small option fees for 3-5 archives, audit 50-150 event candidates under data-room constraints, get 2 buyer LOIs or inspection deposits, and produce legal/DPO memo.

**Why not copyable immediately:** Rare event density is uneven and not purchasable from an equipment vendor. Hospitals with large archives are slow; small retiring practices are ignored.

**Main risk:** Commercial secondary use of EEG health data may be legally hard; private practices may not have enough event-dense archives; buyer universe may be narrower than PSG.

## F3. Orphan Dental Implant Compatibility Bank

**Idea:** Orphan Dental Implant Compatibility Bank buys and options sealed, traceable, discontinued dental implant prosthetic components, drivers, scan bodies, abutment screws, CAD libraries, and compatibility documentation from retiring dentists, closed clinics, and dental labs, then sells urgent exact-match searches and components to dentists treating 50-60-year-old patients with older implants.

**Buyer:** Dentist pays or routes patient payment; economic buyer is a 50-60-year-old patient who wants to avoid explant surgery, bone grafting, months of delay, and a large replacement plan after a crown/abutment/screw failure.

**Control point:** Owned named physical lots plus 90-day options on traceable discontinued components for exact legacy implant systems.

**2-month POC:** Buy/option 500-800 sealed SKUs for 40-70k PLN, build compatibility matrix, secure 5-10 dentists willing to use traceable components when clinically appropriate, collect 10 paid search deposits.

**Why not copyable immediately:** OEMs and distributors prefer selling current systems and avoid obsolete liability-sensitive drawers; supply is fragmented in clinic/lab deadstock.

**Main risk:** Medical-device traceability/resale and dentist willingness can kill it. If many parts are unsealed/untraceable or dentists refuse patient-sourced components, the asset is worthless.

## F4. RGP/Scleral Lens Continuity Rights

**Idea:** Acquire or option retiring independent specialty contact-lens lab assets: trial lens sets, base-curve design libraries, lathes, specialty blanks, and legacy design know-how, then provide continuity remakes/refits through licensed optometrists for 50-60-year-olds dependent on custom RGP or scleral lenses.

**Buyer:** 50-60-year-olds with keratoconus, post-LASIK ectasia, corneal grafts, severe dry eye, or irregular corneas who cannot function when a custom lens breaks and full refit is slow or risky.

**Control point:** Physical trial sets and design-library rights from retiring labs/optometrists.

**2-month POC:** Option one specialty trial set/design library for 20-50k PLN, sign two optometrists for paid clinical execution, collect 10 patient deposits.

**Why not copyable immediately:** Large optical chains prefer standard lenses/new refits and do not hold retiring lab design assets.

**Main risk:** If licensed clinical labor is the true scarce asset rather than the design rights, this becomes a boutique optometry service.

## F5. Genetic Biobank Emergency Spares Bank

**Idea:** A B2B emergency inventory bank for small genetics labs, IVF/genetics labs, academic biobanks, and hospital molecular labs holding frozen DNA/tissue/cell/plasma samples, focused only on model-specific freezer, LN2, rack, controller, tray, canister, and barcode-format spares that official service contracts do not cover quickly.

**Buyer:** Lab manager or owner responsible for irreplaceable sample continuity.

**Control point:** Owned inventory and pre-negotiated shutdown-lab surplus rights for obsolete but common sample-storage systems.

**2-month POC:** Identify 20 common models, buy high-failure spare lots from lab liquidations/surplus dealers, sign 3 paid emergency retainers or LOIs, and document a timed emergency courier drill.

**Why not copyable immediately:** OEMs prefer selling new freezers and do not stock every long-tail rack/canister/legacy part; labs hate idle dead inventory.

**Main risk:** Labs already trust service contracts and may not trust an unknown founder during a sample emergency.

## F6. Genotype-Clear Dog Germplasm Option Pool

**Idea:** Secure exclusive 90-180 day options over frozen semen from high-demand but health-problematic pedigree dog lines, pay for genotype/health documentation where missing, and sell reservation-backed breeding access to serious breeders seeking specific genetic traits without hidden carrier risk.

**Buyer:** Serious pedigree breeders and kennels in one high-inbreeding, high-WTP breed.

**Control point:** Named frozen semen lots plus founder-funded genotype/health test documentation and exclusive reservation rights.

**2-month POC:** Option 10-20 lots from retiring breeders/estates/storage clinics, run genetic documentation, get breeder deposits on 3-5 lots.

**Why not copyable immediately:** The exact named semen lots are unique and vanish into private networks; broad canine marketplaces do not normalize genotype risk for specific legacy lots.

**Main risk:** Breeder politics, veterinary reproduction trust, and small market depth may make it non-repeatable.
