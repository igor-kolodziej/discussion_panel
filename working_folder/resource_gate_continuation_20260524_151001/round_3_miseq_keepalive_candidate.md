# Round 3 Candidate: MiSeq KeepAlive Parts Mandate

## Exact Scored Idea Wording

**MiSeq KeepAlive Parts Mandate** secures exclusive options or salvage-right mandates over named retired, dead, or soon-to-be-decommissioned Illumina MiSeq RUO, MiniSeq, iSeq, and older HiSeq instruments in Poland/CEE/EU, then sells tested, serial-numbered replacement modules and "first-call" part reservations to small genetics labs, university sequencing cores, and independent service technicians trying to keep validated legacy workflows alive through EOL transitions.

## Buyer

Small molecular genetics labs, university sequencing cores, ecology/microbiology sequencing groups, veterinary genetics labs, and independent Illumina service technicians using or servicing older instruments where replacing a platform would require new budget approval, assay revalidation, staff retraining, and delayed sample runs.

## Acute Trigger

A pump, flow-cell stage component, laser/optics module, touch screen, onboard computer, valve, power supply, fluidics element, barcode/scanner, or board fails on an older sequencing instrument after the OEM has announced EOL/supply transition or no longer wants to support that model as a first-priority platform.

The buyer does not need generic advice. They need a specific tested part from the same model family fast enough to avoid replacing a still-useful sequencer.

## Primary Control Point

Signed exclusive 90-180 day option or salvage-right mandate over a named instrument or parts lot:

> Seller A, MiSeq RUO serial/model, status: dead optics but fluidics intact, location, ownership proof, accessory list, software PC present/not present, exclusive parts-harvest mandate for 120 days, no competing resale/service sale during mandate, option fee or revenue share.

The asset is not a directory or service workflow. It is assigned control over named EOL instrument modules before they reach generic surplus dealers.

## 2-Month POC Under 100,000 PLN

- Identify 60-100 EU labs, university cores, liquidators, refurb sellers, and service technicians with older Illumina instruments or upgrade/decommission signals.
- Secure 3-5 signed salvage-right mandates/options over named dead or retired instruments, paying small option fees only after ownership and model details are verified.
- Acquire or option 1-2 low-cost dead instruments/parts lots within 20,000-60,000 PLN total.
- Contract one sequencing-instrument service technician for part-identification, basic module testing, and compatibility triage.
- Publish a private serial-numbered parts availability sheet to a narrow buyer list, not a public marketplace.
- Collect at least 5 paid "first-call" reservations or part-search deposits from labs/service technicians tied to specific model families and modules.

## Why An Unknown Founder Can Secure It

The founder does not need to be an Illumina engineer to start. He needs to control distressed supply before it is scattered:

- retiring labs and university cores often treat dead instruments as storage burden or liquidation clutter;
- small option fees and revenue-share mandates are attractive when the alternative is scrap, storage, or low-value auction;
- a data-science founder can systematically map EOL notices, lab publications, equipment listings, and buyer part requests;
- the technical gap is contractor-fillable by one independent service technician during the POC.

## Why Incumbents Cannot Copy With One Contract

The OEM is structurally disincentivized to create a secondary keep-alive market because it wants migration to current platforms and official support lifecycles. Generic equipment resellers sell whole instruments and do not maintain a verified, module-level, demand-matched inventory for specific EOL failures. Independent service technicians have trust but fragmented supply and limited working capital. Once MiSeq KeepAlive signs a mandate over a named dead instrument, that exact parts source cannot be copied.

## Economics

- Supply option fee: 500-3,000 PLN per named instrument or revenue share.
- Dead instrument/parts lot acquisition: 5,000-40,000 PLN depending on model/completeness.
- Buyer first-call reservation/search deposit: 1,000-5,000 PLN, creditable to purchase.
- Part/module sale: 2,000-20,000 PLN depending on module scarcity and urgency.
- One successful module sale can repay a small option/acquisition; a whole dead instrument can yield multiple sellable modules if compatibility testing works.

## Kill Criteria

- No signed salvage-right mandate over at least three named instruments/lots within 45 days.
- No paid buyer reservations/search deposits for exact model families/modules within 60 days.
- Technician says modules cannot be tested or reused reliably enough to sell.
- Most available instruments are priced near refurbished-working value, destroying margin.
- Buyers require OEM-only parts or refuse third-party parts even for research-use workflows.

## Why This Could Compound

The first inventory map reveals which models fail, which modules are scarce, which labs are upgrading, which service techs have demand, and which part categories command deposits. The company can expand from MiSeq/MiniSeq/iSeq into other EOL genetics instruments, sample-prep robots, qPCR systems, and other lab equipment where OEMs sunset support and small labs cannot immediately migrate.
