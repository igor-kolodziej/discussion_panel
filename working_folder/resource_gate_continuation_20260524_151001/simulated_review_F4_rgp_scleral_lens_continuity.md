# Strict Simulated Zero To One Review: F4 RGP/Scleral Lens Continuity Rights

## Score

**76 / 100**

## Diagnosis

The pain is real: a 50-60-year-old dependent on custom RGP or scleral lenses can lose work capacity and autonomy when a lens breaks, and a slow refit is materially costly. However, the proposed control point is not strong enough yet. Trial sets, old design libraries, blanks, and retiring-lab know-how are useful assets, but they are not clearly the bottleneck if a competent optometrist and active specialty lens lab can refit or remake through existing channels. The business risks becoming a boutique optometry continuity service with some acquired assets, not a company controlling an unavoidable transaction flow.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier 2- / borderline Tier 3.**

The founder can plausibly option or buy a named trial set, design library, or retired-lab asset within 2 months under 100,000 PLN. But the asset does not yet prove buyer lock-in or economic control, because the clinically trusted optometrist and legally compliant lens manufacturer remain more important than the archive itself. If those are just generic signed collaborators, the control point is weak.

## Strongest Copy Risk

A specialty optometry clinic or existing custom contact-lens lab can copy the useful part by offering "rapid remake/refit continuity" using its own trial sets, fitting skill, and existing manufacturer relationships. They do not need the same retired design library unless the old designs are proven to materially reduce chair time, remake failure, or patient downtime.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **Clinical labor may be the real scarce asset.** If the trusted optometrist performs the refit, owns the patient relationship, and chooses the lab, the startup captures little durable value.
- **Design-library scarcity is unproven.** Modern topography, OCT, profilometry, and active specialty labs may make old base-curve libraries less valuable than claimed.
- **Patient acquisition is awkward.** 50-60-year-old patients with keratoconus, grafts, severe dry eye, or post-surgical ectasia usually enter through optometrists or ophthalmologists. Direct acquisition is expensive and trust-heavy.
- **Episodic demand.** Broken or lost lenses are painful, but not frequent enough for strong repeat retention unless the company owns an annual continuity plan through clinics.
- **Regulatory and liability friction.** Custom contact lenses are medical devices; remakes and refits need compliant clinical workflow, prescription control, traceability, and manufacturer accountability.
- **Local domination is possible but small.** Poland/CEE specialty lens continuity could be a niche, but it may not support venture-scale economics unless expanded into broader specialty-eye-care infrastructure.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

Revenue could come from patient emergency fees, annual continuity memberships, optometrist referral fees, or lab remake margins. Gross margins could be acceptable if the company owns manufacturing or exclusive asset access, but weak if it merely coordinates optometrists and labs. CAC is likely unfavorable through direct patient marketing because the condition set is narrow and trust-sensitive. Clinic-side acquisition has better CAC, but then the clinic holds the patient relationship and bargaining power. Payback can work for a small owner-operated business if each emergency case produces 1,000-3,000 PLN gross profit, but it is not yet a clear high-margin, repeatable, defensible company.

## Best Move

**Pivot / research further, do not advance to real validation.**

The only version worth revisiting would make the control point much harder: acquire exclusive rights to a retiring specialty lens lab's active patient remake files plus signed transfer/continuity consent flow, and pair that with prepaid patient continuity memberships before the clinician retires. Without an assigned patient-flow/right or manufacturer-level production control, this remains too service-like and copyable.
