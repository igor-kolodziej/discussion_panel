# Strict Simulated Zero To One Review: F4 RGP/Scleral Lens Continuity Rights

## Score

**79 / 100**

## Diagnosis

The customer pain is real: a 50-60-year-old dependent on custom RGP or scleral lenses can lose functional vision when a lens breaks and the old fitting/lab knowledge has disappeared. The control point is more specific than a generic optometry service because trial sets, design libraries, and retiring-lab know-how can be bought or optioned. The problem is that the scarce resource may not be the asset; it may be licensed clinical fitting skill and patient trust. If execution depends on optometrists and bespoke fitting case-by-case, the business becomes a small specialty-care service rather than a defensible asset company.

## Control Point Tier

**Tier C+ / partial physical and know-how asset.**

Physical trial sets and design records are useful, but they are not sufficient without regulated fitting, prescription, clinical examination, and lens-manufacturing execution. The founder cannot own the full transaction gate unless the acquired assets materially shorten or de-risk remakes in a way optometrists cannot easily replicate.

## Strongest Copy Risk

A specialty contact-lens clinic or independent optometrist can copy the offer by acquiring similar trial sets or simply performing fresh refits. Large chains are less likely to care, but the nearest capable incumbent is not a large chain; it is the niche clinician who already has patient trust.

## Strongest Objections / Caveats

- **Clinical labor is the bottleneck.** The founder's asset may not matter if the patient still needs a full refit.
- **Low scalability:** Each case may require custom clinical judgment and manual coordination.
- **Supply acquisition is uncertain:** Retiring labs and optometrists may not have transferable records or may be bound by privacy/clinical obligations.
- **Weak founder fit:** A data-science founder has no obvious advantage unless the business becomes a design-library and matching engine, which risks becoming a database wrapper.
- **Retention is narrow:** Patients may buy one emergency remake/refit, then disappear for years.

## Profitability / CAC / Payback View

Unit margins can be decent, but not exceptional once clinician and manufacturing costs are included. CAC may be high because the population is small and trust-sensitive. Payback could be acceptable through high-intent search and optometrist referrals, but this requires referral relationships, not just asset ownership. Retention is episodic.

## Best Move

**Kill as a threshold candidate.**

Keep the customer insight as background, but do not spend real validation cycles unless a retiring lab offers exclusive design assets with an existing patient list and a contracted optometrist can prove deposits quickly. In its current form it will not clear the simulated `>90` gate.
