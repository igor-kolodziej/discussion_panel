# Round 4 Hard-Inventory Pivot

## Why Pivot

The first three rounds repeatedly failed for the same structural reason: medical workflows can be insightful but remain trust-heavy unless the founder already controls clinic routing, licensed clinical authority, or a scarce resource clinics cannot easily bypass. This round moves to the eligible 50-60 / soon-elderly domain and looks for a hard resource an unknown founder can actually own or reserve.

## Candidate: StairLift Salvage & Rapid Refit Rail

**Scored wording draft:** Acquire unwanted stairlifts from estates, post-care moves, and apartment cleanouts through a 48-hour removal offer; reserve one qualified installer team; build a small Warsaw inventory of verified used straight-rail stairlifts and reusable components; sell 5-10 day lower-cost installations to 50-60 adult children and pre-elderly homeowners facing fall/discharge pressure, with optional PFRON barrier-removal paperwork support.

## Hidden Insight

The overlooked bottleneck in aging-in-place is not awareness that stairs are dangerous. The bottleneck is that stairlifts are simultaneously urgent for buyers and trash for sellers. Families removing a stairlift after death, sale, or move often value fast removal more than maximum resale price, while families after a fall or hospital discharge cannot wait for slow new-unit quoting or afford a 20k-40k PLN custom path. The company can start by owning the abandoned-supply side instead of begging for healthcare partnerships.

## Primary Control Point

- Signed removal rights / possession of 3-5 used stairlifts or component sets.
- A paid installation-capacity retainer with one qualified local installer team.
- A Warsaw stair-measurement and part-compatibility record that compounds with each removal and install.

## 2-Month Proof Path Under 100,000 PLN

- Spend 8k-15k PLN on search ads, OLX/Otodom/Facebook listings, and estate-agent/funeral-cleanout referral fees for "remove unwanted stairlift in 48h".
- Spend 10k-25k PLN on removals, transport, inspection, storage, and spare parts for the first 3-5 units.
- Spend 10k-20k PLN to reserve installer capacity and pay for certification/inspection support.
- Spend 5k-10k PLN on landing page, measurement workflow, contracts, and PFRON paperwork templates.
- Proof before capital: at least 3 acquired/removal-contracted units, 1 signed installer retainer, 1 paid buyer deposit or completed install, verified gross margin per unit.

## Key Risks

- Existing stairlift companies already sell used units; the wedge must be faster supply capture and local inventory rather than generic resale.
- Safety, warranty, installation liability, and device compliance cannot be improvised.
- Curved stairlifts are harder because rails are custom; the beachhead should start with straight indoor stairs and reusable mainstream brands.
- This can become a local trade business unless the inventory/measurement/refit data and removal acquisition channel compound.

## Initial Research Notes

- Polish public pricing pages indicate new straight stairlifts around 12k-14k PLN, used straight stairlifts around 6k-8k PLN, new curved stairlifts starting around 32k-36k PLN, and used units about 40% cheaper than new.
- PFRON / local barrier-removal funding exists but varies by county and application queue, so it should be treated as upside support rather than the initial control point.
- Existing used stairlift sellers prove demand but also confirm competition; the startup must win on rapid local possession, removal rights, and installation slots.
