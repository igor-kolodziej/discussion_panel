# Round 27 Candidate: VarietyGuard DNA Royalty Enforcement Right

## Candidate

**VarietyGuard DNA Royalty Enforcement Right** - exclusive enforcement and royalty-recovery rights for named protected plant varieties in Poland/CEE, using DNA sampling only as an evidence tool.

## Scored Wording Draft

VarietyGuard DNA Royalty Enforcement Right is not seed testing, agronomy consulting, or a dashboard. It signs a 12-24 month exclusive enforcement/agency agreement with a small Polish/CEE breeder or plant-variety-right holder for 2-5 named protected varieties in a defined territory or channel. The breeder/rightsholder supplies reference material, authorized-licensee lists, suspected leakage channels, allowed sampling rules, and permission to act as the first-line commercial enforcement operator. VarietyGuard then audits selected distributors, propagators, nurseries, farms, or suspicious seed/plant/harvested-material channels; collects chain-of-custody samples; routes DNA/variety identity tests to a contracted lab when useful; sends compliance/royalty offers through a lawyer-reviewed template; and recovers unpaid royalties, settlements, or compliance certificates. Revenue is a modest retainer plus 20-35% of recovered royalties/settlements and per-lot compliance-certificate fees.

## Hidden Insight

Small breeders often own a legal monopoly on a plant variety but do not own an enforcement machine. Labs, lawyers, and seed certifiers can test or advise, but they do not hold the assigned right to monetize leakage. The valuable control point is not DNA; it is a temporary exclusive commercial enforcement right over named varieties. Once signed, an incumbent can copy the mechanics but cannot enforce those same named rights for the term.

## Primary Control Point

- Exclusive 12-24 month agency/enforcement agreement for 2-5 named protected varieties and a defined territory/channel.
- Breeder-supplied reference material or DNA profile and authorized-licensee list.
- Permission to sample named channels and issue compliance/royalty offers under lawyer-reviewed terms.
- Contracted DNA/variety lab route.
- Recovery-share economics and first-line intake of infringement reports.

## 2-Month Proof Under 100,000 PLN

- Build a list of 50 small Polish/CEE breeders/rightsholders from COBORU/CPVO protected-variety records, focusing on ornamental, berry, nursery, seed, and greenhouse varieties with fragmented propagation channels.
- Sign one 12-month exclusive pilot for 2-5 named varieties in Poland or one CEE channel.
- Secure reference material/DNA route and authorized-licensee list.
- Map 30-50 sellers/distributors/propagators for those varieties.
- Conduct 20-40 compliant sample purchases or document audits, using DNA tests only where necessary.
- Find 2 credible infringement/leakage cases or convert 5 sellers into paid compliance certificates/licensing conversations.
- Collect at least 10,000-25,000 PLN in retainer, certificate fees, or first recovery/settlement commitments.

## Unit Economics

- Rightsholder retainer: 5,000-15,000 PLN/month for pilot scope, or smaller retainer plus higher recovery share.
- Recovery share: 20-35% of unpaid royalties/settlements.
- Compliance certificate/licensing verification: 300-800 PLN per seller/lot/channel.
- DNA tests: pass-through or billed with handling margin.
- Contractor/legal review: 5,000-20,000 PLN during POC.
- Target gross margin: 50-75% on enforcement/admin work; recovery share can be high-margin after legal/lab costs.

## Why Unknown Founder Can Secure It

The founder does not need to win trust from large seed companies first. The target is small rightsholders with under-enforced varieties and limited enforcement staff. They already own the legal right but may be leaving royalty money on the table because enforcement is fragmented, awkward, and too small for big law firms. The founder offers found money, pays for initial fieldwork, uses a lawyer for templates, and starts with one narrow territory/channel. This is easier to secure than hospital trust because the rightsholder is the seller of a neglected enforcement asset.

## Key Risks

- Some small breeders may have little real leakage or low royalty value.
- Enforcement may become legal-work heavy; lawyer boundaries and templates are essential.
- Sampling and DNA evidence may be contested or require breeder-specific reference material.
- Large breeders/associations/law firms could copy future mechanics, though not the signed named right during the term.
- Some channels may react negatively to enforcement and hurt breeder relationships.
- Cash conversion depends on finding leakage quickly, not just signing a retainer.
