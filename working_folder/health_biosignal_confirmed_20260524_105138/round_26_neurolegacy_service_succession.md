# Round 26 Candidate: NeuroLegacy Service Succession

## Candidate

**NeuroLegacy Service Succession** - acquisition/option of retiring EEG/EMG/PSG/neurofeedback service technicians' installed-base books, spare parts, tools, service recipes, and customer continuity rights for orphaned or under-supported neurodiagnostic device lines.

## Scored Wording Draft

NeuroLegacy Service Succession does not sell a generic medical-device repair marketplace or compliance desk. It buys or options the service-succession package of a retiring/overloaded Polish or CEE neurodiagnostic equipment technician, micro-distributor, or biomedical service operator who supports a small installed base of legacy EEG, EMG, PSG, sleep-diagnostic, or neurofeedback systems. The acquired resource is not patient data. It is the named installed-base list, existing service contracts or customer continuity introductions, spare parts lot, manuals, calibration/test fixtures, known failure recipes, software/dongle/license-transfer notes where lawful, and 3-6 months of paid transition time from the technician. The founder then sells annual downtime-continuity retainers and emergency repair/parts response to private neurodiagnostic clinics that cannot afford sudden replacement of a 40k-200k PLN system because one cable, amplifier channel, dongle, power supply, electrode interface, or export workstation fails.

## Hidden Insight

Small neurodiagnostic clinics are not forced to replace a whole EEG/EMG/PSG stack because the science is obsolete. They are forced to replace it when the one person who knows the legacy system retires, the local distributor disappears, or spare parts and software know-how stop being findable. Large OEMs and broad biomedical-service firms ignore these small installed bases because each device line is too annoying. That leaves a narrow succession opportunity: buy the retiring technician's trust, parts, and repair memory before it disappears, then become the only continuity layer for that orphaned installed base.

## Primary Control Point

- Signed option/acquisition agreement for one retiring technician/micro-distributor service book covering 15-50 installed devices.
- Assignment or re-signing of existing service contracts where possible.
- Exclusive customer continuity introduction from the seller.
- Owned spare parts, cables, accessories, manuals, calibration/test fixtures, and failure-history notes.
- Paid 3-6 month seller transition and training of a junior contractor.
- Clinic retainers tied to named device serials, not generic repair advice.

## 2-Month Proof Under 100,000 PLN

- Identify 30 retiring/solo biomedical or neurodiagnostic service operators and micro-distributors in Poland/CEE.
- Sign one option/earnout agreement for a named installed-base package: 15+ devices, parts list, customer-introduction rights, and seller transition hours.
- Pay an option or first tranche under 30,000 PLN; keep acquisition price mostly earnout-based.
- Contact 15 installed-base clinics with seller introduction.
- Re-sign 5 clinic downtime-continuity retainers at 2,000-6,000 PLN/year or collect paid emergency deposits.
- Complete 2 paid service/parts jobs with the seller/junior contractor pair.
- Produce 15,000-35,000 PLN in signed revenue/deposits and a verified parts/fixture inventory.

## Unit Economics

- Annual device retainer: 2,000-6,000 PLN per device or clinic-specific shelf.
- Emergency service visit: 1,000-5,000 PLN plus travel.
- Parts/accessory markup: 40-100% on scarce parts if legal/traceable.
- Seller transition: 150-300 PLN/hour or earnout.
- Junior biomedical contractor: 80-180 PLN/hour.
- Gross margin target after transition period: 50-75% on service/parts blend.

## Why Unknown Founder Can Secure It

The seller is not a famous institution; it is a small owner-operator with retirement risk, too small for classic M&A and too operationally messy for large service firms. The founder offers immediate cash, earnout, continuity for the seller's customers, and relief from calls after retirement. Clinics accept the continuity if the seller introduces the founder and stays through the first jobs. This avoids cold medical trust and avoids patient-record transfer.

## Key Risks

- Market may be too small if one installed-base package has few active clinics.
- Some software licenses, dongles, manuals, or parts may be non-transferable.
- Liability must be limited to technical service, not clinical certification unless properly qualified.
- OEMs or larger service firms can compete if the installed base is large enough.
- Founder must avoid becoming dependent on one retiring technician without training a second operator.
- Device safety, UDT/electrical safety, and medical-device service obligations must be handled by qualified contractors.

## Sources To Check

- Visible used EEG/neurofeedback equipment markets prove second-hand and legacy devices remain in circulation.
- Polish private EEG pricing indicates downtime can destroy appointment revenue.
- MDR/economic-operator obligations and manufacturer instructions may affect service boundaries.
