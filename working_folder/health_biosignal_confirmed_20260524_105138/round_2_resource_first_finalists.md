# Round 2 Resource-First Finalists

## Candidate A: HearBook Orphaned Audiology Aftercare Roll-Up

Independent Polish audioprosthetists and tiny hearing-aid shops hold valuable but under-monetized customer books: audiograms, device models, warranty dates, service reminders, batteries/filters, NFZ/PFRON paperwork memory, spouse/child contact context, and future replacement timing. Many are too small or owner-dependent for large chains to acquire cleanly, while 50-70-year-old customers are entering the hearing-aid replacement window but do not identify as "elderly" and dislike sales pressure.

The company buys or options orphaned aftercare books from retiring, burned-out, or under-digitalized independent audioprosthetists on an earnout basis. It does not start as a new clinic, hearing-aid marketplace, or generic hearing app. The first resource is a signed transfer/continuity mandate over a book of 80-300 existing customers, with customer re-consent and a contracted audioprosthetist for service days.

Two-month proof: 2 LOIs or one signed earnout option over at least 150 customer records; 50 customer continuity messages sent with seller introduction; 25 opt-ins; 10 paid service/cleaning/adjustment/battery appointments; 3 replacement consultations; one contracted audioprosthetist; CAC measured from acquired book, not ads.

## Candidate B: HolterBook Micro-Roll-Up

Tiny home-Holter operators, cardiology practices, and diagnostics freelancers own device fleets, phone numbers, domains, and clinic referral relationships, but many are too small to scale or too operationally annoying for larger networks. The company does not compete by buying devices and ads; it buys or options a working micro-book: devices, referral contacts, phone/domain, patient reactivation rights, and past utilization data.

Two-month proof: one signed option or asset/earnout agreement for 4-10 devices plus referral book; 20 reactivated or referred paid monitorings; >55% device-day utilization; contracted cardiologist interpretation capacity; contribution margin proven after consumables/logistics.

## Candidate C: CPAP Resupply And Adherence Book Acquisition

Small CPAP retailers and sleep-service providers often sell a machine once, then lose structured control of masks, filters, humidifier parts, adherence checks, and annual replacement cycles. The valuable asset is not first-14-night coaching; it is the recurring resupply/adherence book and patient contact permission.

Two-month proof: option one book of 100+ CPAP customers from an independent shop or retiring provider; 40 customer reactivation messages; 15 paid resupply/check packages; one mask/accessory supplier term sheet; one clinician reviewer for edge cases; margin after COGS and support.

## Candidate D: Mobility Equipment Route-Book Roll-Up

Small medical-equipment rental operators own messy but valuable urgent-demand phone numbers, local caregiver/referral contacts, hospital-bed/wheelchair inventory, and recurring extension payments. The company buys or options one route book and inventory bundle, then improves logistics and deposits with AI-supported scheduling. This is a 50-60 adult-child crisis wedge, not a generic marketplace.

Two-month proof: signed option on one route book plus 20-50 rental assets; 20 customer/reactivation calls; 10 active rental invoices; positive contribution margin; no medical-service claims.

## Candidate E: NeuroDx Technician Book Acquisition

Instead of building a new EEG capacity router, acquire or option a retiring/overloaded EEG technician's private referral book, phone/domain, equipment access, and recurring neurologist relationships. The asset is already-trusted technician/referral flow, not a generic home EEG landing page.

Two-month proof: one signed option/earnout over referral flow; reserved technician capacity; one interpreting physician agreement; 10 paid home/sleep-deprived EEGs; report turnaround proof. Redundancy risk: close to prior Home EEG Capacity Router, so only valid if framed as micro-book acquisition rather than de novo capacity.

## Candidate F: Hearing-At-Work Continuity Book

Acquire or option aftercare books from industrial hearing-protection/audiometry contractors serving factories with older workers. Convert one-off compliance audiometry into worker-retention and hearing-aid/protection follow-up. Control point is employer audiogram history plus annual retest calendar.

Two-month proof: one contractor routes 200+ historical audiogram records or annual retest calendar under DPA; 30 worker follow-ups; 5 paid hearing/protection interventions. Weak risk: occupational-health buyer trust and privacy.
