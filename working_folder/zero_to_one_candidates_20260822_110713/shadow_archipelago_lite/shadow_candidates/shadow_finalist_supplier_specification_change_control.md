## Frozen Level-2 Dossier

# Level-2 D-006 — Supplier Specification Change Control

## Problem And Payer Evidence

EU Regulation 1169/2011 makes the responsible food business operator accountable for food information and requires ingredient and allergen particulars. It also requires upstream food businesses to provide sufficient information to downstream operators. That creates a real control obligation, but it does not prescribe an automated document-comparison product.

BRCGS Food Safety Issue 9 requires specifications for raw materials, primary packaging, and finished products. Its clauses require specifications to be accurate and current, with reviews and changes documented. Official BRCGS emergency-supply guidance says that a new allergen or ingredient-declaration impact must be addressed before a changed raw material is accepted or used. UK Food Standards Agency guidance similarly says a supplier change should be accompanied by appropriate checks.

The documentary route remains heterogeneous. Public examples include PDF product specifications, standalone allergen questionnaires, and Excel formula/allergen forms. This supports the interoperability problem but does not establish how often suppliers send changed versions without clear notification.

Incident evidence is only a proxy. The EU’s 2022 alert report recorded 210 allergen-related notifications. It does not establish how many were caused by missed supplier-document changes, how many involved Polish manufacturers, or how many could have been prevented by software.

The payer is the technical manager, quality manager, regulatory lead, or owner of a small food manufacturer. No reliable Polish denominator for manufacturers with 50–500 SKUs and multiple emailed supplier specifications was established. BRCGS reported more than 24,500 certified sites worldwide in 2026, but this is neither a Polish count nor the complete payer population.

The willingness proxy is existing compliance spend:

- BRCGS sells its Issue 9 change guide for £140 and requires audited suppliers to access the standard.
- FoodDocs publishes food-safety software prices of USD 99–299 per site per month.
- One compliance vendor lists supplier-approval management at USD 59.
- Public consultant offers include remote supplier audits from £400 and supplier-program setup from £2,500.

These purchases show budget for food-safety systems and qualified review, not willingness to buy automated specification-diff monitoring.

The closest substitutes are a controlled spreadsheet, shared drive, document-management system, consultant review, BRCGS/IFS-aligned supplier approval software, and customer or retailer portals. They persist because a competent person must interpret whether a change affects HACCP, allergen controls, claims, artwork, shelf life, certification, or customer approval.

## Proposed Transaction

The transaction is a subscription for each monitored supplier-product relationship, with an onboarding fee to establish an approved baseline.

The service would:

- Ingest customer-authorized supplier specifications and supporting documents.
- Extract document identity, version, ingredients, compound ingredients, allergens, nutrition, certificate references, shelf life, storage, packaging, and claims.
- Compare each new version with the approved baseline.
- Present field-level changes with original-document evidence.
- Route changes to named qualified customer reviewers.
- Record approval, rejection, request-for-clarification, and affected finished-product/label decisions.
- Never certify safety, approve a supplier, release a product, or modify artwork autonomously.

An interpreted pricing range is PLN 4,000–20,000 for baseline onboarding plus PLN 25–75 per monitored supplier-product relationship per month, with a PLN 1,500–6,000 monthly minimum. The per-relationship model is only workable if the relationship count maps to ongoing document risk; otherwise a site-based subscription may be easier to budget.

Sensitivity exposes the core uncertainty. One hundred relationships at PLN 40 produce PLN 4,000 monthly revenue. If the customer saves only eight hours at an interpreted loaded cost of PLN 100–250 per hour, direct labor value is PLN 800–2,000—below the subscription. Purchase therefore may depend on audit readiness, avoided label rework, or reduced incident exposure, none of which should be converted into guaranteed savings without customer evidence.

A private-proof system can fit approximately PLN 20,000–60,000: secure document intake, versioning, extraction, comparison, review UI, a qualified food-safety adviser, and liability review. Broad supplier portals, retailer integrations, automated label generation, and autonomous compliance decisions are outside scope.

Required access and rights are:

- Customer-owned copies of specifications, certificates, labels, formulations, and version history.
- Contractual confirmation that the customer may process received supplier documents for its internal quality purpose.
- No redistribution of supplier-confidential content or use in cross-customer benchmarks without separate permission.
- Named baseline owner and named qualified approver for each relationship.
- Customer-defined fields, tolerances, and materiality rules.
- Role-based access, immutable version history, retention, deletion, and export.
- A processor agreement for personal data such as supplier contacts.
- Counsel-reviewed confidentiality, intellectual-property, warranty, limitation-of-liability, and “decision support only” terms.

## Acquisition Route

The first route is through independent food-safety consultants and certification advisers serving small manufacturers that receive supplier specifications by email.

The partner offer is a manual-first “specification change census”:

- Select one manufacturer and one recent period.
- Collect old and new versions of supplier documents.
- Compare them manually with extraction assistance.
- Classify changes by field and materiality.
- Ask the customer’s competent quality employee to record the actual approval outcome.
- Measure missed changes, comparison time, clarification cycles, and downstream records affected.

The first customer should have a defined approved-supplier list, an existing allergen matrix or label record, and at least 30 recent document pairs. Without an approved baseline, the engagement becomes a broader specification-remediation project and cannot test monitoring demand cleanly.

Consultants may resist a product that appears to automate their judgment. Positioning should preserve their qualified role: the system gathers evidence, highlights differences, and creates an audit trail; the consultant or customer decides significance.

## Decision-Critical Unknowns

**Verified:** Food operators have binding information responsibilities. Certified sites may have contractual standard requirements for accurate, current specifications and documented changes. Supplier and ingredient changes can require allergen, label, and hazard review. Manual PDF and Excel formats exist.

**Interpreted:** Manufacturers with 50–500 SKUs and many emailed supplier documents are the likely initial payer. Published food-safety software and consulting prices support only a broad budget proxy.

**Unknown:** Polish payer denominator; monthly material-change frequency; labor per comparison; missed-change incidence; extraction accuracy by language and layout; consultant introduction rate; supplier response behavior; number of finished products affected by each change; and willingness to subscribe by relationship.

**Contradicted:** Incident counts do not prove that specification-diff software would have prevented the incidents. A minimum three-year BRCGS review interval does not establish five material changes every month. Existing portals and quality systems may already provide version control for some manufacturers.

**Counsel-required:** Scope of professional responsibility; liability for missed or incorrectly classified changes; confidentiality and reuse of supplier documents; treatment of formulations and trade secrets; contract wording that reserves decisions to qualified personnel; and any regulated claim that the system ensures compliance or food safety.

Not every critical premise is verified. The decisive premise—at least five material monthly changes consuming more than eight staff hours for a 100-SKU manufacturer—is unknown.

The cheapest private proof is a retrospective manual comparison of at least 30 old/new supplier-document pairs for one customer. A PLN 3,000–8,000 budget is plausible. Record true material changes, extraction errors, previously missed changes, reviewer minutes, clarification cycles, and whether each change led to an approved baseline, label, allergen matrix, certificate, or HACCP update. Stop if material changes are rare, qualified review is not measurably faster, or the customer will not pay for ongoing monitoring.

# Fact Closure D-006

## Findings

- **verified:** Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 creates a payer-relevant control obligation: the operator under whose name food is marketed must ensure food-information accuracy, operators must verify requirements relevant to their activities, and upstream operators must provide downstream businesses sufficient information. Ingredients and allergens are mandatory particulars. The Regulation does not prescribe automated specification comparison. [Regulation (EU) 1169/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2011/1169)

- **unknown:** Eurostat and Statistics Poland collect enterprise counts by detailed NACE activity and employment size, including Polish food manufacturing, but the retrieved public material does not isolate manufacturers with 50–500 SKUs, multiple emailed supplier specifications, or an internal technical buyer. The reachable Polish payer denominator remains unknown. [Eurostat Polish SBS metadata](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/sbs_h_esms_pl.htm)

- **interpreted:** A manufacturer’s quality manager, technical manager, regulatory lead, or owner is the likely operational authority because the food business—not its consultant—retains the regulatory responsibility. Public evidence does not establish which role can sign the first PLN 3,000–8,000 census or a recurring subscription.

- **unknown:** No public evidence established a food-safety consultant’s introduction-to-contract conversion rate, authority to purchase on behalf of a manufacturer, or commercial incentive to introduce this service. The first contract must therefore be executed by an authorized manufacturer representative; the consultant route remains an acquisition hypothesis.

- **verified:** Adjacent software has explicit budgets. Foodflou lists document management, approval flows, audit logs, reminders, version control, and a change register from €89 per month. SupplierConnect advertises €3,000 per month for a platform covering 25 suppliers, with higher managed-approval tiers. These are direct prices for adjacent controls, not evidence that manufacturers will buy relationship-priced automated diffs. [Foodflou pricing](https://www.foodflou.com/pricing) [SupplierConnect pricing](https://www.supplierconnect.ai/)

- **interpreted:** Published adjacent prices support the existence of software budgets ranging from low hundreds to several thousand euros monthly, but they do not validate PLN 25–75 per supplier-product relationship, the proposed monthly minimum, or the onboarding fee.

- **counsel-required:** Customer possession of a supplier document does not automatically authorize every downstream use. Directive (EU) 2016/943 treats unauthorized access, copying, use, or disclosure of protected trade secrets—and breach of confidentiality or use restrictions—as unlawful. Customer authorization, supplier-contract restrictions, service-provider access, model-training exclusions, retention, and cross-customer reuse require contractual review. [Directive (EU) 2016/943, Articles 3–4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/943/oj)

- **verified:** Where supplier contacts or other personal data are processed on the customer’s behalf, GDPR Article 28 requires a binding processor contract, documented instructions, controls on subprocessors, and corresponding protection obligations. [GDPR, Article 28](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)

- **verified:** Direct route-arounds already exist. NSF TraQtion provides supplier portals, digital specifications, approval workflows, version-stamped records, allergen and formulation fields, archives, and document tracking. Foodflou provides version control and change registers. These products establish that some manufacturers can route around emailed-document comparison through structured portals or broader quality-management systems. [NSF TraQtion specification management](https://www.nsf.org/traqtion/specification-management) [Foodflou pricing](https://www.foodflou.com/pricing)

- **contradicted:** Any indispensable framing that manufacturers lack incumbent version-control or supplier-portal options is directly contradicted by the published NSF, Foodflou, TIMS, and SupplierConnect offerings. This does not establish adoption among Polish small manufacturers.

- **unknown:** Public implementation references describe onboarding in days or weeks but do not disclose labor, security, adviser, insurance, or liability costs for the proposed private-proof system. The PLN 20,000–60,000 build estimate and PLN 3,000–8,000 census estimate remain unverified capital assumptions. [Foodflou implementation information](https://www.foodflou.com/pricing)

- **verified:** BRCGS guidance requires the effects of changed raw materials to be reviewed and states that a new allergen or ingredient-declaration impact must be addressed before the changed material is used or accepted. This supports qualified review and change control, not autonomous software approval. [BRCGS supplier-change guidance](https://www.brcgs.com/media/2082504/food-safety-covid-19-guideline-unlocked.pdf)

- **interpreted:** The cheapest remaining decision-relevant private proof is a retrospective census using one authorized customer’s existing documents, not a production platform: at least 30 genuine old/new document pairs, a declared sampling period, the approved baseline, and adjudication by the customer’s competent employee.
