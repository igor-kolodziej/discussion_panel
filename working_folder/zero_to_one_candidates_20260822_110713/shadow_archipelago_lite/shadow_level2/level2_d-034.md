# Level-2 D-034 — Subsidy-Safe Heating Quote

## Problem And Payer Evidence

The payer is a heating or renewable installer that receives a household inquiry for a fossil boiler but cannot present an eligible or affordable subsidized configuration. The relevant denominator is abandoned, otherwise-qualified leads—not all heating households or grant applications.

The policy change is verified but narrower than a boiler ban. European Commission guidance says public financial incentives for installing new stand-alone fossil-fuel boilers had to end by 1 January 2025. It expressly preserves previously approved incentives, maintenance, repair, decommissioning, renewable-gas transition costs, income support, and qualifying hybrid incentives. Boiler purchase, ownership, maintenance, and operation are not universally prohibited.

Poland’s July 2026 description of Czyste Powietrze confirms no gas-boiler funding, mandatory energy audits and certificates, cost limits, and income-based support. It reports 70,000 applications and 53,000 agreements worth 3.3 billion PLN under the version operating since 31 March 2025. These are strong programme denominators but do not reveal fossil-boiler leads lost by installers.

The Joint Research Centre reported approximately 2.2 million Polish households heating with gas and about 21% using coal, based on earlier source data. This establishes a large affected stock but not replacement intent. It also reports that 57% of Polish buildings predate 1990, illustrating why building suitability and retrofit scope matter.

Willingness to pay for an alternative-proposal service is unknown. Households often expect quotes free, while installers may absorb design effort into customer acquisition. The willingness proxy is the size and complexity of the underlying transaction: heat-pump installation labour alone was reported at roughly 4,000–10,000 PLN, while grants can cover substantial shares of eligible costs. That does not prove a separate proposal fee.

## Proposed Transaction

The service prepares a verified alternative proposal for a household lead that cannot receive the expected stand-alone fossil-boiler subsidy. It should include:

- Current programme and income-path eligibility, with effective date and official source.
- Building constraints, heat demand, emitter temperatures, electrical capacity, hot-water needs, and required retrofit work.
- Two or three installer-approved configurations, such as eligible heat pump, biomass, electric heating where currently supported, connection to district heat where available, or staged insulation plus later source replacement.
- Eligible equipment checks, expected grant range, household cash requirement, operating-cost assumptions, documents, exclusions, and unresolved engineering questions.
- A comparison that clearly separates verified programme rules, installer estimates, and household-supplied assumptions.

The paid event is delivery of a proposal checked by the installer and, where required, an authorized energy auditor or engineer. An optional success fee becomes payable only when the household signs the installer’s contract after receiving required disclosures. It should not depend on grant approval unless the service assumes responsibility for the application.

An interpreted price is 300–900 PLN per verified proposal, potentially credited against installation, plus a 1,000–3,000 PLN installer-funded success fee. Direct household pricing may face greater resistance than installer-funded lead salvage. Manual delivery could cost 200–800 PLN before engineering escalation. A small private pilot may require 10,000–25,000 PLN; a rules engine, secure household intake, quote generation, and partner workflow could require 40,000–90,000 PLN.

Sensitivity is driven by conversion and contribution margin. For 20 otherwise-lost leads, a 10% conversion rate yields two installations. At a 2,000 PLN success fee, that is 4,000 PLN before proposal costs. If each proposal costs 400 PLN, servicing all 20 costs 8,000 PLN and fails without an upfront fee or higher installer value. At 20% conversion, the same economics approach break-even before fixed costs. The 10% conversion premise is therefore decisive.

## Acquisition Route

The first route is two or three local heating or renewable installers willing to provide a small cohort of leads abandoned after the household learned that a stand-alone gas boiler lacked subsidy. The proof should be manual and confined to a current programme version and one region.

The closest substitutes are the installer’s own redesigned quote, an energy auditor, municipal or programme operator assistance, household comparison portals, and competing installers offering free proposals. These routes persist because engineering, grant documents, price, installation warranty, and sales conversation are tightly coupled. More than 2,000 programme consultation points and over 1,400 municipal operators also provide an established assistance route.

Exact access requires explicit household consent and an installer agreement covering address, ownership, income band, existing heating source, energy bills, plans or measurements, audit, certificate, electrical connection, photographs if supplied by the household, and quote history. The service must not represent itself as a programme operator or grant authority.

An energy audit is obligatory under the current programme, and official guidance says it must be signed by a person entered in the authorized register after the transition period. Eligible heat pumps and biomass equipment must meet current programme and ZUM requirements. The service may organize these professionals but cannot substitute an automated result for their signature or engineering judgment.

Dependencies include continuously current programme documents, ZUM status on the relevant date, anti-smog rules, income and building eligibility, authorized auditors, installer price books, financing availability, electrical and hydraulic design, consumer disclosures, and post-sale support. Consumer-contract terms, cooling-off rights, referral commissions, success fees, credit introductions, engineering liability, and grant representations require counsel.

## Decision-Critical Unknowns

- **VERIFIED:** Public incentives for new stand-alone fossil-fuel boilers ended from 1 January 2025.
- **VERIFIED:** Current Czyste Powietrze rules exclude gas-boiler funding and require an energy audit and certificate.
- **VERIFIED:** Poland has a substantial gas- and coal-heated household stock and a high volume of programme applications.
- **VERIFIED:** Eligible heating equipment and audits are subject to defined technical and professional requirements.
- **INTERPRETED:** Installers may pay to recover value from leads they would otherwise abandon.
- **INTERPRETED:** A 300–900 PLN proposal fee and 1,000–3,000 PLN success fee are testable transaction hypotheses.
- **UNKNOWN:** Monthly lost-lead volume per installer after subsidy changes.
- **UNKNOWN:** The fraction of those leads with technically feasible and affordable eligible alternatives.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether at least 10% of otherwise-lost leads will sign.
- **UNKNOWN:** Household or installer willingness to pay a separate proposal fee.
- **UNKNOWN:** Financing availability, approval rates, objections, engineering effort, and realized installation margin.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether rule and equipment-list maintenance can be performed reliably within part-time operating capacity.
- **CONTRADICTED:** A premise that fossil boilers are generally banned; the policy concerns financial incentives for new stand-alone systems, with explicit exceptions and no universal purchase or operating ban.
- **CONTRADICTED:** A premise that every boiler lead can be redirected to a heat pump; building efficiency, emitter temperature, electricity supply, affordability, and household preference can prevent that route.
- **COUNSEL-REQUIRED:** Consumer contracting, success-fee disclosures, installer commissions, credit or financing introductions, grant representations, data processing, professional signatures, and engineering liability.

The cheapest private proof is to obtain a consented cohort of genuinely abandoned leads from local installers, manually prepare verified alternatives with an authorized auditor or engineer, and track household evaluation, objections, financing, signed contracts, and realized installer margin. A modeled quotation or stated interest is not proof; the decisive observation is a signed outcome from a lead the installer had classified as lost.
