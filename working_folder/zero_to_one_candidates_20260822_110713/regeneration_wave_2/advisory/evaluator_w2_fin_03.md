## Evidence-calibrated assessment

**Verified within the bound evidence**

- Covered entities face registration, update, documentation, contact-person and incident-timing duties.
- Relevant registry changes generally have a 14-day update requirement; serious incidents involve 24-hour, 72-hour and later reporting events.
- Registry filings can be handled by an electronically authorized person.
- Outsourcing does not transfer statutory management responsibility.
- Adjacent paid compliance work exists, including a 4,900 PLN gap-audit offer.
- Cybersecurity outsourcing is common in the cited survey, although the survey did not test this service.
- Self-service KSC products are advertised at 199 and 499 PLN monthly.
- White-label/co-branded cybersecurity delivery through IT partners is already commercially offered.

**Interpreted**

- Fragmented evidence across management, MSPs, vendors and counsel creates more work than a one-time filing.
- The 8,000 PLN dossier price has some support from adjacent compliance spending, but not from a directly comparable transaction.
- A managed operator might justify a premium through human collection, approvals, chronology, vendor coordination and dependable availability. That premium has not been demonstrated.
- Evidence history and partner integrations could create switching friction, but they do not yet constitute a strong moat.

**Counsel-required**

- Whether an outside operator may hold the intended S46 roles or submit incident materials.
- Proxy limits, GDPR roles, evidence retention, referral arrangements and responsibility language.
- Any external incident-submission authority and the treatment of mixed personal or security-sensitive evidence.
- The exact operating boundary must be settled before selling deadline coverage or live incident custody.

**Unknown**

- The addressable count of already-classified medium private entities with insufficient internal capacity.
- Recurring willingness to pay at 2,500 PLN monthly or quarterly prepayment of 7,500 PLN.
- Whether an accessible MSP can provide named, qualified prospects.
- Renewal, delivery hours, evidence volume, approval delays and incident frequency.
- MSSP availability costs, insurance premiums, exclusions and partner compensation.
- Whether the proposed 80% recurring gross margin survives the complete delivery stack.

**Contradicted**

No indispensable frozen-candidate claim is directly contradicted. The evidence does contradict any broader proposition that portal access, forms or reminders alone are scarce or defensible: direct filing and inexpensive self-service products exist. The frozen candidate appropriately claims a managed evidence-operation advantage instead, but that advantage remains unproven.

## Founder Wealth assessment

The model avoids the clearest double-counting error: reinvested cash and founder capital are treated as already represented in the operating company, while only after-tax founder distributions are added to equity value.

- Founder equity of 2.151m PLN plus approximately 0.041m PLN distributions.

### Expected-success case

The arithmetic is coherent. Under these same assumptions, the 5m PLN target would require approximately 1.33m PLN EBITDA and 4.9m PLN annual revenue. That is a plausible mathematical route, but not yet an evidence-supported forecast. The modeled first-hundred channel produces 100 customers, while the expected case requires 300, so additional distribution capacity is implicitly required.

### Strong case

- 800 recurring clients, 36.840m PLN revenue, 12.157m PLN EBITDA.
- 75% founder ownership, 1.5m PLN net debt and a 6× multiple.
- **Founder net worth: 53.582m PLN.**

This is internally reconciled but highly sensitive to maintaining 82% gross margin and 33% operating margin while scaling security-sensitive, deadline-dependent operations. It also assumes substantial organizational leverage, channel performance and professional coverage that have not been established.

### Overall wealth verdict

A path beyond 5m PLN exists without obvious arithmetic double counting, and the expected case retains credible founder ownership despite moderate dilution. It is nevertheless contingent on three unclosed assumptions: several hundred retained clients, low standardized human workload, and partner/24-hour/security costs compatible with high margins. The conservative case misses the target materially, so founder wealth currently depends on expected-case execution rather than a robust downside path.

## Decisive commercial premise

The single decisive premise is:

> Already-classified Polish medium entities will purchase recurring managed evidence custody through accessible MSP or counsel channels at prices that remain profitable after security, insurance, partner and 24/7 delivery costs.

Public evidence **leaves this premise private**. It establishes the legal workflow, adjacent spending, outsourcing behavior and existence of partner channels. It neither closes nor directly contradicts recurring purchase intent at the proposed price.

## Principal failure modes

**Strongest route-around:** An existing MSP, law firm or cybersecurity provider can bundle the checklist and evidence workflow into its present relationship, potentially using a 199–499 PLN self-service platform. The proposed retainer is about 12.6× the 199 PLN plan and 5× the 499 PLN plan.

**Distribution failure:** MSPs may not have enough already-classified medium clients, may refuse to expose those relationships, or may absorb the workflow themselves. No partner commitment or named qualified prospect list is evidenced. Trusted-channel sales also require relationship work that cannot be fully replaced by AI.

**Economic sensitivity:** The modeled first-year contribution is 26,800 PLN per setup-plus-retainer customer. Holding current delivery assumptions constant:

- Reducing the retainer from 2,500 to 1,500 PLN monthly lowers contribution to about 16,000 PLN.
- Doubling recurring delivery cost from 500 to 1,000 PLN monthly lowers it to about 20,800 PLN.
- Combining both changes lowers contribution to about 10,000 PLN—roughly 63% below the model.

Low renewal would further turn the business into a less-scalable dossier service.

**Execution risks:** External-role legality, cyber/E&O coverage, secure multi-tenant custody, immutable logs, out-of-hours contracting, evidence-feed permissions and timely client approvals all must work together. The manual pilot is compatible with the founder’s capital and part-time constraints; sustained incident operations are not founder-only work.

**Lawful role boundary:** Accept only clients whose KSC status is already confirmed by management or qualified counsel. The operator may prepare evidence and perform specifically authorized registry administration, but must not determine classification, make legal reporting decisions, assume management responsibility, represent itself as a CSIRT/SOC/regulator/certifier, or submit incidents without confirmed authority and final client approval.
