# W2-L2-012 — Owned roof-material lifting cells rented by the day

**Research timestamp:** 2026-08-22 14:36 CEST  
**Concept:** Own certified material hoists, temporary edge-protection components and handling fixtures; rent complete cells to solar, roofing and electrical installers on a prepaid daily basis.  
**Research budget used:** exactly eight distinct web queries; eight source-open attempts, seven successfully rendered and one failed to render after its indexed evidence was returned. No local files other than the assigned packet were read. No files were edited.

## 1. Evidence status

### Verified

- Poland installed 4.2 GW of PV in 2024. This confirms substantial continuing installation activity, although the figure includes both rooftop and utility-scale work and does not establish demand for lifting equipment. [IEA PVPS, *Trends in PV Applications 2025*](https://iea-pvps.org/trends_reports/trends-2025/)
- At the end of 2025 Poland had 1,636,673 grid-connected microinstallations; 99.9% used PV. This establishes a large installed base but is not a count of annual roof jobs or prospective renters. [URE microinstallation report](https://ure.gov.pl/pl/urzad/informacje-ogolne/aktualnosci/13173%2CRaport-URE-w-Polsce-mamy-juz-ponad-16-mln-mikroinstalacji-OZE.html)
- UDT reported 6,733 PV installer certificates issued by the end of April 2025. These are certificates issued to people, not active certificates, unique firms, or local prospective customers. [UDT, *Inspektor OZE*](https://www.udt.gov.pl/images/kampania/biuletyn/pdf/INSPEKTOR_OZE_WCAG.pdf)
- Polish work-at-height rules require direct supervision, safeguards and instruction for particularly dangerous work. PIP says collective protection such as guardrails should be used before individual protection where possible. [PIP guidance on particularly dangerous work](https://www.pip.gov.pl/dla-pracodawcow/porady-prawne/prace-szczegolnie-niebezpieczne-oraz-prace-wymagajace-szczegolnej-sprawnosci-psychofizycznej?tmpl=print)
- The employer retains responsibility for occupational safety. Where multiple employers work at one site, they must coordinate, but appointing a coordinator does not release each employer from responsibility for its workers. [PIP employer-responsibility guidance](https://www.pip.gov.pl/dla-pracodawcow/porady-prawne/odpowiedzialnosc-i-podstawowe-obowiazki-pracodawcy)
- A Polish rental catalogue offers a roofing lift for a 24-hour rental period, demonstrating an existing equipment-rental route. The parsed catalogue did not reveal a usable price. [ATUT Rental 2025 catalogue](https://www.atutrental.com.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/ATUT-RENTAL-Katalog-2025-Wypozyczalnia-sprzetu-budowlanego-i-ogrodniczego.pdf)
- A July 2026 commercial listing offered a used 22-metre, 200 kg trailer-mounted roofing lift from 13,900 PLN. This is one asking price, not a market average, inspected-condition guarantee or complete-cell cost. [OLX equipment listing](https://www.olx.pl/d/oferta/winda-drabina-dekarska-emminghaus-pionier-22m-z-zalamaniem-wciagarka-na-przyczepie-do-dachowki-CID5216-ID14jLOy.html)
- UDT states that all vehicle-mounted loading platforms are under technical supervision, with a simplified regime for certain material-only platforms up to 250 kg. That page concerns vehicle tail lifts—not roofing hoists—so its classification cannot lawfully be transferred to the proposed equipment. [UDT loading-platform guidance](https://www.udt.gov.pl/uslugi-udt/dozor-techniczny/urzadzenia-podlegajace-dozorowi-technicznemu/urzadzenia-transportu-bliskiego/podest-ruchomy-zaladowczy-na-pojezdzie)

### Interpretation

The opportunity arises from combining equipment that installers can otherwise rent or buy separately into a job-ready cell with predictable delivery, compatible fixtures, documented limits and one reservation. Local equipment density could reduce transport cost and increase turns. None of the sources establishes that installers will pay the modeled bundle price or save enough crew time to justify it.

### Unknown

Actual paid days per cell, local installer count, crew hours saved, bundle price tolerance, roof compatibility, delivery radius, seasonal utilization, theft and damage incidence, insurance terms, exact conformity requirements, exact UDT classification and who may assemble, operate or maintain each selected model remain unknown.

## 2. Problem and payer

The loss bearer is an installer paying skilled workers while panels, roof materials and tools are moved manually or while the crew assembles fragmented rental equipment. The proposed payer is the installer because it controls the job schedule, bears crew cost and captures any throughput improvement.

The evidence establishes physical installation volume, work-at-height obligations, and existing hoist rental and purchase routes. It does **not** establish:

- how many crew-hours a cell saves;
- whether edge protection is already owned or provided by another contractor;
- whether small PV jobs require a hoist;
- whether installers prefer a 24-hour cell over buying a used lift;
- willingness to prepay.

The first commercial test must therefore measure paid use and actual job economics rather than interest.

## 3. Exact transaction

1. Installer submits job date, postcode, roof height and pitch, access dimensions, material type and count, intended load, power availability and named site supervisor.
2. Venture accepts only configurations covered by the selected equipment’s instructions, load limits, inspection status and insurer conditions.
3. Installer signs a dry-hire agreement identifying the employer/site controller, permitted users, prohibited people-lifting, damage responsibility and cancellation terms.
4. Suggested cash structure: 30% reservation payment at booking and the remaining 70% no later than 72 hours before delivery. Access is fully prepaid.
5. Venture delivers an inventoried cell: material-only hoist, approved handling fixtures, applicable temporary edge-protection components, exclusion-zone items, instructions and inspection records.
6. A documented ground-level handover records condition, configuration, named recipient and load restrictions.
7. The installer controls the site, supervises its workers, erects and uses equipment only through competent personnel, and retains work-at-height obligations.
8. Venture collects the cell, records damage and utilization, performs required checks and returns any damage deposit after inspection.

## 4. Incentive and power map

| Party | Gives / receives | Incentive | Power, information and risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venture | Equipment access, delivery, records, configuration screening | Paid days, asset turns, repeat reservations | Owns fleet and calendar; bears utilization, maintenance, insured damage and residual-value risk |
| Installer | Prepayment and job data; receives a complete cell | Reduce handling time, avoid ownership and fragmented sourcing | Controls booking, workers and site execution; knows roof geometry and job schedule |
| Installation employer | Supervision and safe organization | Complete job without injury or stoppage | Retains statutory responsibility for its workforce |
| Site controller/building owner | Site access and local restrictions | Timely, low-disruption work | Can deny access or impose working-area conditions |
| Equipment manufacturer | Instructions, limits and parts | Equipment sales and compliant use | Determines approved configurations and maintenance requirements |
| UDT or other competent authority | Classification and inspection decisions where applicable | Technical safety | Can require registration, inspection, qualified operation or maintenance depending on equipment class |
| Insurer | Liability, property and transport cover | Premium with controlled exposure | Can exclude renter setup, work at height, theft or unsuitable equipment |
| Wholesaler/referrer | Introductions and possibly pickup location | Installer loyalty and referral income | Controls access to recurring installer traffic |
| Crew | Operates around the equipment | Less manual handling and faster completion | Can refuse impractical equipment; misuse creates safety and damage exposure |

No contract can remove the installer employer’s statutory safety responsibilities. The venture also retains duties arising from equipment ownership, maintenance, delivery and any work its own staff perform at a site.

## 5. Payer denominator

The best verified national proxy is **6,733 PV installer certificates issued by April 2025**. It overstates some aspects of the market because certificates may be expired or held by multiple people at one firm, and understates others because roofers and electrical contractors may rent without appearing as PV certificate holders.

Accordingly:

- **Verified denominator:** 6,733 PV certificates issued, not payers.
- **Exact national payer denominator:** unknown.
- **Local addressable firms within an economical delivery radius:** unknown.
- **Planning comparison only:** 100 renting firms would equal about 1.5% of the certificate-issuance proxy, but that is not a defensible market-share calculation because the units differ.

A usable denominator requires deduplicating active firms by postcode through the UDT register, business registers and wholesaler customer lists.

## 6. Price, unit economics and cash timing

All figures below are explicit model inputs, in PLN excluding VAT. No observed complete-cell rental price was found.

### Modeled central cell-day

| Item | PLN |
|---|---:|
| All-in price within defined delivery zone | 1,350 |
| Delivery and collection | 240 |
| Maintenance and inspection reserve | 85 |
| Damage and theft reserve | 75 |
| Cleaning, consumables and payment cost | 50 |
| Modeled direct cost | 450 |
| Contribution per paid day | 900 |
| Contribution margin | 66.7% |

At a modeled complete-cell capital cost of 45,000 PLN, simple capital recovery is approximately **50 paid days** before fixed overhead, tax, interest and replacement capital.

Customer break-even is demanding: at an assumed loaded crew cost of 70 PLN/hour, a 1,350 PLN invoice requires about **19.3 crew-hours** of savings unless it also replaces other rental, transport or delay costs. For example, four workers saving two hours produce only 560 PLN of modeled labor benefit. This calculation makes measured crew-time savings and avoided alternative costs central to the paid proof.

### Cash timing

- Reservation cash arrives before the asset is dispatched.
- Final payment arrives 72 hours before delivery.
- Fuel, delivery labor and cleaning are paid during or immediately after the job.
- Maintenance, insurance and inspections require cash before or independent of utilization.
- Equipment and trailers require upfront capital; debt payments continue during winter inactivity.
- VAT, corporate tax and personal distribution tax are omitted from transaction contribution but included as model reserves in the wealth cases.

## 7. Founder-wealth cases

### Shared modeling rules

- Ten-year horizon; year-ten figures are annual run rates.
- Founder begins with up to 100,000 PLN.
- Founder remains employed and contributes no more than five hours daily; an operations coordinator is hired once the fleet cannot be handled within that limit.
- Figures exclude VAT and founder salary is included at market cost in operating expenses.
- “Operating margin” is EBIT after modeled depreciation.
- Valuations are debt-free/cash-free multiples of EBITDA.
- A 19% blended company cash-tax reserve and 19% personal tax on distributions are modeling assumptions, not legal advice.
- Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash and distribute at most 5%. Strong success reinvests 100%.
- Reinvested cash is reflected through later fleet size and EBITDA; it is not added again to founder wealth.
- Founder wealth is modeled company equity plus cumulative net distributions. Outside salary and savings are excluded.

## 8. Acquisition route

The initial route is relationship-led and operational, without camera-led or influencer distribution:

1. Family renewable installer supplies the first job access and introductions.
2. Each paid job produces a factual before/after handling log, not promotional testimonials dependent on personal visibility.
3. Regional roofing and electrical wholesalers receive a fixed referral fee or account credit for completed paid rentals.
4. Direct outreach targets installers with scheduled pitched-roof work inside the delivery radius.
5. Local search advertising captures high-intent terms such as roofing-lift rental, panel lift and temporary roof-edge protection.
6. Repeat customers receive calendar priority, standardized saved configurations and consolidated monthly reporting, while rentals remain prepaid.

### First ten customers

- Customer 1: family-connected renewable firm, paying an arm’s-length invoice.
- Customers 2–4: direct referrals from that firm.
- Customers 5–7: introductions through two regional roofing/PV wholesalers.
- Customers 8–10: roofing and electrical firms contacted around confirmed upcoming jobs.

The first ten should cover materially different roof heights, pitches, panel counts and access conditions so the configuration library measures compatibility rather than repeating one easy job type.

### First one hundred customers

| Channel | Customers |
|---|---:|
| Family network and customer referrals | 10 |
| Three to five wholesaler relationships | 30 |
| Roofing-company partnerships | 25 |
| Direct outreach to scheduled installers | 20 |

Expansion should occur in adjacent service clusters so that each additional customer improves route density rather than widening unprofitable transport radii.

## 9. Control and compounding asset

The venture controls:

- the physical fleet;
- the reservation calendar;
- inspection, service and damage records;
- an equipment-to-roof configuration library;
- installer booking history and seasonality data;
- local delivery routes and turnaround procedures;
- records of which fixtures, loads and job types produced repeat bookings.

The compounding mechanism is local density: more repeat reservations within a compact area can reduce transport cost and idle time. Saved job configurations can shorten quoting and reduce incompatibility. This is an operating-data advantage, not exclusive intellectual property; competitors can buy similar hardware.

## 10. Incumbent route-around

Installers can bypass the venture by:

- buying a used lift—the observed listing starts at 13,900 PLN;
- renting only a roofing lift from an established rental company;
- owning edge protection and fixtures separately;
- using scaffolding, manual handling, a telehandler or a truck-mounted crane;
- asking a wholesaler or manufacturer to bundle lifting equipment;
- coordinating jobs internally around an owned asset.

Rental chains can add the missing accessories, wholesalers can create their own cells, and a large installer can purchase equipment once recurring use makes ownership cheaper.

The venture’s practical response is reliable complete-cell availability, configuration screening, delivery, documented handover, fast replacement and dense local logistics. It should not depend on exclusivity, control of installer dispatch or preventing customers from buying equipment.

## 12. Lawful structure

A single Polish limited-liability operating company can own and rent the fleet, subject to professional legal, tax and insurance advice. The simplest operating structure is:

- rent only equipment whose documentation, conformity status and permitted use are verified;
- obtain written equipment classification and inspection requirements for each model;
- prohibit people-lifting physically, contractually and in handover materials;
- supply Polish-language instructions, load limits and inspection records;
- use competent maintenance personnel where required;
- keep the venture’s standard service as delivery and documented dry-hire handover;
- use a separately scoped, trained site contractor if the venture offers installation or dismantling;
- require written coordination with the site controller before any venture personnel enter a work zone;
- insure equipment, transport, third-party liability and renter-caused damage to the extent available;
- stop dispatch when job data falls outside approved configurations.

The installer remains responsible for its workers and work-at-height organization. “Equipment rental only” reduces but does not eliminate the venture’s exposure as owner, maintainer and supplier.

## 13. Dependencies

- Written classification from UDT or qualified technical counsel for each exact model.
- Manufacturer documentation and service-part availability.
- Insurer acceptance of rental, transport, theft, renter assembly and work-at-height exposure.
- Secure yard and trailer storage.
- A transport vehicle and licensed/qualified drivers where required.
- Reliable inspection and repair contractor.
- Handling fixtures specifically approved for the intended materials.
- Usable edge-protection systems across target roof types.
- Installer job data accurate enough for remote screening.
- Sufficient local job density to avoid transport consuming contribution.
- An operations coordinator or contractor before fleet scale exceeds the founder’s time.
- Financing that tolerates seasonal inactivity.

## 14. Fastest paid proof and capital at risk

### Sequence

1. Obtain written positions from the proposed manufacturer, maintenance provider, insurer and UDT or technical adviser for one exact material-only model.
2. Select one narrow configuration: pitched residential roofs within a compact radius, a stated material/load range and a documented exclusion list.
3. Ask the family firm and warm referrals for conditional prepaid reservations tied to real scheduled jobs; refund if the equipment cannot be supplied lawfully.
4. Purchase one inspected hoist, compatible fixtures, a limited edge-protection set and trailer only after the compliance and insurance checks.
5. Run the first paid day with time logs for setup, material movement, crew waiting, delivery, cleaning and return inspection.
6. Invoice subsequent users at arm’s-length pricing and obtain prepayment rather than free trials.

### Capital exposed

The packet’s **25,000–55,000 PLN** range is plausible for a first complete cell but is not fully verified by the sources. The observed 13,900 PLN used-hoist asking price covers only one used lift and says nothing about inspection, refurbishment, edge protection, fixtures, trailer suitability, insurance or working capital.

Capital at risk includes the purchase price, refurbishment, trailer, storage, insurance deposit and any equipment that cannot be used across common roof configurations. A paid conditional reservation reduces demand uncertainty but not technical, liability or resale risk.

## 15. Kill criteria

Stop deploying additional capital if any of the following occurs:

- the exact equipment cannot receive a clear lawful-use and maintenance path;
- available insurance excludes the central rental, setup, theft or dropped-load exposures;
- paid jobs produce negative contribution after actual delivery, maintenance and damage costs;
- representative crews save too little time or avoid too little alternative cost to justify the invoice;
- common roofs require incompatible or bespoke equipment that prevents repeatable cells;
- customers consistently prefer purchasing used lifts or renting components separately;
- safe handover regularly requires venture staff to perform uncontrolled work at height;
- any customer pressures the venture to permit people-lifting, bypass protection or exceed documented loads;
- winter cash commitments cannot be covered without relying on future bookings;
- the family channel fails to produce arm’s-length paying customers outside the related firm.
