# Level-2 Dossier — DIR-002

Lead: `RAW-IAT-05`, refrigerant leak prevention plus reclaimed-gas reserve. Development status: `complete`.

## Problem And Payer Evidence

Qualifying stationary refrigeration requires certified leak checks and repair; only certified undertakings may service circuits or buy servicing F-gases. A rough national ceiling is 11,000–11,300 supermarkets/hypermarkets, with a smaller relevant set of legacy centralized HFC systems. Vendor cases show meaningful leak-cost savings, but EU Q1 2026 evidence described broadly stable supply and reclaimed R404A near/slightly above virgin-equivalent pricing. That contradicts a general scarcity-reserve thesis while leaving leak monitoring and emergency availability plausible.

## Proposed Transaction

Sell monitoring, triage and certified-partner dispatch. Use existing controller telemetry where possible; add direct sensors only at high-risk points. A certified refrigeration partner performs all circuit work and holds any reclaimed-gas allocation. Planning ranges: 2–8k PLN/site setup with existing telemetry, 8–20k with new sensors, and 300–900 PLN/site/month; repair and refrigerant are separate. The founder claims read access and alert/dispatch rights, never gas custody or regulatory sign-off.

## Acquisition Route

Contract first with a UDT-certified refrigeration firm and screen its expiring contracts/emergency history for ten legacy-HFC sites. Sell a 500–1,500 PLN records/telemetry audit. Cheapest proof: three-site historical back-test and 30-day shadow alerts for 10–25k PLN.

## Decision-Critical Unknowns

Actual Polish refill price/availability, telemetry access fees, false positives/missed leaks, partner SLA and liability, enforceability of gas allocation, customer top-up/spoilage spend, conflicts with incumbent maintenance contracts, and irrelevance at CO₂/R290 sites. The reserve component is currently weak; monitoring must carry the value.
