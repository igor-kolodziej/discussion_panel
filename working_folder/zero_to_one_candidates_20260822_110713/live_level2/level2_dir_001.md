# Level-2 Dossier — DIR-001

Lead: `RAW-FPE-02`, dense manual-return route for small deposit-system shops. Development status: `complete`.

## Problem And Payer Evidence

Poland’s deposit system has operated since 1 October 2025. The operator contract controls collection logistics, reconciliation, pickup and handling fees. More than 318,000 shops existed in 2024, but the actionable count of sub-200 m² independent shops voluntarily accepting PET/cans without adequate pickup is unknown. Public examples show only about 0.12–0.15 PLN handling fee per verified unit; 2,500 units/month yields 300–375 PLN/shop. Operators already advertise free standard bags, onboarding and pickup, so retailer pain does not by itself create a third-party fee pool.

## Proposed Transaction

An operator-appointed logistics subcontractor supplies sealed cages/bags, scheduled swaps, scan/weight/timestamp evidence and exception support. The operator retains packaging ownership, verification and deposit settlement. A test hypothesis is 99–199 PLN/shop/month for a premium SLA plus an operator-paid stop/cage/unit rate. A ten-shop pilot needs roughly 30–50k PLN for cages, scanning, van rental, insurance, integration and working capital; it is not viable on the retailer handling fee alone.

## Acquisition Route

Obtain written protocol approval and draft compensation from one operator first, then recruit a compact Ostrów–Kalisz cluster through wholesalers, POS providers and local merchant networks. Cheapest private proof: three shops, sealed units, blind recount and measured route time for 8–15k PLN.

## Decision-Critical Unknowns

Operator appointment requirements and logistics fee; volumes, cage turns and stops/hour; shop willingness to pay above free standard pickup; discrepancy liability; multi-operator interoperability; cash timing and data rights. Without an operator-paid logistics rate, the premise is economically contradicted.
