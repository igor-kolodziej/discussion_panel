# Zero-to-One Pivot: Material-Delay Yield Lots

## Preserved Insight

Use public company-closeout events to buy whole batches of already-accrued post-payment rights from an authorised seller, creating owned receivables plus recovery-pricing data.

## Structural Change

The revision stops buying low-face claims broadly. It buys only recurring commercial invoices with **principal of at least 50,000 PLN and delay of at least 30 days**, after principal has been paid, in lots of at least 20 claims. This changes the economic unit from a 420 PLN right requiring 60,000 annual claims to an estimated 1,200 PLN right requiring 20,000 annual claims. It also removes trivial-delay/nuisance cases and cuts the conservative requirement from 750 eighty-claim lots to 400 fifty-claim lots across Poland/CEE at scale.

## Target Niche and Buyer

Registered liquidators of solvent Polish companies in voluntary liquidation that supplied recurring, non-medical B2B or public contracts during the prior three years. The first fact pattern is a company with at least 20 invoices above 50,000 PLN, each paid at least 30 days late, with completed/undisputed performance and no assignment ban. Bankruptcy estates, healthcare, construction disputes and active supplier relationships are excluded.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The liquidator must realise residual assets and finish the company. A 20-claim lot has estimated face around 24,000 PLN—large enough to matter, small and procedurally repetitive enough to delay closeout if pursued internally. A 30% cash offer produces about 7,200 PLN immediately and a documented disposal, without the seller funding collection.

## Value and Profit Wedge

For a 50,000+ PLN invoice paid 30–60 days late, statutory interest plus 100 EUR compensation can plausibly exceed 1,000 PLN. The model uses a **1,200 PLN average face hypothesis** and must calculate each claim exactly.

| Unit item | Hypothesis |
|---|---:|
| Average accrued interest + compensation face | 1,200 PLN |
| Seller price, 30% of face | 360 PLN |
| Expected collection, 80% of face | 960 PLN |
| Direct notice/legal/payment cost | 100 PLN |
| Contribution after purchase and direct recovery | 500 PLN |
| Gross margin on collected cash | 52% |

Conservative scale: 400 lots × 50 acquired claims × 960 PLN collected = 19.2m PLN revenue; 52% gross margin; 26% operating margin = 4.99m PLN operating profit. At 4× and 80% founder ownership, founder equity is **15.97m PLN** in years 7–9. Expected: 800 lots × 75 claims × 1,050 PLN = 63m PLN revenue; 30% operating margin; 4.5×; 75% ownership = **63.8m PLN**. Strong: 1,600 lots × 100 claims × 1,150 PLN = 184m PLN revenue; 33% operating margin; 5×; 65% ownership = **197.3m PLN**. Conservative/expected employment years reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash with ≤5% optional distributions; strong growth reinvests 100%. Retained cash is not added to enterprise value.

The conservative 400-lot requirement equals 1.6% of 2025 KRS entrepreneur-register removals and about 4.8% of an annualised 8,334 liquidation cross-check before public/B2B and invoice filters. Therefore Poland alone may not suffice; the scenario explicitly requires Poland plus at least two counsel-cleared CEE jurisdictions by scale year. A bottom-up 4,000-eligible-lot census is a pre-scaling kill criterion.

## Domination Wedge

One fact pattern, one claim calculator and one authority chain: solvent closeout, completed recurring contract, 50,000+ PLN invoice, 30+ days late, principal paid, no dispute, no assignment restriction, claim inside limitation and 20+ claims per lot. Quote within 48 hours; buy the whole accepted lot; exclude everything else.

## Acquisition Channel

Daily KRS/MSiG closeout detection joined to BZP/UZP awards and public/company financial histories. Rank recurring contracts likely to generate 20+ large invoices, then write to the registered liquidator. This event-specific list is directly buildable; accountants and liquidation firms are optional later channels after direct CAC is measured.

## Primary Control Point

Executed schedule-level assignment of every claim, source invoice and payment evidence, seller warranties, debtor notice and settlement rights. The company owns each receivable. Across lots it owns the only dataset joining closeout type, clause, debtor, invoice, delay, notice, recovery, time and cost.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

- 8,000 PLN counsel opinion/contracts covering assignment, liquidator authority, notice, licensing, tax and representation.
- 5,000 PLN KRS/BZP matching and secure upload.
- up to 12,000 PLN to buy one or two 20+ claim lots.
- 7,000 PLN bounded notice/enforcement reserve.
- maximum pre-paid-proof exposure: **32,000 PLN**.

Proof within 180 days: one signed 20+ claim lot from an authorised liquidator, at least five debtor payments, at least 300 PLN observed contribution per accepted claim after lot CAC, and a second qualified seller willing to transact. The founder stays employed and does not finance invoice principal.

## Expansion Path

1. Polish voluntary-liquidation public recurring suppliers.
2. Polish solvent B2B recurring-contract closeouts with investment-grade debtors.
3. Czech and Slovak closeouts under local counsel and ring-fenced cohorts.
4. Event/API pricing for liquidation accountants and lawyers.
5. Only after stable recovery, institutional revolving finance for purchased ancillary rights.

## Minimum People Stack

Founder for event data, pricing and product; fixed-scope Polish commercial lawyer; part-time paralegal after recovery; local counsel only per expansion country. No speculative partnership or full-time founder transition.

## Principal Risk

The stricter economic filter may leave too few 20-claim lots. If fewer than 4,000 annual eligible closeout lots can be bottom-up identified across the first three countries, or if a lot averages under 20 claims, the founder-wealth gate fails even though unit economics improve.

## First Disconfirming Test

Within 180 days and 32,000 PLN: counsel clean opinion, 250 annualised Polish closeout candidates after public-history filtering, one acquired 20+ claim lot, five paid claims, ≥300 PLN observed per-claim contribution and one second seller. Missing or ambiguous evidence is failure.

## Evidence Preserved

Statutory and court sources, CJEU assigned-claim precedent, official KRS reports, public procurement history, KSeF timing, BFF incumbent evidence and the first pivot's event-acquisition logic.

## New Hypotheses

- 1,200 PLN average face for the strict 50,000 PLN/30-day cohort.
- 80% collection and 100 PLN direct cost.
- 20–100 claims per closing supplier lot.
- 4,000 annual eligible lots across Poland plus Czechia and Slovakia.
- 30% seller price is acceptable and legally defensible.
- Local-law expansion remains under 100,000 PLN per country only after Polish paid proof.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

It retains the closeout buyer and owned assignment, but materially changes the inventory filter, value per claim, required volume, seller payment, nuisance risk and geographic scale logic. It no longer depends on harvesting every small statutory compensation entitlement or winning an implausible share of Polish liquidations.

## Return-to-Evaluator Priority

Return this revision first. It directly addresses the v1 founder-wealth denominator and nuisance objections while preserving the strongest control point and proof speed. It remains a hypothesis until independently evaluated and validated.
