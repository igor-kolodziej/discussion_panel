# Zero-to-One Pivot: Severe-Delay Yield Lots

## Preserved Insight

Acquire whole batches of post-payment statutory rights from authorised company-closeout sellers, using public closeout events as the acquisition trigger and executed assignment as the control point.

## Structural Change

The previous material-delay revision still needed an average 50 claims per closing company. This version changes the economic filter again: invoice principal must be at least **100,000 PLN**, payment must be at least **60 days late**, and a lot needs at least **10 claims**. At the current Polish commercial late-interest order of magnitude, 60 days on 100,000 PLN plus 100 EUR compensation is close to 3,000 PLN. The conservative scale becomes 400 closing sellers averaging 20 claims, or 8,000 claims—not 20,000 or 60,000.

## Target Niche and Buyer

Registered liquidators of solvent companies in voluntary liquidation with recurring, completed, non-medical public/B2B contracts and at least ten clean 100,000+ PLN invoices paid 60+ days late. No unpaid principal, construction/performance dispute, healthcare, assignment ban, current strategic relationship or court-controlled estate.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

A minimum ten-claim lot has estimated face near 30,000 PLN and a 30% cash price near 9,000 PLN. That is meaningful closeout cash. The liquidator can document an arm's-length disposal and avoid extending the liquidation to pursue ten separate residual rights.

## Value and Profit Wedge

Base hypothesis per acquired claim: 3,000 PLN face, 900 PLN seller price, 2,400 PLN cash collected, 150 PLN direct recovery cost and 1,350 PLN contribution (56% gross margin). A 10-claim minimum lot pays the seller about 9,000 PLN and creates 13,500 PLN expected claim contribution before 1,200 PLN lot CAC.

Conservative: 400 lots × 20 claims × 2,400 PLN = 19.2m PLN revenue; 56% GM; 26% operating margin / 4.99m PLN operating profit; 4× value × 80% ownership = 15.97m PLN founder equity in years 7–9. Expected: 800 × 30 × 2,600 PLN = 62.4m PLN; 30% operating margin; 4.5× × 75% = 63.18m PLN. Strong: 1,600 × 40 × 2,875 PLN = 184m PLN; 33% operating margin; 5× × 65% = 197.34m PLN. Reinvestment and no-double-count rules remain unchanged.

## Domination Wedge

One severe, non-nuisance fact pattern and one schedule: 100,000+ PLN, 60+ days late, principal paid, clean completion, no assignment ban, ten claims per lot and authorised closeout seller.

## Acquisition Channel

Official KRS/MSiG closeout events joined to public procurement and company contract histories. Optional accountants/law firms follow only after direct event CAC is proved.

## Primary Control Point

Executed schedule-level assignments and the resulting severe-delay recovery dataset. The stricter cohort increases value per diligence decision and reduces operational volume.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

6,000 PLN legal opinion/contracts, 4,000 PLN secure event/document tooling, up to 12,000 PLN for one minimum lot, and 6,000 PLN bounded recovery reserve: maximum 28,000 PLN before paid proof. Proof is one ten-claim assignment, five debtor payments, ≥800 PLN realised contribution per accepted claim after lot CAC and a second qualified seller within 180 days.

## Expansion Path

Polish public closeouts, then high-credit private B2B closeouts, then Czechia/Slovakia and further EU countries only after local counsel and ring-fenced recovery cohorts.

## Minimum People Stack

Founder, fixed-scope commercial lawyer and later a part-time paralegal. No partner dependency or full-time transition during proof.

## Principal Risk

Severe-delay lots may be too rare. A bottom-up 4,000-eligible-lot Poland/Czechia/Slovakia census is required so the conservative 400 lots remain at or below 10% share.

## First Disconfirming Test

Counsel clean opinion, 250 annualised Polish public-history candidates, one purchased ten-claim lot, five payments, ≥800 PLN contribution per accepted claim and one second willing seller inside 180 days and 28,000 PLN.

## Evidence Preserved

Polish statute and Supreme Court authority, CJEU assigned-claim case, KRS reports, public procurement data, KSeF timing, BFF competitor proof and the event-acquisition insight.

## New Hypotheses

3,000 PLN average face; 80% collection; 150 PLN direct cost; 20 claims per average lot; 4,000 eligible annual lots across three initial countries; 30% seller price.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

It changes the acquired inventory from low-value compensation claims to severe, high-principal delay rights, cuts required claim volume by two-thirds versus the material-delay revision and by 87% versus the original, increases seller cash per minimum lot, and removes nuisance optics while preserving owned title and event distribution.

## Return-to-Evaluator Priority

Return the exact severe-delay version first; it has the strongest wealth-to-volume ratio and the cleanest disconfirming test. It remains unvalidated.
