# Zero-to-One Candidate: HumanFactor Works

## One-Sentence Thesis

Acquire a retiring Greater-Poland BHP provider's recurring SME contracts with seller financing, retain qualified operators, and use the installed employer distribution to sell standardized instrumented task-rehearsal and smart-PPE programs—turning scarce local contract books into a profitable distribution layer for a future human-augmentation lab.

## Founder Fit

The founder's strongest contribution is acquisition analytics, pricing, process measurement, AI/ML and disciplined reinvestment—not pretending to be a BHP specialist. Polish law allows small employers to use qualified external specialists, so the acquisition must include a qualified operator under an 18-month transition/employment agreement and a signed backup specialist before closing. Industrial context from the family renewable business helps the founder understand field-work customers but provides no validation credit and is not a required buyer.

The structure is compatible with corporate employment because the acquired operator delivers daily service and the founder handles search, finance, analytics and one standardized product day per month. The deal is killed if it is merely the seller's personal freelancing income rather than a transferable contract book with replaceable delivery.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

There are two customers in the initial transaction:

1. **The SME employer:** needs initial/periodic training, risk documentation, incident support and qualified BHP service. It values continuity, local response and a provider that already knows its sites. Changing to an unknown startup merely to try an AI tool is unattractive.
2. **The small BHP-firm owner:** has built 50–100 employer relationships but may lack a successor. A strategic buyer or broker may not care about a micro-deal. The owner may prefer upfront cash, a predictable seller note, continued salary and protection of client relationships over closing the practice.

The current workaround for the employer is a solo local specialist plus spreadsheets and separate equipment/training vendors. The current workaround for an owner without family succession is to keep working, shrink, sell informally or let the client book dissipate. The existence and economics of willing sellers are hypotheses to test, not facts.

## Product Wedge

The first “product” is a controlled acquisition and continuity package:

1. Buy the target company or a legally structured business unit including brand, phone/domain, schedules, equipment, SOPs and customer contracts that are novated or renewed into NewCo.
2. Keep the seller/qualified specialist at a market salary for 18 months, with part of the purchase price paid over 36–48 months and contingent on collected retained revenue.
3. Continue the existing paid BHP service without forcing new software on clients.
4. Within 90 days after transition, offer installed customers an **8,500 PLN Instrumented Task-Rehearsal Day**: a qualified specialist observes a bounded non-production task; phone/depth video or rented IMU/wearable tools quantify movements; workers receive coaching; the employer receives before/after task evidence. The module is not medical diagnosis, employee scoring, continuous surveillance or a substitute for regulated exposure measurements.
5. Only separately consenting workers can contribute de-identified motion features to R&D. Raw video is deleted on a defined schedule. The strategic asset does not depend on this consent—the acquired employer distribution, qualified staff and cash flow already qualify.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Human-augmentation startups commonly build a sensor or AI ergonomics tool and then discover that trusted BHP providers control employer access. Poland's fragmented service market suggests the inverse strategy: acquire a finite book of trusted, recurring contracts before selecting or developing technology. The lab then owns a lawful sales and field-testing channel instead of repeatedly seeking speculative employer partnerships.

The acquisition itself is not technologically novel; its strategic novelty under the founder's constraints is sequencing. Revenue, trust, qualified people and distribution arrive first. Proprietary human-factors IP is allowed to emerge only from paid installed-base demand.

## Underused Momentum

- CIOP-PIB estimates roughly 5,600 BHP service entities in Poland, indicating fragmentation.
- Employers continue to have initial and periodic training and workplace-safety obligations.
- Published Polish pricing shows recurring small-company service retainers and paid per-worker training.
- European safety groups have used buy-and-build extensively, validating consolidation as an economic pattern at larger scale.
- Smartphone pose estimation and rental wearables make small instrumented programs feasible, while their widespread availability confirms that the technology alone is not a moat.

The unproven momentum is owner succession. No numerical claim about retiring owners is made.

## Beachhead Market

One acquisition within roughly 120 km of Ostrów Wielkopolski/Poznań/Wrocław, serving 50–100 industrial, warehouse, construction, installation or service SMEs, with:

- at least 70% of trailing revenue from repeat retainers, scheduled training or other recurring customer work;
- no customer above 10% of revenue;
- at least 70% of clients willing to sign a 12-month renewal or novation;
- normalized operating profit of at least 15% after paying a market salary to replace the owner;
- clean tax, employment, incident and data-transfer diligence;
- a qualified specialist who signs before closing and a second qualified backup.

Do not acquire a practice dominated by one construction project, public tender, founder-only reputation, medical services, litigation, one-off documentation or unpaid receivables.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Initial target hypothesis:

- 70 employer customers, average 800 PLN monthly service revenue = 672,000 PLN annually.
- Approximately 100,000 PLN additional scheduled training/audit revenue = 772,000 PLN total.
- 55% gross margin after directly attributable specialist/trainer time and travel.
- 18% normalized operating margin after a market-paid operating specialist, or about 139,000 PLN.
- Maximum enterprise purchase price: 3.0× verified normalized operating profit, approximately 417,000 PLN—not a claimed market average.
- Proposed funding: 70,000 PLN founder cash after paid proof, 267,000 PLN seller note over four years and up to 80,000 PLN collected-revenue earnout. Exact purchase price falls if customer novation or normalized profit is weaker.

The Instrumented Task-Rehearsal Day is priced at 8,500 PLN. Pilot direct cost hypothesis: 2,000 PLN qualified-specialist time, 800 PLN device rental/consumables, 700 PLN travel/data handling and 1,000 PLN analysis/support; 4,500 PLN gross contribution, or 53%. No proprietary wearable is built during the POC.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

The first ten are not acquired blindly. Under a signed option and customer-contact protocol, at least 15 existing customers must independently renew/novate and pay the first monthly or quarterly invoice to NewCo at the transition closing. Their payments are the Reachability Gate evidence. Any family-company contract is excluded from the required 15.

The first 100 come from the acquired book plus referrals and one neighboring tuck-in only after 12-month retention. Organic outbound is secondary. Every acquired customer is tagged by service effort, margin, travel density, renewal date and suitability for human-factors modules. Growth is measured in retained gross profit, not nominal account count.

## Primary Control Point

The Tier-1 target is a paid exclusive option/no-shop followed by an asset/share purchase that transfers or novates recurring contracts, brand, phone/domain, schedules and documented workflows, plus binding employment/transition agreements with qualified specialists. These are owned or contracted, finite local resources.

The strongest copy risk is another acquirer offering better terms to other BHP owners. It cannot copy the already acquired customer book with one vendor contract. HumanFactor Works must close quietly at micro-deal scale, retain customers and build route density before larger consolidators care.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

### Resource Needed

A transferable BHP business with verified normalized profit, 50–100 repeat employer customers, a qualified operator and a seller willing to finance at least 60% of purchase consideration.

### How It Is Secured

- Build a public list of 150 small providers from CIOP/industry directories, CEIDG/KRS, maps and local training listings.
- Send confidential physical acquisition letters and make compliant direct calls, explicitly offering continuity, seller employment and income—not a free “partnership.”
- Require three years of bank statements, tax returns, invoices, customer cohorts, service calendars and owner time logs before discussing price.
- Pay at most 10,000 PLN for a 90-day exclusive option/no-shop only after preliminary earnings verification. The option is credited to closing and contains diligence/customer-retention conditions.
- Contact customers only under an agreed data/privacy protocol. Require at least 15 independent paid renewals/novations and 70% signed revenue retention before releasing acquisition cash.
- Sign the seller's 18-month market-paid employment/transition agreement and a backup specialist's conditional employment contract before close.

### Why An Unknown Founder Can Secure It

The offer does not rely on fame or institutional endorsement. A micro-provider that is too small for private equity can receive liquidity, continued salary, performance-linked income and a named succession plan. Seller willingness is a testable hypothesis: if 150 letters, 30 conversations and five data-room invitations do not produce one qualifying option, the idea dies before the acquisition.

### Proof Before Serious Capital

Signed option/no-shop, verified normalized earnings after owner replacement, at least 15 independent paid customer renewals into NewCo, 70% contracted revenue retention, two qualified specialist agreements, and one 4,000 PLN deposit for an Instrumented Task-Rehearsal Day. No acquisition close occurs without this proof.

## Distribution Advantage

Acquired contracts solve cold start immediately and create a high-trust local channel to employers and workers. Geographic density lowers travel time and makes specialist capacity more productive. Renewals, incident work and training dates supply natural moments to offer a bounded human-factors program. Later acquisitions add customers and qualified specialists to the same channel; they do not merely add software seats.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

1. **Year 1:** retain the first book; centralize invoicing/scheduling; sell three paid task-rehearsal days; establish worker privacy and product-selection rules.
2. **Years 2–3:** acquire a neighboring book only after 12-month revenue retention exceeds 85% and seller-note coverage exceeds 2×. Standardize operations and create a shared pool of qualified specialists.
3. **Years 3–5:** offer manufacturer-agnostic smart-PPE pilots, manual-handling coaching, instrumented training and exposure-device rental where lawful. Buy devices only against customer deposits.
4. **Years 5–8:** consolidate several regional books, build procurement leverage and launch proprietary HCI/wearable components only where repeated paid use reveals a neglected need.

The company becomes difficult to replace because it owns local contracts, history, response workflow, renewal calendar and qualified specialist coverage. Technology competitors can sell devices; HumanFactor Works decides which devices reach its installed base.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish BHP qualification rules, employer training obligations, local site visits, Polish documentation and a fragmented base of small providers make local contract density meaningful. The model adapts a proven European safety-services consolidation pattern to micro-deals below institutional investors' attention. It is not defended by regulation alone; it is defended by purchased contracts and retained local operators.

## Compounding Advantage

Each acquisition provides recurring gross profit, employers, workers, qualified specialists, renewal timing and site knowledge. Central scheduling and route density expand operating margin. The installed base lowers the cost of testing and distributing future wearables. Paid module use can create proprietary workflow and optional de-identified task data; if data permission fails, employer distribution and cash still compound.

Retained earnings are not counted again as personal cash. Conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax cash available for distribution while the founder remains employed; optional distributions are limited to 5% and excluded below. Strong success reinvests 100%.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

After tax and debt service, 95% of available cash is retained while employed. Priority is: working-capital buffer; seller-note coverage; backup specialist; scheduling/admin automation; second acquisition option; deposit-backed device fleet. No proprietary hardware R&D is funded until three independent customers have paid for the same instrumented workflow and twelve-month core-client retention exceeds 85%.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

- **Days 1–30:** fixed-scope acquisition, labor, data and BHP-regulatory counsel; build target scorecard and 150-owner list.
- **Days 31–60:** 150 letters, 30 owner conversations, five seller meetings and at least one preliminary data room.
- **Days 61–90:** verify bank/tax/invoice cohorts and replacement-owner economics; sign a maximum-10,000 PLN conditional exclusive option.
- **Days 91–130:** under the option and lawful customer-contact plan, secure 15 independent signed renewals/novations, first payments to NewCo at transition, and two qualified specialist agreements.
- **Days 131–160:** offer 20 retained employers an 8,500 PLN Instrumented Task-Rehearsal Day and require one 4,000 PLN deposit; no free custom pilot.
- **Days 161–180:** close only after paid customer evidence, with at most 70,000–80,000 PLN founder cash and seller-financed balance; deliver the paid module through the qualified specialist.

The founder remains employed. The seller/operator runs service; the founder can complete search/diligence outside corporate hours with professional counsel. Paid proof is customer cash, not a seller LOI, reviewer score or historic revenue alone.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

1. **Search and legal screen — 12,000 PLN max:** counsel, accountant, letters, travel. No option.
2. **Conditional option/diligence — cumulative 25,000 PLN max before paid proof:** up to 10,000 PLN creditable option plus diligence; stop if accounts or owner-replacement economics fail.
3. **Paid proof:** at least 15 independent customer payments/renewals and one 4,000 PLN instrumented-module deposit. Only then may total founder cash committed rise above 40,000 PLN.
4. **First close — founder capital hard cap 100,000 PLN:** 70,000–80,000 PLN closing cash plus prior costs; the rest must be a seller note/earnout serviced by acquired cash flow. No family funds.
5. **Second acquisition:** only after 12-month retained revenue above 85%, positive operating contribution, 2× seller-note debt-service coverage and a paid repeatable module signal.
6. **Family's possible 100,000 PLN:** remains excluded until multiple independent payers, repeat demand and a documented risk-adjusted scale case.

Maximum loss before paid evidence is 25,000 PLN. If the first acquisition later fails, total founder capital exposed is capped at 100,000 PLN; seller-note guarantees must be limited to the acquired company and not open-ended personal liabilities.

## Full-Time Transition Gate

The founder leaves corporate employment only after the acquired book has at least 50 independent active paying employers, six months of post-transition retained revenue above 85%, positive operating cash after seller-note service, a qualified operator and backup who do not depend on the founder, one repeatable paid instrumented-module signal, and at least 12 months of personal runway. A signed acquisition, asset valuation or single seller cannot satisfy the gate.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

- If no seller qualifies, loss stops at 25,000 PLN; the founder retains a target database, diligence model and market interviews.
- If instrumented modules fail but the acquired BHP book retains cash and customers, hold or resell the core business; do not force a technology story.
- If one technology vendor fails, the channel is manufacturer-agnostic and can switch devices.
- Acquired distribution, qualified specialists, employer relationships, cash flow, procurement history and lawful product-pilot capability remain useful future-lab assets.

## Critical Evidence

- CIOP estimates roughly 5,600 Polish BHP service providers.
- Polish obligations create recurring training/service events and allow qualified external specialists for small employers.
- Published vendor pricing supports monthly retainers and per-worker training budgets.
- European safety-services platforms have compounded through acquisitions.
- Direct ergonomics/wearable competitors prove technology availability and confirm it cannot be the initial moat.

## Critical Hypotheses

- At least one owner will sign a conditional option and finance at least 60% of consideration.
- A target with 50–100 clients, 70% recurring revenue and 15% normalized operating margin exists within reach.
- At least 70% of revenue can be renewed/novated without seller-person dependency.
- A 3× maximum normalized-profit price can be accepted; no market evidence proves it.
- The founder can acquire seven books over eight years without violating debt coverage or employment limits.
- At least one of 20 installed employers will prepay for an 8,500 PLN instrumented task-rehearsal module.

## Kill Criteria

- No qualifying seller option after 150 letters, 30 substantive owner conversations or 180 days.
- Seller demands more than 40% upfront, more than 3× verified normalized operating profit, or an open personal guarantee.
- Normalized operating margin below 15% after a market replacement salary.
- Any customer exceeds 10% of revenue, recurring/repeat revenue is below 70%, or fewer than 70% of revenue signs renewal/novation.
- No seller transition plus independent qualified backup before close.
- Fewer than 15 independent customers pay NewCo before/simultaneously with transition.
- No paid instrumented-module deposit after 20 installed-base offers; this kills the proprietary human-factors product, though the acquired cash business may be retained only if it independently meets wealth economics.
- Twelve-month retained revenue below 85%, seller-note coverage below 2×, or founder service time exceeds five hours/day; no second acquisition.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Pre-proof maximum: 25,000 PLN.
- Founder-capital hard cap through first close: 100,000 PLN.
- Founder: search, diligence, finance, analytics, vendor selection; maximum five hours/day.
- Seller/qualified BHP specialist: 18-month market-paid operating transition.
- Backup qualified specialist: conditional employment before closing.
- Fixed-scope acquisition/BHP/privacy counsel and accountant.
- No full-time ML or hardware engineer before three paid repeated module purchases.
