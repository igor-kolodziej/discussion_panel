# Zero-to-One Evaluation: HumanFactor Works

## Diagnosis

HumanFactor Works reverses the usual wearable-startup sequence: it proposes buying recurring employer access, qualified delivery capacity and cash flow before developing human-performance technology. That creates a materially stronger initial control point than a sensor, dashboard or speculative partnership, because an acquired and renewed client book is finite, contracted distribution that a technology incumbent cannot reproduce through one equipment purchase. The plan remains exposed to an unproved supply of succession-minded sellers willing to finance micro-deals, and its conservative wealth case needs disciplined repetition across roughly seven books. Those are explicit, cheaply testable acquisition hypotheses rather than hidden prerequisites, so both hard gates pass at the planning stage and the exact candidate may proceed to an independent simulated panel.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: CIOP-PIB reports a highly fragmented Polish BHP-services market; Polish employer obligations create recurring training and safety-service events; qualified external specialists can serve eligible smaller employers; published providers show recurring retainer and training budget categories; European safety-service groups demonstrate that acquired contract distribution can compound; and existing pose-estimation and wearable vendors show that the technology is available but is not itself scarce. Hypotheses: a suitable local seller exists, at least 60% deferred consideration is acceptable, 3.0× normalized operating profit is a feasible ceiling, 70% of revenue can be renewed or novated, owner labor is replaceable, the first book really produces 15%+ normalized operating margin, and one of twenty installed employers will place a 4,000 PLN deposit on the 8,500 PLN instrumented module. Prior reviewer scores and legacy filenames provide no evidence.

## Overall Score

**84/100.** Weighted points are 168.5 out of 200; `10 × (168.5 / 20) = 84.25`, rounded to 84. This is an advisory diagnosis only and is not a simulated or real validation score.

## Control Point

**Tier 1 target, conditional on closing.** A paid exclusive option can reserve one scarce micro-business, and the eventual purchase plus customer renewals directly controls contracts, brand, phone/domain, schedules, workflows and qualified transition capacity. At idea stage none is yet owned, but each is contractible and provable inside the proposed POC. The strongest copy risk is a better-capitalized buyer offering superior terms to other owners before HumanFactor Works builds regional density; it cannot copy an already purchased and retained book with a routine vendor contract.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, with a thin conservative cushion.** The conservative case states 700 clients, 10,300 PLN annual revenue per client, 7.21m PLN revenue, 55% gross margin, 22% operating margin, 400k PLN remaining debt, 90% founder ownership and 95% reinvestment, producing about 5.36m PLN founder equity at year eight after debt. The expected and strong cases state volume, pricing, revenue, margins, acquisition capital, debt, ownership, reinvestment and timing. Retained earnings are not counted again as distributions. The decisive weakness is replication: seven books must be acquired and integrated without customer loss, margin leakage or founder operational dependence. Each new deal is therefore conditional on 85% retained revenue, positive contribution and 2× seller-note coverage.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** The candidate seeks at least 15 independent paid customer renewals or novations and one 4,000 PLN module deposit within 160 days, while limiting pre-proof exposure to 25,000 PLN and retaining corporate employment. Search, diligence, an option, customer transfer, seller financing, a retained qualified operator and a backup specialist form a demanding dependency chain, but the conditional option lets the founder test them sequentially and refuse to close. More than 40,000 PLN is released only after customer cash evidence; total founder capital remains capped at 100,000 PLN and family money is excluded.

## Expansion Check

The first book is a defensible regional beachhead because the company buys account history and renewal timing rather than competing cold for every employer. Adjacent books add route density, staff coverage and cross-sell inventory. European groups validate the broad buy-and-build logic, but larger acquirers could move down-market if the thesis becomes visible; speed, confidentiality, seller-friendly terms and disciplined micro-deal integration are the local edge. Wearable and pose-estimation vendors can copy the instrumented module, but not the installed distribution to which it is sold.

## Fatal Objections

1. No evidence yet proves that a suitable owner will accept a 3.0× ceiling with at least 60% deferred or contingent consideration.
2. The conservative wealth outcome requires roughly seven successful acquisitions; one poor integration, customer attrition or specialist bottleneck can erase its narrow cushion.
3. The first target's modeled debt service may leave little free cash unless normalized profit, collection quality and seller-note terms are verified conservatively.
4. The instrumented module and lawful R&D data rights may fail completely, leaving a valuable safety-services platform but a slower route to proprietary human-augmentation IP.
5. The founder has no demonstrated acquisition or BHP operating record and must avoid becoming the integration or service bottleneck.

None is an immediate veto because the option, renewal conditions, specialist agreements, capital tranches and module deposit test each objection before irreversible expenditure.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Own trusted, recurring employer distribution and qualified field operations first; let paid installed-base use determine which human-performance technology deserves to be built.
