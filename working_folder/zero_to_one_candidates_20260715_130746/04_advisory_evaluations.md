# Advisory Evaluations

These are `$evaluate-zero-to-one` diagnoses. They are neither simulated-panel scores nor validation evidence.

# Zero-to-One Evaluation: BenchHire Direct

## Diagnosis

BenchHire fixes the most important failure in earlier dexterity-data ideas by becoming the paid recruitment intermediary rather than asking a staffing partner to buy a data collection tool. Its narrow role, deposit, low-capital physical test, explicit employer mandate and outcome warranty create a coherent paid-proof path. The weakness is that specialist recruitment plus a work sample remains operationally reproducible: the proposed data loop is useful only after many placements, and the first employer has little reason to believe an unknown agency's rubric is better. The candidate clears both hard gates on conservative assumptions, but its current control point and distribution are not strong enough for a high-confidence pursue recommendation.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: trade shortages and skills mismatch are reported by ELA and the European Commission; KRAZ gives an official low-cost entry route; active specialist agencies and vacancies show a real budget; practical-assessment vendors prove feasibility and direct copy risk. Hypotheses: the 2,500 PLN deposit, 8,500 PLN fee, 78% pilot gross contribution, candidate attendance, predictive signal, separate R&D consent, employer outcome access, reorder rate and valuation multiple. The 720-placement conservative endpoint is arithmetically sufficient but relies on successful expansion beyond the first role. No historic score or legacy filename is evidence.

## Overall Score

**78/100.** Weighted points are 156.5 out of 200; `10 × (156.5 / 20) = 78.25`, rounded to 78. The score remains below 80 because a specialist incumbent can reproduce the initial control point through a routine assessment-and-warranty process before BenchHire has enough linked outcomes to create switching cost.

## Control Point

**Tier 2.** A paid sole-agency mandate would provably route one requisition, and the company directly owns the placement fee and fixture. It is prospective, non-exclusive beyond one requisition, and not scarce. The strongest copy risk is an established electrician/panel-builder agency adding its own standardized audition and replacement warranty while using an existing employer and candidate network.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Pass, thin.** The conservative case reaches approximately 5.29m PLN of founder equity at year eight from 1.47m PLN operating profit, a 4× multiple and 90% ownership, with retained cash not counted again as a distribution. Expected and strong cases include dilution, margins, capital and timing. The decisive weakness is that 720 annual placements require successful replication across five cells; this is not heroic against the broad market denominator, but it is far beyond evidence in the initial niche and must be unlocked one reorder milestone at a time.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** The candidate asks for a 2,500 PLN non-refundable paid mandate by day 75, caps pre-proof loss at 18,000 PLN, defers the instrumented cell until payment, avoids temporary-worker payroll and can use contractors for daytime scheduling. The critical dependency count is acceptable: legal entry, one domain expert, one buyer and candidate attendance. The test fails honestly if employers only accept contingency recruiting.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is narrow enough to learn and potentially lead locally. Adjacent de-energized assembly cells share infrastructure and recruitment workflow. Large agencies can enter quickly; the local advantage is speed and focus, not exclusion. Expansion should receive no credit until two independent employers reorder and outcome access proves usable.

## Fatal Objections

1. The first employer can regard the test as unvalidated theatre and prefer an established specialist recruiter with a candidate network.
2. The initial control point is copyable through one internal process change; compounding data arrives only after the vulnerable launch period.
3. Upfront-deposit willingness to pay is unproved and could collapse the reachability thesis into ordinary contingency recruiting.
4. Outcome linkage and optional candidate data rights may be too incomplete to form the claimed proprietary dataset.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Own the paid placement transaction and underwrite a narrowly defined technical-skill outcome; collect standardized task and post-placement evidence as a byproduct rather than trying to sell data or assessment first.

## Recommendation

**pivot**

# Zero-to-One Evaluation: FallbackLine

## Diagnosis

FallbackLine has a concrete routed call window and a fast paid pilot, but the underlying offer is a vertical answering service assembled from purchasable voice-AI, telephony and contract-dispatch labor. The proposed handoff dataset may improve operations, yet neither caller access nor the fallback technique is scarce, and BPO, telephony and voice-agent vendors can bundle it. The wealth arithmetic depends on retaining 1,100 small accounts across languages while materially improving labor margins, without evidence for CAC or churn. Reachability passes, but durability and the Founder Wealth Gate do not.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: current research and vendors confirm human escalation is technically important; public contractor directories make outbound possible. Hypotheses: contractor willingness to pay, missed-call value, 1,550 PLN ARPA, 58% pilot gross margin, 70% six-month retention, sub-1,800 PLN CAC, dispatcher utilization, lawful trace rights and cross-language transfer. Competition evidence is stronger than evidence for a neglected Polish wedge.

|---|---:|---:|---|
| Core insight | 6 | 1 | Human rescue at the failure boundary is useful but increasingly obvious. |
| Customer pain and willingness to pay | 7 | 2 | Missed urgent calls have value; the exact bundle and price are unproved. |
| Business model and economics | 6 | 2 | Clear hybrid pricing, but dispatcher utilization and churn dominate economics. |
| Profitable domination potential | 5 | 1 | The Polish trade niche is accessible but easy for answering services to enter. |
| Beachhead quality | 7 | 1 | Narrow trade, language and after-hours window are specific. |
| Expansion path | 6 | 1 | More trades/languages expand TAM but also operations. |

| Local or regional advantage | 7 | 0.5 | Polish language and contractor knowledge help initially. |
| Strength of advantage | 5 | 1 | A 30-second rescue may improve conversion but can be matched. |
| Timing and underused momentum | 8 | 0.5 | Voice-agent adoption makes the problem timely. |

| Risk clarity and first-test quality | 8 | 0.5 | Paid three-month pilot and call-level metrics are clear. |

| Reachability and capital efficiency | 8 | 2 | One independent pilot under 35k PLN is plausible while employed. |

## Overall Score

**61/100.** Weighted points are 122 out of 200; `10 × (122 / 20) = 61`.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** A customer contract routes a defined call window, but every required input is commodity-accessible and the contract is easily displaced. The strongest copy risk is an existing answering-service or voice-AI vendor adding human escalation with its installed customer base.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** Although the model shows founder equity above 5m PLN, the conservative path requires 1,100 retained accounts, multilingual service delivery, 45% gross margin and 18% operating margin without an evidenced acquisition or retention engine. Revenue continues to require live coverage and QA labor. The scenario does not yet demonstrate a non-heroic profit-retaining scale-up.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass.** A three-month paid pilot can be sold and manually delivered below 35,000 PLN while the founder remains employed and two dispatch contractors cover calls. The same reachability makes the idea easy for competitors; it does not repair durability.

## Expansion Check

The first trade is narrow, but adjacent trades and languages add operational complexity rather than a proprietary expansion advantage. Telephony/BPO incumbents can move inward more easily than FallbackLine can build broad distribution.

## Fatal Objections

1. Commodity components and service labor produce a Tier-3 control point.
2. The conservative wealth path depends on 1,100 retained SMB accounts with unsupported CAC/churn.
3. Incumbents already possess customers, telephony and staffing.
4. The handoff dataset may remain customer-restricted and does not create early switching cost.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Tie human intervention to a measurable, high-value failure event and contractually route the event rather than selling generic AI automation.

## Recommendation

**kill**

# Zero-to-One Evaluation: GownTrace

## Diagnosis

GownTrace targets a recurring, important human-behavior workflow and avoids falsely claiming autonomous certification. However, formal site QA authority, cleanroom expertise and incumbent trust remain prerequisites, while the founder's initial paid mapping engagement is not the same as winning the annual requalification workflow. Established garment, training and qualification providers can add camera-assisted observation with one equipment and software purchase. The wealth path requires 65 regulated sites and regional expert delivery, making both wealth and control too dependent on external acceptance.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Evidence: EU GMP Annex 1 is live; Polish and international providers sell cleanroom training/qualification; listed course pricing proves a budget category. Hypotheses: buyer access, 7,500 PLN protocol-map WTP, 520 PLN per session, 62% gross margin, annual routing, motion-feature rights, human-review leverage and 65-site scale. No evidence shows small sites are underserved by current providers or will let an unknown startup into a controlled procedure.

|---|---:|---:|---|
| Core insight | 7 | 1 | Measurable gowning behavior is useful, but not a new mandatory event. |
| Customer pain and willingness to pay | 7 | 2 | Qualification matters and training budgets exist; startup-specific WTP is absent. |
| Business model and economics | 7 | 2 | Per-site recurring model is coherent; expert/travel and validation costs are uncertain. |
| Profitable domination potential | 5 | 1 | Small sites are narrow, but trust incumbents can bundle the service. |
| Beachhead quality | 7 | 1 | Clear workforce/site size and bounded practice setting. |

| Strength of advantage | 6 | 1 | Consistent evidence may help QA, but a camera is easy to add. |
| Timing and underused momentum | 8 | 0.5 | Annex 1 and cheap pose estimation support timing. |
| Repeatable distribution | 5 | 2 | Direct site outbound is possible; enterprise trust and procurement remain barriers. |
| Durability and profit retention | 6 | 1.5 | Annual programs can retain, but senior expert labor and incumbents compress profit. |
| Founder fit and minimum people stack | 6 | 0.5 | Technical fit is good; cleanroom credibility is external. |
| Risk clarity and first-test quality | 8 | 0.5 | Paid-map test and staged rig spend are clear. |
| Founder wealth creation potential | 6 | 2 | The endpoint works arithmetically but depends on 65 trusted sites and service leverage. |
| Reachability and capital efficiency | 6 | 2 | A paid map is possible under 35k, but access and conversion to the core workflow are weak. |

## Overall Score

**62/100.** Weighted points are 124.5 out of 200; `10 × (124.5 / 20) = 62.25`, rounded to 62.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** The annual routing contract is aspirational and depends on site QA acceptance. Equipment, pose software and domain experts are purchasable. The strongest copy risk is an incumbent cleanroom trainer or garment/service provider adding camera-assisted sequence review to an existing account.

## Founder Wealth Gate

**Fail.** The conservative case reaches 5.32m PLN only at 65 sites, 25% operating margin, a 5× multiple and 88% ownership. That scale requires repeated regulated-site trust and distributed expert delivery not established by the beachhead. The model is service-heavy and the endpoint is too sensitive to site count, margin and multiple.

## Reachability Gate

**Pass, weak.** A 7,500 PLN paid mapping engagement under 35,000 PLN is a legitimate paid-discovery test while employed. It does not prove that a site will route annual operator sessions or accept the system inside its quality workflow; those remain separate existential dependencies.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is legible, but local incumbents already have credentials and customer references. Each adjacency needs new protocol validation and domain trust, so expansion does not obviously get easier.

## Fatal Objections

1. Customer QA authority and expert credibility precede the core sale.
2. A paid mapping workshop is not proof of annual workflow control.
3. Incumbents can copy capture-assisted observation through routine procurement.
4. Sixty-five regulated sites and senior-review leverage are unsupported wealth assumptions.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Recurring, instrumented assessment of a consequential human procedure can create behavioral data when a customer routes every operator through the workflow.

## Recommendation

**kill**

# Final Advisory Ranking

1. **BenchHire Direct — 78, pivot.** Both hard gates pass, and direct ownership of the placement fee is worth preserving; control/distribution must become materially less copyable before any independent panel.
2. **GownTrace — 62, kill.** Reachable paid discovery, but wealth, authority and control fail.
3. **FallbackLine — 61, kill.** Easy paid pilot, but commodity service economics and control fail.
