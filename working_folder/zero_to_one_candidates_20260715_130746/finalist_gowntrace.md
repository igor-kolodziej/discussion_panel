# Zero-to-One Candidate: GownTrace

## One-Sentence Thesis

Operate the annual gowning-practice and requalification workflow for small Polish cleanroom sites using site-approved protocols, human QA review and privacy-preserving motion capture, so each paid operator session builds a proprietary library of contamination-risk behavior.

## Founder Fit

The founder can build pose/hand-path analytics and a privacy-preserving capture pipeline. Cleanroom procedure design and QA authority are not founder strengths, so a demonstrably experienced cleanroom trainer is a paid prerequisite rather than an unspecified partner. The founder remains responsible for selling and software; the customer's QA function retains formal approval.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is a small sterile-pharma, biotech, medical-device or high-grade technical cleanroom with 40–150 operators. QA needs personnel to follow site-specific gowning sequences consistently and retain defensible records. Current practice combines classroom material, demonstration, observation, microbiological qualification where applicable, spreadsheets and periodic retraining. It is expensive to coordinate and observation is inconsistent, but trusted training and full-service incumbents already exist.

## Product Wedge

Start with a 7,500 PLN paid protocol-mapping engagement. A hired cleanroom expert and the site QA lead encode the site's approved sequence, critical touch zones and human-review rubric. The first delivery is an instrumented practice day—not an autonomous compliance decision. Overhead/depth video is converted into body/hand paths; raw video is deleted on a contractual schedule. A qualified human reviews flagged steps, and the site alone signs formal qualification.

After mapping, offer an annual all-operator program at 520 PLN per session with a 40,000 PLN site minimum, including scheduling, supervised practice, human review, evidence export and quarterly drift analysis.

## Commercial Insight or Market Gap

Training vendors sell knowledge and cleanroom service firms sell garments, cleaning and qualification. The proposed gap is to own the recurring operator-practice workflow and make site-specific sequence adherence measurable without claiming that generic AI replaces QA or microbiology.

## Underused Momentum

EU GMP Annex 1 is live, cleanroom training budgets are visible, and inexpensive pose estimation can reduce raw-video retention. Human behavior remains a major contamination-control variable, while smaller sites may lack internal training infrastructure.

## Beachhead Market

Polish sites with 40–150 operators and an existing written gowning protocol, beginning with training/requalification practice outside live sterile production. Avoid sites requiring the startup to author the SOP, certify microbiological competency, or integrate with validated production systems during the POC.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

- 7,500 PLN paid protocol map, credited 25% against the first annual contract.
- 520 PLN per operator session, 40,000 PLN annual site minimum.
- Hypothesized direct cost at a 100-person site: 14,000 PLN trainer/reviewer labor, 6,000 travel/facility, 3,000 consumables/cloud and 2,000 support; 25,000 PLN total against about 52,000 PLN session revenue plus mapping.
- Hypothesized mature gross margin: 62%; operating margin: 22–28% after regional delivery pods.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

The first ten are sourced directly from Polish cleanroom operator/manufacturer directories, event exhibitor lists and current training buyers. The founder pitches only a paid protocol map and practice pilot. No association endorsement is assumed. Customers 11–100 require a documented outbound/referral channel and trained delivery partners hired under company procedures; they do not arrive through a speculative strategic partnership.

## Primary Control Point

An annual contract can route all named operator practice sessions through GownTrace and requires access to the site-approved protocol and agreed aggregate performance features. The company owns its capture rig, protocol compiler, feature schema and cross-site non-identifying benchmark where rights permit. The site keeps its SOP, qualification decision and identifiable records.

The strongest copy risk is that CWS, PSCT, a cleanroom consultancy or the site itself adds camera-assisted observation.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

- Hire one cleanroom professional with documented training/QA experience for a 5,000 PLN fixed-scope protocol and pilot review; do not imply endorsement.
- Obtain a 6,000 PLN quality/privacy/legal scope defining claims, raw-video deletion and customer authority.
- Build a 7,000 PLN portable overhead/depth capture rig and use disposable garments in a rented training space only after a paid mapping engagement.
- Directly pitch 25 sites; the required first controlled resource is a paid mapping order and written access to one site protocol, not a generic partnership.
- Before serious capital, produce a paid map, site-approved rubric, 10 supervised sessions, human/AI disagreement log, privacy audit and customer decision on annual purchase.

## Distribution Advantage

Direct outbound is triggered by existing cleanroom operation and workforce size. A site reorders annually if the service removes coordination work and produces usable evidence. Cross-site benchmarks may help sales only when contracts permit them; association access is upside, not a dependency.

## Competitive Advantage and Expansion Path

Dominate small Polish sites that are too small to build internal simulation infrastructure. Then add aseptic movement practice, material-transfer sequences and non-pharma technical cleanrooms. Expansion requires site-specific protocol adapters but reuses the motion ontology and review interface.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish-language delivery, EU GMP context, local travel economics and customer-owned protocol mapping distinguish the first offer. The company does not claim a regulatory shortcut. EU expansion occurs only after repeat Polish renewals and a local expert can be hired as a normal paid input.

## Compounding Advantage

Each authorized session can improve detection of sequence omissions, touch-risk patterns and reviewer disagreement. Annual routing creates longitudinal drift data. The advantage fails if sites prohibit aggregate feature retention or incumbents replicate the workflow before enough contracts accumulate.

## Reinvestment and Compounding Model

Cash funds reviewer training, portable rigs, privacy engineering and regional delivery pods. New geography opens only after three Polish renewals, positive site contribution and a reproducible reviewer calibration process.

## Reachability and 180-Day Paid-Proof Plan

Days 1–30: interview 15 QA/training buyers and five cleanroom professionals; recruit one fixed-scope expert. Days 31–60: sell the exact 7,500 PLN mapping engagement to 25 sites before building the rig. Days 61–120: after payment, map the approved protocol, build the bounded rig and run 10 supervised sessions. Days 121–180: deliver disagreement/privacy evidence and request an annual purchase or second paid map. The founder stays employed; the expert attends scheduled sessions.

## Capital Tranches and Maximum Loss

- Interviews, expert screening and legal scoping: 8,000 PLN.
- Pre-proof sales and disposable mock-up: cumulative 15,000 PLN maximum.
- After paid map: rig and delivery to cumulative 35,000 PLN.
- Above 40,000 PLN only after successful delivery and a second payer or annual order; full 100,000 PLN only after multiple customers, positive contribution and repeat demand. Family capital excluded.

## Critical Dependency Map

| Dependency | Test | Response |
|---|---|---|
| QA buyer pays before a validated product | Exact 7,500 PLN map offer to 25 sites | Kill if none pays |
| Expert can be hired as an ordinary input | Fixed-scope paid contract with documented experience | Stop if authority requires equity or speculative endorsement |
| Site permits capture and aggregate features | DPA, deletion schedule and feature-rights clause | Deliver manual training only once; do not call it a data moat |
| Instrumentation adds value beyond observation | Blind human review versus flagged events | Kill automation claim if disagreement is not useful |
| Annual routing and margin | Annual order and measured delivery hours | Do not expand on a one-off workshop |

## Full-Time Transition Gate

At least five independent annual customers, contracted recurring demand, positive contribution for three months, a calibrated substitute reviewer, repeatable acquisition and 12 months of personal runway.

## Failure Recovery and Reusable Assets

The founder retains privacy-preserving pose tooling, a human-review interface, protocol-authoring software and paid cleanroom relationships. These can support future HCI research but are not reasons to continue if sites reject annual routing.

## Critical Evidence

EU GMP Annex 1 is operational; Polish and international providers sell cleanroom training and qualification services; incumbents already own significant trust and distribution.

## Critical Hypotheses

Unknown-founder access, 7,500 PLN upfront willingness to pay, annual all-operator routing, feature reuse rights, 520 PLN session pricing, review labor and 65-site conservative scale are unproven.

## Kill Criteria

- No paid mapping engagement after 25 qualified site pitches or by day 90.
- No qualified expert available under 8,000 PLN for the first scoped engagement.
- Site will not approve the protocol/rubric or any privacy-preserving capture.
- No annual order or second independent paid map by day 180.
- Mature projected gross margin below 55%, or delivery remains proportional to senior-expert hours.
- Incumbent response can match the workflow through one routine camera/software purchase before three renewals.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

POC target 35,000 PLN; founder five hours/day; one paid cleanroom expert; fixed-scope quality/privacy counsel; one part-time engineer only after payment. No cleanroom lease or bespoke hardware before a paid map.
