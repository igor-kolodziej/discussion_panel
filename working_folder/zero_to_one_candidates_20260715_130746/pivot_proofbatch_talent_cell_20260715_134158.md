# Zero-to-One Pivot: ProofBatch Talent Cell

## Preserved Insight

Preserve the insight that the company must own a paid industrial transaction and underwrite a narrowly defined technical outcome, while standardized task and post-work evidence accumulates as a byproduct. Preserve the no-voltage cable/harness niche, founder-fit instrumentation, outcome linkage and staged capital discipline. Do not preserve the assumption that an employer will trust a simulated work sample from an unknown recruiter.

## Structural Change

Change the company from a specialist recruitment agency into a small-batch contract-production cell with a hire option. A manufacturer issues a paid purchase order for a low-complexity, non-safety-critical, customer-supplied-material cable or harness batch. Paid candidate-workers assemble the real batch under an experienced supervisor; the customer receives electrically tested accepted output and may hire a worker whose quality and throughput it has observed. The company earns manufacturing margin on every order and a conversion fee only when a worker is hired.

The paid work order replaces simulated-test credibility. The company controls custody of the batch, worker shifts, acceptance evidence and the conversion transaction. It does not depend on another staffing agency, a robotics-data buyer, or speculative employer recognition of a credential.

## Target Niche and Buyer

The first buyer is a 20–250 employee western-Polish machine builder, control-cabinet maker or cable-assembly producer with both low-volume overflow/NPI work and a live assembler vacancy. The first batch is 20–100 simple units using customer-provided drawings, parts, acceptance limits and a final continuity/test procedure. Exclude automotive, aerospace, medical, mains-voltage, safety-critical, serialized-regulatory and customer-designed-by-startup work during the POC.

## Painful Event and Willingness to Pay

The painful event is simultaneous backlog and understaffing: a production manager needs a small batch completed but established contract manufacturers may prioritize larger runs, while ordinary recruitment reveals capability only after hiring. The customer already budgets for purchased assembly output and recruitment. The first offer is a 5,000 PLN non-refundable supplier-qualification batch deposit, credited to a 12,000–20,000 PLN batch order; a later hire carries a 6,000 PLN conversion fee.

Willingness to pay remains a hypothesis. Existing European firms openly sell prototype and low-volume harness production, and Vernex is building a digital European harness-manufacturing network; this proves a real purchase category and strong competition, not demand for the combined overflow-to-hire wedge.

## Value and Profit Wedge

The customer buys accepted units on a stated deadline, not a score. The hire option is free until exercised. A representative 15,000 PLN pilot batch is hypothesized to carry 8,500 PLN direct material-independent labor, supervision, facility, test and rework cost, producing 6,500 PLN or 43% gross contribution because the customer supplies components. A hire adds a 6,000 PLN high-margin conversion fee.

At scale, the base business must work without data licensing. A conservative year-eight path uses 10m PLN annual production/conversion revenue, 38% gross margin and 15% operating margin, or 1.5m PLN operating profit. At a 4× multiple and 88% founder ownership, founder equity is 5.28m PLN. Expected and strong paths require 20m/45m PLN revenue, 18%/21% operating margin, 85%/75% founder ownership and 4.5×/5× multiples, producing approximately 13.77m/35.44m PLN founder equity. These are hypotheses; conservative and expected cases reinvest 95% of after-tax distributable cash while employed, strong success reinvests 100%, and no retained cash is counted again as a distribution.

## Domination Wedge

Own the category “overflow-to-hire” for simple wire and harness assembly within one driving radius: ten-day small batches, 100% logged electrical acceptance, named supervised workers and an optional evidence-backed hire. Conventional recruiters do not own production QA; conventional contract manufacturers do not monetize worker conversion or design their line as a talent proving ground. The first domination metric is repeat purchase orders from five manufacturers, not dataset size.

## Acquisition Channel

Use two public triggers together: a live assembler vacancy and evidence that the manufacturer offers custom/low-volume electrical assemblies. Pitch the production manager and owner directly with the exact paid qualification batch. Candidate-workers come from local job advertisements and referrals, but are hired and paid lawfully for production time; no candidate fee or unpaid audition is allowed. The family firm may supply dry-run workpieces but is not counted as an independent purchase order.

## Primary Control Point

The provable control point is a signed purchase order plus custody of customer-supplied components and a paid worker roster for the batch. A worker who opts into placement signs a time-bounded representation/conversion arrangement reviewed by counsel; coercive exclusivity is prohibited. The company owns its production fixtures, process logs, QC history and—only with separate lawful worker permission and customer contractual rights—de-identified hand/workpiece traces.

This is stronger than the original mandate because the customer must route a real output batch through the cell and pay regardless of whether it hires. It remains non-exclusive and copyable: the strongest risk is a low-volume contract manufacturer adding recruiting or a large staffing firm buying a workshop.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Before payment, spend at most 15,000 PLN: 6,000 PLN on manufacturing/labor/privacy counsel and insurance scoping, 4,000 PLN on an experienced harness supervisor's feasibility work, 3,000 PLN on a disposable demo fixture and 2,000 PLN on buyer travel/outbound. Ask 25 qualified manufacturers for the exact 5,000 PLN paid supplier-qualification deposit.

Only after a deposit, rent a workshop by the day; buy at most 12,000 PLN of hand tools, crimp tooling, continuity testing, cameras and fixtures; require the customer to supply all production components, acceptance criteria and final design responsibility; hire the supervisor and two workers for scheduled batch time. Before serious capital, produce one accepted paid batch, measured gross contribution, worker-level defect/rework logs, and either a hire conversion or a second independent purchase order.

## Expansion Path

Repeat the same simple harness family until five customers reorder. Then add a second supervised cell and adjacent low-voltage electromechanical kitting. A hub manager, not the founder, owns daily QA. Geographic expansion occurs only after a documented customer-supplied-material template and positive contribution. Long term, standardized workpiece/HCI traces can support robotics or augmentation R&D, but those buyers and revenues are excluded from the wealth model.

## Minimum People Stack

- Founder: sales, instrumentation, analytics and capital allocation, no daily line supervision after proof.
- One experienced harness-production supervisor hired per batch, preferably IPC/WHMA-A-620 trained.
- Two paid candidate-workers for the first batch.
- Fixed-scope Polish manufacturing, labor, privacy and insurance counsel.
- Bookkeeping and workshop rental as ordinary vendors.

No OEM partner, recruitment partner, proprietary robot, full-time engineer or permanent lease is required before paid proof.

## Principal Risk

The combination may solve two problems while being trusted for neither: an unknown workshop may fail supplier qualification, small-batch production may have poor margins, and manufacturers may not want to hire from a temporary production cell. IPC/WHMA-A-620 training and 100% continuity testing improve process discipline but do not grant customer trust or scarce rights. Existing low-volume manufacturers and Vernex prove that the production category is competitive.

## First Disconfirming Test

Within 60 days, pitch 25 manufacturers that meet both triggers and ask for a 5,000 PLN non-refundable deposit on a fully bounded customer-supplied-material qualification batch. Do not offer free production or count a letter of interest. Kill the pivot if none pays, if required pre-qualification spend exceeds 15,000 PLN before the deposit, or if all suitable buyers require certification/equipment outside the 100,000 PLN first-year cap.

## Evidence Preserved

- ELA and European Commission evidence of skilled-trade shortages and matching friction.
- Active panel-builder/electrician recruitment and low-volume harness manufacturing.
- Official KRAZ entry route if later conversion activity requires it.
- WHMA describes IPC/WHMA-A-620 operator training and validation; Polish authorized training exists.
- Existing prototype/low-volume manufacturers establish both demand category and incumbent threat.

## New Hypotheses

- A small manufacturer will pay an unknown cell for a customer-supplied-material qualification batch.
- A 43% pilot gross contribution and 38% mature gross margin are attainable despite supervision and rework.
- The same buyer suffers backlog and hiring pain often enough for the combined wedge to matter.
- Workers will accept paid short production engagements and optional placement terms under Polish labor law.
- Customers will permit process instrumentation and limited aggregate feature rights.
- Repeat orders can scale to 10m PLN revenue without working capital for components or founder-proportional supervision.

## Why This Is Not the Original Failure Shape

BenchHire asked an employer to trust an unknown recruiter's simulated score and paid only when placement occurred. ProofBatch sells accepted production output under a purchase order, uses real work as the audition, earns manufacturing revenue even without a hire, controls the batch and paid shifts, and makes the employer's hire option secondary. This materially changes the paid event, value capture, trust mechanism, controlled resource and business model.

## Pivot Priority

Return ProofBatch to `$evaluate-zero-to-one` first because it directly answers the evaluator's trust and copyability objections with a real paid work order and customer acceptance test. Its manufacturing trust and margin risks are severe enough that no simulated panel should see it until the evaluator judges the exact pivot and both hard gates again.
