## Why No Candidate Advanced

Round 3 deliberately moved from workflow gates to physical resource control. That improved concreteness but did not solve profitability and copy-risk enough to clear the simulated `>85` gate.

The strongest repeated objections:

- retainer willingness to pay was unproven for low-frequency incidents;
- storage, transport, cataloging, legal review, insurance, and failed matches could silently destroy margin;
- supply was lumpy or already closer to incumbents;
- physical inventory/capacity alone did not create repeatable demand ownership;
- incumbents with licenses, field crews, storage, or customer trust could copy after proof;
- founder fit became weaker as ideas drifted toward field logistics and relationship sales.

## Specific Learning From FaultBox

FaultBox was rejected despite the best current-regulatory momentum. The reviewer did not accept the assumed 300-900 PLN/month retainer or 45-60% blended margin without proof. The legal boundary is also fragile: the founder must not become an unlicensed waste custodian, and damaged/defective lithium batteries require specific ADR treatment such as Special Provision 376 / P908-style handling depending on case. A licensed waste collector or ADR carrier could bundle the same visible solution with stronger legitimacy.

## Pivot

The next viable candidate must start from a control point with:

- first cash before meaningful spend;
- repeated buyer urgency, not one-off emergency demand;
- externally acknowledged control or hard-to-copy resource rights;
- higher order value so CAC and compliance work do not crush payback;
- a path where proprietary data, routing, or rights compound beyond one physical inventory pile.

Avoid another broad inventory-bank, route-book, evidence-rail, or retainer-insurance shape unless it materially changes the ownership economics.
