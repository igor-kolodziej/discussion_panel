## Repeated Objections

The second round tested whether mandated micro-workflows, prepaid tokens, QR evidence rails, and compliance calendars could produce a strong enough control point. The answer was no. Even the better candidates had near-term cash conversion and real customer pain, but they did not pass the `>85` simulated gate because:

- the visible feature set can be copied by incumbents already adjacent to the workflow;
- the control point depends on a buyer mandate that is not yet naturally owned by the founder;
- too much field service, expert trust, or partner execution leaks margin;
- recurring revenue is plausible but not structurally forced;
- the best wedge is often an implementation/service layer, not a venture-scale control point.

## Pivot Implication

Do not continue with generic "evidence rail", "prepaid token", or "workflow mandate" shapes unless the founder can secure something more concrete than process preference. The next candidate set should require a harder proof asset within 12 months:

- assigned or purchased recurring cash-flow rights;
- named inventory or capacity under option;
- regulated rights that can be pre-contracted before the expensive work;
- a data/control surface that competitors cannot copy without the founder's early corpus or signed routing rights;
- a buyer-paid transaction flow where the founder collects before delivery and owns the repeat trigger.
