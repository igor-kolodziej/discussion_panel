# Zero-to-One Candidate: Wantlist Reissue

## One-Sentence Thesis

Use transparent collector scarcity and current listener activity to option one out-of-print Polish cult release, require 250 paid preorders before exercising a 500-copy exclusive repress right, and compound the resulting rights catalogue and collector customer graph into a specialist reissue label.

## Founder Fit

The founder can run data-led title screening, rights diligence, presale economics, written fan communication, and outsourced manufacturing without a public personal brand. Contract music counsel and a production coordinator handle chain of title and pressing. The founder owns the signed option, preorder cash flow, masters/artwork delivered under the contract, manufactured stock, label, and customer list.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first customer is a Polish collector/listener who wants an out-of-print local cult album but will not pay the used original's scarcity premium. The purchase trigger is a title with sustained Discogs wantlist/transaction scarcity, current streaming or community activity, no licensed repress, and an artist/rightsholder willing to authorize a high-quality edition.

The buyer currently overpays for a used original, waits indefinitely, buys an unofficial copy, or listens digitally without a physical edition. The rightsholder either ignores a small back-catalogue asset or avoids financing and operating a repress.

## Product Wedge

One licensed, numbered 500-copy vinyl edition with verified master/art chain of title, defined pressing quality, artist/rightsholder approval, and a preorder campaign that does not exercise the manufacturing obligation until 250 paid orders. Refund terms, delivery window, and minimum threshold are explicit. No speculative multi-title inventory and no unauthorized sample campaign.

## Hidden Insight or Market Misunderstanding

Small labels usually select titles by taste and then finance inventory. The non-consensus claim is that collector-market transaction data can identify a narrow price gap between scarce originals and a desired legitimate repress, while an exclusive option converts that information into a controllable asset. Fans, not the founder, finance the first manufacturing run; each successful title makes the next option easier to source and the next preorder cheaper to acquire.

## Underused Momentum

- Discogs exposes historical prices and wantlists.
- Polish vinyl revenue reached 72.1 million PLN in 2025 and grew 13.5% according to ZPAV.
- Small-run pressing is available from ordinary Polish/EU plants.
- Social, streaming, and marketplace activity make dormant-title demand more observable.
- Many artists, heirs, and boutique labels hold back catalogues but lack a low-risk repress operator.

## Beachhead Market

One coherent Polish genre/community with collectors, persistent used-market scarcity, and rights holders reachable without celebrity access—for example overlooked 1980s/1990s electronic, punk, jazz, metal, or regional independent catalogues. The POC chooses one genre after screening 100 titles and contacting 20 rights holders.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Hypotheses:

- Retail: 149–169 PLN including VAT.
- Rights option: 3,000–8,000 PLN; exercise converts to 10–15% net-sales royalty or a minimum guarantee credited against royalty.
- 500-copy pressing quote: roughly €1,779 before VAT/shipping for a classic package, subject to title-specific mastering/art costs.
- Total first-run cash need after threshold: 25,000–35,000 PLN.
- Contribution after VAT, pressing, royalty, packaging, fulfilment, and payment fees: 50–65 PLN/unit.
- Break-even: roughly 280–400 units depending on rights and fulfilment.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First 10:** screen 100 releases with Discogs sales/wantlists, streaming activity, current rights status, and comparable repress performance; interview 20 collectors without revealing an unsecured title; sign one option; publish the authorized campaign; earn ten paid preorders from title-specific search, Discogs-community outreach consistent with platform rules, artist channels controlled under the option, and genre forums.

**First 100:** reach collectors already watching the exact title, use authorized artist/rightsholder announcements, partner with five specialist stores on prepaid allocations, and retarget only consented visitors. Do not spend on broad ads before 100 orders.

After the first sold-out title, use the buyer list and fulfilment proof to option adjacent catalogue titles and offer bundles/subscriptions within the same genre.

## Primary Control Point

An executed exclusive 12-month/500-copy repress option covering master, artwork, mechanical, manufacturing, D2C, wholesale, territory, format, preorder authorization, delivery of source assets, warranties, royalty, approval deadlines, and exercise mechanics. After exercise, control includes finished inventory, customer preorders, the label, and the collector list. Rights must be verified, not inferred from artist participation.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Rights and title research: 3,000 PLN.
2. Music counsel and chain-of-title diligence: 5,000–8,000 PLN.
3. Option fee: 3,000–8,000 PLN.
4. Authorized sample/mastering/art mock-up and campaign: 3,000–6,000 PLN.
5. Pressing, packaging, and freight: 15,000–22,000 PLN, released only after 250 paid orders.
6. Fulfilment reserve and refunds: 6,000–10,000 PLN.

Total first-run cap: 50,000 PLN. An unknown founder can secure the right through ordinary contracts and can prove demand with money before exercise. Externally checkable proof is the option, chain-of-title memo, plant quote, payment dashboard, 250 preorders, delivered stock, and sell-through/margin report.

## Distribution Advantage

Demand is addressable title by title: marketplace wantlists, historical transactions, search, streaming activity, specialist shops, and authorized rightsholder channels identify likely buyers. A signed option allows a legitimate campaign; no influencer or broad cultural status is required. Each fulfilled title adds consented buyers whose tastes are more predictive than generic vinyl audiences.

## Domination and Expansion Path

1. Own one neglected Polish micro-genre's credible reissue standard.
2. Option adjacent releases using fulfilled-demand evidence and repeat buyers.
3. Build a catalogue, bundled drops, store allocations, and cross-title preorders.
4. Expand into licensed digital, CD/cassette, liner-note archives, or neighboring CEE catalogues only where rights and buyer overlap are strong.
5. Use catalogue cash flow to acquire longer-duration rights to the best-performing titles.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

The edge is local rights discovery, Polish-language chain of title, culturally precise title selection, and domestic fulfilment. A foreign label can enter, but it starts without the rights-holder map, collector graph, title economics, and fulfilled reputation in the chosen micro-genre. If the title could be equally launched by any global label from the same public data, the option is not sufficient and the candidate should die.

## Compounding Advantage

- Exclusive options temporarily block identical represses.
- Each release creates a reusable collector cohort and store allocation network.
- Fulfilment proof improves access to adjacent rights.
- Shared mastering, art, packaging, and logistics reduce marginal cost.
- A catalogue supports bundles and longer-duration rights acquisition.

## Critical Evidence

- Discogs provides scarcity/transaction inputs.
- ZPAV documents a growing Polish vinyl revenue category.
- A current 500-copy manufacturing quote fits the POC budget.
- Rights and preorder cash can be secured before manufacturing.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Wantlist and used-price scarcity translate into willingness to buy a repress.
- A reachable rights holder has clean master/art rights and accepts an option/preorder structure.
- The campaign can reach 250 buyers without privileged artist reach.
- Contribution remains at least 50 PLN after all costs and returns.
- Repeat buyers overlap enough across adjacent titles to lower later CAC.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than three clean optionable titles remain after 100-title screening and 20 rights-holder contacts.
- No rightsholder accepts a 12-month exclusive option below 8,000 PLN upfront with preorder permission.
- Fewer than 100 paid preorders arrive in 30 days or 250 in 60 days despite title-specific outreach; refund and do not exercise.
- Chain of title, master quality, artwork rights, mechanical rights, or approval deadlines remain unclear.
- Fully loaded contribution falls below 45 PLN/unit or break-even exceeds 400 copies.
- The first fulfilled title produces fewer than 25% opt-in buyers interested in the next three shortlisted titles.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Month 0–2: screen, rights outreach, three option candidates; 6,000 PLN cap.
- Month 2–4: diligence, option, authorized campaign assets; cumulative 20,000 PLN.
- Month 4–6: preorder gate; no pressing before 250 paid orders.
- Month 6–9: manufacture and fulfil; cumulative 50,000 PLN.
- Month 9–12: second option based on repeat-buyer data; total risk cap remains below 75,000 PLN.

Minimum stack: founder, music/IP counsel, part-time production coordinator, pressing plant, and fulfilment vendor paid as suppliers.
