# Zero-to-One Evaluation: LyoShield After Decision-Critical Research

## Diagnosis

Follow-up evidence resolves LyoShield's main uncertainty against the candidate. BSA-containing ATCC Reagent 18 is a valid comparison recipe, but commercial probiotic research already uses many inexpensive animal-free or milk-based protectants, and the best result depends on strain and storage conditions. The licensed composition therefore does not remove qualification work and does not address a proven recurring BSA budget in the target beachhead. The candidate would become technical formulation sales around a narrow, design-around-prone patent.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Primary literature documents high survival using skim milk, trehalose, sucrose, lactose, maltodextrin, and other combinations. UG's right and laboratory result remain real. Actual exclusive licence terms and a paid customer request remain unknown, but the negative competitive/product evidence is sufficient: the original pain mechanism is not common enough to justify further pursuit.

## Overall Score

**46/100.** Weighted sum `74.0 / 16`; `10 × 4.625 = 46.25`, rounded to 46.

## Control Point

**Tier 2 after licensing, but economically weak.** Strongest copy risk: a buyer uses skim milk, trehalose, sucrose, maltodextrin, or another non-infringing formulation and keeps its strain/process knowledge internal.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead fragments by organism, cycle, and storage condition. Adjacent markets add validation rather than amortize it. The Polish licence does not prevent global ingredient or culture companies from serving the same outcome through alternative formulations.

## Fatal Objections

1. The claimed BSA pain is not evidenced as the dominant commercial workaround.
2. Many low-cost alternative protectants already work, with results depending on strain and storage.
3. Low-volume ingredient sales cannot reliably fund customer-specific qualification.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Objective strain-specific qualification can help sell a microbial-process input, but this patent/formulation is not the right vehicle.
