# Zero-to-One Evaluation: Textile-Bale Yield Underwriting

## Diagnosis

Textile-Bale Yield Underwriting reframes a technical measurement into a purchase-price decision and could compound an origin-to-realized-yield graph. However, representative sampling, process-specific transfer, outcome-data access, buyer record quality, and fee tolerance are all unresolved. The hardware is purchasable by incumbents; only private realized outcomes can make the business durable, and those depend on customers assigning data.

## Evidence and Assumptions

EU policy and EEA evidence support rising textile-waste supply/quality pressure, while NIR/RGB competition proves composition measurement is accessible. Every key economic claim—eight-point prediction error, 1,500 PLN decision fee, 50,000 PLN site licence, avoided-loss magnitude, data assignment, and repeat transaction routing—is a hypothesis.

## Overall Score

**68/100.** Weighted sum `109.5 / 16`; `10 × 6.84375 = 68.4375`, rounded to 68.

## Control Point

**Tier 3 today, potentially Tier 2 after contracts.** Retained samples can be owned, but process-outcome rights and transaction access are speculative. Strongest copy risk: a recycler or NIR/sorting vendor links its own composition data to internal yield and removes the need for an external layer.

## Expansion Check

The one-process beachhead is logical and expansion into grades/transactions is attractive. Competitors already own equipment and operational data; Poland improves access but not exclusivity.

## Fatal Objections

1. Pre-purchase samples may not represent the bale.
2. Customers may refuse or fail to record assignable realized-output data.
3. Field work and bespoke process calibration may prevent software-like margins.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

The commercially valuable variable is process-specific recoverable yield and maximum bid, not fibre identity.
