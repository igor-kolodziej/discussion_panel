# Zero-to-One Candidate: SafeCassette Legacy-Shelter Ventilation Standard

## One-Sentence Thesis

Poland's shelter-modernization wave can support a standardized, serialised filter-ventilation adapter family that converts recurring legacy geometries into fast retrofit installations and a captive replacement-cartridge base.

## Founder Fit

The founder can use data/vision methods to build the geometry and compatibility library, while the family renewable-installation company supplies bounded field surveying, electrical/ventilation installation experience, a workshop, and a credible local reference. Public tenders and funding notices make prospecting list-based. Mechanical and safety engineering are purchased from contractors rather than improvised by the founder.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The first buyer is a municipality, school, housing cooperative, or facility owner whose technical assessment has found an obsolete, incomplete, or non-operational shelter filter-ventilation system. The immediate problem is not shelter awareness. It is turning a legacy opening, duct, power arrangement, and room layout into an installable, documentable system within a funding and procurement window.

Current workarounds are site-specific refurbishment, full replacement by established shelter suppliers, or continued non-operation. Custom work creates slow quoting, repeated engineering, and uncertain replacement-filter continuity.

## Product Wedge

SafeCassette begins with one or two recurring legacy geometries. The product combines:

- A measured adapter/interface kit that avoids rebuilding the entire duct path.
- A specified compatible ventilation/filter unit sourced from an established manufacturer or an independently engineered cartridge only where lawful.
- Serial number, geometry record, installation configuration, filter type, replacement date, and test record.
- A multi-year cartridge/reinspection schedule.
- Fixed-scope installation documentation for the facility's engineer and procurement file.

The POC does not claim to certify a shelter, replace the responsible designer, or manufacture CBRN media without required testing.

## Hidden Insight or Market Misunderstanding

The obvious market is “build or renovate shelters.” The narrower compounding asset may be the installed interface standard. If recurring old ventilation geometries can be normalized into a few adapter families, each installation lowers future survey/engineering cost and locks in a known replacement path. The valuable database is geometry-to-tested-interface compatibility, not another assessment report.

## Underused Momentum

- Poland has enacted a new civil-protection framework and operating/recognition rules.
- The national 2025–2026 program allocates almost 34 billion PLN, with 2026 regional calls financing inventory, modernization, building work, and equipment.
- Municipal technical-assessment procurements are already visible.
- Existing Polish manufacturers prove filter-ventilation supply and modernization demand, but also create a high competitive bar.

## Beachhead Market

Existing small and mid-sized protective structures in Wielkopolskie and Mazowieckie whose assessments identify obsolete RM-type or comparable filter-ventilation layouts and whose owner has a live modernization budget.

The initial geography fits the founder's Warsaw/Ostrów presence and the family company's field radius. One geometry family and one compatible equipment path are sufficient for the POC.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Hypotheses:

- Paid dimensional/compatibility survey: 2,500–6,000 PLN, credited against installation.
- Adapter plus equipment/installation: 18,000–45,000 PLN for a small facility, highly configuration dependent.
- Target blended gross margin: 35–50% after engineering and field labour.
- Replacement cartridge/reinspection: 2,000–8,000 PLN per cycle.
- Framework orders or design reuse should reduce site engineering cost by at least 40% after the first three matching geometries.

The POC requires one paid prototype and two conditional follow-on orders before committing to tooling or stock.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First 10:**

1. Extract 50 facilities from public assessment, modernization, and funding notices.
2. Contact owners and the engineers already assessing them with a fixed paid survey offer for obsolete ventilation only.
3. Complete five paid surveys and test whether geometries repeat.
4. Require one fixed-price prototype order and two conditional orders for the same adapter family.
5. Install only under the required responsible-engineer and manufacturer conditions.

**First 100:**

- List compatible geometry families in a controlled technical catalogue.
- Bid directly or through named installation subcontractors on modernization lots.
- Use serialised installed units and scheduled cartridges for recurring revenue.
- License or supply the adapter/interface to regional shelter contractors where direct installation is uneconomic.

## Primary Control Point

The intended control point is the owned compatibility library, adapter/interface design and tooling, serialised installed base, and contractually scheduled replacement-cartridge flow. Pilot contracts must grant the company rights to retain anonymized geometry/configuration/test data and require compatible replacement parts for a defined term, subject to safety and procurement law.

The control point is not an unspecified supplier partnership. Existing filters/units are bought under ordinary commercial terms; mechanical design, tests, and field work are paid services. The business dies if it cannot own the adapter/interface or if procurements require fully open interchangeable components with no installed-base advantage.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. 10,000 PLN maximum for standards/legal review and five paid or prospect-funded geometry surveys.
2. 15,000–25,000 PLN for mechanical design, digital measurement, prototypes, and engineering review.
3. 20,000–35,000 PLN for one buyer-backed unit, adapter tooling, tests, and installation.
4. 10,000–15,000 PLN for insurance, documentation, and contingency.

Why an unknown founder can secure it: facilities and tenders are public; the offer begins with paid measurement; the family company provides local field capacity; established components can be purchased. Proof before serious capital is repeated geometry across at least three sites, one tested paid installation, two conditional follow-on orders, owned design documentation, and a signed replacement schedule. Total POC cap: 90,000 PLN.

## Distribution Advantage

Public assessments and funding decisions identify buyers at the trigger moment. Geometry matches make quoting faster than bespoke contractors. Each installed serial number creates a future cartridge/reinspection event. The family company provides the first reference but is not the long-term channel moat.

## Domination and Expansion Path

Start with one legacy geometry in two regions; expand into adjacent geometry families, then supply adapters and data to regional shelter contractors. A larger installed base supports replacement logistics, remote condition records, and standardized modernization budgets. Expansion outside Poland occurs only where legacy equipment families and local rules match.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

This is inherently Polish first: law, funding, public-owner workflows, old installed equipment, language, and local field installation matter. Foreign suppliers can sell filters, but they do not automatically own the Polish legacy-geometry map or local installed replacement base.

## Compounding Advantage

- Every site improves the geometry/compatibility library.
- Reused adapters reduce engineering time.
- Serialised units create recurring cartridge and inspection events.
- Framework orders and installed references reduce buyer trust friction.
- A contractor-supply channel expands faster than a founder-led installation service.

## Critical Evidence

- Official Polish law and funding create real modernization activity.
- Recognition and operating rules explicitly involve filter ventilation and replacement.
- Public technical-assessment tenders are live.
- Established manufacturers and renovation specialists prove supply and demand, while showing that the market is not empty.

## Critical Hypotheses

- Legacy geometries repeat enough to standardize.
- Owners will pay for an adapter path instead of a full incumbent replacement.
- Testing and responsible-engineer requirements fit under 100,000 PLN for the POC.
- Procurement terms permit a proprietary adapter and recurring cartridge relationship.
- The family company can lawfully perform or subcontract the necessary field work.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than three materially matching geometries after ten qualified surveys.
- Established vendors already offer equivalent adapters for the target geometry at competitive prices.
- Safety/testing/design obligations exceed the POC budget or require credentials unavailable through paid contractors.
- No paid prototype and two conditional follow-on orders after 50 qualified facilities.
- Procurement rules eliminate installed-base or replacement-flow control.
- Gross margin below 35% or site engineering fails to fall after repeated geometries.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Months 0–3: standards review, facility list, surveys; 15,000 PLN.
- Months 3–6: repeat-geometry design and buyer-backed prototype; cumulative 45,000 PLN.
- Months 6–9: tested paid installation; cumulative 75,000 PLN.
- Months 9–12: two follow-on orders and cartridge schedule; 90,000 PLN total cap.

Minimum stack: founder/product/data lead, family-company field technician, contracted mechanical/ventilation engineer, and required safety/testing support.
