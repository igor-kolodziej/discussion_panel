# Zero-to-One Candidate: ShiftRoster App Buyout

## One-Sentence Thesis

Acquire a small shift-calendar app with transferable code, store listings, reviews, subscribers, and billing, then dominate Polish hospital rota import and fatigue-aware shift planning using an inherited payment flow rather than cold-starting another productivity app.

## Founder Fit

The founder can perform payout/cohort diligence, code/product analysis, rota parsing, analytics, and asynchronous user research. A mobile contractor handles bounded maintenance and a Polish employment/payroll specialist reviews overtime outputs. The acquisition provides distribution and renewals on day one; the founder does not need personal-brand marketing or enterprise hospital procurement.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The initial customer is an individual nurse, resident physician, paramedic, care worker, or other rotating-shift worker. The paid trigger is receiving a monthly rota as a PDF, image, spreadsheet, or hospital-system export and having to recreate shifts in a personal calendar, calculate nights/weekends/overtime, coordinate family life, and notice unsafe sequences.

Today users type shifts manually into generic calendars or use broad shift apps that assume a repeating pattern. Hospital rotas are messy, abbreviations are local, and schedules change. The user wants personal control and correct calendar/pay estimates, not a hospital workforce-management replacement.

## Product Wedge

The acquisition target must already have working shift patterns, reminders, calendar export, paid subscriptions, crash-free releases, and transfer eligibility. The first post-close wedge adds:

- on-device or privacy-preserving import of Polish rota PDFs/images;
- row/name recognition with explicit user confirmation;
- Polish shift abbreviations and department templates;
- night/weekend/holiday/overtime calculations clearly labelled as estimates;
- fatigue flags for short turnarounds and long sequences, without medical claims;
- one-tap corrected export to Apple/Google calendars.

## Hidden Insight or Market Misunderstanding

The obvious business is to build a better shift app and buy downloads. The hidden supply-side insight is that a tiny app's durable asset is its transferable listing, reviews, subscribers, and billing history; code alone is not the control point. Buying that asset below replacement CAC lets the founder specialize for a neglected Polish hospital workflow while renewals finance the change.

## Underused Momentum

- Apple and Google document app-transfer paths that preserve important listing/user history when eligibility rules are met.
- Small software products trade at prices that can fit the founder's capital limit.
- OCR/vision-language models make messy rota import technically more feasible, while confirmation can contain errors.
- Shift work remains widespread and personal schedules sit outside employer workforce systems.
- KSeF and other enterprise changes do not solve individual rota/calendar friction.

## Beachhead Market

Polish nurses and resident physicians who receive non-repeating monthly rotas as PDFs/images and already use iOS or Android personal calendars. The app sells directly to individuals; it does not require hospital integration or employer approval.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Acquisition and product numbers are hypotheses until a seller data room and live store experiment exist.

- Purchase target: 45,000–65,000 PLN for 20,000–30,000 PLN verified annual owner earnings.
- Hold back 15–20% until both app stores, subscriptions, domains, analytics, and repositories transfer.
- Subscription: 59–99 PLN/year or 9–15 PLN/month; preserve existing pricing during initial migration.
- Target store/payment-adjusted gross margin: above 75% before support and development.
- Post-close contractor/product budget: 15,000–25,000 PLN.
- Static acquisition payback target: no more than 36 months on verified trailing earnings; no growth assumed in the purchase case.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

The first ten post-close purchases or renewals come from the inherited subscription base. During the first 30 days, invite 20 Polish users from nursing/resident communities and direct professional-forum outreach to a privacy-safe paid beta; no hospital endorsement or influencer is required.

The first 100 Polish paid users come from store search localization, a free three-import trial, referral to a colleague on the same rota, profession-specific landing pages, and direct outreach to public professional communities. Expansion to paramedics, care homes, police, security, and factory shifts occurs only after the PDF-import wedge improves conversion/retention for hospital users.

## Primary Control Point

At closing, the company must own the complete source and IP, both store listings, ratings/reviews, active users, subscription entitlements, billing/payout history, app name/domain/trademark rights, analytics, support mailbox, signing assets that platforms permit to transfer, and contributor assignments. A 24-month payout/cohort history and store-transfer confirmation are conditions precedent, not seller promises.

After close, the compounding asset is the inherited distribution/payment flow plus a growing proprietary library of rota layouts, user corrections, Polish shift vocabulary, and department templates collected with explicit user rights and privacy controls.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

1. Screen 100 listings/broker leads and contact 30 owners: 2,000 PLN.
2. Legal, code, privacy, platform, payout, and cohort diligence: 8,000–12,000 PLN.
3. Purchase price: maximum 65,000 PLN, with 15–20% holdback and a warranty escrow structure.
4. Critical maintenance and Polish import beta: 15,000–20,000 PLN.
5. Support, localization, insurance, and contingency: 5,000 PLN.

Maximum POC cash: 100,000 PLN. Why an unknown founder can secure it: this is an ordinary asset transaction; the seller is paid for verifiable cash flow and a clean transfer, not the buyer's status. Proof before serious follow-on spend is reconciled bank/store payouts, signed IP assignment, completed store transfers, uninterrupted renewals, a 50-document rota accuracy set, and at least 100 Polish paid users or a measured retention lift.

## Distribution Advantage

The acquired listing and renewals solve the zero-user problem. Polish rota import creates high-intent store-search language and a same-rota colleague referral loop. Each confirmed layout improves the next import. The product remains consumer-distributed and avoids slow hospital procurement.

## Domination and Expansion Path

1. Acquire cash flow rather than launch from zero.
2. Own Polish hospital rota-to-personal-calendar import.
3. Add department templates, shift-pay estimates, and colleague-safe coordination.
4. Localize the rota parser for CEE hospitals and care providers.
5. Offer employer exports or benefits distribution only after consumer density proves value, without becoming a workforce-management system.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

Polish rota abbreviations, public holidays, pay conventions, privacy expectations, and hospital document layouts create a local wedge. A broad foreign shift app can copy features, so the defence requires faster template accumulation, inherited distribution, user correction data with consent, and department referral density. Locality alone is insufficient.

## Compounding Advantage

- Store history and subscriptions survive the acquisition if eligibility is verified.
- Confirmed rota layouts and corrections improve import quality.
- Colleagues share document structures, reducing acquisition cost within departments.
- Subscription cohorts fund ongoing maintenance.
- CEE languages and professions reuse the parsing and schedule engine.

## Critical Evidence

- Apple and Google support eligible app transfers and preserve important listing/user history.
- Small software assets can be acquired through ordinary marketplaces or direct owner outreach.
- The founder can verify payments, cohorts, code, and platform status before closing.

## Critical Hypotheses

- A qualifying profitable app is actually available below 65,000 PLN with clean IP and transferable subscriptions.
- Existing users do not churn after ownership and account migration.
- Rota import is frequent/painful enough to improve paid conversion or retention.
- Privacy-safe parsing reaches at least 95% field accuracy with confirmation on the target layouts.
- Generic shift apps cannot erase the wedge before local templates/referrals compound.

## Kill Criteria

- No qualifying asset after 100 screened leads and 30 owner contacts.
- Seller will not provide 24 months of platform payouts, bank reconciliation, cohort exports, repository history, contributor releases, privacy records, or store-transfer confirmation.
- Price exceeds 36 months of verified owner earnings before growth assumptions.
- More than 10% of subscriptions or reviews cannot transfer, or renewals fall over 15% in the first 90 days post-close beyond seasonal baseline.
- Fewer than 10 of 20 Polish beta users complete three imports or fewer than five pay after the trial.
- Confirmed import accuracy remains below 95% on 50 representative documents.
- Six-month Polish paid conversion/retention does not improve enough to recover the localization spend within 18 months.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

- Month 0–2: deal sourcing and initial diligence; 5,000 PLN cap.
- Month 2–4: full diligence, signing, platform pre-clearance; cumulative 15,000 PLN excluding price.
- Month 4–5: close and complete transfers; cumulative maximum 80,000 PLN.
- Month 5–8: stability, Polish beta, 50-document test set; cumulative 95,000 PLN.
- Month 8–12: 100 Polish paid users or retention proof; 100,000 PLN total cap.

Minimum stack: founder/operator, transaction/privacy counsel, independent mobile code reviewer, and part-time mobile contractor.
