# Zero-to-One Evaluation: RealLine Packaging Changeover Rail

## Diagnosis

RealLine materially fixes SealFault's artificial-defect problem by moving to actual production changeovers and a different operations buyer. It does not fix the deeper control and economics problem: the proposed all-event routing, production access, retained samples, and derived-data rights are speculative, while film suppliers, machine OEMs, and plant engineers already influence the same event with greater authority. The first version remains field-heavy troubleshooting, and the claimed recurring product/data advantage appears only after several difficult customer permissions.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Preserved evidence supports seal/process risk, material-transition pressure, and availability of measurement equipment. The panel verdicts are judgment, not evidence. Changeover loss of 15,000–50,000 PLN, 30% improvement, stated prices, six-month routing, reusable cross-plant parameters, 55% margin, and customer data/sample rights are all unsupported hypotheses. No public source or signed customer proves that small converters buy an external changeover rail instead of using film suppliers, machine OEMs, or internal process engineers.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** The all-changeover mandate, production access, sample custody, and data rights are all hypothetical and depend on a plant granting an unknown founder access to live operations. Strongest copy risk: the existing film supplier or packaging-machine OEM adds the same structured startup protocol through one routine service/process change while using deeper line knowledge and an existing commercial relationship.

## Expansion Check

The line/material beachhead is narrower than SealFault and real-event data improves technical validity. Expansion remains customer-specific because line configuration, film, sealant, product, and release authority vary. Local execution lowers travel and labour cost but does not create asymmetric access.

## Fatal Objections

1. The decisive all-event routing and data/sample rights are speculative customer permissions.
2. Film suppliers, machine OEMs, and plant engineers already own trust and process access at the painful event.
3. Early delivery is on-site troubleshooting; repeatable margins and retention are not credible before several contracts.
4. A five-hour/day unknown founder lacks authority to enter live production and change established release workflows without a stronger channel or owned resource.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Actual line outcomes and retained physical samples are more valuable than manufactured benchmark defects, but they do not create a founder-controllable business here.
