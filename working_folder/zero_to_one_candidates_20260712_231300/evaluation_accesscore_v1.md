# Zero-to-One Evaluation: AccessCore Design-In Module for Small Terminal OEMs

## Diagnosis

AccessCore addresses a real regulatory/product need but enters a market already served by dedicated tactile hardware, screen-reader software, integration services, and an EAA-specific pad. The local-price/localization thesis does not create a hard control point, and an OEM can buy established components through one vendor relationship. Without a proprietary standard or locked distribution channel, this is a lower-credibility hardware copy rather than a zero-to-one wedge.

## Evidence and Assumptions

The EAA, Polish law, and relevant incumbent products are sourced. The existence of underserved small CEE OEMs, three paid design-ins, integration savings, proposed pricing, and production margins are hypotheses.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** The module is commodity-purchasable/reproducible and design-in contracts are only prospective. Strongest copy risk: ANKER, Storm, Vispero, or an OEM bundles the same hardware/software capability through one routine vendor decision.

## Expansion Check

The beachhead is understandable, but foreign incumbents already serve the exact product class and can localize. Poland/CEE does not create a sufficient regulatory or distribution barrier.

## Fatal Objections

1. Direct product substitutes already exist.
2. The control point is a design-in hope, not a scarce asset.
3. Local pricing/support does not create durable profit retention.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Small OEMs experience hardware/software accessibility as one integration problem rather than separate components.
