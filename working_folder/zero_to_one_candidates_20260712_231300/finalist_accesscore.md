# Zero-to-One Candidate: AccessCore Design-In Module for Small Terminal OEMs

## One-Sentence Thesis

Small CEE self-service-terminal manufacturers will pay integration fees and per-unit royalties for a localized hardware/firmware accessibility subsystem that lets low-volume product families support tactile navigation, private audio, offline speech, and repeatable conformity testing without building the stack internally.

## Founder Fit

This is a technical product with list-based B2B distribution, measurable integration milestones, and no influencer requirement. The founder can own product/data/software design and use contractors for electronics, enclosure, accessibility testing, and manufacturing. AI can accelerate firmware, test automation, localization, and documentation, but it is not the product's sole value.

## Customer, Pain, and Current Workaround

The initial buyer is a Polish or CEE kiosk, ticketing, check-in, payment, or information-terminal OEM launching a new low-volume model for a consumer-facing service. It must integrate physical and digital accessibility across hardware, audio privacy, focus/navigation, speech, and application flows.

Current options include internal redesign, global tactile hardware, JAWS/custom scripting, consulting, or avoiding covered use cases. Established products are credible but may be expensive, fragmented, or oversized for a small manufacturer. The unresolved question is whether a local integrated module materially improves cost and release speed.

## Product Wedge

One reference subsystem:

- Tactile directional/function input with high-contrast and configurable legends.
- Private audio output and offline Polish/English speech prompts.
- Firmware/API exposing focus, selection, error, timeout, and help states.
- Reference enclosure/mounting files and electrical interface.
- Automated interaction test harness and model-family integration checklist.
- Optional contracted testing with users who have disabilities; their participation is paid and does not create the core IP.

AccessCore does not promise legal compliance by itself. The OEM remains responsible for the finished product and service.

## Hidden Insight or Market Misunderstanding

The regulation is not the secret. The overlooked bottleneck is that a small OEM must integrate hardware and software behavior repeatedly across low-volume models, while established accessibility components often solve only part of the stack. A reusable design-in subsystem can become sticky once its firmware states, enclosure, application flow, tests, and procurement bill are embedded.

## Underused Momentum

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 and Poland has a binding implementing law. Self-service and payment-terminal manufacturers now face real product requirements. Existing global products prove the category, while small CEE OEMs may remain underserved on localization, price, and integration support.

## Beachhead Market

Polish/CEE terminal OEMs with 10–500 units per product family, no internal accessibility platform, and a live new-model or tender requirement. Start with information/ticket kiosks before banking or high-security payment devices.

## Business Model and Unit Economics

Hypotheses:

- Paid design-in/integration: 20,000–60,000 PLN per model family.
- Prototype module: 1,500–3,000 PLN.
- Production unit: 500–1,200 PLN with 45–65% hardware gross margin at small volumes.
- Annual firmware/localization/test maintenance: 8,000–25,000 PLN per OEM.
- The design-in is attractive only if it saves at least 80,000 PLN or three months versus internal work.

## First 10 and 100 Customers

**First 10:** Build a list from public catalogues, tender awards, and trade fairs; contact 60 OEM product/engineering leads; run 15 technical interviews; obtain three paid design-in commitments before tooling; deliver one reference installation and convert it into two model-family wins.

**First 100:** Sell through direct OEM design-ins, electronics distributors, and documented reference integrations. Expand Polish/English firmware to other CEE languages and add validated application adapters only where several OEMs share the interface.

## Primary Control Point

Owned reference design, firmware/API, interaction-state library, automated test harness, enclosure/tooling files, compatibility results, and model-specific design-in contracts. A signed production/design-in agreement is the proof of workflow access. The control point compounds only if OEM products depend on the module across multiple production runs.

## Cold Start and Resource Bootstrap

Spend up to 10,000 PLN on 15 buyer interviews and competitive teardown; 20,000–35,000 PLN on electronics/enclosure/firmware prototypes; 10,000–20,000 PLN on paid accessibility testing and iteration; and reserve 20,000 PLN for small-batch production and compliance/insurance review. Do not order tooling without three paid commitments. Proof is a working reference module, three signed design-ins, one integrated OEM prototype, and one production-unit order under a 90,000 PLN POC cap.

## Distribution Advantage

Public OEM catalogues and awards reveal prospects at product/tender events. Local language, time zone, lower-volume support, and reference firmware reduce integration friction. Each model integration expands the compatibility/test library and creates recurring production revenue.

## Domination and Expansion Path

Dominate low-volume Polish/CEE information and ticket kiosks, then move into check-in and payment-adjacent terminals. Add language packs, operating-system adapters, and test automation. Avoid broad accessibility consulting; become a component and firmware standard embedded in OEM bills of material.

## Poland/EU or Local Adaptation Defense

The initial edge is Polish/CEE localization, engineering support, low-volume economics, and knowledge of local product/tender requirements. Foreign vendors already exist, so local execution wins only if it is measurably cheaper/faster and secures design-ins before incumbents target these OEMs.

## Compounding Advantage

Firmware states, application adapters, enclosure patterns, test cases, accessibility feedback, OEM production history, and multi-model contracts accumulate. Production revenue and maintenance fund adjacent interfaces.

## Critical Evidence

The EAA and Polish law are in force. ANKER, Storm, and Vispero sell relevant hardware/software, proving demand and showing that the category is competitive.

## Critical Hypotheses

Small CEE OEMs are underserved rather than already buying established components; they will outsource a combined subsystem; three paid design-ins are obtainable without status; module integration creates durable switching cost; product liability and testing remain within budget.

## Kill Criteria

- Fewer than three paid design-ins after 60 qualified outreaches and 15 technical conversations.
- ANKER/Storm/JAWS or another incumbent meets buyer requirements at equal/lower total integration cost.
- Buyers want consulting or one-off customization but no repeat production module.
- Required certification/security pushes POC above 100,000 PLN.
- Production gross margin below 45% or expected annual OEM revenue below 30,000 PLN.

## Budget, Time, and Minimum People Stack

Months 0–3: interviews and commitments, 10,000 PLN. Months 3–6: prototype and tests, cumulative 45,000 PLN. Months 6–9: OEM integration, cumulative 70,000 PLN. Months 9–12: production order, 90,000 PLN total cap. Minimum stack: founder/product lead, contract electronics/firmware engineer, industrial designer, and paid accessibility testers.
