# Zero-to-One Evaluation: SafeCassette Legacy-Shelter Ventilation Standard

## Diagnosis

SafeCassette targets funded, urgent Polish infrastructure and has a credible installed-base model, but established manufacturers already sell multiple shelter systems and explicitly modernize old RM installations. Safety engineering, responsible-design requirements, public procurement, and supplier compatibility sit between the founder and every installation. The proposed interface is copyable and not yet shown to cover recurring geometries, so the business currently lacks an asymmetric entry against incumbents.

## Evidence and Assumptions

Official law, rules, funding, and tenders are sourced. Existing specialist supply and modernization competition are directly documented. Repeated geometries, lawful proprietary adapter ownership, 35–50% margin, procurement-compatible recurring cartridges, and sub-100,000 PLN testing are hypotheses.

## Control Point

**Tier 3.** The current decisive assets—compatible design, testing acceptance, procurement wins, and installed replacement rights—are prospective and depend on engineering/supplier/public-buyer acceptance. Strongest copy risk: FASER, Eurofaser, or another shelter specialist adds the adapter to its existing modernization offer.

## Expansion Check

The regional beachhead and Polish timing are good. Expansion remains a hardware/installation catalogue against better-credentialed incumbents. Local execution helps, but it does not stop established Polish firms moving into any repeated geometry.

## Fatal Objections

1. Direct incumbents already modernize legacy shelter ventilation.
2. Safety and procurement acceptance, rather than geometry data, may be the true control point.
3. The cartridge lock-in may be legally or commercially unavailable in public procurement.

## Strongest Salvageable Insight

Public assessments expose specific funded facilities at the exact modernization trigger.
